## 📖 What Is a Franchise?

A **franchise** is a business deal where a company (the **franchisor**) lets another person (the **franchisee**) open and run a location using the company's name, recipes, products, and system — in exchange for fees and a share of the money earned.

When you walk into a McDonald's in Houston and then another in New York City, they look almost identical and serve the same menu. That's franchising at work. Both locations are almost certainly owned by different local people — but they're all using the McDonald's system.

**Key People in a Franchise:**
| Role | Who They Are | What They Do |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| **Franchisor** | The original company (e.g., McDonald's Corp.) | Owns the brand, rules, recipes, and training system |
| **Franchisee** | The local owner (e.g., you) | Pays fees, runs the location, keeps remaining profits |
| **Territory** | A protected geographic area | Prevents other franchise owners from opening too close |

Kid-Friendly:
- Think of it like getting a license to use someone else's famous recipe and sign. You pay them money to use their name, and they teach you how to run the business their way. You take the financial risk, but you also get the reward if it succeeds.

## 💰 Key Financial Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Franchise Fee** | One-time upfront fee to join the franchise (like a membership) |
| **Royalty Fee** | Ongoing % of your monthly sales paid to the franchisor (usually 4–10%) |
| **Initial Investment** | Total money needed to open — franchise fee + building + equipment + inventory + cash reserve |
| **Working Capital** | Cash on hand to pay bills while you get started (payroll, rent, utilities) |
| **Break-Even Point** | The moment when your total revenue finally equals your total costs |
| **Net Profit** | Revenue minus ALL expenses. What you actually keep. |
| **FDD** | Franchise Disclosure Document — a 23-item legal document the franchisor must give you before you sign |
| **Royalty Rate** | The percentage paid as royalties — e.g., 6% royalty on $500,000 sales = $30,000/year to franchisor |

## 🍔 Restaurant Franchises

Restaurants are the most visible type of franchise. You probably pass several every day.

### McDonald's
- **Founded:** 1940 (Ray Kroc grew it into a national franchise starting in 1954)
- **U.S. Locations:** ~14,000
- **Franchise Fee:** ~$45,000
- **Total Investment:** ~$1,000,000 – $2,300,000
- **Royalty Rate:** ~4% of gross sales (plus rent — McDonald's often owns the land and charges franchisees for it)
- **Avg. Annual Sales per Location:** ~$3,000,000 – $3,500,000
- **Fun Fact:** McDonald's Corporation makes a large portion of its money from real estate — they own the land and lease it back to franchisees.
- Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonald%27s

### Subway
- **Founded:** 1965 (Fred DeLuca and Peter Buck started it with $1,000)
- **U.S. Locations:** ~20,000+
- **Franchise Fee:** ~$15,000
- **Total Investment:** ~$116,000 – $263,000 (one of the lowest upfront costs in fast food)
- **Royalty Rate:** 8% of sales + 4.5% advertising fund
- **Avg. Annual Sales per Location:** ~$400,000 – $500,000
- Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subway_(restaurant)

### Chick-fil-A
- **Founded:** 1946 (S. Truett Cathy)
- **U.S. Locations:** ~3,000
- **Operator Fee:** ~$10,000 (Chick-fil-A funds the buildout — very unusual in franchising)
- **Revenue Share:** ~50% of profits go to Chick-fil-A Corp (the trade-off for the low upfront cost)
- **Avg. Annual Sales per Location:** ~$8,000,000+ (highest per-unit average in fast food)
- **Fun Fact:** Less than 1% of applicants are accepted as Chick-fil-A operators.
- Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chick-fil-A

### Taco Bell
- **Founded:** 1962 (Glen Bell)
- **U.S. Locations:** ~8,000
- **Franchise Fee:** ~$45,000
- **Total Investment:** ~$575,000 – $3,400,000
- **Royalty Rate:** 5.5%
- **Avg. Annual Sales per Location:** ~$1,700,000
- Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taco_Bell

### Dunkin'
- **Founded:** 1950 (William Rosenberg)
- **U.S. Locations:** ~9,500
- **Franchise Fee:** ~$40,000 – $90,000
- **Total Investment:** ~$440,000 – $1,900,000
- **Royalty Rate:** 5.9%
- **Avg. Annual Sales per Location:** ~$1,000,000 – $1,100,000
- Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunkin%27

### Domino's
- **Founded:** 1960 (Tom Monaghan)
- **U.S. Locations:** ~6,700
- **Franchise Fee:** ~$10,000 – $25,000
- **Total Investment:** ~$119,000 – $461,000
- **Royalty Rate:** 5.5%
- **Avg. Annual Sales per Location:** ~$1,300,000
- Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domino%27s_Pizza

### Wingstop
- **Founded:** 1994
- **U.S. Locations:** ~1,800+
- **Franchise Fee:** ~$20,000
- **Total Investment:** ~$303,000 – $926,000
- **Royalty Rate:** 6%
- **Avg. Annual Sales per Location:** ~$1,300,000
- Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wingstop

## 🏢 Non-Restaurant Franchises

Franchising goes well beyond food. Services, retail, fitness, education, and home services are all major franchise categories.

### 7-Eleven (Convenience Store)
- **Founded:** 1927 (Dallas, TX) — America's original convenience store chain
- **U.S. Locations:** ~13,000
- **Total Investment:** ~$37,000 – $1,100,000 (varies widely based on location)
- **Model:** Unique split — franchisee keeps roughly 48–50% of gross profit, the rest goes to 7-Eleven Corp
- Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-Eleven

### Anytime Fitness (Gym)
- **Founded:** 2002
- **U.S. Locations:** ~3,500+
- **Franchise Fee:** ~$42,500
- **Total Investment:** ~$107,000 – $610,000
- **Royalty:** ~$699/month flat fee (not a percentage of sales — very unusual)
- Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anytime_Fitness

### The UPS Store (Shipping & Business Services)
- **Founded:** 1980 (as Mailboxes Etc.; rebranded in 2001)
- **U.S. Locations:** ~5,400
- **Franchise Fee:** ~$29,950
- **Total Investment:** ~$138,000 – $470,000
- **Royalty Rate:** 5%
- Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_UPS_Store

### Kumon (Education & Tutoring)
- **Founded:** 1958 in Japan; U.S. since 1974
- **U.S. Locations:** ~1,500+
- **Franchise Fee:** ~$1,000
- **Total Investment:** ~$67,000 – $145,000
- **Royalty:** Per-student monthly fee (~$37–$47/student enrolled)
- Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumon

### H&R Block (Tax Preparation)
- **Founded:** 1955 (Henry and Richard Bloch, Kansas City)
- **U.S. Locations:** ~7,500
- **Franchise Fee:** ~$2,500 – $80,000
- **Total Investment:** ~$27,000 – $138,000
- **Royalty Rate:** Up to 30% of revenue (very high compared to restaurant franchises)
- Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%26R_Block

## ✊ Black Entrepreneurs in Franchising

Franchising has been one of the most important tools for building Black wealth in America.

- **Herman Petty** — The first Black McDonald's franchisee (1968, Woodlawn neighborhood, Chicago). He became a role model and proved the franchise system could work for Black entrepreneurs in their own communities.
- **Valerie Daniels-Carter** — CEO of V&J Holdings, one of the largest minority-owned restaurant franchise groups in the U.S., operating hundreds of Burger King and Pizza Hut locations and employing thousands.
- **Roland L. Jones** — Pioneering multi-unit Wendy's franchisee who later wrote the book *So You Want to Be a Franchisee?* to help others follow the same path.
- **National Black McDonald's Operators Association (NBMOA)** — Founded in 1972, represents Black operators who collectively run hundreds of McDonald's locations. They have advocated for fair access, corporate support, and community investment within the franchise system.
- **The Legacy of Black Wall Street** — Franchise ownership is one of the strategies modern Black entrepreneurs use to build community wealth, following in the tradition of Black-owned business districts from Tulsa to Atlanta.

Kid-Friendly:
- Many Black business owners have used franchising to build wealth and create jobs in their communities. One person can own many locations and hire many employees — that's how real business empires are built.

## ⚖️ Pros and Cons of Franchising

### ✅ Pros (Why buy a franchise?)
- **Proven system**: The business model is already tested and working
- **Brand recognition**: Customers already know and trust the name
- **Training**: Franchisor trains you how to run the business
- **Marketing support**: National advertising campaigns drive customers to your door
- **Easier financing**: Banks are more comfortable lending for a proven brand
- **Peer network**: Other franchisees share tips and support each other

### ❌ Cons (What's the risk?)
- **High upfront cost**: Can require $200,000 – $2,000,000+ just to open the doors
- **Ongoing royalties**: You give up 4–10% of every dollar you make, forever
- **Less control**: Must follow the franchisor's rules (menu, décor, hours, suppliers)
- **Brand risk**: A corporate scandal or bad news hurts your location even if you did nothing wrong
- **Renewal uncertainty**: Franchise agreements expire; renewal is never guaranteed
- **Territory conflict**: The franchisor can open another franchise nearby if they choose

## 🧮 Simple 3-Year Financial Snapshot (Hypothetical Subway)

This shows why Year 1 almost always shows a loss — and why you must plan for at least 3–5 years.

```
Initial Investment (Year 1, one-time):   $190,000
                                         ─────────
YEAR 1
  Revenue:                               $430,000
  Cost of food (33% of sales):          -$141,900
  Labor:                                -$120,000
  Rent:                                  -$36,000
  Royalty (8%):                          -$34,400
  Advertising fund (4.5%):               -$19,350
  Utilities + insurance:                 -$18,000
  Startup costs (one-time):             -$190,000
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────
  NET YEAR 1:                           -$129,650   ← still in the hole

YEAR 2 (startup costs fully paid; sales growing)
  Revenue:                               $460,000
  All operating costs:                  -$369,650
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────
  NET YEAR 2:                           +$90,350    ← first profitable year!

YEAR 3
  Revenue:                               $490,000
  All operating costs:                  -$373,200
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────
  NET YEAR 3:                           +$116,800   ← building real wealth
```

*Numbers are illustrative. Real results depend on location, management, and market conditions.*
