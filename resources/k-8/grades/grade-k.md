# Kindergarten Planning Profile

Use this profile flexibly with observation and play. An adult leads and records; the learner may answer by talking, pointing, drawing, moving objects, or dictating. The year targets are planning examples, not a readiness gate or guarantee.

## Example measurable outcomes

This planning profile is supplementary. If its suggested sequence differs from the [canonical Grade K scope and sequence](../../../curriculum/grade-k/math/scope-and-sequence.md), the grade-track audit is authoritative for instruction.

- **Math:** Count a set of up to 20 objects once each; compare two sets/numerals within 10; represent addition and subtraction stories within 10 with objects or drawings; name and describe common 2D/3D shapes.
- **Language arts:** Retell a read-aloud with at least 3 key events/details and use 5 recently taught words across a week; build oral language and phonological awareness by identifying/manipulating rhymes and syllables, then phonemic awareness by blending/segmenting taught phonemes; learn letter-sound correspondences in a planned sequence; read cumulative decodable words/text containing only taught patterns; form practiced upper/lowercase letters and dictate or write a sentence.
- **Science:** Observe a plant or local weather across 5 days and record at least 3 observations, including when no change is noticed; sort examples as living/nonliving using an observed feature; ask a testable “what happens if” question and describe one result.
- **Social studies:** Describe one way home/community roles meet needs; use a simple map with a key to locate 3 familiar places; order 3 events from a familiar story or personal routine and explain one classroom fairness rule.

## Four-quarter sequence

| Quarter | Sequence, prerequisite, and evidence check |
|---|---|
| 1 — Routines and noticing | **Prerequisite:** Gather baseline samples; no print-reading prerequisite. **Math:** sort/count to 5 and compare more/less. **ELA:** oral language, rhymes, syllables, sound listening, names and initial letter-sounds; adult read-aloud daily. **Science:** observe weather and living things. **Social studies:** family/classroom roles and simple location words. **Check:** learner counts a new set to 5, retells one read-aloud event, identifies a rhyme, and points to familiar places on a drawn map. |
| 2 — Sound/quantity connections | **Prerequisite:** one-to-one counting and listening games. **Math:** quantities to 10, compose/decompose with objects, shapes. **ELA:** continue sound awareness; explicitly teach a cumulative letter-sound sequence, handwriting and encoding; use matched decodable text only as taught patterns allow. **Science:** compare plant/animal needs. **Social studies:** picture timelines and needs/wants. **Check:** model two ways to make 7; blend/segment taught sounds; read a new decodable phrase from known correspondences; dictate a sentence about a plant. |
| 3 — Stories and measurement | **Prerequisite:** represent small sets and connect some sounds to letters. **Math:** addition/subtraction stories within 10; compare length/weight informally. **ELA:** continue cumulative phonics/decodable practice, oral retell, sentence dictation and letter formation. **Science:** make a prediction and observe a simple material or shadow change. **Social studies:** create a map with a key and compare past/present in a familiar context. **Check:** solve a new story with a drawing, read a short taught-pattern text, and explain one observation. |
| 4 — Explain and revisit | **Prerequisite:** revisit individual weak sound-letter links and quantity combinations. **Math:** count/read/write to 20, solve varied stories within 10, identify shapes. **ELA:** review taught decoding/encoding patterns; read connected decodable text; compose one dictated or independently written sentence and retell a read-aloud. **Science:** share a 5-day observation. **Social studies:** explain a rule/role and use map/timeline. **Check:** adult records independent vs prompted performance on fresh samples; use gaps to plan grade 1 entry. |

## Beyond the core

Offer drawing, singing, rhythm, dramatic play, outdoor walking/climbing, sorting laundry, setting a table, and safe pouring/scooping. Keep activities short and adult-supervised; they supplement explicit instruction.
