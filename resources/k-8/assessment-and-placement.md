# Assessment and Placement

Use this page to choose an instructional starting point and decide what to teach next. It is a family planning aid, not a diagnostic test or a recommendation to skip grades.

## Before placing

1. Ask the learner to explain or demonstrate familiar work without coaching. For early literacy, listen to oral language and speech-sound awareness separately from memorized book reading.
2. Sample prerequisite skills from the previous profile. Offer manipulatives, oral response, drawing, large print, movement, or adult scribing when those supports measure the target more fairly.
3. Record the exact task, date, accuracy, strategy, response mode, and prompts needed. A correct answer after substantial modeling is practice evidence, not independent mastery.
4. Start with the first important unmet prerequisite. Teach it explicitly, then check again with a new but similar task. Do not use one timed quiz or age label as the whole placement decision.

## Evidence cycle

| Moment | Adult action | Example record |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | Gather one short sample per subject before planning | “Counts objects to 17; skips 13; independently uses counters.” |
| During teaching | Ask the learner to explain, perform, read, write, or model the target | Mark independent / with cue / modeled / not yet; note the error pattern |
| End of unit | Use a fresh task that requires transfer, not exact repetition | New word list, unfamiliar word problem, altered investigation, new source |
| Next plan | Continue, add practice, reteach prerequisite, or extend | Name the next observable action and when to check it |

## K–2 reading checks

Adult-led instruction should pair oral language and read-aloud comprehension with explicit, systematic foundational teaching. Check each component separately:

- **Oral language:** Can the learner retell a short read-aloud with key events/details, answer who/what/why questions, and use newly taught words in a sentence?
- **Phonemic awareness:** Can the learner identify, blend, segment, or manipulate spoken sounds at the level taught, without relying on printed letters?
- **Phonics and decoding:** Can the learner say taught letter-sounds and decode unfamiliar words containing only taught patterns? Use cumulative decodable words/text matched to what has been taught.
- **Spelling/encoding:** Can the learner segment sounds and write the corresponding taught spellings? Note which sound-letter links are secure.
- **Handwriting and composition:** Can the learner form practiced letters legibly enough for the task and produce a dictated, copied, or independently composed sentence appropriate to instruction?
- **Connected text:** Listen to a short decodable passage; note accuracy, effortful words, and whether the learner can retell. Do not use a predictable or memorized book as evidence of decoding.

If the learner persistently cannot hear/blend sounds, learn taught letter-sound links, or make progress despite explicit instruction and practice, pause to review instruction and seek qualified reading support. This page does not diagnose dyslexia or other conditions.

## Evidence by subject

- **Math:** Ask for a model or explanation as well as an answer. Use counters, drawings, number lines, equations, or a practical problem. Check whether the learner can transfer the idea when numbers or context change.
- **Science:** Preserve the question, prediction, observation/data, and explanation. Ask what changed, what stayed the same, and which observation supports the claim. Follow adult safety judgment.
- **Social studies:** Ask the learner to pose a question, identify a source or map feature, distinguish observation from claim, and communicate a conclusion. Keep age-appropriate language and content.
- **Writing/reading:** Collect a dated sample and note whether the adult read directions, scribed, or prompted. Separate decoding, fluency, comprehension, spelling, sentence construction, and idea development.

## Short sample prompts

These are illustrative probes, not normed tests. Use only after confirming the learner has had a fair chance to learn the skill; accept an accessible response mode and record supports.

| Area | Prompt | Evidence to notice |
|---|---|---|
| Early number | Put 8 counters down. “Show another way to split these into two groups. How do you know there are still 8?” | Counts each object once; explains the two parts still compose 8, rather than recounting only one part. |
| Place value | “Show 46 with tens and ones. What changes if I add 10?” | Represents 4 tens/6 ones; identifies 5 tens/6 ones after adding 10 and explains the ones did not change. |
| Multiplication | “There are 4 bags with 6 apples each. Draw or build it and find the total.” | Makes 4 equal groups/array; connects repeated addition or known fact to 24. |
| Fractions | “Draw 3 ÷ 1/4. How many one-fourths fit in 3 wholes?” | Partitions each whole into fourths, counts 12 pieces, and distinguishes this from 3 ÷ 4. |
| Linear relationships | “A $4 sign-up fee is followed by $3 per visit. What is the cost for 5 visits? Write a rule.” | Separates fixed fee from repeated cost; writes 4 + 3v and gets $19. This checks grade 8 modeling readiness, not only arithmetic. |
| Foundational reading | After teaching the correspondences, ask the learner to read new words that use only those patterns, then a matched decodable sentence. | Applies sound-spelling links to unfamiliar words; does not rely solely on picture guessing or memorized text. Record each error pattern. |
| Comprehension | Read a short unfamiliar passage aloud if decoding is not the target. Ask for the main idea and two details. | Separates comprehension from decoding; gives relevant details and can point back to the text or illustration. |
| Science inquiry | Show two paper towel samples with the same water amount, then ask what to measure to compare absorbency fairly. | Names a measurable outcome and a factor to keep constant; explains how observations support a conclusion. |
| Source reasoning | Give two short accounts of the same event. Ask who made each and one way they differ. | Notices creator/context and a specific difference; does not treat one source as automatically complete. |

## Practical decision rules

- **Continue:** The learner demonstrates the target independently on more than one occasion and can apply it in a changed example.
- **Practice:** The idea is understood with a cue but remains inconsistent; schedule brief, cumulative retrieval practice.
- **Reteach:** The learner cannot yet explain or model a prerequisite; use a smaller step, explicit modeling, and feedback.
- **Extend:** The learner transfers the skill easily; add a richer problem, more complex text/source, or an independent investigation.
- **Adapt:** Change the response mode or access supports while keeping the learning target stable; document what support was used.

For additional expectations, compare local adopted standards with the [official reference links](./sources.md). This guide does not supply benchmark scores or validated assessments.
