A student-friendly guide to how sentences are built — from the smallest pieces to full, complex thoughts.

## 1. 🏗️ The Two Core Parts of Every Sentence

Every complete sentence must have two things: a **subject** and a **predicate**.

| Part | What It Is | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Subject** | Who or what the sentence is about | **The dog** barked loudly. |
| **Predicate** | What the subject does, is, or has | The dog **barked loudly**. |

> **Rule:** A group of words missing either a subject or a predicate is a **fragment** — not a complete sentence.
> - Fragment: *Running through the park.* (no subject — who is running?)
> - Complete: *She was running through the park.*

---

## 2. 🎯 The Subject

The subject tells us **who or what** the sentence is about.

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Simple subject** | The main noun or pronoun — just the key word | **Marcus** plays basketball. |
| **Complete subject** | The simple subject plus all its describing words | **The tall kid from Chicago** plays basketball. |
| **Compound subject** | Two or more subjects joined by a conjunction | **Marcus and Core learner** play basketball. |

> **Tip:** To find the subject, ask yourself: *Who or what is doing the action (or being described)?*

---

## 3. ⚙️ The Predicate

The predicate tells us **what the subject does, is, or has**.

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Simple predicate** | Just the main verb | The dog **barked**. |
| **Complete predicate** | The verb plus all its modifiers and objects | The dog **barked at the mail carrier all morning**. |
| **Compound predicate** | Two or more verbs with the same subject | She **sang and danced** on stage. |

---

## 4. 📦 Objects

Objects receive the action of the verb.

| Object | Question It Answers | Example |
|--------|--------------------|---------|
| **Direct object (DO)** | Verb + whom? or what? | She threw **the ball**. |
| **Indirect object (IO)** | Verb + to whom? or for whom? | She threw **Marcus** the ball. |

> In the example above: *threw* is the verb, *the ball* is the DO, *Marcus* is the IO.
> Trick: IO always appears **between** the verb and the direct object.

---

## 5. 🔗 Complements

Complements complete the meaning of a linking verb.

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Predicate nominative (PN)** | A noun/pronoun that renames the subject after a linking verb | She is the **captain**. |
| **Predicate adjective (PA)** | An adjective that describes the subject after a linking verb | The soup smells **delicious**. |

> Common linking verbs: *is, are, was, were, am, seem, appear, feel, look, taste, smell, sound, become*

---

## 6. 🧩 Phrases

A **phrase** is a group of related words that works as a unit but does NOT contain both a subject and a verb.

| Phrase Type | What It Contains | Example |
|-------------|-----------------|---------|
| **Noun phrase** | Noun + its modifiers | **The old leather jacket** hung on the hook. |
| **Verb phrase** | Main verb + helping verbs | She **has been running** every morning. |
| **Prepositional phrase** | Preposition + object | He sat **under the old oak tree**. |
| **Participial phrase** | Past/present participle + modifiers (acts as adjective) | **Covered in mud**, the dog ran inside. |
| **Gerund phrase** | Gerund (-ing verb used as noun) + modifiers | **Running every day** builds endurance. |
| **Infinitive phrase** | *to* + verb + modifiers (acts as noun, adj., or adv.) | She wanted **to win the championship**. |
| **Appositive phrase** | Noun phrase that renames the noun beside it | My brother, **a talented musician**, plays guitar. |

---

## 7. 📎 Clauses

A **clause** is a group of words that HAS both a subject and a verb.

| Type | Description | Can Stand Alone? | Example |
|------|-------------|-----------------|---------|
| **Independent clause** | Has a subject and verb; expresses a complete thought | ✅ Yes | *The rain fell all night.* |
| **Dependent clause** | Has a subject and verb; does NOT express a complete thought | ❌ No | *because the rain fell all night* |

### Types of Dependent Clauses

| Clause | Acts As | Signal Words | Example |
|--------|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Adjective (relative) clause** | Adjective — describes a noun | who, whom, whose, which, that | The student **who scored highest** won a prize. |
| **Adverb clause** | Adverb — describes a verb | because, although, when, while, if, since, unless, until | She smiled **when she heard the news**. |
| **Noun clause** | Noun | that, what, whoever, whether, how | **What you said** surprised everyone. |

---

## 8. 🏛️ The Four Sentence Types (by structure)

| Type | Definition | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Simple** | One independent clause | *The bird sang.* |
| **Compound** | Two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS) or semicolon | *The bird sang, **and** the flowers bloomed.* |
| **Complex** | One independent clause + one or more dependent clauses | ***Because it was spring**, the bird sang.* |
| **Compound-Complex** | Two or more independent clauses + one or more dependent clauses | ***Although it was cold**, the bird sang, **and** the crowd cheered.* |

---

## 9. 💬 The Four Sentence Types (by purpose)

| Type | Purpose | Punctuation | Example |
|------|---------|------------|---------|
| **Declarative** | Makes a statement | Period (.) | The library closes at 9. |
| **Interrogative** | Asks a question | Question mark (?) | When does the library close? |
| **Imperative** | Gives a command or request | Period or ! | Please be quiet in the library. |
| **Exclamatory** | Expresses strong emotion | Exclamation mark (!) | I can't believe the library is closed! |

---

## 10. ⚠️ Common Sentence Errors

| Error | What It Is | Example | Fix |
|-------|-----------|---------|-----|
| **Fragment** | Missing subject or predicate | *Running down the street.* | Add a subject: *She was running down the street.* |
| **Run-on** | Two independent clauses joined incorrectly | *I was tired I went to bed.* | Add punctuation/conjunction: *I was tired, so I went to bed.* |
| **Comma splice** | Two independent clauses joined with only a comma | *I was tired, I went to bed.* | Use a semicolon or add a conjunction: *I was tired; I went to bed.* |
