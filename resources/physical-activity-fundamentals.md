# 🏃 Physical Activity Fundamentals

A student-friendly reference covering daily outdoor activity, vitamin D, heart rate, and strength training basics with proper form.

## Why Daily Movement Matters

Regular physical activity helps:
- Build strong bones and muscles
- Improve mood, focus, and sleep
- Maintain a healthy heart
- Learn teamwork and coordination

Kids and teens should aim for at least **60 minutes** of physical activity most days.

## Vitamin D & Sunshine

- Your body makes Vitamin D when skin is exposed to sunlight.
- Vitamin D helps absorb calcium for strong bones.
- About **15–20 minutes** of sunlight a day is usually enough.
- After that, use sunscreen to protect your skin.

## Heart Rate Basics

Your **pulse** is your heart rate — the number of beats per minute (BPM).

| Activity | Typical BPM |
|----------|-------------|
| Resting (sitting) | 70–100 |
| Walking | 100–120 |
| Running/playing hard | 120–160+ |

### How to Check Your Pulse
1. Place two fingers on your wrist (thumb side) or neck.
2. Count beats for 30 seconds.
3. Multiply by 2 to get BPM.

## Activity Intensity

- **Light**: easy breathing, can talk easily (walking)
- **Moderate**: breathing harder, can talk in short sentences (jogging, biking)
- **Vigorous**: breathing hard, hard to talk (sprinting, jumping rope)

## Strength Training Basics

Strength training uses body weight or light resistance to build muscle. It is safe and effective for kids when done with good form.

### Key Principle: Form Over Speed

Slow, controlled movements protect joints and build real strength.

### The Three Core Movements

#### Push-Ups
1. Hands under shoulders, body straight like a plank.
2. Lower chest toward floor (or as far as you can).
3. Push back up fully.

**Easier:** Drop to knees.  
**Harder:** Add a backpack with books, or elevate feet.

#### Squats
1. Feet shoulder-width apart.
2. Lower like sitting in a chair.
3. Keep knees behind toes.
4. Stand back up fully.

**Easier:** Hold a chair for balance.  
**Harder:** Hold a water bottle or backpack at chest.

#### Body-Weight Dead Lift
1. Feet hip-width apart.
2. Hinge at hips — push butt back.
3. Keep back flat, chest up.
4. Lower torso until you feel a stretch in legs.
5. Squeeze glutes and stand back up.

**Easier:** Don’t go as far down; focus on form.  
**Harder:** Hold a heavy book or backpack.

### Warm-Up (Always Do This First)

- Arm circles — 30 seconds
- High knees — 30 seconds
- Torso twists — 30 seconds

### Cool-Down (After Training)

Hold each stretch for 15 seconds:
- Arms across chest
- Hamstring stretch (reach toward toes)
- Quad stretch (pull one foot behind you)

## Structuring a Workout

- **Sets:** How many rounds you do.
- **Reps:** How many times inside one set.
- **Rest:** 60–90 seconds between sets.

Example:
```
Push-ups: 2 sets
Squats: 2 sets
Dead lifts: 2 sets
```

## Hydration & Safety

- Drink water before, during, and after activity.
- Wear proper shoes (sneakers, not flip-flops).
- Stop if something hurts — pain is different from muscle fatigue.
- Use sunscreen and hats when outside.
