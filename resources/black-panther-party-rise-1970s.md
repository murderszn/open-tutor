# Black Panther Party: History and the 1970s

## Summary
The Black Panther Party (BPP) was founded in Oakland, California, in 1966 by Huey P. Newton and Bobby Seale. The group started as a response to police brutality and racial injustice, then grew into a national organization known for community programs, political education, and self-defense activism.

## How the Party Rose in Influence by the 1970s
It is common to hear the phrase “rise to power in the 70s,” but the BPP’s peak growth actually began in the **late 1960s** and carried into the **early 1970s**.

### 1) Visibility and Messaging
- The Panthers used a clear public image: black berets, leather jackets, and visible patrols.
- Their Ten-Point Program gave people a direct explanation of their goals: housing, education, employment, justice, and freedom from police violence.
- The group’s newspaper spread ideas and updates across many cities.

### 2) Rapid National Expansion
- After founding in Oakland, chapters opened across the U.S.
- Local branches organized neighborhood events, classes, and aid programs.
- By the early 1970s, the Panthers were one of the most recognized Black liberation organizations in the country.

### 3) Community “Survival Programs”
The Panthers grew influence by meeting practical needs:
- Free Breakfast for Children
- Free health clinics
- Clothing and shoe drives
- Sickle cell anemia awareness/testing support
- Transportation help for families with incarcerated loved ones

These programs built trust and showed that political movements could also provide daily support.

### 4) Youth, Campus, and Cultural Impact
- Young people and college students connected with the Panthers’ call for dignity and self-determination.
- The BPP influenced art, music, posters, and political language in the 1970s.
- Even people who did not join the party were shaped by its ideas on pride, organizing, and community defense.

## Challenges in the 1970s
As the party became more visible, it also faced intense pressure:
- Government surveillance and disruption (including COINTELPRO-related targeting)
- Arrests, legal battles, and leadership conflicts
- Internal disagreements over strategy

These pressures reduced the party’s national strength over time, though some chapters continued local community work.

## Why the Black Panther Party Still Matters
The BPP helped reshape conversations about:
- Community safety
- Food access and health equity
- Black political organizing
- Youth activism and civic participation

Many modern community aid efforts and justice campaigns still echo strategies the Panthers used.

## Vocabulary
- **Self-determination**: The right of people to control their own future.
- **Mutual aid**: People in a community directly supporting one another.
- **Political education**: Learning how systems of power work and how to participate in change.
- **Surveillance**: Close monitoring by authorities.
