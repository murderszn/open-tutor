# 🧪 Micronutrients — Vitamins & Minerals

A student-friendly reference covering vitamins and minerals: what they are, what each one does, how much you need, where to find it in food, and what happens when you don't get enough. Supports the **Vitamin & Mineral Detective** assignment and any future nutrition work.

## 🗂️ Vocabulary (kid-friendly)

- **Micronutrient:** A nutrient the body needs in small amounts — vitamins and minerals. Unlike macronutrients, they don't provide calories, but they enable almost every chemical reaction in the body.
- **Vitamin:** An organic compound (contains carbon) that the body can't make enough of on its own and must get from food. 13 essential vitamins in total.
- **Mineral:** An inorganic element (does not contain carbon) found in soil and water that plants absorb and animals eat. Examples: calcium, iron, zinc.
- **Fat-soluble vitamin:** Vitamins A, D, E, and K — dissolved and stored in fat tissue and the liver. Can accumulate to toxic levels if you take too much in supplement form.
- **Water-soluble vitamin:** B vitamins and vitamin C — dissolved in water, not stored long-term. Excess is excreted in urine, so you need them regularly. Less risk of toxicity.
- **RDA (Recommended Dietary Allowance):** The daily intake level estimated to meet the needs of 97–98% of healthy people. The standard on nutrition labels is based on adult values.
- **Deficiency:** When the body doesn't get enough of a nutrient — leads to specific symptoms or diseases.
- **Antioxidant:** A molecule that neutralizes free radicals (unstable atoms that damage cells). Vitamins C and E are key antioxidants.
- **Enzyme cofactor:** Many vitamins and minerals help enzymes work — they act as "keys" that activate the enzyme. Without the cofactor, the chemical reaction can't happen.

## ☀️ Fat-Soluble Vitamins (A, D, E, K)

These dissolve in fat, are stored in the liver and fatty tissue, and stay in your body longer. You don't need them every single day — but you do need them consistently.

### Vitamin A

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **What it does** | Vision (especially night vision), immune function, skin and cell growth |
| **Key role** | Forms rhodopsin — the light-sensing pigment in your eyes |
| **RDA (teens)** | ~700–900 mcg/day |
| **Best food sources** | Sweet potato, carrots, spinach, kale, eggs, liver, dairy |
| **Deficiency** | Night blindness (first sign), increased infection risk, dry skin |
| **Toxicity warning** | Too much from supplements (not food) causes headache, liver damage — fat-soluble vitamins accumulate |

### Vitamin D

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **What it does** | Calcium absorption, bone health, immune function, mood regulation |
| **Key role** | Without vitamin D, calcium can't be properly absorbed — even if you eat plenty of calcium |
| **Special fact** | The body makes vitamin D from sunlight — 15–20 min of sun on skin = significant production |
| **RDA (teens)** | 600 IU (15 mcg)/day |
| **Best food sources** | Fatty fish (salmon, tuna), fortified milk, egg yolks, mushrooms exposed to sunlight |
| **Deficiency** | Rickets (soft, weak bones in children), fatigue, increased infections, low mood |
| **Very common deficiency** | Especially in people with darker skin, people who spend little time outdoors, or people in northern climates with less sunlight |

### Vitamin E

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **What it does** | Antioxidant — protects cell membranes from damage; supports immune function |
| **Key role** | Shields cell membranes (made of fat) from oxidative damage, especially in red blood cells |
| **RDA (teens)** | 15 mg/day |
| **Best food sources** | Sunflower seeds, almonds, peanut butter, spinach, avocado, olive oil |
| **Deficiency** | Rare; causes nerve and muscle damage, weakened immune system in severe cases |

### Vitamin K

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **What it does** | Blood clotting and bone mineralization |
| **Key role** | Activates proteins required to form blood clots — without it, wounds don't stop bleeding |
| **Two types** | K1 (leafy greens) — for clotting; K2 (fermented foods, animal products) — for bone/heart health |
| **RDA (teens)** | 75–120 mcg/day |
| **Best food sources** | Kale, spinach, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, fermented cheese, natto (fermented soy) |
| **Deficiency** | Excessive bleeding, easy bruising |

## 💧 Water-Soluble Vitamins (B Vitamins + C)

These dissolve in water and are not stored long-term. Excess is flushed out in urine, so you need them regularly from food. Much lower risk of toxicity from food sources.

### The B Vitamins (B1–B12)

There are 8 B vitamins. They mostly act as **enzyme cofactors** — they help enzymes run the chemical reactions that convert food into energy and build new molecules.

| Vitamin | Also called | Main job | Best sources | Deficiency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Thiamine | Energy from carbs; nerve function | Whole grains, pork, legumes | Beriberi (nerve/heart damage) |
| B2 | Riboflavin | Energy metabolism; cell growth | Dairy, eggs, meat, leafy greens | Sores at corners of mouth, fatigue |
| B3 | Niacin | Energy metabolism; DNA repair | Chicken, tuna, peanuts, brown rice | Pellagra (dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia) |
| B5 | Pantothenic acid | Makes coenzyme A — central to fat + carb metabolism | Almost all foods; widespread | Rare; tingling in feet, fatigue |
| B6 | Pyridoxine | Protein metabolism; neurotransmitter production; immune function | Chicken, fish, potatoes, bananas | Irritability, depression, anemia |
| B7 | Biotin | Fat synthesis; gene expression; hair/nail health | Eggs (especially yolk), almonds, sweet potato | Hair loss, skin rash (rare in healthy diets) |
| B9 | Folate (food) / Folic acid (supplement) | DNA synthesis and repair; critical during pregnancy | Leafy greens, legumes, fortified grains | Neural tube defects in pregnancy; megaloblastic anemia |
| B12 | Cobalamin | Red blood cell formation; nerve health; DNA synthesis | Meat, fish, dairy, eggs — **NO plant sources** | Anemia, nerve damage, fatigue; vegans must supplement |

> **B12 is critical:** It's only found in animal products. Vegans and vegetarians must supplement B12 or eat fortified foods.

### Vitamin C

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **What it does** | Collagen synthesis, antioxidant, immune boosting, iron absorption |
| **Key role** | Collagen is the structural protein in skin, tendons, blood vessels, and bones — vitamin C is required to make it |
| **Also does** | Helps the body absorb non-heme iron (the type in plant foods) — pair leafy greens with orange juice |
| **RDA (teens)** | 65–75 mg/day (easy to hit — one orange = ~70mg) |
| **Best food sources** | Bell peppers (most concentrated source!), citrus fruit, strawberries, broccoli, kiwi |
| **Deficiency** | Scurvy — bleeding gums, fatigue, joint pain, wounds that won't heal. Historically killed sailors on long voyages with no fresh produce |
| **Water soluble** | Excess is excreted — very hard to overdose from food |

## 🪨 Key Minerals

Minerals are elements — they come from the earth, absorbed by plants and passed up the food chain. They don't get broken down by cooking (unlike some vitamins). But they can leach into cooking water, so steaming preserves more than boiling.

### Calcium

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **What it does** | Bone and teeth structure; muscle contraction; nerve signaling; blood clotting |
| **Key fact** | 99% of the body's calcium is stored in bones and teeth — bones are essentially a calcium bank |
| **RDA (teens 9–18)** | 1,300 mg/day — highest of any life stage because bones are still forming |
| **Best food sources** | Dairy (milk, yogurt, cheese), fortified plant milks, sardines, kale, broccoli, almonds |
| **Deficiency** | Bone loss (osteoporosis over time), muscle cramps, dental problems |
| **Needs vitamin D** | Calcium can't be absorbed without adequate vitamin D — they work as a pair |

### Iron

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **What it does** | Carries oxygen in red blood cells (via hemoglobin); energy production; immune function |
| **Key fact** | Iron is the core of hemoglobin — without enough iron, red blood cells can't carry enough oxygen → fatigue |
| **Two types** | Heme iron (meat, fish) — absorbed 2–3× more efficiently; non-heme iron (plants, fortified foods) — absorption improved by vitamin C |
| **RDA (teen boys)** | 11 mg/day |
| **RDA (teen girls)** | 15 mg/day (higher due to menstrual blood loss) |
| **Best food sources** | Red meat, shellfish, liver, lentils, spinach, fortified cereal, tofu |
| **Deficiency** | Iron-deficiency anemia — fatigue, pale skin, weakness, brain fog; most common nutritional deficiency worldwide |

### Potassium

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **What it does** | Fluid balance, nerve signals, muscle contraction, blood pressure regulation |
| **Key fact** | Works with sodium to control fluid inside/outside cells (sodium-potassium pump — every cell uses this) |
| **RDA (teens)** | 2,300–3,000 mg/day |
| **Best food sources** | Bananas, sweet potatoes, avocados, spinach, beans, salmon, dairy |
| **Deficiency** | Muscle weakness, cramps, irregular heartbeat (hypokalemia) |
| **Most Americans** | Get too much sodium and too little potassium — reverses the healthy ratio |

### Magnesium

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **What it does** | 300+ enzyme reactions; muscle and nerve function; blood sugar regulation; protein synthesis; bone strength |
| **Key fact** | Involved in ATP production — without magnesium, cells can't generate energy properly |
| **RDA (teens)** | 360–410 mg/day |
| **Best food sources** | Dark chocolate (yes, really), almonds, spinach, black beans, avocado, whole grains |
| **Deficiency** | Muscle cramps, fatigue, sleep trouble, anxiety; very common (many people are mildly deficient) |

### Zinc

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **What it does** | Immune function, wound healing, taste and smell, DNA synthesis, growth |
| **Key fact** | Required for over 300 enzymes; critical during periods of rapid growth |
| **RDA (teen boys)** | 11 mg/day |
| **Best food sources** | Oysters (highest source), beef, pumpkin seeds, cashews, chickpeas, dairy |
| **Deficiency** | Impaired growth, hair loss, weakened immunity, loss of taste/smell |

### Sodium

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **What it does** | Fluid balance, nerve function, muscle contraction |
| **Key fact** | Works with potassium in the sodium-potassium pump — essential, but most people eat too much |
| **Daily target** | Less than 2,300 mg/day (about 1 teaspoon of salt) |
| **Average American** | Eats ~3,400 mg/day — mostly from processed and restaurant food |
| **Too much** | Raises blood pressure, increases risk of heart disease and stroke over time |

### Phosphorus

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **What it does** | Bone and teeth structure (with calcium); energy storage (part of ATP); cell membranes |
| **Key fact** | ATP — the body's energy currency — literally has phosphorus in its name (adenosine tri*phosphate*) |
| **RDA (teens)** | 1,250 mg/day |
| **Best food sources** | Meat, fish, dairy, eggs, legumes, nuts — widespread; rarely deficient |

## 🗺️ The Big Picture: Deficiency Diseases (Historical + Current)

| Disease | Missing nutrient | Key symptoms | Where it still occurs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scurvy | Vitamin C | Bleeding gums, fatigue, wounds won't heal | Rare; historically killed sailors on long voyages |
| Rickets | Vitamin D + Calcium | Soft, bowed bones in children | Still occurs in regions with low sun + poor diet |
| Beriberi | Vitamin B1 (Thiamine) | Nerve damage, heart failure | Historically common in regions relying on white rice |
| Pellagra | Vitamin B3 (Niacin) | "4 Ds": Dermatitis, Diarrhea, Dementia, Death | Historically common in corn-reliant populations |
| Anemia (iron) | Iron | Fatigue, pale skin, weakness | Most common nutritional deficiency worldwide today |
| Night blindness | Vitamin A | Can't see in low light | Still common in parts of Africa and Southeast Asia |
| Goiter | Iodine | Enlarged thyroid gland | Eliminated in most countries by iodized salt |

## 🥗 "Perfect Day" Micro-Coverage Guide

You don't need to eat every micronutrient every meal — just across the day. Here's how to hit the big ones:

| Micronutrient | One easy food that covers it | Amount per serving |
|---|---|---|
| Vitamin C | 1 bell pepper (red) | 190mg — 2–3× RDA in one pepper |
| Vitamin A | 1 medium sweet potato | ~960 mcg — over 100% RDA |
| Vitamin D | 3 oz salmon | ~570 IU — nearly a full day's worth |
| Vitamin K | 1 cup cooked spinach | ~990 mcg — many times the RDA |
| Vitamin B12 | 3 oz beef | ~2.4 mcg — meets the daily RDA |
| Iron | 1 cup cooked lentils | ~6.6 mg — about 60% of teen daily target |
| Calcium | 1 cup milk or yogurt | ~300 mg — about 23% of teen daily target |
| Potassium | 1 medium baked potato | ~900 mg — about 30–40% of daily target |
| Magnesium | 1 oz dark chocolate (70%+) | ~65 mg — about 15–18% of daily target |
| Zinc | 3 oz beef | ~5–7 mg — about 50–65% of teen daily target |
