## Part 1: Figurative Language

Figurative language uses words in creative, non-literal ways to create images, emotion, and emphasis.

---

### 🔁 Simile
**Comparing two unlike things using *like* or *as*.**

> *Her smile was **like** sunshine.*
> *He was **as** strong **as** an ox.*
> *The classroom was **as quiet as** a library.*

**Tip:** If you see "like" or "as" making a comparison, it's a simile.

---

### 🔃 Metaphor
**Stating that one thing *is* another — a direct comparison without "like" or "as".**

> *Life is a journey.*
> *The world is a stage.*
> *Her voice was music to his ears.*
> *Time is money.*

**Extended metaphor:** A metaphor that is developed over several lines or throughout an entire piece.

**Mixed metaphor:** Accidentally combining two unrelated metaphors (usually a mistake): *"We'll burn that bridge when we cross it."*

---

### 🧍 Personification
**Giving human qualities, feelings, or actions to non-human things.**

> *The wind **whispered** through the trees.*
> *The stars **danced** in the night sky.*
> *Opportunity **knocked** at his door.*
> *The old car **groaned** as it climbed the hill.*

---

### 🔤 Alliteration
**The repetition of the same consonant sound at the beginning of nearby words.**

> ***P**eter **P**iper **p**icked a **p**eck of **p**ickled **p**eppers.*
> *She sells sea shells by the sea shore.*
> *The **s**ilent **s**now **s**lowly **s**ettled.*

**Tip:** Alliteration creates rhythm and is heavily used in poetry, song lyrics, and brand names (*Coca-Cola, Dunkin' Donuts, Best Buy*).

---

### 💥 Onomatopoeia
**Words that imitate the sounds they describe.**

> *The bees **buzzed**. The door **creaked**. The fire **crackled**.*
> *Boom! Crash! Sizzle! Pop! Whisper. Hiss. Thud.*

---

### 📣 Hyperbole
**A wild exaggeration used for emphasis or humor — not meant to be taken literally.**

> *I've told you a **million times**.*
> *I'm so hungry I could eat a horse.*
> *This backpack weighs a **ton**.*
> *I have a **mountain** of homework.*

---

### 🧩 Idiom
**A phrase whose meaning cannot be figured out from the literal meanings of the individual words.**

| Idiom | Actual Meaning |
|-------|----------------|
| *It's raining cats and dogs* | It's raining very hard |
| *Break a leg* | Good luck |
| *Hit the nail on the head* | Got it exactly right |
| *Bite off more than you can chew* | Take on too much |
| *Spill the beans* | Reveal a secret |
| *Under the weather* | Feeling sick |
| *The ball is in your court* | It's your decision now |

---

### 🖼️ Imagery
**Descriptive language that appeals to one or more of the five senses (sight, sound, smell, taste, touch) to create a vivid mental picture.**

> *The fresh bread **smelled** of warm butter and honey. The crust **crackled** under his fingers as he tore it apart.*

Imagery often combines several other devices (simile, metaphor, personification).

---

### 🔣 Symbolism
**When a person, object, place, or event represents something beyond its literal meaning.**

Common symbols in literature:
| Symbol | Often Represents |
|--------|-----------------|
| A dove | Peace |
| A storm | Conflict, turmoil, change |
| Light/dawn | Hope, new beginnings |
| Darkness/night | Fear, the unknown, evil |
| A rose | Love, beauty, but also danger (thorns) |
| A journey or road | Life, growth, change |
| Water | Rebirth, cleansing, the unconscious |
| Chains/cages | Oppression, lack of freedom |

---

### 🎭 Irony
**When the opposite of what is expected happens, or when words mean the opposite of what they say.**

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Verbal irony** | Saying the opposite of what you mean (sarcasm is one form) | *"Oh great, another Monday,"* said with dread. |
| **Situational irony** | An unexpected outcome — the opposite of what would logically happen | A fire station burning down. A police station getting robbed. |
| **Dramatic irony** | The audience knows something the characters don't | In Romeo & Juliet, we know Juliet is alive but Romeo thinks she's dead. |

## Part 2: Story Elements & Narrative Devices

---

### 📖 Plot — The sequence of events in a story

| Stage | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Exposition** | Introduction — characters, setting, and background are established |
| **Rising action** | Events that build tension and lead toward the climax |
| **Climax** | The turning point — the moment of greatest tension or conflict |
| **Falling action** | Events that follow the climax, leading toward resolution |
| **Resolution (Denouement)** | The conclusion — loose ends are tied up |

---

### ⚔️ Conflict — The central struggle in the story

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Character vs. Character** | One person against another | Harry Potter vs. Voldemort |
| **Character vs. Self** | Inner struggle — doubt, fear, temptation | Hamlet deciding whether to act |
| **Character vs. Nature** | Person against the natural world | A sailor surviving a storm |
| **Character vs. Society** | Individual against rules, norms, or systems | Huck Finn defying slavery norms |
| **Character vs. Technology** | Person against machines or systems | Robots threatening humanity |
| **Character vs. Fate/Supernatural** | Forces beyond human control | Greek heroes battling the gods' plans |

---

### 🌍 Setting
**The time and place where a story occurs.** Setting includes:
- Physical location (city, forest, outer space)
- Time period (ancient Rome, the future, the 1960s)
- Weather and atmosphere
- Social/cultural environment

Setting often shapes mood and character behavior.

---

### 👤 Character
**The people (or beings) who take part in the story's events.**

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Protagonist** | The main character — the story follows their journey |
| **Antagonist** | The force or character in opposition to the protagonist |
| **Static character** | Does not change or grow throughout the story |
| **Dynamic character** | Changes significantly as a result of events |
| **Round character** | Complex, multi-dimensional, with strengths and flaws |
| **Flat character** | One-dimensional, simple, often serves one purpose |
| **Foil** | A character who contrasts with another to highlight traits |

**Characterization** — how an author reveals a character's personality:
- **Direct**: The author simply tells you (*"Maria was impatient."*)
- **Indirect**: Shown through dialogue, actions, thoughts, others' reactions (STEAL: Speech, Thoughts, Effect on others, Actions, Looks)

---

### 💡 Theme
**The central message, lesson, or insight about life that a story conveys.** Not the same as the topic.

- Topic: *Friendship*
- Theme: *True friendship means standing by someone even when it costs you something.*

Common literary themes:
- Good vs. evil
- Power and corruption
- Coming of age / growing up
- Identity and belonging
- Sacrifice and love
- Justice and injustice
- Courage in the face of fear

---

### 🎙️ Point of View (POV)
**The perspective from which a story is narrated.**

| POV | Pronouns Used | Description | Example |
|-----|--------------|-------------|---------|
| **First person** | I, me, my, we | Narrator is a character in the story | *I walked into the room and froze.* |
| **Second person** | You, your | Reader is directly addressed (rare in fiction) | *You step through the door and see it.* |
| **Third person limited** | He, she, they | Narrator follows one character's thoughts/feelings | *She wondered if he had seen her.* |
| **Third person omniscient** | He, she, they | Narrator knows ALL characters' thoughts and feelings | *He was nervous; she was furious; neither knew what the other was thinking.* |

---

### 🔮 Foreshadowing
**Hints or clues early in a story that suggest what will happen later.**

> In *Romeo and Juliet*, Romeo says early on: *"My life were better ended by their hate, than death prorogued, wanting of thy love."* — hinting at his eventual fate.

---

### ⏪ Flashback
**A scene that interrupts the present action to show something that happened in the past.**

> A character smells a familiar food and we're transported to a childhood memory that explains why they behave a certain way today.

---

### 😰 Suspense
**A feeling of tension, uncertainty, or excitement about what will happen next.** Authors build suspense through cliffhangers, unanswered questions, and pacing.

---

### 🎨 Tone vs. Mood

| Term | What It Describes | Who Has It |
|------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Tone** | The author's attitude toward the subject | The **author** |
| **Mood** | The emotional feeling a reader gets from the text | The **reader** |

- Tone words: *sarcastic, solemn, playful, bitter, hopeful, melancholy, urgent*
- Mood words: *eerie, tense, joyful, peaceful, gloomy, mysterious*

---

### 🔊 Other Important Devices

| Device | Description | Example |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Anaphora** | Repeating a word or phrase at the start of successive lines | *"We shall fight on the beaches, we shall fight on the landing grounds, we shall fight in the fields…"* — Churchill |
| **Allusion** | A reference to a well-known person, event, place, or text | *"He had a Midas touch with investments."* (alludes to Greek myth) |
| **Paradox** | A statement that seems contradictory but reveals a truth | *"The more you know, the more you realize you don't know."* |
| **Oxymoron** | Two contradictory words placed side by side | *deafening silence, bittersweet, living dead, clearly confused* |
| **Euphemism** | A mild or indirect word substituted for a harsh one | *"passed away"* instead of *"died"* |
| **Motif** | A recurring element (image, symbol, idea) throughout a work | The color green in *The Great Gatsby* representing envy and the American Dream |
