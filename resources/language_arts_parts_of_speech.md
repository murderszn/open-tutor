# 📝 Language Arts — Parts of Speech

A complete, student-friendly guide to the eight parts of speech in the English language. Every word you speak or write belongs to one of these categories.

## 1. 🏷️ Noun — *Names a person, place, thing, or idea*

A noun answers the question **Who?** or **What?**

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Common noun** | A general person, place, or thing | dog, city, book, idea |
| **Proper noun** | A specific name (always capitalized) | Marcus, Chicago, Amazon, God |
| **Concrete noun** | Something you can see, hear, touch, taste, or smell | apple, thunder, fire |
| **Abstract noun** | An idea, feeling, or concept you cannot physically touch | freedom, courage, love, justice |
| **Collective noun** | A group treated as one unit | team, flock, herd, army, jury |
| **Compound noun** | Two words combined to make one noun | football, toothpaste, fire truck |
| **Singular noun** | One person, place, or thing | cat, box, child |
| **Plural noun** | More than one (add -s, -es, or irregular change) | cats, boxes, children |
| **Possessive noun** | Shows ownership (add 's or s') | the dog's bone, the girls' team |

**Kid-Friendly:** Think of nouns as "name tags" — they label everything in the world.

---

## 2. 🔄 Pronoun — *Takes the place of a noun*

Pronouns prevent us from repeating the same noun over and over.

| Type | Examples |
|------|---------|
| **Personal (subject)** | I, you, he, she, it, we, they |
| **Personal (object)** | me, you, him, her, it, us, them |
| **Possessive** | my, your, his, her, its, our, their, mine, yours |
| **Reflexive** | myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselves |
| **Demonstrative** | this, that, these, those |
| **Interrogative** | who, whom, whose, which, what |
| **Relative** | who, whom, whose, which, that |
| **Indefinite** | anyone, everyone, someone, nobody, all, each, few, many, some |

**Example:** Instead of "Marcus ran to Marcus's car and Marcus drove away," we say "Marcus ran to **his** car and **he** drove away."

**Kid-Friendly:** Pronouns are "stand-ins" — they play the noun's role so we don't have to repeat it.

---

## 3. ⚡ Verb — *Shows action or state of being*

Verbs are the engine of a sentence — without one, you don't have a complete thought.

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Action verb** | Shows physical or mental action | run, think, build, imagine, write |
| **Linking verb** | Connects subject to a description (no action) | am, is, are, was, were, become, seem, feel, appear, look |
| **Helping (auxiliary) verb** | Helps the main verb express tense or mood | have, has, had, do, does, did, will, would, can, could, may, might, shall, should, must |
| **Transitive verb** | Action that passes to a direct object | She **kicked** the ball. |
| **Intransitive verb** | Action with no direct object | He **laughed**. The sun **rose**. |

### Verb Tenses

| Tense | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Simple present | I **walk** to school. |
| Simple past | I **walked** to school. |
| Simple future | I **will walk** to school. |
| Present progressive | I **am walking** to school. |
| Past progressive | I **was walking** to school. |
| Present perfect | I **have walked** to school. |
| Past perfect | I **had walked** to school before the bell rang. |

**Kid-Friendly:** If you can put "he/she ____s" in front of a word and it makes sense, it's probably a verb.

---

## 4. 🎨 Adjective — *Describes or modifies a noun or pronoun*

Adjectives answer: **Which one? What kind? How many? How much?**

| Type | Examples |
|------|---------|
| **Descriptive** | tall, red, smooth, ancient, brilliant |
| **Quantitative** | three, many, few, several, all |
| **Demonstrative** | this, that, these, those (when before a noun) |
| **Possessive** | my, your, his, her, our, their (when before a noun) |
| **Interrogative** | which, what, whose (in a question) |
| **Articles** | a, an (indefinite); the (definite) — the most common adjectives |

### Degrees of Comparison

| Degree | Rule | Example |
|--------|------|---------|
| Positive | base form | tall, beautiful |
| Comparative | add -er or use "more" | taller, more beautiful |
| Superlative | add -est or use "most" | tallest, most beautiful |

**Kid-Friendly:** Adjectives "paint a picture" of the noun — they tell you what it looks, feels, or sounds like.

---

## 5. 🚀 Adverb — *Describes or modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb*

Adverbs answer: **How? When? Where? How often? To what degree?**

| Type | Examples |
|------|---------|
| **Manner (How?)** | quickly, slowly, carefully, loudly, well |
| **Time (When?)** | now, then, soon, yesterday, always, never, often |
| **Place (Where?)** | here, there, everywhere, outside, nearby |
| **Degree (How much?)** | very, too, quite, almost, nearly, barely, extremely |
| **Frequency (How often?)** | always, usually, sometimes, rarely, never |

> Many adverbs are formed by adding **-ly** to an adjective: *quick → quickly*, *brave → bravely*.
> But not all -ly words are adverbs (*friendly*, *lovely* are adjectives), and not all adverbs end in -ly (*fast*, *hard*, *well*).

**Kid-Friendly:** Adverbs "turbo-charge" verbs — they tell you *how* or *when* or *where* something happens.

---

## 6. 🔗 Preposition — *Shows the relationship between a noun/pronoun and another word*

Prepositions usually show direction, location, time, or how things relate.

**Common Prepositions:**
> about, above, across, after, against, along, among, around, at, before, behind, below, beneath, beside, between, beyond, by, despite, down, during, except, for, from, in, inside, into, like, near, of, off, on, onto, out, outside, over, past, since, through, throughout, to, toward, under, until, up, upon, with, within, without

**Prepositional phrase** = preposition + its object (noun/pronoun)
- *The book **on the table** is mine.* (tells **where**)
- *We played **until sunset**.* (tells **when**)
- *She ran **through the park**.* (tells **where/how**)

**Kid-Friendly:** Think of prepositions as "location words." Anywhere a cat can go relative to a box: *in* the box, *on* the box, *under* the box, *beside* the box.

---

## 7. 🔀 Conjunction — *Connects words, phrases, or clauses*

| Type | What it does | Examples |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Coordinating** | Joins equal parts (use FANBOYS to remember) | **F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o |
| **Subordinating** | Joins a dependent clause to a main clause | because, although, since, when, while, if, unless, until, after, before, even though |
| **Correlative** | Works in pairs | either…or, neither…nor, both…and, not only…but also, whether…or |

**Examples:**
- *I wanted pizza **and** a soda.* (coordinating)
- *I stayed inside **because** it was raining.* (subordinating)
- **Either** you practice **or** you fall behind. (correlative)

**Kid-Friendly:** Conjunctions are "bridge words" — they connect ideas together.

---

## 8. 😲 Interjection — *Expresses strong emotion or sudden feeling*

Interjections are standalone exclamations. They're not grammatically connected to the rest of the sentence.

| Emotion | Examples |
|---------|---------|
| Surprise | Oh! Wow! Whoa! |
| Pain | Ouch! Ow! |
| Excitement | Yes! Hooray! Yay! |
| Disgust | Ew! Yuck! |
| Hesitation | Um, Uh, Hmm |
| Greeting | Hey! Hi! |

- Strong interjection (followed by **!**): *Wow! That was incredible.*
- Mild interjection (followed by **,**): *Well, I wasn't expecting that.*

**Kid-Friendly:** Interjections are the "reaction words" — the first thing you blurt out when something happens.

## 🧠 Quick Summary Table

| Part of Speech | Job | Key Question |
|----------------|-----|-------------|
| Noun | Names a person, place, thing, idea | Who? What? |
| Pronoun | Replaces a noun | Who? What? (stands in) |
| Verb | Shows action or being | What is happening? |
| Adjective | Describes a noun/pronoun | Which? What kind? How many? |
| Adverb | Describes a verb, adjective, or adverb | How? When? Where? How much? |
| Preposition | Shows relationship/position | Where? When? How related? |
| Conjunction | Connects words, phrases, clauses | And? But? Because? |
| Interjection | Expresses sudden emotion | (no question — just feeling!) |

## 📌 Words That Change Jobs

Many words can serve as different parts of speech depending on how they're used in a sentence:

- *That* → "That **dog** is huge." (adjective) vs. "I know **that** he left." (conjunction) vs. "Give me **that**." (pronoun)
- *Fast* → "He runs **fast**." (adverb) vs. "She is a **fast** runner." (adjective)
- *Light* → "Turn off the **light**." (noun) vs. "She has a **light** backpack." (adjective) vs. "**Light** the candle." (verb)

Always look at how the word is being **used** in the sentence, not just what it looks like.
