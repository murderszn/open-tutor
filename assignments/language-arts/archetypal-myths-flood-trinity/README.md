## 📖 Story Synopses

**Noah’s Ark (Genesis 6–9):** Humanity has grown violent and corrupt, so God decides to wipe the slate clean with a great flood. He warns **Noah**, the one righteous man, to build an ark and bring his family plus a male and female of every animal. It rains for 40 days, the earth floods, and everything outside the ark dies. When the waters recede, Noah releases birds to scout for dry land, eventually sees a rainbow, and receives God’s promise never to destroy the world by flood again.

**Epic of Gilgamesh (Tablet XI, ~2100 BCE Mesopotamia):** King Gilgamesh, searching for immortality, meets **Utnapishtim**, the only human who survived the great flood. Utnapishtim tells him the gods decided to drown humanity for being too noisy and chaotic. The god Ea secretly warned him, told him to build a giant boat, and load it with his family, craftsmen, and animals. The storm lasted six days; when it ended, Utnapishtim released a dove, a swallow, and a raven to find land. The gods rewarded him with immortality. Sound familiar?

**Other flood traditions to know in one sentence each:**
* **Greek (Deucalion):** Zeus floods the world for human wickedness; Deucalion and Pyrrha survive in a chest and repopulate Earth by throwing stones that become people.
* **Hindu (Manu and the fish):** A small fish (an avatar of Vishnu) warns Manu of a coming flood, grows giant, and tows his boat to safety on a mountain.
* **Indigenous (many tribes):** Stories like the Ojibwe “Earth Diver” describe a flood where animals dive for mud to rebuild land on a turtle’s back.

**Triads / Trinity patterns — one-line intros:**
* **Christian Trinity:** One God in three Persons — Father, Son (Jesus), Holy Spirit — distinct but unified.
* **Hindu Trimurti:** Brahma (creator), Vishnu (preserver), Shiva (destroyer) — three forces of the universe.
* **Story triads:** Past/Present/Future, Mind/Body/Spirit, Beginning/Middle/End — three-part patterns show up everywhere because they feel complete.
