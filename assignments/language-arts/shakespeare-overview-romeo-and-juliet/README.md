## 📖 Story Synopsis
*Romeo and Juliet* (Shakespeare, ~1595) is set in **Verona, Italy**, where two rich families — the **Montagues** and the **Capulets** — have been feuding for so long nobody even remembers why. **Romeo Montague**, a teenager in love with love, sneaks into a Capulet party and locks eyes with **Juliet Capulet**, who is only 13. It’s love at first sight. They share a sonnet, a kiss, and a secret plan.

That night Romeo climbs into the Capulet garden — the famous balcony scene where Juliet asks, *“Wherefore art thou Romeo?”* (meaning *why* are you Romeo — why do you have to be from the enemy family?). The next day, **Friar Laurence** secretly marries them, hoping the union will end the feud.

It does the opposite. Juliet’s hot-headed cousin **Tybalt** picks a fight and kills Romeo’s best friend **Mercutio**. Romeo, enraged, kills Tybalt and is exiled from Verona. Meanwhile, Juliet’s parents arrange to marry her off to a nobleman named **Paris**. Desperate, Juliet drinks a potion from Friar Laurence that makes her appear dead for 42 hours, planning to wake up in the tomb and run away with Romeo.

The plan falls apart. The message explaining the potion never reaches Romeo. He hears Juliet is dead, rushes to her tomb, drinks real poison, and dies beside her. Juliet wakes up moments later, sees him dead, and stabs herself with his dagger. Their deaths finally shock the Montagues and Capulets into ending the feud — *“For never was a story of more woe / than this of Juliet and her Romeo.”*

**Famous lines you’ll recognize:** *“A pair of star-crossed lovers,”* *“What’s in a name? That which we call a rose by any other name would smell as sweet,”* and *“Parting is such sweet sorrow.”*

**Big themes:** love vs. hate, impulsive decisions, fate (“star-crossed”), the cost of family feuds, and how adults can fail teenagers.
