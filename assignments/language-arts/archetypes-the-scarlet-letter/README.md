## 📖 Story Synopsis
Set in 1640s Puritan Boston, *The Scarlet Letter* (Nathaniel Hawthorne, 1850) opens with **Hester Prynne** standing on a public scaffold, a baby in her arms and a red letter “A” (for *adulteress*) stitched onto her dress. Her husband had been missing at sea for years; she had a child with another man and refuses to name the father. The town shames her, but Hester quietly raises her daughter **Pearl** by sewing for the community.

The hidden father is the beloved town minister, **Arthur Dimmesdale**, whose guilt eats him alive while Hester carries the punishment alone. Hester’s long-lost husband returns under the false name **Roger Chillingworth**, figures out the secret, and slowly tortures Dimmesdale by pretending to be his doctor and friend. In the end, Dimmesdale climbs the same scaffold, confesses publicly, and dies; Chillingworth withers away; and Hester and Pearl eventually leave town. Hester later returns to live quietly, wearing the “A” by choice — now read by many as “Able” instead of “Adulteress.”

**Big themes:** public shame vs. private guilt, hypocrisy of the “righteous,” secrecy, identity, and the difference between forgiveness from people and forgiveness from God.

## 🚀 The Project Steps
1. Quick Background
   - Time/place: Puritan New England; strict community standards and religious law.
   - Core idea: A person publicly marked by society for moral failure; consequences of shame, secrecy, and judgment.
2. Identify the Archetype
   - “The Mark” archetype: When a character carries a visible or social mark that changes how others treat them.
3. Relevance Reflection
   - Complete the Relevance Reflection (use the template in `assignments/relevance-reflection-template.md`).
   - Provide 2–3 pop culture parallels (TV, movies, music, social media, news) where a person is publicly shamed or marked.
4. (Optional) Bible Connection
   - Briefly note how the themes of sin, confession, grace/forgiveness compare with weekly Bible teachings.
