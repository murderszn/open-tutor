# 🤖 Intro to Algorithms

**Subject**: STEM (Computer Science)
**Goal**: Understand that computers need exact, step-by-step instructions by writing a real-world algorithm and drawing a simple flowchart.

## 📋 The Mission

Computers aren't smart—they only do *exactly* what you tell them to do. If you miss a step, the computer crashes! Your mission is to think like a programmer and write a flawless algorithm.

### Your Tasks:

1. **Write Two Real-World Algorithms to Chain**: 
   *   Write out exact, step-by-step instructions for two simple tasks that can be connected (e.g., Algorithm A: Making a PB&J sandwich. Algorithm B: Packing a lunchbox).
   *   *Trick*: Be incredibly specific! Don't just say "put peanut butter on the bread." Say "1. Open the bag of bread. 2. Take out two slices. 3. Lay them flat on a plate. 4. Open the peanut butter jar..."
2. **Identify Inputs and Outputs**:
   *   For *Algorithm A*, write down its **Input** (what you started with) and its **Output** (the result).
   *   For *Algorithm B*, write down its **Input** and **Output**.
   *   Show how they **Chain**: Explain how the Output of Algorithm A becomes the Input for Algorithm B.
3. **Draw a Flowchart**:
   *   Take your chained algorithms and turn them into a visual flowchart.
   *   Use standard shapes: Ovals for Start/End, Rectangles for Actions, and Diamonds for Decisions (e.g., "Is there enough peanut butter? -> Yes/No").
3. **Test It**:
   *   Have a parent (acting as the "Computer") follow your instructions exactly as written. If they get stuck or do something silly because the instruction wasn't clear, you have to "debug" (fix) your algorithm!

## 📝 Grading Rubric

| Category | Excellent (3) | Good (2) | Needs Work (1) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Specificity** | Instructions are extremely precise; no steps are skipped. | Instructions are mostly clear but assume some human knowledge. | Instructions are vague and would cause a "computer" to fail. |
| **Flowchart** | Uses correct shapes (ovals, rectangles, diamonds) and flows logically. | Uses mostly correct shapes, flow is understandable. | Missing shapes or flow is confusing. |
| **Debugging** | Successfully fixes any errors discovered during the "Test It" phase. | Fixes some errors, but leaves minor bugs. | Did not attempt to fix errors. |
