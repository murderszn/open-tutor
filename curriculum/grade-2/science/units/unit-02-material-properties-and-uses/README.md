# Unit 02 — Material Properties and Uses

**Grade:** 2 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#15](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/15)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 7–10 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, 25–30 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

Everything is made of some *material*, and every material has
**properties** — things you can observe about it, like its color, texture,
hardness, flexibility, absorbency, and whether it sinks or floats. In this
unit the learner becomes a material detective: sorting a collection of
everyday objects by their observable properties, testing materials the
*same way* so the comparison is fair, and then doing the job a scientist or
engineer does — **choosing the best material for a purpose and explaining
why**. The unit's investigation asks which material makes the best rain
cover for a toy: the learner tests two materials fairly, collects data over
repeated trials, and backs the choice with evidence (2-PS1-1, 2-PS1-2).

## Prerequisites (from Unit 01 and grade-1 science, re-taught here as needed)

- Asking questions that can be tested; observing carefully with the senses
  and a hand lens; recording in labeled drawings, tallies, and simple tables.
- Running a **fair test** — changing one thing while keeping everything else
  the same — with repeated trials (Unit 01, Weeks 3–6).
- Comparing materials by observable properties (color, texture, bend,
  sink/float) — entry knowledge from grade-1 science, rebuilt here in full.
- Science safety: adult supervises every investigation; materials stay away
  from the mouth; the adult previews every procedure.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Describe and sort everyday objects by **observable properties** — color,
   texture, hardness, and flexibility — using a consistent sorting rule
   (2-PS1-1).
2. Test materials for **absorbency** and **sink-or-float**, keeping the test
   fair (same amount of water, same wait time, same way each time), and
   record the results in a simple data table (2-PS1-1).
3. Match a property to a job: explain **which property matters** for a given
   use — raincoat vs. towel, bridge vs. cushion, boat hull vs. sail
   (2-PS1-2).
4. Plan and carry out a **fair comparison of two materials for one intended
   purpose**, collecting data across repeated trials (2-PS1-1).
5. Analyze the collected data and **choose the best material for the
   purpose**, stating the choice as an evidence-based claim (2-PS1-2).

## Essential question

*How do I find out what a material is like — and how does what it is like
tell me what job it should do?*

## Vocabulary

**Property** — something you can observe about a material, like its color,
texture, or hardness. · **Material** — what an object is made of (wood,
metal, fabric, plastic, paper). · **Texture** — how a surface feels (smooth,
rough, bumpy, soft). · **Hardness** — how hard it is to scratch or dent a
material. · **Flexibility** — how easily a material bends without breaking.
· **Absorbency** — how much water a material soaks up. · **Sink** — go down
to the bottom of water. · **Float** — stay on top of water. · **Fair test**
— a test that changes only one thing and keeps everything else the same
(from Unit 01). · **Trial** — one try of a test; scientists repeat trials.
· **Evidence** — data used to support an idea. · **Claim** — what you say
is true because of your evidence. · **Purpose** — the job a material needs
to do.

## Standards and source notes

- Reference framework: Next Generation Science Standards, grade-2
  performance expectations. Codes and descriptions were re-verified
  2026-10-16 against the NSTA reproduction of the official NGSS 2-PS1
  document (static.nsta.org) and the ACS Inquiry in Action Grade 2 Lesson
  1.1 NGSS-alignment sheet (acs.org, © 2019 — the sheet names 2-PS1-1 and
  its clarification language, corroborating the scope-and-sequence
  verification of 2026-10-03). No state adoption, accreditation, or
  alignment certification is claimed.
- **2-PS1-1** — Plan and conduct an investigation to describe and classify
  different kinds of materials by their observable properties. Lessons
  01–03 and the project's material tests are this PE's classroom
  investigation; the assessment boundary for the paired PE keeps all
  quantitative measurement to length (Unit 01's centimeters), so this unit
  compares properties *qualitatively* (soaks more/less, floats/sinks,
  bends/does not bend) — a faithful reading of the framework.
- **2-PS1-2 \*** — Analyze data obtained from testing different materials
  to determine which materials have the properties that are best suited for
  an intended purpose. Lessons 04–05, the project, and the "best material
  for the job" review week build the intended-purpose analysis. The asterisk
  marks the framework's engineering integration.
- Science and engineering practices used throughout: **SEP-3** (planning and
  carrying out investigations), **SEP-4** (analyzing and interpreting data),
  **SEP-7** (engaging in argument from evidence). The crosscutting concept
  is **cause and effect** (a material's property causes it to do its job
  well or badly) alongside **patterns** (repeated trials give similar
  results).

## 16-session pacing

Adapt frequency and duration for the learner; the success criterion for each
session is named. K–2 tasks may be oral, pointing, drawing, manipulative, or
adult-scribed; explicit adult directions are built into every lesson.

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Lesson 01](lessons/lesson-01-property-detectives.md): Property detectives — color, texture, hardness, flexibility | Learner sorts 8 objects by one chosen property and names the sorting rule |

| 3 | [Lesson 02](lessons/lesson-02-testing-hardness-flexibility.md): Testing hardness and flexibility | Learner runs the scratch and bend tests the same way on 4 materials and records in a table |
| 4 | Reading-and-talk: "what things are made of" — objects and their materials, then property bingo review | Learner names the material and one property for 5 pictured objects |

**Week 8 — Absorbency, sink-or-float, and matching properties to jobs**

**Week 9 — Fair purpose tests, data, and claims**

## Accessibility and supports

Oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative response modes for all checks; the
adult scribes dictated observations. Short written practice (6–8 tasks) is
reviewed the same day. Texture is a property *by design* in this unit, so
tactile exploration is built in — provide real material samples (fabric,
foil, sandpaper, sponge) for every sort. Visual science word wall with
objects, not just words; sentence frames ("The ___ is ___, so it is good
for ___"); color never the only cue in sorts. Table variant and
active-investigation variant in every lesson. Low-vision learners sort by
texture, hardness, and flexibility with eyes closed and describe aloud.
See [teacher-guide.md](teacher-guide.md) for differentiated supports and
extensions.
