# Project — The Best Rain Cover Challenge

**The job:** A toy left outside gets soaked in the rain. Design and test a
rain cover that keeps the toy dry — then prove with data that your cover's
material is the best for the job.

**What you will deliver:** (1) a written fair-test plan, (2) a data table
from 3 fair trials, (3) a rain cover built from the winning material, and
(4) a poster or talk that states your claim with evidence.

**Materials:** two candidate cover materials cut to the same size (choose
two: plastic-bag square, paper-towel square, aluminum-foil square, felt
square), a small toy (car, block, or figure — the same toy for every
trial), a spoon, a cup of water (standing in for rain), a tray, a dry
cloth to check the toy, science journal, poster paper and crayons.

**Safety:** The adult pours or closely supervises all water. Materials
stay away from the mouth. Wipe up spills right away so nobody slips.

## Steps

**1. Name the job and the property (Session 8).** Write: "My job is to
keep the toy dry in rain." Circle the property that matters: *low
absorbency* (water stays out). Predict which of your two materials will
win, and write one sentence why.

**2. Write the fair-test plan (Session 8).** Fill the plan strip:

- 🎯 Job: keep the toy dry
- 🔄 The ONE change: the cover material (___ vs. ___)
- 🔒 Stays the same: same toy, same-size covers, same spoon of water
  ("rain"), same 30-count wait, same dryness check
- 👀 How I'll check: lift the cover and touch the toy with the dry cloth —
  dry cloth = dry toy; damp cloth = wet toy

**3. Run 3 fair trials per material (Session 10).** Drape the cover over
the toy like a tent (same way each time). Spoon the water over the top —
this is your "rain." Wait 30 counts. Lift the cover. Touch the toy with
the dry cloth. Record "dry" or "wet" in the table. Use a fresh dry cloth
(or a dry corner) for each check.

| Material | Trial 1 (dry / wet) | Trial 2 (dry / wet) | Trial 3 (dry / wet) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ___ | | | |
| ___ | | | |

**4. Build the winner's cover and present (Session 12).** Make a neat rain
cover from the winning material, sized to the toy. Then present your
poster or talk: show the plan, the table, and finish with the sentence
"My evidence is ___, so my claim is ___."

## Rubric (40 points total)

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Good | 2 — Developing | 1 — Starting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fair-test plan** (10 pts) | Names the job, the one change, and 3+ things kept the same; check method described | Names job, change, and 2 things kept the same | Names job and change; 0–1 things kept the same | Plan missing or the "change" is not the material |
| **Data collection** (10 pts) | 3 trials per material, same procedure each time, table complete | 3 trials per material, table complete, one small procedure slip | Fewer than 3 trials per material, or table incomplete | 1 trial or no table |
| **Evidence-based claim** (10 pts) | Claim names the winner AND cites the trial data ("dry in 3 of 3 trials") | Claim names winner with partial data cited | Claim names winner with no data cited ("I liked it") | No claim, or claim contradicts the data |
| **Cover build + presentation** (10 pts) | Cover fits the toy and uses the winning material; talk/poster shows plan, table, and claim | Cover built; talk/poster shows 2 of the 3 (plan, table, claim) | Cover built but from the losing material, or presentation missing 2 parts | No cover or no presentation |

**Scoring note for the adult:** 34–40 = ready to move on; 25–33 = review
the fair-test plan steps and re-test one material together; below 25 =
re-teach Lessons 03–05 with the adult running the trials while the learner
directs each step aloud.

## If the data surprises you

If the "wrong" material wins, or trials disagree, that is a *finding*, not
a failure. Check: Was every trial truly the same? (Same spoon? Same
count? Cover draped the same way?) Re-run the odd trial once. Report what
happened honestly — the rubric rewards honest data over a "correct"
winner.
