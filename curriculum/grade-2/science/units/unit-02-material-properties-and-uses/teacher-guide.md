# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Material Properties and Uses

For the guiding adult. Student-facing pages never include answers; all
solutions live in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## What this unit builds

Unit 01 taught *how* to investigate (fair tests, repeated trials, tables,
graphs). Unit 02 teaches *what* grade-2 physical science investigates:
observable properties of materials (2-PS1-1) and purpose-driven material
choice (2-PS1-2). Unit 03 will reuse the fair-test and observation
machinery for heating/cooling changes — keep the "same test, every
material" language alive.

## Objective-by-objective teaching notes

**Objective 1 — describe and sort by observable properties.** The sorting
rule must be stated *before* sorting and applied to *every* object.
Learners will drift toward sorting by use ("kitchen things"); redirect to
observable properties every time. The four anchor properties are color,
texture, hardness, flexibility — absorbency and sink/float join in Week 8.

**Objective 2 — fair absorbency and sink-or-float tests.** Pre-cut all
material squares to the same size yourself; learners cannot be expected
to cut identically. You pour (or hand-over-hand supervise) the water. The
30-count wait must be the same for every material — count aloud together.
Record predictions *before* testing in the sink-or-float table; the
prediction-vs-result discussion is where learning happens.

**Objective 3 — property-to-job matching.** This is the unit's conceptual
peak. Keep the winners board visible all week: each job crowns a
*different* material. The sentence frame "The ___ is ___, so it is good
for ___" is the assessment language — drill it until it is automatic.

**Objective 4 — fair two-material comparison for one purpose.** Insist on
the written plan *before* testing (project plan strip). If a learner
changes procedure mid-test, stop, name it kindly ("we changed the rules —
that trial doesn't count"), and re-run.

**Objective 5 — evidence-based material choice.** Accept any claim the
data supports, even if it contradicts the learner's prediction. Reward
honest reporting of odd trials explicitly — it is the behavior the
standards describe.

## Likely misconceptions (and responses)

| Misconception | Response |
|---|---|
| "More changes = better test" (Unit 01 revisit) | "If the car went farther, was it the car or the ramp? We can't tell — we learned nothing." |
| Sorting by use instead of property | "You sorted by *job*. A property sorter puts the wooden spoon with the wooden block — same material." |
| "The water disappeared" | "It's *inside* the towel now — feel how heavy it got. Water moves; it doesn't vanish." |
| Hard = strong, soft = weak | "The rubber band is soft to squeeze but hard to scratch. It depends on the *test*." |
| Favorite material wins every job | Point to the winners board: "Plastic won the rain cover. Would you dry your hands with it?" |
| Bigger piece = more absorbent | "That's why we cut same-size pieces — so size can't trick us." |
| One trial is enough | "One try could be luck. Three tries show a pattern." |

## Reteaching guidance

- **Sorting is inconsistent:** return to Lesson 01's worked examples; do
  two more sorts with the learner stating the rule aloud before touching
  objects. Use picture cards if handling is the distraction.
- **Tests are unfair:** run the absorbency test yourself, *deliberately*
  wrong (different water amounts), and let the learner catch you. Then
  re-run it fairly together.
- **Property–job matching is shaky:** replay Lesson 04's image activity
  with real materials — drape plastic and paper towel over two identical
  toys and spoon water over both. The wet toy is unforgettable.
- **Claims lack evidence:** go back to Unit 01's "My evidence is ___,
  so my claim is ___" frame; require the learner to point at the table
  while speaking.

## Readiness to move to Unit 03

The learner is ready when they can: (a) sort 6+ objects by a stated
property rule without switching mid-sort; (b) describe a fair absorbency
test's three "same" elements; (c) match 4 of 5 jobs to the property that
matters with a reason; (d) state one evidence-based material claim from
their project table. The unit assessment's 24/30 bar operationalizes this.

## Differentiation

- **Supports:** oral/pointing/drawing responses throughout; adult scribes;
  pre-cut materials; texture-based sorting with eyes closed for low-vision
  learners; word cards with objects (property, absorbency, fair test);
  home-language labels welcome; table and active variants in every lesson.
- **Extensions:** design a third test (Lesson 02); absorbency limits —
  how many spoons until full (Lesson 03); trade-off objects at home
  (Lesson 04); two-requirement decisions (Lesson 05).

## Materials prep checklist (whole unit)

Material-collection tray (8–10 objects); wooden craft stick, chalk, rubber
band, cardboard strip, sponge, plastic lid, foil square; paper towel,
plastic-bag, foil, and felt squares cut to identical size (make 2 sets —
one for Lesson 03, one for the project); spoon, cup, tray, timer/count;
bin of water for sink-or-float + 6 test objects; job cards (raincoat,
towel, bridge, cushion); poster paper and crayons. Total cost: household
items or a dollar store.

## Safety recap

Adult supervises every investigation and pours (or hand-guides) all water.
Fingernail scratch tests only — no teeth, no sharp tools. Materials stay
away from the mouth. Wipe spills immediately. Sink-or-float bin is
shallow; no electrical items near water. Broken chalk goes straight in
the trash.
