# Answer Key — Unit 02: Material Properties and Uses

For the guiding adult only. Every solution below was worked independently
from the student pages and reconciled against them. Accept any answer the
evidence supports; sample wording is a guide, not a script. "Scribed" =
the adult may write the learner's dictated answer.

## practice.md

**Task 1 — One-rule sort (flexibility).** Bends easily: cotton ball,
rubber band. Does not bend easily: wooden block, metal spoon, rock,
plastic cup. (The plastic cup is borderline — it flexes slightly but holds
its shape; accept it in either pile *if the learner's rule is stated and
applied consistently*. The rule statement is what is scored.)

**Task 2 — Property hunt.** Open-ended. Sample: "The doorknob is metal —
smooth and hard." / "The rug is fabric — rough and flexible." / "The
window is glass — smooth, hard, see-through." Accept any real material +
one observable property. Reject opinions ("pretty") — redirect to
observables.

**Task 3 — Test table.** Expected results (same tests as Lesson 02):

| Material | Scratch test | Bend test |
|---|---|---|
| Chalk | Leaves a white mark; dust — soft | Snaps even when bent gently — not flexible |
| Cardboard strip | Nail dents it slightly — softer than wood | Bends, but creases and stays bent — flexible, no spring-back |
| Sponge | Nail sinks in without scratching — very soft | Bends and squishes easily — very flexible |
| Plastic lid | No mark — hard to scratch | Bends a little, springs back; stiff compared with sponge |

Note: the sponge surprises learners (soft to squeeze ≠ easy to scratch —
hardness is about the *scratch test*, not squeezing). Name the distinction
if it comes up.

**Task 4 — Sink or float.** Predictions vary; scored on honest recording,
not correct predictions. Expected outcomes: metal spoon — sinks; wooden
block — floats; plastic bottle cap — floats; rock — sinks; sponge —
floats (it may sit low and slowly take on water — accept "floats, then
gets soggy"); orange with peel — floats (the peel traps air; a peeled
orange sinks — a fine extension, not required). Discussion point: heavy
things *can* float (wooden block) and light things *can* sink (a small
metal key) — floating is about the material and shape, not just weight.

**Task 5 — Absorbency re-test.** Open-ended; scored on procedure (same
size, one spoon, 30 counts, lift-and-look) and honest recording, not on
which materials were chosen.

**Task 6 — Fair or not?**
a. **Fair** — same-size pieces, same spoon, same wait.
b. **Not fair** — the chalk gets ten hard scratches while the rock gets
   one gentle drag; the *test itself* differs. Fix: one gentle drag on
   each.
c. **Fair** — dropped at the same time, watched the same way; only the
   object (the material) changes.

**Task 7 — Read the table.** a. Wax paper. b. Evidence: wax paper was dry
underneath in all 3 trials; the napkin was wet underneath in all 3 trials.
c. Yes — same-size pieces (given), same spoon of water, same 30-count
wait, 3 trials each; only the material changed.

**Task 8 — Best material for the job.** Sample sentences:
a. "The paper towel is better for a raincoat" — no. Correct: paper towel
   is better for *drying* — wait, the job here is RAINCOAT. Re-read: a.
   Raincoat: **plastic sheet** is better **because its low absorbency
   keeps water out** (water beads and rolls off).
b. Bridge for heavy toy trucks: **wooden planks** are better **because
   they are hard and stiff and hold weight without bending** (spaghetti
   bends and snaps).
c. Pillow: **feathers** are better **because they are soft and squishy**
   (rocks are hard).
d. Winter boots: **rubber** is better **because its low absorbency keeps
   water out** (cloth soaks through).
Common slip: learners pick the towel for the raincoat job — redirect with
Lesson 04's image ("remember the soggy paper-towel car").

## assessments/week-04_quiz.md

1. Any two observable properties: brown color, hard, rough/bumpy texture,
   stiff (does not bend), rectangular shape. (2 properties = full credit.)
2. No — "favorite" is an opinion; different people have different
   favorites. A property must be observable by everyone (color, texture,
   hardness…).
3. Scratch each one time, gently, with a fingernail, and look for a mark.
   Same test on both — otherwise you can't tell whether the difference
   came from the material or from the test.
4. Material A has higher absorbency — the water went *inside* it (tray
   dry underneath); on Material B the water stayed *on top* in a puddle.
5. a. The plastic sheet (left) — the car underneath stays dry. b. **Low**
   absorbency — the job is "keep water out," so the winning property is
   water staying on top and rolling off, not soaking in.
6. Stiff and hard like wood — a bridge must hold heavy trucks without
   bending or breaking; a rubber-band bridge would stretch and drop them.
7. **Not fair.** Two problems: (1) different sizes — the big sponge has
   more material to soak with; (2) different water amounts — three spoons
   vs. one. Fix: same-size pieces, one spoon each, same wait.
8. The wax paper — it was dry underneath in 3 out of 3 trials while the
   napkin was wet in 3 out of 3. A rain hat's job is keeping water out,
   so wax paper wins on the data.

## assessments/unit-assessment.md (30 points)

**Part A**

1. (2 pts) a. Spoon is hard/smooth/shiny; cotton ball is soft/fluffy —
   accept any one real difference. b. Cotton ball is soft and squishy but
   doesn't stretch much; rubber band is stretchy/elastic — accept any one
   real difference (both are soft, so "hard vs. soft" is wrong here —
   probe for the stretch).
2. (2 pts) Bends easily: rubber band, sponge. Does not bend easily:
   wooden stick, chalk. (1 pt for 3 of 4 correct with a stated rule.)
3. (3 pts) Hardness test: one gentle fingernail drag; look for a
   scratch/mark. Flexibility test: hold both ends, bend gently; see if it
   bends, stays stiff, or breaks. Same way every time so that any
   difference in results comes from the *material*, not from a changed
   test. (1 pt per test description + 1 pt for the fairness reason.)
4. (2 pts) Absorbency is how much water a material soaks up —
   "soak-up-ability" earns full credit.
5. (3 pts) a. Prediction: key sinks, block floats (1 pt for a stated
   prediction — any prediction is acceptable if stated *before* testing).
   b. The test shows the key sinks and the block floats (1 pt); the
   learner states whether the prediction matched (1 pt) — honest
   mismatch earns full credit.

**Part B**

6. (3 pts, 1 pt each) a. Sponge — high absorbency (soaks up dish water).
   b. Aluminum foil — low absorbency (keeps water out of the hull).
   c. Thick waterproof fabric — low absorbency + warmth (thin cotton
   soaks through and gets cold). Each needs the material AND the
   property to earn the point.
7. (4 pts) a. Yes — fair (1 pt): same-size squares, same spoon of water,
   same 30-count wait, 3 trials each — any two reasons earn the second
   point (2 pts total). b. Foil (1 pt) — dry underneath in 3 of 3 trials
   vs. felt wet in 3 of 3 (1 pt for quoting the data).
8. (4 pts) a. The ONE change: the tail material — ribbon vs. shoelace
   (1 pt). b. Three kept-same: same kite, same wind (same fan setting /
   same day), same tail length, same attachment point, same flyer — any
   three (1 pt each, max 3… cap at the item's remaining points: award up
   to 2 pts here, 1 pt for two items, 2 pts for three). c. Observe which
   tail keeps the kite flying steadier — less wobbling/diving (1 pt).
   Total for item 8: 4 pts.
9. (3 pts) Sample: "Plastic loses the towel job — a towel must soak up
   water, but plastic's absorbency is low, so water just sits on top and
   the spill stays." Any job + the property reason + evidence from the
   unit earns full credit. (1 pt job, 1 pt property, 1 pt evidence link.)
10. (4 pts) Sample: "Foil is better for a rain cover because in my tests
    the toy was dry in 3 of 3 trials with foil and wet in 3 of 3 with
    paper towel." (2 pts: winner + data cited.) Improvement sample: "I
    would use the same dry cloth check but with a fresh dry corner each
    trial, because the damp cloth might have made a later trial look
    wetter than it was." (2 pts: one concrete change + why.) Accept any
    reasonable improvement with a reason.

**Project scoring:** use the 40-point rubric in project.md. Note the
"honest data" rule — a learner whose data contradicts their prediction
but reports it faithfully can still score 9–10 on the claim criterion.
