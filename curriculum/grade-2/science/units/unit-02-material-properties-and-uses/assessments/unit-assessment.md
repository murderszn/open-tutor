# Unit Assessment — Unit 02: Material Properties and Uses (culminating)

Adult reads each item aloud; the learner may answer orally, by pointing,
by drawing, or in writing (adult scribes dictated answers). About 25
minutes. Score with [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md); 10 items, 30 points
total. 24+ shows readiness to move to Unit 03; 18–23 = re-teach the missed
objectives with one more testing-station session; below 18 = re-teach
Lessons 02–05 before moving on.

**Part A — Properties (Objectives 1–2; 12 points)**

1. (2 pts) Look at these three objects: a metal spoon, a cotton ball, a
   rubber band. Name one property that is DIFFERENT for each pair:
   a. spoon vs. cotton ball — b. cotton ball vs. rubber band —
2. (2 pts) Sort these 4 materials into "bends easily" and "does not bend
   easily": wooden stick, rubber band, chalk, sponge. (Adult may have the
   learner handle real samples.)
3. (3 pts) Describe the hardness test AND the flexibility test exactly as
   you would run them on a new material. Why must both tests be done the
   same way on every material?
4. (2 pts) Define *absorbency* in your own words ("soak-up-ability"
   counts).
5. (3 pts) You drop a metal key and a wooden block into a bin of water at
   the same time.
   a. Predict: which sinks, which floats?
   b. After testing: was your prediction right? What did the test — not
      your guess — show?

**Part B — Best material for the job (Objectives 3–5; 18 points)**

6. (3 pts) Three jobs. For each, name the property that matters MOST and
   choose the better material:
   a. A towel to dry dishes: sponge or plastic wrap?
   b. A boat hull that must keep water out: aluminum foil or paper?
   c. A winter jacket that must keep you warm and dry in snow: thick
      waterproof fabric or thin cotton t-shirt?
7. (4 pts) A scientist wants the best material for lining a plant pot so
   water does not leak onto the table. She tests foil and felt: same-size
   squares, one spoon of water on each, 30-count wait, 3 trials each.
   Results — Foil: dry underneath, dry, dry. Felt: wet underneath, wet,
   wet.
   a. Was her test fair? Give two reasons.
   b. Which material should she choose? Quote the data in your answer.
8. (4 pts) Plan a fair test: Your cousin wants to know which material
   makes a better kite tail — a ribbon or a shoelace — where "better"
   means *heavier enough to steady the kite but still flexible*.
   a. What is the ONE change in your test?
   b. Name three things you will keep the same.
   c. What will you observe or measure to decide the winner?
9. (3 pts) Your friend says, "Plastic is the best material for
   everything." Use something you learned in this unit to disagree with
   evidence. (Name one job where plastic loses and say why.)
10. (4 pts) Look at your project data table (or the sample table in
    Lesson 05). Write a full evidence claim in the frame: "___ is better
    for ___ because in my tests ___." Then name one thing you would do
    differently if you ran the test again, and why.
