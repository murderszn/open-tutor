# Week-04 Quiz — Unit 02: Material Properties and Uses (formative)

**Unit week 4** (track Week 10). Adult reads each item aloud; the learner
may answer orally, by pointing, by drawing, or in writing. 8 items, about
15 minutes. Check the same day — see [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md).
This quiz is practice evidence, not a grade; use misses to re-teach before
the unit assessment.

1. Name two properties a scientist could observe about this wooden block.
   (Adult holds up or points to a wooden block.)
2. Is "my favorite color" a property? Why or why not?
3. You want to know which is harder — a rock or a piece of chalk. Describe
   the fair test you would use.
4. A scientist tested two materials for soak-up-ability. Both pieces were
   the same size. She poured one spoon of water on each and waited 30
   counts.
   - Material A: the water disappeared into it; the tray underneath is dry.
   - Material B: the water sat on top in a puddle.
   Which material has higher absorbency? How do you know?
5. Look at the picture (adult shows
   [rain-cover-comparison.png](../assets/rain-cover-comparison.png), or
   reads the text-only alternative below). Two toy cars in the rain: the
   left car is covered by a shiny plastic sheet and stays dry; the right
   car is covered by a paper towel that soaks through, and the car gets
   wet.
   *Text-only alternative: Two identical toy cars sit in the rain. The
   left car is covered by a shiny plastic sheet; raindrops bead up on the
   sheet and roll off, and the car underneath is dry. The right car is
   covered by a white paper towel; the rain soaks into the towel until it
   is soggy and dripping, and the car underneath is getting wet.*
   a. Which cover keeps the toy dry?
   b. Which property matters for this job — high absorbency or low
      absorbency? Explain.
6. Job: a bridge for heavy toy trucks. Should the bridge material be
   flexible like a rubber band, or stiff and hard like wood? Why?
7. You test a big sponge against a tiny square of paper towel, and you pour
   three spoons of water on the sponge but one spoon on the towel. Is this
   a fair test? Name everything that is unfair about it.
8. A scientist tested wax paper and a napkin 3 times each for a keep-dry
   job. Wax paper: dry, dry, dry. Napkin: wet, wet, wet. Which material
   should she choose for a rain hat? Use the data in your answer.
