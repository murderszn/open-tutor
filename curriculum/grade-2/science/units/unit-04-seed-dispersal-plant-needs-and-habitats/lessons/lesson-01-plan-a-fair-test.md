# Lesson 01 — What Do Plants Need? Plan a Fair Test

**Student goal:** I can plan a fair test that changes only one thing,
so I can find out if plants need sunlight and water to grow.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U04](../README.md) · **Session 1 of 16**
**Adult directions are part of the lesson.** Read the teaching aloud,
let the learner answer orally, by pointing, or by drawing, and scribe
when the learner is not ready to write.

## Materials

- Two small plant pictures or two cups (empty, for planning talk only)
- Paper and crayons for the plan
- The learner's Unit 01 growth-log page (or a fresh log page)

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Name one thing you think a plant needs to grow. How could you test
whether you are right?" (Expected: sunlight, water, or soil; a test
idea such as "grow two plants and give one no water.") If the learner
cannot name a need, replay the Unit 01 fair-test idea: "A fair test
changes one thing and keeps the rest the same."

## Teaching

A plant cannot tell us what it needs. We have to test it. And a test
only tells the truth if it is **fair**: change **one thing** and keep
everything else the same. The one thing we change is called the
**variable**.

Our question is: *Do plants need sunlight and water to grow?* That
question has two needs in it — sunlight and water. So we cannot test
both at once. We pick **one** variable for our test:

- **Sunlight test:** two plants, both watered the same amount. One
  sits in sunlight, one sits in shade. The only difference is
  sunlight.
- **Water test:** two plants, both in sunlight. One gets water, one
  gets no water. The only difference is water.

Everything else stays the same: same kind of seed, same soil, same
size cup, same amount of water (in the sunlight test), same sunny
spot (in the water test). Then we watch for three weeks and measure
how tall each stem grows, in centimeters.

**Key vocabulary:** *fair test*, *variable*, *sunlight*, *water*.
A fair test is a test with one change. The variable is the change.

## Worked example 1 — Fixing an unfair plan (modeled reasoning)

Mia's plan: "Plant A goes in the sun and gets water every day.
Plant B goes in a dark closet and gets no water. Then I will see
which grows taller."

> "Mia changed **two** things: sunlight *and* water. If Plant A
> grows taller, we cannot tell why. Was it the sun? Was it the
> water? Was it both? The test is unfair, so it cannot answer our
> question. Fix it: give Plant B water too, and keep only the
> sunlight different. Now Plant A is sun + water, Plant B is shade +
> water. The only difference is sunlight. That test can answer:
> does sunlight matter?"

**Reasoning to name aloud:** every extra difference is another
possible reason for the result. One change, one answer.

## Worked example 2 — Writing a prediction (modeled reasoning)

A prediction is a guess we can test — it says what we think will
happen, before we see it.

> "Our sunlight test: Plant A in sun, Plant B in shade, both
> watered the same. My prediction: *Plant A will grow taller than
> Plant B, because plants need sunlight to grow.* That prediction
> can turn out wrong — and that is fine. A wrong prediction still
> teaches us something, because the measurements will show what
> really happened."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** a prediction is not a promise. It is a
guess written down *before* the test, so the results can surprise
us honestly.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds orally or with drawings.

1. "Here is a plan: Plant A in sun, watered. Plant B in shade,
   watered. Fair or unfair?" (Expected: fair — only sunlight
   changes.)
2. "Plan: Plant A in sun, watered. Plant B in sun, not watered.
   Fair or unfair? What is the variable?" (Expected: fair —
   variable is water.)
3. "Plan: Plant A in sun, watered every day. Plant B in shade,
   watered once a week. Fair or unfair? Why?" (Expected: unfair —
   two changes: sunlight and how often it is watered.)
4. Error analysis: the adult shows the plan "Plant A: big cup, sun,
   water. Plant B: small cup, shade, no water." Ask: "How many
   things changed? Name them." (Expected: four — cup size,
   sunlight, water, and... the adult accepts "cup, sun, water" as
   three named differences; the point is *more than one*, so the
   test cannot answer one question.)
5. "Choose our variable: sunlight or water? Say your prediction in
   one sentence: 'I predict ___ will grow taller, because ___.'"
   (Expected: any complete prediction naming the chosen variable.)
6. "What will we keep the same in our test? Name three things."
   (Expected: any three of: same seeds, same soil, same cup size,
   same watering (sunlight test) or same sunlight (water test),
   same measuring day.)

## Independent practice

The learner draws the plan: two cups, labels for what is the same
and what is different, and one prediction sentence (adult-scribed
if needed). The drawing must show the variable clearly — for
example, a sun over one cup and shade over the other, with a water
can over both.

## Application

"Think of a test at home: does a plant on the windowsill grow
differently from a plant in a dark corner? Tell me how you would
make that test fair." (Expected: same seeds, same water, only the
light different — or the learner names the unfair parts of an
unfair version.)

## Exit check

"Why can't we change sunlight AND water in the same test?"
(Expected: because then we would not know which change caused the
result. Accept: "We wouldn't know why one grew taller.")

## Supports

- **Language access:** the adult points to "same" and "different"
  picture cards while the learner sorts plan cards.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may build the plan with
  two real cups and props instead of drawing it.
- **Seated-table variant:** all planning at the table with cards.
- **Floor-play variant:** the learner walks two "plants" (toys) to
  a sunny spot and a shady spot and places matching water cans.

## Extension

"If we wanted to test whether plants need soil, what would our two
plants look like? What would stay the same?" (Expected: one plant
in soil, one in wet paper towel or sand, same sunlight, same water.)

## Teacher note

The most common error is the "helpful extra change": learners add
water to the plant they feel sorry for, or move the shade plant
into the sun "for a little while." Name this during planting day
(Session 3): the kindest thing for the test is to keep the plan.
Misconception to watch: "the plant in the closet will grow because
it is resting." Record the prediction without correcting it — the
measurements in Sessions 6, 10, and 14 do the correcting.
