# Lesson 02 — Planting Day and Seed Parts

**Student goal:** I can plant bean seeds for our fair test, and I can
open a soaked bean and name its three parts.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U04](../README.md) · **Session 3 of 16**
**Adult directions are part of the lesson.** The adult soaks beans
overnight before this lesson and handles all water for soaking. The
learner never eats the beans. Wash hands after handling soil and
seeds. Check for bean/legume allergies before starting; if an
allergy is a concern, use the observation-only alternative below.

## Materials

- 2 cups with soil, 2 bean seeds for planting (per learner pair),
  plus 2–3 beans soaked overnight in cool water (adult-prepared)
- A tray, a small watering can or cup, labels, a ruler (centimeters)
- Growth-log page, paper and crayons

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Show me our plan from Lesson 01. What is our variable — sunlight
or water? What stays the same?" (Expected: the learner names the
chosen variable and at least two things kept the same. If the plan
is shaky, re-read the Lesson 01 prediction before planting.)

## Teaching

A seed is a tiny package. Inside it is everything a new plant needs
to start: a **tiny plant**, food for that tiny plant, and a coat to
protect them both. Today we look inside, then we plant.

First, the soaked bean. An adult soaked it overnight so it is soft
enough to open. Gently peel the coat and open the bean into two
halves. Look closely:

1. The **seed coat** — the thin outer covering. It protected the
   seed while it waited. It peels off like a jacket.
2. The **stored food** — the two fat, pale halves. This food feeds
   the tiny plant until it can make its own food with sunlight.
3. The **tiny plant** — a small shape tucked between the halves,
   with a tiny root tip and a tiny shoot tip. This is the plant
   itself, waiting to grow.

Then we plant for the fair test. Two cups, same soil, same kind of
seed, planted at the same depth (about one finger-joint deep), and
labeled with the plan: which cup is which, and what is different.
Only the variable differs from here on — sunlight test: one cup in
sun, one in shade, both watered the same; water test: both in sun,
one watered, one not.

**Key vocabulary:** *seed*, *seed coat*, *stored food*, *tiny
plant*. The coat protects, the food feeds, the tiny plant grows.

## Worked example 1 — Opening a soaked bean (modeled reasoning)

> "I peel the coat slowly. It comes off in one thin skin — that is
> the **seed coat**, the jacket. I open the bean. Two pale halves
> — that is the **stored food**, the packed lunch. And here, tucked
> where the halves meet, a tiny white shape with a point at each
> end — that is the **tiny plant**. One end will become the root
> and grow down; the other will become the shoot and grow up."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the three parts each have a job. If
the coat were missing, the seed could dry out or break. If the
food were missing, the tiny plant would starve before it could
reach sunlight. The parts work as a package.

## Worked example 2 — Planting fairly (modeled reasoning)

> "Cup A and Cup B: same soil, same cup size, same two-bean depth.
> I press each seed down to the same depth — my finger's first
> joint. I label Cup A 'sun + water' and Cup B 'shade + water.'
> I water both with the same small cup. From now on, the *only*
> difference is where they sit. If I water one more 'to be kind,'
> I break the test — and I would have to say so in the log."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** planting fairly is part of the test,
not just the start of it. Labels matter: without them, two cups
look identical and the test is lost.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

1. "Open your soaked bean. Point to the coat, the stored food,
   and the tiny plant." (Expected: correct pointing in any order,
   with the adult naming each part as the learner points.)
2. "Which part protects the seed? Which part feeds the tiny
   plant?" (Expected: coat protects; stored food feeds.)
3. "Draw the opened bean and label its three parts (adult may
   scribe labels the learner dictates)."
4. "Plant your two seeds at the same depth. Show me with your
   finger how deep." (Expected: about one finger-joint deep, same
   for both cups.)
5. "Label your cups. What does each label say, and why does it
   matter?" (Expected: labels match the plan; without labels the
   cups cannot be told apart.)
6. Error analysis: the adult shows two cups, one planted deep and
   one shallow, and asks: "Is this fair? What else must be the
   same?" (Expected: unfair — depth differs; soil, cup, seed kind,
   and watering must also match.)

## Independent practice

The learner completes the growth-log first page: today's date,
both cup labels, the variable, the prediction from Lesson 01, and a
labeled drawing of the opened bean. The first measurement entry is
"Day 0 — planted, 0 cm above soil" for both cups.

## Application

"Some seeds are planted by people; some plant themselves when they
fall. Why would a seed need a coat if it falls on the ground?"
(Expected: to protect it from drying out, breaking, or being
eaten before it grows. Accept any protection reason.)

## Exit check

"Name the three parts of a seed and one job each."
(Expected: coat — protects; stored food — feeds the tiny plant;
tiny plant — grows into the new plant.)

## Supports

- **Language access:** part-name cards (coat / food / tiny plant)
  the learner matches to the opened bean.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may point and tell
  instead of drawing; the adult scribes labels.
- **Observation-only alternative:** if handling beans is not
  possible (allergy or another reason), the adult opens the bean
  and the learner observes with a magnifier and describes each
  part; planting uses the learner's directions while the adult
  handles the seeds.
- **Seated-table variant:** all work on a tray at the table.
- **Floor-play variant:** the learner plants at a low table or on
  a protected floor mat.

## Extension

"Soak a second kind of seed (for example, a pea) overnight. Open
it. Does it have the same three parts? Draw what is the same and
what looks different." (Expected: same three parts; size, color,
or coat thickness may differ.)

## Teacher note

Learners often call the stored food "the seed" and the tiny plant
"a worm" or "a root." Accept the observation, then name it: "That
little shape *is* the plant — roots and shoot are still tiny."
Do not let the dissection become the test: the soaked bean is for
looking, not for planting (soaked beans planted afterwards rot
more often and confuse the results). Keep planted seeds dry until
they are in soil.
