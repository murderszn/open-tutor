# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Seed Dispersal, Plant Needs, and Habitats

**Unit:** [U04](README.md) · **Answer key:** [answer-key.md](answer-key.md)
(adult only — never beside student materials)

## How this unit runs

Sixteen 25–30-minute sessions across track weeks 15–18. Every
session opens with a 2-minute talk warm-up (a "what changed?"
question, a seed-feature call, or a model-part call) and closes
with the lesson's exit check, recorded as **secure / developing /
not yet** per objective. The bean-plant investigation is the
unit's spine: planted in Session 3, measured in Sessions 6, 10,
and 14. Protect those measurement days — if a week is missed,
measure on the next session and note the gap in the log rather
than inventing numbers.

**Session map:** 1 L1 → 2 practice B → 3 L2 → 4 practice A1 →
5 L3 → 6 growth check 1 + practice C → 7 L4 → 8 practice D +
project step 1 → 9 L5 → 10 growth check 2 + practice E → 11
project steps 2–3 → 12 practice F1 → 13 quiz + feedback → 14
growth check 3 + project share → 15 practice F2 + reteach → 16
assessment.

## Objective-by-objective guidance

### Objective 1 — One-variable investigation (L1–L2, growth checks)

The load-bearing habit of the unit. Learners will "help" the
neglected plant (extra water, a move into the sun). Name this on
planting day and make it a rule: *kindness to the test means
keeping the plan*. If a plant is accidentally watered or moved,
record it in the log honestly and discuss what it does to the
conclusion — that discussion is worth more than a perfect dataset.
Accept "did not sprout" and "grew 0 cm" as data, not failure.

### Objective 2 — Seed parts (L2)

Teach parts by job, not by name alone: coat protects, food feeds,
tiny plant grows. If a learner cannot find the tiny plant, open a
second soaked bean slowly together — it hides where the halves
meet. Do not let learners plant the dissected bean; dissection
beans are for looking.

### Objective 3 — Seed travel and features (L3, practice C)

Insist on feature-based reasoning: *what carries it, and what
feature lets it be carried?* Size alone is never the reason.
Correct "the bee carries seeds" on the spot (bees carry pollen;
seeds come after pollination). Card 9 in practice C (a round seed
rolling in the wind) is deliberately tricky: it travels by wind
even without fluff or wings, because the wind pushes it — accept
"wind" with that reason, and accept a thoughtful "wind, but not
by flying" as correct.

### Objectives 4–5 — Models (L4–L5)

A model earns its keep by mapping *and* by differing. Require
both every time: what each part stands for, plus one honest
difference. Correct purpose language ("the dog planted it," "the
bee helps the flower on purpose") gently: animals visit for food
or pass by; plants benefit from ordinary animal behavior. If the
fleece carries no seeds, check the contact — the fur must brush
the seeds, not hover above them.

### Objective 6 — Claim with evidence; habitat judgment (L5, project)

A claim counts when it cites a measurement ("grew 6 cm" beats
"grew a lot"). A habitat judgment counts when it checks all three
needs (sunlight, water, soil), not just the first noticed. Keep
this judgment inside the unit's scope: do not turn it into Unit
05's tally-and-compare work.

## Misconceptions and reteaching

| Misconception | Reteach move |
|---|---|
| Changing two things is fine if one plant "needs help" | Re-run Lesson 01 worked example 1 with the learner's own plan; show that two changes give two possible reasons |
| Plants eat soil / soil is the plant's food | Revisit seed parts: the seed's stored food feeds the tiny plant first; soil holds the plant and holds water; sunlight + water are the tested needs |
| Sunlight only warms the plant | Ask: would a warm dark closet grow a plant? The shade plant in the investigation answers this with data |
| A model must be exactly like the real thing | Lesson 04 worked example 2: name the difference as part of the science, not as a defect |
| Bees carry seeds / bees make seeds | Bee moves pollen; the flower makes seeds after pollination (Lesson 05 readiness check) |
| All seeds travel the same way | Practice C sort: same goal (a new place), different features, different journeys |
| A sunny place is always a good place for a seed | Lesson 05 worked example 2: check water and soil too — a sunny rock fails two of three needs |

## Supports and extensions

- Every check accepts oral, pointing, or drawing responses; the
  adult scribes when the learner is not ready to write.
- Model materials are chosen by property (sticky, fuzzy,
  floating), which revisits Unit 02 without re-teaching it.
- Extension paths: a second seed kind dissected (L2), a smooth
  cloth comparison for the fur model (L4), a wind-pollination
  model change (L5), a soil test as the next investigation
  (project step 4).
- If the bean plants fail entirely (no sprouting), use the
  fictional practice tables in Lesson 03 and the assessment as
  the data for claim-writing, and say so plainly: "Our plants did
  not sprout, so we practiced claiming from another class's data.
  The claim habit is the same; the data is borrowed." Do not
  invent growth for dead plants.

## Readiness criteria

The learner is ready for Unit 05 when they can: (1) state a fair
test with one variable; (2) name the three seed parts and their
jobs; (3) match wind / water / animal to a seed feature; (4)
explain one model part-mapping and one model difference; (5)
state a claim with at least one measurement as evidence. If (1)
or (5) is not yet secure, replay Lessons 01 and 05's claim work
during the flexible review days before Unit 05 — Unit 05's habitat
observations assume the learner can already plan, measure, and
claim.
