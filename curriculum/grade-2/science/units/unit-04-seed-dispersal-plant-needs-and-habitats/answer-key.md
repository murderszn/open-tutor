# Answer Key — Unit 04: Seed Dispersal, Plant Needs, and Habitats

**Adult only — never beside student materials.** Every practice,
quiz, assessment, and project item is solved here. Where a learner
answer may vary (predictions, model trial counts, habitat
judgments), the key gives the acceptable range and the reasoning
that makes an answer count.

## Lesson 01 — Plan a fair test

**Readiness:** a need (sunlight, water, or soil) plus a test idea.

**Guided practice:**

1. Fair — only sunlight changes (both watered).
2. Fair — variable is water (both in sun).
3. Unfair — two changes: sunlight and watering frequency.
4. Three changes: cup size, sunlight, water. More than one change
   means the test cannot answer one question.
5. Accept any complete prediction naming the chosen variable, e.g.,
   "I predict the sunny plant will grow taller, because plants need
   sunlight to grow."
6. Any three kept-same items, e.g., same seeds, same soil, same cup
   size (and same watering in the sunlight test / same sunlight in
   the water test).

**Independent practice:** drawing shows two cups with the variable
clearly different and everything else matching, plus one prediction
sentence. **Application:** a fair windowsill-vs-corner test keeps
seeds, soil, cup, and water the same and changes only light.
**Exit check:** changing two things leaves two possible reasons for
the result, so the cause is unknown.

## Lesson 02 — Planting day and seed parts

**Readiness:** the chosen variable and at least two kept-same items.

**Guided practice:**

1. Pointing: coat (outer skin), stored food (two pale halves), tiny
   plant (small shape between the halves).
2. Coat protects; stored food feeds the tiny plant.
3. Drawing labels all three parts (adult-scribed labels accepted).
4. Both seeds planted about one finger-joint deep.
5. Labels match the plan (e.g., "sun + water" / "shade + water");
   without labels the cups cannot be told apart.
6. Unfair — depth differs; soil, cup, seed kind, and watering must
   also match.

**Independent practice:** log Day 0 row complete: date, both labels,
variable, prediction, labeled bean drawing, "0 cm above soil" for
both cups. **Application:** a fallen seed needs its coat to protect
it from drying out, breaking, or being eaten. **Exit check:** coat
— protects; stored food — feeds; tiny plant — grows.

## Lesson 03 — How seeds travel

**Readiness:** crowding — too many plants sharing sunlight, water,
and soil in one spot.

**Worked example 2 check (re-solved):** sunny + water: 5 − 2 = 3 cm
growth; shady + water: 2 − 1 = 1 cm; sunny, no water: 1 − 1 = 0 cm.

**Guided practice:**

1. Wind traveler: fluffy seeds, left panel.
2. Animal traveler: hooked seeds on fur, middle panel.
3. Water traveler: floating seed at the bottom of the right panel;
   it floats and does not sink.
4. Fluffy → wind; floating → water; hooked → animal.
5. No — a heavy, smooth seed has nothing to catch the wind and is
   too heavy to be carried far.
6. Wrong — smallness is not the clue; the hook catches on fur, so
   it travels by animal.

**Independent practice:** three drawn seeds with matching features
(fluffy / floats / hooks). **Application:** any reasonable
feature-based match (e.g., dandelion fluff → wind; a seed that
sticks to socks → animal). **Exit check:** wind — fluffy/light/
winged; water — floats; animal — hooked/sticky or carried in fruit.

## Lesson 04 — Model animal seed dispersal

**Readiness:** hooked seeds catch on fur and ride until they drop off.

**Worked example 1 check (re-solved):** 6 + 5 + 7 = 18 seeds stuck
across 3 trials; most trials picked up 5 or more.

**Guided practice:**

1. Mapping: fleece → animal fur; Velcro ball → burr seed; field
   tray → parent plant's field; new tray → new place.
2. Trial 1 counts recorded as counted (stuck and dropped should
   match unless a seed rolled away — record both numbers honestly).
3. Pattern: seeds stick in most or all trials.
4. Smooth balls with no Velcro: few or none stick — the hook is
   what makes the ride work.
5. Unfair model test — the fur must brush past the seeds as an
   animal walks through plants; hovering is not how animals meet
   burrs.
6. Any real difference, e.g., the fleece does not walk, eat, groom,
   or choose where to go.

**Independent practice:** 3-trial tally complete, total computed,
"My model shows that ___" finished (e.g., "...fur can carry hooked
seeds to a new place"). **Application:** seeds end up somewhere
new (park, garden, home) with their own space, sunlight, water, and
soil. **Exit check:** fleece → fur; Velcro → burr hooks; one real
difference named.

## Lesson 05 — Model pollination and good places

**Readiness:** the bee visits the flower; it carries pollen, not seeds.

**Guided practice:**

1. Mapping: pom-pom → bee; yellow powder → pollen; paper flowers →
   real flowers.
2. Bee dusty after Flower A; dust visible on Flower B after dabbing.
3. No pollination — pollen never reaches another flower.
4. Any real difference, e.g., the pom-pom does not fly, eat, or
   choose flowers.
5. Good chance — sun, soil, and water are all present.
6. No — sun alone is not enough; the seed also needs water and soil,
   which a bare rock does not give.

**Independent practice:** two-step drawing (pollen on bee at A;
pollen left on B) plus "Pollination is ___" (pollen moving from one
flower to another); two place judgments with reasons. **Application:**
bees moving pollen lets flowers make seeds and fruit, so more
pollination means more seeds and new plants. **Exit check:** powder
→ pollen; pollination → pollen moving flower to flower; a good
place (sun, water, soil) named with its reason.

## Practice

**A1:** 3 practice measurements to the nearest cm; Day 0 row filled
("0 cm above soil"). **A2:** on each check day, both plants measured,
leaf counts noted if any, change computed as new height minus old
height, one observation sentence per plant. (Learner numbers vary;
check the subtraction, not the height.)

**B1:** Unfair — three extra changes: sunlight, water, cup size.
Fixed: same cup size, both watered, sun vs. shade only.
**B2:** Unfair — extra change: plant food. Fixed: no plant food for
either plant, sun vs. shade only, both watered.
**B3:** Unfair for the water question — water does not differ at
all, and depth differs. Fixed: both planted at the same depth, both
in sun, one watered and one not.

**C1:** 1 wind (fluffy, floats in air); 2 water (floats); 3 animal
(hooks stick to sock); 4 wind (wings spin as it falls); 5 water
(floats at pond edge); 6 animal (clings to fur); 7 wind (feathery
parachute); 8 animal (carried inside fruit an animal eats); 9 wind
— the tricky card: it rolls because the wind pushes it, even without
fluff or wings. Target 7 of 9 with feature reasons.

**D1:** 3 trials recorded (stuck and dropped per trial), total
computed by adding the three "stuck" counts, "My model shows that
___" finished; smooth balls with no Velcro would stick rarely or
never; one model difference named.

**E1:** 1 good — sun, water, soil all present; 2 poor — no sun, no
water, no soil; 3 poor — sun only, no soil and no steady water;
4 good — sun (partial shade still gives light; accept "good" with
the reason that soil and steady water are present, and accept
"fair/good with a note about less sun" as correct if the reason
checks all three needs). A reason that checks only one need does
not count.

**F1:** (1) any claim + real evidence, e.g., "My evidence is that
the sunny watered plant grew 3 cm in a week, so my claim is that
plants need sunlight." (2) any claim + real evidence, e.g., "My
evidence is that hooked seeds stuck to the fleece in all 3 trials,
so my claim is that hooked seeds travel by animal." (3) rewritten
claim with evidence, e.g., "Plants grow because they get sunlight
and water — our sunny watered plant grew tallest."
**F2:** relay 4 of 5 correct (adult's features should cover wind,
water, and animal at least once each); review cards: (a) fair test
— change one thing, keep the rest the same; (b) rain cover — not
soaking up water (waterproof); (c) ice → water → ice is reversible;
(d) any travel way + its feature.

## Formative quiz (Session 13)

1. Sunlight and water (full credit). Soil is a real need, but the
   unit's tested needs are sunlight and water — see the note below.
2. Unfair — two things changed (sunlight and water), so the cause
   of any difference is unknown.
3. Seed coat, stored food, tiny plant.
4. Left — wind; middle — animal; bottom of right panel — water.
5. The fluffy, light seed — its feature (light/fluffy, catches the
   wind) is the reason.
6. Fleece → animal fur; Velcro → burr seed hooks.
7. Yellow powder → pollen; pollen moving from one flower to another
   is pollination.
8. Sunny garden bed — reason tied to plants' needs (sunlight, water,
   soil present; the closet gives almost none).

*Quiz note on item 1:* the unit's tested needs are sunlight and
water (2-LS2-1). A learner who answers "sunlight and soil" has one
tested need and one real need; mark it developing and reteach with
the investigation question, which names sunlight and water.

## Unit assessment (Session 16)

**Part A (re-solved from the table):**

- A1: Sunny + water, 8 cm at Week 3.
- A2: Sunny + water: 8 − 2 = 6 cm. Shady + water: 2 − 1 = 1 cm.
  Sunny + no water: 1 − 1 = 0 cm.
- A3: Accept any claim citing a table measurement, e.g., "My claim
  is that plants need sunlight and water to grow. My evidence is
  that the sunny watered plant grew 6 cm while the sunny unwatered
  plant grew 0 cm." A claim with no measurement is developing, not
  secure.
- A4: The garden-bed seed — it has sunlight, water, and soil; the
  rock seed has sunlight only, with no soil and no steady water.

**Part B:**

- B1: Seed coat — protects; stored food — feeds the tiny plant;
  tiny plant — grows into the new plant.
- B2: (a) fluffy and light → wind; (b) able to float → water;
  (c) hooked → animal.
- B3: Fleece → animal fur; Velcro → burr hooks; any real difference
  (does not walk, eat, groom, or choose).
- B4: Powder → pollen. Steps: (1) dab the bee in powder at Flower A
  so pollen sticks to it; (2) dab the bee on Flower B so pollen rubs
  off there. Difference: any real one (does not fly, eat, or choose
  flowers).
- B5: Left — wind; middle — animal; bottom of right panel — water.

**Part C (midyear review):**

- C1: Unfair — two changes (sunlight and water). Fair version: both
  in sun, one watered and one not (water test); or both watered, sun
  vs. shade (sunlight test).
- C2: Not soaking up water — a rain cover must keep water out, so
  the toy stays dry.
- C3: (a) reversible — water can freeze back into ice; (b)
  irreversible — a cooked egg cannot go back to a raw egg.
- C4: Animal travel (hitchhiker seed on fur). Traveling is good
  because the seed gets its own space, sunlight, water, and soil
  instead of crowding under its parent.

**Reflection:** not scored; accept any honest answers.

## Project — scoring guidance

Use the rubric in [project.md](project.md). "Secure" requires:
investigation plan with one variable + all 4 check days with
changes computed; both models demonstrated with full part-mapping
and one difference each; claim citing at least one measurement;
habitat reason naming sunlight, water, and soil. A project that
demonstrates models well but cites no measurement is Developing on
the claim criterion even if the presentation is confident —
measurement is the evidence standard for this unit.
