# Resource Pack — Unit 04: Seed Dispersal, Plant Needs, and Habitats

**Summary (adult planning judgment):** supplements for teaching
plant needs (sunlight and water, one variable at a time), seed
parts, seed dispersal by wind, water, and animals, and simple
dispersal and pollination models to a 2nd grader; every core
objective also has a free, no-account, offline path (bean seeds,
soil, cups, fleece, felt, paper flowers).

**Check date:** 2026-10-30 (all items below). Video runtimes and
caption availability are reported only where the source page
showed them; anything else is marked unverified — preview before
assigning.

## Videos (1 curated + labeled search links)

1. **"How Do Seeds Travel to New Places?" (YouTube)**: walks
   through wind (dandelion parachutes, maple wings), animals
   (hooked burrs on fur, birds carrying fruit seeds), and water
   (floating coconuts), and stresses that arriving is not enough
   — a seed also needs a place where it can grow. Matches Lessons
   03–05 closely. Runtime unverified — preview first.
   <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o_DggcnDVCQ>
2. **Open search (labeled, unverified item):** YouTube search
   `SciShow Kids seed dispersal` — use only after previewing a
   specific result for age fit, ads, and accuracy; prefer a result
   that names wind, water, and animal travel separately.
3. **Open search (labeled, unverified item):** YouTube search
   `plants need sunlight water experiment second grade` — preview
   for the one-variable-at-a-time rule (Lesson 01) before assigning;
   skip any result that changes two things at once.
4. **Open search (labeled, unverified item):** YouTube search
   `pollination bee model elementary pollen flower` — short
   modeling clips only; verify the clip shows pollen moving from
   one flower to another, not just a bee on one flower.

## Web references

1. **Next Generation Science Standards, Grade 2 (California
   Department of Education document)** (framework source; the
   2-LS2-1 and 2-LS2-2 wordings and the one-variable-at-a-time
   assessment boundary used in this unit match this document and
   the track audit's crosswalk; no state adoption claimed).
   <https://docest.com/doc/31371/grade-2-standards-ngss-ca-dept-of-education>
2. **Oak National Academy — "Seed formation and seed dispersal"
   (primary science lesson)**: teacher-led lesson page covering
   wind (dandelion), water (coconut), animal-eaten fruit (holly),
   fur-caught seeds (galium), and exploding pods, with the
   explanation that seeds disperse so they do not compete with
   siblings for what they need. Adult preview recommended — the
   page is written for a slightly older primary audience, so use
   its examples (dandelion, coconut, hooked seeds) rather than
   assigning the page as written.
   <https://www.thenational.academy/pupils/programmes/science-primary-year-3/units/what-plants-do-and-what-they-need/lessons/seed-formation-and-seed-dispersal/video>
3. **AskOwlo — "How Do Seeds Travel?"**: short illustrated story
   in which a child finds fluffy dandelion seeds, learns that
   seeds travel to find space, sunshine, and water, and watches a
   maple seed spin. Gentle read-aloud fit for Lesson 03; free, no
   account stated on the page.
   <https://askowlo.com/stories/how-do-seeds-travel-en>
4. **Carolina Biological — "Using Fast Plants to Teach LS2"**
   (teacher background): explains the grade 1–2 core ideas (plants
   depend on water and light; plants depend on animals for
   pollination and seed movement) and the bee-pollination model
   idea. Adult reading, not a learner assignment; useful if the
   adult wants a longer plant-growing option than beans.
   <https://www.carolina.com/teacher-resources/Interactive/using-wfps-teach-ls2-ecosysytems/tr51001.tr>
5. **Common Core State Standards — Mathematics** (measurement
   support only; stem heights in this unit are recorded in
   centimeters, the Unit 01 measurement skill).
   <https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/>

## Access notes

- Every core objective is teachable fully offline: bean seeds,
  soil, cups, fleece, felt balls, Velcro, paper flowers, and
  cornmeal require no account, no device, and no purchase beyond
  dollar-store supplies.
- All five web references above are free with no account required
  to view, as far as their pages showed on the check date.
- Videos supplement explicit teaching; none replaces the lessons.
  Preview for ads, comments, and age fit (especially open-search
  results).
- Captions/runtime were verified for none of the video items; all
  are marked unverified above.
