# Unit 04 — Seed Dispersal, Plant Needs, and Habitats

**Grade:** 2 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#15](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/15)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 15–18 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, 25–30 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

A plant cannot walk to a better spot, yet new plants appear far from
their parents — across a path, beside a fence, on the far side of a
pond. This unit answers two questions: *what does a plant need to
grow?* and *how do seeds get to a new place where they can grow?*

First the learner runs a real investigation: bean plants grown with
one thing changed at a time — sunlight *or* water, never both at once
— and measured every week in centimeters, the way Unit 01 taught.
Then the learner opens a soaked bean seed and finds three parts
inside: a coat, stored food, and a tiny plant waiting to grow. Then
the learner studies how seeds travel — light seeds on the wind,
floating seeds on water, hooked seeds riding on animal fur — and
builds two simple models: one that mimics an animal carrying seeds,
and one that mimics a bee carrying pollen between flowers. The unit
ends where it started, with evidence: the learner states a claim
about what plants need, backed by the investigation's own numbers,
and judges where a seed would have the best chance to grow.

**Safety is built into every lesson:** the learner never eats seeds
or beans used in class; hands are washed after handling soil and
seeds; the adult checks for bean/legume allergies before planting
day and soaks the dissection beans (the learner never handles hot
water); model "pollen" is a dry powder that is dabbed, never blown;
living things are handled gently and wild plants are observed, never
picked. Every step has an observation-only alternative.

## Prerequisites (re-taught here as needed)

- Asking a question that can be tested, and running a **fair test**
  (change one thing, keep the rest the same) — Unit 01.
- Measuring length in centimeters and recording observations in
  labeled drawings and simple tables; stating a claim with evidence
  ("My evidence is ___ , so my claim is ___") — Unit 01.
- Describing materials by observable properties — Unit 02 (used when
  choosing model materials: what sticks, what floats, what soaks up).
- Science safety practices: keep materials away from the mouth,
  handle living things gently, never look directly at the sun, and
  name the safe alternative when an investigation is unsafe to touch.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Plan and conduct a one-variable-at-a-time investigation to
   determine if plants need sunlight and water to grow, measuring
   stem height in centimeters and recording observations weekly for
   three weeks (2-LS2-1).
2. Describe the three parts of a bean seed — seed coat, stored food,
   and tiny plant — and tell what each part does for the growing
   plant.
3. Describe three ways seeds travel — wind, water, and animals — and
   match a seed's observable feature (light and fluffy, able to
   float, hooked or sticky) to how it travels.
4. Develop a simple model that mimics the function of an animal in
   dispersing seeds, explain what each model part stands for, and
   name one way the model differs from the real thing (2-LS2-2).
5. Develop a simple model that mimics the function of an animal in
   pollinating plants, explain what each model part stands for, and
   name one way the model differs from the real thing (2-LS2-2).
6. Construct an evidence-based claim about what plants need to grow,
   using the investigation's measurements, and judge whether a place
   gives a seed a good chance to grow, giving a reason tied to
   sunlight, water, and soil.

## Essential question

*Plants cannot move — so how do they get what they need, and how do
their seeds find a good place to grow?*

## Vocabulary

**Seed** — a tiny package holding a new plant, with food to start
growing. · **Seed coat** — the outer covering that protects the seed.
· **Stored food** — the food packed inside the seed for the tiny
plant. · **Tiny plant (embryo)** — the baby plant inside the seed.
· **Sunlight** — light from the sun; plants need it to grow. ·
**Water** — what plants drink through their roots. · **Fair test** —
a test that changes one thing and keeps the rest the same. ·
**Variable** — the one thing changed in a fair test. · **Measure** —
to find how long or tall something is (here: stem height in
centimeters). · **Dispersal** — seeds traveling away from the parent
plant to a new place. · **Wind** — moving air that can carry light
seeds. · **Float** — to stay on top of water instead of sinking. ·
**Hitchhiker seed** — a seed with hooks that catches on animal fur
(or clothing) and rides along. · **Pollen** — a fine yellow powder
made by flowers. · **Pollination** — pollen moving from one flower
to another flower, carried by an animal such as a bee. · **Model** —
a simple copy that acts like the real thing in one important way. ·
**Habitat** — a place where a living thing can find what it needs
(here: sunlight, water, and soil for a seed). · **Evidence** —
observations or data used to support an idea. · **Claim** — what you
say is true because of your evidence.

## Standards and source notes

- Reference framework: Next Generation Science Standards, grade-2
  performance expectations. Codes and descriptions below were
  re-verified 2026-10-30 against the California Department of
  Education grade-2 NGSS document (which reproduces the official
  wording, including the 2-LS2-1 assessment boundary) and the track
  audit's crosswalk (2026-10-03), which carries the same codes.
  No state adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification is
  claimed.
- **2-LS2-1** — Plan and conduct an investigation to determine if
  plants need sunlight and water to grow. Assessment boundary:
  testing one variable at a time. This unit changes sunlight *or*
  water in any one comparison, never both at once (Lesson 01).
  Disciplinary core idea: plants depend on water and light to grow.
- **2-LS2-2** — Develop a simple model that mimics the function of
  an animal in dispersing seeds or pollinating plants. Lessons 04
  and 05 are the two halves of this expectation: Lesson 04 models
  dispersal, Lesson 05 models pollination. Disciplinary core idea:
  plants depend on animals for pollination or to move their seeds
  around.
- Objective 2 (seed parts) and Objective 3 (matching seed features
  to travel ways) are the background knowledge these two
  expectations need; they are not presented as separate performance
  expectations. Objective 6's habitat judgment applies 2-LS2-1's
  core idea (a place must offer sunlight, water, and soil); it does
  not teach Unit 05's habitat-comparison work (tallying kinds of
  living things in two habitats), which stays in Unit 05.
- Science and engineering practices used throughout: **SEP-3**
  (planning and carrying out investigations — the bean-plant fair
  test) and **SEP-2** (developing and using models — the dispersal
  and pollination models). The crosscutting concept is **structure
  and function**: a seed's shape (fluffy, floating, hooked) fits
  the job of traveling.
- Original reading passages in this unit were written for this
  unit. No copyrighted text is reproduced. The generated
  illustration is an AI-made practice picture, not a photograph of
  a real experiment, and is labeled as such wherever it appears.
  Practice data tables in lessons and assessments are labeled
  fictional practice data; the learner's own investigation data is
  recorded separately in the growth log and is never replaced by
  these tables.

## 16-session pacing

Adapt frequency and duration for the learner; the success criterion
for each session is named. K–2 tasks may be oral, pointing, drawing,
manipulative, or adult-scribed; explicit adult directions are built
into every lesson. The bean-plant investigation runs as a background
thread: planted in Session 3, measured in Sessions 6, 10, and 14.
