# Practice — Unit 01

Short independent practice tasks (6–8 tasks per session is the grade-2
target). The adult reviews these the same day; oral, pointing, drawing, or
dictated responses all count. Solutions are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md). Use these across Sessions 6 and 11 (and as
spare warm-ups in Week 6).

## Measuring practice (Session 6)

1. **Estimate, then measure.** Choose 3 objects from the room. For each,
   write your estimate in cm, then measure from the 0 to the nearest cm.
   Circle the object where your estimate was closest.
2. **Start-point check.** Your adult measures a crayon starting at the
   ruler's **edge** (on purpose). What is wrong? Re-measure it correctly
   and write both numbers. Which one is honest, and why?
3. **Longer or shorter — by how much?** Measure a spoon and a pencil. Write
   both lengths. How many centimeters longer is the longer one? Show how
   you figured it out.
4. **Measure twice.** Measure the same book's width twice. Are your two
   measurements the same? If they differ by 1 cm, which rule from Lesson 03
   will you double-check?

## Graphing practice (Session 11)

Use this practice dataset (a fictional practice scenario, clearly labeled —
not your own observations):

> *Mia measured three bean plants after two weeks. Plant A: 5 cm. Plant B:
> 8 cm. Plant C: 6 cm.*

5. Draw a **bar graph** of the three plants. Label the bottom axis with the
   plant names and the side axis "height in cm" (one square = 1 cm).
6. Which plant is tallest? Write the claim-evidence sentence: "My claim is
   ___. My evidence is ___."
7. **Compare problem:** How many centimeters taller is Plant B than Plant A?
8. **Error analysis:** A friend drew the bars but forgot to label the axis.
   Why does that make the graph hard to trust? Write or say one reason.

## Spare warm-ups (Week 6 review)

- **Fair or not?** "I tested two balls on the same ramp, same release."
  Fair or unfair? What changed? What stayed the same?
- **Tally sprint:** The adult calls out numbers (3, 5, 2, 4); you tally
  each, then read the tallies back as numbers.
- **Claim or opinion?** "This towel is the best because it's my favorite."
  Claim with evidence, or just an opinion? Fix it into a claim-evidence
  sentence using imaginary data.
