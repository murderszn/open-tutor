# Teacher Guide — Unit 01

**For the guiding adult.** This guide covers objectives, common
misconceptions, differentiated supports, extensions, and what "ready to
move on" looks like. Solutions live in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) —
keep it separate from the learner's materials.

## Objectives recap

1. Ask a testable, fair-test question (one change, rest same).
2. Measure length to the nearest centimeter from the 0.
3. Record in labeled drawings, tallies, and data tables.
4. Plan and run a fair comparison with repeated trials.
5. Graph data (picture/bar) and state a claim with evidence.

## Lesson-by-lesson misconception notes

- **L01:** "More changes = better test." Counter with: "If the car went
  farther, was it the car or the ramp? We can't tell — we learned nothing."
- **L02:** Recording expectations instead of observations ("the leaf is
  green" for a brown leaf). Redirect: "Are your eyes telling you green, or
  are you remembering green?" Also: dishonest data is the unit's recurring
  theme — praise honest unexpected results explicitly.
- **L03:** Starting at the ruler's edge instead of 0; counting spaces
  instead of reading the endpoint. Check the start point *before* the
  reading, every time, until it's automatic.
- **L04:** Treating "change the condition each trial" as repeated trials.
  Repeat = same conditions, three times. Also: hiding an odd trial instead
  of recording it — a bump or push is information.
- **L05:** "Tallest bar = best" regardless of the question. Always ask:
  "What does taller mean *in this graph*?" before any claim.

## Differentiated supports

- **Response modes:** Every check accepts oral, pointing, drawing,
  manipulative, or adult-scribed answers. Never require handwriting to
  demonstrate science understanding.
- **Table variant:** Preprinted observation forms, data tables, and graph
  grids with labeled axes are in each lesson — use them freely; they are
  scaffolds, not shortcuts.
- **Language:** Teach vocabulary with objects first (see L01–L03), keep the
  visual word wall current, welcome home-language labels, and keep the
  claim-evidence sentence frame visible all unit.
- **Attention/pacing:** Sessions are 25–30 minutes by design. If a session
  runs long, split it: teach the worked examples one day, practice the next.
  Movement breaks between the two worked examples help.
- **Low vision:** Tactile material samples, large-print tables, high-contrast
  graph grids; the generated image ships with a full text-only alternative
  (see L04) so the error-analysis question is answerable without seeing it.
- **Fine motor:** The adult holds the ruler steady while the learner reads;
  stamp or sticker tallies are fine.

## Extensions (for learners who are ready)

- L01: design an original fair-test question with three constants.
- L02: three-level "zoom sequence" drawings of one area.
- L03: measure in cm vs. paper-clip units; explain why the numbers differ.
- L04: add a prediction row; compare prediction to data.
- L05: graph all three trials per condition (six bars); explain what the
  full graph reveals.
- Project: test a fourth material, or re-run with a different wait time and
  discuss whether the ranking changed (fairness of comparisons across
  *their own* tests).

## Reteaching guidance

| If the learner… | Revisit | With this change |
|---|---|---|
| Can't separate "change" from "same" | L01 + image activity (L04) | Act it out with real ramps; narrate every constant |
| Starts at ruler edge | L03 Worked example 2 | Cover the ruler's edge with tape marked "NOT ZERO" for a week |
| Estimates wildly off | L03 guided practice | Estimate-then-measure with feedback on 10 quick objects |
| Won't record odd trials | L02 honesty note + L04 | Praise an honest surprise result out loud; model recording your own mistake |
| Claims without evidence | L05 frame | Require the frame verbatim for a week: "My claim is ___. My evidence is ___." |
| Graphs without labels | L05 + practice task 8 | Pre-labeled grids until labeling is automatic |

## Readiness to move on (to Unit 02)

The learner, unprompted, explains why changing two things ruins a test;
measures from 0 to the nearest cm; keeps every trial in a table; and states
a claim with data. The project rubric's "Readiness to move on" line and the
unit assessment's 13+ threshold (no zeros in Part B) are the formal checks.
Unit 02 (material properties) reuses the fair-test and data-table routines
from day one — they must be secure, not just familiar.
