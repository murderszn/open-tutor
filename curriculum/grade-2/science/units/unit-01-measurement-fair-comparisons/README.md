# Unit 01 — Measurement, Fair Comparisons, and Recording Evidence

**Grade:** 2 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#15](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/15)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 3–6 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, 25–30 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

This unit teaches the *practices* that power the rest of the year: how to ask a
question that can be tested, how to observe carefully, how to measure length to
the nearest centimeter, how to run a **fair test** (change one thing, keep the
rest the same), and how to record data in drawings, tallies, tables, and simple
graphs so a claim can be supported with evidence. The unit's investigation —
testing which material absorbs the most water — is a fair comparison of
materials (2-PS1-1, 2-PS1-2), and the centimeter-measuring skill honors the
framework's assessment boundary for 2-PS1-2: quantitative measurement is limited
to length at grade 2.

## Prerequisites (from grade-1 science, re-taught here as needed)

- Asking questions that can be explored by looking, touching, or testing.
- Observing with the senses and simple tools (hand lens, flashlight, ruler);
  recording in words, drawings, or tallies; communicating findings.
- Comparing materials by observable properties (color, texture, bend, sink/float).
- Science safety practices: keep materials away from the mouth, handle tools
  gently, never look directly at the sun, name the safe alternative when an
  investigation is unsafe to touch.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Ask a question that can be tested by changing **one thing** while keeping
   everything else the same (a fair test), and explain why the test is fair.
2. Measure the length of an object to the nearest **centimeter** with a ruler,
   starting from the 0 mark (not the ruler's edge).
3. Record observations in a **labeled drawing**, a **tally chart**, and a
   **simple data table**.
4. Plan and carry out a fair comparison of two materials, collecting and
   recording data over repeated trials (2-PS1-1).
5. Build and read a **picture graph** and a **bar graph** from the collected
   data, and state an evidence-based claim (2-PS1-2).

## Essential question

*How can I make sure a comparison is fair, and how do my measurements and
records help me believe what I found?*

## Vocabulary

**Fair test** — a test that changes only one thing while everything else stays
the same. · **Observation** — something noticed with the senses. · **Measure** —
find out how long (or how much) with a tool. · **Centimeter** — a small unit of
length; this unit's ruler unit. · **Estimate** — a careful guess *before*
measuring. · **Trial** — one try of a test (scientists repeat trials). ·
**Data** — the measurements and observations recorded. · **Table** — rows and
columns that hold data. · **Tally** — marks that count (|||| with a cross for
the fifth). · **Graph** — a picture of data (bars or symbols). · **Evidence** —
data used to support an idea. · **Claim** — what you say is true because of
your evidence.

## Standards and source notes

- Reference framework: Next Generation Science Standards, grade-2 performance
  expectations. Codes and descriptions below were re-verified 2026-10-09
  against the NSTA reproduction of the official NGSS 2-PS1 document
  (static.nsta.org) and the CDE CA NGSS page for 2-PS1-1; the engineering
  asterisk and assessment boundaries were corroborated in the scope-and-sequence
  (2026-10-03). No state adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification is
  claimed.
- **2-PS1-1** — Plan and conduct an investigation to describe and classify
  different kinds of materials by their observable properties. This unit's
  investigation compares materials' absorbency fairly; measurement practice
  uses length, one of the observable properties named in the clarification
  statement.
- **2-PS1-2 \*** — Analyze data obtained from testing different materials to
  determine which materials have the properties that are best suited for an
  intended purpose. The unit's graphing lessons and final claim address the
  "analyze data" practice; the unit keeps all quantitative measurement to
  length, per this PE's assessment boundary.
- Science and engineering practices used throughout: **SEP-3** (planning and
  carrying out investigations), **SEP-4** (analyzing and interpreting data),
  **SEP-7** (engaging in argument from evidence). The crosscutting concept is
  **patterns** (repeated trials show similar results; graphs reveal patterns).

## 16-session pacing

Adapt frequency and duration for the learner; the success criterion for each
session is named. K–2 tasks may be oral, pointing, drawing, manipulative, or
adult-scribed; explicit adult directions are built into every lesson.

**Week 3 — Questions and observations**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Lesson 01](lessons/lesson-01-fair-test-questions.md): Fair-test questions | Learner sorts 4 questions into "can test" / "cannot test" and explains one choice |
| 2 | Observation walk: choose an object, observe with the hand lens, draw it | Labeled drawing with at least 3 labels (adult may scribe) |
| 3 | [Lesson 02](lessons/lesson-02-observing-and-recording.md): Observing carefully and recording | Learner records observations in words, drawing, and tallies for one object |
| 4 | Reading-and-talk: what did scientists notice this week? | Learner retells one observation and asks one new testable question |

**Week 4 — Measuring and fair tests**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | [Lesson 03](lessons/lesson-03-measuring-centimeters.md): Measuring to the nearest centimeter | 4 of 5 objects measured correctly from the 0 mark, nearest cm |
| 6 | Measuring practice: [practice.md](practice.md) tasks 1–4 | Independent measuring with estimates first |
| 7 | [Lesson 04](lessons/lesson-04-fair-tests-tables.md): Fair tests and data tables (uses generated image activity) | Learner names the fair test in the image and spots both changes in the unfair test |
| 8 | Project plan: [project.md](project.md) steps 1–4 | Fair-test plan written: question, one change, what stays same, how to measure |

**Week 5 — Data, graphs, and claims**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | [Lesson 05](lessons/lesson-05-graphs-and-claims.md): Graphs and evidence claims | Learner reads a bar graph and states a claim with evidence |
| 10 | Project data collection: 3 trials of the absorbency test | Data table complete with units |
| 11 | Graphing practice: [practice.md](practice.md) tasks 5–7 | Picture graph and bar graph of the learner's own data |
| 12 | Project conclusion: evidence talk and poster | "My evidence is ___ , so my claim is ___" stated aloud or in writing |

**Week 6 — Review and assessment**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | Review games: measurement relay, "fair or not?" card sort | Oral explanations correct for 4 of 5 cards |
| 14 | Re-measure challenge: re-measure week-4 objects independently | Measurements within 1 cm of the earlier values; differences discussed, not hidden |
| 15 | [Formative quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md) + project poster share | Quiz checked same day; learner presents one finding |
| 16 | [Unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md) | Completed with adult as scribe if needed; keys in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) |

Retrieval: each session's warm-up revisits the previous session's skill
(e.g., Week 4 opens with "sort this question — testable or not?"; Week 6 opens
with cm measuring). Review games in Week 6 re-check every objective.

## Accessibility and supports

Oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative response modes for all checks; the
adult scribes dictated observations. Short written practice (6–8 tasks) is
reviewed the same day. Visual science word wall with objects, not just words;
sentence frames ("I observed ___, so I think ___"); color never the only cue
in graphs. Table variant and active-investigation variant in every lesson.
See [teacher-guide.md](teacher-guide.md) for differentiated supports and
extensions.
