# Answer Key — Unit 01

**For the guiding adult only.** Every item below was solved independently
against the question as written; acceptable variations are noted. Keep this
file separate from the learner's materials.

## practice.md

**Measuring practice**

1. Estimates vary; measured values must be from the 0, nearest cm. Accept
   any honest pair; the circled "closest" object is the learner's own data.
2. Edge-starting adds ~1 cm (or the edge offset). The 0-start measurement
   is honest because the ruler's numbers begin at the 0 mark, not the
   physical edge. Re-measured value should match the Lesson-03-style correct
   reading.
3. Example: spoon 13 cm, pencil 18 cm → pencil is 5 cm longer (18 − 13).
   Any correct subtraction of the two recorded lengths is acceptable; the
   method (subtract, or count up on the ruler) must be shown.
4. Two measurements should agree within 1 cm. If they differ, re-check
   rule 2 (start at 0) first, then rule 3 (nearest cm).

**Graphing practice** (practice dataset: A = 5 cm, B = 8 cm, C = 6 cm)

5. Bar graph: three bars at heights 5, 6, 8 squares; bottom axis labeled
   Plant A / B / C; side axis "height in cm"; one square = 1 cm.
6. Acceptable: "My claim is Plant B is tallest. My evidence is Plant B's
   bar shows 8 cm, taller than A's 5 cm and C's 6 cm." Any equivalent
   claim-evidence pair using the data is correct.
7. 8 − 5 = **3 cm taller**.
8. Acceptable: "Without the axis label you can't tell what the numbers
   mean — 8 of what? It could be cm, spoons, anything." Any answer naming
   the missing-unit problem is correct.

**Spare warm-ups**

- Fair — changed: the ball; same: ramp, release. (If the learner says
  "the ramp is the same and the release is the same," accept.)
- Tallies: 3 = |||, 5 = |||| with crossbar, 2 = ||, 4 = ||||. Reading back:
  3, 5, 2, 4.
- Opinion. Fixed example: "My claim is towel A is better for spills. My
  evidence is it held 6 spoons and towel B held 3." Any claim citing data
  is acceptable.

## assessments/week-04_quiz.md

1. **Fair.** Kept the same: drop height, floor (accept any one; both is
   better). 2 points with the "same" named; 1 for "fair" without it.
2. **Unfair:** two changes — the object (car vs. truck) and the ramp height.
   Fix: same vehicle on both ramps (question becomes "does ramp height
   matter?") OR same-height ramps with the two vehicles (question becomes
   "which vehicle rolls farther?"). Either fix is correct.
3. Learner points to the 0 (not the edge) and reads the nearest cm. Accept
   ±1 cm; the procedure is what is scored. (Actual length depends on the
   household crayon — verify against your own measurement.)
4. **Sponge.** Evidence: every sponge trial (5, 6, 5 spoons) beats every
   foil trial (1, 1, 0 spoons). Accept pointing to any correct comparison.
5. **Left** station is fair. The right station is unfair because **two**
   things change: the ramp height (tall vs. short) and the object (red car
   vs. yellow ball). 2 points for both changes named; 1 for one.
6. **Claim with evidence** — but only once the evidence is added. Acceptable
   completion: "My evidence is the sponge held 5, 6, and 5 spoons and the
   foil held 1, 1, and 0 spoons." Any correct data citation works.
7. **Not yet.** One trial could be luck (a bump, a crooked release). We need
   three repeated trials close together before we trust the result.

## assessments/unit-assessment.md (18 points)

**A1.** (2 pts) Estimate stated aloud + measurement from 0 to nearest cm.
Full credit for honest procedure; ±1 cm tolerance on the reading.
**A2.** (2 pts) Drawing with two sensible labels. 1 pt for one label or
unlabeled drawing.

**B1.** (2 pts) (a) the paper (kind of paper); (b) any two of: same thrower,
same throwing force, same starting line, same paper size, same folding
method kept identical across planes — note: the *fold* must be the same for
all planes, otherwise it is a second change; (c) measure distance with a
ruler/tape in centimeters (accept "ruler, cm").
**B2.** (2 pts) **Plan X is fair** — same height, only the object changes;
the question it answers is "which falls first," not "which is heavier."
Plan Y is unfair — height AND object change, so a difference in landing
order can't be attributed to either. (Subtle: accept "X is fair" with any
correct rationale.)

**C1.** (2 pts) Bars at 4 (or 5) and 0 (or 1) — middle trials are Cloth = 4,
Plastic = 0; accept either median or any single stated representative
trial as long as it is labeled which. Axis labeled "spoons of water held."
**C2.** (2 pts) "My claim is the cloth holds more water. My evidence is the
cloth held 4–5 spoons and the plastic held 0–1 spoons." Any data-cited
equivalent.
**C3.** (2 pts) Cloth — because the data show it holds (4–5 spoons vs.
0–1), so it is best suited for mopping (2-PS1-2 reasoning).

**D1.** (2 pts) **Disagree.** It was not a fair test (two changes: ramp
height and car), so there is no evidence that the height caused the result —
the car could have caused it. Must use both "fair test" and "evidence"
(or clear synonyms) for full credit; 1 pt for the right idea without the
vocabulary.

**Project rubric scoring** is in [project.md](project.md); the 9 trial
values are the learner's own data — check the *procedure* (same size
squares, same spoon, same wait) and the *honesty* (all trials recorded,
including surprises), not the winner.
