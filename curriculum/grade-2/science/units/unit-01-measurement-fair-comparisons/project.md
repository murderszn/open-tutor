# Project — The Great Absorbency Test (a fair investigation)

**Time:** three 25–30 minute sessions (Sessions 8, 10, 12) · **Adult
supervision required throughout.**
**Question:** *Which material holds the most water — and how do we know?*

This is the unit's investigation (2-PS1-1, 2-PS1-2): the learner plans a
fair test, collects data over repeated trials, graphs it, and argues from
evidence which material is best suited for soaking up spills.

## Materials (all household / dollar-store; adult gathers)

- 3 materials to compare: a paper towel square, a cotton cloth square
  (washcloth), and a plastic bag square — all cut to **the same size**
  (adult pre-cuts, e.g., 15 cm × 15 cm)
- A measuring spoon (1-tablespoon), a small cup of water, a tray or baking
  pan, a timer or clock
- Science journal, pencil, ruler, crayons
- Paper towels for cleanup

**Safety:** Water only — no other liquids. Materials stay away from the
mouth. Wipe spills immediately so nobody slips. If any material has sharp
edges, the adult handles cutting.

## Steps

1. **Plan (Session 8).** Write the question at the top of a journal page.
   Name the **one change** (the material) and **three things kept the same**:
   same-size squares, same amount of water per spoon, same waiting time
   (count slowly to 20 while the water soaks in). Draw the data table:
   one row per material, three trial columns, and a "spoons held" label.
2. **Predict.** Before testing, the learner predicts a winner and says why
   ("I think the towel wins because it feels fluffy"). The prediction is
   recorded — it is fine to be wrong.
3. **Test (Session 10).** For each material, lay it flat on the tray, add
   water **one spoon at a time**, and count how many spoons it holds before
   water runs off the edge. Wait the same count-to-20 between spoons.
   Repeat for **3 trials** per material. Record every trial.
4. **Graph.** Build a bar graph of the middle (median) trial for each
   material; axis labeled "spoons of water held." (Optional: also graph all
   three trials — see Lesson 05 extension.)
5. **Conclude (Session 12).** Answer: which material held the most water?
   Write or dictate: "My claim is ___. My evidence is ___." Then answer the
   purpose question: "Which material would you choose to clean up a spill,
   and why does the data support that choice?" (2-PS1-2)
6. **Poster.** Make a one-page "fair test poster": question, labeled drawing
   of the setup, the table, the graph, and the claim-evidence sentence.
   Share it with the household (Session 15).

## Deliverables

1. Fair-test plan page (question, one change, three constants, prediction).
2. Data table with all 9 trials and the unit (spoons).
3. Bar graph with labeled axis.
4. Claim-evidence conclusion + purpose choice.
5. Fair-test poster.

## Rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fair-test plan | Names the one change AND ≥2 things kept the same | Names the change but not the constants | Cannot separate change from constants — reteach L01 |
| Data collection | All 9 trials recorded with unit (spoons); same procedure each time | Trials recorded but procedure varied or unit missing | Data missing or fabricated — redo with adult modeling (see L02 honesty note) |
| Graph | Bars correct heights, axis labeled "spoons of water held" | Bars correct but axis unlabeled | Bars don't match data — rebuild from table |
| Claim & evidence | Claim names the winner AND cites data ("6 spoons vs 3") | Claim names winner without data | Opinion only ("my favorite") — return to L05 frame |
| Purpose choice | Chooses material for the spill-cleanup job and links choice to data | Chooses with a non-data reason | No choice — discuss what "best for a purpose" means |

**Readiness to move on:** The learner can explain, unprompted, why changing
two things would have ruined the test. If not, replay the image activity
from Lesson 04 before Unit 02.

**Simulation alternative:** If water play is not possible (no tray space,
sensory needs), run the same fair test as a *thought experiment* with a
clearly labeled fictional dataset (adult provides 9 numbers), and the
learner builds the table, graph, and claim from it. The rubric applies
unchanged; the plan and claim are still the learner's own work.
