## Lesson guided practice (solutions)

**L01:** (1) Changed: shape and identity (car → boat). Stayed the
same: 6 bricks, same colors. (2) One piece is missing or still in the
box — count again to check. (3) Any new arrangement of the same cups
counts; the cup count must stay the same. (4) No — same pieces kept
their colors; only the arrangement changed. (5) Sam added a brick or
counted one twice; same pieces means the same count.

**L02:** (1) It melted; warming from the sun caused it. (2) The sunny
cup — its ice melted more (4 of 4 vs. 1 of 4). (3) Firm → soft; it
softened because it warmed. (4) Same-size cubes in sun vs. shade —
only the place changes. (5) It did not disappear; it became liquid
water in the cup. Melted ice is still water.

**L03:** (1) It froze; cooling caused it. (2) It hardened; cooling
caused it. (3) Warming — melting is a warming change. (4) Order:
liquid water → (cooling) → ice → (warming) → liquid water. (5) It did
not disappear; it is the same water, now solid, and it can melt back.

**L04:** (1) CAN GO BACK — water can freeze into ice again.
(2) CAN GO BACK — ice can melt into water again. (3) CAN GO BACK —
soft butter hardens in the refrigerator. (4) CANNOT GO BACK — cooling
cannot make a cooked egg raw again. (5) CANNOT GO BACK — thawing does
not make a frozen leaf fresh and green again. (6) Counterexample: a
cooked egg — waiting and cooling never make it raw again.

**L05:** (1) Evidence: water from melted ice freezes into ice again
in the freezer. (2) Evidence: the cooked egg stays white and firm
when cooled; it never becomes raw again. (3) Just a claim — it has no
evidence yet. Complete it with: "Soft butter hardened in the
refrigerator." (4) "…a frozen leaf stayed brown and limp after it
thawed; it did not become fresh and green again." (5) Sam's error: one
example cannot prove "all," and the cooked egg disproves "all." The
correct claim is "some can, some cannot," with one example of each.

## Practice (solutions)

**Task 1:** Before count = after count = 6 bricks (or the count of the
model used). Changed: shape/identity. Stayed the same: the pieces and
their count. Any new object built from exactly the same bricks is
correct.

**Task 2:** One piece is missing (left in the box, under the table, or
miscounted). Mira should recount the pieces and search the work area,
then rebuild and count again.

**Task 3:** (1) Ice melts into liquid water — warming. (2) Firm butter
softens and spreads easily — warming. (3) Liquid water freezes into
solid ice — cooling. (4) Soft butter hardens into firm butter —
cooling.

**Task 4 (fictional practice data):** (1) Fastest: sunny windowsill —
4 of 4 cubes melted in 30 minutes, more than any other place.
(2) Slowest: refrigerator — 0 of 4 melted. (3) "My evidence is that
4 of 4 cubes melted in the sun and only 1 of 4 melted in the shade
(and 0 of 4 in the refrigerator) in the same 30 minutes."

**Task 5:** CAN GO BACK: (1) ice melts, (2) water freezes, (3) butter
softens, (4) butter hardens. CANNOT GO BACK: (5) egg cooked, (6) leaf
frozen brown. Reasons: melted ice can refreeze; frozen water can
remelt; softened butter can re-harden; hardened butter can re-soften;
a cooked egg cannot become raw; a frozen brown leaf cannot become
fresh and green.

**Task 6 (sample full argument):** "My claim is that some changes
caused by heating or cooling can be reversed and some cannot. My
reversible evidence is that ice melted into water in the sun and the
water froze back into ice in the freezer. My irreversible evidence is
that a cooked egg stayed white and firm when cooled and never became
raw again. This shows that some changes can go back and some cannot,
because the ice went back and the egg did not."

## Week-04 quiz (solutions)

1. Stayed the same: 8 bricks (same pieces, same colors). Changed:
   shape and identity (house → bridge).
2. Warming — the sun warmed the ice, and warming melts ice.
3. Solid ice; the change is called freezing.
4. Reversible — soft butter hardens back into firm butter when cooled
   (in the refrigerator).
5. Left scene can go back (melting ice, soft butter). Right scene,
   cannot go back: the cooked egg (cooling cannot make it raw again)
   or the frost-covered brown leaf (thawing does not restore it).
6. Sunny windowsill melted faster. "My evidence is that 4 of 4 cubes
   melted in the sun and only 1 of 4 melted in the shade in 30
   minutes."
7. No — a cooked egg stays white and firm when cooled; it cannot
   become a raw egg again. That is an irreversible change.
8. It is a claim that still needs evidence (not yet a full argument).
   Completing evidence: "Water from melted ice froze back into ice in
   the freezer" (or the butter round trip).

## Unit assessment (solutions)

1. (a) Changed: shape/identity of the object. (b) Stayed the same:
   the 7 bricks, their colors and sizes. (c) Counting proves it: the
   before count and the after count are both 7, so no piece was added
   or lost. (Any correctly counted model the adult used is accepted;
   the counts must match.)
2. One piece is missing or miscounted. Sam should recount, search the
   work area, and rebuild and count again.
3. The ice melts into liquid water. Change: melting. Cause: warming
   (warm sunlight).
4. The butter softens and spreads easily; its edges droop. Change:
   softening. Cause: warming (warm room).
5. The water freezes into solid ice. Change: freezing. Cause: cooling
   (freezer).
6. The butter hardens back into firm butter; it holds its shape.
   Change: hardening. Cause: cooling (refrigerator).
7. CAN GO BACK: (a) ice melts into water — water can freeze again;
   (c) water freezes into ice — ice can melt again. CANNOT GO BACK:
   (b) egg cooked — cooling cannot make it raw; (d) leaf frozen brown
   — thawing does not restore it.
8. (a) Left scene (melting ice, soft butter). (b) Either right-scene
   change with its reason: cooked egg — cooling cannot make it raw
   again; or frost-covered brown leaf — thawing does not make it fresh
   and green again.
9. No, it cannot go back. Heating paper is irreversible: the browned,
   curled, stiff paper stays browned and curled when cooled; warming
   or cooling cannot restore flat white paper.
10. Full marks require: the "some can, some cannot" claim; one
    reversible piece of evidence (melted ice refreezing, frozen water
    remelting, butter softening/hardening round trip); one
    irreversible piece of evidence (cooked egg, frozen leaf, heated
    paper); and a "this shows…" link connecting the evidence to the
    claim. Sample: see Practice Task 6 above.

## Project (scoring notes)

- **Melt table:** accept the learner's own counts; the rubric scores
  whether both cups were counted after the same 30 minutes, not
  whether the counts match Lesson 02's demonstration (4 vs. 1). If the
  learner's sunny cup did not melt fastest (cloudy day, different
  cups), score the honesty of the recorded data, and use the journal
  discussion to revisit fair-test constants — do not penalize weather.
- **Sort:** all seven changes (melted ice, frozen water, softened
  butter, hardened butter, cooked egg, frozen leaf, heated paper) with
  observation-based reasons; see Practice Task 5 for the correct
  groups.
- **Argument:** same requirements as Assessment Q10. The talk may be
  fully oral with the poster as support.
- **Sunlight unavailable:** warm room vs. cool room is an accepted
  substitute for sun vs. shade, provided the one-change plan is kept.
