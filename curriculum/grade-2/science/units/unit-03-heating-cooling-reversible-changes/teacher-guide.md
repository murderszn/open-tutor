# Teacher Guide — Unit 03

**For the guiding adult.** This guide covers objectives, common
misconceptions, differentiated supports, extensions, and what "ready to
move on" looks like. Solutions live in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) —
keep it separate from the learner's materials.

## Objectives recap

1. Take apart a small-piece object and rebuild it; describe what
   changed and what stayed the same (2-PS1-3).
2. Observe and describe heating changes: ice melting, butter softening.
3. Observe and describe cooling changes: water freezing, butter
   hardening.
4. Sort heating/cooling changes into reversible and irreversible, with
   reasons.
5. Argue with evidence that some changes can be reversed and some
   cannot (2-PS1-4).

## Lesson-by-lesson misconception notes

- **L01:** "Rebuilding changes the pieces themselves." Counter with
  the count: "Count them. Same bricks — new arrangement." Also:
  learners who add or lose a piece and still call it "the same" — the
  count is the check, every time.
- **L02:** "Melted means gone." Counter by pointing to the liquid in
  the cup: "It is still here — it changed form." Also: "the sun made
  the ice" — no, the sun warmed ice that was already there.
- **L03:** "The freezer creates ice from nothing." Counter: "The water
  was already there. Cooling changed it, the way warming changed the
  ice back." Also: confusing *freeze* (liquid → solid) with *harden*
  (soft → firm) — keep one example of each visible.
- **L04:** "Irreversible means broken or ruined." Counter: "A cooked
  egg is not ruined — it is food. Irreversible only means it cannot go
  back to raw." Also: "cold can undo anything" — the frozen leaf is
  the counterexample that breaks it.
- **L05:** "More examples always make a better argument." Counter:
  "Two well-chosen examples — one of each kind — beat ten examples of
  the same kind. What matters is that the evidence matches the claim."
  Also: a claim with no evidence is not yet an argument — always ask
  "what did you see?"

## Differentiated supports

- **Response modes:** Every check accepts oral, pointing, drawing,
  manipulative, or adult-scribed answers. Never require handwriting to
  demonstrate science understanding.
- **Sentence frames:** "Before ___. After ___. Warming/cooling caused
  ___." / "___ can go back because ___." / "My claim is ___. My
  evidence is ___." Keep all three visible all unit.
- **Language:** Teach vocabulary with objects first (ice, butter, egg
  samples, leaf), keep the visual word wall current, welcome
  home-language labels, and pair *reversible* with a circle gesture
  and *irreversible* with a one-way-arrow gesture.
- **Attention/pacing:** Sessions are 25–30 minutes by design. If a
  session runs long, split it: teach the worked examples one day,
  practice the next. Movement breaks between the two worked examples
  help — especially the freeze/harden gesture game in Lesson 03.
- **Low vision:** Tactile material samples (real ice in a cup, firm
  vs. soft butter to touch), large-print tables, high-contrast sort
  cards; the generated image ships with a full text-only alternative
  (see Lesson 04, the quiz, and the assessment) so every image
  question is answerable without seeing the picture.
- **Fine motor:** The adult holds cups and trays steady while the
  learner observes and counts; sticker or stamp counts are fine for
  the melt table.

## Extensions (for learners who are ready)

- L01: rebuild the same pieces into *two* different new objects and
  compare all three; or write one sentence with the piece count.
- L02: predict, then fair-test, whether one big cube or four small
  cubes melt first in the same sun (small cubes: more surface meets
  the warmth).
- L03: freeze one safe extension item (for example, juice in a small
  cup) and compare with water; label it an extension observation, not
  a framework example.
- L04: "Is rebuilding bricks reversible?" (Yes — the original object
  can be rebuilt from the same pieces.)
- L05: "Convince a skeptic" — answer "but what if the melted ice is
  gone forever?" with the freezer evidence; or write the argument as
  a 4-sentence paragraph instead of the frame.

## Safety summary (applies to every session)

- The learner never touches a stove, flame, oven, kettle, or hot
  water. The adult handles all warm water and all cooking, and the
  learner observes from a safe distance or from pre-prepared cooled
  samples and photographs.
- Paper is never heated in front of the learner; the browned-paper
  sample is prepared and cooled by the adult beforehand.
- The adult handles freezer and refrigerator items; the learner touches
  only items the adult says are safe to touch.
- Keep all materials away from the mouth. If any step is unsafe,
  unavailable, or inaccessible, use the observation-only alternative
  named in the lesson — never skip the learning goal, and never force
  the risky step.

## Readiness and reteach

- **Ready to move on (Unit 04):** the learner sorts new changes
  correctly with reasons, states a two-sided argument (one reversible,
  one irreversible example), and rebuilds a take-apart object with a
  matching piece count. Assessment 16–20, or project rubric 10–12.
- **Reteach triggers:** a learner who sorts by "hot vs. cold" instead
  of "can it go back" repeats Lesson 04 with new cards; a learner who
  gives claims without evidence repeats Lesson 05 with the frame and
  the learner's own melt data; a learner who loses the piece count
  repeats Lesson 01 with a 6-brick bag.
- **What "ready" does not mean:** handwriting quality, spelling, or
  speed. Oral, drawn, and scribed responses count fully.
