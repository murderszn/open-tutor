# Unit 03 — Heating, Cooling, and Reversible Changes

**Grade:** 2 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#15](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/15)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 11–14 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, 25–30 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

This unit answers a question every 7-year-old has watched happen: *when
something is heated or cooled, can it go back to the way it was?* The
learner first takes apart an object made of a small set of pieces and
rebuilds the same pieces into something new, and describes what changed
and what stayed the same (2-PS1-3). Then the learner watches heating and
cooling at work — ice melting in warmth, butter softening, water freezing,
melted butter hardening again — and learns to sort those changes into two
groups: changes that **can be undone** (reversible) and changes that
**cannot be undone** (irreversible). The unit ends with the learner
building a short argument — a claim, plus evidence from observations —
that some changes caused by heating or cooling can be reversed and some
cannot (2-PS1-4).

All heating and cooling in this unit stays at the observation level the
framework names: water and butter at different temperatures for reversible
changes; cooking an egg, freezing a plant leaf, and heating paper for
irreversible changes. **Safety is built into every lesson:** the learner
never touches a stove, flame, oven, or hot water. The adult handles all hot
water and all cooking, and the learner observes from a safe distance or
from pre-prepared cooled samples and photographs. Every heating step has
an observation-only alternative, and every learner hands-on step uses only
sunlight, shade, a refrigerator, a freezer, and room-temperature or cool
water.

## Prerequisites (re-taught here as needed)

- Asking a question that can be tested, and running a **fair test**
  (change one thing, keep the rest the same) — Unit 01.
- Describing and classifying everyday materials by observable properties
  — color, texture, hardness, flexibility — and testing materials for a
  purpose — Unit 02.
- Recording observations in labeled drawings, tallies, and simple tables;
  stating a claim with evidence ("My evidence is ___ , so my claim is ___").
- Science safety practices: keep materials away from the mouth, handle
  living things gently, never look directly at the sun, and name the safe
  alternative when an investigation is unsafe to touch.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Take apart an object made of a small set of pieces and rebuild the
   same pieces into a new object, and describe with observations what
   changed and what stayed the same (2-PS1-3).
2. Observe and describe changes caused by **heating** — ice melting and
   butter softening — using warm sunlight or adult-handled warm water
   only, and compare before-and-after observations.
3. Observe and describe changes caused by **cooling** — water freezing
   and melted butter hardening again — and compare before-and-after
   observations.
4. Sort heating and cooling changes into **reversible** (can go back)
   and **irreversible** (cannot go back), giving an observation-based
   reason for each sort.
5. Construct an argument with evidence that some changes caused by
   heating or cooling can be reversed and some cannot, using at least
   one reversible and one irreversible example (2-PS1-4).

## Essential question

*When heating or cooling changes something, how can I tell whether it can
go back to the way it was — and what is my evidence?*

## Vocabulary

**Pieces** — the small parts an object is made of. · **Take apart** — to
separate an object into its pieces. · **Rebuild** — to put the same pieces
together into something new. · **Heat / warming** — making something
warmer (here: sunlight, warm water, a warm room). · **Cooling** — making
something colder (here: shade, a refrigerator, a freezer). · **Melt** — a
solid turning into a liquid when warmed (ice → water). · **Freeze** — a
liquid turning into a solid when cooled (water → ice). · **Soften** —
becoming soft and easy to spread or bend when warmed (firm butter →
soft butter). · **Harden** — becoming firm again when cooled (soft
butter → firm butter). · **Reversible** — a change that can go back to
the way it was. · **Irreversible** — a change that cannot go back to the
way it was. · **Evidence** — observations or data used to support an
idea. · **Claim** — what you say is true because of your evidence. ·
**Argument** — a claim plus the evidence that supports it.

- Reference framework: Next Generation Science Standards, grade-2
  performance expectations. Codes and descriptions below were
  re-verified 2026-10-23 against the official-text reproductions used
  by the track audit (the NGSS 2-PS1 document and the California
  Department of Education 2-PS1-4 page, which reproduce the same
  wording), and against the American Chemical Society's Grade 2
  Lesson 5.1 NGSS alignment sheet for 2-PS1-4 (opened 2026-10-23). The
  track audit's crosswalk (2026-10-03) carries the same codes and
  clarification statements. No state adoption, accreditation, or
  alignment certification is claimed.
- **2-PS1-3** — Make observations to construct an evidence-based
  account of how an object made of a small set of pieces can be
  disassembled and made into a new object. Clarification: examples of
  pieces include blocks, building bricks, or other assorted small
  objects. Lesson 01 is this expectation, taught with building bricks
  and a take-apart toy.
- **2-PS1-4** — Construct an argument with evidence that some changes
  caused by heating or cooling can be reversed and some cannot.
  Clarification: examples of reversible changes include water and
  butter at different temperatures; examples of irreversible changes
  include cooking an egg, freezing a plant leaf, and heating paper.
  Lessons 02–05 teach exactly these examples — no others are presented
  as framework examples.
- Science and engineering practices used throughout: **SEP-6**
  (constructing explanations — the take-apart account in Lesson 01) and
  **SEP-7** (engaging in argument from evidence — the claim-and-evidence
  argument in Lesson 05). The crosscutting concept is **cause and
  effect**: heating or cooling causes an observable change, and
  sometimes that change can be undone.

## 16-session pacing

Adapt frequency and duration for the learner; the success criterion for
each session is named. K–2 tasks may be oral, pointing, drawing,
manipulative, or adult-scribed; explicit adult directions are built into
every lesson.

**Week 11 — Take apart and rebuild (2-PS1-3)**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Lesson 01](lessons/lesson-01-take-apart-and-rebuild.md): Take apart and rebuild | Learner rebuilds the same pieces into a new object and names 1 thing that changed and 1 thing that stayed the same |
| 2 | Take-apart practice: [practice.md](practice.md) tasks 1–2, journal drawings | Two labeled before/after drawings with piece counts that match |
