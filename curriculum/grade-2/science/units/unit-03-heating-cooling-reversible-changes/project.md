# Project — The Melt-and-Freeze Detective Fair

**Type:** Investigation with a poster and an evidence talk.
**Time:** Sessions 6, 8, 12, and 14 (plus observation checks between
sessions). **Adult role:** plans safety, handles the freezer and any
warm water, scribes as needed, and asks the argument questions at the
share.

## Question

*When ice melts, can the water go back to ice — and what other changes
can or cannot go back?*

## Materials

- 8 same-size ice cubes, 2 clear cups, a sunny windowsill and a shady
  spot, a timer or clock
- Science journal, pencil/crayons, poster paper, the Lesson 04 sort
  cards, the generated illustration
  ([assets/reversible-irreversible-changes.png](assets/reversible-irreversible-changes.png))
- One small cup for refreezing meltwater (adult handles the freezer)
- Pre-prepared adult-demonstration samples or photographs: a cooked egg
  (cooked by the adult at the stove, shown cooled), a frozen-then-thawed
  leaf (frozen by the adult overnight), a browned paper scrap (browned
  and cooled by the adult beforehand)

**Safety:** The learner never touches a stove, flame, oven, or hot
water, and never heats paper. The learner's hands-on work uses only
sunlight, shade, cool water, and the refrigerator. If sunlight is
unavailable, use a warm room vs. a cool room instead of sun vs. shade,
and keep everything else the same. Observation-only alternatives
(photographs, pre-prepared samples) replace any step the adult judges
unsafe or unavailable.

## Steps

1. **Plan (Session 6).** Write the question, the one change (sun vs.
   shade), and everything kept the same (same-size cubes, same cups,
   same 30 minutes). Predict: which cup will melt faster, and why?
2. **Set up (Session 6).** Place 4 cubes in each cup. Draw both cups
   before the wait.
3. **First observations (Session 6–8).** After 30 minutes, count melted
   cubes in each cup and record the melt table. Draw both cups after
   the wait.
4. **The round trip (Session 8).** The adult pours one cup of meltwater
   into a tray and freezes it overnight. Next session, observe: the
   water is ice again. Add the round-trip drawing (water → ice →
   water) to the journal.
5. **Sort (Session 12).** Add a "Can it go back?" column to the poster.
   Sort every change observed or demonstrated in the unit — melted ice,
   frozen water, softened butter, hardened butter, cooked egg, frozen
   leaf, heated paper — with a one-line reason each.
6. **Argue (Sessions 12 and 14).** Write the claim-and-evidence argument
   on the poster (one reversible example, one irreversible example).
   Present it at the share: state the claim, point to the melt data,
   and answer two follow-up questions from the adult.

## Deliverables

1. A melt table with counts for both cups (sun vs. shade) after
   30 minutes.
2. Before/after journal drawings for the melt race and for the
   refreeze round trip.
3. A "Can it go back?" poster: the melt data, all seven sorted changes
   with reasons, and the claim-and-evidence argument.
4. An evidence talk at the share (oral, with the poster as support).

## Rubric

| Criterion | Beginning (1) | On the way (2) | Strong (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fair plan | The plan changes more than one thing, or names no constants | One change named; most constants named | One change (sun vs. shade) and constants (same cubes, cups, time) all named |
| Observations and data | Melt counts missing or guessed | One cup counted; drawings lack labels | Both cups counted after 30 minutes; labeled before/after drawings; refreeze observed |
| Sort with reasons | Sorts are guesses, or no reasons | Most sorts correct, reasons for some | All seven changes sorted correctly, each with an observation-based reason |
| Argument | Claim only, or evidence from one side only | Claim with evidence from both sides, but the link ("this shows…") is missing | Claim plus one reversible and one irreversible piece of evidence plus a "this shows…" link |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = ready to move on. 7–9 = move on with the Session 15
reteach of missed items. 4–6 = repeat the sort and argument sessions
before the unit assessment. The rubric scores the poster and talk, not
handwriting or spelling — oral, drawn, and scribed responses count
fully.
