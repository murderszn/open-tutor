# Lesson 01 — What Is a Habitat?

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** science journal, pencil/crayons,
4 word cards (FOOD, WATER, SHELTER, SPACE), a small log or a picture of
a log, a cup of water, a cracker

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will learn what a **habitat**
is. A habitat is the place where a living thing lives — and it gives
that living thing four things it needs.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "Where does a bird live? What does it
need there?" Any answer that names a place (a tree, a nest) and one need
(food, water, a safe spot) shows readiness. If the learner has none,
model one: "A robin lives in a tree. The tree gives it a nest and a
safe place, and the yard gives it worms to eat."

**Safety note:** Today is a talking-and-looking lesson. No collecting
and no touching of wild animals. If you step outside to look, stay on
paths and wash hands when you come back in.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

A **habitat** is the place where a living thing lives. It is its home.
But a habitat is more than an address. A good habitat gives the living
thing **four things** it needs to live:

1. **Food** — something to eat.
2. **Water** — something to drink.
3. **Shelter** — a safe place to hide, rest, and stay out of bad weather.
4. **Space** — room to move, grow, and live.

Adult holds up the four word cards one at a time and says each word
with the learner, then asks the learner to point to something in the
room that gives one of the four things. (A kitchen gives food. A tap
gives water. A roof gives shelter. A floor gives space to move.)

**Worked example 1 — a sparrow, modeled out loud, slowly:**

> Adult: "Think of a sparrow, a small brown bird. Its habitat is the
> yard and the tree. Food: it eats seeds and little insects it finds
> in the grass. Water: it drinks from a puddle or a birdbath. Shelter:
> it sleeps in the tree, where cats cannot reach it. Space: it flies
> from the tree to the grass to the fence — room to move. Four things,
> one habitat. If the tree were gone, the sparrow would lose its
> shelter. If the puddle dried up, it would lose its water. A habitat
> has to give all four."

Adult draws a quick four-box picture in the journal — food, water,
shelter, space — and the learner draws or names one thing in each box
for the sparrow.

**Worked example 2 — an earthworm, and a habitat inside a habitat:**

> Adult: "Now think of an earthworm. Its habitat is the soil under the
> garden. Food: it eats tiny dead leaves in the soil. Water: the soil
> is damp, so the worm stays wet. Shelter: the soil itself hides it
> from birds. Space: it can wiggle through the soil in every
> direction. Now look at this log. Under the log it is dark and damp.
> A tiny beetle lives there. That damp place under the log is a
> **microhabitat** — a very small habitat inside a bigger one. The
> garden is the big habitat. The place under the log is a small habitat
> inside it."

Key idea to say plainly: **A habitat is a home that gives food, water,
shelter, and space. A microhabitat is a tiny habitat inside a big one.**

**Original reading passage (adult reads aloud; learner retells):**

> "Milo lifts a log in the garden. Under the log it is dark and damp.
> A beetle hurries away. A worm curls in the wet soil. Milo puts the
> log back down, gently, the way it was. 'This is your home,' he
> whispers. The garden gives the beetle food, water, shelter, and
> space. The dark, damp place under the log gives it even more shelter.
> Milo does not take the beetle home. A habitat is a home — you visit
> it, you do not carry it away."

## Guided practice (adult leads, learner decides)

Adult reads each item; the learner answers orally, by pointing, or by
drawing. Adult notes are in parentheses.

1. "A robin's habitat is the yard. Name its food." *(Seeds, worms, or
   insects — any food a robin eats.)* "Name its shelter." *(A tree, a
   nest, a bush.)*
2. "A goldfish lives in a pond. What is its water?" *(The pond water
   itself — the fish lives in its water.)* "What is its space?" *(Room
   to swim around the pond.)*
3. "I see a plant growing in a crack in the sidewalk. Is the crack a
   habitat?" *(Yes, if it gives the plant soil, water, and light to
   grow — even a small, hard place can be a habitat.)*
4. "A desert gives a cactus very little water. Is the desert still the
   cactus's habitat?" *(Yes — the cactus needs only a little water,
   and the desert gives it sun and space. Different living things need
   different amounts.)*
5. Error-analysis: "Sam says, 'A habitat is just the place where an
   animal sleeps.' What is missing from Sam's idea?" *(A habitat also
   gives food, water, and space — not just a sleeping place. Sleeping
   is only the shelter part.)*

## Independent practice

The learner picks one living thing they know (a pet, a garden plant, a
bird they have seen) and draws its habitat with four labels or four
spoken labels: food, water, shelter, space. The adult scribes dictated
words.

## Application

Start the unit's "Habitat Words" page in the journal. The learner
writes or draws the four words — food, water, shelter, space — and one
example of each from a habitat near home. This page will be reused in
the project poster.

## Exit check

Adult asks: "What four things does a habitat give a living thing?"
Acceptable: food, water, shelter, space (any order; pointing to the
word cards counts). Then: "What is a microhabitat?" Acceptable: "A tiny
habitat inside a big one" / "The place under a log."

**Table variant:** The learner sorts 4 picture cards (a berry, a
puddle, a nest, an open field) onto the four word cards: food, water,
shelter, space.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** The four word cards stay on the table all unit; the
  adult scribes all labels.
- **Language:** Teach *habitat* with a place first (the yard, the
  garden), the word second; welcome home-language names for the four
  needs alongside English.
- **Extension:** Name a microhabitat the learner has actually seen (a
  puddle, a rock, a flowerpot) and list its four things; or write one
  sentence: "A ___ lives in ___. Its habitat gives it ___."

**Misconception note:** Learners often think a habitat is only a
shelter ("where it sleeps") or only a place on a map ("the desert").
Counter with the four-things check: "Does it give food? Water?
Shelter? Space? Then it is a habitat." See [teacher-guide.md](../teacher-guide.md) and
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) (Lesson 01).
