# Unit 05 — Biodiversity and Local Living Things

**Grade:** 2 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#15](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/15)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 19–22 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, 25–30 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

Some places are full of life and some hold only a few kinds of living
things — and a 7-year-old can find out why by looking carefully and
counting. This unit answers one question: *how many different kinds of
living things live in a habitat, and how do two habitats compare?*

The learner first learns what a **habitat** is: the place where a living
thing lives and finds the food, water, shelter, and space it needs.
Then the learner becomes a habitat detective: visiting one local habitat
(a garden bed, a patch of lawn, a pond edge), observing quietly, and
tallying the **different kinds** of plants and animals seen — counting
kinds, not how many of each kind. A second habitat gets the same fair
treatment. Then the learner compares the two tally charts, says which
habitat holds more kinds of living things, and backs the claim with the
counts. The unit ends with a pattern: places that offer more food,
water, shelter, and space usually hold more kinds of living things —
and plants give animals food and shelter, so a place with many kinds of
plants often holds many kinds of animals too.

This unit teaches exactly one performance expectation, **2-LS4-1**:
make observations of plants and animals to compare the diversity of
life in different habitats. Its assessment boundary is honored
throughout: **no specific animal or plant names in specific habitats
are assessed.** Learners use everyday names descriptively while
observing ("a yellow flower," "a small brown bird") and are never
asked to identify species. What counts is the comparison: more kinds
here, fewer kinds there, and the evidence for it.

**Safety and care are built into every lesson:** living things are
observed, never collected; plants are not picked; the learner stays on
paths, washes hands after outdoor work, and never touches an animal
they do not know. Every outdoor step has an indoor picture-based
alternative using the unit's generated image.

## Prerequisites (re-taught here as needed)

- Asking a question that can be tested, and running a **fair test**
  (change one thing, keep the rest the same) — Unit 01. Here the
  fairness rule is: count both habitats the same way, for the same
  amount of time, and write down what you actually saw.
- Measuring length in centimeters and recording observations in
  labeled drawings, tallies, and simple tables; stating a claim with
  evidence ("My evidence is ___ , so my claim is ___") — Unit 01.
- What plants need to live and grow (sunlight, water, soil) and how
  animals help plants (seed dispersal, pollination) — Unit 04. The
  bean plants from Unit 04 keep growing through this unit; the learner
  checks them once a week as a background thread.
- Science safety practices: keep materials away from the mouth, handle
  living things gently, never look directly at the sun, and name the
  safe alternative when an investigation is unsafe to touch.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Define **habitat** in their own words and name the four things a
   habitat gives living things: food, water, shelter, and space.
2. Observe one local habitat and record the **different kinds** of
   plants and animals seen in a tally chart, counting kinds rather
   than how many of each kind.
3. Observe a second local habitat with the same rules and record its
   kinds in a matching tally chart.
4. Compare two habitats' tally charts and state which habitat holds
   more kinds of living things, giving the counts as evidence
   (2-LS4-1).
5. Describe a pattern in the comparison — for example, that a habitat
   with more kinds of plants often holds more kinds of animals, or
   that water, shade, and shelter help a place hold more kinds — and
   explain it with observations from the two habitats.

## Essential question

*How many different kinds of living things live in a habitat — and how
can counting kinds help me compare two habitats fairly?*

## Vocabulary

**Habitat** — the place where a living thing lives and finds what it
needs. · **Living thing** — a plant or an animal (anything that grows,
needs food and water, and can make more of its kind). · **Kind** — one
type of living thing (all the dandelions are one kind; a dandelion and
a daisy are two kinds). · **Biodiversity** — how many different kinds
of living things live in a place (a big word for "lots of different
kinds"). · **Tally** — a mark made each time you see one more of
something, used to count. · **Tally chart** — a table with a tally
mark for each kind you see. · **Observe** — to look, listen, and
notice carefully. · **Compare** — to look at two things side by side
and say how they are the same and how they are different. · **Shelter**
— a safe place to hide, rest, or stay out of bad weather. · **Space**
— room to move, grow, and live. · **Microhabitat** — a very small
habitat inside a bigger one, like the damp place under a log. ·
**Pattern** — something that happens again and again in the same way.
· **Evidence** — observations or data used to support an idea. ·
**Claim** — what you say is true because of your evidence.

## Standards and source notes

- Reference framework: Next Generation Science Standards, grade-2
  performance expectation **2-LS4-1**. Code and description below were
  verified 2026-11-05 against the California Department of Education
  NGSS content-standards page for 2-LS4-1 and the Oklahoma Science
  Framework "Second Grade Diversity in Habitats" page, which reproduce
  the official text; the track audit's crosswalk (2026-10-03) carries
  the same code and clarification statement. No state adoption,
  accreditation, or alignment certification is claimed.
- **2-LS4-1** — Make observations of plants and animals to compare
  the diversity of life in different habitats. Clarification
  statement: emphasis is on the diversity of living things in each of
  a variety of different habitats. Assessment boundary: assessment does
  not include specific animal and plant names in specific habitats.
  Disciplinary core idea LS4.D: there are many different kinds of
  living things in any area, and they exist in different places on land
  and in water. Lessons 02–05 teach exactly this expectation — no
  species identification is taught or assessed.
- Science and engineering practice used throughout: **SEP-3**
  (planning and carrying out investigations — firsthand and
  picture-based observation with tally-chart data collection). The
  crosscutting concept is **patterns**: the learner looks for what
  repeats across the two habitats (more food, water, shelter, and
  space goes with more kinds).
- Original reading passages in this unit were written for this unit.
  No copyrighted text is reproduced. The generated illustration is an
  AI-made practice picture, not a photograph of a real place, and is
  labeled as such wherever it appears. Tally counts for the picture
  activity were counted from the inspected image itself (see
  [assets/README.md](assets/README.md)); practice tally examples
  elsewhere are labeled fictional practice data.

## 16-session pacing

Adapt frequency and duration for the learner; the success criterion for
each session is named. K–2 tasks may be oral, pointing, drawing,
manipulative, or adult-scribed; explicit adult directions are built into
every lesson. The Unit 04 bean plants are checked once a week (Session
2 and Session 10) as a background thread — measure, draw, record.
