# Practice — Unit 05

**For the learner.** The adult reads each task aloud; the learner
answers orally, by pointing, by drawing, or in short writing, and the
adult scribes dictated words. Answers are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md) — keep it separate from this page.

**Tally rule for every task:** count **kinds**, not how many. One row
per kind. Three carrots are one kind.

## Task 1 — The four things (Lesson 01)

Match each picture word to what it gives. Say or draw one example for
each.

- FOOD — a berry a bird eats
- WATER — a puddle a robin drinks from
- SHELTER — a nest in a tree
- SPACE — an open field to fly across

Now answer: a snail lives under a damp leaf. Name one thing the leaf
gives the snail for each of the four words.

## Task 2 — Kinds, not how many (Lesson 02)

A learner sees these living things in a garden bed:

- 6 ants, 1 robin, 2 dandelions, 1 daisy, 4 carrots

a) How many kinds of animals did the learner see?
b) How many kinds of plants did the learner see?
c) How many kinds of living things in all?

## Task 3 — Count the garden picture (Lessons 02, 04)

Look at the left side of the picture in
[lessons/lesson-04-compare-two-habitats.md](lessons/lesson-04-compare-two-habitats.md)
(the garden bed) or in

a) List the plant kinds you can see. How many plant kinds?
b) List the animal kinds you can see. How many animal kinds?
c) How many kinds in all?

## Task 4 — Count the lawn picture (Lessons 03, 04)

Look at the right side of the same picture (the lawn).

a) List the plant kinds you can see. How many plant kinds?
b) List the animal kinds you can see. How many animal kinds?
c) How many kinds in all?
d) Which pictured habitat holds more kinds — the garden or the lawn?
   What is your evidence?

## Task 5 — Compare two practice charts (Lessons 03–04)

These two tally charts are **fictional practice data** — made-up
numbers for counting practice, not real observations.

**Meadow chart:** grass, clover, daisy, buttercup, bee, butterfly,
ant, sparrow, rabbit — 9 kinds.
**Parking-lot edge chart:** grass, ant — 2 kinds.

a) Which habitat holds more kinds?
b) Finish the sentence: "My claim is ___. My evidence is ___."
c) Why might the meadow hold more kinds? Name one of the four things
   (food, water, shelter, space) that the meadow gives more of.

## Task 6 — Pattern and care (Lesson 05)

a) Finish the sentence: "A habitat with many kinds of plants often
   holds many kinds of animals because the plants give animals
   ___ and ___."
b) Sam says, "The garden holds more kinds, so the lawn is a bad
   habitat." Is Sam right? Why or why not?
c) Name one way to care for a habitat while you observe it.
