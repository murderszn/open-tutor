## Lesson guided practice (solutions)

**L01:** (1) Food: seeds, worms, or insects; shelter: a tree, a nest,
or a bush. (2) Water: the pond water itself; space: room to swim
around the pond. (3) Yes — if the crack gives the plant soil, water,
and light to grow, it is a habitat, even a small hard one. (4) Yes —
the cactus needs only a little water, and the desert gives it sun and
space; different living things need different amounts. (5) Sam's idea
is missing food, water, and space — sleeping is only the shelter part.

**L02:** (1) 2 kinds — ant and robin (5 ants are one kind). (2) 3
kinds — dandelion, daisy, clover. (3) Garden picture: plants —
sunflower, tomato, carrot, bean = 4 kinds; animals — bee, ladybug,
sparrow, earthworm = 4 kinds; total 8 kinds. (4) No new row — it is
the same kind, already counted; seeing it twice does not make two
kinds. (5) Sam split one kind into three rows: a carrot, its orange
root, and its green tops are the same kind — one row.

**L03:** (1) Yes — same time, same rules. (2) No — different times of
day change what can be seen; count both at about the same time of
day. (3) Lawn picture: plants — grass, dandelion = 2 kinds; animals —
ant, robin = 2 kinds; total 4 kinds. (4) Habitat A — 8 is more than 4;
the evidence is the two counts. (5) Two people looking find more
kinds than one person; both habitats need the same number of lookers,
or the comparison is not fair.

**L04:** (1) Garden picture: 4 plant kinds (sunflower, tomato,
carrot, bean); 4 animal kinds (bee, ladybug, sparrow, earthworm).
(2) Lawn picture: 4 kinds in all (grass, dandelion, ant, robin).
(3) The garden — 8 kinds against the lawn's 4 kinds. (4) Neither —
they hold the same number of kinds; "the same" is a real answer.
(5) "Pretty" is not a count; the evidence must be the numbers, 8
kinds and 4 kinds.

**L05:** (1) More kinds of plants goes with more kinds of animals —
the plants give food and shelter. (2) The pond edge — it gives more
of the four things (water, shelter, space). (3) No — the kinds that
live in the desert need very little water, and the desert gives them
what they need; "more kinds" is not "better." (4) Food and shelter —
for example, flowers feed bees and leaves hide ladybugs. (5) Sam
turned a pattern from two habitats into an always-rule; a pattern is
"usually," not "always" — and a lawn is a habitat too.

## Practice (solutions)

**Task 1:** Snail under a damp leaf — food: dead leaves or tiny bits
of plant; water: the dampness of the leaf and soil; shelter: the leaf
itself, hiding the snail; space: room under the leaf to move. Any
reasonable example per word counts.
**Task 2:** (a) 2 kinds of animals (ant, robin). (b) 3 kinds of plants
(dandelion, daisy, carrot). (c) 5 kinds in all (2 + 3 = 5).
**Task 3:** (a) Plants: sunflower, tomato, carrot, bean — 4 kinds.
(b) Animals: bee, ladybug, sparrow, earthworm — 4 kinds. (c) 8 kinds
in all.
**Task 4:** (a) Plants: grass, dandelion — 2 kinds. (b) Animals: ant,
robin — 2 kinds. (c) 4 kinds in all. (d) The garden holds more kinds;
evidence: 8 kinds in the garden, 4 in the lawn.
**Task 5:** (a) The meadow holds more kinds. (b) "My claim is the
meadow holds more kinds. My evidence is I counted 9 kinds in the
meadow and 2 kinds at the parking-lot edge." (c) The meadow gives more
of any one of the four things — for example, more food (many plants)
or more shelter (tall grass to hide in).
**Task 6:** (a) Food and shelter. (b) No — more kinds is not better;
the lawn is a habitat too and gives what its living things need.
(c) Any one: observe only; do not pick plants; do not chase or touch
animals; put logs and rocks back the way they were; wash hands after.

## Unit assessment (solutions)

1. A habitat is the place where a living thing lives and finds what it
   needs; the four things are food, water, shelter, and space.
2. Yes — the cactus needs very little water, and the desert gives it
   the sun and space it needs; a habitat is judged by what its living
   things need, not by how much water it holds.
3. 4 kinds of plants: sunflower, tomato, carrot, bean.
4. 2 kinds of animals: ant, robin.
5. Garden: 8 kinds in all (4 plants + 4 animals). Lawn: 4 kinds in
   all (2 plants + 2 animals).
6. The garden holds more kinds. Acceptable sentence: "My claim is the
   garden holds more kinds. My evidence is I counted 8 kinds in the
   garden and 4 kinds in the lawn."
7. Not fair — Maya looked at the lawn for 30 minutes but the garden
   for only 10 minutes, so the lawn had more time to show its kinds.
   A fair comparison needs the same time in both habitats.
8. Food and shelter.
9. Any one: observe only; do not pick plants; do not chase or touch
   animals; put logs and rocks back; wash hands after outdoor work.
10. (a) Plants: reeds, lily pads = 2 kinds. Animals: duck, frog,
    dragonfly = 3 kinds (two ducks are one kind). Total: 5 kinds.
    (b) The pond edge holds fewer kinds than the garden picture;
    evidence: 5 kinds at the pond edge, 8 kinds in the garden.

## Project (scoring guidance)

Use the rubric in [project.md](project.md). Watch for these common
errors when scoring: tally charts that count individuals (5 ants as 5
kinds) lose the tally-accuracy row; a claim with only one count, or
with feelings instead of counts, loses the comparison-claim row; a
pattern sentence that says "gardens are better" loses the pattern row
(it is an always-rule, not a pattern). A poster built from the
generated picture instead of a real survey is acceptable when the
adult notes it on the poster (indoor alternative) — score the counting
and comparing, not the setting.
