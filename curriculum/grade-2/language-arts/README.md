# Grade 2 Language Arts

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are delivered;
Unit 01 is delivered as a validated draft and Units 02–08 are planned, not yet written. See [scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained for
one grade-2 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 2 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-2 language arts course organized around
vowel-team phonics and word parts, fluent reading with comprehension
monitoring, story structure and characters' perspectives, informational
reading and text features, grammar and sentence expansion, narrative writing,
opinion and explanatory writing with evidence, and shared research, poetry,
speaking, and portfolio revision. Sessions are 25–30 minutes, about four per
week, and built on explicit phonics teaching, repeated oral reading,
read-aloud discussion, word work with tiles and cards, and guided writing the
adult reviews the same day.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Decode grade-level words: distinguish long and short vowels when reading
   regularly spelled one-syllable words; read additional common vowel teams;
   decode regularly spelled two-syllable words with long vowels; decode words
   with common prefixes and suffixes; identify words with inconsistent but
   common spelling-sound correspondences; read grade-appropriate irregularly
   spelled words.
2. Read grade-level text with purpose and understanding — orally with
   accuracy, appropriate rate, and expression on successive readings — and use
   context to confirm or self-correct word recognition.
3. In stories: ask and answer who/what/where/when/why/how questions about key
   details; recount stories (including fables and folktales from diverse
   cultures) and determine the central message, lesson, or moral; describe how
   characters respond to major events and challenges; describe how words and
   phrases supply rhythm and meaning; describe the overall story structure
   (beginning introduces, ending concludes); acknowledge differences in
   characters' points of view; use illustrations and words to demonstrate
   understanding of characters, setting, or plot; compare and contrast two or
   more versions of the same story.
4. In informational text: ask and answer who/what/where/when/why/how
   questions about key details; identify the main topic of a multiparagraph
   text and the focus of specific paragraphs; describe connections between
   historical events, scientific ideas, or steps in technical procedures;
   determine word meaning relevant to grade-2 topics; use text features
   (captions, bold print, subheadings, glossaries, indexes, electronic menus,
   icons) to locate key facts efficiently; identify the main purpose of a
   text (what the author wants to answer, explain, or describe); explain how
   specific images contribute to and clarify a text; describe how reasons
   support specific points; compare and contrast the most important points
   presented by two texts on the same topic.
5. Write opinion pieces (topic or book, an opinion, reasons that support it,
   linking words such as *because*, *and*, *also*, a concluding statement or
   section), informative/explanatory texts (a topic, facts and definitions, a
   concluding statement or section), and narratives (a well-elaborated event
   or short sequence of events with details of actions, thoughts, and
   feelings, temporal words to signal event order, a sense of closure);
   strengthen writing by revising and editing with adult and peer guidance;
   use digital tools with adult guidance; take part in shared research and
   writing projects; recall information from experiences or gather information
   from provided sources to answer a question.
6. Join discussions: follow discussion rules (gain the floor in respectful
   ways, listen with care, speak one at a time), link comments to others'
   remarks, ask for clarification as needed; recount or describe key ideas
   from read-alouds and other media; ask and answer questions about what a
   speaker says to clarify comprehension; tell a story or recount an
   experience with appropriate facts and descriptive details, speaking audibly
   in coherent sentences; create audio recordings of stories or poems and add
   drawings or visual displays; produce complete sentences to give requested
   detail or clarification.
7. Use grade-2 language conventions: collective nouns; frequently occurring
   irregular plural nouns; reflexive pronouns; past tense of frequently
   occurring irregular verbs; adjectives and adverbs chosen for what is
   modified; complete simple and compound sentences produced, expanded, and
   rearranged. Capitalize holidays, product names, and geographic names; use
   commas in greetings and closings of letters; use apostrophes for
   contractions and frequently occurring possessives; generalize learned
   spelling patterns; consult beginning dictionaries. Compare formal and
   informal uses of English.
8. Clarify word meanings with sentence-level context, known prefixes on known
   words (*happy/unhappy*, *tell/retell*), known root words (*addition*,
   *additional*), compound-word parts (*birdhouse*, *lighthouse*), and
   glossaries and beginning dictionaries; describe real-life connections
   between words and their use; distinguish shades of meaning among closely
   related verbs (*toss*, *throw*, *hurl*) and adjectives (*thin*, *slender*,
   *skinny*, *scrawny*); use adjectives and adverbs acquired through
   conversation, reading, and being read to in order to describe.

## Standards reference

Objectives reference the Common Core State Standards for English Language Arts,
grade 2 strands (verified against the official framework 2026-10-03; not a claim
of state adoption or accreditation). Full crosswalk in
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md#4-standards-crosswalk):

- Reading: Foundational Skills: RF.2.3a–f, RF.2.4a–c
- Reading: Literature: RL.2.1–7, RL.2.9–10 (RL.2.8 is not applicable to literature)
- Reading: Informational Text: RI.2.1–10
- Writing: W.2.1–3, W.2.5–8 (W.2.4, W.2.9–10 begin in grades 3–4)
- Speaking and Listening: SL.2.1a–c, SL.2.2–6
- Language: L.2.1a–f, L.2.2a–e, L.2.3a, L.2.4a–e, L.2.5a–b, L.2.6

## For the guiding adult

Grade-2 language arts shifts from "learning to read" to reading with stamina
and purpose, and the diagnostic weeks (Weeks 1–2) exist precisely so nothing is
assumed. Read the scope-and-sequence before starting; it lists safe household
materials, accessibility supports, and the diagnostic, catch-up, and review
weeks that keep pacing humane. Sessions run 25–30 minutes, four per week, with
short independent written practice (6–8 tasks) that the adult reviews the same
day. All reading passages in future units will be original or lawful
public-domain texts, and every unit will include its own teacher guide and
answer key.
