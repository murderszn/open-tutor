# Answer Key — Unit 05

**For the guiding adult only.** Never show this key to the learner, and
never place it beside a student question. Every answer below was solved
independently from the unit texts and reconciled with the lesson,
practice, quiz, and assessment items.

## Lesson 01 — Collective nouns

**Guided:** 1. A flock. 2. A herd. 3. A team — "The team won the game."
4. A swarm. 5. No — birds fly in a *flock*; a herd is cows, deer, or
goats. The group word must match the members.

**Independent:** 1. school. 2. class. 3. pack. 4. crowd. 5. Accept any
complete sentence using *family* correctly (e.g., "My family eats
dinner together."). 6. swarm.

## Lesson 02 — Irregular plurals and reflexive pronouns

**Guided:** 1. teeth. 2. mice; feet. 3. children; fish (stays the
same). 4. myself. 5. No — one mouse, two **mice**; *mouse* is on the
special list, so no *-s*.

**Independent:** 1. men. 2. geese. 3. sheep. 4. himself. 5. ourselves.
6. Accept any two correct sentences, e.g., "The children played in
the park." and "I poured my juice myself."

## Lesson 03 — Adjectives and adverbs

**Guided:** 1. *Quick* — it tells about the dog, a thing. 2. *Quickly*
— it tells about *runs*, an action. 3. *Slow* — it tells about the
turtle. 4. *Slowly* — it tells about *crawls*. 5. No — *runs* is an
action, so it needs the adverb: "The dog runs quickly."

**Independent:** 1. red. 2. high (an adverb that does not end in -ly).
3. gently. 4. small. 5. happily. 6. Accept any two correct sentences,
e.g., adjective: "The red slide is big." Adverb: "The dog runs
quickly." Each describing word must tell about the right kind of word.

## Lesson 04 — Irregular past tense and expanding sentences

**Guided:** 1. ran — "Yesterday, Sam ran to the park." 2. told. 3.
Accept any where added, e.g., "The dog barked in the park." 4. "It
rained, so we stayed inside." (comma before *so*.) 5. No — *go* is on
the special list: "Yesterday I **went** to the park."

**Independent:** 1. ate. 2. saw. 3. wrote. 4. Accept any where and
when added, e.g., "The turtle crawled on the stone path this morning."
5. "Mia read a book, and Ben drew a picture." (comma before *and*.)
6. "At noon, we ate lunch in the kitchen."

## Lesson 05 — Capitals and commas in letters

**Guided:** 1. "We swam in Lake Michigan." 2. "I want LEGO blocks for
Christmas." 3. "Dear Mr. Chen," 4. "Love," (comma after *Love*), then
Ana on the next line. 5. Two fixes: *Dear* needs a capital (first word
of the greeting) and *Crayola* is a product name: "Dear Ana, thank you
for the Crayola markers."

**Independent:** 1. Halloween. 2. Texas. 3. Nike. 4. "Dear Ms. Patel,"
5. Accept "Your friend," or "Love," — either closing with its comma.
6. "Dear Sam, Happy Fourth of July! Love, Ana"

## Lesson 06 — Apostrophes: contractions and possessives

**Guided:** 1. she's. 2. we'll. 3. dog's — "The dog's bone." 4. girls'
— plural ending in *-s*, so just the apostrophe after the *-s*. 5. No —
here it means "it is raining," so it needs the apostrophe: "**It's**
raining." ("The dog wagged **its** tail" is right — *its* shows
belonging, with no apostrophe.)

**Independent:** 1. I'm. 2. can't. 3. they're. 4. Sam's. 5. children's.
6. **It's** a sunny day, and the dog wagged **its** tail.

## Practice A — Group words

1. birds → flock; cows → herd; players → team; bees → swarm.
2. swarm. 3. herd. 4. Accept any complete sentence using *class*
   correctly (e.g., "Our class read a story."). 5. No — "A **flock**
   of birds flew by." (A pack is wolves or dogs.) 6. school.

## Practice B — Plural and self words

1. feet; teeth. 2. women; people. 3. deer; sheep. 4. myself. 5. itself.
6. No — "Two **children** played on the slide."

## Practice C — Describing words

1. quick. 2. small. 3. slowly. 4. happily. 5. gently. 6. Accept any
   two correct sentences, one with an adjective and one with an adverb,
   e.g., "The red slide is big." and "The birds fly high." Each
   describing word must tell about the right kind of word.

## Practice D — Yesterday words and bigger sentences

1. "Yesterday, he sat on the grass." 2. "She caught the ball."
3. "They swam in the pool." 4. Accept any where and when added, e.g.,
   "Ben read in the park yesterday." 5. "It was cold, but we played
   outside." (comma before *but*.) 6. "In art class, Ana drew a bird."

## Practice E — Spelling patterns, dictionary, formal and informal

1. **bridge** — it spells the /j/ sound -dge after a short vowel, like
   *badge*. *Boy* has no /j/ sound; *cage* spells the sound -ge.
2. **coin** — it spells the sound -oi- inside the word, like *boil*.
   *Cage* has no oi sound; *boy* spells the sound -oy at the end.
3. Accept the dictionary's wording in the learner's own words, e.g.,
   "a small piece of metal or cloth that shows who someone is or what
   group they belong to." Any meaning matching the entry is correct.
4. Formal — a polite greeting with a name, for a teacher.
5. Informal — "Hey" and "gimme" are relaxed words for friends.
6. Accept any polite full-word version, e.g., "May I please have some
   juice?"

## Unit assessment (Week 22, Session 16)

**Task A — Group words and special plurals:** 1. herd. 2. team.
3. feet. 4. teeth. 5. mice. 6. sheep. Pass: 5 of 6.

**Task B — Self words and yesterday words:** 1. myself. 2. themselves.
3. sat. 4. told. 5. swam. 6. wrote. Pass: 5 of 6.

**Task C — Describing words from the picture:** 1. quick. 2. quickly.
3. slow. 4. small. 5–6. Accept any correct sentences, e.g., adjective:
"The red slide is big." Adverb: "The turtle crawls slowly." Pass: 4
of 6.

**Task D — Bigger sentences:** 1. Accept any where and when added,
e.g., "The cat slept on the rug last night." 2. "Mia read a book, and
Ben drew a picture." 3. "It rained, so we stayed inside." 4. "At noon,
we ate lunch in the kitchen." (Accept "In the kitchen, we ate lunch at
noon.") Pass: 3 of 4. Deduct for a missing comma before *and*/*so*;
count the item wrong only if the comma is missing — a missing comma
and a correct join is one error, not two.

**Task E — Capitals, commas, apostrophes:** 1. "My birthday is on
Halloween." 2. "We drove to Texas for the summer." 3. "Dear Ms.
Patel," 4. Accept "Your friend," or "Love," — either closing with its
comma. 5. can't. 6. children's. Pass: 5 of 6.

**Task F — Spelling patterns, dictionary, formal/informal, fluency:**
1. bridge (same reason as Practice E, item 1). 2. coin (same reason as
Practice E, item 2). 3. Accept the dictionary's wording in the
learner's own words, e.g., "to heat a liquid until it bubbles" or
"bubbles rise in hot water." 4. Formal — polite, full words; use it
with teachers and people you do not know well. 5. Fluency reread:
observed, not scored — the learner reads "The Park on Saturday"
accurately on a second reading, self-correcting with context if
needed. Pass: 3 of 4 on items 1–4, plus the reread observed.

## Project — A Letter to Someone Special

Score with the rubric in [project.md](project.md). Acceptable
variation: any real person the learner knows; any news worth writing
about; any drawing that matches the letter. The six sentence jobs may
appear in any order. A contraction that is also a possessive error
("its" for "it's") counts under Conventions, not under Grammar words.
A letter that meets the checklist but has one convention error is a 2
on Conventions, not a 1.
