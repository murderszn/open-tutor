# Unit 05 — Grammar, Sentence Expansion, and Conventions

**Grade:** 2 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 19–22
**Backlog:** [issue #16](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/16), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Words have jobs. A group of birds is a *flock*, not "a lot of birds." One
*foot* becomes two *feet* — never "foots." *Myself* points back to the person
doing the action. Yesterday's verbs change their shape: *sat*, *hid*,
*told*, *ran*. A *quick* dog runs *quickly* — the describing word changes
depending on whether it tells about a thing or an action. Short sentences
grow when the learner adds where, when, and how, and two short sentences
can join into one strong compound sentence. Then the rules get written
down in a real letter: capitals for holidays, products, and places; commas
in greetings and closings; apostrophes for contractions and possessives.
Every text in this unit is original and written for grade 2, except for
the linked outside references in the Resource Pack, which are never
reproduced here.

## Essential question

How do I make my sentences say exactly what I mean — and write them
down so a reader can follow them?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks and Units 01–04:

- Read grade-level text orally with accuracy and expression on successive
  readings; use context to confirm or self-correct a word (RF.2.4)
- Ask and answer who/what/where/when/why/how questions about key details
  and point to the sentence that answers each question (RL.2.1, RI.2.1)
- Retell a short text in order; identify a main topic and paragraph focus
  (RI.2.2)
- Read and spell common grade-2 words, including vowel teams, two-syllable
  words, and words with prefixes and suffixes (RF.2.3)
- Write complete sentences in earlier practice and project work; use
  sentence context for a new word (L.2.4a)

If a learner cannot yet produce a complete simple sentence (a who/what
plus a what-it-does), re-teach that with Lesson 04's sentence frames
before assessing expansion or compounding.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Use collective nouns — *team, class, flock, herd, family, crowd, pack,
   swarm, school* — to name a whole group as one thing (L.2.1a).
2. Form and use frequently occurring irregular plural nouns (*feet, teeth,
   children, mice, fish, sheep, men, women, people, geese*) and reflexive
   pronouns (*myself, ourselves, himself, herself, itself, themselves*)
   in speaking and writing (L.2.1b–c).
3. Form and use the past tense of frequently occurring irregular verbs
   (*sat, hid, told, ran, ate, saw, went, took, swam, wrote, sang,
   caught, gave, drew*) (L.2.1d).
4. Choose adjectives and adverbs by what they modify — adjectives tell
   about nouns, adverbs tell about verbs — and use both to describe the
   park picture and in own sentences (L.2.1e).
5. Produce, expand, and rearrange complete simple and compound sentences:
   add who/what/where/when/how details, join two simple sentences with
   *and*, *but*, or *so* (with a comma before the joining word), and move
   a where/when phrase to the front of a sentence (L.2.1f; SL.2.6).
6. Apply grade-2 conventions in a finished letter: capitalize holidays,
   product names, and geographic names; use commas in greetings and
   closings of letters; use apostrophes for contractions and frequently
   occurring possessives; generalize learned spelling patterns
   (*cage → badge*; *boy → boil*); consult a beginning dictionary to
   check a spelling; compare formal and informal uses of English
   (L.2.2a–e; L.2.3a).

## The unit's texts (all original; adult reads aloud first, learner re-reads)

- **"The Park on Saturday"** (Lesson 03, Practice C, Assessment Task C
  and F): the picture text — a quick brown dog, a slow green turtle,
  a girl in a red dress, a boy in a blue shirt, five small birds, a big
  red slide, a tall green tree, and yellow and purple flowers. The
  lesson's text-only alternative lists everything the picture shows, so
  the activities never depend on seeing the image.
- **"What Sam Did Yesterday"** (Lesson 04, Practice D): a short story
  told in irregular past-tense verbs — Sam sat, hid, ran, ate, saw,
  went, took, swam, and told.
- **"A Letter from Grandma Rosa"** (Lesson 05): a model friendly letter
  with a comma after the greeting and the closing, capitals for a
  holiday, a product, and a place, and expanded sentences throughout.
- **"Ana's Birthday"** (Lesson 06): short sentences built around
  contractions (*can't, don't, it's, we're*) and possessives (*Ana's
  cake, the children's gifts, the girls' game*).

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for English Language
Arts**, grade 2, Language and Speaking and Listening, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/)
(codes and descriptions re-checked against the official wording in
search results 2026-11-05; no state adoption or accreditation claimed).

| Unit objective | Standard | Official description (grade 2) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | L.2.1a | Use collective nouns (e.g., group). |
| 2 | L.2.1b; L.2.1c | Form and use frequently occurring irregular plural nouns (e.g., feet, children, teeth, mice, fish). / Use reflexive pronouns (e.g., myself, ourselves). |
| 3 | L.2.1d | Form and use the past tense of frequently occurring irregular verbs (e.g., sat, hid, told). |
| 4 | L.2.1e | Use adjectives and adverbs, and choose between them depending on what is to be modified. |
| 5 | L.2.1f; SL.2.6 | Produce, expand, and rearrange complete simple and compound sentences (e.g., The boy watched the movie; The little boy watched the movie; The action movie was watched by the little boy). / Produce complete sentences when appropriate to task and situation in order to provide requested detail or clarification. |
| 6 | L.2.2a | Capitalize holidays, product names, and geographic names. |
| 6 | L.2.2b | Use commas in greetings and closings of letters. |
| 6 | L.2.2c | Use an apostrophe to form contractions and frequently occurring possessives. |
| 6 | L.2.2d | Generalize learned spelling patterns when writing words (e.g., cage → badge; boy → boil). |
| 6 | L.2.2e | Consult reference materials, including beginning dictionaries, as needed to check and correct spellings. |
| 6 | L.2.3a | Compare formal and informal uses of English. |
| Speaking | SL.2.1; SL.2.4 | Participate in collaborative conversations about grade 2 topics and texts; build on others' talk and ask for clarification. / Tell a story or recount an experience with appropriate facts and relevant, descriptive details, speaking audibly in coherent sentences. |

Teacher note on L.2.1f: the official example includes a passive sentence
("The action movie was watched by the little boy"). The passive is adult
reference only in this unit; learners rearrange sentences by moving a
where/when phrase to the front, which is the grade-2 rearrangement taught
here. The spelling-pattern examples (*cage → badge*; *boy → boil*) are the
standard's own examples and are taught as patterns to generalize, not as
rules about every word.

## 16-session pacing

Four sessions per week, 25–30 minutes each. K–2 tasks stay oral,
pointing, drawing, manipulative, or adult-scribed as needed; grade 2
adds short independent written practice (6 tasks) that the adult
reviews the same day. The unit opens with a fluency reread of a Unit 02
passage (retrieval warm-up) and ends with a fluency reread inside the
unit assessment (Task F).

| Session | Week | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 19 | Lesson 01: Collective nouns | Names the group word for 4 groups; uses 2 in own sentences |
| 2 | 19 | Practice A: group words | Completes 6 tasks; each group word matches its members |
| 3 | 19 | Lesson 02: Irregular plurals and reflexive pronouns | Gives correct plurals for 6 words; uses myself/ourselves correctly |
| 4 | 19 | Practice B: plural and self words | Completes 6 tasks |

| 6 | 20 | Practice C: describing words | Completes 6 tasks from the picture + text alternative |
| 7 | 20 | Project work 1: plan the letter | Letter plan complete: who, why, 6 sentence jobs |
| 8 | 20 | Lesson 04: Irregular past tense and expanding sentences | Past forms for 6 verbs; expands 2 sentences; joins 2 sentences |
| 9 | 21 | Practice D: yesterday words and bigger sentences | Completes 6 tasks |
| 10 | 21 | Lesson 05: Capitals and commas in letters | Capitalizes 3 name kinds; punctuates greeting + closing |
| 11 | 21 | Project work 2: draft the letter | Draft has greeting, 4+ expanded sentences, closing |

## For the guiding adult

Read the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) before Session 1. It lists
materials (paper, crayons, word cards, a beginning dictionary — nothing
to buy), the adult-scribed and drawing options for every written task,
and what to do when a plural is regularized ("childs"), a reflexive
pronoun is swapped for a personal pronoun ("hurt me" for "hurt myself"
when the doer and the hurt person are the same), or an adjective is
used where an adverb belongs ("runs quick"). Never show the learner the
answer key: lesson teacher notes name it for the adult only, and no
answer ever appears beside a student question.
