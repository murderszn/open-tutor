# Lesson 01 — Collective Nouns: One Word for a Whole Group

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can name a whole group with one word — a
*flock* of birds, a *team* of players — and use that word in a sentence."
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** word cards (flock, herd, team,
class, swarm, pack, family, crowd, school) or a pencil and paper.
**Standards:** L.2.1a.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult asks: "What do you call a lot of birds flying together?" If
the learner says "birds," the adult says: "Yes — and there is one special
word that means a whole group of birds: a *flock*. Today we learn the
special group words." If the learner already says "a flock," ask for one
more group word they know.

## Explicit teaching

A **collective noun** is one word that names a whole group as if it were
one thing. The group word comes first, then *of*, then the members:

- a **flock** of birds — many birds, one word: *flock*
- a **herd** of cows — many cows, one word: *herd*
- a **team** of players — many players, one word: *team*
- a **class** of children — many children, one word: *class*
- a **swarm** of bees — many bees, one word: *swarm*
- a **pack** of wolves — many wolves, one word: *pack*
- a **school** of fish — many fish, one word: *school*
- a **crowd** of people — many people, one word: *crowd*
- a **family** — a group of people who live together and care for each other

Rule: the group word is the name of the group. The members after *of*
tell you who is in it. "A flock of birds" — *flock* is the group word,
*birds* are the members. Different groups have different group words:
birds fly in a flock, cows walk in a herd, bees fly in a swarm.

### Modeled example 1 — Birds

Adult thinks aloud: "I see ten birds in the sky. I do not have to say
'ten birds' every time. One word names the whole group: *flock*. 'A
flock of birds flew over the park.' *Flock* is the group word; *birds*
are the members. If I say 'a flock of cows,' that is wrong — cows are
a herd, not a flock. The group word has to match the members."

### Modeled example 2 — Our class

"The children in Room 2 learn together, read together, and play
together. One word names them as a group: *class*. 'Our class read a
story about beavers.' *Class* is the group word; the children are the
members. I can also say 'a team of players won the game' — *team* is
the group word for players."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. "What is the group word for birds?" (A flock.) "Say the whole
   phrase: 'a flock of birds.'"
2. "What is the group word for cows?" (A herd.) "Say it: 'a herd of
   cows.'"
3. "What is the group word for players?" (A team.) "Use it in a
   sentence: 'The team won the game.'"
4. "What is the group word for bees?" (A swarm.) "Say it: 'a swarm of
   bees.'"
5. **Error-analysis:** the adult says, "A herd of birds flew by."
   "Is that right?" (No — birds fly in a *flock*; a herd is cows, deer,
   or goats. The group word has to match the members.)

## Independent practice (adult-scribed or written; 6 tasks)

1. Fill in the group word: a ___ of fish. (school)
2. Fill in the group word: a ___ of children in Room 2. (class)
3. Fill in the group word: a ___ of wolves. (pack)
4. Fill in the group word: a ___ of people at the fair. (crowd)
5. Write or say a sentence using the group word *family*.
6. Draw or point to the picture the adult shows of bees flying
   together, and say the group word. (swarm; if no picture is handy,
   the adult says "bees flying together" and the learner answers.)

## Applied task

The learner names three groups they saw this week — at home, at school,
or outside — and gives the group word for each. (Examples: "a class of
children," "a team of players," "a family at the park.") The adult
writes the three group words on cards for the learner's word wall.

## Exit check

"Name the group word for a group of birds and a group of cows, and use
one of them in a sentence." Ready to move on: both group words named,
and one used in a full sentence.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading access:** the adult reads every group word aloud first; the
  learner repeats and points to the word card.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the right word card, draw the
  group, or dictate the answer for the adult to scribe.
- **Extension:** the learner sorts six new groups — goats, ducks,
  singers, ants, sheep, runners — into "I know the group word" and
  "I need to look it up," and the adult supplies the new words
  (herd, flock, choir, colony, flock, team).

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: any group word for any group ("a herd of birds").
Reteach with the match test: "Say the members first, then ask what
their special group word is." Some group words have more than one right
answer in real life (a group of fish can be a *school*; a group of
people can be a *crowd* or a *group*); accept any group word the learner
can defend with "the members are…". Answer-key reference:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md), Lesson 01.
