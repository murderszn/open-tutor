# Lesson 02 — Irregular Plurals and Reflexive Pronouns

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can say the special plural for words like
*child* and *foot*, and I can use words like *myself* when I do
something to my own self."
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** word cards (foot/feet,
tooth/teeth, child/children, mouse/mice, man/men, woman/women,
person/people, sheep, fish, deer, goose/geese) and self-word cards
(myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves,
themselves).
**Standards:** L.2.1b, L.2.1c.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult asks: "One dog, two…?" (dogs) "One cat, two…?" (cats) Then:
"One child, two…?" If the learner says "childs," the adult says: "That
is the tricky part — some words do not add *-s*. Today we learn the
special plurals you have to know by heart."

## Explicit teaching

**Part 1 — Irregular plurals.** Most plurals add *-s*: one dog, two
dogs. Some words change their shape instead, or stay the same. There
is no rule to work out — you learn these by heart:

- one foot → two **feet** · one tooth → two **teeth**
- one child → two **children** · one mouse → two **mice**
- one man → two **men** · one woman → two **women**
- one person → two **people** · one goose → two **geese**
- one sheep → three **sheep** · one fish → two **fish** · one deer → two **deer**

Rule: if the word is on the special list, do not add *-s*. "Childs,"
"foots," "mouses," and "sheeps" are not words.

**Part 2 — Reflexive pronouns.** A **reflexive pronoun** is a word that
ends in *-self* or *-selves* and points back to the person doing the
action — the doer and the one the action happens to are the same person:

- I → **myself** · you → **yourself** · he → **himself** · she → **herself**
- it → **itself** · we → **ourselves** · you (many) → **yourselves** ·
  they → **themselves**

"I hurt **myself**" means I hurt me — the same person did it and felt
it. "We built the fort **ourselves**" means we built it with no help.
Test: if you can swap in the person's name and it still points to the
same person, the *-self* word is right. "Ana hurt herself" — *herself*
is Ana.

### Modeled example 1 — Children, not "childs"

Adult thinks aloud: "One child plays on the slide. Two… I want to add
*-s* and say 'childs,' but *child* is on the special list. One child,
two **children**. The word changes its shape: *child* → *children*.
Same with foot: one foot, two **feet**. I say the pair out loud three
times so my ear learns it: child–children, foot–feet, tooth–teeth."

### Modeled example 2 — Herself points back

"Ana tied her shoe. Who tied it? Ana. Who wore the shoe? Ana again.
So I say: 'Ana tied her shoe **herself**.' *Herself* points back to
Ana — the doer and the wearer are the same person. If her friend tied
it, I would say 'her friend tied it for her,' not 'herself.'"

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. "One tooth, two…?" (teeth.) "Say the pair: tooth–teeth."
2. "One mouse, two…?" (mice.) "One foot, two…?" (feet.)
3. "One child, two…?" (children.) "One fish, two…?" (fish — it stays
   the same.)
4. "I made the card ___." (myself.) "Who made it? I did — so the word
   points back to me."
5. **Error-analysis:** the adult says, "The mouses ran across the
   floor." "Is that right?" (No — one mouse, two **mice**. *Mouse* is
   on the special list, so no *-s*.)

## Independent practice (adult-scribed or written; 6 tasks)

1. One man → two ___. (men)
2. One goose → two ___. (geese)
3. One sheep → three ___. (sheep)
4. "Sam taught ___ to whistle." (himself)
5. "Ana and I made lunch ___." (ourselves)
6. Write or say two sentences: one with the plural *children* and one
   with the word *myself*.

## Applied task

The learner tells the adult about something they did all by themselves
this week, using a *-self* word ("I poured my juice myself," "We
cleaned our room ourselves"), and names one plural from the special
list that came up in the story ("The children played outside").

## Exit check

"Give me the plurals of *foot*, *child*, and *sheep*, and finish this
sentence: 'I tied my shoes ___.'" Ready to move on: three plurals
correct and *myself* used correctly.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading access:** the adult reads each pair aloud; the learner
  repeats the pair three times and points to the word card.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the right card, draw one and
  many (one foot, two feet), or dictate for the adult to scribe.
- **Extension:** the learner adds two more pairs to the special list
  from books they read this week (for example, *woman → women*,
  *person → people*), and writes one sentence with *themselves*.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: regularizing every plural ("childs," "foots,"
"mouses") and using a personal pronoun where a reflexive belongs
("I hurt me" when the doer and the hurt person are the same — for
grade 2, accept "I hurt me" in speech but model "I hurt myself" and
ask the learner to repeat the *-self* form). Also watch for "theirselves"
— the correct form is *themselves*. Answer-key reference:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md), Lesson 02.
