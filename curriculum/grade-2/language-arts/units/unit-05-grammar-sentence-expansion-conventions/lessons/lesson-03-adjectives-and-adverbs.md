# Lesson 03 — Adjectives and Adverbs: Describing Things and Actions

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can tell if a describing word tells about
a thing or an action — and pick the right one."
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** the generated picture below
(or its text-only list); a pencil or pointer.
**Standards:** L.2.1e.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult asks: "What is the dog doing in the picture?" (Running.)
"Tell me one word about the dog." (Brown, quick, happy.) Then: "Today
we learn two kinds of describing words — one kind tells about things,
one kind tells about actions."

## Explicit teaching

Two kinds of describing words:

- An **adjective** tells about a **noun** — a person, place, or thing.
  It answers: what kind? which one? how many? *Quick* tells about the
  dog. *Red* tells about the slide. *Five* and *small* tell about the
  birds.
- An **adverb** tells about a **verb** — an action. It answers: how?
  when? where? *Quickly* tells about *runs*. *Slowly* tells about
  *crawls*. *High* tells about *jumps* and *fly*.

Test: ask "What word does it tell about?" If it tells about a thing,
it is an adjective. If it tells about an action, it is an adverb.

- Many adverbs end in **-ly**: quick → quickly, slow → slowly, happy →
  happily, gentle → gently. But not all adverbs end in -ly: *fast*,
  *high*, *well*, *hard* are adverbs too ("runs fast," "jumps high").
- The same idea changes shape: *quick* dog (adjective) runs *quickly*
  (adverb). *Slow* turtle (adjective) crawls *slowly* (adverb).

### Modeled example 1 — Quick dog, runs quickly

Adult thinks aloud: "Look at the dog. What kind of dog? *Quick* — that
tells about the dog, a thing, so *quick* is an adjective. Now what is
the dog doing? *Runs*. How does it run? *Quickly* — that tells about
*runs*, an action, so *quickly* is an adverb. One sentence: 'The quick
dog runs quickly.' *Quick* goes with the dog; *quickly* goes with
*runs*."

### Modeled example 2 — Slow turtle, crawls slowly

"Look at the turtle. What kind of turtle? *Slow* — adjective, it tells
about the turtle. How does it crawl? *Slowly* — adverb, it tells about
*crawls*. 'The slow turtle crawls slowly.' If I said 'the slowly
turtle,' that would be wrong — *slowly* cannot tell about a thing. If
I said 'the turtle crawls slow,' that would be wrong too — *crawls*
needs the adverb *slowly*."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

Use the picture or the text-only list above.

1. "The (quick / quickly) dog runs." Which word fits? (*Quick* —
   it tells about the dog, a thing.)
2. "The dog runs (quick / quickly)." Which word fits? (*Quickly* —
   it tells about *runs*, an action.)
3. "The (slow / slowly) turtle crawls." (*Slow* — it tells about the
   turtle.)
4. "The turtle crawls (slow / slowly)." (*Slowly* — it tells about
   *crawls*.)
5. **Error-analysis:** the adult says, "The dog runs quick." "Is that
   right?" (No — *runs* is an action, so it needs the adverb:
   "The dog runs quickly.")

## Independent practice (adult-scribed or written; 6 tasks)

Use the picture or the text-only list above.

1. "The (red / redly) slide is big." (red)
2. "The birds fly (high / highly)." (high — an adverb that does not
   end in -ly)
3. "The boy throws the ball (gentle / gently)." (gently)
4. "Five (small / smallly) birds fly." (small)
5. "The girl jumps (happy / happily)." (happily)
6. Write or say two sentences about the picture: one with an adjective
   and one with an adverb. Underline or point to the describing word
   in each.

## Applied task

The learner describes a pet, a family member, or a friend with two
adjectives ("my grandma is kind and funny") and one adverb sentence
("She sings softly"). The adult asks after each describing word: "Does
that tell about a thing or an action?"

## Exit check

"Tell me one adjective and one adverb from the picture, and tell me the
word each one tells about." Ready to move on: one of each named, with
the right word each tells about.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading access:** the adult reads the text-only list aloud first;
  the learner points to each thing in the picture (or the list) as it
  is named.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the picture part, draw the
  describing word's target, or dictate for the adult to scribe.
- **Extension:** the learner finds one more adjective–adverb pair in
  the picture ("the *tall* tree stands *still*" — *tall* tells about
  the tree, *still* tells about *stands*) and writes both sentences.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: using the adjective form for the adverb ("runs
quick," "crawls slow," "jumps happy"). Reteach with the test question:
"What word does it tell about — a thing or an action?" Also watch for
the belief that every adverb ends in -ly; *fast*, *high*, *well*, and
*hard* are the grade-2 counterexamples used here. Answer-key
