# Lesson 06 — Apostrophes: Contractions and Possessives

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can use an apostrophe to squeeze two
words into one — and to show that something belongs to someone."
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** word cards (I'm, you're,
he's, she's, it's, we're, they'll, can't, don't, isn't, didn't,
won't, couldn't); the sentences below; a pencil or pointer.
**Standards:** L.2.2c.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult asks: "What two words make *don't*?" (do not) "What letter
disappeared?" (The *o* in *not*.) "The little mark that sits where the
letter went is called an apostrophe. Today we use it two ways."

## The text — "Ana's Birthday" (original; adult reads aloud first, learner re-reads)

Ana's birthday is on Saturday. It's a sunny day, and we're all happy.
Ana can't wait to open the gifts. "Don't peek!" she says. The
children's gifts are on the table. The girls' game is ready, and Sam's
cake has seven candles. "I'll blow them out!" Ana says. The dog wagged
its tail all morning — it's excited, too.

## Explicit teaching

**Part 1 — Contractions.** A **contraction** squeezes two words into
one. The **apostrophe** (') sits exactly where letters were taken out:

- I am → **I'm** · you are → **you're** · he is → **he's** · she is → **she's**
- it is → **it's** · we are → **we're** · they will → **they'll**
- can not → **can't** · do not → **don't** · is not → **isn't**
- did not → **didn't** · could not → **couldn't** · I will → **I'll**
- will not → **won't** (a special one — it changes shape; learn it by
  heart)

Rule: read the contraction out loud as the two words. If "it's" means
"it is," the apostrophe is right.

**Part 2 — Possessives.** An apostrophe also shows that something
**belongs** to someone or something:

- **Mia's bike** — the bike belongs to Mia. **Sam's cake** — the cake
  belongs to Sam. **The dog's bone** — the bone belongs to the dog.
- **The children's gifts** — the gifts belong to the children. When
  the owner word is already plural and does not end in *-s*, add *'s*.
- **The girls' game** — the game belongs to the girls. When the owner
  word is plural and ends in *-s*, add just the apostrophe after the
  *-s*.
- **Its tail** — the tail belongs to *it*. *Its* is a possessive with
  **no apostrophe**. *It's* (with the apostrophe) always means "it
  is." Test: if you can swap in "it is," use *it's*; if not, use *its*.

### Modeled example 1 — Don't

Adult thinks aloud: "*Do not* becomes *don't*. The *o* from *not* is
taken out, and the apostrophe sits in its place: don**'**t. I can see
the missing letter's spot. *Can't* is the same: can + not → can't,
with the apostrophe where the letters went. *Won't* is the tricky one:
will + not → won't — it changes shape, so I learn it by heart."

### Modeled example 2 — Ana's cake

"'Ana's cake has seven candles.' Whose cake? Ana's. The *'s* shows
the cake belongs to Ana. Now: 'The girls' game is ready.' Whose game?
The girls'. *Girls* already ends in *-s* because there are many
girls, so I add just the apostrophe after the *-s*. And: 'The dog
wagged its tail.' Whose tail? The dog's — but *its* has no apostrophe
here, because it shows belonging. 'It's excited' does have one,
because it means 'it is excited.'"

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. "She + is → ?" (she's)
2. "We + will → ?" (we'll)
3. "Whose bone is it? The ___ bone." (dog's — "The dog's bone.")
4. "The ___ (girls) team won." Which form? (girls' — plural ending
   in *-s*, so just the apostrophe.)
5. **Error-analysis:** the adult shows: "Its raining." "Is that
   right?" (No — here it means "it is raining," so it needs the
   apostrophe: "**It's** raining." But "The dog wagged **its** tail"
   is right — *its* shows belonging, with no apostrophe.)

## Independent practice (adult-scribed or written; 6 tasks)

1. I + am → ? (I'm)
2. Can + not → ? (can't)
3. They + are → ? (they're)
4. "The ___ (Sam) lunchbox is red." (Sam's)
5. "The ___ (children) coats are on the hooks." (children's)
6. Choose the right word in each blank: "___ (Its / It's) a sunny
   day, and the dog wagged ___ (its / it's) tail." (It's; its)

## Applied task

The learner writes or dictates two sentences about their own day: one
with a contraction ("I'm going to the park") and one with a possessive
("My mom's car is blue"). The adult asks after each apostrophe: "Does
it stand for missing letters, or does it show belonging?"

## Exit check

"Make a contraction from *do not* and a possessive from *Mia*, and
tell me when to use *its* and when to use *it's*." Ready to move on:
don't and Mia's correct, and the its/it's rule stated.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading access:** the adult reads "Ana's Birthday" aloud first;
  the learner points to each apostrophe and says what it does.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the apostrophe's spot, draw
  the owner and the owned thing, or dictate for the adult to scribe.
- **Extension:** the learner finds one more possessive and one more
  contraction in a book they are reading, and sorts them into "missing
  letters" and "belonging."

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: putting the apostrophe in the wrong place
("do'nt," "ca'nt"), using *it's* for belonging ("it's tail"), and
adding *'s* to a plural that ends in *-s* ("the girls's game").
Reteach with the two questions: "What letters went missing?" and
"Who does it belong to?" For plurals ending in *-s*, the rule is
"apostrophe after the *-s*, nothing more." Answer-key reference:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md), Lesson 06.
