# Lesson 04 — Irregular Past Tense and Expanding Sentences

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can tell what happened yesterday with the
right verb — *sat*, *hid*, *told* — and I can make my sentences bigger
and stronger."
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** the story below; word cards
for the yesterday verbs; a pencil or pointer.
**Standards:** L.2.1d, L.2.1f.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult asks: "Today I *sit*. Yesterday I…?" (sat) "Today I *run*.
Yesterday I…?" (ran) Then: "Some yesterday words change their shape
instead of adding *-ed*. Today we learn those, and then we use them to
grow our sentences."

## The text — "What Sam Did Yesterday" (original; adult reads aloud first, learner re-reads)

Yesterday, Sam went to the park. He sat on the green grass and ate a
red apple. He saw a quick brown dog, and he ran fast to catch up with
it. The dog hid behind the big red slide. Sam took a yellow ball from
his bag and threw it. He swam in the small pool. At home, Sam wrote a
note and told his mom about the day. He sang a happy song before bed.

## Explicit teaching

**Part 1 — Irregular past tense.** Most past-tense verbs add *-ed*:
walk → walked, jump → jumped. Some verbs change their shape instead.
These are the yesterday words for this unit — learn them by heart:

- sit → **sat** · hide → **hid** · tell → **told** · run → **ran**
- eat → **ate** · see → **saw** · go → **went** · take → **took**
- swim → **swam** · write → **wrote** · sing → **sang** · catch → **caught**
- give → **gave** · draw → **drew** · drink → **drank**

Rule: "goed," "eated," "taked," and "runned" are not words. If the verb
is on the special list, use its changed shape. The words *yesterday*,
*last night*, and *last week* are clues that the past tense belongs.

**Part 2 — Expanding sentences.** A short sentence can grow by adding
details — who, what, where, when, how:

- "Sam played." → "Sam played soccer in the park yesterday."
  (added *what*, *where*, *when*)
- "Ana sang." → "Ana sang a happy song at the show last night."
  (added *what*, *where*, *when*)

Two short sentences can join into one **compound sentence** with
*and*, *but*, or *so* — and a comma goes before the joining word:

- "It rained. We stayed inside." → "It rained, so we stayed inside."
- "Ana sang a happy song, and the crowd clapped."

A sentence can also be **rearranged** by moving the where/when part to
the front:

- "We ate lunch at noon in the kitchen." → "At noon, we ate lunch in
  the kitchen." → "In the kitchen, we ate lunch at noon."

A complete sentence still needs a who/what and a what-it-does — growing
a sentence never means dropping either part.

### Modeled example 1 — Hid, not "hided"

Adult thinks aloud: "Today the dog *hides* behind the slide.
Yesterday it… I want to add *-ed* and say 'hided,' but *hide* is on
the special list. Yesterday the dog **hid** behind the slide. Same
with sit: today I sit, yesterday I **sat**. I say the pair out loud:
hide–hid, sit–sat, tell–told, run–ran."

### Modeled example 2 — Growing "Ana sang"

"'Ana sang.' That is a whole sentence, but it is small. What did she
sing? A happy song. Where? At the show. When? Last night. Now it is
bigger: 'Ana sang a happy song at the show last night.' And I can join
it to another sentence: 'Ana sang a happy song, and the crowd
clapped.' The comma goes before *and*."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. "Yesterday, Sam (run) to the park." Which word? (*Ran* — "Yesterday,
   Sam ran to the park.")
2. "Last night, Ana (tell) a story." (*Told*.)
3. Expand this sentence by adding where: "The dog barked." (Accept:
   "The dog barked in the park / at the house / behind the slide.")
4. Join these two sentences with *so*: "It rained. We stayed inside."
   ("It rained, so we stayed inside." — comma before *so*.)
5. **Error-analysis:** the adult says, "Yesterday I goed to the
   park." "Is that right?" (No — *go* is on the special list:
   "Yesterday I **went** to the park.")

## Independent practice (adult-scribed or written; 6 tasks)

1. Yesterday I (eat) two apples. (ate)
2. Last week we (see) a deer. (saw)
3. She (write) a note. (wrote)
4. Expand "The turtle crawled." by adding where and when.
   (Accept: "The turtle crawled on the stone path this morning.")
5. Join with *and*: "Mia read a book. Ben drew a picture."
   ("Mia read a book, and Ben drew a picture.")
6. Rearrange: "We ate lunch at noon in the kitchen." Move the when
   part to the front. ("At noon, we ate lunch in the kitchen.")

## Applied task

The learner tells the adult about something they did yesterday in three
sentences, using at least two yesterday verbs from the special list,
then picks one sentence and expands it with a where and a when.

## Exit check

"Give me the yesterday words for *sit*, *tell*, and *go*, and expand
this sentence: 'The cat slept.'" Ready to move on: three past forms
correct and one detail added.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading access:** the adult reads the story aloud first; the
  learner re-reads one sentence at a time and points to each yesterday
  verb.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the right verb card, draw the
  yesterday action, or dictate for the adult to scribe.
- **Extension:** the learner writes a four-sentence "yesterday story"
  using four different special-list verbs, then joins two of the
  sentences with *but* ("It was cold, but we played outside").

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: regularizing past tense ("goed," "eated,"
"runned," "hided") and joining sentences without the comma ("It rained
so we stayed inside"). For the comma, teach the pattern as a habit:
"joining word, comma before it." Also watch for fragments when
expanding ("In the park yesterday" — no who, no action): ask "Who did
it, and what did they do?" Answer-key reference:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md), Lesson 04.
