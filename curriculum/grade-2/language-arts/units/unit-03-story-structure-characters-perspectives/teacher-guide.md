# Teacher Guide — Unit 03 (adults only)

This guide is for the guiding adult. Do not show it to the learner
alongside student pages; the [answer key](answer-key.md) is separate
again, so a learner who finds this guide still does not find answers.

## Session-by-session notes

| Session | What to watch for |
|---|---|
| 1 (L01) | Insist on proof for every answer ("the story says…"). If the learner answers from memory, cover the story and ask them to find the sentence. |
| 2 (Practice A) | Why-questions are the hardest: accept the event first, then ask "and what did the character *think*?" |
| 3 (L02) | Do not accept a retell that skips the middle. Use the three cards until the middle is named without prompting. |
| 4 (Practice B) | Lesson vs. retell confusion is normal here; use the test: "Does it say what happened, or what we learn?" |
| 5 (L03) | Stop invented details kindly but firmly: "The story does not say Jon spoke — what does it actually say he did?" |
| 6 (Practice C) | Beginning/ending sort: an ending must settle the trouble. A last sentence that leaves the book lost is not an ending. |
| 7 (L04) | Model both voices before asking the learner to perform. If voices do not differ, change speed first (slow vs. fast) — it is easier than changing pitch. |
| 8 (Practice D) | Use the text-only alternative whenever the picture is unavailable; it carries every needed detail. |
| 9 (L05) | Keep rhyme, repetition, and alliteration distinct; praise corrections ("fly, fly, fly is repetition, not rhyme"). |
| 10–12 (Project) | Check the story map before drafting: if the two points of view are identical, send the learner back to Step 2 — the book cannot be fixed later by voices alone. |
| 13 (L06) | Enforce "both sides" for every different. A one-sided different is an incomplete answer, not a wrong idea. |
| 14 (Practice E) | Watch the "waters every day" trap: it is true in A, false in B, so it is not a same. |
| 15 (Quiz) | Re-teach every miss the same day; do not let a missed objective ride into the assessment. |
| 16 (Assessment + sharing) | Score the assessment before celebrating the project, or the other way round — but record both; a strong project does not cancel a failed retell objective. |

## Misconceptions and reteaching

- **Retell = summary of feelings.** Reteach: retell = events in order.
  Use the three cards and the sentence starters "At the beginning… /
  Then the trouble was… / At the end…".
- **Lesson = detail or feeling.** Reteach: ask "What did the character
  *learn to do*?" Lessons are about actions and choices.
- **Feeling = whole response.** Reteach: feeling + words + action.
  Use the feels / says / does chart from Lesson 03.
- **Point of view = disagreeing about facts.** Reteach: Ana and Ben
  agree it rains; they disagree about whether rain is good, because
  they wanted different things.
- **Same = appears in one version.** Reteach: a same must be true in
  both versions; check each version before claiming it.
- **Ending = last sentence.** Reteach: an ending settles the trouble.
  Test: "Is anything still broken, lost, or stuck?" If yes, the story
  has not ended.
- **Invented plausible details.** Reteach: "Show me where the story
  says that." Plausible is not the same as stated. This matters most
  for Jon (no dialogue in the story) and Mr. Lee (uses a pole; does
  not climb the tree).

## Materials and accessibility

- Paper, crayons/pencils, three labeled cards (BEGINNING / MIDDLE /
  END), six question-word cards. Nothing to buy.
- Every written task has an oral, pointing, drawing, or adult-scribed
  option; record the learner's words exactly when scribing, then read
  them back for approval.
- The generated picture always has a text-only alternative (Lesson 04);
  no task requires seeing the picture. For learners who cannot hear
  voice differences, pair each voice with a visible signal (slow/fast
  hand sign, sad/happy face card).
- Sessions are 25–30 minutes; stop at the timer even mid-task and
  resume next session rather than pushing a tired learner through a
  performance task (especially the two-voice reading).

## Scoring thresholds

- **Practice:** no points; the adult checks each set the same day and
  re-teaches misses before the next lesson that builds on them.
- **Quiz:** 16–20 ready for the assessment; 12–15 repeat Practice Sets
  B and D; below 12 repeat Lessons 02 and 04.
- **Unit assessment:** 32–40 ready for Unit 04, with no task at 0;
  24–31 re-teach the missed task's lesson and re-check with two new
  questions; below 24 re-teach the whole missed objective before
  Unit 04.
- **Project:** 32–40 ready, no criterion at 0; below 24, or any
  criterion at 0, revise that page with the adult before sharing.
