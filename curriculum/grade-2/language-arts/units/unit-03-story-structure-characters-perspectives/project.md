# Project — My Two-Voice Story Book

**What you make:** a four-page story book (paper folded in half, or four
separate pages stapled together) about one shared event seen by two
characters in different ways, read aloud in two voices at the end.
**Sessions:** project work 1–3 in the unit pacing (Sessions 10–12), plus
sharing in Session 16. **Standards:** RL.2.2, RL.2.3, RL.2.5, RL.2.6,
RL.2.7, SL.2.4, SL.2.5.

## Materials

Paper (4 pages), crayons or pencils, the story-map boxes below (adult
draws them on scrap paper). Nothing to buy. The learner may dictate any
text for the adult to scribe; drawings count as story content when a
dictated caption accompanies them.

## Steps

1. **Pick a shared event** (Session 10). Choose one event both
   characters experience together, for example: rain spoils a picnic
   (like Ana and Ben), a ball gets stuck in a tree, a lost shoe is found
   in the garden, or a power cut turns off the lights during a game.
   The event must be safe, everyday, and free of hazards — no climbing,
   no water deeper than a puddle, no strangers.
2. **Plan on the story map** (Session 10). Fill in, in words or
   drawings with dictated labels:
   - Characters (2, with names)
   - Setting (where, when)
   - Problem (what goes wrong in the shared event)
   - Character 1's point of view: feeling + one thing they say + one
     thing they do
   - Character 2's point of view: feeling + one thing they say + one
     thing they do (must differ from Character 1's)
   - Ending: how the event concludes (the trouble must be settled, not
     left hanging)
   - Lesson: what the story teaches, in the learner's own words
3. **Draft the book** (Session 11), one page at a time:
   - Page 1 (cover + beginning): title, both characters, setting.
   - Page 2 (middle): the problem, and Character 1's response.
   - Page 3 (middle continued): Character 2's response — a different
     feeling and a different action.
   - Page 4 (ending + lesson): how it concludes, the lesson in one
     sentence, and one illustration that matches the words.
   Each page needs 2–4 sentences (dictated or written) plus at least one
   drawing. The two characters' dialogue lines must be marked (for
   example, with different colors or with "said in a ___ voice" notes)
   so the reader knows which voice to use.
4. **Rehearse in two voices** (Session 12). Read the dialogue aloud:
   one voice per character, clearly different (high/low, fast/slow,
   happy/sad — the learner's choice, but consistent). The adult coaches
   once, then the learner reads the book through without stopping.
5. **Share** (Session 16). Read or tell the book to the adult (or to
   family, if the learner wishes — never recorded or posted without
   adult decision; nothing from this project goes in the repository).
   After sharing, the learner names their own lesson in one sentence
   without reading it off the page.

## Deliverables

- A completed story map (Session 10).
- A four-page book: beginning, middle (two views), ending + lesson,
  with at least one illustration that matches the words.
- A two-voice read-aloud (Session 12 rehearsal + Session 16 sharing).

## Rubric (40 points; adult scores; see the answer key for sample responses)

| Criterion | 10 points | 7 points | 4 points | 0 points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Story structure (RL.2.5, RL.2.2) | Beginning introduces characters + setting; middle has a clear problem; ending concludes it; events in order | One part weak (e.g., ending leaves the trouble half-settled) but order intact | Two parts missing or out of order | No recognizable beginning/middle/end |
| Character responses (RL.2.3) | Each character's feeling + words + action are shown, and each response fits the problem | One character fully shown; the other partial | Feelings only, no actions; or responses do not fit the problem | No responses shown |
| Two points of view (RL.2.6, RL.2.7) | The two views clearly differ (different feelings and actions about the same event); dialogue read in two distinct, consistent voices | Views differ, but voices are only slightly different | Views are nearly identical, or voices do not change | One view only, or no dialogue |
| Lesson + illustration (RL.2.2, RL.2.7, SL.2.4–5) | Lesson stated in own words and matches the story; illustration matches the words; sharing is audible and in order | Lesson matches but is copied phrasing, or illustration loosely matches | Lesson does not match the story, or illustration unrelated | No lesson or no illustration |

**Ready:** 32–40 points, with no criterion at 0. **Re-teach and revise:**
any criterion at 0 or total below 24 — revise that page with the adult
before sharing, using the teacher guide's reteaching notes.
