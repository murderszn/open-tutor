# Practice — Unit 03: Story Structure, Characters, and Multiple Perspectives

**How to use it:** one set per practice session (see the unit README
pacing). The adult reads the story or question aloud as needed; the
learner may answer orally, by pointing or drawing, or in writing, and
the adult may scribe. The adult reviews the same day. Answers are in the
[answer key](answer-key.md) — adults only.

Stories are in the lessons: "Milo and the Runaway Kite" in
[Lesson 01](lessons/lesson-01-key-detail-questions.md), "The Two
Builders" in [Lesson 02](lessons/lesson-02-retell-and-lesson.md), "A
Rainy Day, Two Ways" and the generated picture in
[Lesson 04](lessons/lesson-04-two-points-of-view.md), and the two
sunflower versions in [Lesson 06](lessons/lesson-06-two-versions-one-story.md).

## Set A — Question detectives (Lesson 01; RL.2.1)

Answer with proof: point to the sentence or picture that answers you.

1. Who takes the kite to the park, and who goes with him?
2. Where does the kite get caught?
3. When does Milo fly the kite — morning or evening? How do you know?
4. Why does Milo feel sad in the middle of the story?
5. How does Mr. Lee get the kite down? (Name what he uses.)
6. How does Milo fix the kite's tear?

## Set B — Retell chains (Lesson 02; RL.2.2)

1. Retell Milo's story in three parts: beginning, middle, end. (One
   sentence per part.)
2. What is Milo's lesson, in your own words?
3. Retell "The Two Builders" in three parts.
4. What is the builders' lesson, in your own words?
5. Put these Milo events in order (write 1, 2, 3): ___ Milo tapes the
   tear. ___ The string slips. ___ Milo takes the kite to the park.
6. Which is a lesson and which is a retell? (a) "Milo's kite caught in
   a tree." (b) "When something goes wrong, ask a grown-up for help."
   Explain your choice in one sentence.

## Set C — Responses and structure (Lesson 03; RL.2.3, RL.2.5)

1. How does Milo respond when the kite catches in the tree? Name his
   feeling and one thing he does next.
2. How does Maya respond when her tower falls? Name her feeling and one
   thing she does next.
3. Sort into beginning / middle / end: (a) "Maya and Jon loved building
   with blocks." (b) "Her second tower stood tall too." (c) "Her tower
   wobbled and fell down with a crash."
4. Write a beginning sentence for a story about a child who loses a
   library book. It must introduce who and where.
5. Write an ending sentence for that library-book story. It must
   conclude the trouble — the book cannot still be lost.
6. True or false, with a reason: "Jon laughs at Maya when her tower
   falls." (Hint: check what the story actually says about Jon.)

## Set D — Picture voices (Lessons 04–05; RL.2.6, RL.2.7, RL.2.4)

Use the picture in Lesson 04 (or its text-only alternative).

1. Whose point of view is "This is the worst day!" — Ana's or Ben's?
   What proves it?
2. Whose point of view is "This is the best day!"? What proves it?
3. Name two things that are the same for Ana and Ben, and two things
   that are different.
4. Name one thing the picture shows that the story's words do not spell
   out.
5. Read this line in the right voice: "Oh no! My picnic is spoiled."
   (Whose voice? Low and slow, or high and happy?)
6. Make a rhyming pair for the kite chant: finish "Kite in the tree, /
   kite in the ___." (Any true rhyme for *tree* passes: free, me, see,
   knee. Then say what the repeated line does.)

## Set E — Same or different (Lesson 06; RL.2.9)

Use the two sunflower versions in Lesson 06.

1. Name two sames that are true in both versions.
2. Name two differents, naming both sides each time.
3. True or false, with a reason: "In both versions, Tomas waters the
   sunflower every single day."
4. Which version has classmates? What do they do?
5. Which version has a family dinner? What happens there?
6. A new version puts Tomas on a farm with his uncle. Name one thing
   that must stay the same for it to still be the same story, and one
   thing that may change.
