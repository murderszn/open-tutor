# Unit 03 — Story Structure, Characters, and Multiple Perspectives

**Grade:** 2 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #16](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/16), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Stories have a shape, characters have feelings, and two characters can see
the very same day in completely different ways. In this unit the learner
reads and retells original stories, names what happens at the beginning,
middle, and end, describes how characters respond when something goes wrong,
hears the difference when two characters speak in their own voices, and
compares two versions of the same story. Every story in this unit is
original and written for grade 2, except for the public-domain Aesop fable
the adult may read aloud from the Library of Congress (linked, not
reproduced, in the Resource Pack).

## Essential question

How do I know what a story is really about — and how would it sound if
another character told it?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks, Unit 01, and Unit 02:

- Read grade-level text orally with accuracy and expression on successive
  readings; use context to confirm or self-correct a word (RF.2.4)
- Ask and answer who/what/where/when/why/how questions about a short
  read-aloud (grade-1 skill, re-taught in Lesson 01)
- Retell a short story in order with adult support
- Read and spell common grade-2 words; use sentence context for a new word

If retelling in order is not yet secure, Lesson 02 re-teaches it before
any new vocabulary (point of view, structure) is assessed.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Ask and answer who, what, where, when, why, and how questions about key
   details in a story, pointing to the sentence or picture that answers
   each question (RL.2.1).
2. Retell a story in order (beginning, middle, end) and name its central
   message, lesson, or moral in their own words (RL.2.2).
3. Describe how a character responds to a major event or challenge, using
   the character's actions and words as evidence (RL.2.3).
4. Describe the overall structure of a story: how the beginning introduces
   the characters and setting, and how the ending concludes the action
   (RL.2.5); describe how repeated words, rhymes, or beats supply rhythm
   and meaning (RL.2.4).
5. Acknowledge differences in characters' points of view, including by
   reading dialogue aloud in a different voice for each character, and use
   information from illustrations and words together to explain characters,
   setting, or plot (RL.2.6, RL.2.7).
6. Compare and contrast two versions of the same story, naming at least
   two ways they are the same and two ways they are different (RL.2.9),
   and read grade-2 stories in the grades 2–3 band with scaffolding as
   needed (RL.2.10).

## Vocabulary (taught in stories first, definitions second)

character · setting · beginning · middle · end · problem · solution ·
retell · lesson (moral) · respond · point of view · voice · dialogue ·
rhythm · repeated line · version · same · different · illustration

## The unit's stories (all original; read aloud by the adult, then re-read by the learner)

- **"Milo and the Runaway Kite"** (Lessons 01–03, Practice A–C): Milo and
  his sister Ana take a new red kite to the park on Saturday morning. The
  string slips, the kite catches in a tall oak tree, and Milo thinks it is
  lost. The park keeper, Mr. Lee, helps with a long pole; Milo tapes a
  small tear and flies the kite again, holding the string tight with both
  hands. Lesson: *when something goes wrong, stay calm and ask a grown-up
  for help.*
- **"The Two Builders"** (Lesson 02, Practice B): Maya builds a block
  tower fast and it falls; Jon builds slowly and carefully and his tower
  stands; Maya tries Jon's careful way and her second tower stands too.
  Lesson: *slow and careful work lasts.*
- **"A Rainy Day, Two Ways"** (Lesson 04, the generated picture, Practice
  D): the same rainy Saturday in the same garden. Ana, in a yellow coat,
  is sad because rain spoils her picnic. Ben, in a red coat, is delighted
  because rain makes the best puddles. Same rain, two points of view.
- **"Tomas and the Tall Sunflower," two versions** (Lesson 06,
  Practice E): Version A grows the sunflower in the school garden with
  help from classmates; Version B grows it in Grandma's yard with help
  from Grandma. Same boy, same seed, same tall sunflower, same sharing
  of seeds — different place, helper, and ending guests.

## 16-session pacing

Four sessions per week, 25–30 minutes each. K–2 tasks stay oral, pointing,
drawing, manipulative, or adult-scribed as needed; grade 2 adds short
independent written practice (6–8 tasks) that the adult reviews the same
day.

| Session | Week | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 11 | Lesson 01: Key-detail questions | Answers 5 of 6 who/what/where/when/why/how questions with evidence |
| 2 | 11 | Practice A: question detectives | Completes 6 tasks; each answer names its evidence sentence |
| 3 | 11 | Lesson 02: Retell and find the lesson | Retells "Milo" in order (3 parts) and names its lesson |
| 4 | 11 | Practice B: retell chains | Retells both unit stories in order; names each lesson |
| 5 | 12 | Lesson 03: How characters respond; beginning and ending | Describes Milo's and Maya's responses with actions + words |
| 6 | 12 | Practice C: response and structure sort | Sorts 6 events into beginning/middle/end; explains 2 responses |
| 7 | 12 | Lesson 04: Two points of view | Reads Ana's and Ben's lines in two different voices |
| 8 | 12 | Practice D: picture voices | Uses the generated picture + text alternative to name each child's view |
| 9 | 13 | Lesson 05: Rhythm, repeated lines, and pictures | Finds the repeated line; explains what a picture adds |
| 10 | 13 | Project work 1: plan the Two-Voice Story Book | Story map complete: characters, setting, problem, two views, ending, lesson |
| 11 | 13 | Project work 2: draft and illustrate | Draft complete in order; one illustration matches the words |
| 12 | 13 | Project work 3: rehearse in two voices | Reads dialogue in two voices; lesson stated in own words |
| 13 | 14 | Lesson 06: Two versions of one story | Names 2 same + 2 different between the sunflower versions |
| 14 | 14 | Practice E: same-or-different sort | Completes 6 comparison tasks with evidence |
| 15 | 14 | Formative quiz (week-04 quiz) | 8 of 10 items correct; any miss re-taught same day |
| 16 | 14 | Unit assessment + project sharing | Assessment and project rubric complete; results recorded |

## For the guiding adult

Read the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) before Session 1. It lists
materials (paper, crayons, a hand-drawn story map — nothing to buy),
the adult-scribed and drawing options for every written task, and what to
do when a retelling skips the middle or a lesson is guessed instead of
found. Never show the learner the answer key: lesson teacher notes name
it for the adult only, and no answer ever appears beside a student
question.
