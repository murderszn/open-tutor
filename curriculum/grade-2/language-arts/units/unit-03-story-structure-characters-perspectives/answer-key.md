# Answer Key — Unit 03 (adults only — never show the learner)

Every practice item, lesson task, quiz item, and assessment task is
solved here. For open responses, the sample is one acceptable answer;
accept any answer that keeps the same facts and reasoning. "Own words"
answers must not be copied from the lesson text word for word, but
wording may be close.

## Lesson independent-practice answers (summary; full reasoning in each lesson)

- **L01:** (1) Milo, Ana, Mr. Lee. (2) A tall oak tree in the park.
  (3) After Mr. Lee helps. (4) So it will not slip again — he learned
  from the slip. (5) With a long pole. (6) Drawing of sad Milo by the
  tree; sentence: "Milo is sad because he thinks his kite is lost."
- **L02:** Milo retell — beginning: Milo and Ana take a new red kite
  to the park Saturday morning; middle: string slips, kite catches in
  the oak, Mr. Lee lifts it down with a pole; end: Milo tapes the tear
  and flies again holding tight. Milo's lesson: when something goes
  wrong, stay calm and ask a grown-up for help. Builders retell —
  beginning: Maya and Jon love building with blocks; middle: Maya's
  fast tower falls, Jon's careful tower stands, Maya tries the careful
  way; end: her second tower stands too. Builders' lesson: slow and
  careful work lasts. (5) Milo's lesson is about asking for help — the
  kite is rescued by asking Mr. Lee. (6) Drawing of the middle trouble
  (kite in tree, or tower falling) with a one-sentence label.
- **L03:** (1) Milo: sad; then asks for help, tapes the tear, flies
  again holding tight. (2) Maya: cross; watches Jon, tries the careful
  way, second tower stands. (3) Beginning sentence must name a child
  and a library (or other place). (4) Ending sentence must settle it:
  the book is found, returned, or replaced. (5) Sorted: beginning =
  (b) Milo takes the kite to the park; middle = (c) the string slips;
  end = (a) Milo tapes the tear. (6) Drawing
  of Milo flying the kite at the end, holding tight, with a sentence
  about the change (sad/stuck → calm/trying again).
- **L04:** (1) Ana: "Ana thinks the rain spoiled her picnic because
  her blanket and bread got wet." (2) Ben: "Ben thinks the rain is
  great because it made the best puddles." (3) Same: same garden,
  same rain, same Saturday. (4) Different: what each wanted (picnic
  vs. puddles); how each feels (sad vs. delighted). (5) Two faces:
  Ana frowning/sad, Ben laughing/happy. (6) Accept any title pair
  matching the views, e.g., "The Spoiled Picnic" (Ana) vs. "The Best
  Puddles" (Ben).
- **L05:** (1) small/all. (2) Any repeated line for Milo, e.g., "Hold
  on tight, hold on tight!" (3) Fast — short words, three in a row,
  like tugging. (4) Splash drawing + a sentence the picture adds
  (splash height, laughing face). (5) The wet blanket — it belongs to
  Ana's picnic. (6) The expressive reading (voice, speed, pauses)
  helps feeling come through; accept either reading if the learner
  names what changed.
- **L06:** (1) Two sames, e.g., Tomas plants a seed; the sunflower
  grows very tall; Tomas shares seeds. (2) Two differents with both
  sides, e.g., place: school garden (A) vs. Grandma's yard (B); helper:
  classmates pull weeds (A) vs. Grandma helps water (B). (3) A: the
  class measures with a long tape. (4) B: Tomas forgets to water for
  one week; Grandma helps water every morning. (5) Two-sentence
  retells with the shared core circled. (6) Prediction: same = Tomas,
  seed, tall sunflower, sharing seeds; change = place and helper.

## Practice answers

### Set A

1. Milo takes the kite; his sister Ana goes with him.
2. In the branches of a tall oak tree in the park.
3. Morning — "On Saturday morning, Milo took his new red kite…".
4. Because he thought his new kite was lost.
5. Mr. Lee uses a long pole to lift the kite down.
6. Milo puts tape on the small tear.

### Set B

1. Beginning: Milo and Ana take a new red kite to the park Saturday
   morning. Middle: the string slips, the kite catches in the oak tree,
   Mr. Lee lifts it down with a long pole. End: Milo tapes the tear and
   flies the kite again, holding tight with both hands.
2. Milo's lesson (own words): when something goes wrong, stay calm and
   ask a grown-up for help.
3. Beginning: Maya and Jon love building with blocks. Middle: Maya's
   fast tower falls; Jon's careful tower stands; Maya tries the careful
   way. End: her second tower stands too.
4. Builders' lesson (own words): slow and careful work lasts.
5. Order: park = 1, string slips = 2, tapes the tear = 3.
6. (a) is a retell (it says what happened); (b) is a lesson (it says
   what we learn to do). Reason: a retell reports events; a lesson
   gives advice drawn from the pattern.

### Set C

1. Milo: feels sad (thought the kite was lost); then asks for help with
   Ana, tapes the tear, flies again holding tight.
2. Maya: feels cross; watches Jon, tries his careful way, second tower
   stands.
3. (a) beginning; (c) middle; (b) end.
4. Beginning sentence: must introduce a child and a place (e.g., "On
   Monday, Sam went to the school library to borrow a book.").
5. Ending sentence: must settle the trouble (e.g., "Sam found the book
   under his bed and took it back the next day.").
6. False. The story never says Jon laughs; Jon keeps building carefully
   and smiles only at the end, when Maya's second tower stands.

### Set D

1. Ana's — she says "This is the worst day" about her spoiled picnic;
   proof: the wet blanket and wet bread, and her low, slow voice.
2. Ben's — he says "This is the best day" about the puddles; proof:
   the big splash and his laughing face.
3. Same (any two): same garden, same rain, same Saturday. Different
   (any two): wanted a picnic vs. wanted puddles; sad vs. delighted;
   soggy blanket vs. big splash.
4. Any picture-only detail: the apple rolling away, the splash rising
   above Ben's knees, Ana's crossed arms, the puddles on the blanket.
5. Low and slow — that is Ana's sad voice.
6. Any true rhyme for *tree*: free, me, see, knee. The repeated line
   ("Kite in the tree") makes the stuck kite — and then the flying
   kite — feel important and easy to remember.

### Set E

1. Any two sames true in both: Tomas plants a sunflower seed; the
   sunflower grows very tall; Tomas shares seeds at the end.
2. Any two differents with both sides: place — school garden (A) vs.
   Grandma's yard (B); helper — classmates pull weeds (A) vs. Grandma
   helps water (B); measurement — taller than the fence, measured with
   a tape (A) vs. taller than Grandma (B); seed recipients — classmates
   (A) vs. family at dinner (B).
3. False. In A Tomas waters every day after lunch; in B he forgets one
   week and Grandma helps him water every morning.
4. Version A: classmates help Tomas pull weeds (and measure the
   sunflower).
5. Version B: at family dinner Tomas shares the seeds, and the family
   saves seeds to plant next year.
6. Same (any one core item): Tomas, a sunflower seed, a very tall
   sunflower, sharing seeds. Change (any one detail): place, helper,
   or who receives seeds.

## Unit assessment answers (40 points)

- **A1 (2):** Lena, from the school library. **A2 (2):** *The Big Blue
  Sea*, about the sea (whales and waves). **A3 (2):** Monday morning,
  under her bed. **A4 (2):** Because the book slipped off her bed in
  the night and was lost until Monday — keeping it in her backpack
  stops that happening again (accept any reason tied to the slip/loss).
- **B (6):** Beginning: on Friday Lena borrows *The Big Blue Sea*
  from the school library. Middle: she reads it, puts it on her bed,
  and on Monday cannot find it until she looks under the bed. End: she
  finds it, decides to keep it in her backpack, and takes it back to
  the library. (2 points per part, in order.)
- **C (6):** Response (3): she looks for it — on the bed, then under
  the bed — instead of quitting; she does not cry or give up in the
  passage, and inventing that loses the point. Changed habit (3): she
  will keep the book in her backpack from now on.
- **D1 (3):** "On Friday, Lena borrowed a book about the sea from the
  school library." It introduces who (Lena) and where (the school
  library) plus what starts (borrowing the book). **D2 (3):** "On
  Monday, Lena took the book back to the library." It settles the
  trouble: the borrowed book is returned. (Accept the librarian's
  thank-you line as the closing line if the learner explains it as the
  return being accepted — but the return sentence is the concluding
  action.)
- **E1 (3):** Lena — it is in quotation marks and the sentence says
  "Lena said." **E2 (3):** The narrator calls her "Lena" and "she"
  (third person) instead of "I" throughout; only quoted speech uses
  "I."
- **F (4):** Ben. Picture detail (2): laughing face, arms wide, big
  splash, jumping in the puddle. Reason (2): the rain made the best
  puddles, which is what Ben wanted.
- **G (4):** Priya is right (2). Reasons (2, any two): Lena finds the
  book under her bed on Monday morning; it had slipped off the bed in
  the night; she takes it back to the library and the librarian thanks
  her. Sam's "forever" contradicts all three.

## Project sample (for scoring, not for the learner to copy)

A passing story map: event — a ball stuck in a tree. Characters: Sam
(wants his ball back for the game, worried) and Priya (thinks climbing
talk is exciting, then relieved when an adult helps with a long pole —
adult help only, no climbing). Beginning: Sam and Priya, the park,
Saturday. Ending: ball down, game resumes. Lesson (own words): "When
something is stuck, ask a grown-up instead of climbing." Two voices:
Sam low/worried, Priya bright/relieved. Score against the rubric in
[project.md](project.md); a book whose two characters feel and act the
same fails the point-of-view criterion even if the voices differ.
