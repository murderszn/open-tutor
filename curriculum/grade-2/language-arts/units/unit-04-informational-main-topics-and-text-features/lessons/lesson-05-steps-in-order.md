# Lesson 05 — Steps in Order

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can follow steps in order and retell them
in order — because in a how-to text, the order is part of the meaning."
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** the text below; six step
cards the adult writes by hand (one step per card, shuffled for the
guided practice).
**Standards:** RI.2.3, RI.2.1.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult asks: "Tell me the steps to brush your teeth, in order." If
the learner lists them out of order (paste after brushing), say: "Order
matters — you cannot brush before the paste is on." If the learner
lists them in order, say: "That is exactly how a how-to text works."

## The text — "How a Beaver Builds a Dam" (original; adult reads aloud first, learner re-reads)

Beavers build dams step by step. Here are the steps, in order.

**Step 1.** Find a narrow place in a stream where the water runs fast
over rocks.

**Step 2.** Cut down small trees and drag the branches to the narrow
place.

**Step 3.** Lay the branches across the stream, one over another.

**Step 4.** Pack mud and stones between the branches so water cannot
slip through easily.

**Step 5.** Keep adding branches and mud until the wall is high
enough to slow the water.

**Step 6.** Watch the pond form behind the dam. When the pond is deep
enough, build a lodge in it.

## Explicit teaching

Some information texts do not just list facts — they show a
**connection between steps**: each step makes the next step possible.
Three clue words help:

- **First / step 1** — where it starts.
- **Then / next / after that** — what follows.
- **Until / when** — what tells you a step is finished or what happens
  as a result.

To retell steps in order, ask after each step: "What had to happen
before this step could work?" If Step 4 (pack mud) came before Step 3
(lay branches), there would be nothing to pack mud between. That is
how you know the order is right.

### Modeled example 1 — Why Step 2 comes before Step 3

Adult thinks aloud: "Step 2 says cut trees and drag branches to the
narrow place. Step 3 says lay branches across the stream. You cannot
lay branches you have not brought yet. So Step 2 must come first. The
order is not decoration — it is the instruction."

### Modeled example 2 — What "until" tells you

"Step 5 says 'keep adding branches and mud *until* the wall is high
enough to slow the water.' *Until* tells me when to stop: when the
water slows. If I stopped too early, the water would run straight
through, and Step 6 — the pond forming — could never happen. Each
step's ending starts the next step."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. The adult lays the six shuffled step cards face up. The learner
   puts them in order, saying each step aloud.
2. "What happens in Step 4? Why must it come after Step 3?"
   (Packing mud and stones between branches; mud needs branches to
   pack between.)
3. "What tells the beaver Step 5 is finished?" (The wall is high
   enough to slow the water.)
4. "What is the connection between Step 5 and Step 6?" (The wall
   slows the water, so the pond forms; the pond must be deep enough
   before the lodge is built.)
5. **Error-analysis:** the adult retells the steps with Steps 3 and 4
   swapped. "Did I get it right?" (No — you cannot pack mud between
   branches that are not laid yet. Say the correct order for Steps 3
   and 4.)

## Independent practice (adult-scribed or written; 6 tasks)

1. Write or dictate the six steps in order, using numbers.
2. What does the beaver do first? Last?
3. Why does the beaver pick a narrow place in Step 1? (Answer from the
   text: it is where water runs fast over rocks — a good dam site.)
4. What would go wrong if Step 4 were skipped? Explain in one or two
   sentences.
5. Name one clue word in the text that shows order (first, then,
   until, when — any one with its sentence).
6. Draw the six steps as six small pictures in order, or point to
   each step card in order and say what happens.

## Applied task

The learner writes or dictates a 4-step how-to of their own (for
example, how to make a sandwich or build a block tower) with order
clue words. The adult follows the steps exactly as written — including
any step the learner left out — so a missing step becomes visible and
funny ("You never said to open the bread bag!"). Then the learner
fixes the steps.

## Exit check

"Retell the six dam steps in order without looking." Ready to move on:
all six steps, in order, with at most one adult prompt.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading access:** adult reads the steps aloud; the learner handles
  the step cards as a manipulative.
- **Alternate response mode:** order the cards, draw the steps, or
  dictate the retelling.
- **Extension:** the learner adds a Step 7 of their own ("What the
  beaver does next") that follows logically from Step 6, and defends
  its place in the order.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: retelling steps as a loose list ("beavers use
sticks and mud") without order, or swapping steps that depend on each
other. Reteach with the "what had to happen before this?" question.
