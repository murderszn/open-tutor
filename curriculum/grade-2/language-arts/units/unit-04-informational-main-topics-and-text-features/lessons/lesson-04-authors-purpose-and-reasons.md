# Lesson 04 — Author's Purpose and Reasons

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can tell what the author wants to answer,
explain, or describe — and I can show how the author's reasons support
the point."
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** the text below; a pencil or
pointer.
**Standards:** RI.2.6, RI.2.8.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult asks: "If I say, 'You should wear a coat because it is cold
outside,' what is my point, and what is my reason?" (Point: wear a
coat. Reason: it is cold.) If the learner mixes them up, model it once
more with a new example ("Eat lunch because you are hungry") and let
the learner name point and reason.

## The text — "Why Ponds Help Other Animals" (original; adult reads aloud first, learner re-reads)

Beavers build ponds, and ponds help other animals too. That is the
point of this text.

First, a pond gives ducks safe water. Ducks swim on the pond and hide
their nests in the tall grass at its edge. The deep water keeps
raccoons and other land animals away from the nests.

Second, a pond gives fish and frogs food and room to live. Small fish
swim in the pond's cool water. Frogs lay eggs in the still water, and
tadpoles grow there.

Third, a pond gives animals shelter near the lodge. Muskrats build
their own small homes in the pond's banks. Birds rest in the trees
that grow where the pond keeps the ground wet.

So a beaver pond is not just for beavers. It is a home and a food
store for many animals.

## Explicit teaching

Every information text has a **purpose**: what the author wants to
do. In grade 2, ask three questions:

- Does the author want to **answer** a question? (For example: "Why
  do beavers build dams?")
- Does the author want to **explain** how something works or why
  something happens? (For example: how a pond helps other animals.)
- Does the author want to **describe** something? (For example: what
  a lodge looks like.)

A **point** is the author's main idea — what the author wants you to
believe or know. A **reason** is a fact the author gives to support
the point. Test a reason: if you remove it, is the point weaker? If
yes, it is a real reason, not just an extra fact.

### Modeled example 1 — Purpose

Adult thinks aloud: "The first sentence says, 'Beavers build ponds,
and ponds help other animals too. That is the point of this text.'
The author is not mainly describing a pond — the author is explaining
*why* ponds help other animals. So the purpose is to explain, and the
point is: ponds help other animals."

### Modeled example 2 — Reasons support the point

"The author gives three reasons. Reason 1: ducks get safe water and
hidden nests. Reason 2: fish and frogs get food and room to live.
Reason 3: muskrats and birds get shelter. Each reason is about a
different animal, but all three answer the same question: how does a
pond help other animals? Take away all three reasons and the point is
just a claim with no proof."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. "What is the author's point in one sentence?" (Beaver ponds help
   other animals too.)
2. "Name one reason the author gives, and say which animal it helps."
   (Any of the three, correctly matched.)
3. "Is the author's purpose to answer, explain, or describe? How do
   you know?" (Explain — the text explains why ponds help; the first
   sentence announces the point and the rest gives reasons.)
4. "Which reason helps ducks? Point to the sentence." (The "First, a
   pond gives ducks safe water" paragraph.)
5. **Error-analysis:** the adult says, "The point is that ducks hide
   their nests." "Is that the point, or a reason?" (A reason — it
   supports the bigger point that ponds help other animals. The duck
   fact alone is too small to be the whole point.)

## Independent practice (adult-scribed or written; 6 tasks)

1. Write the author's point in your own words.
2. List the three reasons, one per line, with the animal each helps.
3. Which reason helps fish and frogs? Copy or retell its sentence.
4. Why does deep water help ducks? Answer with a reason from the text.
5. The author ends: "It is a home and a food store for many animals."
   Is that sentence a new point or a closing repeat of the point?
   Explain in one sentence.
6. Draw or point to one animal the pond helps, and say the reason in
   your own words.

## Applied task

The learner makes a point of their own ("Recess helps kids") and gives
the adult two reasons. The adult checks: does each reason really
support the point? Then they swap: the adult gives a weak reason
("Recess helps kids because the sky is blue") and the learner explains
why it does not support the point.

## Exit check

"Tell me the purpose, the point, and one reason — and show me the
sentence for the reason." Ready to move on: all three named, reason
matched to the point.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading access:** adult reads aloud first; learner re-reads the
  "First / Second / Third" markers as signposts.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the reason sentences, draw the
  animal each reason helps, or dictate answers.
- **Extension:** the learner adds a fourth reason of their own (for
  example, deer drink at the pond) and says where it would fit in the
  text's order.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: calling any interesting fact a reason, or
calling a reason the point. Reteach with the "take it away" test: if
removing it weakens the point, it is a reason. Answer-key reference:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md), Lesson 04.
