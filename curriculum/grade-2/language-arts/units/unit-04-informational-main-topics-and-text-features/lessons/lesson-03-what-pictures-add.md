# Lesson 03 — What Pictures Add

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can explain what a picture shows that the
words do not say — and use the picture to understand the text better."
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** the generated picture below
(or its text-only alternative); Lesson 01's text.
**Standards:** RI.2.7.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult asks: "What does a beaver's dam look like?" If the learner
describes sticks across water, say: "You pictured it. Today we study a
real picture and ask what it adds." If the learner only repeats words
from Lesson 01, say: "Words tell us sticks and mud. A picture can show
us how big, what shape, and what else is nearby."

## The picture — "Beaver at the dam" (AI-generated illustration)

**Caption:** "An AI-generated picture. A beaver adds a stick to its
dam, with its stick-and-mud lodge waiting across the pond."

**Text-only alternative** (carries every detail the activities need,
so this lesson works without the picture): "In front, on the left, a
brown beaver stands in shallow blue pond water. It has a wide, flat,
dark-brown tail behind it and holds a small stick with a tiny branch
in its front paws. To the right, a dam of piled sticks, branches, and
brown mud stretches across the water. Behind the dam is a calm pond.
On the far bank sits a dome-shaped lodge made of sticks and mud, with
a dark opening. Green trees, grass, cattails, and lily pads surround
the pond under a blue sky with white clouds. No words appear in the
picture."

## Explicit teaching

Pictures in information books are not decoration. They do three jobs:

1. **Show what words cannot easily describe** — the shape of a dam,
   the flat tail, how sticks pile together.
2. **Show how parts fit together** — the dam in front, the pond behind
   it, the lodge across the pond. One picture shows the whole home
   area at once.
3. **Prove or add to the words** — the text says a dam is sticks and
   mud; the picture shows *how many* sticks, *how* they pile, and that
   the beaver carries one stick at a time.

Rule: a good answer names the picture detail *and* says what it adds:
"The picture shows ___, which the words do not say." A caption helps,
but the picture itself is the evidence.

### Modeled example 1 — What the picture adds about the dam

Adult thinks aloud: "The words say a dam is 'a wall made of sticks,
branches, and mud.' The picture shows the sticks are piled every which
way, crossing over each other, with mud packed at the bottom edge
where the dam meets the water. The words never say the sticks cross
like that. So the picture adds: how the sticks are arranged."

### Modeled example 2 — What the picture adds about the beaver's work

"The words say beavers 'pile sticks across the stream.' The picture
shows the beaver holding one small stick in its front paws, standing
in the water right at the dam. That tells me the beaver builds the dam
stick by stick, by itself, in the water — the words say it piles
sticks, but the picture shows *one beaver, one stick, at a time*."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. "Point to the beaver's tail in the picture (or name it from the
   text alternative). What shape is it?" (Wide and flat.) "Do the
   Lesson 01 words say the tail's shape?" (Yes — 'wide flat tail' —
   so here the picture *confirms* the words.)
2. "What does the picture show about the lodge that the words do not
   say?" (It sits on the far bank at the pond's edge, dome-shaped,
   with a dark opening; the words say it is dome-shaped but not where
   it sits in the scene.)
3. "What does the picture show about the dam's size compared to the
   beaver?" (The dam is much longer than the beaver is tall; it
   stretches across the whole water.)
4. "Name one thing in the picture that is NOT in the Lesson 01 text."
   (Cattails, lily pads, clouds, the beaver carrying a single stick —
   any one, defended.)
5. **Error-analysis:** the adult says, "The picture proves beavers
   live alone, because only one beaver is shown." "Is that right?"
   (No — a picture shows one moment, not the whole truth. The text
   says a beaver family lives in the lodge together. One beaver in a
   picture does not mean only one beaver lives there.)

## Independent practice (adult-scribed or written; 6 tasks)

1. Name two facts the picture shows that the Lesson 01 words do not
   state.
2. How does the picture help you understand the word **dam** better
   than the definition alone? Answer in one or two sentences.
3. What does the caption tell you? Copy it in your own words.
4. Draw a small copy of one part of the picture (the dam, the lodge,
   or the beaver) and label what it shows.
5. The text says lodge doors are under the water. Can you see the
   doors in this picture? Explain why or why not.
6. If you could add one thing to this picture to teach more about
   beavers, what would you add, and what would it teach?

## Applied task

The learner covers the picture (or sets the text alternative aside)
and tells the adult three things they remember from it. Then they
check: which of the three were also in the words, and which came only
from the picture? The picture-only facts are the picture's real job.

## Exit check

"Tell me one fact you learned from the picture alone, and one fact the
picture confirmed from the words." Ready to move on: both facts named,
and the picture-only fact is truly not in the words.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading access:** learners who cannot see the picture use the
  text-only alternative above; every question is answerable from it.
- **Alternate response mode:** point, draw, or dictate answers.
- **Extension:** the learner writes a new, better caption for the
  picture that names one dam fact and one lodge fact.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: treating the picture as proof of everything
("only one beaver, so beavers live alone") or as decoration with
nothing to teach. Reteach with Guided practice item 5 and the
"picture-only fact" check in the applied task. The picture is an
AI-generated illustration of a fictional scene, not a photograph or
observation: say so, and note that the lodge in the picture sits on
the far bank, while real lodges are often surrounded by water with
underwater entrances, as the text states. Answer-key reference:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md), Lesson 03.
