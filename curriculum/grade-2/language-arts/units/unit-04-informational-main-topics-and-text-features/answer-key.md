# Answer Key — Unit 04

**For the guiding adult only.** Never show this key to the learner, and
never place it beside a student question. Every answer below was solved
independently from the unit texts and reconciled with the lesson,
practice, quiz, and assessment items.

## Lesson 01 — Main topic and paragraph focus

**Guided:** 1. Main topic: beavers — how they build and live. Proof
sentences: intro ("Beavers are large rodents…"), body ("A beaver has
brown fur…"), dam ("A beaver builds a dam…"), lodge ("beavers build a
lodge"), food ("Beavers eat only plants"). 2. Focus: what a beaver's
body is like. 3. Focus: the beaver's home. 4. Focus: beaver food and
babies. 5. No — ponds are only one paragraph's result, not the whole
text's topic; the main topic must fit every paragraph.

**Independent:** 1. Accept: beavers / how beavers build and live /
beavers: nature's builders. 2. "A Beaver's Body" (teeth sentence:
"Its teeth never stop growing"). 3. Any one: dam is sticks, branches,
mud; wall slows water; pond forms behind it; pond must stay deep
enough not to freeze fully. 4. A beaver family ("A beaver family lives
in the lodge together"). 5. Because its teeth never stop growing, so
it must gnaw to keep them the right length. 6. "What Beavers Eat";
focus: beaver food and babies.

## Lesson 02 — Text features scavenger hunt

**Guided:** 1. Index → *teeth — A Beaver's Body*; sentence: "Its
teeth never stop growing, so a beaver must gnaw…" 2. "What Beavers
Eat". 3. A wall built across a stream to slow water and make a pond.
4. Any two: **dam** (wall across a stream…), **lodge** (dome-shaped
home of branches and mud), **kit** (baby beaver), **gnaw** (chew on
something hard, again and again). 5. No — the index gives locations,
not meanings; the glossary gives meanings.

**Independent:** 1. *pond — Building a Dam*. 2. A baby beaver.
3. "A Beaver's Body" (clear lids "like goggles"; swimming paragraph).
4. **gnaw** — to chew on something hard, again and again.
5. Glossary — the question asks a meaning, and the glossary is the
meaning tool. (Accept: index first to locate, glossary to define, if
both steps are named.) 6. Any icon with a stated meaning (e.g.,
magnifying glass = look closer); accept reasonable drawings.

## Lesson 03 — What pictures add

**Guided:** 1. Wide and flat tail; words confirm it ("wide flat
tail") — picture confirms, not adds. 2. The lodge sits on the far
bank at the pond's edge, dome-shaped with a dark opening; the words
say dome-shaped but not its place in the scene. 3. The dam is much
longer than the beaver is tall and stretches across the water.
4. Any one: cattails, lily pads, clouds, the beaver carrying a single
stick, grass at the water's edge. 5. No — one beaver in one picture
does not show who lives in the lodge; the text says a family lives
there together.

**Independent:** 1. Any two picture-only facts: sticks piled crossing
over each other; mud packed at the dam's bottom edge; beaver carrying
one stick at a time in the water; lodge on the far bank; cattails /
lily pads / clouds. 2. Any confirmed fact: flat tail; dam of sticks
and mud; dome-shaped lodge; pond behind the dam. 3. Accept any
faithful restatement of the caption: a beaver adds a stick to its dam,
with its stick-and-mud lodge across the pond. 4. Accept a drawing of
dam, lodge, or beaver with one correct teaching sentence. 5. No —
the doors are underwater and this view shows the lodge from the bank
side; the dark opening shown is the lodge's front, not its underwater
doors. (Accept: the picture does not show underwater doors; the text
is the source for that fact.) 6. Any addition with a stated teaching
purpose (e.g., kits swimming = babies learn to swim; a cut tree stump
= teeth cut trees).

## Lesson 04 — Author's purpose and reasons

**Guided:** 1. Beaver ponds help other animals too. 2. Any one:
ducks — safe water/hidden nests; fish and frogs — food and room;
muskrats and birds — shelter. 3. Explain — the text explains why
ponds help; its first sentence announces the point and the rest gives
reasons. 4. "First, a pond gives ducks safe water" paragraph.
5. A reason, not the point — it is one support for the bigger point.

**Independent:** 1. Accept: beaver ponds help other animals / ponds
are homes and food stores for many animals. 2. Ducks: safe water,
hidden nests; fish and frogs: food and room to live; muskrats and
birds: shelter near the lodge. 3. Fish and frogs get food and room:
"Small fish swim in the pond's cool water. Frogs lay eggs in the still
water, and tadpoles grow there." 4. Deep water keeps raccoons and
other land animals away from the nests. 5. A closing repeat of the
point (same idea, restated as a conclusion), not a new point.
6. Any animal the pond helps with its reason in the learner's own
words (duck, fish, frog, muskrat, bird).

## Lesson 05 — Steps in order

**Guided:** 1. Order: 1 find a narrow place; 2 cut trees and drag
branches; 3 lay branches across; 4 pack mud and stones; 5 keep adding
until high enough; 6 watch the pond form, then build a lodge.
2. Step 4: pack mud and stones between branches; it must follow Step 3
because mud needs laid branches to pack between. 3. When the wall is
high enough to slow the water. 4. The slowed water forms the pond;
the pond must be deep enough before the lodge is built. 5. No — Steps
3 and 4 swapped cannot work: there is nothing to pack mud between
before branches are laid.

**Independent:** 1. Same six steps in order as Guided 1. 2. First:
find a narrow place. Last: watch the pond form, then build a lodge.
3. It is where the water runs fast over rocks — a good dam site
(accept: narrow places are easier to dam across). 4. Water would slip
through the gaps; the wall would not slow the water, so no pond would
form. 5. Any one: "Step 1 / first"; "then / next"; "until" (Step 5);
"when" (Step 6) — with its sentence. 6. Six pictures or cards in the
order of Guided 1.

## Lesson 06 — Two texts, same topic

**Guided:** 1. Beavers. 2. Any one: kits stay about two years; beavers
build dams and lodges; families live in the lodge. 3. Any one:
teeth grow all their lives and look orange; beavers eat bark, twigs,
leaves; lodge doors are under water; beavers swim with clear lids.
4. Any one: yearlings help drag branches and watch kits swim; kits
stay in the lodge for the first month; parents do the biggest jobs.
5. No — leaving something out is a difference in focus, not an error;
it would be wrong only if it stated something false.

**Independent:** 1. Two same points with a proof from each text (see
Guided 2). 2. Two different points naming their texts (see Guided 3–4).
3. "Beavers: Nature's Builders" — it has the "What Beavers Eat"
paragraph. 4. "Beaver Families" — it names yearling jobs. 5. No —
the texts agree where they overlap; they differ only in focus, not in
contradiction. 6. Two-circle chart: middle = kits stay about two
years; sides = yearlings watch kits (Families), beavers eat bark
(Builders).

## Unit assessment

**Task A:** (1) Beavers — how they build and live (≤5 words).
(2) The beaver's home: dome-shaped branches-and-mud home, underwater
doors, dry room, family lives there. (3) Its teeth never stop growing,
so it must gnaw to keep them the right length.

**Task B:** (1) Glossary: a baby beaver. (2) Heading: "What Beavers
Eat" (found via heading scan or index entry *bark*). (3) Use an index
when you want to *find where* a topic is; use a glossary when you
want to *know what* a word means.

**Task C:** (1) Two picture-only facts (see Lesson 03 Independent 1).
(2) One confirmed fact (flat tail; sticks-and-mud dam; dome lodge).
(3) Caption naming one dam fact and one lodge fact (e.g., "A beaver
adds a stick to its dam while its lodge waits across the pond").

**Task D:** (1) Purpose: explain; point: beaver ponds help other
animals; two reasons with animals (see Lesson 04 Independent 2).
(2) Six steps in order (see Lesson 05 Guided 1).

**Task E:** Two same points with two proofs each; two different
points naming their texts (see Lesson 06 Independent 1–2).

**Task F:** Checkpoint only: note the Unit 01 sentence read and the
Unit 03 retelling result in the teacher guide; any skill gap becomes
a Week 18 re-teaching item, not a unit failure on its own.
