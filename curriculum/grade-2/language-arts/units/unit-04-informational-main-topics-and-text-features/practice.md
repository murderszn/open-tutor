# Practice — Unit 04

Short independent practice (4–6 tasks per set), adult-reviewed the same
day. K–2 tasks may be oral, pointing, drawing, manipulative, or
adult-scribed. No answers appear on this page; the adult checks against
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## Practice A — Topic detectives (after Lesson 01)

Use "Beavers: Nature's Builders" ([Lesson 01](lessons/lesson-01-main-topic-and-paragraph-focus.md)).

1. What is the main topic of the whole text? Say it in five words or
   fewer.
2. What is the focus of the paragraph under "A Beaver's Body"?
3. What is the focus of the paragraph under "Building a Dam"?
4. Name one key detail from the "A Home Called a Lodge" paragraph, and
   point to its sentence.
5. Why do beavers store branches under the water in fall? Answer with
   a sentence from the text.
6. Which paragraph would you read to find out what color a beaver's
   front teeth are? Say its heading.

## Practice B — Feature finder (after Lesson 02)

Use the Lesson 01 text, its glossary, and its index
([Lesson 02](lessons/lesson-02-text-features-scavenger-hunt.md)).

1. Use the index: where do you find *bark*? Write the heading.
2. Use the glossary: what is a **lodge**?
3. Use a heading: which paragraph explains how a pond forms?
4. Use bold print: find the bold word in the "A Beaver's Body"
   paragraph and tell what it means.
5. A friend asks, "What does *gnaw* mean?" Which feature answers
   fastest — glossary or index? Explain in one sentence.
6. Draw an icon for "new word" (for example, a book or a star) and say
   what it would tell a reader.

## Practice C — Picture detectives (after Lesson 03)

Use the generated picture and its text-only alternative
([Lesson 03](lessons/lesson-03-what-pictures-add.md)).

1. Name one fact the picture shows that the Lesson 01 words do not
   state.
2. Name one fact the picture confirms from the Lesson 01 words.
3. What does the picture show about how the dam is built? Answer in
   one or two sentences.
4. Where is the lodge in the picture? Describe its place in the scene.
5. Can you see the lodge's underwater doors in this picture? Explain
   why or why not.
6. Draw one part of the picture (dam, lodge, or beaver) and write or
   dictate one sentence about what that part teaches.

## Practice D — Purpose and reasons sort (after Lesson 04)

Use "Why Ponds Help Other Animals"
([Lesson 04](lessons/lesson-04-authors-purpose-and-reasons.md)).

1. What is the author's point, in your own words?
2. Match each reason to the animal it helps: (a) safe water and hidden
   nests, (b) food and room to live, (c) shelter near the lodge.
   Animals: ducks, fish and frogs, muskrats and birds.
3. Is the author's purpose to answer, explain, or describe? Explain in
   one sentence.
4. Which reason helps fish and frogs? Retell its sentence.
5. Take away the duck reason. Is the point weaker, stronger, or the
   same? Explain in one sentence.
6. Make a point of your own ("School gardens help kids") and give one
   reason that supports it.

## Practice E — Steps and compare sort (after Lessons 05–06)

Use "How a Beaver Builds a Dam"
([Lesson 05](lessons/lesson-05-steps-in-order.md)) and "Beaver
Families" ([Lesson 06](lessons/lesson-06-two-texts-same-topic.md)).

1. Put these steps in order: pack mud between branches; find a narrow
   place; lay branches across the stream; cut down small trees.
2. What would go wrong if the beaver skipped packing mud? Explain in
   one sentence.
3. Name one point both beaver texts make, with proof from each text.
4. Name one point only "Beaver Families" makes.
5. Which text would you use to answer "How does a beaver keep its teeth
   the right length?" Why?
6. Sort these claims into "Both texts," "Builders only," or "Families
   only": (a) kits stay about two years; (b) yearlings watch kits
   swim; (c) beavers eat bark.
