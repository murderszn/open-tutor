# Unit 04 — Informational Reading: Main Topics, Details, and Text Features

**Grade:** 2 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #16](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/16), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Stories tell you what happened. Information books tell you about the real
world: how beavers build dams, what they eat, and why ponds help other
animals. In this unit the learner reads original information texts about
beavers, finds the main topic and the focus of each paragraph, uses
headings, bold words, captions, glossaries, and indexes to find facts
fast, explains what a picture adds that words alone do not say, names
what the author wants to answer or explain, follows steps in order,
follows reasons that support a point, and compares two texts about the
same animal. Every text in this unit is original and written for grade 2,
except for the linked outside references in the Resource Pack, which are
never reproduced here.

## Essential question

How do I find the important facts in an information book — and prove
them with the book itself?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks and Units 01–03:

- Read grade-level text orally with accuracy and expression on successive
  readings; use context to confirm or self-correct a word (RF.2.4)
- Ask and answer who/what/where/when/why/how questions about a short text
  and point to the sentence that answers each question (RL.2.1, re-taught
  for information text in Lesson 01)
- Retell a short text in order with adult support
- Read and spell common grade-2 words; use sentence context for a new word

If finding a main topic is not yet secure, Lesson 01 re-teaches it with a
one-paragraph text before any multiparagraph text is assessed.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Ask and answer who, what, where, when, why, and how questions about
   key details in an information text, pointing to the sentence or
   picture that answers each question (RI.2.1).
2. Identify the main topic of a multiparagraph text and the focus of
   specific paragraphs within it (RI.2.2).
3. Know and use text features — headings, bold print, captions,
   glossaries, indexes, icons — to locate key facts efficiently, and
   determine the meaning of grade-2 topic words from context and the
   glossary (RI.2.5, RI.2.4).
4. Explain how a specific image contributes to and clarifies a text,
   naming at least one fact the picture shows that the words do not
   state (RI.2.7).
5. Identify the main purpose of a text (what the author wants to answer,
   explain, or describe); describe how reasons support specific points
   the author makes; and describe the connection between steps in a
   technical procedure (RI.2.6, RI.2.8, RI.2.3).
6. Compare and contrast the most important points presented by two texts
   on the same topic, naming at least two points that are the same and
   two that are different, and read grade 2–3 band informational text
   with scaffolding as needed (RI.2.9, RI.2.10).

## Vocabulary (taught in texts first, definitions second)

pond · stick · mud · bark

## The unit's texts (all original; adult reads aloud first, learner re-reads)

- **"Beavers: Nature's Builders"** (Lessons 01–03, Practice A–C): a
  four-paragraph information text with headings. Paragraph 1 introduces
  beavers; "A Beaver's Body" covers the flat tail, orange front teeth
  that keep growing, and swimming; "Building a Dam" covers sticks, mud,
  and the pond a dam makes; "A Home Called a Lodge" covers the
  stick-and-mud home and its underwater doors; "What Beavers Eat"
  covers bark, twigs, and leaves. Bold words: **dam**, **lodge**,
  **kit**, **gnaw**. Glossary and index at the end.
- **"Why Ponds Help Other Animals"** (Lesson 04, Practice D): a short
  text with one point — beaver ponds help other animals — and three
  reasons: safe water for ducks, food for fish and frogs, and shelter
  near the lodge.
- **"How a Beaver Builds a Dam"** (Lesson 05, Practice E): six steps in
  order, from finding a narrow stream to packing mud between sticks.
- **"Beaver Families"** (Lesson 06, Practice E, assessment): a second
  text on the same topic as "Beavers: Nature's Builders," focused on
  family life — parents, yearlings, and kits living and working
  together, kits staying about two years.

Beaver facts in the unit texts were checked 2026-10-30 against the
National Wildlife Federation's American Beaver guide, the U.S. National
Park Service's "Leave it to Beavers" narrative, and the University of
Michigan BioKIDS American beaver page (all linked, not reproduced, in
the Resource Pack): beavers are plant-eating rodents; they build dams
of sticks, branches, and mud across streams; the dam makes a pond; they
build dome-shaped lodges of branches and mud with underwater entrances;
their large front teeth grow all their lives and look orange; baby
beavers are called kits and stay with the family about two years. The
generated picture is an illustration of a fictional scene, not a
photograph or observation.

## 16-session pacing

Four sessions per week, 25–30 minutes each. K–2 tasks stay oral,
pointing, drawing, manipulative, or adult-scribed as needed; grade 2
adds short independent written practice (4–6 tasks) that the adult
reviews the same day. Week 18 doubles as the track's midyear review:
Session 16 revisits one story skill from Unit 03 and one phonics skill
from Unit 01 alongside the unit assessment and project sharing.

| Session | Week | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 | Lesson 01: Main topic and paragraph focus | Names the main topic and each paragraph's focus with evidence |
| 2 | 15 | Practice A: topic detectives | Completes 6 tasks; each answer names its evidence sentence |
| 3 | 15 | Lesson 02: Text features scavenger hunt | Finds 5 facts using 5 different features, each named |
| 4 | 15 | Practice B: feature finder | Completes 6 tasks using headings, bold print, caption, glossary, index |
| 5 | 16 | Lesson 03: What pictures add (generated image) | Names 2 facts the picture shows that words do not state |
| 6 | 16 | Practice C: picture detectives | Completes 6 tasks from the picture + text alternative |
| 7 | 16 | Project work 1: plan My Information Book | Book plan complete: topic, 4 pages, features, glossary words |
| 8 | 16 | Lesson 04: Author's purpose and reasons | Names the purpose and 2 reasons that support the point |
| 9 | 17 | Practice D: purpose and reasons sort | Completes 6 tasks; reasons matched to the point they support |
| 10 | 17 | Lesson 05: Steps in order | Retells the 6 dam-building steps in order |
| 11 | 17 | Project work 2: draft and illustrate | Draft complete in order; one illustration matches the words |
| 12 | 17 | Lesson 06: Two texts, same topic | Names 2 same + 2 different points between the two beaver texts |
| 13 | 18 | Practice E: steps and compare sort | Completes 6 tasks with evidence |
| 14 | 18 | Project work 3: finish and rehearse | Book finished; learner reads one page aloud with its feature named |
| 15 | 18 | Formative quiz (week-04 quiz) | 8 of 10 items correct; any miss re-taught same day |
| 16 | 18 | Unit assessment + project sharing + midyear review | Assessment and project rubric complete; one Unit 01 and one Unit 03 skill revisited |

## For the guiding adult

Read the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) before Session 1. It lists
materials (paper, crayons, a hand-drawn book plan — nothing to buy),
the adult-scribed and drawing options for every written task, and what
to do when a main topic is guessed from the title alone or a text
feature is named but not used. Never show the learner the answer key:
lesson teacher notes name it for the adult only, and no answer ever
appears beside a student question.
