3. Sense fails — kites go up, not shoes; the story would never say that.

## Lesson 03

**Guided practice:**
1. Echo of "Then — **snap**! The string broke." with sharp gasp on *snap*,
   matched after two tries.
2. Expression mark on "Look by the fence!" — e.g., ⬆ or ! — read with
   excited, calling-out feeling (Lee spots the kite).
3. Just right = conversational: fast enough to follow the story, slow
   enough to get every word. Racing loses meaning; crawling loses the story.
4. **?** — "Where is my kite?" (voice lifts). **!** — "I see red!" (big
   feeling). Quotation marks — any dialogue line, read in a character voice.
5. Not fully fluent — accuracy without expression: the listener cannot feel
   Sam's worry or joy.

**Independent practice:**
1–3. Three successive reads logged; adult rubric scores recorded.
4. Third read is easiest because the words are familiar — decoding takes
   less brain, leaving room for feeling.
5. Accept: worried/excited/proud matched to each line with punctuation
   evidence.

**Application:** the picture shows expressive reading through the open
mouth mid-story and wide storytelling eyes; the dog leans in listening.
Text-only alternative: "Where is my kite?" = worried (? lifts); "I see
red!" = excited (!); "You are the best kite ever" = proud (declaration +
*best*).

**Exit check:** two echo-read lines matched for phrasing and expression,
then soloed with at least one meaning-fitting expression choice.

## Lesson 04

**Guided practice:**
1. *gusty* = full of gusts / very windy. Clue: the next sentence defines
   "A **gust** is a sudden burst of wind."
2. *tangle* = to get twisted into a knot. Clue: the next sentence —
   "A **tangle** is a messy knot of string" (definition right there).
3. *fast* contrasts with slow; kites need fast-moving air to rise — the
   contrast tells you wind must move quickly.
4. "The first letter of the word" is not a context clue.
5. Wrong — the sentence says the kite drifts **down**; *drifts* cannot mean
   up.

**Independent practice:**
1. Five bold words found: *invisible, gusty, tangle, drifts, flutters*.
2. *gusty*: definition-in-next-sentence → "full of sudden bursts of wind."
3. *tangle*: definition right there → "twisted into a messy knot."
4. *drifts*: scene/contrast → "moves down slowly and gently." *flutters*:
   scene → "comes down with quick little wobbles."
5. Own words prove real understanding and transfer to talking and writing;
   memorized definitions often cannot be used.

**Exit check:** *snarl* = a messy twisted knot; clue: "a messy knot" is
given right in the sentence (definition).

## Lesson 05

**Guided practice:**
1. Any two: *strong, invisible, flat, gusty, best, sudden, messy, bright,
   windy*.
2. *fast* tells how the wind moves — adverb.
3. Adjectives: *green, dirty, best*. Verbs: *tied, blew*. Adverb: *gently*.
4. walk → stroll → march.
5. Wrong — *quickly* describes **how** someone moves (an action), so it is
   an adverb; the -ly ending is the clue.

**Independent practice:**
1. Four adjectives with nouns, e.g., *red kite, tall grass, green trees,
   big kite*.
2. Two adverbs/how-words, e.g., *fast* ("moves air fast"), *up* ("up, up,
   up" — how it rose).
3. Six or more words placed under ADJECTIVES / ADVERBS columns.
4. Upgraded shade used in a sentence, e.g., "The **huge** red kite flew on
   the **gusty** day."
5. *Sudden* paints the surprise — gusts arrive fast and you feel them;
   plain "wind" does not paint that picture.

**Exit check:** *brightly* — adverb; *tall* — adjective; *carefully* —
adverb. One used in a sentence about Passage A or B, e.g., "Lee looked
**carefully** in the tall grass."

## Lesson 06

**Guided practice:**
1. Big idea: "It tells how kites fly (and what can go wrong)."
2. *What* holds the kite: the string. *Why* gusty days are best: sudden
   bursts of wind lift the kite.
3. Middle retell in order: gusty days are best → wind can tangle the
   string into a messy knot.
4. "Kites come in many colors" does not belong — the passage never says it.
5. Wrong — when the wind stops the kite drifts **down**, not higher; check
   the text.

**Independent practice (after one adult read-aloud of Passage B):**
1. Big idea in one sentence: "It tells how kites fly."
2. Three key details in order, e.g.: wind pushes the kite up → string holds
   it → gusty days are best (then: tangles happen / no wind means down).
3. *Who* would like this text: someone learning to fly a kite.
4. *How* wind helps: pushes against the kite and lifts it. *What* can go
   wrong: the string tangles, or the wind stops and the kite falls.
5. Heard-only retells are harder because you cannot reread a voice — you
   must catch the big idea while listening.

**Exit check (first three sentences of Passage A, read aloud):** big idea —
Sam takes his kite to the park on a windy day. Two details in order —
wind pulls it up over the trees; the string snaps; it falls in the tall
grass.

**Practice D:**

   - *invisible* — cannot be seen — "The wind is a strong, invisible force"

     of wind."
   - *tangle* — twisted knot — "A tangle is a messy knot of string."
   - *drifts* — moves down slowly — "If the wind stops, the kite drifts down."
   - *flutters* — wobbles down — "It flutters to the ground."
   - *force* — power/strength — "strong… force… pushes."
   - *burst* — sudden rush — "a sudden burst of wind."
   - *knot* — tied lump — "a messy knot of string."
3. Defined-in-sentence: *gusty, tangle*. From the scene: *invisible,
   drifts, flutters, force, burst, knot*.

**Practice E:**

2. Two adverbs/how-words, e.g., *fast, up*.
3. Shade pairs, e.g., chilly → cold → freezing; large → big → huge;

4. Dark-shade word used in a kite sentence.

   (action-word) pair explained.
