# Unit 02 — Fluency, Vocabulary, and Comprehension Monitoring

**Grade:** 2 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #16](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/16), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Unit 01 made the learner a word detective. This unit makes the learner a
**reader**: someone who reads on purpose, notices when something does not
make sense, fixes it, reads smoothly with feeling, figures out new words
from the words around them, and collects words that describe the world.
Two original passages anchor every activity: the story **"The Lost Red
Kite"** and the informational text **"How Kites Fly"** (both authored for
this unit below).

## Essential question

How does a good reader sound, and how does a good reader figure things out
while reading?

## Prerequisites

From Unit 01 and the diagnostic weeks:

- Decode regularly spelled one- and two-syllable words (vowel teams,
  clap-and-break, prefixes/suffixes)
- Blend and segment sounds; read grade-1 high-frequency words
- Retell a story in order (beginning, middle, end)

The fluency checks in this unit are not timed tests. Reading speed grows
with rereading, not with racing.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Read grade-level text **with a purpose** and tell whether the reading
   achieved that purpose (RF.2.4.a).
2. **Monitor** while reading: notice when a word or sentence does not sound
   right or make sense, use sentence context to confirm or self-correct
   word recognition, and reread as necessary (RF.2.4.c).
3. Read grade-level text orally with **accuracy, appropriate rate, and
   expression on successive readings** — including echo and partner
   rereading (RF.2.4.b).
4. Use **sentence-level context** as a clue to figure out the meaning of an
   unfamiliar word (L.2.4.a).
5. Use **adjectives and adverbs to describe** — collect describing words
   from conversation and reading, sort them, and distinguish shades of
   meaning among near-synonyms (L.2.6).
6. **Recount or describe key ideas and details** from a text read aloud —
   who, what, where, when, why, and how (SL.2.2).

## Vocabulary (taught in context first, definitions second)

fluency · accuracy · rate (reading speed) · expression · purpose · monitor ·
self-correct · reread · echo reading · context clue · adjective · adverb ·
shades of meaning · retell · word wall

## Unit passages (original; authored for this unit, reused across lessons and practice)

**Passage A — "The Lost Red Kite" (story, Lessons 01–03, practice, project)**

> It was a windy day. Sam ran to the park with a big red kite.
> The wind pulled the kite up, up, up. It flew over the green trees.
> Then — snap! The string broke. The red kite fell into the tall grass.
> Sam looked and looked. "Where is my kite?" he asked.
> His friend Lee came running. "I see red!" she said. "Look by the fence!"
> Sam pulled the kite out of the grass. It was wet and dirty.
> "Can we fix it?" Sam asked.
> Lee tied the string back on. The wind blew again.
> Up went the kite. Sam smiled. "You are the best kite ever," he said.

**Passage B — "How Kites Fly" (informational, Lessons 04–06, practice, project)**

> A kite can stay up in the air when the wind pushes against it.
> The wind is a strong, invisible force. It moves air fast.
> When the wind hits the flat kite, the kite rises up. The string holds
> the kite in place so it does not fly away.
> A gusty day is the best day to fly a kite. A gust is a sudden burst of wind.
> Wind can also tangle the string. A tangle is a messy knot of string.
> If the wind stops, the kite drifts down. It flutters to the ground.
> Fly a kite on a bright, windy day and watch it dance in the sky.

Both passages are original and grade-2 appropriate; neither reproduces any
published text. Bold clue words in Passage B (*invisible, gusty, tangle,
drifts, flutters*) are the targets of Lesson 04.

## 16-session pacing

Four sessions per week, 25–30 minutes each. K–2 tasks stay oral,
pointing, drawing, or adult-scribed as needed; grade 2 begins short
independent written work that the adult reviews the same day.

| Session | Week | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7 | Lesson 01: Read with a purpose | States the purpose before reading; answers the purpose question after reading Passage A |
| 2 | 7 | Practice A: purpose rereads | Completes two purpose-guided rereads of Passage A; answers both purpose checks |
| 3 | 7 | Lesson 02: Catch it and fix it (self-correct) | Catches 3 of 4 planted errors and fixes each by rereading |
| 4 | 7 | Practice B: error-hunt rereading | Finds and fixes all 4 planted errors in the Passage A copy; explains one fix |
| 5 | 8 | Lesson 03: Smooth and full of feeling | Echo-reads Passage A with matched phrasing; marks 2 expression spots |
| 6 | 8 | Practice C: reread relay | Three successive readings of Passage A; adult scores fluency rubric at "growing" or better |
| 7 | 8 | Lesson 04: Clue words | Figures out 4 of 5 bold words in Passage B from sentence context |
| 8 | 8 | Practice D: context-clue cards | Defines 6 of 8 clue words with a sentence of evidence for each |
| 9 | 9 | Lesson 05: Words that describe | Collects 8 adjectives/adverbs from the passages; sorts them correctly |
| 10 | 9 | Practice E: word-wall build | Builds the describing-word wall; explains one shade-of-meaning pair |
| 11 | 9 | Lesson 06: Tell it back | Recounts Passage B's key ideas in order with 3+ details |
| 12 | 9 | Project Part 1: rehearse Fluency Theater | Three rehearsal readings logged; describing-word wall drafted |
| 13 | 10 | Review: fluency games and clue-word races | Leads one echo read; wins or completes the clue-word sort race |
| 14 | 10 | Project Part 2: perform and gallery | Performs Passage A fluently; word wall complete and labeled |
| 15 | 10 | Formative quiz + reteach | Missed items re-taught the same session; learner can name the fix |
| 16 | 10 | Unit assessment (unseen passages) | Adult scores against the answer key; reteach list recorded |

If a lesson runs long, move its application to the next practice session
rather than rushing the exit check — the exit check is your data.
