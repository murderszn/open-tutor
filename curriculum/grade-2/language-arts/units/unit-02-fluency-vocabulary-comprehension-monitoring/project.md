# Project — Fluency Theater and the Describing-Word Wall

**Sessions:** 12 (rehearse) and 14 (perform + gallery) · **Time:** 25–30
minutes each · **Materials:** Passage A, the describing-word wall,
fix-it detective reports, kite purpose cards, crayons, the generated
illustration [fluency-expression-readers.png](assets/fluency-expression-readers.png).

## The idea

The learner becomes a **reader-performer**: rehearse "The Lost Red Kite"
across the week until it can be performed smoothly and with feeling — like
the reader in the illustration, reading *to* someone. Beside the stage
goes a **gallery wall**: the describing-word wall, shade strips, purpose
kite cards, and fix-it detective reports from the unit.

## Part 1 — Rehearse (Session 12)

1. **Three rehearsal readings** of Passage A, logged on paper:
   - Read 1: accuracy — mark any words that still need a fix.
   - Read 2: rate — talking speed, no rushing.
   - Read 3: expression — perform the three dialogue lines with Sam's,
     Lee's, and the narrator's voices.
2. **Fix-it sweep:** run Stop → Check → Reread on any word that wobbled in
   Read 1; log the fix on a detective report.
3. **Wall draft:** finish the describing-word wall — all adjectives and
   adverbs found, shade strips complete, every word labeled.
4. **Dress rehearsal:** perform once for a stuffed listener (or the dog in
   the illustration). The adult scores with the
   [fluency rubric](teacher-guide.md#fluency-rubric) and notes one glow and
   one grow.

## Part 2 — Perform and gallery (Session 14)

1. **The performance:** the learner reads Passage A aloud for a real
   audience of at least one (adult, family member, or video for family).
   Successive readings from Part 1 count — this is Read 4 or 5, so it
   should sound smooth.
2. **Gallery tour:** the learner walks the audience through the wall —
   points to three describing words and uses each in a sentence about the
   story; explains one shade strip; reads one fix-it detective report.
3. **Audience question:** the audience asks one *who/what/where/when/why*
   question about the passage; the learner answers from the text.
4. **Self-score:** the learner scores their own performance with the
   fluency rubric (adult confirms) and names one thing that improved since
   Read 1.

## Deliverables

- Rehearsal log (three dated readings with accuracy/rate/expression notes)
- Performance of Passage A (live or recorded)
- Completed describing-word wall with shade strips and labels
- One fix-it detective report and one purpose kite card on display

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (3) | Growing (2) | Starting (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Accuracy** | Reads with 0–2 errors; self-corrects any slip | 3–5 errors; fixes most when prompted | 6+ errors; keeps going past mistakes |
| **Rate** | Conversational speed throughout | Mostly conversational; rushes or drags in spots | Word-by-word or racing |
| **Expression** | Voice matches meaning in dialogue and narration; punctuation honored | Some expression; flat in places | Monotone |
| **Word wall** | 10+ describing words, correctly sorted; 3 shade strips labeled | 6–9 words sorted; 1–2 shade strips | Few words; sorting mixed up |
| **Retell/Q&A** | Answers the audience question with text evidence | Answers with partial evidence | Cannot answer from the text |

**Scoring:** 13–15 = strong finish; 9–12 = growing, celebrate and name one
next step; 5–8 = reteach fluency routines (echo reading, successive reads)
before Unit 03. The rubric is a teaching tool, not a grade — see
[teacher-guide](teacher-guide.md#using-the-rubric).
