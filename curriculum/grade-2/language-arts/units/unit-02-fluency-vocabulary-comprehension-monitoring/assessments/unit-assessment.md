# Unit Assessment — Unit 02: Fluency, Vocabulary, and Comprehension Monitoring

**Session:** 16 · **Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Format:** adult-led; the
learner reads the passages aloud (fluency items) and answers orally or in
writing — adult scribes as needed. Both passages below are **unseen**:
written for this assessment, never used in lessons.
**Answers:** [answer-key](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment).

## Passage C — "Mole and the Puddle" (story)

> Mole peeked out of his dark hole. Plip! Plop! Rain was falling.
> A big puddle grew by the old oak tree. It looked like a bright mirror.
> Mole had never seen his own face. He leaned over the puddle. "Hello!" he
> said to the mole in the water.
> The other mole moved just like him. Mole laughed and laughed.
> Then his friend Squirrel ran up. "Come play!" she said.
> Mole jumped in the puddle. Splash! They both got wet.
> "Rainy days are the best days," Mole said.

## Passage D — "Why Birds Sing" (informational)

> Birds sing in the morning. Their loud, cheerful songs fill the air.
> A bird sings to **mark** its home. The song says, "This is my tree!"
> A bird also sings to call a mate. The sweetest singer may find a partner.
> Some birds copy sounds. A mockingbird can **mimic** a car alarm or a
> phone ring.
> Birds do not sing when they are **eager** to sleep. They rest at night.
> In the spring, more birds sing than in winter. Listen for them at
> **dawn**.

## Questions

**Fluency — read Passage C aloud (items 1–3, adult observes)**

1. Read the whole passage aloud. (Adult counts accuracy errors.)
2. Read it a second time at talking speed. (Adult notes rate.)
3. Read the dialogue lines ("Hello!", "Come play!", "Rainy days are the
   best days,") with the feeling each character would have. (Adult notes
   expression.)

**Purpose and monitoring (items 4–5)**

4. Before reading Passage D, the learner states a purpose: "I want to find
   out **why** birds sing." After reading, did the reading answer it? What
   is the answer? (Yes — to mark its home and to call a mate.)
5. The adult reads this line from Passage C with a planted error: "A big
   **puzzle** grew by the old oak tree." The learner catches it, states the
   fix (*puddle*), and names the clues. (Letters: p-u-d-d-l-e; sense: rain
   makes puddles, and the next sentences are about the puddle.)

**Context clues (items 6–7)**

6. "A bird sings to **mark** its home. The song says, 'This is my tree!'"
   What does *mark* mean here, and what are your clue words? (To claim or
   show ownership — "this is my home"; clue: the quoted song "This is my
   tree!")
7. "A mockingbird can **mimic** a car alarm or a phone ring." What does
   *mimic* mean, and what are your clue words? (To copy or imitate sounds;
   clue: "copy sounds" in the sentence before.)

**Describing words (items 8–9)**

8. Find two adjectives in Passage D and tell what each describes. (*loud*
   songs, *cheerful* songs, *sweetest* singer, *old* — any two with their
   nouns.)
9. Order these shades from lightest to darkest: *tired, exhausted, sleepy*.
   (*sleepy → tired → exhausted*.)

**Retell (items 10–12)**

10. The adult reads Passage D aloud once. The learner states the big idea
    in one sentence. (It tells why birds sing.)
11. The learner tells back two key details in order. (They sing to mark
    home / to call a mate; mockingbirds copy sounds; they rest at night —
    any two in order.)
12. Answer from the text: **when** do more birds sing — spring or winter?
    (*Spring* — "In the spring, more birds sing than in winter.")

## Scoring guide

- Items 1–3: use the [fluency rubric](../teacher-guide.md#fluency-rubric);
  strong = 0–2 errors, conversational rate, matched expression.
- Items 4–12: 1 point each for a correct answer with evidence or reasoning
  (9 points). 8–9 = strong; 6–7 = growing (reteach the missed skill the
  same week); 5 or fewer = reteach the unit's core routines before Unit 03
  and use the [teacher-guide](../teacher-guide.md#reteach-signals).
- This is a teaching check, not a standardized or validated test.
