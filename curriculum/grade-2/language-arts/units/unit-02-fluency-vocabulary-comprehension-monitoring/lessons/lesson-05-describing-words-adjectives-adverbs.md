# Lesson 05 — Words That Describe: Adjectives, Adverbs, and Shades of Meaning

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can find describing words in what I read, sort
them, and pick the one with just the right strength."
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** Passages A and B
([unit README](../README.md)); word-wall poster or wall space; word cards.
**Standards:** L.2.6.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult says: "a kite." Then: "a **red** kite." Then: "a **big red**
kite flying **high**." Ask: "Which kite can you see best in your head?"
(The last one.) "The extra words are **describing words** — they paint the
picture. Today we collect them."

## Explicit teaching

**Adjectives** describe nouns — people, places, things: *red* kite, *tall*
grass, *windy* day, *messy* knot, *bright* mirror. Ask: *which one? what
kind? how many?*

**Adverbs** describe verbs — actions — and often end in **-ly**: the kite
flew *smoothly*, Sam smiled *proudly*, Lee ran *quickly*. Ask: *how? when?
where?*

Writers choose describing words the way painters choose colors — and
near-synonyms come in **shades of meaning**, light to dark:

- *walk → stroll → march* (slow and easy → brisk and proud)
- *happy → glad → joyful → thrilled* (mild → bursting)
- *fast → quick → swift* (quick is a snap; swift is smooth and gliding)

A *gust* is not just wind — it is a **sudden** burst. *Sudden* is a shade
stronger than *quick*. Picking the right shade is a writer's power move.

### Modeled example 1 — sorting from Passage A

*The adult writes six words on cards: red, windy, tall, smiled, looked,
quickly.*

"Sort: describing words vs. action words. **Adjectives** — *red* (which
kite?), *windy* (what kind of day?), *tall* (what kind of grass?). **Verb +
adverb** — *smiled* is the action; how did he smile? The passage says 'Sam
smiled' — but we can add an adverb: smiled **proudly**. *Quickly* describes
how Lee ran — adverb. *Looked* is the action Sam did — verb."

### Modeled example 2 — shades of meaning

*The adult draws a shades strip: light → dark.*

"*Happy, glad, joyful.* Which is strongest? Let's test: 'Sam was **happy**
his kite flew.' Fine. 'Sam was **joyful** his kite flew.' — bigger feeling,
arms in the air! *Joyful* is the darkest shade. When Sam gets his kite
back after losing it, which shade fits best?" (*Joyful* — the feeling is
big.) "Shades are not right-or-wrong; they are *which picture do you
want?*"

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. From Passage B, find two adjectives. (*strong, invisible, flat, gusty,
   best, sudden, messy, bright, windy* — any two.)
2. From Passage B, find the verb *moves* in "It moves air fast." Which word
   tells **how**? (*fast* — adverb.)
3. **Sort race:** cards *green, dirty, tied, best, blew, gently* → adjectives
   (*green, dirty, best*), verbs (*tied, blew*), adverb (*gently*).
4. **Shade order:** put *walk, stroll, march* in order from slowest to
   fastest. (walk → stroll → march.)
5. **Error-analysis:** the adult says "'*Quickly* is an adjective because it
   describes the kite.'" ("No — *quickly* describes **how** something moves,
   an action. It is an adverb. The -ly ending is the clue.")

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

1. Hunt Passage A for 4 adjectives; write or dictate each with the noun it
   describes ("red kite").
2. Hunt both passages for 2 adverbs (or adverb-like *how* words: *fast*,
   *up, up, up* counts as describing how it rose — accept with discussion).
3. Start the **describing-word wall**: place the 6+ found words under
   ADJECTIVES and ADVERBS columns.
4. Pick one adjective from the wall and upgrade its shade: *big → huge*,
   *happy → joyful*, *windy → gusty*. Use it in a sentence about the kite.
5. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "Why did the author write 'sudden burst of
   wind' instead of just 'wind'?" (Because *sudden* paints the surprise —
   gusts come fast and you feel them; plain 'wind' does not paint that.)

## Application

"Shade strips." The learner makes three shade strips on the word wall —
HAPPY (*glad → happy → joyful → thrilled*), FAST (*slow → quick → fast →
swift*), LOUD (*quiet → loud → noisy → deafening*) — with a tiny drawing
for each end. These strips stay up for the project gallery.

## Exit check

The adult says three words: *brightly, tall, carefully*. The learner sorts
each into adjective or adverb and uses one in a sentence about Passage A or
B. (*brightly* — adverb; *tall* — adjective; *carefully* — adverb.)

## Supports and alternate response modes

- The learner may sort by placing cards under picture-labeled columns
  (a paint palette for adjectives, a running shoe for adverbs).
- Adult scribes all wall words; the learner's job is finding and sorting.
- For shades, act each word out (walk vs. march) instead of ordering on paper.

## Extension

The learner rewrites one Passage A sentence twice — once with "light"
describing words ("Sam walked to the park") and once with "dark" shades
("Sam **marched** to the park, **eager**") — and explains how the feeling
changed.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: every -ly word is an adverb and every describing
word is an adjective. Note the friendly exceptions later (e.g., *friendly*
is an adjective); for grade 2, the -ly test plus the "does it describe a
thing or an action?" question is enough. Answers: see
[answer-key](../answer-key.md#lesson-05).
