# Lesson 05 — Word Parts: Prefixes and Suffixes

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can strip the parts off a long word, read the root, and figure out what the whole word means."
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** the unit word sets (prefix/suffix
lists); word cards; scissors or fold-lines to physically "strip" parts.
**Standards:** RF.2.3.d, L.2.4.b.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

"Say *happy*. Now say *unhappy*." (The learner adds *un*-.) "What changed
about the meaning?" (It flipped — not happy.) "The tiny part *un*- is a
**prefix** — it rides on the FRONT of a word and changes its meaning.
Today we collect front-riders and back-riders."

## Explicit teaching

Many long words are built from parts. The **root word** is the main word
you already know (*happy, tell, help, play*). A **prefix** attaches to the
front; a **suffix** attaches to the back.

Grade-2 prefixes:
- **un-** means "not" or "the opposite": *unhappy* (not happy), *unkind*,
  *untie* (do the opposite of tie), *unlock*.
- **re-** means "again": *retell* (tell again), *redo, replay, rewrite*.
- **pre-** means "before": *preview* (view before), *preschool, preheat*.

Grade-2 suffixes:
- **-ful** means "full of": *helpful* (full of help), *careful, thankful*.
- **-less** means "without": *hopeless* (without hope), *fearless, careless*.
- **-ed** marks past time: *played, wanted, hopped, jumped*.
- **-ing** marks happening now: *playing, running, singing, reading*.

The **strip-and-read** strategy: (1) strip the prefix or suffix, (2) read
the root you know, (3) add the part's meaning, (4) check in a sentence.

### Modeled example 1 — unhappy

*Adult writes* unhappy *and thinks aloud, covering* un- *with a finger:*
"Front-rider! Strip it: *un*- … the root is *happy*, I know that word.
*un*- means 'not'. Put it together: *unhappy* = not happy. Check: 'She
was unhappy when the rain came.' Yes — not happy fits."

### Modeled example 2 — retell, then careful

*Adult writes* retell: "Strip the front: *re*- … root *tell*. *re*- means
'again' — *retell* = tell again. 'Can you retell the story?' — tell it
again. Fits."
*Adult writes* careful: "Back-rider this time: -*ful* … root *care*.
-*ful* means 'full of' — *careful* = full of care. 'Be careful with the
glass.' — full of care. Fits."
"Front or back, the trick is the same: strip, read the root, add the
part's meaning."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. "Strip *unkind*. Read the root. What does the whole word mean?"
   (*un* + *kind* = not kind.)
2. "Strip *replay*." (*re* + *play* = play again.)
3. "Strip *fearless*." (*fear* + *less* = without fear.) "Is a fearless
   kitten scared?" (No — without fear.)
4. "Strip *jumped*. What time does it tell?" (Past — -*ed*.) "Now *singing*?"
   (Happening now — -*ing*.)
5. **Error-analysis:** the adult reads *preview* as "pre-vee-oo," sounding
   out every letter. "Is there a faster way?" (Strip *pre*-, read *view*,
   blend: *preview*.) "What does it mean?" (View before.)

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

1. Strip and read 6 word cards, saying "part + root = meaning" for each:
   *untie, rewrite, thankful, hopeless, wanted, reading*.
2. Build with tiles: add *un*- to *lock, kind, happy*; add -*ful* to
   *help, care*. Read each new word.
3. "Which prefix means 'again'? Write 2 new words with it." (*re*-:
   *redo, replay* — or any real words.)
4. Sentence check — does the meaning fit? "The careful driver went fast
   and crashed." (No — *careful* = full of care; a careful driver goes
   slow.) Fix the sentence: "The careless driver went fast and crashed."
5. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "How did you figure out *preschool*
   without ever being taught it?" (Strip *pre*- = before; *school* =
   school; before school.)

## Application

"Word-part workshop." The learner gets 6 root cards (*tie, tell, help,
hope, play, care*) and part cards (*un-, re-, pre-, -ful, -less, -ed,
-ing*), and builds as many real words as possible in 5 minutes, reading
each and stating its meaning ("*re* + *tie* = *retie* — tie again").
Non-words are rejected at the "real-word check." The 8 best go on the
workshop wall (fridge, door) for the review week.

## Exit check

1. "What does *un*- mean?" (Not / opposite.)
2. Strip *unlock* and tell the meaning. (*un* + *lock* = do the opposite of lock — open.)
3. "Is -*ful* a prefix or a suffix?" (Suffix — back-rider.)
4. "What does *preheat* mean?" (Heat before — *pre*- = before.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** act out *untie* (tie a shoe, untie it), *rewrite*
  (write, write again) before any print.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may physically cover the part
  with a finger or fold the card, then read the root, then the whole word.
- **Seated-table variant:** two trays — FRONT-RIDERS and BACK-RIDERS —
  sort part cards before building.
- **Floor-play variant:** the learner stands on a PART mat, jumps to a
  ROOT mat, and lands saying the whole word and its meaning.
- **Extension:** find prefixed/suffixed words in a picture book (6);
  predict the meaning of an untaught one (*dislike, kindly*) and check
  with the adult.

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Reading the part as its own word:* "*un-hap-py*" with equal stress —
  model the natural stress: "un-HAP-py. The root keeps the beat."
- *Every word has parts:* "*uncle*" is not *un* + *cle*. The real-word
  check catches fakes: "Is *cle* a word you know? Then *un*- is not a
  prefix here."
- *-ed's three sounds:* *played* (/d/), *wanted* (/ĭd/), *hopped* (/t/) —
  name all three; the meaning (past) never changes.
- *Answer-key reference:* see [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-05) —
  all guided/independent items solved and reconciled.
