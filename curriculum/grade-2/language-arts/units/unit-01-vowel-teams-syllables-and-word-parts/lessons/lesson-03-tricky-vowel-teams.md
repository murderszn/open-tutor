# Lesson 03 — Tricky Teams: igh, oa/ow, oo, ou/ow

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can read the vowel teams that have three letters or two sounds — and I know which sound to try first."
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** the unit word sets; the boat card
from [`assets/vowel-team-friends.png`](../assets/vowel-team-friends.png);
letter tiles.
**Standards:** RF.2.3.b, RF.2.3.e.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Show the boat card. "Boat — b-ōōōt. The team is **oa**, saying /ō/."
Now the adult says *snow* and *cow*: "Same two letters at the end — **ow**
— but different sounds! /ō/ in *snow*, /ou/ in *cow*. Today we meet the
teams with a split personality. Rule: try the first sound you learned;
if the word is not a real word, flip to the other sound."

## Explicit teaching

Four more teams, each with its own quirk:

- **igh** — three letters, one sound: /ī/, as in *light, night, bright,
  high, sigh*. The gh is always quiet. Think: "i says its name, gh
  says nothing."
- **oa** usually sits in the middle (*boat, road, coat*); **ow** usually
  sits at the end — and **ow** has two sounds: /ō/ in *snow, show, grow,
  yellow*, and /ou/ in *cow, down, brown*. Same seat rule as ai/ay, plus
  a sound flip.
- **oo** also has two sounds: the long "moon" sound /oo/ in *moon, soon,
  room, food*, and the short "book" sound /ŏŏ/ in *book, look, foot*.
- **ou** says /ou/ in *cloud, house, out*. Notice: **ou** and **ow** can
  spell the same sound — *house* and *cow* rhyme!

Reading strategy for two-sound teams — **try and flip**: try the first
sound; blend; ask "Is that a real word?" If not, flip to the second
sound and blend again. Real readers do this silently every day.

### Modeled example 1 — night

*Adult writes* night *and thinks aloud:*
"Three-letter team — **igh**. The gh is quiet, so the team says /ī/.
/n/ /ī/ /t/ … *night*, when it is dark. One sound from three letters —
that is the igh trick."

### Modeled example 2 — cow (try and flip)

*Adult writes* cow: "Team **ow** at the end. First try: /ō/ — /k/ /ō/ …
'koe'? Not a real word. Flip: /ou/ — /k/ /ou/ … *cow*! The animal that
says moo. Trying the wrong sound first is not a mistake — it is the
strategy working."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. "Read *light*." (Spot **igh** — /ī/ — *light*.) "Which letters are quiet?" (g and h.)
2. "Read *coat*." (**oa** in the middle — *coat*.) "Now *show*." (**ow** at the end, first try /ō/ — *show*.)
3. "Read *brown* with try-and-flip." (First try /ō/ → "brone"? Not a word. Flip → *brown*.)
4. "Read *moon*. Now read *book*." (Both **oo** — different sounds. "Two sounds, one team.")
5. **Error-analysis:** the adult reads *house* as "hose." "Did I read that
   right?" (No — **ou** says /ou/: *house*.) "Which team did I skip?" (ou.)

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

1. Sort 8 word cards into team trays — igh, oa/ow, oo, ou: *night, boat,
   moon, cloud, bright, snow, foot, house*.
2. Build with tiles, then read: *high, grow, room, out*. Mark each team's
   sound (moon-oo or book-oo; snow-ow or cow-ow) with a dot the adult draws.
3. Read these two original sentences: "The bright light is on the boat at
   night." / "The brown cow ran down to the house."
4. Try-and-flip drill (ear + print): *down, yellow, soon, look* — say both
   tries aloud for the two-sound teams before settling.
5. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "Why does *ow* need try-and-flip but *igh*
   does not?" (*igh* always says /ī/; *ow* spells two different sounds.)

## Application

"Two-sound detective sheet." The learner draws two columns — SNOW-OW and
COW-OW, MOON-OO and BOOK-OO — and sorts 8 new words from the unit word
sets (*show, grow, yellow, down, brown, soon, food, look*) into the four
columns, reading each aloud with its try-and-flip. The adult checks each
row like a chief inspector stamping "SOLVED." The sheet stays in the word
tray for the review week.

## Exit check

1. Read *sigh*. (Three-letter team **igh** — *sigh*.)
2. Read *down*. (Flip to /ou/ — *down*.)
3. "Name the two sounds of *oo*." (Moon-oo and book-oo.)
4. "In *yellow*, which sound does *ow* make?" (/ō/ — the snow sound.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** act out the two-sound flip with a hand gesture —
  palm up for the first try, flip the hand for the second — before any print.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may say both tries and point to
  the one that makes a real word, then blend it.
- **Seated-table variant:** a two-column "try / flip" mat under each
  two-sound word card.
- **Floor-play variant:** two floor spots labeled TRY and FLIP — the
  learner stands on TRY, says the first blend, then steps to FLIP.
- **Extension:** hunt *ow* and *oo* words in a picture book and tally
  which sound each one uses; predict which sound is more common (oo:
  moon-sound wins in most early texts).

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Flipping feels like failing:* normalize it — "Every good reader tries
  and flips. The flip IS the skill."
- *gh pronounced:* "*ni-guh-t*" — remind: "In the igh team, gh is always
  quiet. Cross it out with your finger."
- *ou vs. ow for the /ou/ sound:* for reading it does not matter — both
  spell /ou/. Spelling picks later (usually *ou* in the middle, *ow* at
  the end).
