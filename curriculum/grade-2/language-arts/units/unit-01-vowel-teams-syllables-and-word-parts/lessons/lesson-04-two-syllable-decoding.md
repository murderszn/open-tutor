# Lesson 04 — Clap It Apart: Reading Two-Syllable Words

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can break a long word into syllables, read each part, and put it back together."
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** the unit word sets (two-syllable
list); word cards; two small boxes or trays labeled PART 1 and PART 2.
**Standards:** RF.2.3.c.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

"Say your name. Now clap its beats." The adult claps the learner's name
and counts: "Two claps — two beats — two **syllables**." Then: "*robot*."
(Clap: ro-bot — 2.) "*pencil*." (pen-cil — 2.) "Every beat you clap is one
syllable, and every syllable has exactly one vowel sound. That is why
breaking words into beats makes long words readable."

## Explicit teaching

A **syllable** is one beat of a word — one clap, one vowel sound. Big
words are just small words holding hands. The **clap-and-break** strategy:

1. **Clap** the beats and count them: *sun-set* (2), *rab-bit* (2).
2. **Break** — cover all but the first beat and read it: *sun*.
3. Read the second beat: *set*.
4. **Blend** the beats: *sunset* — "a sunset! The sun going down."
5. **Check** — is it a real word? Does it make sense?

Most two-syllable words break between two consonants in the middle
(*rab-bit, pic-nic, bas-ket, nap-kin*), or before a single middle
consonant where the first vowel is long (*ro-bot, ti-ger, pa-per, o-pen*).
You do not need the rule's name — clap, break, read, blend, check.

### Modeled example 1 — sunset

*Adult writes* sunset *and thinks aloud, clapping:*
"Clap it: sun-set — two beats. Cover the second half: *sun* — easy.
Now the second beat: *set*. Blend: *sunset*. Check: real word — the sun
going down. Two easy words holding hands."

### Modeled example 2 — robot

*Adult writes* robot: "Clap: ro-bot — two beats. First beat: *ro* — the
o is long, /rō/. Second beat: *bot*. Blend: *robot*. Check: a robot —
real word. Notice the first beat used a long vowel — that is why Lessons
01–03 came first."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. "Clap *picnic*." (pic-nic — 2.) "Break and read each beat, then blend." (*picnic*.)
2. "Clap *basket*." (bas-ket.) "Read the beats." (*basket*.)
3. "Clap *tiger*." (ti-ger.) "Which beat has the long vowel?" (First — *ti*, /tī/.)
4. **Cover-and-reveal:** the adult covers *-bit* in *rabbit*; the learner
   reads *rab*, then the reveal, then blends: *rabbit*.
5. **Error-analysis:** the adult reads *lemon* as "le-mon" with a long o
   ("lee-mon"). "Did I read that right?" (No — the o is short here:
   *lem-on*, the fruit.) "What should I have done?" (Clap-and-break, then
   check — 'lee-mon' is not a word.)

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

1. Clap-and-break 6 word cards, sorting into the PART 1 / PART 2 trays as
   you go: *pocket, napkin, window, pencil, music, paper*.
2. Read each word's beats separately, then blend. Point to the vowel team
   or long vowel in each first beat (*pocket* — o; *music* — u…).
3. "Which of these has a long vowel in the first beat: *sunset, robot,
   rabbit*?" (*robot* — /rō/.)
4. Read this original passage (adult points under each word; clap-and-break
   any stuck word):
   > "Lemon is a small robot. The robot can run and hop. At sunset, Lemon
   > sits by the window and hums a happy song."
5. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "Why does breaking a word into beats make it
   easier to read?" (Each beat is short — one vowel sound — so you only
   ever read a small word at a time.)

## Application

"Beat cards." The learner picks 6 two-syllable words from the unit word
sets, writes each on a card split into beats (*bas|ket* — adult scribes
if needed), and draws one quick picture for each word's meaning on the
back. The learner shuffles the cards, reads them beat-by-beat to the
adult, and the adult guesses the picture from the reading. The beat
cards join the word tray for the review-week decoding races.

## Exit check

1. Clap the beats in *follow*. (fol-low — 2.)
2. Read *open* with clap-and-break. (*o-pen* — long o in the first beat.)
3. "How many vowel sounds are in *basket*?" (Two — one per beat.)
4. "What do you do after you blend the beats?" (Check — is it a real word?)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** clap familiar two-beat words from the learner's
  life first (their street, a favorite food) before word-list words.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may clap and say only the
  beats, with the adult doing the final blend, then swap roles.
- **Seated-table variant:** slide a card or ruler to cover one beat at a
  time instead of pointing.
- **Floor-play variant:** two floor hoops — the learner steps into hoop 1
  to read beat 1, hoop 2 for beat 2, then jumps to blend.
- **Extension:** hunt two-syllable words in a picture book (8 examples);
  mark which ones break between two consonants and which break before
  one consonant.

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Reading straight through without breaking:* if the learner guesses
  from the first letters, return the cover: "Beats first. Cover, read,
  blend."
- *One beat read, one beat guessed:* insist both beats get sounded out —
  the blend only works from two real reads.
- *Clapping the letters, not the beats:* "Clap what you HEAR, not what
  you see."
