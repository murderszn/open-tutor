# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Vowel Teams, Syllables, and Word Parts

Adult-facing. Learner materials never link here with answers visible; keep
this file and [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) out of the learner's reach.

## How this unit runs

Four 25–30-minute sessions per week. Every session opens with the same
30-second ritual — "reading hands ready?" (tiles or pointer in hands, eyes
on the words) — and closes with one sentence of specific praise tied to
the objective ("You flipped that ow team like a detective"). Short, warm,
and repetitive beats long and novel at this age.

**Pacing judgment:** the [16-session pacing](README.md#16-session-pacing)
alternates lesson and practice days. If a lesson runs long, move its
application to the next practice session rather than rushing the exit
check — the exit check is your data.

**Sound-before-print rule:** Lessons 01–03 begin with the ear. When a task
is about hearing a sound (long/short sorts, the friend-card game), cover
or remove print; bring print back for the spot-say-blend work. Children
who peek at spelling during ear tasks learn to guess from letters instead
of listening.

**The three moves that carry the unit:** (1) *spot–say–blend* for vowel
teams, (2) *try-and-flip* for two-sound teams, (3) *strip–read–define* for
word parts, plus *clap-and-break* for long words. Name the moves every
time — the learner should be able to recite them by Session 13.

## Session-by-session guidance

- **Sessions 1–2 (long/short review):** keep the quiet-e pattern visual —
  point at the e while saying "bossy but silent." If the learner reads the
  e aloud, cover it with a finger until the habit breaks.
- **Sessions 3–4 (ai/ay, ee/ea):** hammer the seat rule (ai middle, ay
  end) with the friend cards. Do not introduce spelling yet — reading
  only.
- **Sessions 5–6 (tricky teams):** celebrate the flip. If the learner is
  embarrassed by a wrong first try, model it yourself first: "Watch me
  get it wrong on purpose — koe? … flip! … cow!"
- **Sessions 7–8 (two-syllable):** enforce all five clap-and-break steps
  for two weeks, then let the check become silent and quick. Never skip
  the check entirely — it is the self-correction habit.
- **Sessions 9–10 (prefixes/suffixes):** act out the parts (*untie* a
  shoe) before print. The meaning step matters as much as the reading
  step — always ask "so what does it mean?"
- **Sessions 11–12 (compounds, tricky words, project part 1):** start the
  tricky word wall in Session 11 and read it at the start of every
  remaining session. Project drafting is reading work — every casebook
  word gets spot-say-blend before it goes on the page.
- **Session 13 (review games):** let the learner lead the sort. Your job
  is to ask "how did you know?" after correct answers — reasoning spoken
  aloud is what transfers.
- **Session 14 (project part 2 + share):** the teach-back is the real
  assessment. If the learner cannot name the three steps, the project is
  not done — rehearse once more before the share counts.
- **Session 15 (quiz + reteach):** quiz first, then re-teach misses
  immediately (see below). End with a win — re-ask one fixed item and
  praise the fix by name.
- **Session 16 (unit assessment):** score against the answer key the same
  day. Record the reteach list for Unit 02's first week.

## Session 15 — quiz and reteach

| Missed quiz item | Reteach move (same session) |
|---|---|
| 1–2 (team reading) | Re-run the Lesson 02/03 sort with 6 fresh words; learner names team before blending |
| 3 (two-syllable) | Cover-and-reveal with 4 new words; insist on all five clap-and-break steps |
| 4 (word parts) | Physical strip — fold the card over the part; "part + root = meaning" × 4 |
| 5 (friend cards) | Ear game: adult says a word, learner points to the friend with the same team |
| 6 (tricky words) | Add misses to the wall; rapid-fire flash 3 rounds |
| 7 (compounds) | Build 3 new compounds from part cards; guess-then-check each meaning |
| 8 (dictation) | Tile the word, say the team, write it, read it back — 4 words |
| 9 (try-and-flip) | Model 3 flips aloud, then learner leads 3 |
| 10 (strategy) | Learner teaches YOU the three steps with a fresh word |

## Common misconceptions (whole unit)

- *Two letters must make two sounds* → "The team is one sound. Cover it
  with a finger, say the sound, blend."
- *Flipping means failing* → "The flip IS the strategy. Every good reader
  flips."
- *Tricky words can be sounded out with effort* → "They break the
  patterns. Wall + repetition."
- *Prefixes are separate words* → "The root keeps the beat: un-HAP-py."
- *Compound meanings are always literal* → "Guess, then check."

## Accessibility and differentiation

All lessons include language-access, alternate-response, seated-table, and
floor-play variants plus an extension. For learners needing more: halve
the word lists, double the ear games, and keep every response oral or
pointed. For learners racing ahead: the lesson extensions add book hunts,
tally work, and untaught-word prediction — never push Unit 02 content
early. If sessions consistently run over 30 minutes, split one lesson
across two sessions rather than dropping the exit check.
