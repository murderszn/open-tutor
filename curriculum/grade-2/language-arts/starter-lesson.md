# Starter Lesson — Find a topic and supporting details

**Status:** draft supplement. **Time:** three 25-minute sessions. **Goal:** Identify the main topic of a short original informational text, distinguish details, and write or dictate a short explanation.

## Adult preparation and readiness

Use the passage and pencil. Readiness: ask learner to recall one fact after a short read-aloud. Read aloud as needed; the purpose is comprehension, not a decoding assessment.

## Original passage: “How a Sidewalk Tree Helps”

> A tree beside a sidewalk can give people shade on a sunny day. Its roots take in water from the soil. Leaves use sunlight, air, and water to make food for the tree. Birds may rest in its branches. A tree needs enough space for its roots and branches, so people plan carefully before planting one near a path.

## Teach and model

Explain: the **topic** is what the whole passage is mostly about; **details** tell more about that topic. Model asking which idea fits every sentence, not just one.

**Worked example 1:** Topic = ways a sidewalk tree helps and what it needs. “A tree gives shade” is a detail because it tells one way it helps.

**Worked example 2:** “A tree needs room for roots and branches” is a supporting detail about planning. “All birds live in trees” is not supported by the passage.

## Pacing and tasks

Session 1: adult reads and models topic vs detail; guided tasks 1–2. Session 2: learner highlights/details tasks 3–4 with adult check. Session 3: write/dictate paragraph, error check, and exit response.

1. **Guided:** Choose the best topic: sidewalk trees / all birds / kinds of sidewalks.
2. **Guided:** Name one thing the tree does or needs, such as providing shade or taking in water through roots.
3. **Independent:** Underline two details that explain something the tree does or needs.
4. **Independent:** Mark “supported” or “not supported”: roots take in water; birds may rest in branches; every sidewalk tree is large.
5. **Adult-guided error check:** A reader says the passage is only about birds because birds appear once. Ask which topic covers more sentences.
6. **Application:** Complete “A sidewalk tree can ___, and it needs ___.” Use two passage details.

**Answer checks:** 1) sidewalk trees; 2) answers may name shade, roots taking in water, leaves making food, or birds resting; 3) any two accurate; 4) supported, supported, not stated; 5) trees are the broader topic; 6) sentence uses one accurate detail about what the tree does and one need.

## Exit check and product

Learner gives a topic and two details. Product: a 3–4 sentence explanatory paragraph; dictated sentence sequence is acceptable, with adult scribing kept private.

## Review rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Topic | Names an idea that fits the whole passage | Chooses from prompts | Reread and compare headings |
| Details | Selects two stated facts | Selects one with support | Reread one sentence at a time |
| Explanation | Connects topic and details in organized language | Gives oral list | Use sentence frame |

Use read-aloud, large print, chunking, bilingual vocabulary, drawing, or dictation. This passage is original, so the activity works offline. Do not infer a learner's reading level from one task; seek systematic instruction for decoding needs.
