## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-2 social studies course organized around the
four disciplines of the C3 Framework inquiry arc: **geography** (map tools,
directions, community maps, landforms, environments, and how people move and
meet their needs), **history** (timelines, comparing past and present, local
history, historical sources, and multiple viewpoints), **civics** (rules,
leadership, democratic principles, discussion and voting, and improving the
community), and **economics** (scarcity and choices, work and income, prices,
saving, and trade). Every unit is driven by a compelling question, supported by
guided questions, evidence from one or two age-appropriate sources, and a
simple argument or explanation the learner communicates orally, in drawings, or
with adult-scribed writing. Sessions are 25–30 minutes, about four per week,
built on read-alouds, picture-based discussion, hands-on maps and timelines,
role play, a classroom "market," and community observation walks with an adult.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. **Use geographic tools:** construct a simple map of a familiar place with a
   title, symbols, a key, a compass rose, and labels; name the cardinal
   directions; use maps, globes, and photographs to describe places and how
   people use them.
2. **Connect people and environment:** explain how weather, landforms, and
   other environmental characteristics affect daily life in a place; describe
   how people change places and how places shape people's choices.
3. **Do history with timelines and evidence:** create a chronological sequence
   of events; compare life in the past to life today; identify different kinds
   of historical sources (photographs, objects, interviews); ask questions
   about individuals and groups who shaped a significant change; compare
   different accounts of the same event.
4. **Practice citizenship:** explain the need for and purposes of rules in
   settings inside and outside school; describe the roles of people in
   authority; apply civic virtues (equality, fairness, respect) in school
   settings; use listening, discussion, consensus, and voting to make group
   decisions.
5. **Make economic choices:** explain how scarcity necessitates decision making;
   identify benefits and costs of personal decisions; describe the skills and
   knowledge needed to produce goods and services; explain how people earn
   income; identify prices in a local market; explain why people save; describe
   why people trade with people in other places.
6. **Understand communities and movement:** explain why and how people, goods,
   and ideas move from place to place; identify cultural and environmental
   characteristics of communities; compare their own point of view with the
   perspectives of others.
7. **Gather evidence and communicate conclusions:** with adult support, gather
   relevant information from one or two sources; tell fact from opinion;
   construct a simple argument with reasons and an explanation in correct
   sequence; present a summary orally, with drawings, or with digital tools.
8. **Take informed action:** describe how people have tried to improve their
   communities over time; identify ways to help address a local problem; carry
   out a small, adult-supervised evidence-based community project.
