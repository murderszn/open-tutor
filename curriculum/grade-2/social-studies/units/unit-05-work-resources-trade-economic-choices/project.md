# Project — Maple Lane Market Day

**Product (one stall plan, reviewed the same day):** a market stall plan
with a price list, a save-and-spend plan, and a trade card — presented
orally with drawings.

## What the learner makes

1. **Stall plan and price list (Session 11).** The learner chooses 3
   goods or services to sell at their own Maple Lane market stall and
   writes (or the adult scribes) the price list using the unit's fixed
   prices (apple 2 · bread 3 · flowers 2 · book 4 · toy car 5 · haircut
   3). The plan also names the producer of each item, one skill the
   producer needs, and one tool the producer uses. Example: "I sell
   bread (3 coins) — the baker makes it; her skill is measuring flour;
   her tool is the oven."
2. **Save-and-spend plan (Session 12).** The learner starts with 10 play
   coins and writes a plan: what they will buy at their own stall (or at
   a neighbor's stall), what it costs, and what they will save. The plan
   must show the addition and the coins left over (e.g., "I buy bread 3
   + flowers 2 = 5 coins. I save 5 coins."). The learner also draws
   their SAVE jar with the saved coins in it.
3. **Trade card (Session 12).** One small card with two sides: one good
   that comes from far away (a banana — from Guatemala, Ecuador, or
   Costa Rica, warm countries far away) and how it travels (truck and
   ship); and one good Maple Lane makes that could travel to another
   town's market (bread, flowers, or a book). The learner also names one
   government service everyone can use (road, school, library, park)
   and says why it matters.
4. **Showcase (Session 15).** The learner presents the stall plan orally
   (or by audio recording): reads the price list, explains the
   save-and-spend plan in order, shows the trade card, and names the
   government service. The adult asks one follow-up question ("What did
   you give up when you chose to save those coins?") and the learner
   answers in their own words.

## Materials

Paper, crayons or colored pencils, the fixed price list card, 10 play
coins, the SAVE jar, the market illustration for reference, picture cards
for the trade sort. Play coins are counted out and counted back in. No
real money is used; no real family money or job is discussed. Learner
work stays in private storage — never in this public repository.

## Steps

1. Adult and learner choose the stall's 3 goods/services together and
   check the prices against the fixed list out loud.
2. Learner draws or places one picture per item and writes (or dictates)
   its price; adult checks each price against the fixed list before the
   learner moves on.
3. Learner writes the save-and-spend plan with 10 coins: what is bought,
   what it costs (addition shown), what is saved (subtraction shown).
4. Learner makes the trade card: far-away good + how it travels on one
   side; Maple Lane good that can travel on the other side; one
   government service named underneath.
5. Learner presents the plan; adult asks one follow-up question and
   records (privately) what was strong and what to re-teach.

## Rubric (8 points; 6+ meets the project bar)

| Criterion (2 points each) | 2 — Strong | 1 — Developing | 0 — Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price list | 3 goods/services with correct fixed prices; producer, skill, and tool named for each | 3 items priced, but one price wrong or a skill/tool missing | Fewer than 3 items, or two or more prices wrong |
| Save-and-spend plan | Buys and saving shown with correct addition and subtraction from 10 coins | Plan shown but one sum wrong, or saving not stated | No plan, or sums do not use 10 coins |
| Trade card | Far-away good named with its home place and travel; Maple Lane good that can travel; one government service named | Far-away good named but travel or home place missing, or government service missing | No far-away good or no Maple Lane good |
| Presentation | Reads the price list, explains the plan in order, shows the trade card, and answers the follow-up question | Presents with heavy prompting, or misses one part | Cannot present the plan |

**Scoring notes for the adult:** Accept oral, pointed, drawn, and
dictated responses equally. A stall with different items scores the same
as the example if the prices match the fixed list. "I don't know" on the
follow-up question scores 1 for that row if the learner tried ("I gave
up the toy car") — naming what was given up is itself good economics
work. Check every sum before scoring: the fixed prices are apple 2,
bread 3, flowers 2, book 4, toy car 5, haircut 3.
