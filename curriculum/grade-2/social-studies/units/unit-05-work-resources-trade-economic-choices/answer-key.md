# Answer Key — Unit 05: Work, Resources, Trade, and Economic Choices

For the guiding adult only. Every practice, quiz, assessment, project, and
lesson exit-check item is solved here independently; the adult re-solves
any item the learner disputes before re-teaching. Accept oral, pointed,
drawn, and dictated answers equally. The Maple Lane market, its prices,
and its play coins are fictional practice material; the banana fact is the
unit's one checked real-world claim (USDA, 2026-11-05).

## Lesson exit checks

- **Lesson 01:** A picture book is a want (accept a reasoned "need" only
  if the learner argues it is the only book for learning to read).
  Scarcity means there is not enough of something for everyone to have
  all they want, so we must choose.
- **Lesson 02:** 10 − 5 = 5 coins left after buying the toy car. Given
  up: any item not bought (book, bread, flowers, apple, or haircut).
- **Lesson 03:** A bus ride is a service. The bus driver is the
  producer; they earn income because they are paid for their work.
- **Lesson 04:** 2 + 3 = 5 coins spent; 10 − 5 = 5 coins left. The
  baker's cost to make one loaf is 2 coins (flour 1 + oven and time 1).
- **Lesson 05:** 3 + 3 = 6 coins after two weeks; 6 − 4 = 2 coins saved.
  Government service: road, school, library, or park (accept
  firefighters/police).

## Practice set A — Week 19 (wants, needs, and choices)

1. Water = need (bodies must have water); toy car = want; warm coat in
   winter = need; flowers = want; food for dinner = need. Each with a
   reason.
2. Scarcity: 2 apples, 3 children — not enough for everyone. Fair fixes:
   share, take turns, choose something else.
3. Toy car (5): benefit — a car to play with; cost — the other items
   given up. Book (4) + apple (2): benefit — reading and a snack; cost —
   the toy car, bread, flowers, or haircut given up.
4. 5 + 4 + 2 = 11 coins, more than 10 — cannot buy all three.
5. Apple 2 + bread 3 + flowers 2 + haircut 3 = 10 coins exactly. Given
   up: the book and the toy car.

## Practice set B — Week 20 (work, prices, and money)

1. Bread = good; haircut = service; picture book = good; bus ride =
   service; flowers = good.
2. Producer: the baker. Consumer: the buyer. Earns income: the baker
   (paid for her work).
3. Any sensible skill/tool pair per worker, e.g., farmer — skill: knowing
   when apples are ripe; tool: ladder or baskets. Teacher — skill:
   knowing how to read and explain; tool: books or a board.
4. (a) Any two of: apples, bread, flowers, picture books. (b) Gold coins.
   (c) A loaf of bread.
5. Cost to make: 1 + 1 = 2 coins. Left over: 3 − 2 = 1 coin.

## Practice set C — Week 21 (saving, trade, and government services)

1. 3 + 3 = 6 coins; 6 − 4 = 2 coins saved.
2. Book + flowers: 4 + 2 = 6 spent, 6 − 6 = 0 saved. Book only: 6 − 4 =
   2 coins saved.
3. Banana = FAR AWAY (travels by truck and ship). Bread, flowers, apple
   = NEAR HERE.
4. A bank keeps money safe and keeps a record of how much each person
   saved. People save to buy something later, or for something important
   later.
5. Government services: road, school, library, park. The loaf of bread
   is a good sold at a market.

## Formative quiz — Week 22 (10 items)

1. Water = need (bodies must have water); toy car = want (fun, but we
   can live without it). Each with a reason.
2. Scarcity: not enough of something for everyone to have all they want,
   so we have to choose.
3. 4 + 5 = 9 coins spent; 10 − 9 = 1 coin left. Given up: any of apple,
   bread, flowers, haircut.
4. Bread = good; haircut = service.
5. The baker makes the bread; she earns income because she is paid for
   her work.
6. Any sensible pair, e.g., farmer — skill: knowing when apples are
   ripe; tool: ladder or baskets.
7. 2 + 3 = 5 coins spent; 10 − 5 = 5 coins left.
8. Cost to make: 1 + 1 = 2 coins; left over: 3 − 2 = 1 coin.
9. 3 + 3 = 6 coins; 6 − 4 = 2 coins saved.
10. Any two of: apples, bread, flowers, picture books. The SAVE jar holds
    gold coins.

## Culminating unit assessment (13 points)

- **Part 1 (3):** (a) Water = need — bodies must have water. (b) Toy car
  = want — fun, but we can live without it. (c) Warm coat in winter =
  need — staying warm and dry keeps us healthy. One point each (sort +
  reason).
- **Part 2 (4):** (a) 4 + 5 = 9 coins spent (1 point); 10 − 9 = 1 coin
  left (1 point). (b) Benefit: a book to read and a car to play with
  (1 point); cost: something given up — apple, bread, flowers, or
  haircut (1 point).
- **Part 3 (3):** (a) Haircut = service. (b) 3 − 2 = 1 coin left over.
  (c) Any sensible baker skill/tool pair (e.g., measuring flour; the
  oven).
- **Part 4 (3):** (a) 6 − 4 = 2 coins saved. (b) Far away: banana;
  near here: bread, flowers, or apple. (c) Road, school, library, or
  park (accept fire truck / firefighters).
