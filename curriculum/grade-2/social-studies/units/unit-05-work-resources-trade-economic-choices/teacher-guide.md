# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Work, Resources, Trade, and Economic Choices

For the guiding adult. Learner-facing materials never include answers; all
solutions live in the [answer key](answer-key.md). Sessions are 25–30
minutes; every session opens with a 2-minute retrieval warm-up. Play coins
are counted out and counted back in every session; no real money is used.

## What "ready to move on" looks like

The learner is ready for Unit 06 when they can, with at most light
prompting: sort wants from needs with a reason; explain scarcity; name a
benefit and a cost of a choice and say what was given up; sort goods from
services; name a producer, a skill, and a tool, and explain income; read
two prices from the fixed list and work the baker's 2-coin cost; explain
Maya's saving (3 + 3 = 6, 6 − 4 = 2); name a far-away good and a
government service. Evidence: project rubric 6+/8, quiz 7+/10, assessment
10+/13 with Part 2(a) and Part 4(a) correct. If the learner is below this,
re-teach with the targeted follow-ups below before Unit 06 — the
play-store routine and the "what did you give up?" question return in
Unit 08, so gaps compound.

## Session-by-session notes

- **Sessions 1–2 (wants/needs/scarcity):** Keep the sort physical — two
  piles, real cards. The "a need is about living and staying healthy and
  safe, not about liking something a lot" correction is the week's
  highest-leverage move. Session 2's Practice set A is oral/pointing/
  drawn; 4 of 5 is the bar.
- **Sessions 3–4 (benefits/costs):** Count real coins on the table for
  every sum. The "the cost is what you give up, not the price you pay"
  correction is the week's highest-leverage move. Session 4's market-play
  choice game: the learner must say the cost out loud before the coins
  move — no silent spending.
- **Sessions 5–6 (work/income):** Name the skill and the tool for every
  worker card; never let "producer" become a label without a product
  behind it. Session 6's Practice set B is oral/pointing/drawn; 4 of 5
  is the bar.
- **Sessions 7–8 (prices/money):** Name the illustration as an
  AI-generated practice picture out loud, every time. Use the text
  alternative at least once even when the picture is used, so the
  alternative stays familiar. The price tags in the picture are blank on
  purpose — prices come only from the fixed price list. Session 8's
  price-tag game: check every tag against the fixed list before play.
- **Sessions 9–10 (saving/trade):** Do Maya's math with real coins in
  the SAVE jar — 3 in, 3 in, 4 out, 2 left. Say the banana fact as a
  checked fact ("grown-ups checked this with farm reports") and the
  play-store numbers as pretend, in the same breath, so the learner
  learns the difference. Session 10's Practice set C is oral/pointing/
  drawn; 4 of 5 is the bar.
- **Sessions 11–12 (project):** Approve every price list *before* the
  save-and-spend plan starts — most stall plans fail on a wrong price,
  not on the plan. The dictated plan is the learner's words; do not
  correct the arithmetic into your own — note errors and re-teach
  instead.
- **Sessions 13–16 (review/product):** Quiz in session 13, reviewed the
  same day; re-teach misses immediately. Session 14's review game:
  market relay (adult calls a price, learner runs to the matching tag)
  and trade sort race (learner sorts far-away vs. near-here cards
  against the clock, then checks their own errors). Session 15 is the
  showcase — the learner presents; applaud the benefit/cost reasoning
  and the saving by name. Session 16's assessment is the last word, not
  a surprise: every item mirrors practiced tasks.

## Misconception bank

| Misconception | Re-teach move |
|---|---|
| A favorite toy is a "need" because I like it a lot | "A need is about living and staying healthy and safe, not about liking something a lot. You can love a toy and still live without it." |
| Scarcity means "there is none left at all" | "Scarcity means *not enough for everyone to have all they want* — there may be some, just not enough for everybody." |
| The cost of a choice is its price ("the cost is 5 coins") | "The coins are what you *pay*. The cost is what you *give up* — the other things you did not buy." |
| "Cost" sounds like a punishment | "Every choice has a cost, even a wonderful choice. Cost is not a bad word — it is just the other side of choosing." |
| The buyer is the producer because "they produce the money" | "A producer makes the good or does the service. Money is what the consumer *pays with*, not what the consumer produces." |
| "Skill" and "tool" are the same thing | "A skill lives in a person — something they learned. A tool is a thing you can hold." |
| Income is free money | "Income is pay for work. People earn it by doing a job." |
| The price *is* the cost of making something | "The price is 3 coins. The cost of *making* it is 2 coins. The 1 coin left over is not the same as the price." |
| Saving means never spending | "Saving means spending *later*, not never. Maya saved so she could buy the book." |
| A bank keeps your money forever and you never get it back | "A bank keeps your money safe *until you need it* — you can take it out." |
| Trade means giving things away for nothing | "Trade is an exchange — both sides get something they want. The banana farmer gets coins; we get bananas." |
| Government services are free because no one pays at the door | "The library is free to *use*, but the town pays for it with money collected from the people who live there. That is why we take care of it." |
| The AI illustration is a photograph of a real market | "This is a pretend practice picture made by a computer program. Our prices are pretend too. The banana fact is the one checked real fact." |

## Differentiation

- **Support:** sentence frames ("A ___ is a need because ___.", "My
  benefit is ___. My cost is ___.", "I saved ___ coins because ___."),
  pre-printed price lists, picture cards for every good and service,
  adult scribing, oral/pointing response modes, large-print price tags,
  real objects on the table beside their cards.
- **Extension:** error-hunt (learner plants a wrong price tag for the
  adult to catch); a second, learner-invented market stall with its own
  fixed prices that still add up within 10 coins; a "what if the price
  went up?" question for one good; a savings plan for a bigger pretend
  goal (three weeks at 3 coins a week = 9 coins).
- **Language access:** vocabulary with objects/pictures first, word cards
  second; visual word wall; home-language labels welcomed alongside
  English; every price and sum counted with real coins before any
  question.

## Safety reminders

Adult supervises every activity and previews every video, read-aloud, and
practice item. Play coins are counted out and counted back in every
session; no real money is used and no learner's family money, income, or
job is discussed or collected. Practice prices and the Maple Lane market
are fictional; the adult says so. The banana fact is labeled as a checked
fact; no other origin statistics are taught. No real addresses, learner
photos, or personal details in any map, price list, or written product.
Market play stays on familiar, safe ground with the adult. No sharp tools.
