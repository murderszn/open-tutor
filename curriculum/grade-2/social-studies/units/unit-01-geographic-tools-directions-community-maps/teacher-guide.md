# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Geographic Tools, Directions, and Community Maps

For the guiding adult. Learner-facing materials never include answers; all
solutions live in the [answer key](answer-key.md). Sessions are 25–30
minutes; every session opens with a 2-minute retrieval warm-up.

## What "ready to move on" looks like

The learner is ready for Unit 02 when they can, with at most light
prompting: name and point to the five map parts on a sample map; name the
four cardinal directions and point north in the room; use a key to find a
place on the Maple Lane map and give a two-step route with direction words;
say what a map, a globe, and a photograph each show best; and name 5 of 7
continents and 3 of 5 oceans on the globe. Evidence: project rubric 6+/8,
quiz 7+/10, assessment 9+/12 parts. If the learner is below this, re-teach
with the targeted follow-ups below before Unit 02 — the map-parts checklist
and compass-rose routine return in Units 02, 06, and 08, so gaps compound.

## Session-by-session notes

- **Sessions 1–2 (map parts):** Keep the five-part chant brisk
  ("title–symbols–key–labels–compass rose"). The key-first habit is the
  unit's highest-leverage skill — insist on it in every later session.
  Session 2's Practice set A is oral/pointing/drawn; 4 of 5 is the bar.
- **Sessions 3–4 (directions):** Fix the room's north on day one (tape a
  small N card on the north wall) and never move it. Lesson 02's body trick
  only works while facing north — re-teach that explicitly when learners
  turn around and get confused. Session 4's direction game should be
  physical and silly; accuracy matters more than speed.
- **Sessions 5–6 (Maple Lane):** Do the key-first/scan-second routine
  together, slowly, before any independent finding. The Maple Lane map is a
  fictional, AI-generated practice illustration — say so out loud. Every
  image-based question has a full text-only alternative in Lesson 03; the
  lesson works fully without the picture.
- **Sessions 7–8 (tools comparison):** Line up the real objects — a folded
  map, a globe, a printed photo — and let the learner handle each. The
  "which tool for which job" frame is the whole lesson; avoid turning it
  into a lecture about projections or scale.
- **Sessions 9–10 (continents/oceans):** Chant with the globe in hand; do
  not drill from a flat list. Accept approximate pointing — naming is the
  goal, not precision geography. The two-places comparison stays spoken and
  general; never record family details, addresses, or personal stories.
- **Sessions 11–12 (project):** Approve every map plan *before* drawing
  starts — most plans fail on "the key must match the symbols," so check
  that first. The dictated directions are the adult's scribing, the
  learner's words; do not correct the learner's direction words into your
  own — note errors and re-teach instead.
- **Sessions 13–16 (review/product):** Quiz in session 13, reviewed the same
  day; re-teach misses immediately. Session 14's review game: direction
  relay (adult calls a place, learner points the direction from a fixed
  spot) and map-parts race (learner slaps the named part on a sample map).
  Session 15 is the showcase — the learner presents; applaud the map parts
  by name. Session 16's assessment is the last word, not a surprise: every
  item mirrors practiced tasks.

## Misconception bank

| Misconception | Re-teach move |
|---|---|
| The symbol *is* the thing (the little tree is a tiny tree) | "The symbol is a stand-in." Cover symbols; ask what real thing each stands for |
| The key is decoration; symbols can be guessed | Cover the key; ask what a new symbol means — the failure teaches the habit |
| "North" means "up" (in the sky / on the wall) | "On the map, north is drawn at the top. In the room, north is a direction you walk." Point, don't just say |
| East/west flip after turning around | Re-teach: the body trick only works facing north; turn, then re-face north and try again |
| Giving directions from the destination backward | Sentence frame every time: "Start at ___. Walk ___ on ___. Then ___." |
| A photograph "shows everything" | Cover half a photo: "What can't we see? What's behind the camera?" |
| The flat world map *is* Earth's shape | Globe and map side by side: "Same Earth, two pictures. The map is flattened so we can carry it." |
| Each blue patch on a map is a different ocean | Spin the globe: "All the blue is one connected water; we name its big parts." |
| A continent is a country ("Texas is a continent") | "Continents are the biggest land pieces; countries are smaller pieces inside them." |
| "My map doesn't need a key/rose — I know what it means" | "Maps are for *other* people. The visitor test: could a stranger use it?" |

## Differentiation

- **Support:** sentence frames ("Start at ___. Walk ___ on ___. Then
  ___.", "The ___ shows ___ best because ___."), pre-drawn key boxes and
  compass-rose outlines, wall-mounted N/S/E/W cards, adult scribing,
  oral/pointing response modes, large-print symbol cards, textured route
  strings for low-vision learners.
- **Extension:** error-hunt (learner plants a wrong route for the adult to
  catch); adding a new place + symbol + key entry to the Maple Lane map;
  naming which continents touch each other; the "what can no tool show?"
  discussion (smells, yesterday, feelings).
- **Language access:** vocabulary with objects/pictures first, word cards
  second; visual word wall; home-language labels welcomed alongside
  English; chant continent/ocean names to a clapping rhythm before any
  writing.

## Safety reminders

Adult supervises every activity and previews every video and read-aloud. No
real addresses, learner photos, or personal details in any map or written
product. Window observations are adult-supervised; a map-only alternative
replaces any outdoor observation for safety, weather, or access reasons. Map
walks stay on familiar, safe ground with the adult. No sharp tools.
