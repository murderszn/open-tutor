# Answer Key — Unit 01: Geographic Tools, Directions, and Community Maps

For the guiding adult only. Every item below is solved independently with
reasoning; open responses list acceptable variation. Student files contain
no answers.

## Practice set A — Week 3 (map parts and directions)

The adult uses a hand-drawn sample map *with* a key for this set.

1. **Point-and-name the parts.** Accept when the learner correctly points
   to: the title, the key box, any one symbol, and any one label on the
   sample map. For each, the learner should say what it does (title: tells
   what the map shows; key: explains symbols; symbol: stands for a real
   thing; label: names a place).
2. **Sort.** Correct matches: "title" → the title words; "a tree symbol" →
   the tree picture; the word *library* → the label; "the key box" → the
   key; "the compass rose" → the direction picture. 4 of 5 correct meets
   the bar.
3. **Cover-the-key.** Reasoning: with the key covered, the learner is
   guessing — the star *might* mean "park," but nothing on the map proves
   it. After uncovering: the key settles it (whatever the adult's key says,
   e.g., star = school). The teaching point: never trust a guess when the
   key can settle it.
4. **Playground symbol.** Accept any simple picture (slide, swing, ball)
   drawn in a key box with the label "playground" beside it. The symbol
   must be a picture, not the written word "playground" — if the learner
   writes the word, note the distinction kindly and ask for a picture.
5. **Direction Simon-says.** Facing the room's fixed north: east = right
   hand, south = behind, west = left hand, north = ahead. 4 of 5 calls
   correct meets the bar. If the learner turns around mid-game, stop and
   re-teach: the body trick only works while facing north.

## Practice set B — Week 4 (reading community maps)

Map facts used: School = NW block; Fire Station = NE block; Library = SW
block; Pond = SW, south of the library; Park = SE block; Grocery Store =
SE block, east of the park. Maple Street runs east–west; Oak Avenue runs
north–south.

1. **Find the library.** Read the labels, then scan: the Library is in the
   **southwest block — south of Maple Street and west of Oak Avenue**.
   Accept either the block name or both street relations.
2. **Hungry visitor at the School.** The **Grocery Store** (southeast
   block, east of the park). Accept "the grocery store."
3. **School → Grocery Store directions.** Acceptable: "Start at the School.
   Walk **south** on Oak Avenue. Cross Maple Street. Turn **east** (left)
   on Maple Street. The Grocery Store is in the southeast block, east of
   the park." Any route using correct direction words in order counts.
4. **True/false.** **False.** The Pond is **south** of the Library, not
   east. Accept: "False — the pond is just south of the library."
5. **Fire Station → School route.** Acceptable: "Start at the Fire Station.
   Walk **west** on Maple Street. Cross Oak Avenue. The School is on your
   right — **north** of Maple Street, in the northwest block." (Facing
   west, north is on the right.) The learner must name each step with a
   direction word; pointing along the trace counts as naming if the adult
   hears the words.

## Practice set C — Week 5 (continents and oceans)

1. **Seven-continent chant.** The seven, in any order: North America, South
   America, Africa, Europe, Asia, Australia, Antarctica. Accept approximate
   pointing; naming is the goal.
2. **Five-ocean chant.** The five, in any order: Pacific, Atlantic, Indian,
   Arctic, Southern. (If the learner says "Antarctic Ocean" from the
   JunyTony video, accept it and note: "That song says Antarctic; geographers
   also call it the Southern Ocean — same water.")
3. **Our continent.** **North America**; west side = **Pacific Ocean**;
   east side = **Atlantic Ocean**.
4. **Australia's oceans.** The **Pacific Ocean** (east side) and the
   **Indian Ocean** (west side).
5. **Two places compared.** Accept any accurate pair, e.g.: environmental —
   "our town is flat and hot in summer; Grandma's town has hills and snow";
   cultural — "we speak Spanish at home and eat tacos; they speak English
   and eat pot pie." One nature difference + one people difference meets
   the bar. Nothing is written down except at the learner's wish.

## Formative quiz (session 13)

1. **Title.** The learner points to "Maple Lane." A
   title tells **what the map shows**.
2. **Symbol.** A symbol is **a small picture on a map that stands for a
   real thing**. Accept any one correct pair, e.g., the schoolhouse picture
   = the School; the tree/swing picture = the Park; the fire-truck picture
   = the Fire Station.
3. **Key.** The key **shows each symbol and what it means**. Without a key,
   readers must **guess** what the symbols stand for — and guesses can be
   wrong.
4. **Cardinal directions.** **North, south, east, west** (any order for
   naming).
5. **Point east.** Facing north, east is the **right-hand** direction. The
   adult checks against the room's fixed north.
6. a. The **School** (north of Maple Street, west of Oak Avenue).
   b. **East** of Oak Avenue.
7. **School → Library.** Acceptable: "Start at the School. Walk **south**
   on Oak Avenue. Cross Maple Street. The Library is on your right,
   **west** of Oak Avenue." Steps in order with direction words. (Facing
   south, west is on the right.)
8. **Earth's round shape.** The **globe** — because it is round, just like
   Earth. The flat map cannot show roundness; the photo shows only one
   small spot.
9. **Walking route.** The **map** — because it shows streets, labels, and
   the compass rose: exactly what a walker needs. The photo shows no street
   names; the globe is far too big for one neighborhood.
10. **Counts.** **Seven** continents — any three correct (e.g., North
    America, Africa, Asia). **Five** oceans — any two correct (e.g.,
    Pacific, Atlantic).

**Scoring:** 7 of 10 items meets the bar; the adult re-teaches misses the
same day.

## Unit assessment (session 16)

1. **Mini-map.** Check the parts, not the art: a title present; two symbols
   drawn; a key box matching both symbols; two labels. 3 of 4 part-types
   correct = meets.
2. **"What does this square mean?"** The learner should say: **"Look at the
   key — it tells what each symbol means."** Reasoning: the key, not
   guessing, settles what a symbol stands for.
3. **Name and point.** North, south, east, west named; learner faces north
   and points to each. Accept pointing within roughly the right quarter.
4. **Door vs. bookshelf.** The correct direction **depends on the real
   room** — the adult verifies against the room's fixed north. Accept any
   direction the learner derives by the correct method: face north, then
   judge where the door sits relative to the bookshelf. Method matters more
   than the compass point; if the method is wrong, re-teach the body trick.
5. **Fire Station → Park.** The place is the **Park**; it is **south**
   of Maple Street (southeast block).
6. **Directions.** Acceptable: "Start at the Fire Station. Walk **south**
   on Oak Avenue. Cross Maple Street. The Park is on your left — **east**
   of Oak Avenue, in the southeast block." (Facing south, east is on the
   left.) Any correct ordered route with direction words counts.
7. **Grocery Store → Pond.** Acceptable: "Start at the Grocery Store. Walk
   **west** past the Park to Oak Avenue. Cross Oak Avenue. Walk **south**
   past the Library. The Pond is just south of the library." (Simplest
   acceptable version: "Walk west to Oak Avenue, cross it, then walk south
   past the Library to the Pond.")
8. **Continents in true shape.** The **globe** — it is round like Earth, so
   the continents keep their true shapes and positions; a flat map
   stretches them and a photo shows only one spot.
9. **What the playground looks like.** The **photograph** — it shows real
   colors, details, and what the place actually looks like; a map shows
   positions, not appearances, and a globe is far too big.
10. **Seven continents.** North America, South America, Africa, Europe,
    Asia, Australia, Antarctica. 5 of 7 named while pointing = meets.
11. **Five oceans.** Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic, Southern. The ocean
    on North America's west side is the **Pacific Ocean**.
12. **Error analysis.**
    a. Any **two** of: **title** (the visitor doesn't know what the map
       shows), **key** (the visitor can't tell what the drawings stand
       for), **compass rose** (the visitor doesn't know which way is
       which). Each needs its reason to count.
    b. Without a key, the visitor must **guess** what each drawing means —
       a square might be a desk, a door, or a window; wrong guesses send
       the visitor to the wrong place.
    c. The mapmaker knows where north is, but the **visitor** doesn't. The
       compass rose tells the visitor which way is north, south, east, and
       west, so direction words on the map ("walk south") mean something to
       a stranger.

**Scoring:** 9 of 12 parts meets the bar (parts 6, 7, and 12 count each
sub-part as met when the reasoning is sound, even if wording differs).

## Project — Welcome Visitor map packet

Score with the rubric in [project.md](project.md) (2/1/0 per criterion, 8
points total; 6+ = ready to move on).

- **Map parts (2):** all five present and correct — title says what the map
  shows; 3+ symbols; key matches every symbol; 3+ labels; rose with N/S/E/W
  in the right positions. Deduct to 1 if one part is missing or wrong
  (most often: a key that doesn't match the symbols, or a rose with letters
  in the wrong places).
- **Key and symbols (2):** every symbol on the map appears in the key with
  the right meaning. If the learner drew symbols as words, score 1 and
  re-teach "symbols are pictures."
- **Compass rose and directions (2):** the dictated route uses 2+
  cardinal words in order and would actually lead a newcomer to the target.
  Trace the route yourself: if a step's direction word is wrong, score 1
  and re-teach that step with the body trick — do not silently correct the
  learner's words.
- **Showcase talk (2):** the learner points to each part by name and gives
  the full route aloud. Whispered, recorded, or adult-voiced delivery all
  count — content, not volume, is scored.
