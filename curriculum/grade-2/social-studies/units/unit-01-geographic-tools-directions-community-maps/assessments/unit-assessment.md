# Unit Assessment — Unit 01: Geographic Tools, Directions, and Community Maps

**Given in:** session 16 (culminating). **Time:** 25–30 minutes. The learner
may answer orally (adult scribes), by pointing, or with drawings where
noted. All places are fictional. Solutions and scoring in the
[answer key](../answer-key.md).

## Part 1 — Map parts

1. Draw a tiny map of our classroom (or your bedroom) from above. Include a
   **title**, **two symbols** with a **key**, and **two labels**. *(The
   adult checks the parts, not the art.)*
2. Your friend looks at your map and asks, "What does this little square
   mean?" What do you tell them to look at, and why?

## Part 2 — Cardinal directions

3. Name the four cardinal directions. Then stand up, face north, and point
   to each one as you name it.
4. The bookshelf is on the north wall of our room. Is the door north,
   south, east, or west of the bookshelf? Explain how you figured it out.

## Part 3 — Reading the community map

Use the Maple Lane map. *(Text-only alternative: the map's top is north.
Maple Street runs east–west across the map; Oak Avenue runs north–south
through the middle. School: northwest block (north of Maple Street, west of
Oak Avenue). Fire Station: northeast block. Library: southwest block. Pond:
southwest, just south of the library. Park: southeast block. Grocery Store:
southeast block, east of the park. Every place is labeled directly; there is
no key box.)*

5. A visitor at the Fire Station wants to visit the park. Which place are
   they going to, and is it north or south of Maple Street?
6. Give the visitor directions from the Fire Station to that place. Use
   direction words and street names, in order.
7. A visitor at the Grocery Store wants to see the Pond. Give directions
   with direction words, in order.

## Part 4 — Map, globe, photograph

8. Which tool shows **all the continents** in their true round-Earth shape:
   a map, a globe, or a photograph? Tell why.
9. Your cousin wants to know what our school's playground *really looks
   like*. Which tool is best — a map, a globe, or a photograph? Tell why.

## Part 5 — Continents and oceans

10. Point to each continent on the globe as the adult names it, then name
    all seven yourself: which seven did you name?
11. Name the five oceans. Then answer: which ocean touches North America on
    its west side?

## Part 6 — Applied error-analysis task

12. A classmate drew a "map" of the classroom. It has beautiful drawings of
    the desks and the door, but it has **no title**, **no key**, and **no
    compass rose**.
    a. Name the two most important missing parts for a *visitor* who has
       never been here. *(Any two of: title, key, compass rose — with a
       reason each.)*
    b. Explain why the missing **key** would confuse the visitor.
    c. The classmate says, "My map doesn't need a compass rose because I
       know where north is." Explain why a visitor still needs one.
