# Resource Pack — Unit 01: Geographic Tools, Directions, and Community Maps

**Goal (one sentence):** Give the guiding adult short, grade-2-appropriate
videos, references, and search routes that reinforce map parts, cardinal
directions, community-map reading, the map/globe/photograph comparison, and
continents and oceans.
**Age fit:** grade 2 (ages 7–8); the adult previews every video and
co-views with the learner.

**Fact-check record (2026-10-10):** continent names verified against
[Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/assembly/view/148401)
(opened today; "Earth has seven continents: Africa, Antarctica, Asia,
Australia (Oceania), Europe, North America, and South America"); ocean names
verified against [NOAA](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/howmanyoceans.html)
(opened today; five recognized oceans: Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic,
Southern/Antarctic). The unit teaches **Australia** and the **Southern
Ocean**; one video below uses "Oceania"/"Antarctic" — the difference is
flagged so the adult can name it.

## Web references

1. **NOAA — "How many oceans are there?"** —
   https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/howmanyoceans.html
   *Why good:* the authoritative source for the unit's five-ocean list
   (Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic, Southern); short, plain language.
   Best for the adult's background and for answering "why five?" Adult may
   read the first two paragraphs aloud. *Opened and verified live
   2026-10-10; free, no account.*
2. **Britannica Kids — "Continents" (Students level)** —
   https://kids.britannica.com/students/assembly/view/148401
   *Why good:* states the seven-continent list the unit teaches; Students
   reading level suits grade 2 with adult support. *Opened and verified
   live 2026-10-10; free content visible without login.*
3. **National Geographic Education — "Map" (reference)** —
   https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/map/
   *Why good:* explains what maps are, symbols/keys, and why globes show
   Earth's shape more truly than flat maps — the adult background for
   Lessons 01 and 04. General-audience reading level: adult reads and
   adapts; not assigned to the learner. *Opened and verified live
   2026-10-10; free, no account.*
4. **Library of Congress — "Teaching About Community and Place with Library
   of Congress Maps" (Teaching with the Library blog)** —
   https://blogs.loc.gov/teachers/2025/11/teaching-about-community-and-place-with-library-of-congress-maps
   *Why good:* teacher-facing ideas for using community maps (including
   bird's-eye-view neighborhood maps and map keys) with elementary
   students; supports the Lesson 03 Maple Lane work and Unit 06 preview.
   Adult background and activity ideas only. *Opened and verified live
   2026-10-10; free, no account.*
5. **SDPB (South Dakota Public Broadcasting) — "Cardinal Directions"
   Activity Starter** —
   https://www.sdpb.org/CardinalDirectionsActivityStarter
   *Why good:* a short animated video introducing cardinal directions plus a
   hands-on activity (draw a map with a compass rose and place photos or
   drawings at N/S/E/W) that mirrors this unit's Lesson 02 and project.
   PBS-backed; free, no account. *Opened and verified live 2026-10-10;
   adult previews the embedded video.*
