# Unit 01 — Geographic Tools, Directions, and Community Maps

**Grade:** 2 (ages 7–8) · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track issue:** [#17](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/17)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 3–6 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, 25–30 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

This unit teaches the geographic tools the rest of the year reuses: how to
read and build a simple map with a **title**, **symbols**, a **key**,
**labels**, and a **compass rose**; how to name and use the four **cardinal
directions** (north, south, east, west); how to read a simple community map
to find places and describe a route; how the same place looks different on a
**map**, a **globe**, and a **photograph**; and what **continents** and
**oceans** are on a globe. The unit's project — a "Welcome Visitor" map
packet — is a one-labeled-map product (with title, key, and compass rose)
plus dictated cardinal-direction directions, reviewed by the adult the same
day. The compass-rose routine and the map-parts checklist return in Units 02,
06, and 08.

## Prerequisites (developmental expectations; re-taught here as needed)

- Name familiar people, places, and roles in home, school, and neighborhood.
- Follow simple rules and routines; take turns; listen when others speak.
- Sort and compare objects by one or two attributes.
- Retell a sequence of 2–3 familiar events in order (first, next, last).
- Point, draw, and describe observations; dictate a sentence for an adult
  to scribe.

Diagnostic Weeks 1–2 probe these with play-based tasks (draw your route from
bed to breakfast; point toward the classroom door, then name the direction).

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Build** a simple map that includes a title, symbols, a key, labels, and
   a compass rose (D2.Geo.1.K-2).
2. **Name** the four cardinal directions and **use** them to describe where
   things are and how to get from one place to another (D2.Geo.2.K-2).
3. **Read** a simple community map to find places and describe a route from
   one place to another (D2.Geo.2.K-2).
4. **Compare** a map, a globe, and a photograph of the same place and
   describe what each shows best (D2.Geo.2.K-2).
5. **Name** Earth's seven continents and five oceans on a globe and
   **compare** cultural and environmental characteristics of two familiar
   places (D2.Geo.3.K-2).

With adult support, the learner asks the unit's compelling question, uses
the map key and globe as helpful sources (D1.1.K-2, D1.3.K-2, D1.5.K-2), and
presents the project packet as an oral summary with drawings (D4.3.K-2).

## Essential question

*How can a map help someone who has never been to our classroom find their
way?*

## Vocabulary

**Map** — a drawing that shows where things are. · **Symbol** — a small
picture on a map that stands for a real thing. · **Key** (also called a
*legend*) — the box on a map that shows each symbol and what it means. ·
**Label** — a word written on a map to name a place or thing. · **Title** —
the words at the top of a map that tell what the map shows. · **Compass
rose** — the picture on a map that shows directions. · **Cardinal
directions** — the four main directions: north, south, east, west. ·
**Route** — the way you go from one place to another. · **Globe** — a round
model of Earth. · **Photograph** — a picture taken with a camera of a real
place. · **Continent** — one of Earth's seven very large pieces of land. ·
**Ocean** — one of Earth's five very large areas of water.

## Materials

Paper and crayons or colored pencils; a ruler; a wall map and a globe (an
inflatable globe works); the printed [Maple Lane neighborhood
map](assets/maple-lane-map.png) *(AI-generated illustration, inspected
2026-10-10 — every image-based task ships with a full text-only
alternative)*;
printed map-symbol cards and a large compass-rose card; photo prints of one
familiar place (e.g., the school or a park) for the map/globe/photograph
comparison; a blank map frame for the project (adult-made or drawn). All
photo and window observations are adult-supervised; a from-the-window or
map-only alternative replaces any outdoor observation for safety, weather,
or access reasons. No real addresses, learner photos, or personal
information appear anywhere in the learner's work.

## Differentiation and accessibility

- Oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative response modes for every check;
  the adult scribes dictated writing; short labeled products reviewed the
  same day.
- Large-print, high-contrast map symbols and compass-rose cards; raised or
  textured pieces (felt symbols, string routes) for low-vision learners.
- Vocabulary taught with objects and pictures first, word second; visual
  word wall; home-language labels welcomed alongside English.
- Short sessions with movement breaks; every lesson has a seated-table and a
  floor-play variant.
- Every map, diagram, and generated image ships with a text-only
  alternative; color is never the only cue (shape + label always accompany
  color).
- Hearing support: face the learner during direction games and discussions;
  visual turn-taking cues; written/drawn option for every spoken check.
- Speaking tasks accept audio recording in place of live presentation; the
  adult may record on the learner's behalf.

## Safety

Adult supervises every activity and previews every procedure and read-aloud.
Window observations are adult-supervised; a map-only alternative replaces any
outdoor observation. No sharp tools; map walks stay on familiar, safe ground
with the adult. No real addresses or learner-identifying details appear in
any map or written product.
