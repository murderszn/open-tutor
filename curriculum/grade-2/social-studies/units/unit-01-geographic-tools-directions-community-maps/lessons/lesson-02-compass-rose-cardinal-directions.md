# Lesson 02 — The Compass Rose and the Four Cardinal Directions

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** paper, crayons, a large compass-rose
card, the unit's vocabulary word cards (compass rose, cardinal directions,
north, south, east, west)

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will learn the four cardinal
directions — north, south, east, west — and how the compass rose on a map
tells you which way is which. Then you will find the directions in our own
room.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "If I say 'the door is that way,' how could
we name *which way* 'that way' is?" Accept any naming attempt. If stuck,
model: "Grown-ups use special direction words: north, south, east, west."

Show the compass rose (the diagram below is the model — copy it large on
paper or print the SVG).

Say: "This is a **compass rose**. A **compass rose** is the picture on a map
that shows directions. The four main directions are called the **cardinal
directions**: **north**, **south**, **east**, and **west**. On most maps,
north is drawn at the top."

Teach the body trick: "Stand up. Face the way the map calls north. Now your
right hand points **east**, your left hand points **west**, and **south** is
behind you. The order around the rose is: north, east, south, west — it goes
around like a clock."

A memory sentence some children like: "**N**ever **E**at **S**oggy
**W**affles" — N, E, S, W, in clockwise order.

**Worked example 1 — using the rose to describe a location:**

> Adult: "Look at our classroom map. The compass rose says north is up.
> The door is drawn above the desks. So the door is *north* of the desks.
> I used the rose in two steps: first I found which way is north, then I
> described where the door is *compared to* the desks. 'North of' is how
> map readers talk."

**Worked example 2 — east and west with the body trick:**

> Adult: "I am facing north — the same way the map's north points. My right
> hand points east. The window is on my right side, so the window is *east*
> of me. Let me check with the rose: north at top, east at right. Yes —
> east. The trick works because my body is lined up with the map."

Key idea to say plainly: **The compass rose tells which way is north,
south, east, and west. Face north, and east is on your right, west is on
your left, south is behind you.**

## Guided practice (adult leads, learner decides)

Adult and learner stand together, facing the room's north (the adult
decides north for the room and keeps it fixed all unit).

1. "Point north." *(learner points up-room toward north)*
2. "Without turning, point east." *(right hand — check it is 90° right of
   north)*
3. "Now point west, then south." *(left hand; behind)*
4. "The bookshelf is on the north wall. Where is the door — north, south,
   east, or west of the bookshelf?" *(adult confirms from the fixed room
   north)*
5. "Take two steps east. Which way did you walk?" *(learner names it)*

## Independent practice

The learner draws and labels their own compass rose: N at top, S at bottom,
E at right, W at left. Color the north arrow red if they wish (color is
never the only cue — the letters must be there).

## Application

Adult names a real spot in the room: "Stand by the sink. Which direction
is the front door from you?" The learner walks there, faces north, and
answers with a direction word.

## Exit check

Prompt: "Name the four cardinal directions. Now point to north without
looking at the compass rose card." Accept: north, south, east, west (any
order for naming); pointing within roughly the right quarter.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** Tape N/S/E/W letter cards on the four walls before the
  lesson; the learner matches direction words to wall cards first. Allow
  pointing-only answers.
- **Reading/language access:** Teach the words with the body trick first,
  letter cards second; welcome home-language direction words beside English.
- **Alternate response mode:** The learner may whisper directions to the
  adult, who speaks them; or record audio.
- **Extension:** The learner teaches the body trick to a stuffed animal,
  naming each direction the animal "faces." Or: the learner draws the route
  from the door to their seat using one direction word per step ("Walk
  south two steps, then east three steps").

## Teacher note

**Likely misconception:** "North means up." Learners often think north is
*up in the sky* or *up on the wall*. Re-anchor gently and often: "On the
*map*, north is drawn at the top. In the *room*, north is a direction you
can walk toward — it is not up." A second slip: mixing left/right with
east/west after turning. Re-teach: the trick only works when you are facing
north; turn around and east switches hands. Keep the room's north fixed
(tape a small N card on the north wall) for the whole unit.
