# Unit 02 — Landforms, Environments, and Human Choices

**Grade:** 2 (ages 7–8) · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track issue:** [#17](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/17)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 7–10 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, 25–30 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

This unit answers the compelling question *"How does where you live change
how you live?"* First, the learner meets six **landforms** — hill, valley,
plain, river, lake, and coast — using labeled, reproducible diagrams (exact
SVG drawings, not AI geography) and finds examples in photographs of the
region. Then the learner separates **weather** (what the sky is doing
today) from **climate** (what the weather is usually like in a place) and
traces how each shapes daily life: clothing, food, shelter, and
activities. Next comes the two-way street between people and places: how
**people change places** (farms, roads, buildings) and how **places shape
people's work and choices** — including what local people produce and what
comes from other communities. The unit closes by comparing two
communities, a city and a countryside community, and asking how each uses
its local environment to meet daily needs. The project — a "Design a
Community for a Landform" poster — is presented orally with drawings and
reviewed by the adult the same day. The landform routine and the
people-and-places two-way question return in Units 05, 06, and 08.

## Prerequisites (developmental expectations; re-taught here as needed)

- Read a simple community map: title, symbols, key, labels, compass rose
  (Unit 01).
- Name the four cardinal directions and use them to describe a route
  (Unit 01).
- Name familiar people, places, and roles in home, school, and
  neighborhood.
- Sort objects or pictures by one or two attributes; compare two familiar
  places.
- Retell a sequence of 2–3 events in order; dictate a sentence for an adult
  to scribe.

Diagnostic Weeks 1–2 probed these with play-based tasks. Unit 02 opens
with a compass-rose check from Unit 01 before any new content.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Name and identify** six landforms — hill, valley, plain, river, lake,
   coast — on labeled diagrams and photographs (D2.Geo.6.K-2).
2. **Explain** how weather and climate affect daily life: clothing, food,
   shelter, and activities (D2.Geo.4.K-2).
3. **Describe** how people change the environment (farms, roads, buildings)
   and how the environment shapes people's work and choices
   (D2.Geo.5.K-2, D2.Geo.9.K-2).
4. **Compare** how two communities — a city and a countryside community —
   use their local environments to meet daily needs (D2.Geo.8.K-2).
5. **Describe** goods and services the local community produces and goods
   that are produced in other communities (D2.Eco.4.K-2).

With adult support, the learner asks the unit's compelling question,
treats the landform diagrams and the community illustration as helpful
sources (D1.1.K-2, D1.3.K-2, D1.5.K-2), and presents the project poster as
an oral summary with drawings (D4.3.K-2).

## Essential question

*How does where you live change how you live?*

## Vocabulary

**Landform** — a shape of the land, like a hill or a valley. ·
**Hill** — raised land; lower than a mountain. · **Valley** — low land
between hills or mountains. · **Plain** — wide, flat land; good for
farms. · **River** — water that flows across the land. · **Lake** — water
with land all around it. · **Coast** — where land meets the ocean. ·
**Weather** — what the sky is doing today (rain, sun, wind, hot, cold). ·
**Climate** — what the weather is usually like in a place, year after
year. · **Environment** — everything around a place: the land, the water,
the weather, the plants, and the animals. · **Community** — the people
who live in one place and share it. · **Farm** — land where people grow
crops or raise animals. · **City** — a community with many buildings and
many people close together. · **Countryside** — land outside cities with
farms, fields, and fewer, spread-out homes. · **Goods** — things people
make or grow. · **Services** — helpful work people do for others. ·
**Choice** — deciding between two or more things.

## Materials

Paper and crayons or colored pencils; the printed
[landform diagrams](assets/landform-diagrams.svg) (exact, reproducible —
the unit's canonical landform pictures); the printed [Riverside Valley
illustration](assets/riverside-valley-illustration.png) *(AI-generated
illustration, inspected 2026-10-17 — every image-based task ships with a
full text-only alternative)*; 6–10 adult-curated regional photographs
showing local landforms (from a library book, a magazine, or the adult's
own photos — a text-only description replaces any photo the learner cannot
access); vocabulary word cards; the Unit 01 compass-rose card; clothing
picture cards (coat, rain boots, sandals, hat, umbrella); a blank
community poster frame for the project (adult-made or drawn). All photo
and window observations are adult-supervised; a map-only or text-only
alternative replaces any outdoor observation for safety, weather, or
access reasons. No real addresses, learner photos, or personal
information appear anywhere in the learner's work.

## Differentiation and accessibility

- Oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative response modes for every check;
  the adult scribes dictated writing; short labeled products reviewed the
  same day.
- Large-print, high-contrast landform diagrams and word cards; raised or
  textured pieces (sandpaper hill, foil river) for low-vision learners.
- Vocabulary taught with objects and pictures first, word second; visual
  word wall; home-language labels welcomed alongside English.
- Short sessions with movement breaks; every lesson has a seated-table and
  a floor-play variant.
- Every map, diagram, and generated image ships with a text-only
  alternative; color is never the only cue (shape + label always accompany
  color).
- Hearing support: face the learner during games and discussions; visual
  turn-taking cues; written/drawn option for every spoken check.
- Speaking tasks accept audio recording in place of live presentation; the
  adult may record on the learner's behalf.

## Safety

Adult supervises every activity and previews every procedure and read-aloud.
Photo and window observations are adult-supervised; a diagram-only or
text-only alternative replaces any outdoor observation. No sharp tools; any
map or photo walk stays on familiar, safe ground with the adult. No real
addresses or learner-identifying details appear in any drawing or written
product.
