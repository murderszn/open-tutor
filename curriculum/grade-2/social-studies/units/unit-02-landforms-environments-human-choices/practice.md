# Practice Sets — Unit 02

Short oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative tasks. The adult reads
each task aloud; the learner may answer by speaking, pointing, drawing,
or arranging cards. The adult scribes any dictated writing and reviews
each set the same day. Answer guidance lives in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

---

## Week 7 — Landforms

**Practice set A** (Session 2; also reused in Session 3's photo hunt)

1. **Diagram roll call.** Adult points to the six panels of
   [landform-diagrams.svg](assets/landform-diagrams.svg) in mixed order.
   Learner names each landform. *(4 of 6 correct passes; adult re-teaches
   misses on the spot.)*
2. **Water or land sort.** Learner sorts the six vocabulary word cards
   into three piles: "water" (river, lake), "land" (hill, valley, plain),
   "both" (coast). Then explains the "both" pile: "The coast is where
   land meets the ocean."
3. **Finish the diagram.** Adult covers the labels on the printed
   diagrams with sticky notes. Learner points to each panel and says its
   name; the adult uncovers each label to check together.
4. **Clue match.** Adult reads clues; learner points to the matching
   panel:
   - "Water that flows across the land." → river
   - "Low land between hills." → valley
   - "Water with land all around it." → lake
   - "Where land meets the ocean." → coast
   - "Wide, flat land, good for farms." → plain
5. **Draw and defend.** Learner draws one landform from memory, labels it
   (adult scribes on request), and tells the adult one clue that proves
   the drawing is correct ("My river has wavy lines because it flows").

**Observation prompt for the adult:** Can the learner tell river from
lake without prompting? If the two blues blur together, replay the
"flows versus sits still" contrast with a poured-water demo (adult pours
a little water down a tray = river; into a bowl = lake).

## Week 8 — Weather and climate

**Practice set B** (Session 6; the read-aloud passage is used in Session 7)

### Read-aloud: "A Year in Two Places" (original passage)

*(The adult reads this aloud in Session 7, slowly, twice. The second
reading is for the learner to interrupt with observations.)*

"Maya lives where the climate is hot and dry. Almost every day the sun
shines, and rain comes only a few times a year. Her house has thick walls
that stay cool inside, and a shade tree grows by the door. Every morning
Maya puts on light clothes, a sun hat, and sandals, and fills her water
bottle — the climate tells her family to always be ready for heat. On the
rare day when rain does fall — the *weather* surprising them — the
children cheer and splash, because rain is a treat.

Leo lives where the climate is cold and snowy. Winter is long, and snow
falls every year. His house has a strong heater and small windows that
keep the warm air in. Every morning from December to March, Leo puts on a
heavy coat, boots, and mittens — the climate tells his family to always
be ready for cold. On a sunny winter day — the *weather* surprising them
— the snow sparkles, and the children build snowmen before lunch.

Maya and Leo both eat, play, and go to school every day. But the climate
of each place shapes *how*: what they wear, what their houses look like,
and what they do for fun."

**After reading:** ask "What is one weather surprise in Maya's town? In
Leo's town?" (Rain for Maya; a sunny winter day for Leo.) "What does the
*climate* make each family always keep ready?" (Maya: light clothes,
water, shade; Leo: heavy coat, boots, heater.)


1. **Today or usually?** Adult reads sentences; learner holds up a "sun
   card" (today = weather) or a "calendar card" (usually = climate):
   - "It is raining this morning." → weather
   - "Winters here are cold and snowy every year." → weather… *(no —
     climate; this is the teaching item, see the key)*
   - "The wind is strong today." → weather
   - "Summers are long and hot." → climate
   - "We wear coats from December to March." → climate
2. **Dress the character.** Using the clothing picture cards, the learner
   dresses a paper figure for (a) today's actual weather, (b) a hot dry
   climate, (c) a cold snowy climate — saying one reason per outfit.
3. **Home for the climate.** Learner draws two houses: one for a rainy
   climate (slanted roof, umbrella by the door) and one for a hot dry
   climate (thick walls, shade tree, water jar). Adult scribes one
   dictated sentence under each: "This house fits a ___ climate because
   ___."
4. **Activity match.** Adult names activities (swimming, building a
   snowman, flying a kite on a windy day, picking apples in fall);
   learner says whether each needs today's weather or a season's climate
   to plan — and names the clothing that goes with it.
5. **Two-place retell.** After the "A Year in Two Places" read-aloud, the
   learner retells one weather choice from each place in order: "In the
   hot place they ___ because ___. In the cold place they ___ because
   ___."

**Observation prompt for the adult:** Does the learner reach for time
clues ("today," "every year") on their own? If not, underline the clue
words in each sentence before re-asking.

## Week 9 — People and places

**Practice set C** (Session 10)

1. **Made or grown?** Using the Riverside Valley illustration (or its
   text description in [Lesson 03](lessons/lesson-03-people-change-places.md)),
   the learner points to three things people made (farm fields, road,
   houses, bridge) and two things nature made (hills, river, trees),
   naming each.
2. **Before/after tell.** Adult covers the right half of the learner's
   "before and after" drawing (Lesson 03 independent practice). Learner
   describes the "before" valley; adult uncovers the "after" half and the
   learner names the change and how it helps.
3. **Job-to-land match.** Adult names jobs; learner points to where in
   the valley picture that work happens and says why: farmer → plain;
   fisher → river/lake; builder → flat land near the fields; bridge
   keeper… *(there is none — this is the trick item; see the key)*.
4. **Near or far sort.** The learner sorts six item cards (bread from the
   local bakery, nearby orchard apples, bananas, a storybook, shoes, milk
   from a local dairy — adjust to true local facts) into "made near us"
   and "made far away," giving one clue per card.
5. **The two-way street.** Learner completes two sentences aloud:
   - "People changed the valley by ___." (building a farm / road /
     houses / bridge)
   - "The valley changed people by ___." (deciding where to farm, fish,
     and build)

**Observation prompt for the adult:** Can the learner keep the two
directions straight (people→place vs. place→people)? If they flip them,
point to the sentence starters above and rebuild one example together.
