# Lesson 01 — Meeting the Landforms

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** the printed
[landform diagrams](../assets/landform-diagrams.svg) (six labeled panels),
vocabulary word cards (landform, hill, valley, plain, river, lake, coast),
paper and crayons

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will learn what a landform is
and meet six landforms — hill, valley, plain, river, lake, and coast —
and you will practice naming each one on our diagrams.

**Readiness check:** Adult holds up the Unit 01 compass-rose card. "Show
me north on this card. Now point to where north is in our room." If the
learner hesitates, the adult points and the learner copies. Then: "Last
unit we read maps of places. Today we look at the *shapes* of the land
itself."

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

"A **landform** is a shape of the land — like a hill or a valley. The land
is not flat everywhere. Let's meet six landforms."

Point to each panel of the printed diagrams as you read its line. Say the
name, then the meaning, then point to the shape.

1. "**Hill** — raised land. It goes up, but it is lower than a mountain."
   Trace the bump with a finger.
2. "**Valley** — low land between hills or mountains." Point to the low
   green middle between the two hill shapes.
3. "**Plain** — wide, flat land. It is good for farms because machines and
   animals can move easily on flat ground." Sweep a hand across the flat
   green strip.
4. "**River** — water that flows across the land." Trace the blue winding
   band from top to bottom. "See how it keeps moving? A river does not
   sit still."
5. "**Lake** — water with land all around it." Point to the blue oval.
   "The land hugs the water on every side. A lake does not flow away like
   a river."
6. "**Coast** — where land meets the ocean." Point to the line where the
   sandy land touches the blue water. "Stand on a coast and you see land
   on one side and ocean on the other."

Key idea to say plainly: **Land is shaped in different ways. A hill goes
up. A valley sits low between hills. A plain is wide and flat. A river
flows. A lake is still water with land around it. A coast is where land
meets the ocean.**

**Worked example 1 — sorting by shape:**

> Adult: "I will describe a place, and you tell me the landform. Listen:
> 'Water is flowing past our town, moving fast over rocks.' Hmm. Which
> landform moves and flows? A hill does not flow. A lake does not flow. A
> **river** is water that flows across the land. The answer is river. I
> matched the clue 'flows' to the river's meaning."

**Worked example 2 — using the diagram to check:**

> Adult: "Now you try one, and I will think out loud first: 'We are
> standing on flat land, and we can see for a long way in every direction.'
> Flat… wide… Let me check the diagrams. The hill panel goes up — no. The
> valley panel sits low between hills — no. The plain panel is wide and
> flat — yes! The answer is **plain**. When you are unsure, point to the
> diagrams and match the shape to the words."

## Guided practice (adult leads, learner decides)

Adult points to one diagram panel at a time, in mixed order; the learner
names the landform and says one thing about it. (Hints in parentheses are
for the adult.)

1. Point to the valley. "What is this landform?" *(valley — low land
   between hills)*
2. Point to the lake. "What is this, and how is it different from a
   river?" *(lake — water with land all around; a river flows, a lake
   sits still)*
3. Point to the coast. "What is this?" *(coast — where land meets the
   ocean)*
4. Point to the hill. "Is this a hill or a mountain?" *(hill — raised
   land, lower than a mountain)*
5. Point to the plain. "Why is a plain good for farms?" *(wide and flat —
   easy to move on)*

## Independent practice

The learner picks **two** landforms and draws each on paper: the shape
plus its label (adult scribes the word on request, or the learner copies
from the word cards). Under each drawing, the learner dictates one
sentence about it ("A river flows"; "A lake has land all around it"),
which the adult writes word-for-word.

## Application

Look again at the six diagrams. The learner finds two landforms that hold
**water** and two that are only **land**, and sorts the six word cards
into a "water" pile and a "land" pile. (Coast goes in "both" — land and
water meet there.)

## Exit check

Prompt: "Name all six landforms. Then point to the one that is water
with land all around it." Accept: hill, valley, plain, river, lake,
coast (any order); points to the lake. If the learner names five of six,
the adult supplies the sixth and the learner repeats it.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** Teach three landforms first (hill, river, lake), add the
  other three tomorrow. Let the learner point instead of speaking.
- **Reading/language access:** Teach each word with the diagram first,
  word card second; welcome home-language labels next to English ones.
- **Alternate response mode:** The learner may answer every check by
  pointing at the diagrams, or record an audio answer the adult keeps.
- **Extension:** "Which two landforms could be neighbors?" (A river can
  run through a valley or across a plain; a lake can sit in a valley.) The
  learner draws the pair touching.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconception:** learners often call every raised bump a
"mountain" and every blue shape "the ocean." Re-anchor with size and
flow: "A hill is a bump; a mountain is a giant — our diagram's bump is a
hill." "Blue water that *flows* is a river; blue water *surrounded by
land* is a lake; blue water touching sand at the land's edge is the
ocean at a coast." A second common slip: saying "valley" for any low
place — insist on "low land *between hills*," pointing at the diagram.
