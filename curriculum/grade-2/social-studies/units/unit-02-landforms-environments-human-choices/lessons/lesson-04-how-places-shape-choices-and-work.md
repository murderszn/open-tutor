# Lesson 04 — How Places Shape Choices and Work

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** the printed [Riverside Valley
illustration](../assets/riverside-valley-illustration.png) (or its
text-only description in [Lesson 03](lesson-03-people-change-places.md)),
vocabulary word cards (goods, services, choice), paper and crayons

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will learn the other direction
of the two-way street: how the land shapes what work people do and what
choices they make — and what your community makes versus what comes from
far away.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "Yesterday we learned people change
places. Can places change *people* back? Think: would you wear the same
clothes at the beach and in the snow?" Accept any reasoning. "Today the
land gets its turn."

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

"Last lesson, people changed the valley. But the valley changed the
people first. Look at the Riverside Valley picture again and ask: **why**
did people farm *here* and fish *there*?"

Point to each pairing:

1. "**Flat plain → farm work.** The land is wide and flat, so people
   grow crops in neat rows. The plain *shaped* their work: you cannot
   plow rows on a steep hill."
2. "**River and lake → fishing.** The water holds fish, so two people
   fish from the shore. The water *shaped* their work too."
3. "**Hills → where houses are NOT.** The houses sit on flat land near
   the fields, not on the steep hills. The hills shaped a *choice*: build
   where the land is flat."

"Now the **goods** — things people make or grow. This valley's goods
might be vegetables from the farm and fish from the lake. But the people
here do not make *everything*. Their clothes, their tools, the wood for
the bridge maybe came from **other communities** — places with different
land. Every community makes some goods and gets other goods from far
away. **Services** are helpful work people do: teaching, healing,
fixing things, driving the delivery truck."

Key idea to say plainly: **The land shapes people's work and choices.
Flat land invites farming; water invites fishing. Each community makes
some goods and services, and trades for the rest from other
communities.**

**Worked example 1 — matching work to land:**

> Adult: "I will name a job, and we match it to the land. 'Fisher.' Where
> in the picture would a fisher work — on the hilltop or at the lake? The
> lake, because fish live in water. The **landform picked the job**. Now
> 'farmer': hilltop or plain? The plain — flat land for rows of crops. I
> matched the job to what the land offers."

**Worked example 2 — our goods and far-away goods:**

> Adult: "Think of our own community. What do people here make or grow?
> *(Adult and learner name 1–2 true local examples together — for
> example, 'the bakery on Main Street makes bread; the farm outside town
> grows corn.' Use only real, safe examples the adult knows.)* Now what
> comes from far away? Bananas do not grow in cold places — they travel
> from warm places. Shoes, books, and toys are made in other communities
> too. **Every place makes some things and welcomes things made
> elsewhere.**"

## Guided practice (adult leads, learner decides)

Using the illustration or its description: (Hints in parentheses are for
the adult.)

1. "Point to where a farmer works. Why there?" *(the flat plain — flat
   land for crop rows)*
2. "Point to where a fisher works. Why there?" *(the river/lake — fish
   live in water)*
3. "Why are the houses on flat land instead of on the steep hills?"
   *(flat land is easier and safer to build on — the land shaped the
   choice)*
4. "Name one good the valley people make." *(vegetables/crops, fish —
   from the farm and water)*
5. "Name one thing the valley people probably get from another
   community." *(clothes, tools, wood — things the valley does not make;
   accept any reasonable answer)*

## Independent practice

The learner draws a line down the middle of a paper. Left side: "Made
here" — draw one good the Riverside Valley makes (crops, fish). Right
side: "Comes from far away" — draw one thing the valley gets from
another community (clothes, tools). The adult scribes dictated labels.

## Application

Our-community sort: the adult names 6 familiar items (bread from the
local bakery, apples from a nearby orchard, bananas, a storybook, shoes,
milk from a local dairy — adjust to true local facts) and the learner
sorts each into "made near us" or "made far away," saying one clue for
each ("Bananas need a hot climate — far away").

## Exit check

Prompt: "Tell me one job and the landform that fits it, and one thing
your community gets from another community." Accept: e.g., "Farmers work
on plains"; "Fishers work at lakes and rivers"; "We get bananas (or
books, or shoes) from far away."

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** Keep to the picture — farmer/plain, fisher/water — before
  trying the local-community sort. Let the learner point instead of
  speaking.
- **Reading/language access:** Teach "goods" (things you can hold) and
  "services" (helpful work) with real objects and actions first.
- **Alternate response mode:** The learner may sort picture cards (farm
  tools, fish, shirt, book) onto "valley-made" and "far-away" mats.
- **Extension:** "Why would a town on the coast have different jobs than
  a town on a plain?" (Coast: fishing, boats, visitors; plain: farms.)
  The learner draws both towns' main job.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconception:** learners often think everything they use is
made where they live ("our town makes bananas"). Re-anchor gently:
"Could bananas grow in our winter? No — so they must travel from a warm
place." A second common slip: reversing the direction ("farming made the
plain flat") — replay the two-way street: "The plain was flat first;
*that* is why people chose to farm it."
