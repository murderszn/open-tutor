# Lesson 05 — Two Communities, One Earth

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** the printed [Riverside Valley
illustration](../assets/riverside-valley-illustration.png) (or its
text-only description in [Lesson 03](lesson-03-people-change-places.md)),
the "City" text portrait below, vocabulary word cards (city,
countryside, community), paper and crayons

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will compare two communities —
a countryside community and a city — and explain how each one uses its
environment to meet daily needs like food, water, and shelter.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "Is our community more like a city or
more like the countryside? What do you see around us — many tall
buildings close together, or open land with farms and spread-out homes?"
Accept the learner's description. "Today we compare the two."

## Two community portraits

**Portrait A — Riverside Valley (countryside).** Use the printed
illustration or read its text-only description from Lesson 03: a valley
with hills, a river, farm fields on a plain, a lake, a few houses, a
dirt road, a bridge, people farming and fishing.

**Portrait B — Harbor City (city).** *(Original text portrait — read
aloud; no real city is named or pictured.)*

"Harbor City sits on a **coast**, where land meets the ocean. Tall
buildings stand close together, and streets are paved and busy. There
are no farm fields inside the city — food arrives on trucks from farms
far away and on boats from the harbor. Many people work in stores,
offices, schools, and hospitals — those are **services**. Some people
fish from the harbor, because the ocean is right there. Children play in
small parks between the buildings, and families live in apartments
stacked on top of each other. When it rains, the paved streets carry the
water away through drains."

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

"A **community** is the people who live in one place and share it. Let's
compare our two communities side by side — same questions, different
answers."

Draw a simple two-column chart (adult draws; learner dictates):

| Question | Riverside Valley (countryside) | Harbor City (city) |
|---|---|---|
| Where do homes stand? | A few houses, spread out on flat land | Many buildings close together, apartments stacked up |
| Where does food come from? | Farm fields and the lake, nearby | Trucks from far-away farms; boats from the harbor |
| What work do people do? | Farming, fishing | Store, office, school, and hospital work; harbor fishing |
| How does the land shape it? | Flat plain → farms; water → fishing | Coast → harbor and fishing; little open land → build upward |

"Now the big idea: **both communities meet the same daily needs** —
food, water, shelter — but they use their environments **differently**.
The valley uses its plain and water directly. The city uses its coast
for the harbor, and trades with far-away farms for food. Different land,
different plan — same needs met."

Key idea to say plainly: **A city and a countryside community both need
food, water, and shelter. The countryside uses nearby land and water;
the city builds close together and brings in what it needs from far
away.**

**Worked example 1 — same need, different answer:**

> Adult: "Need: food. In Riverside Valley, where does food come from? The
> farm fields and the lake — right nearby. In Harbor City, where does
> food come from? Trucks bring it from farms far away, and boats bring
> fish from the harbor. **Same need — food. Different answer**, because
> the city has no farm fields inside it. I compared by asking the same
> question of both places."

**Worked example 2 — the land decides:**

> Adult: "Why doesn't Harbor City just plant farm fields downtown? Look
> at the portrait: tall buildings stand close together; streets are paved.
> There is no open flat land left for crops. The **environment shaped the
> choice** — with no fields, the city trades for food instead of growing
> it. The valley has the opposite: open plain, so it farms."

## Guided practice (adult leads, learner decides)

The learner answers from the two portraits. (Hints in parentheses are
for the adult.)

1. "Where do homes stand in each community?" *(valley: few houses spread
   out; city: many buildings close together)*
2. "Where does each community's food come from?" *(valley: nearby farms
   and lake; city: trucks from far-away farms, boats from the harbor)*
3. "Name one job from each place." *(valley: farmer, fisher; city: store
   worker, teacher, harbor fisher — accept any portrait-based job)*
4. "Which community has a coast? How does the coast help it?" *(Harbor
   City — the harbor brings boats, fish, and traded goods)*
5. "Name one daily need both communities meet." *(food, water, or
   shelter — both meet all three, differently)*

## Independent practice

The learner draws the two communities side by side: on the left, one
valley home with a field; on the right, one city block with stacked
apartments. Under each, the adult scribes the learner's dictated
sentence: "In the valley, food grows ___" / "In the city, food comes
___."

## Application

Which community? The adult reads clues and the learner points to the
valley picture or says "Harbor City": "This community gets fish from the
ocean harbor" (city); "This community grows vegetables in rows on a
plain" (valley); "This community's children play in a small park between
tall buildings" (city); "This community crosses a wooden bridge over a
river" (valley).

## Exit check

Prompt: "Tell me one way the city and the countryside are different, and
one need they both meet." Accept: any true difference (homes close vs.
spread out; food nearby vs. brought in; jobs) plus food, water, or
shelter as the shared need.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** Compare only homes and food first; add work and the
  coast after. Let the learner point to the chart instead of speaking.
- **Reading/language access:** Keep the two-column chart visible the
  whole lesson; point to the column as each question is asked.
- **Alternate response mode:** The learner may sort clue cards onto
  "valley" and "city" mats instead of answering aloud.
- **Extension:** "What would happen if the trucks stopped coming to
  Harbor City for one week?" (Food would run short — the city *depends*
  on far-away farms. The valley would still have its fields.) The
  learner draws what each community eats that week.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconception:** learners often rank the communities ("the city
is better" or "farms are boring"). Re-anchor: "Different is not better
or worse — each community fits its land. The question is only: how does
each place meet its needs?" A second common slip: saying city people
"don't need farms" — correct gently: "They need farms *very much*; the
farms are just far away, and trucks bring the food."
