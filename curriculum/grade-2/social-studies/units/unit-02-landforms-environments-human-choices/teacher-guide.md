# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Landforms, Environments, and Human Choices

**For the guiding adult.** This is the only file that discusses pacing
judgments, readiness calls, and re-teaching. Keep it with your planning
materials, not with the learner's pages.

## Unit at a glance

- **Weeks:** 7–10 of the track · **Sessions:** 16, at 25–30 minutes, 4 per
  week · **Compelling question:** *How does where you live change how you
  live?*
- **Arc:** Week 7 names the land (six landforms) → Week 8 separates
  weather from climate and traces effects on daily life → Week 9 walks
  the two-way street (people change places; places shape work and
  choices) → Week 10 compares a city and a countryside community, then
  reviews.
- **Routines to protect:** the 2-minute retrieval warm-up that opens
  every session (start each week with the compass-rose check from
  Unit 01); the "today = weather / usually = climate" time-clue habit;
  the before/after frame for people-made change. These return in Units
  05, 06, and 08 — teach them as reusable tools, not one-week tricks.

## What "ready for Unit 03" looks like

The learner is ready when **all** of these are true:

1. Names 5–6 landforms on the diagrams without labels and tells river
   from lake by flow ("a river flows; a lake sits still").
2. Sorts new "today / usually" sentences correctly and gives one
   weather effect and one climate effect on daily life.
3. Names two people-made changes in the Riverside Valley picture and one
   job–landform pair with a reason.
4. Compares the two communities on at least homes and food, naming one
   difference and one shared need.
5. Project rubric score of 6–8 **or** 4–5 with a successful re-present.

If the unit assessment lands at 8–10, run **one** targeted review
session on the weakest part (see below), then move on — do not repeat
the whole unit. Below 8, re-teach the weakest part across 2–3 short
sessions before Unit 03.

## Reteach map (by objective)

| Weak spot | Reteach move (15–20 min) |
|---|---|
| Landform naming (Obj. 1) | Back to three: hill, river, lake. Pour-water demo (tray = river, bowl = lake). Add the other three only when the first three are instant. |
| Weather vs. climate (Obj. 2) | One week of "morning weather reports": learner states today's weather daily, then you ask "and what is *usually* true in this season?" Keep a two-column tally on paper. |
| People→place direction (Obj. 3a) | Replay the before/after drawing with eyes-closed erasing: "Erase every people-made thing. What is left?" Sort 6 new items into "people made / nature made." |
| Place→people direction (Obj. 3b) | Job-to-land matching with the valley picture only. Ask "could a farmer work on the steep hill? Show me why not" until the learner points at the slope. |
| City vs. countryside (Obj. 4) | Keep the two-column chart up for a full week. Ask the same two questions (homes? food?) about a third place you invent together. |
| Local vs. far-away goods (Obj. 5) | Empty one grocery bag together (real or pretend). For each item ask "could this grow or be made near us?" Let the climate decide. |

## Session-by-session notes

- **Sessions 1–4 (Week 7):** Do not rush the six diagrams. If Session 1
  ends with only hill/river/lake solid, teach valley/plain/coast in
  Session 2's warm-up and shift the photo hunt to Session 3 — the pacing
  table's success criteria still hold.
- **Session 3 (photo hunt):** pre-screen every photo yourself. Reject any
  image with readable street signs, license plates, or identifiable
  people. If a photo is ambiguous (is that a hill or a mountain?),
  **say so out loud** — "geographers would argue about this one" is a
  good lesson, not a failure.
- **Session 7 (read-aloud):** "A Year in Two Places" is an original
  passage in [practice.md](practice.md) (Practice set B, item 5 context).
  Read it slowly, twice; the second read is for the learner to interrupt
  with observations.
- **Sessions 12 & 14 (project):** approve the plan *before* drawing
  begins — most project failures are plan failures (a coast community
  with wheat fields and no boats). During the showcase, score silently
  and give one strength + one next step aloud.
- **Session 15 (quiz + review game):** quiz first, then the review game:
  *Landform Charades* (act a landform; others guess), *Weather/Climate
  Snap* (slap the table for "today," clap for "usually"), and the
  compass-rose relay from Unit 01. End with the learner's hardest-won
  item from the quiz, answered correctly — confidence matters going into
  Session 16.
- **Session 16 (assessment):** read every item aloud, allow pointing and
  drawing, and take breaks. Score afterward with the answer key; share
  one strength and one next step with the learner the same day.

## Misconceptions to watch (also in each lesson's teacher note)

1. "Mountain" for every bump; "ocean" for every blue shape. Fix with
   size (hill < mountain) and flow (river flows, lake sits, ocean meets
   land at a coast).
2. Weather = climate. Fix with the time clue, every time: *today* versus
   *usually, year after year*.
3. Farms and roads "were always there." Fix with the eyes-closed erasing
   routine.
4. "Our town makes everything." Fix with one impossible local product
   (bananas in a cold climate is the classic).
5. Ranking the communities. Fix: "Different land, different plan — same
   needs."
