# Project — Design a Community for a Landform

**A design-and-present project.** Session 12 (plan the community; the
adult approves the plan) and Session 14 (finish the poster and present
it). Showcased in Session 14; the adult reviews the poster the same day.
Adult-supervised throughout.

## Driving question

*If your family moved to a new landform, how would you build a community
that fits the land?*

## Materials

Large paper or poster board; crayons or colored pencils; the printed
[landform diagrams](assets/landform-diagrams.svg); the printed
[Riverside Valley illustration](assets/riverside-valley-illustration.png)
as a model (or its text-only description in [Lesson
03](lessons/lesson-03-people-change-places.md)); vocabulary word cards.
No real addresses, learner photos, or personal details go on the poster.

## Steps

1. **Choose your landform.** With the adult, pick one of the six
   landforms the community will be built on or beside: hill, valley,
   plain, river, lake, or coast. Say why: "I pick the coast because I
   want boats."
2. **Plan before drawing.** Tell the adult your plan and get approval:
   - Which **landform** is your community on? (Name it and point to it
     on the diagrams.)
   - What **two or more buildings** will people build? (Houses, a farm
     barn, a fishing dock, a store — your choice, but they must fit the
     land.)
   - What **work** will people do there? (Name one job and the landform
     that fits it: "Farmers on the plain.")
   - What is the **weather or climate** like, and what will people wear
     or build because of it? (Name one choice: "Rainy climate — slanted
     roofs.")
   The adult approves the plan or asks for one fix.
3. **Draw the poster.** Draw the community from above or from the side
   (learner's choice). Include:
   - the **landform**, drawn with its true shape and **labeled**;
   - **2 or more buildings** people made;
   - **one road, path, or bridge** connecting the buildings;
   - **one weather/climate detail** (sun, rain, snow, wind — plus the
     clothing or shelter choice it causes);
   - **people doing the work** you named (tiny figures are fine).
4. **Dictate the tour.** Pretend a newcomer just arrived. Dictate **3 or
   more sentences** the adult scribes word-for-word under or beside the
   drawing: where the community is, what people built, what work they
   do, and one weather/climate choice. Example: "Our town is on a plain.
   We built houses and a barn. We are farmers because the land is flat.
   It is usually hot, so our houses have shade trees."
5. **Present.** In Session 14, present the poster to the adult (or record
   audio): point to each part and give the tour aloud. The adult scores
   with the rubric below and tells the learner one strength and one
   next step the same day.

## Deliverables

- One poster: labeled landform, 2+ buildings, one road/path/bridge, one
  weather/climate detail, people at work.
- One dictated tour of 3+ sentences, scribed by the adult.
- One oral (or recorded) presentation of the poster.

## Rubric

Score each row 0–2. **6–8: ready to move on · 4–5: one reteach session,
then present again · 0–3: rebuild with the adult's help.**

| Row | 2 — Strong | 1 — Getting there | 0 — Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Landform accuracy** | The landform is drawn with its true shape (e.g., flowing lines for a river, land all around a lake) and labeled correctly | The shape is close but the label is missing or mismatched | The landform cannot be recognized or is mislabeled |
| **People changed the place** | 2+ buildings plus a road, path, or bridge, all drawn and named in the tour | Only buildings *or* only a path; the other is missing | No people-made structures are shown |
| **Place shaped the choices** | The job fits the landform AND one weather/climate choice is shown and explained ("farmers on the plain"; "slanted roofs for rain") | The job fits the land *or* a weather choice is shown, but not both | The work and weather choices do not connect to the landform |
| **Communication** | The 3+ sentence tour names the landform, the buildings, the work, and the weather choice in order | The tour covers two or three of the four | The tour covers one or none, or cannot be followed |

**What "ready" looks like:** the poster shows a recognizable, labeled
landform; people clearly changed the place (buildings + a way to travel);
the work and at least one weather/climate choice fit the land; and the
learner can give the tour in order. Keep the poster with the unit
materials — Unit 08's civic project reuses the "fit the land" routine.
