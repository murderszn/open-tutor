# Answer Key — Unit 02: Landforms, Environments, and Human Choices

**For the guiding adult only.** Never place this file beside a student
question. Every answer below was solved independently against the lesson
prose and the unit's images, then reconciled. Accept any wording that
carries the same meaning; grade-2 answers may be oral, pointed, or drawn.

---

## Lesson 01 — Meeting the landforms

**Guided practice**

1. Valley — low land between hills (or mountains). Accept "the low place
   between the hills."
2. Lake — water with land all around it. Difference from a river: a river
   *flows* across the land; a lake sits still, hugged by land.
3. Coast — where land meets the ocean.
4. Hill — raised land, lower than a mountain. (If the learner says
   "mountain," re-anchor: "Our diagram's bump is a hill; a mountain is
   much taller.")
5. A plain is wide and flat, so farm machines, animals, and people can
   move easily and plant straight rows.

**Independent practice:** accept any two recognizable landform drawings
with a correct label (adult-scribed labels count). The dictated sentence
must be true of the drawing ("A river flows"; "A lake has land all
around it").

**Application:** water pile = river, lake; land pile = hill, valley,
plain; "both" = coast, with the reason "land meets the ocean there."

**Exit check:** all six names in any order — hill, valley, plain, river,
lake, coast — and a point to the lake panel. Five of six plus the adult
supplying the sixth (learner repeats) counts as passing with support.

## Lesson 02 — Weather and climate

**Guided practice**

1. Weather — "today."
2. Climate — "almost every afternoon" / "every year" (usually). Shelter
   built for usual rain.
3. Weather — "this morning" (today).
4. Climate — "summers" / "long and hot" (usually); evening play shaped by
   usual heat.
5. Umbrella: weather — grab it for rain *today*. Accept "both" with a
   reason: a rainy climate means the family owns one.

**Independent practice:** two pictures, one showing a single day's
weather and one showing a usual pattern; the learner correctly circles
"today" (weather) and "usually" (climate). Dictated sentences must match
the picture.

**Application (dress the character):** (a) matches the adult's stated
real weather with a reason; (b) light clothes, sun hat, water bottle —
"usually hot and dry"; (c) heavy coat, boots, mittens — "usually cold
and snowy." 3 of 4 reasoned choices pass.

**Exit check:** weather = what the sky is doing today (one example);
climate = what weather is usually like in a place, year after year (one
example). One clear example of each = full credit.

## Lesson 03 — People change places

**Guided practice** (against the Riverside Valley illustration and its
text description)

1. Farm: people cleared flat grassy land and planted neat crop rows.
2. The road: open grassland — people packed down the dirt path.
3. Houses sit on flat land near the fields so farm families live close
   to their work; flat land is easier to build on than steep hills.
4. The bridge solves crossing the river — walk across dry instead of
   wading or being stuck.
5. Any natural feature: the hills, the river itself, the trees, the sun,
   the lake water.

**Independent practice:** "before" side shows only natural features;
"after" side adds one people-made change; the dictated caption fills all
three blanks truthfully.

**Application:** any one true, safe example of a people-made change to a
familiar place ("people built the school"; "people planted the trees in
rows"; "people made the sidewalk").

**Exit check:** any two of farms, roads, buildings/bridge + one helping
reason (food, travel, shelter, dry crossing).

## Lesson 04 — Places shape choices and work

**Guided practice**

1. Farmer → the flat plain: flat land fits straight crop rows; you cannot
   plow a steep hill.
2. Fisher → the river/lake: fish live in water.
3. Houses are on flat land because it is easier and safer to build on
   than steep hills — the land shaped the choice.
4. Valley goods: vegetables/crops from the farm fields; fish from the
   river/lake.
5. Far-away goods: clothes, tools, lumber, or anything the valley does
   not make — accept any reasonable answer with a reason.

**Independent practice:** left side shows one valley-made good (crops,
fish); right side shows one far-away good (clothes, tools); labels
dictated correctly.

**Application (our-community sort):** judge against true local facts.
Typical answers: local bakery bread → near; nearby orchard apples → near;
bananas → far (need a hot climate); storybook/shoes → far (made in other
communities); local dairy milk → near. The clue matters more than the
pile: "Bananas need a hot climate — far away."

**Exit check:** one job–landform pair (farmer/plain, fisher/river or
lake) + one far-away good with a reason.

## Lesson 05 — Two communities

**Guided practice**

1. Homes: valley — a few houses spread out on flat land; city — many
   buildings close together, apartments stacked up.
2. Food: valley — farm fields and the lake nearby; city — trucks from
   far-away farms and boats from the harbor.
3. Jobs: valley — farmer, fisher; city — store/office/school/hospital
   worker, harbor fisher. Accept any job stated in the portraits.
4. Harbor City has the coast; the harbor brings boats, fish, and traded
   goods.
5. Food, water, or shelter — both communities meet all three, differently.

**Independent practice:** two drawings with dictated sentences that
correctly complete "In the valley, food grows ___" (in the fields /
nearby) and "In the city, food comes ___" (on trucks / from far away).

**Application (which community?):** harbor fishing → city; vegetable rows
on a plain → valley; small park between tall buildings → city; wooden
bridge over a river → valley.

**Exit check:** one true difference + one shared need (food, water, or
shelter).

## Practice set A (Week 7)

1. 4 of 6 named = pass; re-teach misses on the spot.
2. Water: river, lake. Land: hill, valley, plain. Both: coast — "where
   land meets the ocean."
3. Covered-label naming: same standard as Lesson 01 exit check.
4. Clue match: river, valley, lake, coast, plain (in the order given).
5. Drawing + label + one true proving sentence.

## Practice set B (Week 8)

1. "Raining this morning" → weather. "Winters cold and snowy every
   year" → **climate** (the teaching item — "every year" is the clue).
   "Wind strong today" → weather. "Summers long and hot" → climate. "Wear
   coats December to March" → climate (a usual pattern).
2. Three outfits, each with a true reason (see Lesson 02 application).
3. Two houses with climate-fitting details + one true dictated sentence
   each.
4. Activity answers must pair the activity with a time frame and
   clothing (e.g., "snowman — winter climate — coat and mittens").
5. Retell: one true weather choice per place with its reason.

## Practice set C (Week 9)

1. People-made (3): farm fields, road, houses, bridge — any three.
   Nature-made (2): hills, river, trees, lake, sun — any two.
2. Before/after tell: "before" names natural features only; "after"
   names the change + how it helps.
3. Farmer → plain; fisher → river/lake; builder → flat land near fields.
   "Bridge keeper": there is none in the picture — the correct answer is
   "that job is not in the picture" (or any answer noting the trick).
   Praise noticing.
4. Near/far sort judged against true local facts; the spoken clue counts
   most.
5. "People changed the valley by ___" — farm/road/houses/bridge.
   "The valley changed people by ___" — where to farm, fish, or build
   (the land's shape deciding).

## Week-04 quiz (10 items; 7 to pass)

1. 5–6 named = 1 point; fewer = 0.
2. River — "flowing" is the clue; lakes sit still.
3. Weather — clue "today."
4. Climate — clue "every winter" (usually, year after year).
5. Any two of: farm fields, dirt road, houses, wooden bridge.
6. Any true pair with a reason, e.g., "Farmers work on the plain because
   it is flat for crop rows."
7. The city (Harbor City) — tall buildings close together, food arriving
   on trucks.
8. Drawing shows land touching water at an edge, labeled "coast"; the
   spoken definition: "where land meets the ocean."
9. One true local good + one true far-away good (adult judges against
   local facts; examples: local bread/milk/apples vs. bananas, shoes,
   storybooks).
10. Any one true example from the unit, e.g., "People near the lake fish
    because the water has fish"; "We wear coats because winter is usually
    cold"; "Farmers use plains because the land is flat."

## Unit assessment (14 points)

1. 6 correct = 2; 4–5 = 1; fewer = 0.
2. Shape + label = 1; true proving sentence = 1.
3. (a) weather — "this afternoon"; (b) climate — "every year." 1 point
   per item (label + clue word both required).
4. Any true adaptation: slanted roofs shed rain; umbrellas/rain gear by
   the door; raised floors; gutters. Accept reasonable answers.
5. Any two of: farm fields, dirt road, houses, wooden bridge. 1 point
   each.
6. Fish live in water — 1 point.
7. One true local example + one true far-away example — 1 point (adult
   judges against local facts).
8. Riverside Valley grows food nearby = 1; Harbor City brings food by
   truck from far-away farms and by boat from the harbor = 1.
9. One true difference + one shared need (food, water, shelter) = 1.
