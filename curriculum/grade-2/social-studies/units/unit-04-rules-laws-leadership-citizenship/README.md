# Unit 04 — Rules, Laws, Leadership, and Citizenship

**Grade:** 2 (ages 7–8) · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track issue:** [#17](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/17)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 15–18 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, 25–30 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

This unit teaches the civics tools the rest of the year reuses: why
communities need **rules** and what rules are for; how a **law** differs
from a classroom or family rule; who **people in authority** are and what
their jobs are; what a **government** is and some of the services it
provides; that **all people** — not just leaders — play important roles
in a community; the democratic principles of **equality**, **fairness**,
and **respect** for legitimate authority and rules; how to follow
**agreed-upon rules for discussions**; how to compare your own
**point of view** with someone else's; and how to make a group decision
through **listening**, **consensus**, and **voting**. The unit's project
— "Our Classroom Rulebook and Community Helper Plan" — is a three-rule
rulebook with reasons, a real class vote run by the learner's group, and
one small, adult-supervised plan to help the community. The discussion
rules and the vote routine return in Units 06, 07, and 08.

All of the unit's local examples use the fictional Maple Lane
neighborhood from Units 01 and 03. Maple Lane is invented for practice.
Nothing in this unit is a real town's law or a real person's story, and
no real officeholder is named. The adult says so out loud whenever a
practice example is shown: "This is a practice story, not a real town's
rule." The unit never asks the learner about their family's real
opinions, votes, or political views; every vote in this unit is about a
classroom choice (story time or block time, which game, which book).

## Prerequisites (developmental expectations; re-taught here as needed)

- Follow simple rules and routines; take turns; listen when others speak.
- Name familiar people, places, and roles in home, school, and neighborhood.
- Retell a sequence of 2–3 familiar events in order (first, next, last).
- Point, draw, and describe observations; dictate a sentence for an adult
  to scribe.
- Read a simple map with a title, symbols, a key, labels, and a compass
  rose (Unit 01); compare life in the past with life today (Unit 03).

Diagnostic Weeks 1–2 probed these with play-based tasks. If turn-taking
or listening is shaky, re-teach Lesson 05's discussion rules as a game
before any decision-making content.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Explain** the need for and purposes of rules in settings inside and
   outside of school, and **identify** how rules function in public
   (classroom and school) settings (D2.Civ.3.K-2, D2.Civ.12.K-2).
2. **Distinguish** a rule from a law: a law is a rule made by a
   government that applies to everyone in that place; **give** one
   classroom rule, one family rule, and one community law, each with its
   purpose (D2.Civ.3.K-2, D2.Civ.5.K-2).
3. **Describe** roles and responsibilities of people in authority
   (teacher, principal, police officer, firefighter, mayor, council
   member); **explain** what governments are and some of their
   functions; and **explain** how all people, not just official leaders,
   play important roles in a community (D2.Civ.1.K-2, D2.Civ.2.K-2,
   D2.Civ.5.K-2, D2.Civ.6.K-2).
4. **Apply** civic virtues and democratic principles in school settings:
   name what is fair about a rule, tell whether everyone is treated
   equally by it, and show respect for legitimate authority and rules
   (D2.Civ.7.K-2, D2.Civ.8.K-2).
5. **Follow** agreed-upon rules for discussions while responding
   attentively to others; **compare** their own point of view with
   another person's perspective; and **explain** how people can work
   together to make decisions in the classroom (D2.Civ.9.K-2,
   D2.Civ.10.K-2, D2.Civ.11.K-2).
6. **Use** listening, consensus-building, and voting procedures to decide
   on and take action in the classroom; **construct** an argument with
   reasons for a classroom choice; and **describe** how people have tried
   to improve their communities over time (D4.8.K-2, D4.1.K-2,
   D2.Civ.14.K-2).

With adult support, the learner identifies the disciplinary ideas behind
the unit's compelling question — rules, laws, leaders, fairness, voting
(D1.2.K-2).

## Essential question

*Who decides what's fair, and how?*

## Vocabulary

**Rule** — an agreement about how to act in a place, so everyone stays
safe, is treated fairly, and can get along and learn. · **Law** — a rule
made by a government that applies to everyone in that place. ·
**Government** — the people and offices that make and carry out rules
for a community and provide services everyone shares. · **Authority** —
a person whose job gives them the responsibility to lead, decide, or
enforce rules in a place (a teacher in a classroom, a principal in a
school, a mayor in a town). · **Leader** — a person who guides a group.
· **Mayor** — the person who leads a town or city government. ·
**Council** — a group of people chosen to help make rules and decisions
for a town or city. · **Service** — help a government provides for
everyone, such as schools, roads, parks, libraries, and clean water. ·
**Fair** — when everyone is treated by the same rules and everyone gets
a fair chance. · **Equality** — everyone counts the same; no one counts
extra. · **Respect** — treating people, rules, and authority with care,
even when you disagree. · **Virtue** — a good way of acting, such as
honesty, kindness, and responsibility. · **Point of view** — what one
person thinks or wants; different people can see the same choice
differently. · **Discussion** — talking together about an idea, taking
turns and listening. · **Consensus** — a decision everyone in the group
agrees to, or can live with, after talking it over. · **Vote** — a way
to decide in which each person chooses once, and the choice with the most
votes wins. · **Citizen** (in this unit's sense) — a member of a
community who follows its rules, respects others, and helps make it
better. · **Community** — a group of people who live, learn, or work in
the same place and share rules and services.

(teacher, principal, police officer, firefighter, mayor, council member,
crossing guard, librarian, bus driver, neighbor, child); the printed
[Maple Lane class-vote illustration](assets/maple-lane-class-vote.png)
*(AI-generated illustration, inspected 2026-10-30 — every image-based
task ships with a full text-only alternative)*; a small box or jar for
the practice ballot; paper and crayons for the rulebook and helper-plan
poster. All activities are adult-supervised. No real addresses, learner
photos, family opinions, or personal information appear anywhere in the
learner's work; the learner's own rulebook uses only rules and choices
the learner and adult choose together, kept in private storage.

## Differentiation and accessibility

- Oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative response modes for every check;
  the adult scribes dictated writing; short labeled products reviewed the
  same day.
- Large-print rule and role cards; role-play with costumes or props for
  learners who learn by doing; a talking stick or turn-taking card for
  discussion rounds.
- Vocabulary taught with pictures and actions first, word second; visual
  word wall; home-language labels welcomed alongside English.
- Short sessions with movement breaks; every lesson has a seated-table
  and a floor-play variant (rule sorting on the floor, vote by standing
  behind a choice card).
- Every illustration ships with a text-only alternative; color is never
  the only cue (labels always accompany color).
- Hearing support: face the learner during discussions; visual
  turn-taking cues; written/drawn option for every spoken check.
- Speaking tasks accept audio recording in place of live presentation; the
  adult may record on the learner's behalf.

## Safety

Adult supervises every activity and previews every procedure, read-aloud,
and practice example. Practice examples are fictional and adult-prepared;
no real family opinions, votes, or political views are collected or
discussed. Learner rulebooks stay in private storage — never in this
public repository. No real addresses or learner-identifying details
appear in any product. Role-play stays indoors or on familiar, safe
ground with the adult. No sharp tools. If a real community-helper visit
or walk is arranged, it is planned and supervised by the adult outside
this unit's materials.
