# Practice — Unit 04: Rules, Laws, Leadership, and Citizenship

All tasks are oral, pointed, or drawn unless noted; the adult scribes
dictated writing. Each set is 4–6 short tasks. Bar: 4 of 5 correct
(sets A and B have 5 tasks; set C has 6 — bar 5 of 6).

## Week 15 — Rules and laws

### Set A

1. **Rule sort.** The adult names four rules; the learner points to the
   HOME, CLASSROOM, PLAYGROUND, or PUBLIC card for each: "put toys away"
   (home), "raise your hand" (classroom), "take turns on the slide"
   (playground), "use quiet voices in the library" (public).
2. **Purpose match.** The adult names a rule; the learner names its
   purpose — safety, fairness, or getting along: "walk in the hallway"
   (safety), "wait in line" (fairness), "listen when others speak"
   (getting along).
3. **Rule or law?** The adult shows four cards; the learner sorts them
   into RULE or LAW: "raise your hand in class" (rule), "use quiet
   voices in the library" (rule), "stop at a red light" (law), "do not
   take what belongs to someone else" (law).
4. **The two-question test.** The adult asks about "stop at a red
   light": "Who made it? Who must follow it?" The learner answers:
   the government made it; every driver must follow it. Then the
   learner repeats the test for "raise your hand in class" (teacher;
   the class).
5. **No-rules story.** The adult reads: "In a game with no rules, Mia
   grabbed the ball and ran away. Nobody else got to play." The learner
   names what went wrong and one rule that would fix it (for example,
   "take turns with the ball").

## Week 16 — Helpers and government services

### Set B

1. **Role match.** The adult shows five role cards; the learner matches
   each to its job: teacher → teaches the class; principal → leads the
   school; police officer → keeps people safe and enforces laws;
   firefighter → puts out fires and rescues people; mayor → leads the
   town.
2. **Service sort.** The adult names six things; the learner points to
   SERVICE (provided for everyone) or NOT A SERVICE: school (service),
   road (service), park (service), library (service), your own lunchbox
   (not a service), a toy you own (not a service).
3. **Who helps?** The adult asks: "A tree fell across the Maple Lane
   path. Who helps?" The learner names two roles that must work
   together (for example, town workers clear the tree; neighbors tell
   people the path is closed; the council decides on a new tree).
4. **Everyone's role.** The adult asks: "What is one job a child does in
   a community?" The learner answers (follows rules, helps classmates,
   votes in class choices, keeps shared places clean).
5. **Maple Lane cleanup.** The adult narrates: "The park has litter."
   The learner orders three steps: first the council decides a cleanup
   day, next town workers bring bins, last neighbors and children pick
   up litter — and says why one role alone cannot do it all.

## Week 17 — Fairness, viewpoints, and votes

### Set C

1. **Fairness test.** The adult proposes: "Only children wearing blue
   get the new books first." The learner runs the three questions
   (same rule for everyone? fair chance? safe and getting along?) and
   gives a verdict: not fair, because question one fails.
2. **Two points of view.** The adult reads: "Sam wants tag. Priya wants
   hide-and-seek." The learner retells Sam's view and Priya's view in
   their own words, then names one fair way to decide (take turns, or
   vote).
3. **Vote counts.** The adult gives three mini-votes; the learner names
   the winner and says whether it was fair: 4–1 for story time (story
   time wins, fair); 2–2 tie (tie — vote again or take turns; the
   adult explains a tie is not a win); 5 votes from 4 voters (not fair
   — someone voted twice).
4. **Losing fairly.** The adult asks: "Your choice lost 3–2. Was the
   vote unfair?" The learner answers: no, the vote was fair — everyone
   got one vote; losing is not unfair; your turn comes another day.
5. **Discussion rules.** The learner recites the four discussion rules
   (listen; wait your turn; speak kindly; respond to the idea, not the
   person) and shows each one with an action (finger to lips for
   listen, raised hand for wait, gentle voice for kindly).
6. **One-reason speech.** The learner gives a one-reason speech for a
   classroom choice: "We should choose ___ because ___." The adult
   checks: a choice, a reason, and a calm voice.

## Error analysis (any week)

The adult plants one mistake and the learner catches it: "I say the
library rule is a law, because libraries are big buildings." The
learner corrects: a library rule is a rule, not a law — the government
did not make it for everyone in town; it is the library's rule for
people inside the library. (Accept any correction that uses the
who-made-it / who-must-follow-it test.)
