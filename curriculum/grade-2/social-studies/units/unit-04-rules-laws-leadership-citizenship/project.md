# Project — Our Classroom Rulebook and Community Helper Plan

**Product (reviewed the same day):** a three-rule rulebook page with a
reason for each rule, one real class vote run by the learner's group,
and one community helper-plan poster — presented orally with drawings.

## What the learner makes

1. **Rulebook page (Session 12).** Three rules for the learner's own
   classroom (or, for a home learner, the learning space), each with a
   purpose or reason the learner can say aloud: "Rule: ___. It is for
   ___." The learner draws or places a picture per rule; the adult
   scribes the words. At least one rule must pass the fairness test
   when the adult checks it (same rule for everyone, fair chance, safe
   and getting along).
2. **Real class vote (Session 13).** The learner's group runs a real
   vote on a classroom choice (story time or block time, which game,
   which book) using the Lesson 05 routine: two choice cards, a ballot
   box or raised hands, one person one vote, count out loud, announce
   the winner. The learner records the result in one dictated sentence:
   "We voted. ___ won with ___ votes." The vote must be a real choice
   the group then follows.
3. **Helper-plan poster (Session 13).** One small, adult-supervised way
   to help the community, drawn as a poster: what the action is (for
   example, "pick up litter in the park," "make a poster that says
   'quiet voices in the library,'" "plant flowers by the path"), who it
   helps, and which adult will supervise it. The poster is a plan, not
   a promise to go out alone — no unsupervised community action is part
   of this project.
4. **Showcase (Session 15).** The learner presents all three parts
   orally (or by audio recording): reads the rulebook, reports the vote
   result, and explains the helper plan. The adult asks one follow-up
   question ("What made your vote fair?") and the learner answers in
   their own words.

## Materials

Paper, crayons or colored pencils, rule and role cards from the unit,
two choice cards, a small box or jar for the ballot, the unit's
class-vote illustration for reference. Rulebooks and posters stay in
private storage — never in this public repository. No real addresses,
learner photos, family opinions, or personal details appear in any
product.

## Steps

1. Adult and learner choose the rulebook's three rules together and
   check each one with the fairness test out loud before final drawing
   starts — most rulebooks fail on fairness, not on art.
2. Learner draws or places one picture per rule; adult scribes the rule
   and its reason.
3. Learner runs the vote: shows the two choice cards, collects one vote
   per voter, counts out loud, announces the winner, and dictates the
   result sentence. The adult checks: one person, one vote; no one
   voted twice; the result is followed.
4. Learner draws the helper-plan poster: the action, who it helps, and
   the supervising adult. The adult approves the plan for safety before
   the showcase.
5. Learner presents all three parts; adult asks one follow-up question
   and records (privately) what was strong and what to re-teach.

## Rubric (8 points; 6+ meets the project bar)

| Criterion (2 points each) | 2 — Strong | 1 — Developing | 0 — Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rulebook rules | 3 rules, each with a purpose or reason the learner can say aloud; all pass the fairness test | 2 rules with reasons, or one rule fails the fairness test | Fewer than 2 rules, or no reasons |
| Vote | Real vote run with one person one vote; votes counted out loud; winner announced and result followed | Vote run but counting unclear, or one voter voted twice and it was not caught | No real vote, or the result ignored |
| Helper plan | Poster names an action, who it helps, and a supervising adult; action is safe and small | Action named but who it helps or the supervisor is missing | No plan, or an unsafe/unsupervised plan |
| Presentation | Reads the rulebook, reports the vote result, explains the helper plan, and answers the follow-up question | Presents with heavy prompting, or misses one part | Cannot present the project |

**Scoring notes for the adult:** Accept oral, pointed, drawn, and
dictated responses equally. A home learner's rulebook (learning-space
rules) scores the same as a classroom rulebook if the rules pass the
fairness test. A tied vote scores 2 for that row if the learner
re-votes or agrees a fair tie-break (take turns) instead of declaring a
false winner. "I don't know" on the follow-up question scores 1 for
that row if the learner tried ("everyone got one vote, so it was
fair") — naming the fairness rule is itself good civics work.
