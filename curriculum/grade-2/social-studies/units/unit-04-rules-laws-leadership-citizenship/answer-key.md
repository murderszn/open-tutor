# Answer Key — Unit 04: Rules, Laws, Leadership, and Citizenship

For the guiding adult only. Every solution below was solved
independently of the lesson text and reconciled with it. Accept any
answer that matches the stated reasoning, even if the wording differs.
Oral, pointed, drawn, and dictated answers all count.

## Lesson 01 — Why we have rules

**Guided practice:** 1. Classroom. 2. Fairness (safety also accepted
with a reason: waiting in line keeps children from pushing). 3. Safety.
4. "Eat ice cream every day" is not a rule — it is a wish, not an
agreement for a place. 5. Any real home rule with a sensible purpose
(for example, "put toys away — so nobody trips and the next person can
play").

**Independent practice:** Four pictures — home, classroom, playground,
public place — each with a dictated sentence "The rule is ___. It is
for ___." Check: each rule belongs to its place and has a sensible
purpose (safety, fairness, or getting along).

**Application:** The no-rules game breaks down (grabbing, no turns,
arguments). The fixing rule is any turn-taking or sharing rule the
learner names (for example, "take turns").

**Exit check:** Any real rule with its place and a sensible purpose
(for example, "Walk in the hallway, at school, so nobody gets hurt").

## Lesson 02 — School rules and community laws

**Guided practice:** 1. Rule — made by the teacher, for the class.
2. Law — made by the government, for every driver. 3. A public place
(the library); everyone in the library must follow it. 4. Law — taking
what is not yours is against the law everywhere in town; full credit
for "law, because it is for everyone." 5. Any real public-place rule
with a sensible purpose (for example, "walk at the pool, so nobody
slips").

**Independent practice:** Two columns — RULE and LAW — with one
learner-chosen example each, plus the sentence "A rule is for one place
or group. A law is made by the government for everyone." (Wording may
vary; both halves of the distinction must be present.)

**Application:** Two park rules with purposes (for example, "put trash
in the bin — so the park stays nice" and "take turns on the swings — so
everyone gets a turn"). The learner's law pick must come with a reason
tied to the whole town (for example, "the trash rule, because a clean
park matters to everyone in town").

**Exit check:** Law, because the government made it and every driver
must follow it. Both parts of the test are required.

## Lesson 03 — People in authority and government jobs

**Guided practice:** 1. The mayor. 2. A firefighter. 3. Any shared
service: school, road, park, library, clean water. 4. A helper whose
job matters because children get to school safely (crossing guard).
5. Any sensible child role: following rules, helping classmates, voting
in class choices, keeping shared places clean.

**Independent practice:** Three cards — one person in authority, one
helper, one child — each with a dictated sentence "___ helps our
community by ___." Check: each sentence names a real job for that role.

**Application:** The learner plays one role in the park cleanup and
names one other role that must help too, with a reason one role alone
cannot clean the whole park (for example, "the council decides, but
workers must bring the bins").

**Exit check:** Government = the people and offices that make rules for
a community (or "the group that runs the town") plus one shared service
(school, road, park, library, clean water).

## Lesson 04 — Fairness, equality, respect

**Guided practice:** 1. Fair — same rule for everyone, everyone gets
the same job, keeps the room usable. 2. Not fair — question one fails:
the rule does not apply to everyone. 3. Sam: tag is fast and fun.
Priya: hide-and-seek is quiet and clever. 4. Any answer showing care
without shouting or breaking the rule (say so calmly and listen, follow
the rule while it stands, ask for a fair way to change it). 5. 3 each;
yes, that is equality, because everyone counts the same.

**Independent practice:** One fair rule with a "fair because" sentence
and one unfair rule with a "not fair because" sentence; two speech
bubbles, one per side of an invented disagreement. Check: the fair
rule passes at least the first two test questions; the unfair rule
fails at least one.

**Application:** The proposed rule ("only children who finish first
get to choose the story") fails the test — question one fails (not the
same rule for everyone in effect) and question two fails (not everyone
gets a fair chance to choose). The fairer version ("everyone takes
turns choosing the story") passes: same rule, fair chance, helps the
class get along.

**Exit check:** The three questions, any order: does the same rule
apply to everyone? Does everyone get a fair chance? Does it keep people
safe and help us get along?

## Lesson 05 — Listening, consensus, voting

**Guided practice:** 1. Story time and block time. 2. Children have one
hand raised; there is a ballot box; the chart shows two choices (any
one of these observations earns credit; two is strong). 3. Each person
chooses once; no one votes twice or for someone else. 4. Story time
wins (3 votes to 1); yes, fair, because everyone got one vote. 5. Accept
the result; try the choice another day — losing is not unfair.

**Independent practice:** A real vote (story time or block time) with
one vote per voter, counted out loud, winner announced, and the
dictated sentence "We voted. ___ won with ___ votes. I accept the
result because everyone got one vote." Check the counts: the announced
winner must match the counted votes.

**Application:** A one-reason speech ("We should choose ___ because
___") in a calm voice, plus the learner's check that the pair listened,
waited, and spoke kindly.

**Exit check:** The three steps in order: listen, try to agree
(consensus), vote. Fair vote: one person, one vote; most votes wins.

## Practice sets

**Set A:** 1. Home / classroom / playground / public, in the order
given. 2. Safety / fairness / getting along, in the order given.
3. Rule, rule, law, law, in the order given. 4. Red light: government;
every driver. Raise hand: teacher; the class. 5. What went wrong:
grabbing, no turns, nobody else got to play; fixing rule: any
turn-taking or sharing rule.

**Set B:** 1. Teacher → teaches the class; principal → leads the
school; police officer → keeps people safe and enforces laws;
firefighter → puts out fires and rescues people; mayor → leads the
town. 2. Service, service, service, service, not a service, not a
service, in the order given. 3. Any two roles that must work together
(for example, town workers + neighbors; council + workers). 4. Any
sensible child role (see Lesson 03). 5. First council decides, next
workers bring bins, last neighbors and children pick up litter; one
role alone cannot do it all because deciding is not doing, and doing
needs many hands.

**Set C:** 1. Not fair — question one fails (the rule does not apply
to everyone). 2. Sam's view and Priya's view retold (see Lesson 04);
fair way: take turns or vote. 3. Story time wins, fair (4–1, one vote
each); tie is not a win — vote again or take turns; 5 votes from 4
voters is not fair — someone voted twice. 4. No, the vote was not
unfair — everyone got one vote; losing is not unfair. 5. Listen; wait
your turn; speak kindly; respond to the idea, not the person — each
with a matching action. 6. A choice, a reason, and a calm voice.

**Error analysis:** The library rule is a rule, not a law — the
government did not make it for everyone in town; it is the library's
rule for people inside the library. Any correction using the
who-made-it / who-must-follow-it test earns credit.

   of 4 correct). 2. A law is a rule made by a government that applies
   to everyone in that place (both halves required). 3. Any real
