# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Rules, Laws, Leadership, and Citizenship

For the guiding adult. Learner-facing materials never include answers; all
solutions live in the [answer key](answer-key.md). Sessions are 25–30
minutes; every session opens with a 2-minute retrieval warm-up.

## What "ready to move on" looks like

The learner is ready for Unit 05 when they can, with at most light
prompting: name rules from three places with a purpose each; sort rule
versus law using the who-made-it / who-must-follow-it test; match four
authority roles to their jobs; name two government services; run the
fairness test on a rule; retell both sides of a disagreement; run a fair
vote (one person, one vote) and accept its result; and give a one-reason
argument for a classroom choice. Evidence: project rubric 6+/8, quiz
7+/10, assessment 10+/13 with items 2 and 11 correct. If the learner is
below this, re-teach with the targeted follow-ups below before Unit 05
— the discussion rules and the vote routine return in Units 06, 07, and
08, so gaps compound.

## Session-by-session notes

- **Sessions 1–2 (why rules):** Keep the three purposes physical —
  safety (hands guarding), fairness (taking turns with a counter),
  getting along (listening ears). The "every rule has a job" correction
  is the week's highest-leverage move. Session 2's Practice set A is
  oral/pointing/drawn; 4 of 5 is the bar.
- **Sessions 3–4 (rules and laws):** Drill the two-question test until
  it is automatic: "Who made it? Who must follow it?" Expect learners
  to call strict rules laws; that is the teachable moment, not a
  failure. Session 4's card game is oral/pointing; 4 of 5 sorts is the
  bar.
- **Sessions 5–6 (authority and services):** Name the Maple Lane mayor
  and council as invented practice characters out loud, every time.
  The "one person cannot run a town alone" correction is the week's
  highest-leverage move. Session 6's Practice set B is
  oral/pointing/drawn; 4 of 5 is the bar.
- **Sessions 7–8 (role-play and review):** Approve the cleanup role-play
  roles before acting starts — every role must have a job, including
  the child's. Session 8's picture quiz round: the learner corrects
  their own error in each round.
- **Sessions 9–10 (fairness and decisions):** The fairness test must be
  said out loud, all three questions, before any verdict — no verdict
  first, reasons after. In Session 10, name the class-vote illustration
  as an AI-generated practice picture out loud, every time, and use the
  text alternative at least once even when the picture is used.
- **Sessions 11–12 (practice and rulebook):** Session 11's Practice set
  C has 6 tasks; 5 of 6 is the bar. Check every rulebook rule with the
  fairness test *before* final drawing starts — most rulebooks fail on
  fairness, not on art. The dictated reason is the learner's words; do
  not correct the reason into your own — note errors and re-teach.
- **Sessions 13–16 (vote, quiz, review, assessment):** The Session 13
  vote must be a real choice the group then follows; a pretend vote
  teaches nothing. Quiz in session 14, reviewed the same day; re-teach
  misses immediately. Session 15's midyear review game: one question
  from each of Units 01–04 (a map question, a timeline question, a
  source question, a rules question, a vote question), then the project
  showcase — the learner presents; applaud the fairness reasoning and
  the vote procedure by name. Session 16's assessment is the last word,
  not a surprise: every item mirrors practiced tasks.

## Misconception bank

| Misconception | Re-teach move |
|---|---|
| Rules exist "because grown-ups said so" | "Every rule has a job. Does it keep someone safe? Make things fair? Help us get along?" |
| Every wish is a rule ("eat ice cream every day") | "A rule is an agreement for a place. A wish is something one person wants." |
| Every strict rule is a law | "Who made it? Who must follow it? Government + everyone in town = law. Otherwise a rule — even an important one." |
| Laws only exist far away, in the capital | "Laws live in your town too — the red light on your street is a law." |
| The mayor does everything | "The mayor decides with the council, workers do the work, neighbors help. One person cannot run a town alone." |
| Government services are free | "Grown-ups pay money called taxes, and the town uses it for things everyone shares. You don't pay each time, but the town still pays." |
| Fair means "I get what I want" | "Fair is the same rules and a fair chance for everyone — even when the result is not your favorite." |
| Respect means agreeing | "You can respect a person or a rule and still disagree. Respect is how you disagree — calmly, and by listening." |
| Equal means exactly the same | "Fairness means a fair chance, not identical treatment — a large-print book for a child who cannot see well is still fair." |
| Losing a vote means the vote was unfair | "Fair is about the process: one person, one vote, everyone heard. Losing a fair vote is normal." |
| Voting for what a friend votes for | "Your vote is yours. Listen to reasons, then choose what you think is best." |
| The AI illustration is a photograph of a real class | "This is a pretend practice picture made by a computer program. Real classes vote the same way, but this picture is invented." |

## Differentiation

- **Support:** sentence frames ("The rule is ___. It is for ___.",
  "This rule is fair because ___.", "We should choose ___ because
  ___.", "I know because ___."), pre-sorted rule/law cards, role cards
  with pictures, adult scribing, oral/pointing response modes,
  large-print cards, a talking stick or turn-taking card for discussions.
- **Extension:** error-hunt (learner plants a wrong rule/law sort for
  the adult to catch); a second, learner-invented public-place rule with
  its purpose; a secret-ballot vote and an explanation of when secret
  ballots are fairer; a "what if there were no mayor?" thought
  experiment answered with roles, not with one hero.
- **Language access:** vocabulary with pictures and actions first, word
  cards second; visual word wall; home-language labels welcomed
  alongside English; every story and rule read aloud before any question.

## Safety reminders

Adult supervises every activity and previews every video, read-aloud,
and practice example. Practice examples are fictional and adult-prepared;
no real family opinions, votes, or political views are collected or
discussed. Learner rulebooks and posters stay in private storage. No
real addresses, learner photos, or personal details in any product.
Role-play stays indoors or on familiar, safe ground with the adult. No
sharp tools. Any real community-helper visit or walk is planned and
supervised by the adult outside this unit's materials.
