# Lesson 01 — Past, Present, Future, and Putting Events in Order

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** paper, crayons, three picture cards
(baby, first day of school, today in grade 2) or adult-drawn equivalents,
word cards: past, present, future, first, next, last

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will learn three time words —
past, present, and future — and you will practice putting events in the
order they happened: first, next, last.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "What did you do first this morning?"
Accept any true first step (woke up, got dressed). If the learner is stuck,
model one: "First I woke up. Then I ate breakfast. What did you do after
you woke up?"

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

Say: "Time has three parts. The **past** is time that already happened.
Yesterday is past. When you were a baby — that is past. The **present** is
time happening now. Right now, in this lesson — that is present. The
**future** is time that has not happened yet. Tomorrow is future. Next
summer is future."

Hold up the three picture cards in mixed order. "An **event** is something
that happened. Being a baby was an event. Starting school was an event.
Being in grade 2 now is an event. **Order** means putting events in the
sequence they happened. We use the words **first**, **next**, and
**last**."

**Worked example 1 — one day in order:**

> Adult: "Here is my day, all mixed up: 'I ate lunch. I woke up. I went to
> school.' Which happened first? Waking up — you cannot eat lunch before
> you wake up! So: *first* I woke up. *Next* I went to school. *Last* I ate
> lunch. Wait — is that right? Think again with me. At school, lunch comes
> in the middle of the day, and going home comes after. So the full order
> is: first I woke up, next I went to school, then I ate lunch at school,
> last I went home. I fixed my order by thinking about what must come
> before what."

**Worked example 2 — past, present, future sort:**

> Adult: "Three words: 'yesterday,' 'today,' 'tomorrow.' Yesterday already
> happened, so yesterday is **past**. Today is happening now, so today is
> **present**. Tomorrow has not happened yet, so tomorrow is **future**.
> Now try: 'last year' — past, because it already happened. 'Right now' —
> present. 'Next week' — future, because it has not happened yet."

Key idea to say plainly: **The past already happened, the present is
happening now, and the future has not happened yet. Events go in order:
first, next, then, last.**

## Guided practice (adult leads, learner decides)

Adult shows cards or names events; the learner answers orally, by pointing,
or by arranging cards. (Hints in parentheses are for the adult.)

1. "You were a baby. Is that past, present, or future?" *(past)*
2. "You are in grade 2 now. Past, present, or future?" *(present)*
3. "You will be in grade 3 someday. Past, present, or future?" *(future)*
4. Put these in order: "brushed teeth, woke up, put on shoes." *(first
   woke up, next brushed teeth, last put on shoes — accept any sensible
   order the learner can defend, e.g., shoes before teeth, if the sequence
   is consistent)*
5. "Which word tells the order: first, big, last?" *(first and last are
   order words; big is not)*

## Independent practice

The learner draws or arranges three pictures of their own day — morning,
midday, evening — in order, and labels them (adult scribes) "first,"
"next," "last." Then the learner points to each and says one sentence
about it.

## Application

The learner retells a familiar routine (getting ready for school, or the
school day) in order to the adult, using first, next, then, last. The adult
listens for order, not for perfect sentences.

## Exit check

Prompt: "Is 'last summer' past, present, or future? And put these in
order: 'ate dinner, woke up, played outside.'" Accept: last summer is
past; first woke up, next played outside, last ate dinner (or another
defensible order the learner explains).

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** Use only two events at first (first, last); add "next"
  when two-event ordering is solid. Let the learner point instead of
  speaking.
- **Reading/language access:** Teach each word with a picture first
  ("this baby picture is past"), word card second; welcome home-language
  time words next to English ones.
- **Alternate response mode:** The learner may arrange picture cards, act
  out the sequence, or record an audio answer the adult keeps.
- **Extension:** The learner orders four events from a story the adult
  reads aloud, and explains what clue told them the order (a time word,
  or what must happen first).

## Teacher note

**Likely misconception:** learners often treat "past" as "anything I
remember, including this morning's breakfast" — which is correct, but then
also call "this morning" present because it feels recent. Re-anchor: "If
it already happened, even one minute ago, it is past. Present is only
right now." A second common slip: ordering by importance ("lunch is my
favorite, so it is first") instead of by time. Re-anchor: "Order is about
*time*, not about favorites. What had to happen first?"
