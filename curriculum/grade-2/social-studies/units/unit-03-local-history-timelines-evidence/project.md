# Project — Then-and-Now Timeline Exhibit

**Product (one exhibit, reviewed the same day):** a four-event timeline
strip, one then-and-now drawing pair, and one evidence-backed reason
sentence — presented orally with drawings.

## What the learner makes

1. **Timeline strip (Session 11).** A paper strip with four events in
   order, labeled first → last (adult scribes words; the learner draws or
   places a picture for each event). The learner may use the Maple Lane
   practice events (schoolhouse opens → library opens → swings added →
   today) or, with adult help, a personal timeline of 3–4 events they
   choose to share. The strip must show which event is first, which is
   last, and where "today" (or "now") sits.
2. **Then-and-now drawing pair (Session 12).** Two small drawings of the
   same corner or place: one "then" (past) and one "now" (today), showing
   one clear change (for example, horse cart vs. school bus, or small
   schoolhouse vs. bigger school). Labels "PAST" and "TODAY" are scribed
   by the adult.
3. **Reason sentence (Session 12).** One dictated sentence about a change
   in the exhibit, with evidence: "___ changed because ___. I know
   because ___." For the Maple Lane library version: "The library got
   bigger because people wanted more room and books. I know because the
   photograph shows a crowded reading room." (Any accurate evidence from
   the unit's practice set counts.)
4. **Showcase (Session 15).** The learner presents the exhibit orally
   (or by audio recording): reads the strip in order, points out the
   change in the drawing pair, and reads the reason sentence. The adult
   asks one follow-up question ("What is one thing your sources cannot
   tell us?") and the learner answers in their own words.

## Materials

Long paper strip, crayons or colored pencils, picture cards (optional),
the unit's then-and-now illustration for reference, the practice evidence
cards from Lesson 05. Personal timelines stay in private storage — never
in this public repository. No real family photographs, letters, or stories
are required.

## Steps

1. Adult and learner choose the strip's four events together and check
   the order out loud (first, next, then, last).
2. Learner draws or places one picture per event; adult scribes labels
   and numbers (1–4).
3. Learner draws the then-and-now pair, one change only, and labels the
   panels PAST and TODAY.
4. Learner dictates the reason sentence; adult scribes it exactly as
   spoken, then reads it back for the learner to approve or fix.
5. Learner presents the exhibit; adult asks one follow-up question and
   records (privately) what was strong and what to re-teach.

## Rubric (8 points; 6+ meets the project bar)

| Criterion (2 points each) | 2 — Strong | 1 — Developing | 0 — Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timeline order | 4 events in correct order; first, last, and "today/now" marked | 3 events in order, or first/last unclear | Events out of order or fewer than 3 |
| Then-and-now pair | One clear change shown; panels labeled PAST and TODAY | Change unclear, or a label missing | No clear change or no pair |
| Reason with evidence | Reason sentence names a change, a reason, and one piece of evidence | Reason given but evidence missing or unrelated | No reason or no evidence |
| Presentation | Reads strip in order, names the change, and answers the follow-up question | Presents with heavy prompting, or misses one part | Cannot present the exhibit |

**Scoring notes for the adult:** Accept oral, pointed, drawn, and
dictated responses equally. A personal timeline with different events
scores the same as the Maple Lane strip if the order is correct. "I don't
know" on the follow-up question scores 1 for that row if the learner
tried ("our sources don't say") — saying what sources cannot show is
itself good history work.
