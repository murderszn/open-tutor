# Answer Key — Unit 03: Local History, Timelines, and Evidence

For the guiding adult only. Every practice, quiz, assessment, project, and
lesson exit-check item is solved here independently; the adult re-solves
any item the learner disputes before re-teaching. Accept oral, pointed,
drawn, and dictated answers equally. The Maple Lane examples are fictional
practice material.

## Lesson exit checks

- **Lesson 01:** "Last summer" is past. Order: first woke up, next played
  outside, last ate dinner (any defensible order the learner explains
  counts; the key check is that waking up is first).
- **Lesson 02:** The mixed strip (library, schoolhouse, today, swings) is
  out of order. Fixed order: schoolhouse opens, library opens, swings
  added, today.
- **Lesson 03:** Any accurate change + sameness pair counts, e.g.,
  change — horse cart vs. school bus; sameness — a school stands on the
  corner in both panels.
- **Lesson 04:** Two kinds from photograph, object, interview, letter;
  Ana's letter: maker Ana, date June 12, place Maple Lane.
- **Lesson 05:** Likelier reason — people wanted more books and room;
  evidence — the crowded-room photograph and/or the neighbors' letter.

## Practice set A — Week 11 (ordering and timelines)

1. yesterday = past; right now = present; tomorrow = future; last year =
   past; next week = future.
2. First woke up, next ate lunch at school, last went home (accept: first
   woke up, next went to school, last went home, with lunch placed during
   school — the check is waking up first and going home last).
3. First event: the schoolhouse opens. Last event: today (new school
   building). Between the library and today: swings added to the park.
4. Fixed strip: schoolhouse opens → library opens → swings added →
   today.
5. Swings came after the library opened. The schoolhouse opened before
   the swings were added.

## Practice set B — Week 12 (then and now and sources)

1. Horse cart = past; school bus = today; gas lamp = past; electric
   streetlight = today.
2. (a) A horse pulls the cart. (b) The oak tree (also acceptable: the
   school building). (c) Any sameness: a school on the corner, a road
   people travel on, a tree, a person walking.
3. Dirt road / paved road = change. A school on the corner in both times
   = sameness. Basket / backpack = change.
4. (a) Photograph. (b) Object. (c) Interview. (d) Letter.
5. Maker Ana, date June 12, place Maple Lane. "Ana's letter is dated June
   12" = fact. "Ana's school was wonderful" = opinion.

## Practice set C — Week 13 (questions, reasons, and evidence)

1. Any two genuine questions count (who asked, who built, when, why,
   what). Adult notes: answerable from the set — did many people want a
   bigger library (yes, Evidence A + B); not answerable — who built it,
   the exact year (no source names them).
2. Account 1: people wanted more books and room, so the town built a
   bigger library. Account 2: a storm broke the roof, so the town built
   a new library.
3. Crowded-room photograph → supports reason 1. Neighbors' letter →
   supports reason 1. "No storm record in our set" → supports neither
   (it is the absence of evidence for reason 2, not evidence for
   reason 1).
4. Likelier: reason 1 (people wanted more books and room), because the
   crowded-room photograph and/or the neighbors' letter support it.
5. "The reading room was crowded" = fact (shown in the photograph).
   "The new library is the best library in the world" = opinion (best
   is a judgment, not checkable).

## Formative quiz — Week 14 (10 items)

1. First was a baby, next learned to walk, last started grade 2.
2. Yesterday = past; right now = present; tomorrow = future.
3. First: the schoolhouse opens. Last: today (new school building).
4. Horse cart on a dirt road = past; yellow school bus on a paved road =
   today.
5. (a) The old photograph is the historical source — it gives information
   about the past. A brand-new cartoon is not a historical source.
6. Maker Ana, date June 12, place Maple Lane.
7. "The schoolhouse had a bell tower" = fact. "The little school was the
   best school ever" = opinion.
8. Any one: they saw different parts of the event, remember it
   differently, or care about different things.
9. Likelier: people wanted more books and room; evidence: the
   crowded-room photograph and/or the neighbors' letter. (The storm
   reason has no evidence in the practice set.)
10. A horse-drawn cart carries the boxes and hay. Big today, small in
    the past: the oak tree (the school building is also acceptable).

## Culminating unit assessment (13 points)

- **Part 1 (4):** 1 = schoolhouse opens, 2 = library opens, 3 = swings
  added, 4 = today. One point per card in its correct position.
- **Part 2 (4):** Travel: horse cart / school bus. School building:
  small schoolhouse / bigger modern school. Street light: gas lamp /
  electric light. Carrying things: basket / backpack. One point per
  accurate row pair.
- **Part 3 (3):** (a) Photograph. (b) Object. (c) Interview.
- **Part 4 (2):** 1 point for the likelier reason (people wanted more
  books and room); 1 point for one supporting piece of evidence (the
  crowded-room photograph or the neighbors' letter).

## Project rubric (8 points)

See [project.md](project.md). Bar: 6+/8. Timeline order: 4 events in
order with first/last/"today" marked (2). Then-and-now pair: one clear
change, panels labeled PAST and TODAY (2). Reason sentence: change +
reason + one piece of evidence (2). Presentation: strip read in order,
change named, follow-up question answered (2; "our sources don't say"
counts as an attempted answer for the follow-up).
