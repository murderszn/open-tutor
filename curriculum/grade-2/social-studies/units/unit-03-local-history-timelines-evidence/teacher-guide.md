# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Local History, Timelines, and Evidence

For the guiding adult. Learner-facing materials never include answers; all
solutions live in the [answer key](answer-key.md). Sessions are 25–30
minutes; every session opens with a 2-minute retrieval warm-up.

## What "ready to move on" looks like

The learner is ready for Unit 04 when they can, with at most light
prompting: sort events into past/present/future; read and build a
four-event timeline in order; name three then-and-now differences and one
sameness; name the four source kinds and find a letter's maker, date, and
place from its heading; distinguish a fact from an opinion; and pick a
likelier reason with one piece of evidence. Evidence: project rubric 6+/8,
quiz 7+/10, assessment 10+/13 with Part 1 in order and Part 4's reason
correct. If the learner is below this, re-teach with the targeted
follow-ups below before Unit 04 — the timeline routine and the source
questions return in Units 06, 07, and 08, so gaps compound.

## Session-by-session notes

- **Sessions 1–2 (ordering):** Keep the three time words physical — past
  behind you, present where you stand, future ahead. The "already
  happened, even one minute ago, is past" correction is the week's
  highest-leverage move. Session 2's Practice set A is oral/pointing/drawn;
  4 of 5 is the bar.
- **Sessions 3–4 (timelines):** Walk the strip with a finger, always start
  to end. Approve every personal timeline's events before labeling —
  only events the learner wants to share, kept in private storage. The
  Maple Lane strip is fictional; say so.
- **Sessions 5–6 (then and now):** Name the illustration as an
  AI-generated practice picture out loud, every time. Use the text
  alternative at least once even when the picture is used, so the
  alternative stays familiar. Guard the "different is not better or
  worse" frame; do not let the lesson become a ranking of eras.
- **Sessions 7–8 (sources):** Read every practice source aloud; point to
  the letter heading word by word. The "what can it show / what can't it
  show" pair is the whole lesson — insist on both halves for every source.
- **Sessions 9–10 (change and reasons):** Keep the two accounts on
  separate cards. Expect learners to pick the storm account first (it is
  more dramatic); that is the teachable moment, not a failure. Session
  10's Practice set C is oral/pointing/drawn; 4 of 5 is the bar.
- **Sessions 11–12 (project):** Approve every timeline's order *before*
  final drawing starts — most exhibits fail on order, not on art. The
  dictated reason sentence is the learner's words; do not correct the
  evidence into your own — note errors and re-teach instead.
- **Sessions 13–16 (review/product):** Quiz in session 13, reviewed the
  same day; re-teach misses immediately. Session 14's review game:
  timeline relay (adult calls an event, learner runs to its strip
  position) and source sort race (learner sorts kind cards against the
  clock, then checks their own errors). Session 15 is the showcase — the
  learner presents; applaud the order and the evidence by name. Session
  16's assessment is the last word, not a surprise: every item mirrors
  practiced tasks.

## Misconception bank

| Misconception | Re-teach move |
|---|---|
| "This morning" is present because it feels recent | "If it already happened, even one minute ago, it is past. Present is only right now." |
| Ordering by importance ("lunch first — it's my favorite") | "Order is about *time*, not favorites. What had to happen first?" |
| Reading a timeline right-to-left or by favorite picture | Finger walk: "Start at the earliest event. Each step is later in time." |
| Equal gaps on a timeline mean equal time / "nothing happened" in a gap | "A timeline shows order, not equal time. Gaps can be long or short." |
| Everything in the past was worse / everything today is better | "Different is not better or worse. We compare; we do not rank whole times." |
| The AI illustration is a photograph of a real place | "This is a pretend practice picture made by a computer program. Real evidence is Lesson 04's sources." |
| Every old thing is proof of whatever I believe | "A source is a clue, not a verdict. One photo shows one moment from one angle." |
| An opinion is a lie | "An opinion is what someone thinks or feels. We just cannot use it as proof the way we use a fact." |
| The more exciting account (the storm!) must be true | "Exciting is not the same as supported. We follow the evidence, not the drama." |
| "Likelier" means "proven forever" | "Likelier means: with the evidence we have right now. New evidence could change our answer." |
| One person's memory is the whole story | "One memory is one source. We check it against other sources." |

## Differentiation

- **Support:** sentence frames ("First ___. Next ___. Last ___.",
  "I know because ___.", "This source shows ___ but cannot show ___."),
  pre-numbered timeline strips, picture cards for every source kind,
  adult scribing, oral/pointing response modes, large-print strips,
  textured timeline strings for low-vision learners.
- **Extension:** error-hunt (learner plants a swapped timeline card for
  the adult to catch); a second, learner-invented practice source with
  its maker/date/place; a "what source could answer this?" question for
  an unanswerable question; a future panel added to the then-and-now pair,
  labeled as imagination.
- **Language access:** vocabulary with objects/pictures first, word cards
  second; visual word wall; home-language labels welcomed alongside
  English; every source read aloud before any question.

## Safety reminders

Adult supervises every activity and previews every video, read-aloud, and
practice source. Practice sources are fictional and adult-prepared; no
real family photographs, letters, or stories are required or collected.
Learner timelines stay in private storage. No real addresses, learner
photos, or personal details in any map, timeline, or written product.
Timeline walks stay on familiar, safe ground with the adult. No sharp tools.
