# Unit 03 — Local History, Timelines, and Evidence

**Grade:** 2 (ages 7–8) · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track issue:** [#17](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/17)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 11–14 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, 25–30 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

This unit teaches the history tools the rest of the year reuses: how to tell
**past**, **present**, and **future** apart; how to put events in
**chronological order** (first, next, then, last) on a **timeline**; how to
compare life in the **past** with life **today** without assuming everything
changed the same way everywhere; how to name kinds of **historical sources**
(photographs, objects, interviews, letters); how to find a source's
**maker**, **date**, and **place** from information inside the source
itself; how to tell a **fact** from an **opinion**; and how to ask questions
about a local change, weigh **possible reasons**, and pick the **likelier
reason** using **evidence**. The unit's project — a "Then-and-Now Timeline
Exhibit" — is a four-event timeline strip plus one then-and-now comparison
and one evidence-backed reason, presented orally with drawings. The
timeline routine and the "who made it, when, where?" source questions return
in Units 06, 07, and 08.

All of the unit's local examples use the fictional Maple Lane neighborhood
from Unit 01. Maple Lane is invented for practice. Nothing in this unit is a
real place's history, and no real person's story is used. The adult says so
out loud whenever a practice source is shown: "This is a practice story, not
a real person's history."

## Prerequisites (developmental expectations; re-taught here as needed)

- Retell a sequence of 2–3 familiar events in order (first, next, last).
- Name familiar people, places, and roles in home, school, and neighborhood.
- Read a simple map with a title, symbols, a key, labels, and a compass rose
  (Unit 01); describe how people change places and places shape choices
  (Unit 02, draft).
- Point, draw, and describe observations; dictate a sentence for an adult
  to scribe.

Diagnostic Weeks 1–2 probed these with play-based tasks. If ordering is
shaky, re-teach Lesson 01 with the learner's own day (wake up, breakfast,
school) before any history content.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Order** events chronologically — past, present, future; first, next,
   then, last — and **build** a timeline of personal and neighborhood
   events (D2.His.1.K-2).
2. **Compare** life in the past with life today in school, travel, light,
   and communication, and **compare** the perspectives of people in the past
   with people in the present (D2.His.2.K-2, D2.His.4.K-2).
3. **Identify** different kinds of historical sources (photograph, object,
   interview, letter) and **explain** how each can be used to study the past
   (D2.His.9.K-2, D2.His.10.K-2).
4. **Identify** the maker, date, and place of origin for a historical source
   from information within the source itself, and **distinguish** a fact
   from an opinion about a source (D2.His.11.K-2, D3.2.K-2).
5. **Generate** questions about individuals and groups who shaped a local
   change; **generate** possible reasons for that change and **select** the
   reason likelier than others, giving evidence from one or two sources
   (D2.His.3.K-2, D2.His.14.K-2, D2.His.16.K-2, D3.1.K-2).
6. **Compare** two different accounts of the same event and **present** a
   short explanation in correct sequence with reasons (D2.His.6.K-2,
   D4.2.K-2, D4.3.K-2).

With adult support, the learner asks the unit's compelling question,
identifies facts and concepts for supporting questions, and determines which
sources will help answer them (D1.3.K-2, D1.5.K-2).

## Essential question

*What was our neighborhood like before we lived here, and how do we know?*

## Vocabulary

**Past** — time that already happened. · **Present** — time happening now.
· **Future** — time that has not happened yet. · **Event** — something
that happened. · **Order** — putting events in the sequence they happened.
· **Timeline** — a line that shows events in the order they happened, with
earlier events on one end and later events on the other. · **Source** —
something that gives information about the past. · **Historical source** —
a photograph, object, interview, letter, or other item from (or about) the
past that helps us study it. · **Photograph** — a picture taken with a
camera of a real place or person. · **Object** — a real thing people used,
such as a lunch pail. · **Interview** — asking a person questions and
listening to (or reading) their answers. · **Evidence** — information from
a source that supports an answer or a reason. · **Fact** — something that
can be checked and shown to be true. · **Opinion** — what someone thinks
or feels; it cannot be checked the way a fact can. · **Account** — one
person's telling of what happened. · **Reason** — why something happened.
· **Change** — when something becomes different over time.

## Materials

Paper strips and crayons or colored pencils; picture cards for the
practice timeline (schoolhouse, library, swings, today); the printed
[Maple Lane then-and-now illustration](assets/maple-lane-then-and-now.png)
*(AI-generated illustration, inspected 2026-10-23 — every image-based task
ships with a full text-only alternative)*; the adult-prepared practice
sources in Lesson 04 (a described old photograph, an everyday object such
as a lunch pail or an old-style lunchbox, a short fictional interview
excerpt read aloud, and a short fictional letter with a maker/date/place
heading); a blank timeline strip for the project (adult-made or drawn). All
observations are adult-supervised. No real addresses, learner photos,
family stories, or personal information appear anywhere in the learner's
work; the learner's own timeline uses only events the learner and adult
choose to share, kept in private storage.

## Differentiation and accessibility

- Oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative response modes for every check;
  the adult scribes dictated writing; short labeled products reviewed the
  same day.
- Large-print timeline strips and picture cards; raised or textured pieces
  (string timelines, felt event cards) for low-vision learners.
- Vocabulary taught with objects and pictures first, word second; visual
  word wall; home-language labels welcomed alongside English.
- Short sessions with movement breaks; every lesson has a seated-table and
  a floor-play variant (timeline on the floor, walked in order).
- Every illustration ships with a text-only alternative; color is never the
  only cue (labels always accompany color).
- Hearing support: face the learner during discussions; visual turn-taking
  cues; written/drawn option for every spoken check.
- Speaking tasks accept audio recording in place of live presentation; the
  adult may record on the learner's behalf.

## Safety

Adult supervises every activity and previews every procedure, read-aloud,
and practice source. Practice sources are fictional and adult-prepared; no
real family photographs, letters, or stories are required or collected.
Learner timelines stay in private storage — never in this public
repository. No sharp tools; timeline walks stay on familiar, safe ground
with the adult. No real addresses or learner-identifying details appear in
any product.
