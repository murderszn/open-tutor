# Project — The Array Garden

**Unit:** [U05](README.md) · **Sessions:** 12 (Part 1) and 15 (Part 2) of 16
**Time:** two 25–30-minute sessions, plus a short share at the end of
Session 15

## The job

The learner plans a small garden made of rectangular beds. Each bed is
a rectangle partitioned into same-size squares, one seed per square.
The learner draws the beds, counts the squares by rows and by columns,
sorts the seed packets into odd and even, and shows that the two ways
of counting each bed agree.

## Materials

- Graph paper (large squares), crayons, a ruler
- Counters (one per square, to build a bed before drawing it)
- Four seed-packet cards (the adult writes the counts: 8, 11, 14, 19)

## Part 1 — Plan the beds (Session 12)

1. Draw three garden beds on graph paper. Each bed is a rectangle
   partitioned into same-size squares, with no gaps and no overlaps:
   - Bed A: 3 rows, 4 squares in each row
   - Bed B: 2 rows, 5 squares in each row
   - Bed C: 4 rows, 3 squares in each row
2. For each bed, count the squares by rows and write the
   repeated-addition equation and the total. Then count the same bed by
   columns and write that equation too. The two totals must match —
   if they do not, re-partition and recount.
3. Sort the four seed packets into odd and even by pairing counters:
   8, 11, 14, and 19 seeds. For each even packet, write it as a sum of
   two equal addends. For each odd packet, write how many are left over
   after pairing.
4. Choose one bed and write one sentence explaining why its row count
   and its column count give the same total.

## Part 2 — Build one bed and share (Session 15)

1. Pick one bed from Part 1. Build it with counters — one counter in
   each square of the drawn rectangle — and recount it by rows and by
   columns. Mark a tick on the plan when both counts agree with the
   drawing.
2. Share: the learner presents one bed to the adult, points to a row
   and a column, reads both equations aloud, and answers: "What would
   happen to the total if one more row were added to this bed?" (The
   total would grow by one more group — the number of squares in a row.
   For Bed A, one more row of 4 would make 12 + 4 = 16 squares.)

## Deliverables

1. A garden plan with three partitioned rectangles (Beds A, B, C),
   each with its row equation, column equation, and total.
2. An odd/even sort of the four seed packets (8, 11, 14, 19), with the
   even packets written as two equal addends and the odd packets' leftovers
   named.
3. One built bed (counters on the drawn rectangle) with both counts
   ticked as agreeing.
4. A short share (oral, drawn, or adult-scribed) of one bed, including
   the one-more-row question above.

## Worked sample (adult model — not a learner answer)

Bed A is 3 rows of 4 squares. By rows: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 squares. By
columns: 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12 squares. The totals agree, so the bed holds
12 seeds, one per square. Seed packets: 8 is even (4 + 4), 11 is odd
(5 pairs, 1 left over), 14 is even (7 + 7), 19 is odd (9 pairs, 1 left
over). One more row of 4 on Bed A would make 12 + 4 = 16 squares.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Secure (2) | Developing (1) | Not yet (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Array accuracy | All three beds partitioned into same-size squares with the right rows and columns | Two beds correct, or squares uneven on one bed | Fewer than two beds correct, or squares not same-size |
| Equations | Every bed has a correct row equation and a correct column equation, totals matching | Most equations correct, or one bed missing its column check | Equations missing, or row size confused with row count |
| Odd/even sort | All four packets sorted correctly, even packets as two equal addends, odd leftovers named | Three packets correct, or one equal-addend split missing | Two or fewer correct, or odd/even guessed without pairing |
| Build check | The built bed's row and column counts both match the drawing, ticks shown | Built bed counted one way only, or a mismatch left standing | No build, or counts not checked |
| Explanation | Share explains that rows and columns count the same squares two ways, and answers the one-more-row question correctly | Share partly explains the two counts, or the one-more-row answer is partly right | No explanation, or the one-more-row question unanswered |

**Project score:** 8–10 = secure; 5–7 = developing (re-teach the
lowest-scoring criterion before Unit 06); 0–4 = not yet (replay the
matching lesson, then redo one bed and one packet sort).

## Safety and access

Adult-supervised table work only; small counters are kept in a tray, not
carried around the room. Every deliverable accepts oral, pointing,
drawing, or adult-scribed responses. No real garden, seeds, or personal
information is involved — bed sizes and packet counts are invented for
the game, and the seed packets are paper cards, not real seeds.
