# Unit 05 — Equal Groups, Arrays, and Repeated Addition

**Grade:** 2 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 19–22
**Backlog:** [issue #14](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/14), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Equal groups are the bridge from adding to multiplying — but this unit
never crosses it. Everything here is still addition: the same number,
added again and again. First the learner pairs objects and discovers
that some numbers split into two equal parts (even) and some always
leave one over (odd). Then equal groups become rows, rows become
rectangular arrays, and a rectangle partitioned into same-size squares
is an array the learner can count two ways — by rows or by columns —
and get the same total. No multiplication symbol appears anywhere in
this unit; "groups of" and repeated addition do all the work.

## Essential question

When things come in equal groups, how can adding the same number again
and again tell us how many there are altogether — and how do rows and
columns show it?

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–04 (and the diagnostic weeks):

- Count within 1000; skip-count by 5s, 10s, and 100s (Unit 01)
- Fluently add and subtract within 20, including doubles (Unit 02)
- Add within 100 with place-value strategies; add up to four two-digit
  numbers (Unit 02)
- Read a short story problem, name what is missing, and write an
  equation with a symbol for the unknown (Unit 04, draft PR #291)

Counting by 2s is taught here, not assumed: it grows out of the
doubles the learner already knows (2, 4, 6, 8, 10 from Unit 02).

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Determine whether a group of up to 20 objects has an odd or even
   number of members by pairing objects or counting by 2s.
   (2.OA.C.3)
2. Write an even number up to 20 as a sum of two equal addends.
   (2.OA.C.3)
3. Find the total of equal groups (up to 5 groups) by repeated
   addition, writing an equation with equal addends.
   (2.OA.C.4)
4. Find the total of a rectangular array (up to 5 rows and 5 columns)
   by addition, writing the total as a sum of equal addends — by rows
   and by columns. (2.OA.C.4)
5. Partition a rectangle into rows and columns of same-size squares
   and count the squares, relating the count to an array and a
   repeated-addition equation. (2.G.A.2)

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**,
grade 2, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/Math](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/)
(codes and descriptions cross-checked against the official grade-2
domain pages during the A00 audit, 2026-10-02, and re-verified against
independent reproductions of the official text on 2026-11-05; no state

| Unit objective | Standard | Official description (grade 2) |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 2 | 2.OA.C.3 | Determine whether a group of objects (up to 20) has an odd or even number of members, e.g., by pairing objects or counting them by 2s; write an equation to express an even number as a sum of two equal addends. |
| 3, 4 | 2.OA.C.4 | Use addition to find the total number of objects arranged in rectangular arrays with up to 5 rows and up to 5 columns; write an equation to express the total as a sum of equal addends. |
| 5 | 2.G.A.2 | Partition a rectangle into rows and columns of same-size squares and count to find the total number of them. |

Objective 3 (equal groups before arrays) is the repeated-addition
work named inside 2.OA.C.4, taught first on loose groups so the array
lessons can focus on rows and columns; it is not a separate standard
claim. This unit uses no multiplication symbol: "4 groups of 3" is
always written 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12, never 4 × 3.

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials
list): counters (buttons, beans, blocks — 25 or more), sorting trays or
paper plates, graph paper (large squares), crayons, number cards 1–20,
the generated illustration
[`assets/apple-array-4-by-5.png`](assets/apple-array-4-by-5.png),
paper and a ruler for drawing rectangles. No sharp tools; supervise
small parts; check food-allergen safety before using food as counters.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, or drawing responses; the adult scribes
when the learner isn't ready to write. Counters are distinguished by
being moved into pairs and rows, never by color alone. Each lesson includes
a seated-table and a floor-play variant, a language-access support, an
alternate response mode, and an extension. The generated illustration ships
with a text-only alternative containing every count the activities need.
Arrays are always built with real objects before any picture is counted.
