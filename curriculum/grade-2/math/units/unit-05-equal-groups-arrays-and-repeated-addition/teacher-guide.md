# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Equal Groups, Arrays, and Repeated Addition

**Unit:** [U05](README.md) · **Answer key:** [answer-key.md](answer-key.md)
(adult only — never beside student materials)

## How this unit runs

Sixteen 25–30-minute sessions across track weeks 19–22. Every session
opens with a 2-minute skip-counting warm-up (count by 2s, then by 3s,
4s, and 5s as the unit progresses) and closes with the lesson's exit
check, recorded as **secure / developing / not yet** per objective. Keep
sessions hands-on: at grade 2, counters first, drawing second, symbols
last. The repeated-addition equation is always written after the groups
are built — never as a rule to memorize first.

**Session map:** 1 L1 → 2 practice A → 3 L2 → 4 game → 5 L3 → 6 practice
B → 7 L4 → 8 practice D → 9 L5 → 10 game → 11 L6 → 12 project P1 → 13
review E + re-teach → 14 quiz + feedback → 15 project P2 + share → 16
assessment.

## Objective-by-objective guidance

### Objective 1 — odd and even by pairing (L1)

The unit's load-bearing idea, in one test: pair them up and look for a
leftover. Do not let the learner decide odd/even from the ones digit
yet — the digit pattern (1, 3, 5, 7, 9) is a shortcut for later, and a
learner who memorizes it without pairing will call 12 odd because "2 is
even and…". Pairing is the definition in this unit; the digit pattern is
an observation to notice afterwards, never the test.

### Objective 2 — even numbers as two equal addends (L2)

Teach the split as two equal piles, not as half of a memorized fact.
The piles are the proof: if the piles come out different, the split is
wrong. Counting by 2s is the check that the number is on the even list.
If a learner writes 12 = 5 + 7, rebuild the piles — 5 and 7 are not
equal, so the pairing must have left one over somewhere (it did not:
12 pairs cleanly into 6 + 6).

### Objective 3 — equal groups and repeated addition (L3)

The equality check comes before the equation, every time. A learner
who writes an equation for unequal groups has answered a different
problem. Drill the two questions in order: "How many are IN one group?"
(the addend) and "How many groups?" (how many addends). The most common
error in the whole unit is swapping those two numbers.

### Objective 4 — arrays by rows and by columns (L4, L6)

Teach rows and columns as two walks over the same objects: walk across
each row, then walk down each column. The two totals must agree; a
disagreement is a recount signal, not a choice. The apple picture
(4 rows of 5, 20 apples) is the model to imitate — count the rows, add
the 5s, count the columns, add the 4s, compare. Keep arrays within 5 × 5
throughout; 5 × 5 = 25 is the ceiling.

### Objective 5 — partitioning rectangles (L5, project)

Same-size squares are the whole rule: if the squares differ, the rows
are not equal groups and no equation can be trusted. Check the tiling
before the counting. The rectangle work is the bridge to Unit 08
(shapes and partitions) — a learner who can tile a 3-by-4 rectangle and
count it two ways is ready for halves, thirds, and fourths of the same
rectangles.

## Misconception watch

| Signal | Likely cause | Reteaching move |
|---|---|---|
| Odd/even decided from the pairs, leftover ignored ("9 is even — 4 pairs") | Leftover not treated as evidence | Always ask "and what is left over?" before accepting an answer; the leftover decides |
| "Equal addends" written as any two addends (12 = 5 + 7) | Addends not checked for equality | Build two equal piles; if the piles differ, the split is wrong |
| Row count written as the addend ("4 rows of 5" as 4 + 4 + 4 + 4) | Group count confused with group size | Ask "how many are IN one row?" and point at a row before writing |
| Rows and columns mixed up | No physical trace of the walk | Trace a row across with a finger, then a column down, before counting |
| Unequal squares counted as an array | Same-size rule skipped | Re-partition with same-size squares first; the count waits |
| Rows and columns added together (12 squares counted as 12 + 12) | Two counts treated as two piles | Name it: rows and columns are two counts of the same squares, never added |
| Array assumed to be square | Only square examples seen | Build 2-by-5 and 5-by-2 early; the rectangle is the point |

## Re-teaching (Session 13 and flexible weeks)

Pick the highest-need objective from exit checks; replay the matching
[practice session](practice.md) variant, then give 4 fresh prompts — 3 of
4 correct to move on. If two or more objectives are "not yet" after
Session 16, use the track's flexible weeks (scope-and-sequence §5) before
Unit 06; equal groups and arrays are prerequisite for the measurement
and data work that follows, and for Unit 08's partitions.

## Readiness criteria

**Ready for Unit 06** when the learner is secure or developing on all
five objectives: exit checks secure on at least 4 of 5 objectives,
formative quiz ≥ 5/6, unit assessment ≥ 8/10, project score ≥ 5/10. A
"developing" on the story objective alone may ride along (Unit 06's
length word problems revisit story reading daily); a "not yet" on
pairing, equal groups, or arrays must be re-taught first — Unit 06
assumes the learner can count equal groups reliably.
