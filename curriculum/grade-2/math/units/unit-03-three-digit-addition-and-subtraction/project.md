# Project — Three-Digit Supply Shop

**Unit:** [U03](README.md) · **Sessions:** 12 (Part 1) and 15 (Part 2) of 16
**Time:** two 25–30-minute sessions, plus a short share at the end of
Session 15

## The job

The learner runs a small supply shop. The shop starts with stock, receives
deliveries (stock goes up — addition), and fills orders (stock goes down —
subtraction). By the end, the shop's ledger must balance: every delivery
and order is recorded, checked, and explainable with blocks.

## Materials

- Base-ten blocks, place-value mat, paper and crayons
- A shop ledger (a ruled sheet with columns: item, start, delivery/order,
  end, check) — the adult draws it; the learner fills it
- Play order slips (small cards naming an item and a quantity)

## Part 1 — Stock the shop (Session 12)

1. Choose three shop items (for example: pencils, notebooks, erasers —
   any three the learner names; no real shop, money, or personal
   information is involved).
2. Give each item a starting stock between 100 and 900 (for example:
   pencils 342, notebooks 517, erasers 260). Build each starting stock
   with blocks and write it in the ledger.
3. Receive two deliveries per item. Each delivery is a three-digit
   quantity. Record each delivery as an addition problem, build it, and
   write the new stock. At least one delivery in the ledger must need a
   compose (a ten or a hundred trade).
4. Draw the blocks for one delivery problem (flats, rods, cubes, with the
   trade circled) and write one sentence explaining the trade.

## Part 2 — Fill the orders (Session 15)

1. Fill two orders per item. Each order is a three-digit quantity smaller
   than the current stock. Record each order as a subtraction problem,
   build it, and write the new stock. At least one order in the ledger
   must need a decompose (a ten or a hundred trade), and at least one
   must be an across-zero trade or a mental ±100 step — the learner
   labels which.
2. Check every ledger line by the inverse operation: each delivery total
   is checked by subtraction, each order total by addition. Mark each
   check with a tick when it agrees.
3. Share: the learner presents one delivery and one order to the adult,
   explains the trade in each, and answers: "What would happen to the
   stock if one more delivery of 100 arrived?" (Expected: the hundreds
   digit of that item's stock goes up by 1; the tens and ones do not move.)

## Deliverables

1. A completed shop ledger with at least 6 delivery additions and 6 order
   subtractions, each built with blocks before writing, each checked by
   the inverse operation.
2. One block drawing of a delivery problem, with the trade circled and a
   one-sentence trade explanation.
3. A short share (oral, drawn, or adult-scribed) of one delivery and one
   order, including the ±100 question above.

## Worked sample (adult model — not a learner answer)

Pencils start at 342. Delivery of 166: 342 + 166 = 508 (ones 2 + 6 = 8;
tens 4 + 6 = 10 → 0 tens, carry 1 hundred; hundreds 3 + 1 + 1 = 5). Check:
508 − 166 = 342. Order of 125, taken from the new stock: 508 − 125 = 383
(ones 8 − 5 = 3; tens 0 − 2, so decompose a hundred — the block build
holds 0 tens and 5 hundreds, so trade 1 hundred for 10 tens: tens
10 − 2 = 8; hundreds 4 − 1 = 3).
Check: 383 + 125 = 508. This sample shows why the block build, not just
the written digits, is the authority when a trade is involved.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Secure (2) | Developing (1) | Not yet (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Addition accuracy | At least 5 of 6 delivery sums correct, with trades shown | 3–4 of 6 correct, or trades missing on some lines | Fewer than 3 correct, or sums written without builds |
| Subtraction accuracy | At least 5 of 6 order differences correct, with trades marked | 3–4 of 6 correct, or trades missing on some lines | Fewer than 3 correct, or differences written without builds |
| Trade range | Ledger includes a compose, a decompose, and an across-zero or mental ±100 step, each labeled | Two of the three trade kinds present and labeled | Fewer than two trade kinds present |
| Checking | Every ledger line checked by the inverse operation, ticks shown, disagreements reworked | Most lines checked, or a disagreement left standing | Checks missing or not used to fix errors |
| Explanation | Block drawing shows the trade circled; share explains the trade and answers the ±100 question correctly | Drawing or share partly explains the trade; ±100 answer partly right | No trade explanation, or ±100 question unanswered |

**Project score:** 8–10 = secure; 5–7 = developing (re-teach the
lowest-scoring criterion before Unit 04); 0–4 = not yet (replay the
matching lesson, then redo one delivery and one order).

## Safety and access

Adult-supervised table work only; small blocks are kept in a tray, not
carried around the room. Every deliverable accepts oral, pointing,
drawing, or adult-scribed responses. No real money, no real shop
inventory, and no personal information is recorded in the ledger — item
names and quantities are invented for the game.
