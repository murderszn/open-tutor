# Unit 03 — Three-Digit Addition and Subtraction

**Grade:** 2 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #14](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/14), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Three-digit numbers are no longer just for reading and comparing — now we
add and subtract them. Every new computation starts in base-ten blocks:
hundreds with hundreds, tens with tens, ones with ones. When a column holds
ten or more, we **compose** (trade ten of one kind for one of the next kind
up); when a column does not hold enough to subtract, we **decompose**
(trade one of a larger kind for ten of the next kind down). The written
method is introduced only as a record of the block trade the learner has
already made with their hands.

## Essential question

How do composing and decomposing keep a three-digit computation honest —
the same total, or the same difference, before and after the trade?

## Prerequisites

From Unit 01 and Unit 02 (and the diagnostic weeks):

- Build three-digit numbers with hundred-flats, ten-rods, and one-cubes;
  explain that 10 tens make 1 hundred and 10 ones make 1 ten
- Read and write numbers to 1000 as numerals, number names, and expanded form
- Add and subtract within 100 with place-value strategies (break apart by
  tens and ones); add up to four two-digit numbers
- Mentally add or subtract 10 from a number 100–900; explain a within-100
  strategy using place value, with drawings or objects as support
- Compare three-digit numbers by hundreds, then tens, then ones

No new place-value idea is assumed beyond Unit 01: composing and
decomposing are the same bundling idea, now used inside a computation.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Add three-digit numbers with concrete models or drawings and
   place-value strategies, relating the strategy to a written method;
   compose a ten or a hundred when a column reaches ten or more.
   (2.NBT.B.7)
2. Subtract three-digit numbers with concrete models or drawings and
   place-value strategies, relating the strategy to a written method;
   decompose a ten or a hundred when a column does not hold enough,
   including decomposing across a zero tens place. (2.NBT.B.7)
3. Mentally add 100 or subtract 100 from a number 100–900, and mentally
   add or subtract 10 in a three-digit number, explaining which digit
   changes and why. (2.NBT.B.8)
4. Choose among blocks, break-apart, written method, and mental computation
   for a three-digit problem, and explain why the chosen strategy works
   using place value and the properties of operations (explanations may
   use drawings or objects). (2.NBT.B.9)
5. Check a three-digit sum by subtraction and a three-digit difference by
   addition, and say whether the check agrees with the first answer.

## Vocabulary (taught with objects first, word second)

hundred-flat · ten-rod · one-cube · compose (trade up) · decompose
(trade down) · regroup · written method · column (ones, tens, hundreds) ·
mental computation · inverse operation (addition undoes subtraction) ·
check · strategy · explain why

[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials

number cards 100–999, the generated illustration

tools; supervise small parts; check food-allergen safety before using food
as counters.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, or drawing responses; the adult scribes
when the learner isn't ready to write. Flats, rods, and cubes are
distinguishable by size and shape, never by color alone. Each lesson includes
a seated-table and a floor-play variant, a language-access support, an
alternate response mode, and an extension. The generated illustration ships
with a text-only alternative containing every count the activities need.
Subtraction is always taught as "take away blocks you can see" before any
written borrowing marks are made.
