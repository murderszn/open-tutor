## Project — Three-Digit Supply Shop

No single correct ledger: item names, starting stocks, deliveries, and
orders are the learner's choices. The adult checks each line against the
rubric in [project.md](project.md): every addition and subtraction built
with blocks first, every trade shown or marked, every line checked by the
inverse operation, and the trade range (a compose, a decompose, and an
across-zero or mental ±100 step) present and labeled.

**Worked sample (from the project page):** pencils 342 + 166 = **508**
(ones 8; tens 4 + 6 = 10 → 0 tens, carry 1 hundred; hundreds 3 + 1 + 1 =
5); check 508 − 166 = **342**. Order 508 − 125 = **383** (ones 3; tens
0 − 2 → decompose a hundred: 10 − 2 = 8; hundreds 4 − 1 = 3); check
383 + 125 = **508**. Share question: one more delivery of 100 raises
that item's hundreds digit by 1; tens and ones do not move.

## Formative quiz (Session 14)

1. 342 + 125 = **467** (ones 7, tens 6, hundreds 4).
2. 253 + 166 = **419**; trade in the tens column — 5 tens + 6 tens = 11
   tens, composed to 1 hundred and 1 ten.
3. 465 − 139 = **326** (ones 15 − 9 = 6; tens 5 − 3 = 2 after the trade;
   hundreds 4 − 1 = 3).
4. 403 − 158 = **245**: the tens column starts at zero, so the trade
   jumps from the hundreds — 1 hundred becomes 10 tens, then 1 ten
   becomes 10 ones, leaving 9 tens and 13 ones before subtracting.
5. 546 + 100 = **646**; 937 − 100 = **837**; the hundreds digit changed
   in both.
6. Acceptable: "10 tens and 1 hundred are the same amount, just bundled
   differently, so trading one for the other does not change the total."
