# Lesson 03 — Add and Compose a Hundred

**Student goal:** I can add three-digit numbers when the tens column
reaches ten tens, by composing ten tens into one hundred, and I can follow
a problem that needs two trades in a row.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U03](../README.md) · **Session 5 of 16**

## Materials

- Base-ten blocks: hundred-flats, ten-rods, one-cubes
- Place-value mat

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Add 253 + 166 from last lesson's picture, from memory of the trade."
(Expected: **419** — the tens composed to a hundred.) If the tens trade is
shaky, replay Lesson 02's picture round before starting.

## Teaching

Composing a hundred is the same trade as composing a ten, one column to
the left: **10 tens become 1 hundred**. Sometimes a problem needs two
trades in a row — ones into tens, then tens into hundreds. Work right to
left, one column at a time, and never skip the carried block.

1. Add ones. Compose if the ones total is ten or more.
2. Add tens, **including any carried ten**. Compose if the tens total is
   ten or more: trade 10 tens for 1 hundred, keep the leftover tens.
3. Add hundreds, **including any carried hundred**.
4. Read the final build. If a column total ever stays two digits in the
   written method, a trade was missed.

**Key vocabulary:** *compose a hundred*, *two trades*. The number of
trades is not the difficulty — the habit "add the carried block before
moving on" is.

## Worked example 1 — 386 + 274 (two trades)

Build 386 and 274.

> "Ones: 6 + 4 = 10 ones. Compose: trade 10 ones for 1 ten, keep 0 ones.
> Tens: 8 + 7 + the carried 1 = 16 tens. Compose: trade 10 tens for 1
> hundred, keep 6 tens. Hundreds: 3 + 2 + the carried 1 = 6 hundreds.
> The sum is **660**."

Written method:

```text
  11
  386
+ 274
-----
  660
```

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the two small "1"s above the tens and
hundreds columns are the two trades, in order. The first trade made the
second one possible — without the carried ten, the tens total would have
been 15, still a trade, but the habit of adding the carry first is what
keeps the count right.

## Worked example 2 — 457 + 183

Build 457 and 183. Ones: 7 + 3 = 10 → 0 ones, carry 1 ten. Tens: 5 + 8 + 1
= 14 → 4 tens, carry 1 hundred. Hundreds: 4 + 1 + 1 = 6 hundreds.

> "Two trades again, and the same shape of answer: a zero in the ones
> column is not a mistake — it is the honest leftover after composing all
> ten ones. The sum is **640**."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** a zero leftover after a trade is expected.
Learners who "fix" a traded zero by writing the pre-trade total are
undoing the trade.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds with the blocks.

1. "Build and add 296 + 167." (Expected: ones 6 + 7 = 13 → 3 ones, carry 1
   ten; tens 9 + 6 + 1 = 16 → 6 tens, carry 1 hundred; hundreds 2 + 1 + 1 =
   4 — **463**.)
2. "Build and add 378 + 245." (Expected: ones 8 + 5 = 13 → 3 ones, carry 1
   ten; tens 7 + 4 + 1 = 12 → 2 tens, carry 1 hundred; hundreds 3 + 2 + 1 =
   6 — **623**.)
3. "Build and add 329 + 286." (Expected: ones 9 + 6 = 15 → 5 ones, carry 1
   ten; tens 2 + 8 + 1 = 11 → 1 ten, carry 1 hundred; hundreds 3 + 2 + 1 =
   6 — **615**.)
4. Error analysis: the adult shows "549 + 231 = 770" and asks: "Is it?
   Rebuild the ones first." (Expected: wrong — ones 9 + 1 = 10 → 0 ones,
   carry 1 ten; tens 4 + 3 + 1 = 8 (not 7); hundreds 5 + 2 = 7. Correct sum
   **780**. The writer forgot the carried ten.)
5. "How do you know a problem will need a hundred trade before you finish
   it?" (Expected: you cannot know for sure until you add the tens —
   but if the tens digits are large (7 + 8, 9 + 6), expect a trade.)

## Independent practice

The learner builds and writes, without hints: 468 + 152, 275 + 386,
194 + 278. The adult checks that each written method shows both carried
digits (or one, where only one trade happens) before the final answer.

*Alternate response mode:* build with blocks and dictate the trade
sentences ("ten ones become one ten; ten tens become one hundred"); the
adult writes the written method.

## Application

Book drive: "The class collected 329 books in the fall and 286 books in
the spring. How many books in all?" (Expected: **615** books — the same
computation as guided round 3, now as a real total; the adult asks the
learner to notice the match.)

## Exit check

"Build 487 + 196 and tell me the sum, naming every trade." Expected: ones
7 + 6 = 13 → 3 ones, carry 1 ten; tens 8 + 9 + 1 = 18 → 8 tens, carry 1
hundred; hundreds 4 + 1 + 1 = 6 — **683**. Record: secure / developing /
not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** say every trade as a full sentence before writing
  it; the small carried digit is never written silently.
- **Alternate response mode:** use two mats — one for the "before trade"
  build and one for the "after trade" build — so the trade is visible.
- **Floor-play variant:** when a tens heap reaches 10 rods, sweep them
  away and place 1 flat; count the flats again before continuing.

## Extension

"Add 598 + 375. How many trades, and what is the sum?" (Ones 8 + 5 = 13 →
3 ones, carry 1 ten; tens 9 + 7 + 1 = 17 → 7 tens, carry 1 hundred;
hundreds 5 + 3 + 1 = 9 — **973**, two trades.) Or: "Invent the largest
three-digit addition you can that needs exactly one trade, and prove it."

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) forgetting the carried block in the next
column — the most common error in this unit; fix it by requiring the
learner to point at the carried digit and say it aloud before adding the
column; (2) "fixing" a traded zero (writing 10 instead of 0 ones); (3)
trading in the wrong direction (turning 10 tens into 10 ones) — re-anchor
with the physical swap: the big block replaces the pile, never the reverse.
