# Lesson 05 — Subtract and Decompose a Hundred, Including Across Zero

**Student goal:** I can subtract three-digit numbers when the tens column
does not hold enough, by decomposing one hundred into ten tens — even
when the tens column starts at zero.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U03](../README.md) · **Session 9 of 16**

## Materials

- Base-ten blocks: hundred-flats, ten-rods, one-cubes
- Place-value mat

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Build 521. Take away 376 in your head, column by column — where do you
get stuck, and how many times?" (Expected: stuck twice — ones 1 < 6, then
tens 1 < 7 after the first trade. Two trades, one of them from the
hundreds.) If Lesson 04's ten trade is shaky, replay its worked example
first.

## Teaching

When the tens column does not hold enough, the hundreds column pays: **1
hundred becomes 10 tens**. When the tens column starts at zero, the chain
is two trades long: hundred → tens, then ten → ones. Do the trades in
order, and re-count each column after every trade.

1. Build the starting number. Subtract ones; trade from tens if needed.
2. Subtract tens, after any ones trade. If the tens column does not hold
   enough, decompose 1 hundred into 10 tens, then subtract.
3. If the tens column is zero, trade in two steps: first 1 hundred
   becomes 10 tens, then 1 of those tens becomes 10 ones. What is left is
   one fewer hundred, nine tens, and ten more ones than you started with
   in the ones column.
4. Subtract hundreds last. Read what is left, and check by adding back.

**Key vocabulary:** *decompose a hundred*, *across zero*. "Across zero"
means the trade jumps over an empty tens column — the hundreds column
still pays for it.

## Worked example 1 — 521 − 376 (two trades)

Build 521 (5 flats, 2 rods, 1 cube). Subtract 376.

> "Ones: 1 < 6. Decompose a ten: trade 1 rod for 10 cubes. Ones hold 11,
> tens hold 1 rod. Ones: 11 − 6 = 5. Tens: 1 < 7. Decompose a hundred:
> trade 1 flat for 10 rods. Tens hold 11, hundreds hold 4 flats. Tens:
> 11 − 7 = 4. Hundreds: 4 − 3 = 1. The difference is **145**."

Check by adding back: 145 + 376 = 521 (ones 5 + 6 = 11 → 1 one, carry 1
ten; tens 4 + 7 + 1 = 12 → 2 tens, carry 1 hundred; hundreds 1 + 3 + 1 =
5). The check agrees, so 145 stands.

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the hundreds column paid twice in this
problem — once indirectly (its flat became the tens that fed the ones)
and once directly. Counting the flats left at the end (4, then 4 − 3 =
1) is the only way to keep that straight.

## Worked example 2 — 403 − 158 (across zero)

Build 403 (4 flats, 0 rods, 3 cubes). Subtract 158.

> "Ones: 3 < 8, but the tens column holds zero rods — there is nothing
> to trade there. Go one column further: decompose 1 hundred-flat into
> 10 ten-rods. Now the tens hold 10. Decompose 1 of those rods into 10
> one-cubes. Now the build is 3 flats, 9 rods, 13 cubes — the same number,
> 403, re-bundled. Ones: 13 − 8 = 5. Tens: 9 − 5 = 4. Hundreds: 3 − 1 =
> 2. The difference is **245**."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the zero tens column is not a wall — it is
a column waiting to be filled by the hundred trade. After the double
trade, the tens hold 9 (not 10), because one of the ten new rods was spent
on the ones. Forgetting that second step is the classic across-zero error.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds with the blocks.

1. "Build and subtract 632 − 478." (Expected: ones 2 < 8 → 12 − 8 = 4;
   tens: the tens hold 3 rods, gave 1 away, hold 2 — 2 < 7, so decompose a hundred: 12 − 7 = 5; hundreds 5 − 4 =
   1 — **154**.)
2. "Build and subtract 705 − 269." (Expected: across zero — ones 5 < 9,
   tens 0: trade a hundred into 10 tens, then a ten into 10 ones: ones
   15 − 9 = 6; tens 9 − 6 = 3; hundreds 6 − 2 = 4 — **436**.)
3. "Build and subtract 824 − 567." (Expected: ones 4 < 7 → 14 − 7 = 7;
   tens: the tens hold 2 rods, gave 1 away, hold 1 — 1 < 6, so decompose a hundred: 11 − 6 = 5; hundreds 7 − 5 = 2 —
   **257**.)
4. Error analysis: the adult shows "500 − 243 = 357" and asks: "Is it?
   Rebuild the hundreds after the trades." (Expected: wrong — across
   zero, the build becomes 4 flats, 9 rods, 10 cubes; ones 10 − 3 = 7;
   tens 9 − 4 = 5; hundreds 4 − 2 = 2 (not 3). Correct difference **257**.
   The writer forgot the hundreds column shrank when it paid for the
   trades.)
5. "Say the across-zero trade chain in one sentence." (Expected: "One
   hundred becomes ten tens, and one ten becomes ten ones, leaving nine
   tens.")

## Independent practice

The learner builds and writes, without hints: 600 − 381, 915 − 648,
430 − 187. The adult checks that every trade is marked on the written
method before the answer is read, and that the across-zero round shows
the intermediate 9 tens.

*Alternate response mode:* build the "before" and "after" builds on two
mats side by side and point to what changed in each column; the adult
writes the written method.

## Application

Game scores: "Maya scored 430 points. She spent 187 points in the game
shop. How many points are left?" (Expected: ones 0 < 7 → trade a ten:
10 − 7 = 3; tens: the tens hold 3 rods, gave 1 away, hold 2 — 2 < 8, so decompose a hundred: 12 − 8 = 4; hundreds 3 − 1
= 2 — **243** points left.)

## Exit check

"Build 712 − 456 and tell me the difference, naming every trade."
Expected: ones 2 < 6 → 12 − 6 = 6; tens: the tens hold 1 rod, gave 1 away, hold 0 — 0 < 5, so decompose a hundred: 10 − 5 = 5; hundreds 6 − 4 = 2 — **256**. Record: secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** always say the chain in order ("hundred → tens →
  ones") before marking anything; never let the two trades collapse into
  one mark.
- **Alternate response mode:** use a "trade tray" — traded-away blocks
  sit in the tray, traded-in blocks sit on the mat — so nothing vanishes.
- **Floor-play variant:** lay the starting build in a row; when a column
  runs short, physically carry a flat from the hundreds pile, break it
  into rods in front of the learner, and only then take blocks away.

## Extension

"Subtract 1000 − 1 with blocks or a drawing. What happens?"
(1000 − 1 = 999: the thousand unbundles across every column — this is a
teaser for larger numbers, not assessed.) Or: "Invent a subtraction that
needs an across-zero trade and a second trade, and prove the difference
by adding back."

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) writing 10 tens after the hundred trade
and forgetting to spend one ten on the ones (leaving tens at 10 instead
of 9); (2) subtracting the hundreds as if no trade happened (500 − 243
→ 357); (3) subtracting smaller-from-larger in each column regardless
of position. All three are caught by the same habit: re-count every
column after every trade, and check by adding back.
