# Lesson 06 — Mental ±100, Strategy Choice, and Explain Why

**Student goal:** I can add or subtract 100 in my head, I can pick the
best strategy for a three-digit problem, and I can explain why my
strategy works using place value.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U03](../README.md) · **Session 11 of 16**

## Materials

- Number cards 100–999 (or a thousand chart)
- Base-ten blocks (available, not required for every round)

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Add 100 to 347. Subtract 100 from 562. What changed?" (Expected: 447 and
462 — only the hundreds digit changed, by 1.) If mental ±10 from Unit 02
is shaky, replay a ±10 chain first: 347, 357, 367.

## Teaching

100 is 1 hundred, 0 tens, and 0 ones. Adding or subtracting 100 therefore
changes **only the hundreds digit** — the tens and ones digits stay
exactly as they were. That is a mental computation: no blocks, no written
method needed, as long as the number stays in the 100–900 range the
standard names (so 900 + 100 = 1000 is a next-step teaser, not today's
work).

Strategy choice is the unit's payoff. Ask, in order:

1. **Can I do it mentally?** (±100, ±10, or a no-trade pair with small
   digits.)
2. **Do I need blocks?** (A new trade, a double trade, or an across-zero
   trade — build it first.)
3. **Is the written method the fastest record?** (Once the trade is
   understood, write it.)
4. **How will I check?** (Addition checks subtraction; subtraction checks
   addition.)

Explaining why means naming the place-value fact, not just the steps:
"10 tens make 1 hundred, so composing keeps the total the same" is an
explanation; "I carried the 1" is only a description.

## Worked example 1 — mental ±100

> "347 + 100: the hundreds digit 3 becomes 4; tens 4 and ones 7 do not
> move. **447**. 562 − 100: the hundreds digit 5 becomes 4; tens 6 and
> ones 2 do not move. **462**."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** 100 has zero tens and zero ones, so there is
nothing to add in those columns. If the tens digit changed, something
went wrong — that is the self-check for mental ±100.

## Worked example 2 — choose a strategy, then explain why

Problem A: 900 − 100. Strategy: mental. "9 hundreds minus 1 hundred is 8
hundreds — **800**. No blocks needed, because only the hundreds digit
changes."

Problem B: 583 + 217. Strategy: written method, after one block check.
"Ones: 3 + 7 = 10 → 0 ones, carry 1 ten. Tens: 8 + 1 + 1 = 10 → 0 tens,
carry 1 hundred. Hundreds: 5 + 2 + 1 = 8. **800**. Why it works: 10 ones
make 1 ten and 10 tens make 1 hundred, so both trades keep the total the
same — the answer is still the same amount, re-bundled twice."

Problem C: 783 − 459. Strategy: blocks first. "The ones and tens both run
short, so build it: ones 3 < 9 → 13 − 9 = 4; tens: the tens hold 8 rods,
gave 1 away, hold 7 — 7 − 5 = 2; hundreds 7 − 4 = 3. **324**. Check by adding back: 324 + 459 = 783."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the best strategy is not always the
fastest-looking one. Blocks are the right choice when the trades are new
or double — speed comes after understanding, never instead of it.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds.

1. "Mentally: 234 + 100." (Expected: **334** — hundreds 2 → 3.)
2. "Mentally: 781 − 100." (Expected: **681** — hundreds 7 → 6.)
3. "Mentally: 509 + 100 and 850 − 100." (Expected: **609** and **750**.)
4. "Choose a strategy for 365 + 200, then solve it." (Expected: mental
   hundreds — 3 hundreds + 2 hundreds = 5 hundreds, tens 6 and ones 5
   unchanged — **565**.)
5. Error analysis: the adult says, "Adding 100 changes the tens digit,"
   and asks: "Is that right? Test it on 372 + 100." (Expected: wrong —
   372 + 100 = **472**; the hundreds digit 3 became 4, the tens digit
   stayed 7. 100 has zero tens, so the tens cannot change.)
6. "Explain why composing 10 tens into 1 hundred keeps a sum the same."
   (Expected: because 10 tens and 1 hundred are the same amount — the
   trade re-bundles, it does not add or remove.)

## Independent practice

The learner works without hints: mentally solve 418 + 100, 673 − 100,
290 + 100, 804 − 100; then solve 500 + 250 by a chosen strategy and write
one sentence explaining why it works.

*Alternate response mode:* say each mental answer and the digit that
changed aloud ("hundreds 4 → 5"); the adult writes the numerals.

## Application

Savings: "Jae had 635 stickers. Jae earned 100 more stickers. How many
now?" (Expected: **735** — hundreds 6 → 7, mentally.) "Then Jae gave 100
stickers away from 821. How many left?" (Expected: **721** — hundreds 8
→ 7, mentally.) "Which strategy did you use, and why was it enough?"
(Expected: mental ±100 — only the hundreds digit changes.)

## Exit check

"Mentally: 546 + 100 and 937 − 100. Then, in your own words, why does
adding 100 change only the hundreds digit?" Expected: **646** and **837**;
explanation: "100 is 1 hundred, 0 tens, 0 ones, so only the hundreds
digit moves." Record: secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** always name the digit that changed ("hundreds 3 →
  4") — never just the new number.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the hundreds digit on a number
  card and move it up or down by 1; the adult says the new number.
- **Floor-play variant:** lay out hundred-flats only; adding 100 is
  placing one more flat, subtracting 100 is removing one flat — the rods
  and cubes never move.

## Extension

"Mentally add 200 to 456, then subtract 300 from the result. Explain each
step." (456 + 200 = 656 — hundreds 4 → 6; 656 − 300 = 356 — hundreds 6 →
3. Each step changes only the hundreds digit.) Or: "For 999 + 100, what
breaks, and why is it not today's problem?" (Hundreds 9 + 1 = 10 hundreds
= 1000 — a new place, the thousands, which Unit 01 named only as a teaser.)

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) changing the tens digit for ±100 — the
self-check is "did any digit besides hundreds move?"; (2) choosing the
written method for every problem, including mental ones, and then making
column errors — strategy choice is a taught skill, not a preference to
skip; (3) describing steps instead of explaining ("I carried the 1") —
prompt with "what place-value fact makes that step legal?"
