# Lesson 04 — Subtract: No Regrouping, Then Decompose a Ten

**Student goal:** I can subtract three-digit numbers with blocks, and
when a column does not hold enough, I can decompose one ten into ten ones
(or one hundred into ten tens) and keep the difference honest.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U03](../README.md) · **Session 7 of 16**

## Materials

- Base-ten blocks: hundred-flats, ten-rods, one-cubes
- Place-value mat

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Build 453. Take away 128 in your head, column by column — where do you
get stuck?" (Expected: the ones — 3 ones cannot give up 8 ones. That stuck
place is exactly where today's trade happens.) If within-100 take-away is
shaky, replay a Unit 02 break-apart subtraction round first.

## Teaching

Subtraction is the reverse of addition: start with a build, take blocks
away, and what is left is the difference. Work right to left, ones first.

1. Build the starting (top) number on the mat.
2. Subtract ones. If there are not enough ones, **decompose** (trade
   down): swap 1 ten-rod for 10 one-cubes, then subtract the ones.
3. Subtract tens, **after** the trade — the tens column now holds one
   fewer rod. If there are not enough tens, decompose 1 hundred-flat
   into 10 ten-rods, then subtract.
4. Subtract hundreds. Read what is left.
5. The total taken away never changes during a trade — 1 ten and 10 ones
   are the same amount, just unbundled.

**Key vocabulary:** *decompose*, *trade down*. Composing bundles up;
decomposing unbundles down. Addition composes; subtraction decomposes.

## Worked example 1 — 486 − 231 (no regrouping)

Build 486. Take away 231: ones 6 − 1 = 5, tens 8 − 3 = 5, hundreds 4 − 2 =
2.

> "Nothing needed trading, because every column held enough. The
> difference is **255**."

Written method:

```text
  486
− 231
-----
  255
```

**Reasoning to name aloud:** no-regrouping subtraction is the mirror of
Lesson 01 — same columns, opposite direction. Check it by adding back:
255 + 231 = 486.

## Worked example 2 — 453 − 128 (decompose a ten)

Build 453 (4 flats, 5 rods, 3 cubes). Subtract 128.

> "Ones: 3 ones cannot give up 8 ones. Decompose: trade 1 ten-rod for 10
> one-cubes. Now the ones hold 13, and the tens hold 4 rods. Ones: 13 − 8
> = 5. Tens: 4 − 2 = 2. Hundreds: 4 − 1 = 3. The difference is **325**."

Written method, with the trade marked (cross out the 5 tens, write 4
above it; write 13 above the ones):

```text
   4 13
  4 5 3
− 1 2 8
---------
  3 2 5
```

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the crossed-out 5 becoming 4 is the whole
lesson in one mark: the tens column paid for the ones column's trade.
Forgetting that the tens column shrank is the classic error — and it is
exactly what the next practice round hunts for.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds with the blocks.

1. "Build and subtract 679 − 342." (Expected: ones 9 − 2 = 7, tens 7 − 4
   = 3, hundreds 6 − 3 = 3 — **337**, no trade.)
2. "Build and subtract 542 − 117." (Expected: ones 2 < 7 → decompose a
   ten: 12 − 7 = 5; tens 3 − 1 = 2 (4 tens became 3, then 3 − 1); hundreds
   5 − 1 = 4 — **425**.)
3. "Build and subtract 861 − 235." (Expected: ones 1 < 5 → 11 − 5 = 6;
   tens 5 − 3 = 2 (6 tens became 5, then 5 − 3); hundreds 8 − 2 = 6 —
   **626**.)
4. Error analysis: the adult shows "746 − 128 = 628" and asks: "Is it?
   Rebuild the tens after the trade." (Expected: wrong — ones 6 < 8 →
   16 − 8 = 8; tens 4 − 2 = 1 (the 4 tens became 3 after giving one away,
   then 3 − 2 = 1, not 2); hundreds 7 − 1 = 6. Correct difference **618**.
   The writer forgot the tens column shrank.)
5. "When do we decompose, and when do we not?" (Expected: decompose only
   when the top digit in a column is smaller than the digit being
   subtracted in that column.)

## Independent practice

The learner builds and writes, without hints: 958 − 421, 673 − 248,
594 − 176. The adult checks that every trade is marked on the written
method (crossed-out digit, new digit above) before the answer is read.

*Alternate response mode:* physically swap the rod for 10 cubes on the
mat and say "one ten becomes ten ones" aloud; the adult marks the written
method.

## Application

Pencils: "The box held 732 pencils. The class used 214 pencils. How many
pencils are left?" (Expected: ones 2 < 4 → 12 − 4 = 8; tens 2 − 1 = 1
(3 tens became 2, then 2 − 1); hundreds 7 − 2 = 5 — **518** pencils left.)

## Exit check

"Build 465 − 139 and tell me the difference, naming every trade."
Expected: ones 5 < 9 → 15 − 9 = 6; tens 5 − 3 = 2 (6 tens became 5, then
5 − 3); hundreds 4 − 1 = 3 — **326**. Record: secure / developing / not
yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** always say the trade as a full sentence ("one ten
  becomes ten ones") before marking the written method.
- **Alternate response mode:** keep the traded-away rod visible beside
  the mat (not hidden) until the column is subtracted, so the shrinkage
  is seen, not just remembered.
- **Floor-play variant:** lay the starting build in a row; physically
  remove the subtracted blocks from each column, trading first where a
  column runs short.

## Extension

"Subtract 900 − 347 with blocks. Where do the trades happen?"
(Ones 0 < 7 → decompose a ten from 0 tens? No tens to give — this needs
the hundred trade from Lesson 05, so treat it as a teaser: trade 1
hundred into 10 tens, then 1 ten into 10 ones: ones 10 − 7 = 3, tens 9 − 4
= 5, hundreds 8 − 3 = 5 — **553**. Full teaching of across-zero trades is
Lesson 05; do not assess this today.)
Or: "Invent a subtraction that needs exactly one ten trade, and prove
the difference by adding back."

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) subtracting the smaller digit from the
larger digit in each column regardless of position (e.g., 453 − 128
answered as 435, by doing 8 − 3 in the ones) — the blocks forbid this:
you cannot take 8 cubes from a pile of 3; (2) forgetting the column
shrank after a trade — always re-count the column after trading; (3)
trading when no trade is needed, out of habit — ask "does this column
hold enough?" first.
