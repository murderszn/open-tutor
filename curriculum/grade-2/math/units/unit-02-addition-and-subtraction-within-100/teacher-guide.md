# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Addition and Subtraction within 100

Adult-facing. Student materials never link here except through the clearly
labeled unit README. Solutions live in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md), kept
separate from the learner.

## The unit's big idea

Every strategy in this unit is the same move at different sizes: **regroup
into tens, then work with the easy piles.** Make-ten regroups ones into a
ten; break-apart sorts tens from ones; mental ±10 moves whole tens. When the
learner explains (2.NBT.B.9), listen for "nothing was added or taken away,
only regrouped" — that sentence is the unit's thesis.

## Session-by-session guidance

- **S1–S4 (Week 7, fluency):** Keep the ten-frame on the table all week.
  Speed is not the goal yet; correct splits are. If the learner counts all
  cubes from 1, return to the "say the need first" routine.
- **S5–S7:** Doubles to memory start here (5+5 through 9+9); near-doubles
  stay visual (cover the extra cube). Don't let "almost a double" become
  the double — the +1 must be said.
- **S8–S9:** The "add back the ones" step is the whole lesson. Use the
  fist-hold routine from Lesson 03 until it's automatic.
- **S10–S12:** Insist on the tens/ones sort before any combining — chant
  "tens with tens, ones with ones." Let the blocks stay out; paper-only
  too early produces digit-adders (Nico's error, Lesson 04).
- **S13–S14:** Trade physically (ten bank walk) at least three times before
  allowing the step-down shortcut. The check (`difference + subtracted =
  start`) is mandatory, not optional.
- **S15–S16:** Collect the explanation cards; they are your 2.NBT.B.9
  evidence. The quiz is formative — use it to aim Session 16's re-teach.

## Misconceptions and fixes

| Misconception | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong split (any two parts) | 9 + 6 → 3 + 3 | "Say the need first": 9 needs 1 |
| Double without the +1 | 7 + 8 = 14 | Cover the extra cube; say the double, uncover, add 1 |
| Forgetting to add back the ones | 13 − 6 → 4 | Fist-hold the set-aside ones; add them last, every time |
| Digit-adding | 46 + 32: 4 + 3 = 7 | Name the place: "4 *tens* + 3 *tens* = 70" |
| Subtracting the smaller ones digit regardless of position | 74 − 38 → 48 | Physical trade — you can't remove what isn't there |
| Concatenating for ±10 | 460 + 10 → 4610 | Drop one rod onto the pile; count what's there |

## Reteaching

- **Fluency within 20 shaky:** 5 extra minutes of strategy trays (Session A)
  at the start of every session for a week; never drill bare facts without
  the strategy attached.
- **Break-apart not transferring to paper:** keep blocks beside the paper
  and have the learner *draw* the rods/cubes they just built — drawing is
  the bridge.
- **Explanation cards vague:** go oral. Ask "what did the tens do? what did
  the ones do? did we add or remove anything?" Scribe the answers; the
  learner copies their own words.

## Readiness criteria

- **Ready for Unit 03:** quiz 8+/10 with strategies shown, assessment 17+,
  project 12+ with no criterion at 1.
- **Not yet:** use a flexible track week on the lowest objective. Unit 03
  assumes secure break-apart within 100 — entering without it turns
  three-digit work into digit-adding.

## Catch-up plan (flexible weeks)

Day 1: strategy trays (fluency). Day 2: build-and-break-apart with blocks
only. Day 3: one shop order slip re-done with full explanation. Day 4:
re-take the quiz items missed, then the parallel assessment tasks from the
answer key's alternates.
