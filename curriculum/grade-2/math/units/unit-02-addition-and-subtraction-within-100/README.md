# Unit 02 — Addition and Subtraction within 100

**Grade:** 2 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #14](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/14), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Big addition problems are small problems in disguise. In this unit the learner
builds mental fluency within 20 — making ten, doubling, counting on and back —
then extends those instincts to two-digit numbers by breaking them into tens
and ones, recombining with place-value reasoning. The unit ends with adding up
to four two-digit numbers, mentally adding and subtracting 10 from numbers
100–900, and explaining *why* each strategy works — because tens combine with
tens and ones combine with ones, never the other way around.

## Essential question

How do we break big addition and subtraction into small, safe steps we can do
in our heads?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks and Unit 01 (the grade-1 track feeds the same list):

- Adding and subtracting within 20 (counting on, making ten, fact families) —
  this unit pushes those skills toward fluency and memory
- Three-digit place value: hundred-flats, ten-rods, one-cubes; hundreds first,
  then tens, then ones
- Comparing and ordering numbers; 10 more / 10 less than a two-digit number

The first session opens with a place-value retrieval warm-up from Unit 01: the
learner builds a two-digit number with rods and cubes and names its tens and
ones before any addition happens.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Add and subtract fluently within 20 using make-ten, doubles, near-doubles,
   and count-on/count-back; demonstrate growing memory for sums of two
   one-digit numbers. (2.OA.B.2)
2. Add within 100 by breaking each number into tens and ones, adding the tens
   and the ones separately, and recombining — explaining the steps with
   blocks, drawings, or words. (2.NBT.B.5)
3. Subtract within 100 using break-apart strategies (subtract the tens, then
   the ones; decompose a ten when the ones run short), relating the steps to
   a written method. (2.NBT.B.5)
4. Add up to four two-digit numbers by grouping tens together and ones
   together, using make-ten groups among the ones when it helps. (2.NBT.B.6)
5. Mentally add or subtract 10 from any number 100–900 and explain, using place
   value, why a chosen strategy works — for example, why adding 10 changes
   only the tens digit. (2.NBT.B.8, 2.NBT.B.9)

## Vocabulary (taught with objects first, word second)

make-ten · double · near-double · count on · count back · break apart ·
recombine · tens · ones · compose a ten · decompose a ten · sum · difference ·
equation · unknown · strategy · explain

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials
list): ten-rods and one-cubes (or bundles of 10 straws plus loose straws),
number cards, a 0–120 number-line strip, two dice, paper "order slips" and
blank price tags, the generated illustration
[`assets/shop-counter-blank-tags.png`](assets/shop-counter-blank-tags.png),
paper and crayons, a hundreds chart. No sharp tools; supervise small parts;
check food-allergen safety before using food as counters.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, or drawing responses; the adult scribes
when the learner isn't ready to write. Rods and cubes are distinguishable by
size and shape, never by color alone. Each lesson includes a seated-table and
a floor-play variant, a language-access support, an alternate response mode,
and an extension. The generated illustration ships with alt text, a caption,
and a full text alternative so the project works without seeing the picture.
Strategy explanations may be spoken, drawn, or acted out with blocks — the
2.NBT.B.9 explanation standard explicitly permits objects and drawings.
