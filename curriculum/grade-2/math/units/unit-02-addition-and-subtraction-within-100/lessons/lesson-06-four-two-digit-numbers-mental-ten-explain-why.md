# Lesson 06 — Add Up to Four Two-Digit Numbers, Mentally ±10, and Explain Why

**Student goal:** I can add several two-digit numbers, add or subtract 10 in
my head, and explain why my strategy works.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U02](../README.md) · **Session 14 of 16**

## Materials

- Ten-rods and one-cubes
- Hundreds chart
- Explanation cards (blank index cards): "My strategy works because…"

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"23 + 45 with break-apart, fast." (Expected: 20 + 40 = 60, 3 + 5 = 8 → 68.)
Today adds more addends and a new mental trick — then the learner becomes the
teacher by explaining.

## Teaching

**Part 1 — four two-digit numbers (2.NBT.B.6).** Same recipe, bigger pile:
group all the tens, group all the ones, recombine. Hunt for make-ten groups
among the ones.

Example: 23 + 45 + 17 + 12. Tens: 20 + 40 + 10 + 10 = 80. Ones: 3 + 5 + 7
+ 2 = 17 — spot 3 + 7 = 10 hiding inside! So ones = 10 + 5 + 2 = 17.
Recombine: 80 + 17 = 97.

**Part 2 — mentally ±10 (2.NBT.B.8).** 10 is exactly one ten. Adding 10 grows
the tens digit by 1 and touches nothing else: 340 + 10 = 350. Subtracting 10
shrinks the tens digit by 1: 589 − 10 = 579. The ones digit *never* changes —
because we added zero ones.

**Part 3 — explain why (2.NBT.B.9).** A strategy works when every step keeps
the total the same. "Break-apart works because tens are groups of ten: 3
tens + 2 tens is 5 tens no matter how you pile them, and ones join ones.
Nothing was added or removed, only regrouped."

**Key vocabulary:** *group*, *mentally*, *explain*.

## Worked example 1 — 34 + 26 + 18 + 22

The adult builds all four numbers with rods and cubes, then sorts.

> "Tens pile: 30 + 20 + 10 + 20 = 80. Ones pile: 4 + 6 + 8 + 2. Hunt for
> make-ten: 4 + 6 = 10, and 8 + 2 = 10 — two perfect tens hiding! Ones = 10
> + 10 = 20. Recombine: 80 + 20 = **100**. Check another way: 34 + 26 = 60,
> 18 + 22 = 40, 60 + 40 = 100. ✓"

**Reasoning to name aloud:** grouping is safe because addition doesn't care
about order (commutativity, in plain words: "adding in any order gives the
same total"). We reordered to find the easy pairs.

## Worked example 2 — 275 + 10 and 275 − 10

The adult shows 275 on the hundreds chart (2 flats worth, 7 rods, 5 cubes).

> "Add 10: drop in exactly one more ten-rod. Nothing else changes. 270
> becomes 280 — I mean 275 becomes **285**. Subtract 10: take one rod away:
> **265**. The ones digit stayed 5 both times. Why? Ten is one ten and zero
> ones — there are no new ones to change the ones digit, and no new hundreds
> to change the hundreds digit. Only the tens digit can move."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** this is the explanation 2.NBT.B.9 asks for —
not "because that's the rule" but "because 10 = 1 ten + 0 ones." Place
value *is* the reason.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

1. "24 + 31 + 15." (Expected: tens 20 + 30 + 10 = 60; ones 4 + 1 + 5 = 10;
   60 + 10 = **70**.)
2. "52 + 28 + 30." (Expected: tens 50 + 20 + 30 = 100; ones 2 + 8 + 0 = 10;
   100 + 10 = **110**. Note: 110 is past 100 — fine, and a nice preview of
   hundreds.)
3. "460 + 10; 460 − 10." (Expected: **470**; **450**. Ones digit 0 stays.)
4. "599 − 10." (Expected: **589**. Tens digit 9 → 8.)
5. "Fill an explanation card: '46 + 38 works with break-apart because…'"
   (Expected something like: "…tens go with tens: 40 + 30 = 70; ones go
   with ones: 6 + 8 = 14; nothing was added or taken away, just regrouped,
   so 70 + 14 = 84.")

## Independent practice

4 tasks — 36 + 24 + 18 (tens 30+20+10=60, ones 6+4+8=18 → **78**); 380 + 10
(**390**); 525 − 10 (**515**); one more explanation card: "72 − 35 with
step-down works because…" (Expected: "…I took away the 30 first and then the
5, and 30 + 5 is the whole 35, so I took away exactly 35: 42 − 5 = 37.")

## Application

"Three shop orders: 25, 38, and 41 tokens. What is the total? Which ones
made a ten?" Expected: tens 20 + 30 + 40 = 90; ones 5 + 8 + 1 = 14 (8 + 1 =
9? No — 5 + 8 + 1: 5 + 5 = 10 hiding? 8 = 5 + 3, so 5 + (5 + 3) + 1 = 10 + 4
= 14); total 90 + 14 = **104** tokens.

## Exit check

"Explain: why does adding 10 change only the tens digit?" Expected: "10 is
one ten and zero ones, so the ones can't change and the hundreds can't
change — only tens grow by one."

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** sort rods/cubes into labeled tens/ones trays for
  each addend before combining.
- **Floor-play variant:** four learners each hold one addend's blocks, then
  walk their tens to the TENS zone.
- **Language access:** sentence frame on the explanation card: "My strategy
  works because ___." Learner fills the blank orally first.
- **Alternate response mode:** learner arranges pre-written tens/ones cards;
  adult scribes the explanation.
- **Extension:** "Add 99 + 99." (Break-apart: 90 + 90 = 180, 9 + 9 = 18,
  180 + 18 = 198 — or near-hundred: 100 + 100 − 2 = 198.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: when adding four numbers, adding *all digits* as ones
(2 + 4 + 1 + 1 = 8 tens-place…). Keep the tens/ones sort physical until the
learner self-sorts. For ±10, watch for "460 + 10 = 470? or 4610?" — the
learner who writes 4610 is concatenating, not adding; the rod-drop demo
fixes it. The explanation cards are the unit's 2.NBT.B.9 evidence — collect
them; they feed the project rubric and the assessment. Answer-key reference:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-06).
