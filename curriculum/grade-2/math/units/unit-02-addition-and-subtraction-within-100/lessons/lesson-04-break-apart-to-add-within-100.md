# Lesson 04 — Break Apart to Add within 100

**Student goal:** I can add two-digit numbers by breaking them into tens and
ones, and I can show why it works with blocks.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U02](../README.md) · **Session 10 of 16**

## Materials

- Ten-rods and one-cubes (or straw bundles + loose straws)
- Paper and crayon for drawing the break-apart
- Number cards 10–99

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Build 47 with rods and cubes. Name the tens and ones." (Expected: 4
ten-rods, 7 one-cubes; 4 tens + 7 ones.) Then: "If I add 2 more rods, what
number?" (Expected: 67.) This lesson adds the tens and the ones as two
separate piles.

## Teaching

Two-digit addition is just ones-addition plus tens-addition, done together.

**Break-apart recipe:**

1. Break each number into tens + ones. (34 = 30 + 4; 25 = 20 + 5.)
2. Add the tens: 30 + 20 = 50.
3. Add the ones: 4 + 5 = 9.
4. Recombine: 50 + 9 = 59.

Golden rule: **tens combine with tens, ones combine with ones** — never mix
a ten with a one. Show every step with rods and cubes first; the written
version comes later.

**Key vocabulary:** *break apart*, *recombine*.

## Worked example 1 — 34 + 25

The adult builds 34 (3 rods, 4 cubes) and 25 (2 rods, 5 cubes) in two groups.

> "Break apart: 34 = 30 + 4, and 25 = 20 + 5. Push the rods together: 3 rods
> + 2 rods = 5 rods — that's 50. Push the cubes together: 4 + 5 = 9 ones.
> Recombine: 50 + 9 = **59**. Check by building 59 directly: 5 rods, 9
> cubes — same pile. It works because we sorted the same objects by size."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the rods were always 30 + 20; we just counted
them together instead of separately. Sorting by place before combining is
the whole trick — it turns one hard problem into two easy ones.

## Worked example 2 — 47 + 28 (ones make a new ten)

The adult builds 47 and 28.

> "Break apart: 40 + 7 and 20 + 8. Tens: 40 + 20 = 60. Ones: 7 + 8 — that's
> 15, which is a ten and 5 ones! Trade 10 cubes for 1 rod: now I have 7
> rods and 5 cubes. Recombine: 60 + 15 = **75**."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** sometimes the ones pile grows past 10. That is
fine — 15 ones *is* 1 ten + 5 ones, so we trade and keep going. The total
never changed; only the grouping did. This is composing a ten, the skill
Lesson 01's make-ten was secretly practicing.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Use rods and cubes for each; draw the break-apart after building.

1. "23 + 45." (Expected: 20 + 40 = 60; 3 + 5 = 8; 68.)
2. "52 + 37." (Expected: 50 + 30 = 80; 2 + 7 = 9; 89.)
3. "35 + 27." (Expected: 30 + 20 = 50; 5 + 7 = 12 → trade to 1 ten + 2 ones;
   60 + 2 = 62.)
4. Error analysis: "Nico adds 46 + 32 and says 46 + 32 = 70, because 4 + 3 =
   7 and 6 + 2 = 8, so… 78? He can't decide." Ask: "What is Nico mixing up?"
   (Expected: he added the *digits* 4 + 3 = 7 but forgot the digits stand
   for tens: 40 + 30 = 70; then ones 6 + 2 = 8; recombine 70 + 8 = **78**.)

## Independent practice

4 sums; learner builds, then writes the break-apart — 41 + 36, 63 + 24,
28 + 46, 55 + 39. Expected: 77, 87, 74, 94. Adult checks the tens/ones
sorting on each.

(41 + 36: 40 + 30 = 70, 1 + 6 = 7 → 77. 63 + 24: 60 + 20 = 80, 3 + 4 = 7 →
87. 28 + 46: 20 + 40 = 60, 8 + 6 = 14 → 1 ten + 4 ones → 74. 55 + 39:
50 + 30 = 80, 5 + 9 = 14 → 80 + 14 = 94.)

## Application

Two shop price tags read 36 and 42 (tokens). "A customer buys both. What is
the total? Show the break-apart." Expected: 30 + 40 = 70, 6 + 2 = 8, total
**78** tokens.

## Exit check

"26 + 58. Break apart and recombine." Expected: 20 + 50 = 70; 6 + 8 = 14
→ 1 ten + 4 ones; 70 + 14 = **84**.

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** sort rods left, cubes right in two labeled zones.
- **Floor-play variant:** big floor zones "TENS" and "ONES"; learner carries
  rods to TENS, cubes to ONES.
- **Language access:** chant "tens with tens, ones with ones" while sorting;
  learner repeats before combining.
- **Alternate response mode:** learner places pre-written number cards
  (30, 20, 4, 5) into tens/ones boxes; adult writes the recombination.
- **Extension:** "Add 34 + 25 + 18." (Preview of Lesson 06: tens 30 + 20 +
  10 = 60; ones 4 + 5 + 8 = 17; 60 + 17 = 77.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconception (Nico's error): adding digits as if they were all ones
(4 + 3 = 7 instead of 40 + 30 = 70). Every break-apart must name the place:
"4 *tens* + 3 *tens*." This is 2.NBT.B.5 in action — strategies based on
place value. If the learner can do it with blocks but not on paper, that is
developmentally normal at grade 2; keep the blocks beside the paper for
weeks. Answer-key reference: [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-04).
