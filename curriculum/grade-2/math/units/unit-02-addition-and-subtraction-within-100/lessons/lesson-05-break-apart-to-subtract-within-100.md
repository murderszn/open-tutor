# Lesson 05 — Break Apart to Subtract within 100

**Student goal:** I can subtract two-digit numbers by breaking them apart,
and I can trade a ten for ones when I need to.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U02](../README.md) · **Session 13 of 16**

## Materials

- Ten-rods and one-cubes
- Paper for the step-down number line

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"13 − 5 with take-from-ten." (Expected: 13 = 10 + 3; 10 − 5 = 5; 5 + 3 =
8.) Today's lesson is the same idea, one size bigger: subtract the tens,
then the ones, trading a ten when the ones run short.

## Teaching

Two strategies; both are break-apart.

**Strategy A — easy ones (no trade needed):** break both numbers, subtract
tens from tens, ones from ones, recombine. 46 − 23: 40 − 20 = 20; 6 − 3 = 3;
20 + 3 = 23.

**Strategy B — short ones (trade a ten):** when the ones digit on top is
smaller, trade one ten-rod for 10 ones first. 72 − 35: 72 = 60 + 12 (traded
one ten); 60 − 30 = 30; 12 − 5 = 7; 30 + 7 = 37.

**Strategy C — step-down:** subtract in two jumps on a number line. 72 − 35:
jump back 30 → 42, then jump back 5 in small steps: 42 → 40 → 37.

Pick whichever fits; always check with addition: 37 + 35 should be 72.

**Key vocabulary:** *decompose a ten* — trading 1 ten-rod for 10 ones.

## Worked example 1 — 68 − 41 (no trade)

The adult builds 68 (6 rods, 8 cubes).

> "No trade needed — 8 ones cover the 4 ones I'm taking away. Break apart:
> 68 = 60 + 8, 41 = 40 + 1. Tens: 60 − 40 = 20. Ones: 8 − 1 = 7. Recombine:
> 20 + 7 = **27**. Check: 27 + 41 = 68. ✓"

**Reasoning to name aloud:** subtraction undoes addition, so the check is a
real proof, not a guess. We sorted by place first — tens with tens, ones
with ones — exactly like addition.

## Worked example 2 — 72 − 35 (trade a ten)

The adult builds 72 and holds 35 beside it.

> "5 ones need to come off, but I only see 2 loose cubes. Trade one ten-rod
> for 10 ones: now I have 6 rods and 12 cubes — still 72, just regrouped.
> Tens: 60 − 30 = 30. Ones: 12 − 5 = 7. Recombine: 30 + 7 = **37**. Check:
> 37 + 35 = 72. ✓ Or step-down: 72 − 30 = 42, then 42 − 5 = 37. Same answer."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the trade is legal because 1 ten *is* 10 ones —
the total never changed, only the packaging. This is the physical meaning of
"borrowing"; we never borrow from nowhere.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

1. "53 − 16 — which strategy?" (Expected: trade or step-down; step-down:
   53 − 10 = 43, 43 − 6 = 37; trade: 50 → 40 + 13, 40 − 10 = 30, 13 − 6 =
   7, 30 + 7 = 37. Check: 37 + 16 = 53 ✓.)
2. "85 − 27." (Expected: step-down: 85 − 20 = 65, 65 − 7 = 58; trade:
   80 → 70 + 15, 70 − 20 = 50, 15 − 7 = 8, 50 + 8 = 58. Check: 58 + 27 =
   85 ✓.)
3. "49 − 22." (Expected: no trade; 40 − 20 = 20, 9 − 2 = 7 → 27. Check:
   27 + 22 = 49 ✓.)
4. Error analysis: "Ava computes 74 − 38 as 70 − 30 = 40, 4 − 8 = … and gets
   stuck, so she writes 48." Ask: "Where did Ava go wrong?" (Expected: 4
   ones can't cover 8 — she must trade a ten first: 70 → 60 + 14;
   60 − 30 = 30, 14 − 8 = 6, answer **36**. Check: 36 + 38 = 74 ✓.)

## Independent practice

4 differences; learner shows the trade or step-down each time — 74 − 32,
96 − 58, 61 − 24, 83 − 45. Expected: 42, 38, 37, 38.

(74 − 32: 70 − 30 = 40, 4 − 2 = 2 → 42. 96 − 58: 96 − 50 = 46, 46 − 8 =
38. 61 − 24: 61 − 20 = 41, 41 − 4 = 37. 83 − 45: 83 − 40 = 43, 43 − 5 =
38.)

## Application

"A shop basket costs 52 tokens. A customer returns a 26-token scoop. What
is the new total?" Expected: 52 − 20 = 32, 32 − 6 = 26; answer **26**
tokens. Check with addition: 26 + 26 = 52 ✓.

## Exit check

"47 − 29. Show the trade or the step-down, then check." Expected: 47 − 20 =
27, 27 − 9 = 18; check 18 + 29 = 47 ✓.

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** physically trade the rod for 10 cubes; count the
  10 aloud.
- **Floor-play variant:** "ten bank" — the learner walks one rod to the
  bank and walks back with 10 cubes.
- **Language access:** gesture a rod snapping into ten cubes while saying
  "one ten becomes ten ones."
- **Alternate response mode:** learner points to the trade on a drawn
  picture; adult performs it and writes.
- **Extension:** "Explain why 72 − 35 and 62 − 25 give the same answer."
  (Both drop each number by 10 — shifting both numbers keeps the difference.
  Preview of compensation thinking.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconception (Ava's error): subtracting the smaller ones digit from
the larger regardless of position (4 − 8 → "8 − 4 = 4"). The physical trade
is the cure — you cannot remove what isn't there. Second misconception:
forgetting the check; make `difference + subtracted = start` a habit, not a
suggestion. Decomposition here is 2.NBT.B.5 fluency; the written algorithm
with regrouping arrives formally in Unit 03 with three-digit numbers.
