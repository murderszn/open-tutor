# Lesson 03 — Subtract within 20: Take from Ten and Think Addition

**Student goal:** I can subtract within 20 by taking from a ten, and I can
check subtraction with addition.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U02](../README.md) · **Session 8 of 16**

## Materials

- 20 one-cubes
- Ten-frame card
- Number cards 11–20 and 1–9

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Show 13 as 10 + 3 with the ten-frame and loose cubes." (Expected: full
frame plus 3 loose.) Every problem today splits a teen number into 10 + ones
first — if the split is shaky, practice 14, 16, 18 as 10 + ones.

## Teaching

Subtraction within 20 has two friend-strategies, and they check each other.

1. **Take from ten:** split the starting number into 10 + ones. Subtract from
   the 10 (easy), then add the ones back. For 13 − 5: 13 = 10 + 3; 10 − 5 =
   5; 5 + 3 = 8.
2. **Think addition:** subtraction asks "what's missing?" Turn it around:
   13 − 5 = ☐ becomes 5 + ☐ = 13. If you know 5 + 8 = 13, then 13 − 5 = 8.

Rule for take-from-ten: *subtract from the ten, then add back the ones you
set aside.* Forgetting to add the ones back is the #1 error — watch for it.

**Key vocabulary:** *take from ten*, *think addition*, *check*.

## Worked example 1 — 15 − 7 with take-from-ten

The adult sets out 15 cubes: a full ten-frame plus 5 loose.

> "Split: 15 = 10 + 5. Take the 7 from the full frame first: 10 − 7 = 3.
> Now add back the 5 ones I set aside: 3 + 5 = **8**. So 15 − 7 = 8."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** 10 − 7 = 3 is easy because it's within 10. The
5 loose cubes were never part of the subtraction — they were waiting on the
side, and they must come back into the answer. Adding them back is not
optional; it's what makes 15 − 7 different from 10 − 7.

## Worked example 2 — 13 − 5 with think-addition

The adult writes `13 − 5 = ☐` and beside it `5 + ☐ = 13`.

> "Start at 5 and climb to 13: 5 to 10 is 5 more, 10 to 13 is 3 more.
> 5 + 3 = 13, so I climbed 8 total. That means ☐ = **8**: 13 − 5 = 8. Same
> answer as take-from-ten gave — two roads, one town."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** think-addition works because addition and
subtraction undo each other (2.NBT.B.5 names this relationship). When two
different strategies agree, the answer is almost certainly right — that is
the beginning of self-checking.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

1. "14 − 6, take from ten." (Expected: 14 = 10 + 4; 10 − 6 = 4; 4 + 4 = 8.)
2. "16 − 9, take from ten." (Expected: 16 = 10 + 6; 10 − 9 = 1; 1 + 6 = 7.)
3. "12 − 4, think addition." (Expected: 4 + ☐ = 12; climb 4→10 is 6,
   10→12 is 2; total 8; 12 − 4 = 8.)
4. "11 − 5, both ways." (Expected: take-from-ten: 10 − 5 = 5, 5 + 1 = 6;
   think-addition: 5 + 6 = 11; answer 6, strategies agree.)
5. Error analysis: "Lena splits 13 into 10 + 3 and says 10 − 6 = 4, so
   13 − 6 = 4." Ask: "What did Lena forget?" (Expected: she forgot to add
   the 3 ones back: 4 + 3 = **7**.)

## Independent practice

4 solves, learner picks the strategy and names it — 15 − 6, 13 − 7, 17 − 8,
14 − 5. Expected: 9, 6, 9, 9. Adult checks that the "add back the ones" step
appears in every take-from-ten.

## Application

"You have 17 beads and give 9 to a friend. How many are left? Solve it one
way, then check it the other way." Expected: 17 − 9 = 8 (take-from-ten:
17 − 7 = 10, 10 − 2 = 8); check: 9 + 8 = 17. ✓

## Exit check

"16 − 8. Show both strategies." Expected: take-from-ten: 16 = 10 + 6;
10 − 8 = 2; 2 + 6 = 8. Think-addition: 8 + 8 = 16, so 16 − 8 = 8.

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** ten-frame plus loose cubes; physically move the
  taken-away cubes off the frame, then slide the loose ones next to the
  remainder.
- **Floor-play variant:** ten big floor circles (tape); the learner steps
  cubes out.
- **Language access:** hold up the "set aside" ones in a fist while
  subtracting from the ten — the fist is the reminder to add them back.
- **Alternate response mode:** learner points: frame → minus card → loose
  cubes → plus card; adult writes each step.
- **Extension:** "What is 23 − 9?" (Take from the *twenty*: 23 = 20 + 3;
  20 − 9 = 11; 11 + 3 = 14 — a preview of Lesson 05's step-down.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconception (Lena's error): treating the problem as 10 − n instead
of (10 − n) + ones. The fist-hold routine fixes it physically. Second
misconception: in think-addition, counting up from the wrong number
(13 − 5 → "5, 6, 7…" counts 9 steps instead of 8). Insist on the bridge
through 10: "5 to 10, then 10 to 13." These within-20 strategies are the
mental engine for Lessons 04–06; do not rush past them. Answer-key reference:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-03).
