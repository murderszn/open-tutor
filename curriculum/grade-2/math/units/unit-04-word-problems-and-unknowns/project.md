# Project — Our Story Problem Book

**Unit:** [U04](README.md) · **Sessions:** 12 (draft) and 15 (finish, check, share)
**Time:** two 25–30-minute sessions, plus sharing time

## The task

Make a story problem book with **six original stories** — one of each
kind below — each on its own page. The market-stall picture in
[Lesson 02](lessons/lesson-02-put-together-take-apart.md) may inspire
a story, but every story in the book must be the learner's own words
and numbers.

1. An **add-to** story with the result unknown.
2. A **take-from** story with the result unknown.
3. A **put-together / take-apart** story with a part unknown.
4. A **compare** story asking "how many more" (difference unknown).
5. A story with a **hidden start or change** (start unknown or change
   unknown).
6. A **two-step** story.

## Materials

- A folded-paper book (6 pages) or a folder with 6 sheets
- Crayons, counters, number-line strip
- The unit's story cards from practice sessions (as models, not to copy)

## Steps

### Part 1 (Session 12) — draft three stories

1. Choose the first three kinds (add-to, take-from, part unknown).
2. For each story, write or dictate: the story in 2–3 sentences, a
   drawing or bar, and an equation with a box (□) for the unknown.
3. Solve each story and write the answer in a full sentence ("Maya
   has 65 apples in all.").
4. Check each answer one way: estimate, inverse, or story fit — and
   write the check beside the answer.

### Part 2 (Session 15) — finish, check, share

5. Write the remaining three stories (compare, hidden unknown,
   two-step) the same way. For the two-step story, circle the middle
   amount.
6. Swap books with the adult: the adult solves each story without
   looking at the learner's answers, then the two compare. Any story
   the adult cannot solve from the words alone gets rewritten until
   it is clear.
7. Share: read one favorite story aloud and explain its equation and
   its check.

## Deliverables

- Six pages, one story per page, each with: story text, drawing or
  bar, equation with □, answer sentence, and a written check.
- A cover with a title and a drawing (the market-stall picture may be
  copied by hand as inspiration; do not paste the generated image
  itself into learner work — it stays in the unit's assets).

## Rubric (adult scores each criterion 0–2)

| Criterion | 2 — strong | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Story kinds (all six present and correctly typed)** | All six kinds present; each story matches its kind | 4–5 kinds present, or one story mistyped | Fewer than 4 kinds, or stories copied from lessons |
| **Equation matches the story** | Every equation uses the story's numbers with □ in the right place | Most equations match; one box misplaced | Equations do not follow the stories |
| **Computation within 100** | All six answers correct | 4–5 answers correct | Fewer than 4 correct |
| **Check and explanation** | Every page has a written check (estimate, inverse, or story fit) that actually tests the answer | Some pages checked, or checks that do not test the answer | No checks, or checks copied without meaning |
| **Clarity (could the adult solve it?)** | Every story solvable from its words alone | One story needed a rewrite to be solvable | Two or more stories unclear after rewrite |

**Scoring:** 9–10 = secure (ready for the unit assessment); 6–8 =
developing (revise the weakest pages before Session 16); 0–5 = not
yet (replay the matching lessons, then revise with adult support).
The book itself is the evidence: keep it in private storage, never in
this public repository.
