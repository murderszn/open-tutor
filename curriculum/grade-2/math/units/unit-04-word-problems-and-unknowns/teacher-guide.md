# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Word Problems and Unknowns

**Unit:** [U04](README.md) · **Answer key:** [answer-key.md](answer-key.md)
(adult only — never beside student materials)

## How this unit runs

Sixteen 25–30-minute sessions across track weeks 15–18. Every session
opens with a 2-minute number-talk warm-up (a within-100 computation
from Units 02–03, or a "grew or shrank?" story call) and closes with
the lesson's exit check, recorded as **secure / developing / not yet**
per objective. Keep sessions story-first: at grade 2, acting and
drawing come before symbols, and the equation is introduced as a
record of the story — never as a rule to memorize first.

**Session map:** 1 L1 → 2 practice A → 3 L2 → 4 game B → 5 L3 →
6 practice C → 7 L4 → 8 game D → 9 L5 → 10 practice E → 11 L6 →
12 project P1 → 13 review F + re-teach → 14 quiz + feedback →
15 project P2 + share → 16 assessment.

## Objective-by-objective guidance

### Objective 1 — add-to / take-from, result unknown (L1)

The unit's load-bearing habit, in four moves: act/draw, name the
unknown, write the equation, check the story. Do not rush the "grew
or shrank?" question: a learner who computes before acting will
choose operations from keywords and fail every later lesson. Lesson
01's error round (46 + 28 = 64) is the model to imitate — re-act the
story, re-break-apart the tens and ones, and name the dropped ten.

### Objective 2 — put together / take apart (L2)

Teach the bar as a picture of a relationship, not an action: the whole
bar never moves. The generated illustration is a story prompt, not a
counting test — the learner invents the numbers, and the adult must
never ask "how many apples are in the picture?" as if the picture
held an exact count. The key sentence is "the parts remake the
whole": every part-unknown answer is checked by adding the parts back
to the whole.

### Objective 3 — compare stories (L3)

The hardest objective in the unit, and the one most likely to need
re-teaching. The teaching order matters: difference unknown first
(the gap is visible in the bars), then bigger unknown, then smaller
unknown (the trap: the bigger amount is named first). If a learner
adds for every "more," return to the bars: line both amounts up from
the same edge and point to what is actually missing — the gap, the
longer bar, or the shorter bar.

### Objective 4 — unknowns in all positions (L4)

Start-unknown problems feel impossible because the learner cannot
"start" the story. The cure is the backward walk: physically remove
the added counters or return the taken counters, then replay forward.
Insist on the replay for every start-unknown item until the habit
holds — it is the only check that catches "solved with the story's
operation instead of its inverse."

### Objective 5 — two-step stories (L5)

Read the whole story before computing — this is a reading habit as
much as a math habit. The circled middle amount is non-negotiable:
an answer without it is unfinished, even if the final number is
right, because the next unit's problems will punish skipped middle
amounts. Lesson 05's error round (right answer, wrong order) teaches
why order is part of correctness.

### Objective 6 — checking for reasonableness (L6)

Teach the three checks as a fixed routine: estimate first (fast, catches
wild answers), inverse second (catches small slips), story fit always
(catches wrong equations). A learner who estimates after computing
exactly has not learned the habit — the estimate must come first, on
paper, before the exact computation.

## Midyear review (Week 18)

Sessions 13–16 are the track's midyear review, not just this unit's
review week. Practice Session F and assessment Part B revisit Units
01–03: three-digit comparison (hundreds, then tens, then ones),
three-digit addition and subtraction with trades (including
decomposing across zero), and mental ±100/±10. Use the results to
write the re-teach plan below before Unit 05 begins; do not carry an
unaddressed "not yet" into the equal-groups unit, which assumes
within-100 fluency.

## Misconceptions to watch

- **Keyword hunting** ("more = add," "left = subtract"): ask "what
  happened?" and "who has more?" before any operation is named.
- **Adding for a difference**: the bars refute it — a total is not a
  gap.
- **Part bigger than whole**: the "parts remake the whole" check
  catches it; a part answer larger than the whole is impossible.
- **Start-unknown solved forward**: the replay check catches it; if
  the replay does not land on the given end, the answer is wrong.
- **One-step answers to two-step stories**: "does the story end
  here?" after step 1, every time.
- **Estimate treated as answer**: the estimate is a neighborhood;
  the exact computation still follows.

## Readiness criteria

Ready for Unit 05 when the learner, across the quiz and assessment:

- Solves one-step stories of all five situations with the unknown in
  any position, writing a matching equation (Objectives 1–4).
- Solves two-step stories with both steps written and the middle
  amount circled (Objective 5).
- Estimates before computing and inverse-checks at least the
  assessment's reasonableness item (Objective 6).
- Scores Part B 3–4 (midyear skills holding) or has a dated re-teach
  plan for any missed item.

## Re-teach paths

- **Objective 1 weak:** replay Lesson 01 with counters only (no
  written equations) for two sessions, then reintroduce equations.
- **Objective 2 weak:** replay Lesson 02's bars with two-color
  counters; drill "parts remake the whole" as the check after every
  item.
- **Objective 3 weak:** replay Lesson 03 with paper strips cut to
  length; do not move on until the learner names who is bigger
  before computing, five times running.
- **Objective 4 weak:** replay Lesson 04's backward walk with real
  objects; require the forward replay in writing for every item.
- **Objective 5 weak:** replay Lesson 05 with the three-mat
  floor-play variant; require the circled middle amount on every
  item, including one-step items (circle "not applicable" is not
  accepted — instead, the learner writes "one step" explicitly).
- **Objective 6 weak:** replay Lesson 06 with the estimate written
  before the exact computation, enforced by covering the exact-answer
  space until the estimate is down.
- **Part B weak:** replay the matching Unit 01–03 lesson (place
  value, three-digit trades, mental ±100/±10) in the flexible weeks;
  Unit 05's equal-groups work can start while a single Part B item
  is being re-taught, but not while within-100 fluency itself is
  "not yet."
