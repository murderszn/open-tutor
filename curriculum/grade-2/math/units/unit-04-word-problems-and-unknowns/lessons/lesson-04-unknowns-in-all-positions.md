# Lesson 04 — The Unknown Can Hide Anywhere

**Student goal:** I can find a missing start or a missing change by
undoing the story, and write an equation with the box in any position.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U04](../README.md) · **Session 7 of 16**

## Materials

- Counters
- Paper and crayons
- Number-line strip 0–100

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Some counters are in a bag. I add 17 more, and now there are 59.
How many were in the bag first? Try it with counters." (Expected:
59 − 17 = **42**.) If the learner is stuck on where to begin, run the
story backwards: "Take the 17 back out."

## Teaching

So far the missing number has usually sat at the end. But a story can
hide its missing number anywhere:

- **Start unknown:** `□ + change = end` or `□ − change = end`.
  Find it by **undoing**: run the story backwards from the end.
- **Change unknown:** `start + □ = end` or `start − □ = end`.
  Find it by comparing the start and the end.
- **Result unknown:** `start ± change = □` (Lessons 01–03).

The undo rules, said aloud:

- If the story **added** and the start is missing, **subtract** the
  change from the end: `□ + 17 = 59` → `59 − 17 = 42`.
- If the story **took away** and the start is missing, **add** the
  change back to the end: `□ − 19 = 46` → `46 + 19 = 65`.
- If the change is missing, **compare** start and end: for an add-to
  story, `end − start`; for a take-from story, `start − end`.

After solving, **replay the story forward** with the found number.
If the replay lands on the given end, the answer is right.

**Key vocabulary:** *start unknown*, *change unknown*, *undo* (run
the story backwards), *replay* (check forward).

## Worked example 1 — start unknown, add to

"Some stickers were in Ana's book. Her aunt gave her 17 more stickers.
Now Ana has 59 stickers. How many stickers did Ana have first?"

> "Equation: □ + 17 = 59. The start is missing, and the story added,
> so undo with subtraction: 59 − 17 = □. 59 − 10 = 49, 49 − 7 =
> **42**. Replay forward: 42 + 17 = 59 — it lands exactly. Ana had
> **42 stickers** first."

## Worked example 2 — change unknown, take from

"A baker had 73 rolls. She sold some rolls. Now she has 48 rolls. How
many rolls did she sell?"

> "Equation: 73 − □ = 48. The change is missing. Compare start and
> end: 73 − 48 = □. 73 − 40 = 33, 33 − 8 = **25**. Replay: 73 − 25 =
> 48 — it lands exactly. She sold **25 rolls**."

## Worked example 3 — change unknown, add to

"There were 36 children on the playground. More children arrived. Now
there are 81 children. How many children arrived?"

> "Equation: 36 + □ = 81. Compare end and start: 81 − 36 = □.
> 81 − 30 = 51, 51 − 6 = **45**. Replay: 36 + 45 = 81. **45 children**
> arrived."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** a box at the front of an equation is not
a different kind of math — it is the same story with the curtain
closed on a different part. Undoing is just replaying the story in
reverse, and the forward replay is the check that proves it.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds with counters.

1. "Some marbles were in a jar. 26 more were added. Now there are
   70. How many were in the jar first?" (Expected: start unknown,
   □ + 26 = 70, 70 − 26 = **44**.)
2. "A shop had 85 balloons. Some were sold. Now there are 57. How
   many were sold?" (Expected: change unknown, 85 − □ = 57,
   85 − 57 = **28**.)
3. "Some pencils were in a box. 19 were given away. Now there are 46
   left. How many were in the box first?" (Expected: start unknown,
   □ − 19 = 46, 46 + 19 = **65**.)
4. Error analysis: the adult shows "□ + 24 = 68, so □ = 68 + 24 = 92"
   and asks: "Replay it." (Expected: wrong — replaying 92 + 24 = 116,
   not 68. Undo an addition with subtraction: 68 − 24 = **44**;
   replay 44 + 24 = 68.)
5. "When do you add to undo, and when do you subtract?" (Expected:
   "Undo an addition with subtraction; undo a subtraction with
   addition. Then replay forward to check.")

## Independent practice

The learner writes the equation first, then solves, without hints:
"Some beads were in a pouch. 38 more were added. Now there are 91.
How many first?" (□ + 38 = 91, 91 − 38 = **53**); "A crate held 64
apples. Some were taken. Now there are 39. How many were taken?"
(64 − □ = 39, 64 − 39 = **25**); "Some crayons were in a box. 27 were
handed out. Now there are 58 left. How many first?" (□ − 27 = 58,
58 + 27 = **85**).

*Alternate response mode:* act the story backwards with counters
("put the taken ones back" / "take the added ones away") and say the
found number aloud.

## Application

Pencil story: "Some pencils were in a drawer. 24 pencils were given
to a class. Now 37 pencils are left in the drawer. How many pencils
were in the drawer first?" (Expected: start unknown, □ − 24 = 37,
37 + 24 = **61 pencils**. Replay: 61 − 24 = 37.)

## Exit check

"Some shells were on a beach. 29 more washed up. Now there are 76.
How many shells were there first? Say the equation and the replay."
Expected: □ + 29 = 76, 76 − 29 = **47**, replay 47 + 29 = 76.
Record: secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** cover the missing number with a hand and ask
  "which part of the story is hiding — the beginning, the change, or
  the end?" before writing the equation.
- **Alternate response mode:** draw the story as three boxes (start,
  change, end) and point to the empty box; fill it by running the
  arrows backwards.
- **Floor-play variant:** walk the story forward (start → change →
  end), then walk it backwards to undo.

## Extension

"Write a start-unknown take-from story with numbers within 100, solve
it by undoing, and show both the undo equation and the forward replay
equation." (Example: "Some juice boxes were in a cooler. 33 were
drunk. Now 41 are left. □ − 33 = 41, 41 + 33 = **74** first; replay
74 − 33 = 41.)

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) solving a start-unknown problem with
the story's operation (adding the change to the end) — the replay
check is the cure, and it must be done every time until the habit
holds; (2) treating "some" as zero or as the change — "some" marks
the hidden number, not an amount; (3) confusing change-unknown with
result-unknown when the end is given — name the three boxes first.
