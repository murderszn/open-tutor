# Lesson 05 — Two-Step Stories: Find the Middle Amount

**Student goal:** I can solve a story that needs two calculations by
finding the middle amount first, then using it for the final question.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U04](../README.md) · **Session 9 of 16**

## Materials

- Counters
- Paper and crayons
- Number-line strip 0–100

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Priya had 46 beads, then got 28 more. How many does she have before
anything else happens?" (Expected: 46 + 28 = **74**.) This "before
anything else happens" number is today's hero: the middle amount.

## Teaching

A two-step story has **two changes** (or a combine and a change) and
**one final question**. You cannot answer it with one equation, and
you cannot skip to the end. The method:

1. **Read the whole story first.** Do not compute after the first
   sentence.
2. **Do step 1** — the first change or combination. Write its
   equation. Its answer is the **middle amount**.
3. **Do step 2** — use the middle amount in the second change.
   Write its equation.
4. **Check both steps** against the story: does each step's action
   match what happened, in order?

Two-step stories come in patterns: add then subtract, subtract then
add, add then add, subtract then subtract, combine then take away.
The pattern does not matter — the middle amount does.

**Key vocabulary:** *two-step*, *middle amount* (the answer to step
1, used in step 2), *in order* (stories happen in sequence; so must
the steps).

## Worked example 1 — add, then subtract

"Priya had 46 beads. She got 28 more beads. Then she gave 19 beads to
a friend. How many beads does Priya have now?"

> "Step 1 (got more): 46 + 28 = □. 40 + 20 = 60, 6 + 8 = 14,
> 60 + 14 = **74** — the middle amount. Step 2 (gave away): 74 − 19 =
> □. 74 − 10 = 64, 64 − 9 = **55**. Check in order: 46 grew to 74,
> then shrank to 55. Priya has **55 beads** now. Notice: answering
> only step 1 (74) would be wrong — the story did not stop there."

## Worked example 2 — combine, then take away

"A basket holds 33 red apples and 27 green apples. A family takes 18
apples from the basket. How many apples are left in the basket?"

> "Step 1 (combine the parts): 33 + 27 = □. 30 + 20 = 50, 3 + 7 = 10,
> 50 + 10 = **60** apples to start — the middle amount. Step 2 (take
> away): 60 − 18 = □. 60 − 10 = 50, 50 − 8 = **42**. Check: 42 left +
> 18 taken = 60, the combined whole. **42 apples** are left."

## Worked example 3 — subtract, then add

"A jar held 82 marbles. Sam took 36 marbles out. Then he found 15
marbles and put them in the jar. How many marbles are in the jar now?"

> "Step 1 (took out): 82 − 36 = □. 82 − 30 = 52, 52 − 6 = **46** —
> the middle amount. Step 2 (put in): 46 + 15 = □. 40 + 10 = 50,
> 6 + 5 = 11, 50 + 11 = **61**. Check in order: 82 shrank to 46,
> then grew to 61. **61 marbles** are in the jar now."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the middle amount is never asked for in
the question — that is why it is easy to skip. Underline or circle it
every time. A two-step answer without a circled middle amount is
unfinished work.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds with counters.

1. "Jae had 57 stickers. He got 23 more, then gave 14 away. How many
   now?" (Expected: step 1: 57 + 23 = **80**; step 2: 80 − 14 =
   **66**.)
2. "A class collected 41 cans on Monday, 29 on Tuesday, and 18 on
   Wednesday. How many cans in all?" (Expected: step 1: 41 + 29 =
   **70**; step 2: 70 + 18 = **88**.)
3. "A shop had 90 ribbons. It sold 27 before lunch and 19 after
   lunch. How many ribbons are left?" (Expected: step 1: 90 − 27 =
   **63**; step 2: 63 − 19 = **44**.)
4. Error analysis: the adult shows "46 + 28 − 19: first 28 − 19 = 9,
   then 46 + 9 = 55" for Worked example 1 and asks: "The answer
   matches — is the method right?" (Expected: the answer happens to
   match here, but the method is wrong: it changes the story's order
   and the middle amount (74) never appears. Steps must follow the
   story: 46 + 28 = 74 first, then 74 − 19 = 55. Order matters even
   when a shortcut lands on the same number.)
5. "What do you circle in every two-step problem, and why?"
   (Expected: "The middle amount — step 2 needs it, and the question
   never names it.")

## Independent practice

The learner writes both equations, circling the middle amount, without
hints: "Mia had 38 shells. She found 45 more, then gave 26 to a
friend" (38 + 45 = **83**; 83 − 26 = **57**); "A shelf held 63 books.
Students borrowed 28, then returned 17" (63 − 28 = **35**; 35 + 17 =
**52**); "A garden grew 29 carrots, 36 beans, and 24 peas" (29 + 36 =
**65**; 65 + 24 = **89** vegetables).

*Alternate response mode:* act out each step with counters in two
piles (before-step-2 pile, after-step-2 pile) and say the middle
amount aloud before moving on.

## Application

Sticker story: "Ana had 87 stickers. She gave 25 stickers to one
friend and 18 stickers to another friend. How many stickers does Ana
have left?" (Expected: step 1: 87 − 25 = **62**; step 2: 62 − 18 =
**44 stickers**. Check: 44 + 25 + 18 = 87.)

## Exit check

"Sam had 54 apples. He bought 27 more, then gave 33 away. How many
now? Show both steps." Expected: 54 + 27 = **81**; 81 − 33 = **48**.
Record: secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** retell the story as "first… then… in the
  end…" with a finger on each part before computing.
- **Alternate response mode:** draw a two-arrow chain (start →
  middle → end) and write one equation under each arrow.
- **Floor-play variant:** place three mats on the floor (start,
  middle, end) and move the counters from mat to mat.

## Extension

"Write a two-step story that needs subtract-then-add, with a middle
amount between 40 and 60. Solve it, then prove it by checking the
steps backwards from the end." (Example: "A box held 71 pencils. 24
were handed out, then 12 were returned. 71 − 24 = **47**; 47 + 12 =
**59**. Backwards check: 59 − 12 = 47, 47 + 24 = 71.)

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) stopping after step 1 — circling the
middle amount and asking "does the story end here?" prevents it;
(2) computing the two numbers in the wrong order — retell "first…
then…" before writing; (3) combining all three numbers at once
(46 + 28 + 19) — the story's second action is a subtraction, and
lumping numbers erases it.
