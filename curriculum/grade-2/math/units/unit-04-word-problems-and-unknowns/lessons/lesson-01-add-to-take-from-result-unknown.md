# Lesson 01 — Add-To and Take-From Stories, Result Unknown

**Student goal:** I can act out, draw, and write an equation for an
add-to or take-from story, and find the missing end number.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U04](../README.md) · **Session 1 of 16**

## Materials

- Counters (buttons, beans, or blocks)
- Paper and crayons
- Number-line strip 0–100

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Show me 34 with counters, then add 16 more counters. How many now?"
(Expected: 34 + 16 = 50.) If counting on from 34 is shaky, count the
two piles together by tens and ones first.

## Teaching

A story problem is a little play with numbers. It always has three
parts:

1. **A start** — how many there are at the beginning.
2. **A change** — what happens: something joins (add to) or something
   leaves (take from).
3. **A question** — what we need to find out. Today the question is
   about the **end**: the result.

The four moves, every time:

1. **Act it or draw it.** Move the counters the way the story moves.
2. **Name the unknown.** "The end number is missing. I will write a
   box, □, for it."
3. **Write the equation.** Add to → `start + change = □`. Take from →
   `start − change = □`.
4. **Check the story.** Does the answer make sense? Adding to must end
   bigger than the start; taking from must end smaller.

**Key vocabulary:** *add to* (something joins, the pile grows),
*take from* (something leaves, the pile shrinks), *result unknown*
(the end number is the missing one).

## Worked example 1 — add to: Sam's marbles

"Sam has 34 marbles. He finds 16 more marbles. How many marbles does
Sam have now?"

Act it: put out 34 counters, then push 16 more into the pile. The pile
grew, so this is **add to**. The end is unknown.

> "Start 34, change +16, end □. Equation: 34 + 16 = □. Break apart:
> 30 + 10 = 40 tens-part, 4 + 6 = 10 ones-part, 40 + 10 = **50**.
> Check: 50 is bigger than 34, and Sam gained marbles — the story
> fits. Sam has **50 marbles**."

## Worked example 2 — take from: the baker's muffins

"A baker has 57 muffins. She sells 23 muffins. How many muffins are
left?"

Act it: put out 57 counters, then take 23 away. The pile shrank, so
this is **take from**. The end is unknown.

> "Start 57, change −23, end □. Equation: 57 − 23 = □. Break apart:
> 50 − 20 = 30, 7 − 3 = 4, 30 + 4 = **34**. Check: 34 is smaller than
> 57, and muffins were sold — the story fits. **34 muffins** are left."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the operation comes from what happened
in the story (joined or left), not from a magic word. "More" appears
in add-to stories, but it also appears in compare stories (Lesson 03),
where it means something else — so we always act the story first.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds with counters.

1. "Mia has 41 stickers. She gets 25 more. How many now?" (Expected:
   add to, 41 + 25 = **66**.)
2. "A box holds 68 pencils. 31 pencils are taken out. How many are
   left?" (Expected: take from, 68 − 31 = **37**.)
3. "Leo has 29 rocks. He finds 43 more. How many now?" (Expected:
   add to, 29 + 43 = **72**.)
4. Error analysis: the adult shows "46 + 28 = 64" for "Nina had 46
   beads and got 28 more" and asks: "Is it? Act it." (Expected: wrong
   — 40 + 20 = 60, 6 + 8 = 14, 60 + 14 = **74**. The writer added the
   tens but dropped the ten inside 14.)
5. "How do you know whether to add or subtract, before you compute?"
   (Expected: "Did the pile grow or shrink in the story? Grow → add,
   shrink → subtract.")

## Independent practice

The learner draws and writes, without hints: "Ana has 52 ribbons and
gets 38 more" (52 + 38 = **90**); "A jar holds 83 buttons and 45 are
taken out" (83 − 45 = **38**); "Tom has 27 cards and wins 54 more"
(27 + 54 = **81**). The adult checks that each equation matches the
story's action before checking the arithmetic.

*Alternate response mode:* act out each story with real counters and
say the equation aloud; the adult writes it.

## Application

Library story: "The library shelf holds 63 books. Children borrow 18
books. How many books are left on the shelf?" (Expected: take from,
63 − 18 = **45 books**. Check: 45 is smaller than 63, and books left
the shelf — the story fits.)

## Exit check

"Act out: 47 apples, then 26 more apples arrive. How many apples now?
Say the equation." Expected: add to, 47 + 26 = **73**. Record:
secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** retell every story in the learner's own words
  ("What happened first? What changed?") before any numbers are
  written.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the growing or shrinking pile
  and say "grew" or "shrank" instead of naming the operation.
- **Floor-play variant:** lay the counters in a long line; walk the
  "join" counters in and the "leave" counters out.

## Extension

"Write your own add-to story with numbers that cross a ten (like
48 + 35), solve it two ways (counters, then break-apart), and explain
why both ways give the same answer." (Example answer: 48 + 35 = **83**;
40 + 30 = 70, 8 + 5 = 13, 70 + 13 = 83.)

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) choosing the operation from a keyword
("more = add") instead of the action — always ask "grew or shrank?"
first; (2) adding in a take-from story because both numbers are
visible — act it out; a shrinking pile cannot end bigger than it
started; (3) place-value slips when the ones cross ten (46 + 28) —
break apart by tens and ones, as in Unit 02.
