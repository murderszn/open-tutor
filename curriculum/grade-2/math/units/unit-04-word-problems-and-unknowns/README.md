# Unit 04 — Word Problems and Unknowns

**Grade:** 2 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #14](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/14), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Stories are where addition and subtraction earn their keep. In this
unit the learner stops asking "do I add or subtract?" from a keyword
and starts asking "what happened in the story, what do I know, and what
is missing?" Every story gets the same four moves: **act it or draw
it, name the unknown, write an equation with a box for the unknown, and
check that the answer fits the story.** The unknown is not always at
the end: sometimes the start is missing, sometimes the change, sometimes
a part, sometimes the difference. Two-step stories add one more move —
find the middle amount first, then use it to answer the question.

Week 18 is the track's midyear review week: the unit assessment's Part
B revisits Units 01–03 (place value, comparison, within-100 fluency,
three-digit computation) alongside the new story-problem objectives,
and the teacher guide names what to re-teach before Unit 05.

## Essential question

How can one story be told as a drawing, a bar, and an equation — and
how do you know your answer fits the story?

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–03 (and the diagnostic weeks):

- Add and subtract within 100 with place-value strategies (break apart
  by tens and ones); fluently add and subtract within 20
- Add and subtract three-digit numbers with models (used only in the
  midyear-review items, not in new story problems, which stay within
  100)
- Compare two numbers and record with `>`, `=`, `<`
- Read and write numbers to 1000; explain hundreds, tens, and ones
- Act out, draw, and retell a short story in order (first, then, now)

No new computation is introduced in this unit: the new skill is
matching a story to an equation. If computation within 100 is shaky,
replay the matching Unit 02 lesson before starting Lesson 01.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Solve one-step add-to and take-from stories within 100 with the
   result unknown, using drawings and equations. (2.OA.A.1)
2. Solve one-step put-together and take-apart stories within 100 with
   the whole unknown or a part unknown, using drawings and equations
   with a symbol for the unknown. (2.OA.A.1)
3. Solve one-step compare stories within 100 — difference unknown,
   bigger unknown, and smaller unknown — and explain why "how many
   more" asks for a difference, not a total. (2.OA.A.1)
4. Solve one-step stories within 100 with the unknown in any position
   (start unknown, change unknown, part unknown), including undoing a
   change to find a start. (2.OA.A.1)
5. Solve two-step stories within 100 by finding the middle amount
   first and writing one equation per step. (2.OA.A.1)
6. Check a story answer for reasonableness by estimating (rounding to
   the nearest ten), by the inverse operation, and by asking whether
   the answer fits what happened in the story. (2.OA.A.1, 2.NBT.B.5)

## Vocabulary (taught with stories first, word second)

story problem · unknown (the box, □) · add to · take from ·
put together · take apart · compare · difference ("how many more /
fewer") · bigger unknown · smaller unknown · start unknown ·
change unknown · part unknown · whole · middle amount · two-step ·
estimate · check · inverse operation (addition undoes subtraction) ·
reasonable

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**,
grade 2, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/Math](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/)
(codes and descriptions cross-checked against the official grade-2 domain
pages during the A00 audit, 2026-10-02; no state adoption or accreditation
claimed).

| Unit objective | Standard | Official description (grade 2) |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | 2.OA.A.1 | Use addition and subtraction within 100 to solve one- and two-step word problems involving situations of adding to, taking from, putting together, taking apart, and comparing, with unknowns in all positions, e.g., by using drawings and equations with a symbol for the unknown number to represent the problem. |
| 1–6 | 2.OA.B.2 | Fluently add and subtract within 20 using mental strategies. By end of Grade 2, know from memory all sums of two one-digit numbers. |
| 6 | 2.NBT.B.5 | Fluently add and subtract within 100 using strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction. |

Objective 6's estimation habit (round to the nearest ten, then compare)
is a checking strategy applied to 2.OA.A.1 problems; it is not a
separate standard claim. The situation names used here (add to,
take from, put together, take apart, compare) are the five situations
named in 2.OA.A.1 itself; the start/change/result and
bigger/smaller/difference unknown positions are the standard's
"unknowns in all positions," spelled out for teaching.

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials
list): counters (buttons, beans, blocks), ten frames, a number-line
strip 0–100, number cards, paper and crayons, the generated illustration
[`assets/market-stall-story-picture.png`](assets/market-stall-story-picture.png),

a "story book" folder for the project. No sharp tools; supervise small
parts; check food-allergen safety before using food as counters.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, or drawing responses; the adult scribes
when the learner isn't ready to write. Stories are read aloud by the
adult on first hearing, then re-read by the learner where ready. Bar
drawings use length, never color alone, to show which amount is bigger.
Each lesson includes a seated-table and a floor-play variant, a
language-access support, an alternate response mode, and an extension.
The generated illustration ships with a text-only alternative describing
the scene, and the picture activity asks the learner to invent their own
numbers — no counting claim is made from the picture. No keyword tricks
are taught: "more" can mean add, compare, or neither, depending on the
story, and the lessons say so explicitly.
