# Project — The Hundred-Block Supply Shop

**Sessions:** 11 (Part 1) and 15 (Part 2), 25–30 minutes each ·
**Unit:** [U01](README.md)

The learner runs a supply shop that sells base-ten block "builder kits."
Part 1 stocks the shelves with labeled kits; Part 2 fills customer orders
and compares order sizes to award the Biggest Build badge.

## Materials

- Hundred-flats, ten-rods, one-cubes
- 6 blank "inventory cards" (paper) and 4 blank "order slips"
- Crayons; the [answer key](answer-key.md#project) for the adult

## Part 1 — Stock the shelves (Session 11)

1. The adult assigns 6 kit numbers (use: 245, 306, 518, 630, 807, 452 —
   each exercises a different place pattern, including two zero-place
   numbers).
2. For each kit, the learner:
   - builds it with blocks on the shelf (table),
   - draws the build on the inventory card,
   - labels the card with all three forms: numeral, number name, expanded
     form.
3. The learner arranges the 6 kits on the shelf in order, least → greatest,
   and reads the chain aloud with `<` between them.

*Check:* 245 < 306 < 452 < 518 < 630 < 807. Verify: hundreds 2,3,4,5,6,8 —
strictly increasing, so the order is by hundreds alone.

## Part 2 — Fill the orders (Session 15)

1. The adult writes 4 order slips in "parts language":
   - Order A: 3 hundreds, 1 ten, 9 ones
   - Order B: 2 hundreds, 9 tens, 9 ones
   - Order C: 4 hundreds, 0 tens, 6 ones
   - Order D: 3 hundreds, 4 tens, 2 ones
2. The learner builds each order, writes its numeral on the slip (A: 319;
   B: 299; C: 406; D: 342), and checks the build against the slip.
3. **Biggest Build badge:** the learner compares the four order totals,
   hundreds first, and awards the badge to the greatest. (406 > 342 > 319 >
   299 — hundreds 4 > 3 = 3 > 2; then A vs D by tens 1 < 4.)
4. The learner explains the badge choice in one hundreds-first sentence,
   recorded by the adult.

## Deliverables

1. 6 inventory cards, each with a drawn build + numeral + number name +
   expanded form.
2. Shelf order chain (least → greatest) read aloud.
3. 4 completed order slips (build verified, numeral written).
4. Badge decision with a hundreds-first comparison sentence.

## Rubric

| Criterion | 3 — Secure | 2 — Developing | 1 — Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Build accuracy** (Obj. 1) | All 10 builds match their numbers; zero places built as empty columns | 7–9 builds correct; zero places sometimes skipped | Fewer than 7 correct; place confusion |
| **Three-form labels** (Obj. 2) | All 6 cards show numeral, name, and expanded form correctly | 4–5 cards complete and correct | 3 or fewer complete |
| **Comparison reasoning** (Obj. 4) | Badge choice justified hundreds-first, then tens; chain ordered correctly | Correct order but reasoning starts at ones, or one link wrong | Order wrong; no place-based reasoning |
| **Communication** | Place vocabulary (hundreds, tens, ones, expanded form) used correctly throughout | Vocabulary mostly correct | Vocabulary missing or misused |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = secure on the unit's objectives; 7–9 = developing —
re-teach the missed criterion in the track's flexible weeks; 6 or below =
not yet — re-teach before Unit 02.

## Accessibility and safety

Oral, pointing, and drawing responses accepted throughout; the adult scribes
labels the learner dictates. Blocks distinguished by size/shape, never color
alone. Small parts supervised. No hazards; all work is tabletop or floor
play.
