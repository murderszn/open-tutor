# Lesson 02 — Three-Digit Numbers: Hundreds, Tens, Ones

**Student goal:** I can build any three-digit number with flats, rods, and
ones, and I can tell what each digit means.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U01](../README.md) · **Session 5 of 16**

## Materials

- Hundred-flats, ten-rods, one-cubes (built in Lesson 01)
- A hundreds/tens/ones place-value mat (three labeled columns) — draw or print
- Number cards 100–999

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Build 40 with rods. Now build 400 with flats. How are they alike?"
(Expected: both use 4 of something; 4 tens vs. 4 hundreds.) If flat-building
is shaky, re-do Lesson 01's exit check first.

## Teaching

Every three-digit number is a recipe with three ingredients:

1. **Hundreds digit** → how many flats.
2. **Tens digit** → how many rods.
3. **Ones digit** → how many cubes.

Read the digits left to right: hundreds, tens, ones. The places never change
order — the **place** tells the **value**.

The tricky part is **zero**. In 405, the 0 means "zero tens" — no rods at
all. Zero is a placeholder: it holds the tens place so the 4 still means
4 hundreds and the 5 still means 5 ones. Never skip a zero place when
building.

**Key vocabulary:** *digit*, *place*, *place value*, *placeholder*.

## Worked example 1 — 234: two hundreds, three tens, four ones

The adult builds on the mat while narrating:

> "2 flats in the hundreds column — that's 200. 3 rods in the tens column —
> that's 30. 4 cubes in the ones column — that's 4. Two hundreds, three tens,
> four ones: **234**."

Then the adult covers the blocks and points to the numeral 234: "The 2 means
2 hundreds, the 3 means 3 tens, the 4 means 4 ones. The digit's **place**
tells its value."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** we never counted 234 singles. The places did
the counting for us — that's the whole power of place value.

## Worked example 2 — 405: the zero placeholder

The adult builds 4 flats, then pauses at the tens column:

> "4 hundreds — 400. Now the tens digit is 0: **zero rods**. We leave the
> tens column empty on purpose. Then 5 ones. Four hundreds, zero tens, five
> ones: **405**."

The adult shows what goes wrong without the placeholder: "If I forgot the
zero and wrote 45, that's forty-five — a totally different number. The zero
holds the tens place so the 4 keeps meaning 4 hundreds."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** zero is doing a job here — holding a place —
not "meaning nothing." This is the most common error to watch for this week.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds. Always: build first, then name each place.

1. Build 351: "How many of each?" (Expected: 3 flats, 5 rods, 1 cube —
   "3 hundreds, 5 tens, 1 one.")
2. Build 208: (Expected: 2 flats, 0 rods, 8 cubes — "zero tens" said aloud.)
3. The adult builds 3 flats + 7 cubes (no rods): "What number? Write it."
   (Expected: 307 — the zero must be written.)
4. Error analysis: the adult builds 4 flats + 6 cubes (no rods) but writes
   "460." Ask: "Right or wrong? Fix it." (Expected: wrong — 6 cubes are
   ones, not tens; the build is 406. The 6 belongs in the ones place, and
   the zero holds the tens place.)
5. Draw-a-number: the learner draws 512 as quick flats/rods/cubes on paper
   (rectangles, lines, dots allowed).
6. Reverse: "Show me 4 hundreds, 1 ten, 9 ones." (Expected: builds 419.)

## Independent practice

The learner draws a number card (from 120–980, adult-chosen) and builds it
on the mat, then writes one sentence per digit: "The 6 in 463 means 6 tens."
Do 4 cards. The adult checks that zero-place builds include the zero in the
written numeral.

*Alternate response mode:* build with blocks and say each place aloud; the
adult writes the sentences.

## Application

Recipe cards: the learner makes 4 "recipe cards" (index cards or paper):
front shows the numeral, back shows the build drawn as flats/rods/cubes with
the place labels. These become the deck for Session 7's matching game.

## Exit check

"Build 126 and tell me what each digit means." Expected: 1 flat, 2 rods,
6 cubes; "1 hundred, 2 tens, 6 ones." Record: secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** sentence frame posted: "The ___ in ___ means ___
  ___s." Point to the column while saying it.
- **Alternate response mode:** sort pre-built block groups into the right
  mat columns instead of building from scratch.
- **Floor-play variant:** three hula-hoops or tape squares labeled Hundreds /
  Tens / Ones on the floor; the learner carries blocks to the right hoop.

## Extension

"Build 999. Now add one more cube — what happens?" (10 ones make a new ten;
10 tens make a new hundred — 1000, ten hundreds. Name it; the full
thousand-place story belongs to Unit 02.) Or: "Write the largest and
smallest three-digit numbers and build both." (999; 100.)

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) dropping zero placeholders (406 → 46) — build
both and compare magnitudes; (2) reading digits right-to-left; (3) thinking
the leftmost digit is "the biggest digit" rather than the hundreds counter —
always name the place with the digit.
