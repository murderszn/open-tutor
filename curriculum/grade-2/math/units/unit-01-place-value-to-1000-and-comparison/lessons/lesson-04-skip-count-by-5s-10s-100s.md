# Lesson 04 — Skip-Count by 5s, 10s, and 100s

**Student goal:** I can skip-count by 5s, 10s, and 100s within 1000, starting
from any number.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U01](../README.md) · **Session 10 of 16**

## Materials

- 0–999 thousand chart (print or draw; hundreds rows shaded alternately)
- Ten-rods and hundred-flats for modeling jumps
- Crayons for chart paths

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Count by tens from 30 to 100." (Expected: 30, 40, …, 100.) If tens
skip-counting is shaky, march it with rods first — this lesson extends that
skill upward and to 5s and 100s.

## Teaching

Skip-counting is counting by jumps. The jump size never changes mid-chain.

1. **By 100s:** each jump adds one flat: 100, 200, 300, …, 900. (Landing on
   1000 — ten hundreds — is the chain's end; name it, don't drill it.)
2. **By 10s:** each jump adds one rod. From a multiple of 10 it's easy
   (370, 380, 390, …); from a non-multiple, the ones digit rides along
   unchanged (47, 57, 67, … — the 7 never moves).
3. **By 5s:** each jump adds five ones. Ones digits alternate 5, 0, 5, 0, …
   (35, 40, 45, 50, …). This is the fives pattern from telling time, reused.

The thousand chart is the map: by-100 jumps go straight down a column,
by-10 jumps go down one row, by-5 jumps zigzag. If a chain ever feels lost,
the blocks or the chart re-anchor it.

**Key vocabulary:** *skip-count*, *jump*.

## Worked example 1 — by 100s: 100 to 1000

The adult stacks flats one at a time while the learner chants:

> "100 (one flat), 200 (two flats), 300, 400, 500, 600, 700, 800, 900 —
> nine flats. One more flat makes ten hundreds: **1000**."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** every landing is a hundreds count — the
hundreds digit climbs 1, 2, 3 while tens and ones stay zero. Skip-counting by
100s is just counting flats.

## Worked example 2 — by 10s from 45: the riding ones digit

The adult starts at 45 on the chart (4 rods + 5 cubes built beside it):

> "45… add one rod: 55. Add one rod: 65. 75, 85, 95 — watch the ones: 5, 5,
> 5, 5, 5. The ones digit **never changes** when we jump by tens. Only the
> tens digit climbs: 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9."

Then by 5s from 35: "35, 40, 45, 50 — ones go 5, 0, 5, 0. Fives always land
on 5 or 0."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** naming what stays the same is as important as
naming what changes — it turns skip-counting from memorization into place
value in motion.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds; chart or blocks always available.

1. Chain by 100s from 100 — learner continues to 1000 alone. (Expected:
   100–1000 correct.)
2. By 10s from 370: "370, 380, …" to 430. (Expected: 370, 380, 390, 400,
   410, 420, 430 — note the hundreds rollover 390 → 400.)
3. By 5s from 55 to 85. (Expected: 55, 60, 65, 70, 75, 80, 85.)
4. Error analysis: the adult skip-counts "300, 400, 500, 700, 800." Ask:
   "What went wrong?" (Expected: skipped 600 — jumps must stay the same
   size; the missing landing is 600.)
5. Chart path: "Start at 250, jump by 10s three times. Where do you land?"
   (Expected: 280.)
6. Backwards teaser (oral only): by 10s down from 100: 100, 90, …, 10.
   (No written work; just the pattern.)

## Independent practice

The learner traces 3 chart paths in different colors: by 100s from 100 to
900, by 10s from 115 to 175 (115, 125, 135, 145, 155, 165, 175), by 5s from
5 to 50. Then writes the "rule" under each in their own words ("the ones
digit stays 5"). The adult checks landings against the chart.

*Alternate response mode:* chant the chains while hopping or clapping each
landing; the adult records.

## Application

Skip-count store: items cost 100, 10, or 5 "math dollars." The learner is
the cashier: "3 hundred-dollar toys — that's 100, 200, 300." Then mixed:
"a 100 toy and four 10 toys: 100, 110, 120, 130, 140." Real skip-counting,
real totals.

## Exit check

"Skip-count by 100s from 100 to 1000." Expected: 100, 200, …, 1000 with no
skips. Record: secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** chant with a steady beat; pair each landing with its
  blocks for the first rounds.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to each chart landing in rhythm while
  the adult says the numbers.
- **Floor-play variant:** tape a giant hundred-chart row on the floor; the
  learner jumps the landings.

## Extension

"Start at 990 and skip-count by 10s — what happens?" (990, 1000 — the
chain crosses into thousands; discuss what "ten hundreds" means.) Or: "How
many jumps of 100 from 0 to reach 700?" (7 jumps.)

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) changing jump size mid-chain (300, 400, 500,
700) — enforce "same jump every time"; (2) the hundreds rollover (390 → 400,
not "three-ninety-ten") — model with rods becoming a flat; (3) by-5s landing
on non-5/0 digits — the 5/0 alternation is the self-check.
