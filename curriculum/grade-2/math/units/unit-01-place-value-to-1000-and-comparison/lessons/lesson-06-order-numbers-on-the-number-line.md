# Lesson 06 — Order Numbers on the Number Line

**Student goal:** I can put three-digit numbers in order from least to
greatest (and back), and I can show where a number lives on a number line.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Unit:** [U01](../README.md) · **Session 14 of 16**

## Materials

- Number cards 100–999 (at least 9)
- A number-line strip marked 0–1000 in hundreds, with ten subdivisions
  between each hundred (draw or print)
- Symbol cards `>`, `<`, `=`

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Compare 381 and 318." (Expected: hundreds tie 3 = 3; tens 8 > 1; 381 >
318.) If hundreds-then-tens ordering is shaky, re-do Lesson 05's exit check
first.

## Teaching

Ordering is comparing, repeated. For least → greatest:

1. Line the numbers up and compare the **hundreds** — smallest hundreds go
   first.
2. For numbers with equal hundreds, compare their **tens**.
3. For numbers with equal hundreds AND tens, compare their **ones**.
4. Check the chain: each number should be less than the next. Read the whole
   chain with `<` between them.

The number line is the same idea drawn out: numbers grow left to right.
Between two hundred-marks, each small subdivision is one ten. A number like
350 sits halfway between 300 and 400 — because 50 is half of 100.

**Key vocabulary:** *order*, *least to greatest*, *greatest to least*,
*number line*.

## Worked example 1 — order 234, 241, 223 least to greatest

The adult writes the three numbers and thinks aloud:

> "Hundreds first: 2, 2, 2 — all tie. Tens: 3, 4, 2. Smallest tens is 2, so
> **223** goes first. Next tens is 3: **234**. Last: **241**."
> "Check the chain: 223 < 234 < 241. Each step: 223 < 234 (tens 2 < 3),
> 234 < 241 (tens 3 < 4). The chain holds."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** when one place ties for everybody, it drops out
of the decision and the next place votes. Ordering never needs the ones
unless hundreds and tens both tie.

## Worked example 2 — place 350 on the number line

The adult points to the strip marked 300 … 400:

> "350 is between 300 and 400 — 3 hundreds for sure. The 5 tens are half of
> the ten tens that make the next hundred, so 350 sits **halfway** between
> 300 and 400."
> "And 370? Seven tens — past halfway, closer to 400. 320? Two tens — just
> past 300."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the number line turns place value into
distance — tens are steps between the hundred landmarks. This is the picture
Unit 06 will reuse for measurement.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds.

1. Order 345, 354, 335 least → greatest. (Expected: 335, 345, 354 —
   hundreds all tie at 3; tens 3 < 4 < 5.)
2. Order the same triple greatest → least. (Expected: 354, 345, 335.)
3. "Place 640 on the number line." (Expected: between 600 and 700, just
   below the midpoint — 4 tens.)
4. Error analysis: the adult orders 305, 350, 335 as "305, 335, 350" but
   says "I compared the ones first." Ask: "Is the order right? Is the
   method right?" (Expected: the order happens to be right — 305 < 335 <
   350 — but the method is unreliable; ones-first fails whenever hundreds
   or tens differ. Redo it hundreds-first to prove it.)
5. "Which is greater: 609 or 690? Place both on the number line."
   (Expected: 690 > 609; 690 sits near 700, 609 just past 600.)
6. Chain check: "Is 199 < 201 < 210 true?" (Expected: yes — 199 < 201 by
   hundreds 1 < 2; 201 < 210 by tens 0 < 1.)

## Independent practice

The learner draws 3 new number cards and orders them least → greatest with
the `<` chain written out, then places the middle number on the number-line
strip and explains the placement ("470 is between 400 and 500, seven tens
past 400"). Do this twice (6 cards). The adult checks each chain link.

*Alternate response mode:* arrange the cards physically left-to-right and
say the chain aloud; the adult writes it.

## Application

Line-up parade: the learner deals 5 number cards face-up and arranges them
in a parade from least to greatest, then marches along reading the chain.
A family member (or the adult) tries to sneak one card out of order — the
learner catches the break in the chain and fixes it.

## Exit check

"Order 615, 651, 605 greatest → least." Expected: 651, 615, 605 (hundreds
all 6; tens 5 > 1 > 0). Record: secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** chain-reading frame: "___ is less than ___, which is
  less than ___."
- **Alternate response mode:** sort the cards by hundreds piles first, then
  order within each pile.
- **Floor-play variant:** giant floor number line; the learner walks to each
  number's home.

## Extension

"Order 1000, 999, 1001 — wait, 1001 isn't in our unit! What do you notice?"
(1001 > 1000 > 999 — the pattern holds past our boundary; thousands get
their own place next unit.) Or: "Find a number between 450 and 460."
(Any 451–459 — infinitely many… well, nine.)

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) sorting by ones when hundreds tie only
partially — enforce the full hundreds→tens→ones cascade; (2) placing numbers
like 640 at the wrong landmark (near 600's left edge) — count the tens
subdivisions; (3) a "right order, wrong method" pass — always ask for the
hundreds-first justification, not just the order.
