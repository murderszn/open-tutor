# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Place Value to 1000 and Number Comparison

**Unit:** [U01](README.md) · **Answer key:** [answer-key.md](answer-key.md)
(adult only — never beside student materials)

## How this unit runs

Sixteen 25–30-minute sessions across track weeks 3–6. Every session opens
with a 2-minute number-talk warm-up (count by tens, name a block build, or
read a numeral aloud) and closes with the lesson's exit check, recorded as
**secure / developing / not yet** per objective. Keep sessions playful and
hands-on: at grade 2, blocks first, drawing second, symbols last.

**Session map:** 1 L1 → 2 practice → 3 game → 4 review → 5 L2 → 6 practice
→ 7 game → 8 L3 → 9 practice → 10 L4 → 11 project P1 → 12 re-teach → 13 L5 →
14 L6 → 15 project P2 + quiz → 16 assessment.

## Objective-by-objective guidance

### Objective 1 — hundreds as ten tens; build three-digit numbers (L1–L2)

The unit's load-bearing idea. Don't rush Lesson 01: the learner must
physically bundle 10 rods into a flat and say "ten tens make one hundred"
before any three-digit work. If the flat feels like "just a big block,"
rebuild it from rods in front of them. Zero placeholders (405, 307) deserve
a full guided round — build the empty tens column visibly.

### Objective 2 — numerals, names, expanded form (L3)

Teach the three forms as "three costumes, one number." The expanded→numeral
direction is usually harder; drill it both ways. Number names: model without
"and" ("six hundred forty-seven"); accept "and" orally without correcting
mid-flow. Teen names (thirteen–nineteen) need separate repetition — they
don't follow the tens pattern.

### Objective 3 — skip-counting (L4)

Anchor every chain in blocks or the thousand chart; never drill chains as
pure recitation. Name what stays the same (the riding ones digit in by-10s;
the 5/0 alternation in by-5s) — that's the place-value insight. The 390 →
400 rollover is the week's hard moment: model rods becoming a flat.

### Objective 4 — comparing (L5)

Chant "hundreds first" until it's automatic. The zero-tens comparison
(405 vs. 450) is the key diagnostic: a learner who compares 5 ones vs. 0
ones first hasn't internalized place order. The generated illustration's
worked example (234 vs. 241) is the model to imitate.

### Objective 5 — ordering and the number line (L6)

Ordering is just repeated comparing — insist on the hundreds-first
justification even when the order "looks obvious." On the number line,
count tens subdivisions explicitly; don't let placements be guesses.

## Misconception watch

| Signal | Likely cause | Reteaching move |
|---|---|---|
| Flat called "ten" | Rods-to-flat bundling not internalized | Rebuild a flat from 10 rods; count the rods inside (10 × 10 = 100) |
| 406 written as 46 | Zero placeholder dropped | Build both 406 and 46; compare magnitudes (400s vs. 40s) |
| 318 > 321 | Ones compared first | Rebuild; chant "hundreds first"; cover the ones column and re-decide |
| 405 > 450 | Zero tens skipped | Build both; point at the empty tens column: "zero tens is a real vote" |
| 390 → "three-ninety-ten" | Rollover not modeled | 9 rods + 1 rod = 10 rods = 1 flat; say "four hundred" while placing it |
| Skip-chain changes jump size | "Counting" habit | Enforce "same jump every time"; re-anchor on the chart |

## Re-teaching (Session 12 and flexible weeks)

Pick the highest-need objective from exit checks; replay the matching
[practice session](practice.md) variant, then give 4 fresh prompts — 3 of 4
correct to move on. If two or more objectives are "not yet" after Session
16, use the track's flexible weeks (scope-and-sequence §5) before Unit 02;
place value is prerequisite for everything that follows.

## Readiness criteria

**Ready for Unit 02** when the learner is secure or developing on all five
objectives: exit checks secure on at least 4 of 5 objectives, formative
quiz ≥ 5/6, unit assessment ≥ 14/16. A "developing" on skip-counting alone
may ride along (Unit 02's warm-ups revisit it); a "not yet" on hundreds
building or comparing may not — re-teach first.

## Notes on materials

- Flats can be paper 10×10 grids the learner tapes together from 10 rod
  strips — assembling the flat *is* the lesson.
- The generated illustration's large squares stand for hundreds by the
  agreement established in Lesson 01 (the learner built a real flat first);
  the picture does not prove each square holds 100 cells. See
  [assets/README.md](assets/README.md).
- Keep the hundreds/tens/ones mat, thousand chart, and number-line strip —
  the track reuses them in Units 02–06.
