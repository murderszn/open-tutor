# Unit 01 — Place Value to 1000 and Number Comparison

**Grade:** 2 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #14](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/14), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Big numbers are easier when we bundle in hundreds. In this unit the learner
discovers that ten tens make one hundred, builds three-digit numbers with
hundred-flats, ten-rods, and one-cubes, reads and writes numbers to 1000 as
numerals, number names, and expanded form, skip-counts by 5s, 10s, and 100s,
and compares and orders three-digit numbers place by place — hundreds first,
then tens, then ones.

## Essential question

How does grouping in hundreds help us read, write, and compare big numbers?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the grade-1 track:

- Counting to 120 from any starting number; reading and writing numerals to 120
- Two-digit place value: tens and ones; comparing two-digit numbers with
  `>`, `=`, `<` (tens first, then ones)
- Mentally finding 10 more / 10 less than a two-digit number

No new addition/subtraction instruction is required before this unit. The
diagnostic re-teaches any insecure grade-1 skill in this unit's first two
sessions.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Build three-digit numbers with hundred-flats, ten-rods, and one-cubes;
   explain that 10 tens make 1 hundred and that 100, 200, …, 900 are one
   through nine hundreds. (2.NBT.A.1)
2. Read and write numbers to 1000 as base-ten numerals, number names, and
   expanded form; count within 1000. (2.NBT.A.2, 2.NBT.A.3)
3. Skip-count by 5s, 10s, and 100s within 1000. (2.NBT.A.2)
4. Compare two three-digit numbers by hundreds, then tens, then ones, and
   record the comparison with `>`, `=`, or `<`. (2.NBT.A.4)
5. Order three three-digit numbers from least to greatest (and back), and
   place a three-digit number on a labeled number line. (2.NBT.A.4)

## Vocabulary (taught with objects first, word second)

hundred · hundred-flat · ten-rod · one-cube · digit · place · place value ·
expanded form · number name · numeral · skip-count · compare · greater than /
less than (`>`, `<`) · equal to (`=`) · order (least to greatest) · number line

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials
list): base-ten blocks (or 10 ten-rods of banded straws plus paper flats the
learner assembles from 10 rods in Lesson 01), one-cubes or counters, number
cards 100–999, a 0–999 thousand chart (print or draw), a number-line strip
marked in hundreds with ten subdivisions, the generated illustration
[`assets/compare-234-vs-241.png`](assets/compare-234-vs-241.png), paper and
crayons, play "order slips" for the project. No sharp tools; supervise small
parts; check food-allergen safety before using food as counters.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, or drawing responses; the adult scribes
when the learner isn't ready to write. Flats, rods, and cubes are
distinguishable by size and shape, never by color alone. Each lesson includes
a seated-table and a floor-play variant, a language-access support, an
alternate response mode, and an extension. The generated illustration ships
with a text-only alternative containing every count the activities need.
