# Grade 2 Mathematics

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are delivered;
the eight units are planned, not yet written. See [scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained for
one grade-2 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 2 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-2 math course organized around place value to
1000 and three-digit comparison, fluent addition and subtraction within 100,
three-digit computation with composing and decomposing, one- and two-step word
problems of every situation type, equal groups and arrays as repeated addition,
length measurement in standard units with estimation, time to five minutes,
money, data displays and line plots, and shape attributes with partitions into
halves, thirds, and fourths. Sessions are 25–30 minutes, about four per week,
blending hands-on models (base-ten blocks, number lines, rulers, clocks, play
money) with short independent written practice (6–8 tasks) reviewed by the
adult the same day.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Explain three-digit numbers as hundreds, tens, and ones (100 as a bundle of
   ten tens; 100–900 as groups of one hundred); count within 1000; skip-count by
   5s, 10s, and 100s; read and write numbers to 1000 as numerals, number names,
   and expanded form.
2. Compare two three-digit numbers using the meanings of the hundreds, tens,
   and ones digits, recording with `>`, `=`, `<`.
3. Fluently add and subtract within 20 using mental strategies; know from memory
   all sums of two one-digit numbers.
4. Fluently add and subtract within 100 using place-value strategies, properties
   of operations, and addition–subtraction relationships; add up to four
   two-digit numbers; mentally add or subtract 10; explain why a strategy works
   using place value (with drawings or objects as support).
5. Add and subtract within 1000 using concrete models or drawings and
   place-value strategies, relating the strategy to a written method; compose
   and decompose tens and hundreds as needed; mentally add or subtract 100.
6. Solve one- and two-step word problems within 100 of every situation type
   (add to, take from, put together, take apart, compare), with unknowns in all
   positions, using drawings and equations with a symbol for the unknown.
7. Determine whether a group of up to 20 objects is odd or even by pairing or
   skip-counting by 2s, writing an even number as a sum of two equal addends;
   find the total of a rectangular array (up to 5 rows × 5 columns) by repeated
   addition, writing the total as a sum of equal addends.
8. Measure lengths with appropriate standard tools (rulers, yardsticks, meter
   sticks, measuring tapes); estimate lengths in inches, feet, centimeters, and
   meters; measure the same object with two different unit sizes and describe
   how the counts relate to unit size; find how much longer one object is than
   another; solve length word problems within 100; show whole numbers and
   sums/differences within 100 on a number line.
9. Tell and write time to the nearest five minutes on analog and digital clocks,
   using a.m. and p.m.; solve word problems with dollar bills, quarters, dimes,
   nickels, and pennies, using `$` and `¢` correctly.
10. Generate measurement data (nearest whole unit) and show it on a line plot;
    draw picture graphs and bar graphs (single-unit scale) for data with up to
    four categories; solve put-together, take-apart, and compare questions from
    a bar graph.
11. Recognize and draw shapes with specified attributes (triangles,
    quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons, cubes); partition a rectangle into rows
    and columns of same-size squares and count them; partition circles and
    rectangles into halves, thirds, and fourths, name the shares, and describe
    the whole as two halves, three thirds, or four fourths.

## For the guiding adult

Grade-2 math turns place value into a working tool: every new computation first
lives in base-ten blocks, hundred charts, and number lines before it appears as
a written method. Read the scope-and-sequence before starting; it lists safe
household materials, accessibility supports, and the diagnostic, midyear, and
review weeks that keep pacing humane. Fluency is strategy-based — no timed speed
tests. Each future unit will include its own teacher guide and answer key.
