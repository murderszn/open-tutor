# Starter Lesson — Draw a map of a familiar room

**Status:** draft supplement. **Time:** three 15–20 minute sessions. **Goal:** Use position words, draw simple map symbols, and explain what one map can and cannot show.

## Adult preparation and readiness

Use paper and crayons. Pick a room or fictional playroom; do not record an address or publish a home map. Readiness: ask the learner what is beside a chair. Accept pointing. Adult can describe the activity for a learner who cannot move around the room.

## Teach and model

Explain that a map is a drawing/model of a place from above. Model a simple room: a large rectangle, a square for a table, a circle for a chair. Add a key that explains each symbol. Words such as beside, behind, and near describe relative positions.

**Worked example 1:** A circle symbol in the map key means “chair”; the circle at the left side represents where the chair is located.

**Worked example 2:** From the door to the table, say “door, then rug, then table.” The sequence gives a route using landmarks rather than a picture of every detail.

## Guided and independent tasks

1. Point to an object beside another object; say or select “beside.”
2. Draw a large outline for the chosen room/place.
3. Add two symbols and make a matching key.
4. Tell a two-step route from one symbol to another.
5. Error check: show a key where a star means “chair,” then label a star as a table. Ask what needs fixing.
6. Explain one detail the map leaves out (such as sound or exact size).

**Answer checks:** Symbol and key must agree; route names landmarks in a usable order; map omissions vary but must be a detail not represented.

## Exit check and product

Ask learner to show one symbol and explain it. Product: titled room/place map, two-symbol key, and an oral/scribed route.

## Review rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Position | Uses a position word accurately | Uses with gesture/prompt | Revisit objects physically or verbally |
| Map model | Symbols match the key | One mismatch with prompting | Adult models one symbol |
| Route | Names landmarks in order | Names start/end only | Use a toy/finger route |

Use a toy on a paper map, dictate instead of drawing, or make a tactile map with large safe shapes. Map a fictional room if privacy or mobility makes home mapping unsuitable. Never share a home address, routine, or map publicly.
