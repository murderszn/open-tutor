# Lesson 01 — Needs and Wants

**Student goal:** I can tell a need from a want, and I can sort pictures
into needs and wants.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U05](../README.md) · **Session 1 of 16**

## Materials

- 8 picture cards: apple, bread, water bottle, warm coat, bed, ball,
  teddy bear, sticker
- Two sorting mats: NEED (picture of a house with a heart) and WANT
  (picture of a star)
- A small bell or clapping signal for the vote

**Privacy note for the adult:** this lesson never asks the learner to say or
write their name, address, school name, family details, or anything about
what their family buys or can afford. All examples are pretend. Restate
the Unit 01 safety rule at the start: "Names, addresses, and photos stay
private."

## Readiness check (1 minute)

"Point to something in this room that helps you stay healthy and safe."
(A chair, a water bottle, a coat — accept anything, then bridge.) "Some
things we must have. Some things are nice to have. Today we learn the
words for both." If the learner is ready to listen and point, begin.

**Opening retrieval — the Unit 02 fair-turn vote (2 minutes):** our
classroom shop needs a name. Offer two choices: "Sunny Shop" or "Rainbow
Shop." Each person gets one turn, one vote, and the adult counts aloud.
Announce the winner and use that name all unit. (This is D4.8.K-2 in
action: fair turns, one vote each, and we accept the group's choice
even if it was not our own.)

## Teaching

A **need** is something we must have to stay healthy and safe. A
**want** is something that is nice to have, but we can stay healthy and
safe without it. Wants are not bad! We all have wants. We just choose
needs first, and wants when we can.

Four needs to know by heart:

- **Food** — apples, bread, and other food keep our bodies strong.
- **Water** — water to drink keeps our bodies working.
- **Shelter** — a home, a house, a safe place to sleep. (Point to the
  bed card: we sleep in a bed inside our home.)
- **Clothing** — clothes that keep us warm and safe, like a warm coat
  on a cold day.

Three wants in this lesson: a ball (fun to play with), a teddy bear
(nice to hug), a sticker (pretty to look at). We can be healthy and safe
without them — and they are still wonderful to have.

The sorting question to ask every time: **"If we did not have this,
would our body still be healthy and safe?"** If the answer is no, it is
a need. If the answer is yes, it is a want.

(Gesture for each word: hug yourself and shiver for "need — I must stay
warm and safe"; reach out happily and then shrug for "want — nice to
have, but I am still fine without it.")

## Worked example 1 — the apple, step by step (modeled)

Hold up the apple card.

> "What is this? An apple. Food. Ask our question: if we did not have
> this apple, would our body still be healthy and safe? We need food to
> stay strong, so food is a need. Apple goes on the NEED mat. Apple,
> food, need — three pieces, every time."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** we did not just guess. We asked the
question, and the question told us the answer.

## Worked example 2 — the teddy bear, and a tricky case (modeled)

Hold up the teddy bear card, then the warm coat card.

> "What is this? A teddy bear. Ask our question: if we did not have
> this teddy bear, would our body still be healthy and safe? Yes — we
> would miss the hug, but our body would still be healthy and safe. So
> a teddy bear is a want. It goes on the WANT mat."
>
> "Now the coat. If we did not have a warm coat on a cold day, would
> our body still be healthy and safe? No — we would be cold, and cold
> can make us sick. So a warm coat is a need. Coat goes on the NEED
> mat. The teddy bear is for hugs; the coat is for warmth. That is how
> we tell them apart."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** a thing can be loved and still be a want.
Love does not make it a need; keeping the body healthy and safe does.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds. Offer choices; never demand a spoken answer.

1. Hold up the bread card. "Need or want? Ask our question." *(Need —
   food keeps our bodies strong.)*
2. Hold up the ball card. "Need or want?" *(Want — fun to play with,
   but our body stays healthy and safe without it.)*
3. Hold up the water bottle card. "Need or want?" *(Need — water to
   drink keeps our bodies working.)*
4. Error-analysis round: the adult announces, very seriously: "A
   sticker is a need, because I really, really want one!" Ask: "Is that
   right? What does our question say?" *(Expected: no — wanting
   something a lot does not make it a need. Without a sticker our body
   is still healthy and safe, so a sticker is a want.)*
5. Hold up the bed card. "Need or want? What need is it?" *(Need —
   shelter, a safe place to sleep.)*
6. If the learner is verbal: "Finish the sentence: a need is something
   we ___." *(Must have to stay healthy and safe.)*

## Independent practice

The learner sorts all 8 cards onto the two mats without help, then
points to one card on each mat and tells the adult why it belongs there.
The adult sits nearby and observes: did the learner use the question,
or guess? The adult scribes one sentence for private storage: "An apple
is a need because ___." / "A ball is a want because ___."

*Alternate response mode:* the learner points to the mat instead of
naming it, and mimes the reason (eating for food, drinking for water,
shivering-then-warm for the coat, hugging for the teddy bear).

## Application

"Tonight, notice one need and one want in your room — just notice, in
your head. Tomorrow, tell me: what was the need, and what was the want?"
(In a one-on-one setting, the learner reports back at the next session.
Never ask what the learner's family owns or buys — noticing is enough.)

## Exit check

The adult holds up two cards (for example, coat and sticker) and asks
for each: "Need or want? How does our question help?" The learner
should sort both correctly and give a reason for at least one (words,
pointing, or mime). The adult records: secure / developing / not yet.
A second probe: "Name one more need and one more want from today."
(Any correct pair counts.)

## Supports

- **Language access:** teach each word by action first, word second.
  Mime eating, drinking, shivering, hugging before saying the word.
  Accept home-language words alongside English.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing, mime, sorting cards, drawing.
- **Floor-play variant:** lay the two mats on the floor; the learner
  "delivers" each card to its mat by hopping, crawling, or driving a toy
  car to it.
- **Seated-table variant:** do the whole lesson at the table with the
  picture cards and mats.

## Extension

"Is there something that is a need in winter but a want in summer — or
the other way around?" (A warm coat is the unit's example: a need on a
cold day, less needed on a hot day. Accept any sensible answer with a
reason — the reasoning is the learning, and needs can change with the
weather. Preview: Lesson 04 will ask what happens when we cannot have
everything.)

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) "I want it a lot, so it is a need" —
the error-analysis round teaches that strong wanting is not needing;
(2) "loved things are needs" — the teddy bear example separates love
from bodily need, gently, without dismissing the feeling; (3) "wants
are bad" — correct this at once: wants are fine, we simply choose
needs first.

**Answer-key reference:** [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-01) has the
expected responses for the error-analysis round and exit check.
