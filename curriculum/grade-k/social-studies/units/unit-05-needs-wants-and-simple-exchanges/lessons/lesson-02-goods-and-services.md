# Lesson 02 — Goods and Services

**Student goal:** I can tell a good (a thing you can hold) from a
service (help someone does for you), and I can name goods and services
in our community.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U05](../README.md) · **Session 3 of 16**

## Materials

- 8 picture cards: apple, loaf of bread, teddy bear, crayons, haircut
  (scissors + hair), story read aloud (open book + listening ears),
  song sung (music notes), bus ride (toy bus)
- Two sorting mats: GOOD (picture of a hand holding an apple) and
  SERVICE (picture of hands helping)
- The Unit 03 helper cards (grocer, baker, bus driver) for retrieval

**Privacy note for the adult:** this lesson never asks the learner to say or
write their name, address, school name, or family details. Restate the
Unit 01 safety rule at the start: "Names, addresses, and photos stay
private."

## Readiness check (1 minute)

Hold up the apple card from Lesson 01. "Need or want?" (Need.) "Today
we ask a new question about the same things: can I hold it and take it
home, or is it help someone does for me?" If the learner is ready to
listen and point, begin.

## Teaching

A **good** is a thing you can hold and take home. A **service** is
help someone does for you — you cannot hold it, but it helps you.

The sorting question to ask every time: **"Can I hold it and take it
home?"** If yes, it is a good. If no — if it is something someone
*does* — it is a service.

Goods in this lesson: an apple (hold it, eat it later), a loaf of bread
(hold it, take it home), a teddy bear (hold it, hug it), crayons (hold
them, draw with them).

Services in this lesson: a haircut (someone cuts your hair — you cannot
take the haircut home in a bag; the help is the haircut itself), a story
read aloud (someone reads to you), a song sung (someone sings for you),
a bus ride (the driver drives you somewhere — the ride is the help).

Retrieval from Unit 03: the grocer sells goods (apples, bread). The
baker makes a good (bread). The bus driver does a service (the ride).
One helper can sell goods and another can do a service — and both help
the community.

(Gesture for each word: hold out both hands as if carrying something
for "good — I can hold it"; mime helping hands for "service — someone
does it for me.")

## Worked example 1 — the loaf of bread, step by step (modeled)

Hold up the bread card.

> "What is this? A loaf of bread. Ask our question: can I hold it and
> take it home? Yes! I can hold the bread and carry it home. So bread
> is a good. Bread, hold it, good — three pieces, every time."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** we did not just name the thing. We asked
the question, and the question told us the answer.

## Worked example 2 — the haircut, and a tricky case (modeled)

Hold up the haircut card, then the crayons card.

> "What is this? A haircut. Ask our question: can I hold a haircut and
> take it home? No! A haircut is something someone does for you — the
> barber or hairdresser cuts your hair. The help is the haircut. So a
> haircut is a service."
>
> "Now crayons. Can I hold crayons and take them home? Yes! So crayons
> are a good. A haircut is help; crayons are a thing. That is how we
> tell them apart."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** a service disappears when it is done —
the song ends, the ride ends — but the help was real. A good stays in
your hands.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds. Offer choices; never demand a spoken answer.

1. Hold up the apple card. "Good or service? Ask our question." *(Good
   — you can hold it and take it home.)*
2. Hold up the story-read-aloud card. "Good or service?" *(Service —
   someone reads to you; you cannot hold the reading.)*
3. Hold up the teddy bear card. "Good or service?" *(Good — you can
   hold it and take it home.)*
4. Error-analysis round: the adult announces, very seriously: "A bus
   ride is a good, because I can hold my bus ticket!" Ask: "Is that
   right? What is the ride, and what is the ticket?" *(Expected: no —
   the ticket is a good you can hold, but the ride itself is a service:
   the driver driving you somewhere. The ticket is not the ride.)*
5. "Which community helper does a service — the grocer or the bus
   driver?" *(The bus driver — the ride is a service. The grocer sells
   goods.)*
6. If the learner is verbal: "Finish the sentence: a service is ___."
   *(Help someone does for you.)*

## Independent practice

The learner sorts all 8 cards onto the two mats without help, then
points to one card on each mat and tells the adult why it belongs there.
The adult sits nearby and observes: did the learner use the question,
or guess? The adult scribes one sentence for private storage: "Bread is
a good because ___." / "A song is a service because ___."

*Alternate response mode:* the learner points to the mat instead of
naming it, and mimes the reason (carrying for a good, helping hands for
a service).

## Application

"Tomorrow, notice one good and one service in our classroom or school —
just notice, in your head. Tomorrow, tell me: what was the good, and
what was the service?" (In a one-on-one setting, the learner reports
back at the next session.)

## Exit check

The adult holds up two cards (for example, crayons and song sung) and
asks for each: "Good or service? How does our question help?" The
learner should sort both correctly and give a reason for at least one
(words, pointing, or mime). The adult records: secure / developing /
not yet. A second probe: "Name one more good and one more service from
today." (Any correct pair counts.)

## Supports

- **Language access:** teach each word by action first, word second.
  Mime carrying, then mime helping, before saying the words. Accept
  home-language words alongside English.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing, mime, sorting cards, drawing.
- **Floor-play variant:** lay the two mats on the floor; the learner
  "delivers" each card to its mat.
- **Seated-table variant:** do the whole lesson at the table with the
  picture cards and mats.

## Extension

"Can one person do both — sell a good and do a service?" (Yes: a baker
sells bread, a good, and can also teach you how to knead dough, a
service — help someone does for you. Accept any sensible example.
Preview: in Lesson 03, our shop will sell goods and offer one service
stall.)

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) "the ticket is the ride" — the
error-analysis round separates the thing you hold from the help you
receive; (2) "services are not real because you cannot hold them" —
name the help out loud every time (the ride got you to school; the song
made you happy); (3) "everything in a shop is a good" — the Lesson 03
shop will include a service stall to break this.

expected responses for the error-analysis round and exit check.
