# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Needs, Wants, and Simple Exchanges

**Audience:** the guiding adult only. Do not hand this guide to the learner
with the lessons — it contains the answers and the re-teaching paths.

## How this unit is built

The unit repeats one question per idea until it is automatic: needs/wants
asks "would our body still be healthy and safe without it?"; goods/services
asks "can I hold it and take it home?"; shopping asks "what does the price
card say, and how many coins are left?"; choosing asks "what do I get,
and what do I give up?"; saving asks "now or later?"; near/far asks
"what was the journey?" Lesson 01 teaches the need/want question on the
most familiar set. Lesson 02 repeats the question routine on a new
distinction (goods/services). Lesson 03 changes the question: from
sorting to exchanging — prices and coins left. Lesson 04 changes it
again: from paying to choosing under scarcity, plus saving. Lesson 05
closes the economics arc: where things come from, and trading kindly.
The project is the unit's public performance: a market the learner can
actually run.

| 7 (L4) | Claiming both items while paying for one | Count 3 + 3 = 6 aloud, then point to the 5-coin purse |

## Misconceptions and re-teaching

- **"I want it a lot, so it is a need."** Wanting strongly is not
  needing. Re-teach: Lesson 01, Worked example 2 and the error-analysis
  round; Practice A with 4 cards.
- **"Loved things are needs."** The teddy bear is loved and still a
  want. Say so gently; never dismiss the feeling. Re-teach: Lesson 01,
  Worked example 2.
- **"Wants are bad."** Correct at once: wants are fine; we choose needs
  first. Re-teach: Lesson 01 teaching section, last paragraph.
- **"The ticket is the ride."** The thing you hold is not the help you
  receive. Re-teach: Lesson 02, error-analysis round; Practice B.
- **"Services are not real because you cannot hold them."** Name the
  help out loud every time. Re-teach: Lesson 02, Worked example 2.
- **"My favorite number is the price."** The price card sets the price.
  Re-teach: Lesson 03, Worked example 2; Practice E.
- **"The shopkeeper gets money for free."** The shopkeeper works, and
  prices pay for work and materials. Re-teach: Lesson 03 teaching
  section ("what a maker needs").
- **"I can have both if I want them enough."** Counting decides, not
  wanting. Re-teach: Lesson 04, readiness check and Worked example 1.
- **"Saving means losing the coin."** The jar makes saving visible.
  Re-teach: Lesson 04, Worked example 2; Practice F, Scenario 4.
- **"Giving something up means I chose wrong."** A give-up is part of
  every choice, not a mistake. Re-teach: Lesson 04 teaching section.
- **"It is in our shop, so it is nearby."** Where a thing is sold is
  not where it was made. Re-teach: Lesson 05, Worked example 1.
- **"Trading means taking."** Both sides give, both sides agree, a
  trusted adult is nearby. Re-teach: Lesson 05 trade game.

## Readiness: what "ready for Unit 06" looks like

The learner sorts needs from wants (7/8 or better), sorts goods from
services, pays exact prices from 5 coins and states the coins left,
chooses one item under scarcity and says what was given up, explains
saving as keeping money for later, and names one faraway and one nearby
item with a journey reason. The unit assessment total of 7/10 or more,
with no task at 0, is the operational check; the project rubric total
of 9/12 or more is the performance check. If either falls short,
re-teach only the lowest criterion — do not repeat the whole unit —
using the matching lesson and practice session, then re-check with the
matching quiz question.

## Sensitivity notes (read before Session 1)

Never ask about a real family's money, shopping, income, or what a
family can or cannot afford — not in lessons, not in the market, not in
the assessment. If the learner volunteers something about home, listen
kindly, do not probe, and do not record it. If a learner says their
family cannot buy something, the adult's answer is: "Families choose
differently, and every family takes care of its needs first. In our
pretend shop, everyone gets 5 coins." Never let a pretend choice become
a judgment about a real family. If the learner becomes upset during
choice or saving games, stop the game, return to sorting play, and try
again another day — the objectives wait; the learner's trust does not.
