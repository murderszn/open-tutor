# Project — Our Classroom Market

**Type:** investigation / dramatic-play market · **Sessions:** 8 (setup),
11 (Part 1), 14 (Part 2) · **Time:** 15–20 minutes per session, plus
setup

## The question

Can our class run a fair market where buyers choose, pay exact prices,
and sometimes save — and where every stall shows whether its goods were
made nearby or far away?

## Materials

- The shoebox shop, the voted shop name from Session 1, shop signs and
  price cards made in Session 8
- 5 play coins per buyer; the 6 shop items (apple, bread shape, water
  bottle, ball, teddy bear, toy coat); the generated illustration
  [`assets/classroom-market-stall.png`](assets/classroom-market-stall.png)
  as the market's sign
- Two stalls: the NEAR stall (bread, apple from a nearby tree, crayons)
  and the FAR stall (banana picture, toy ball); one SERVICE stall
  (a story read aloud or a song sung, price 1 coin — a service the
  seller performs on the spot)
- SAVE and SPEND jars; a purchase record sheet (adult-scribed): buyer
  (no names — use a drawing or a number the adult assigns privately),
  item, price, coins left

**Safety and privacy:** play coins only; no real money, no real
purchases, no real family shopping. Buyers and sellers are never asked
about real family money. The service stall never asks a learner to
perform for strangers — the "customers" are the adult and the learner's
own classroom group only. Records stay in private storage.

## Steps

### Part 1 — Build the stalls (Session 11)

1. Sort the goods into the NEAR stall and the FAR stall. The learner
   says one journey sentence for each stall: "Bread is made nearby."
   "Bananas travel far away."
2. Set the service stall: the seller chooses a service (read a short
   story aloud, or sing a song). Price: 1 coin. The seller practices
   the service once for the adult.
3. Write the price cards together (picture + coin dots): apple 1,
   bread 2, water bottle 2, ball 3, teddy bear 3, warm coat 3, banana
   picture 2, service 1. Check every card against the unit price list —
   the six unit items keep their unit prices, and the two new market
   items (banana picture 2, service 1) are used only in this project.
4. Place the SAVE jar at the market entrance with its picture label.

### Part 2 — Run the market (Session 14)

1. Each buyer gets a purse of 5 coins and a turn. Fair turns, one buyer
   at a time (Unit 02 retrieval: waiting for a turn is a rule that
   keeps the market fair).
2. Each buyer makes at least two exchanges: one purchase of a need
   (apple, bread, water bottle, or coat) and one free choice (any item,
   or the service stall). After each purchase the buyer says the coins
   left.
3. Each buyer faces one choice moment: with the coins left, the buyer
   is offered two items that together cost more than the coins left,
   chooses one, and says: "I get the ___, and I give up the ___."
4. Each buyer decides about the last coins: spend them, or put at least
   1 coin in the SAVE jar "for later." Either choice is recorded with
   its reason.
5. Close the market: sellers count their coins together (no
   competition — the count is a check, not a contest), the adult reads
   the purchase record aloud, and every buyer says one thank-you to a
   seller (Unit 03 retrieval: thank helpers and sellers respectfully,
   without interrupting busy work).

## Deliverables

1. Two labeled stalls (NEAR and FAR) with correct goods sorted.
2. Price cards for every item sold, with correct coin dots.
3. A purchase record (adult-scribed) showing at least two exchanges per
   buyer, with prices and coins left.
4. One save-or-spend decision per buyer, with a reason.
5. One choice sentence per buyer ("I get ___, and I give up ___").

## Rubric (12 points — adult-observed)

| Criterion (0–3) | 3 — strong | 2 — developing | 1 — beginning | 0 — not yet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sort near/far** | All goods on the correct stall, with a journey sentence for each stall | Most goods correct, or one journey sentence missing | Several goods misplaced, no journey sentence | No sorting attempted |
| **Pay exact prices** | Every purchase paid exactly, coins left stated correctly | One price or coins-left error, corrected with a hint | Two or more errors, even with hints | No purchase completed |
| **Choose and give up** | Chooses one item and says both parts ("I get ___, and I give up ___") | Chooses one item but names only what was gotten | Chooses only with heavy adult direction, or claims both items | No choice made |
| **Save-or-spend with a reason** | Decides save or spend and gives a reason tied to later or now | Decides with a reason that does not match the choice | Decides without a reason | No decision made |

**Scoring:** 9–12 is strong; 6–8 is developing (re-teach the lowest
criterion with its lesson, then re-check with the matching quiz
question); below 6 means re-run Part 2 with fewer items (3 items, 5
coins) before the unit assessment. The rubric total is a teaching
signal, not a grade for a public record.
