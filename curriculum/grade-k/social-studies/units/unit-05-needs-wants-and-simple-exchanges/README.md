# Unit 05 — Needs, Wants, and Simple Exchanges

**Grade:** Kindergarten · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track weeks:** 19–22
**Backlog:** [issue #9](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/9), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Every day we choose. We eat food because our bodies need it. We drink
water because our bodies need it. We wear a warm coat because our bodies
need to stay warm. And sometimes we want something extra — a ball, a
teddy bear, a shiny sticker. We cannot have everything at once, so we
choose. Grown-ups work to earn money, and money lets us exchange: we
give coins and get something back. Sometimes we spend, and sometimes we
save our coins for later. In this unit the learner sorts needs from
wants, tells goods (things you can hold) from services (help someone
does for you), runs a classroom shop with play coins, practices choosing
when coins run out, and discovers that some things we buy were made far
away and traveled a long way to reach our shop.

**Privacy and kindness by design:** this unit never asks about a real
family's money, shopping, income, or what a family can or cannot afford.
All shopping uses play coins and a pretend shop; no real money is spent,
and nothing is bought from a real store. Wants are never "bad" and needs
are never a test of a family's situation — different families choose
differently, and every choice in this unit is pretend. The adult keeps
all records in private storage. The Unit 01 safety rule is restated every
session: names, addresses, and photos stay private.

## Essential question

What do we need, and how do we choose?

## Prerequisites

- Unit 01: identity, belonging, and classroom community (the privacy
  rule; belonging routines; retrieved in every session)
- Unit 02: rules, fairness, and cooperative choices (fair turns and the
  class vote; retrieved in Session 1, when the class votes on the shop's
  name, and in every shop turn)
- Unit 03: community helpers (the grocer sells food; the baker makes
  bread; helpers do services; retrieved in Sessions 3, 9, and 12)
- Unit 04: maps, symbols, and nearby places (near and far; retrieved in
  Session 9 — "far away" means far on the map)
- Can sit for a 5–10 minute circle time with movement breaks
- Follows one-step directions; two-step directions emerging
- Counts objects to 5 (from the math track's everyday counting; the
  shop never uses more than 5 play coins)
- No reading or writing required. The adult reads everything aloud and scribes.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Sort at least 7 of 8 picture cards into needs and wants, and explain
   in child words that a need is something we must have to stay healthy
   and safe, while a want is something nice to have.
2. Sort goods from services with at most one error, naming at least two
   goods and two services found in the community.
3. Explain in child words that people earn money by working, and, in the
   classroom shop, pay a stated price (1–3 play coins) for an item and
   say how many coins are left from a purse of 5.
4. When offered two items but only enough coins for one, choose one and
   say in child words what they get and what they give up.
5. Explain saving in child words ("keeping money for later"), and in a
   pretend scenario choose to save or spend and give a reason for the
   choice.
6. Name one thing made far away and sold here (for example, bananas
   grown in a warm, faraway place) and one thing made nearby, and explain
   exchange in child words ("we give something and get something back").

## The classroom shop price list (used in every lesson, practice, quiz, and assessment)

Play coins only. Every buyer starts with a purse of **5 coins**.

| Item | Price | Why this price is fixed |
|---|---|---|
| Apple | 1 coin | The cheapest need in the shop |
| Loaf of bread | 2 coins | A need that takes more work to make |
| Water bottle | 2 coins | A need that takes more work to bring |
| Ball | 3 coins | A want that costs more than an apple |
| Teddy bear | 3 coins | A want that costs more than an apple |
| Warm coat | 3 coins | A need that costs more than an apple |

Worked coin facts the adult checks before teaching: 5 − 1 = 4; 5 − 2 = 3;
5 − 3 = 2; 2 + 1 = 3; 2 + 2 = 4; 1 + 2 = 3; 3 + 2 = 5; 3 + 3 = 6 (more
than 5 — cannot buy both). Every coin problem in this unit uses only
these facts.

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md#8-safe-materials) for the full
safe-materials list): picture cards (drawn by the adult or cut from
magazines) — apple, bread, water bottle, coat, bed, ball, teddy bear,
sticker, crayons, haircut, story read aloud, song sung, bus ride;
two sorting mats labeled (by the adult) with a picture, not words alone:
a heart-and-house picture for NEED and a star picture for WANT; 5 large
play coins per buyer (cardboard circles, bottle caps, or toy coins —
never real coins, and checked for choking hazards); a shoebox "shop"
with the generated illustration
[`assets/classroom-market-stall.png`](assets/classroom-market-stall.png)
as its sign; price cards (picture + 1, 2, or 3 coin dots — dots, not
numerals alone, so a non-reader can count them); two jars labeled with
pictures: SPEND (open hand) and SAVE (closed jar with a lid); a toy cash
register or a small box for the shopkeeper's coins; a basket, a loaf
shape (play dough or a drawn loaf), a toy ball, a teddy bear, a toy coat
or a drawn coat. No sharp tools; no food allergens used as counters or
props without checking; no real money changes hands at any point.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, drawing, or gesture responses; the adult
scribes. Each lesson includes a seated-table and a floor-play variant, a
language-access support, an alternate response mode, and an extension.
Prices are shown as countable coin dots, never numerals alone, so a
learner who cannot yet read numerals can still pay correctly. Needs and
wants are never distinguished by color alone: every card carries a
picture and the adult names it aloud. The generated illustration ships
with a text-only alternative carrying every item the activities ask about.
