# Practice — Unit 05: Needs, Wants, and Simple Exchanges

**How to use this file:** the adult reads everything aloud. Sessions are
15–20 minutes. Every response mode counts: spoken words, pointing, mime,
or drawing. The adult scribes results for private storage. Prices are
always the [unit price list](README.md#the-classroom-shop-price-list-used-in-every-lesson-practice-quiz-and-assessment):
apple 1, bread 2, water bottle 2, ball 3, teddy bear 3, warm coat 3. Every
buyer starts with 5 play coins. No real money is used at any point.

## Session A — Need-or-want sort game

**Goal:** sort needs from wants without help, using the Lesson 01 question.

1. Shuffle the 8 Lesson 01 cards: apple, bread, water bottle, warm coat,
   bed, ball, teddy bear, sticker.
2. The learner places each card on the NEED mat (house + heart) or the
   WANT mat (star), saying the question aloud for at least two cards:
   "If we did not have this, would our body still be healthy and safe?"
3. Score together: needs are apple, bread, water bottle, warm coat, bed;
   wants are ball, teddy bear, sticker. 7–8 correct is secure; 5–6 is
   developing (re-run with 4 cards only); fewer than 5 means re-teach
   Lesson 01 before Session 3.
4. Finish with the chant, clapping once per word: "Needs keep us healthy
   and safe! Wants are nice to have!"

*Alternate mode:* the learner points to the mat and mimes the reason
instead of explaining.

## Session B — Goods-and-services hunt

**Goal:** find goods and services in the room, and retrieve Unit 03 helpers.

1. The adult hides (in plain sight) 6 items or pictures: an apple, a
   book, crayons (goods); a picture of a haircut, a song card, a bus
   picture (services).
2. The learner "hunts" for each one and sorts it onto the GOOD mat
   (hand holding an apple) or the SERVICE mat (helping hands), asking:
   "Can I hold it and take it home?"
3. Unit 03 retrieval: "Which helper sells goods? Which helper does a
   service?" *(Grocer sells goods; bus driver does a service — the ride.
   The baker makes a good — bread.)*
4. Score together: apple, book, crayons are goods; haircut, song, bus
   ride are services. 5–6 correct is secure; fewer means re-run with
   4 items only.

*Alternate mode:* the learner points and mimes (carrying for a good,
helping hands for a service).

## Session C — Read-aloud: "Sam and the Classroom Shop"

**Goal:** listen, retell in order, and name Sam's need, want, choice, and saving.

*Original story, written for this unit. The adult reads it aloud with
expression; the learner listens and then retells with picture prompts.*

> Sam has 5 play coins. Sam walks to the classroom shop. The shopkeeper
> smiles. "Welcome to the Sunny Shop!" says the shopkeeper. (If the
> class voted a different shop name in Session 1, use that name here.)
>
> Sam looks at the stall. There is bread for 2 coins. There is a ball
> for 3 coins. There is an apple for 1 coin. Sam's tummy rumbles.
> "Bread is food," says Sam. "Food is a need. I will buy the bread."
> Sam counts out 2 coins — one, two — and hands them to the shopkeeper.
> "How many coins do I have left?" asks Sam. "Five take away two is
> three," says the shopkeeper. "You have 3 coins left."
>
> Now Sam looks at the ball. The ball costs 3 coins. Sam has 3 coins.
> Sam could buy the ball and have 0 coins left. Or Sam could save the
> coins for later. Sam thinks. "The ball is a want," says Sam. "I want
> it, but I do not need it today. I will save my coins for later."
> Sam puts the 3 coins in the save jar. "Saving means keeping money
> for later," says Sam.
>
> On the way home, Sam tells the shopkeeper: "Thank you! I got bread,
> and I gave up the ball today. Tomorrow I can choose again." The
> shopkeeper smiles. "That is a good choice, Sam. Needs first, and
> saving for later."

**After the story — retell in order (4 steps):** (1) Sam buys bread for
2 coins; (2) Sam has 3 coins left (5 − 2 = 3); (3) Sam chooses to save
instead of buying the ball; (4) Sam says what Sam got and gave up.
The learner retells with the adult's picture prompts, then draws one
scene: Sam at the shop, Sam with the save jar, or Sam saying thank you.
The adult scribes one dictated sentence under the drawing for private
storage.

*Alternate mode:* the learner orders 4 picture cards (bread, 3 coins,
save jar, thank-you wave) instead of retelling in words.

## Session D — Two-choice game

**Goal:** choose one of two items with 5 coins and say what you get and
what you give up. Also sets up the project: the learner makes one shop
sign and one price card for the market.

1. Round 1: ball (3) vs. teddy bear (3). The learner chooses one and
   says: "I get the ___, and I give up the ___." Coins left either way:
   5 − 3 = 2.
2. Round 2: bread (2) + apple (1) vs. ball (3). The learner may buy the
   pair (2 + 1 = 3, leaving 2) or the ball (3, leaving 2) — either way 2
   coins are left, so the learner compares what each choice gives:
   two needs, or one want.
3. Round 3 (tricky): warm coat (3) + apple (1) = 4, leaving 1 — the
   learner checks: does 4 fit in 5? Yes. Then: coat (3) + ball (3) = 6 —
   does 6 fit in 5? No. The learner says which pair fits and which does
   not.
4. Project setup: the learner draws one shop sign (the voted shop name
   from Session 1, scribed by the adult) and one price card (a picture
   with 1, 2, or 3 coin dots). These go into the project market.

*Alternate mode:* the learner points to the chosen card and the
given-up card instead of saying the sentence, and counts coins onto the
counter.

## Session E — Shop counting relay

**Goal:** pay exact prices from 5 coins and say the coins left, quickly
and correctly.

1. Set out the 6 shop items with price cards. The learner is the buyer
   with 5 coins; the adult is the shopkeeper.
2. The adult calls out 5 purchases, one at a time. The learner pays and
   says the coins left before the next call:
   - Buy an apple (1) → 4 left (5 − 1 = 4)
   - Buy bread (2) → 3 left (5 − 2 = 3)
   - Buy a ball (3) → 2 left (5 − 3 = 2)
   - Buy a water bottle (2) → 3 left (5 − 2 = 3)
   - Buy a teddy bear (3) → 2 left (5 − 3 = 2)
   Each round starts fresh with 5 coins — the relay is five separate
   purchases, not one purse spent five times.
3. Speed round (only if the learner is secure): the adult holds up a
   price card and the learner holds up that many fingers or counts out
   that many coins. 4–5 correct is secure.
4. Celebrate the rule out loud together: "Pay the price — no more, no
   less!"

*Alternate mode:* the learner counts coins onto the counter and shows
the leftover pile instead of naming numbers.

## Session F — Save-or-spend jar game

**Goal:** decide, with a reason, whether to save or spend in four
pretend scenarios.

1. The learner gets 5 coins and two jars: SPEND and SAVE.
2. The adult reads each scenario aloud. The learner puts coins in a jar
   and gives a reason (words, pointing to a reason picture, or mime):
   - **Scenario 1:** "You have 5 coins. Bread costs 2 coins and you are
     hungry. Spend or save?" *(Spend 2 on bread — a need comes first.
     3 coins left.)*
   - **Scenario 2:** "You have 3 coins left. A ball costs 3 coins. You
     want the ball, but tomorrow the shop will have a warm coat for 3
     coins and winter is coming. Spend or save?" *(Either answer counts
     with a reason: spend — "I want the ball now"; or save — "I will
     need the coat tomorrow." The reason is the learning, not the jar.)*
   - **Scenario 3:** "You have 5 coins. You buy an apple for 1 coin.
     You have 4 coins left. Save 2 and spend 2, or save them all?"
     *(Any split counts if the learner can show where the coins went:
     for example, 2 in SAVE, 2 kept for spending. The adult checks the
     total: coins in jars + coins in hand = 5.)*
   - **Scenario 4:** "Your save jar has 3 coins. A teddy bear costs 3
     coins. You take the coins out of the save jar and buy the teddy
     bear. What happened to your savings?" *(The savings are spent —
     the jar is empty, 0 coins, and the learner has the teddy bear.
     Saving for later can end in spending later, and that is fine.)*
3. Close with the rule: "Saving is not losing. Saved coins wait for
   later — and later, you may choose to spend them."

*Alternate mode:* the learner moves coins between jars and points to
the "now" picture (spend) or the "later" picture (save) instead of
explaining.

## Session G — Our market in action review

**Goal:** retrieve every objective before the formative quiz.

1. **Needs/wants lightning sort:** 6 cards, fast — apple, coat, ball,
   bread, teddy bear, water bottle. (Needs: apple, coat, bread, water
   bottle. Wants: ball, teddy bear.)
2. **Goods/services lightning sort:** 4 cards — crayons (good), song
   (service), bread (good), bus ride (service).
3. **Shop check:** buy bread (2) from 5 coins → 3 left; buy an apple (1)
   from 3 coins → 2 left. Say both steps aloud.
4. **Choice check:** ball (3) vs. teddy bear (3) with 5 coins — choose
   one, say what you get and give up.
5. **Save check:** "Show me saving." (A coin in the SAVE jar, "for
   later.")
6. **Near/far check:** banana (far away) vs. bread (nearby); "What is
   trading?" (Giving something and getting something back.)
7. Any check the learner misses becomes the Session 15 re-teach before
   the quiz — re-teach first, quiz second, never the other way around.

## The market song (review, Session 12)

*Sung to any familiar clapping tune. The adult sings; the learner joins
in, claps, or mimes. New verses are welcome — the learner supplies the
item and the price.*

> Needs first, needs first, food and water, coat and bed!
> Wants are nice, wants are nice, ball and bear and sticker, too!
> Count the dots, pay the price, give the coins and take your treat!
> Save a coin, save a coin, keep it safe for later days!
> Near and far, near and far, bread is near and bananas travel far!
> Trade with friends, trade with friends, give and get and smile again!
