# Unit 03 — Community Helpers: Roles and Responsibilities

**Grade:** Kindergarten · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #9](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/9), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A community is people who live and work together — and a lot of those people
have jobs that help everyone else. In this unit the learner meets the helpers
in the school first (teacher, custodian, school nurse, bus driver), then the
helpers in the neighborhood (firefighter, mail carrier, grocer, doctor), and
learns the same three questions about every helper: **What does this helper
do? What tools does this helper use? How does this helper's work help us?**
Then helpers start working together: a letter travels from a mailbox to a
house because several helpers pass it along, and city helpers — the people
who fix roads, collect trash, and keep the library open — are how a city
takes care of itself. The unit ends with a Helper Fair, where the learner
plays a helper, shows the helper's tools, and delivers a real thank-you to a
real school helper.

**Privacy by design:** helper work at this age must not collect personal
information. Thank-you cards carry drawings and the words "thank you," never
the learner's name, address, school name, or photo. The class helper chart
records jobs ("the nurse helps when we are hurt"), never who was hurt or why.
The adult keeps all records in private storage. Helpers are never approached
outside school without a trusted adult, and the learner never shares personal
details with a helper they do not know.

## Essential question

Who helps our community, and how?

## Prerequisites

- Unit 01: identity, belonging, and classroom community (belonging routines;
  the safety rule "names, addresses, and photos stay private" is restated
  every session)
- Unit 02: rules, fairness, and cooperative choices (helpers follow rules
  too — safety rules at work, fair turns with shared tools; retrieved in
  Sessions 4 and 12)
- Can sit for a 5–10 minute circle time with movement breaks
- Follows one-step directions; two-step directions emerging
- No reading or writing required. The adult reads everything aloud and scribes.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Name five or more community helpers and describe what each one does.
2. Name at least one tool (or uniform clue) for each helper named, and say
   what the tool is for.
3. Explain, in child words, how a helper's work helps the community
   ("the firefighter keeps us safe"; "the grocer sells food we eat").
4. Trace a simple chain of helpers working together — a letter's journey
   from mailbox to house, or a sick person's journey from school nurse to
   doctor — naming at least two helpers in order.
5. Describe, in child words, how city helpers care for the city: they fix
   roads, collect trash, and keep shared places (like the library) open and
   usable.
6. Take a helper role in dramatic play, use the helper's tools correctly in
   the role, and thank a real helper respectfully.

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md#8-safe-materials) for the full
safe-materials list): large paper, crayons or markers, picture cards (drawn by
the adult or cut from magazines) of the twelve helpers in this unit, toy tools
or safe stand-ins (a toy stethoscope or a paper-tube "listening tube," a
spray bottle for the custodian's "cleaning spray," a small bag for mail, a
play cash register, a toy hose or coiled rope), dress-up pieces (a cap, a
yellow raincoat for the firefighter, an apron for the grocer, a white coat
made from an old shirt), four envelopes and a shoebox "mailbox," a shoebox
"mail truck," the generated illustration
[`assets/community-helpers-street.png`](assets/community-helpers-street.png).
No sharp tools; props checked for choking hazards; no food allergens used as
counters or props without checking. Real tools (a real hose, a real ladder)
are never used by the learner — helpers' real tools are observed only, from
a safe distance, with a trusted adult.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, drawing, or gesture responses; the adult
scribes. Each lesson includes a seated-table and a floor-play variant, a
language-access support, an alternate response mode, and an extension.
Helper uniforms are never the only cue: every helper is also named by job,
tool, and workplace, so a learner who cannot distinguish colors can still
match by tool or place. The generated illustration ships with a text-only
alternative carrying every helper and tool the activities ask about.
