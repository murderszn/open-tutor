# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Community Helpers

**Audience:** the guiding adult only. Do not hand this guide to the learner
with the lessons — it contains the answers and the re-teaching paths.

## How this unit is built

The unit repeats one routine until it is automatic: for every helper, ask
**what does this helper do, what tool does this helper use, and how does
this helper's work help us?** Lesson 01 teaches the routine on school
helpers (the most familiar set). Lesson 02 repeats it on neighborhood
helpers and adds picture-reading with the generated illustration. Lesson 03
changes the question: helpers do not work alone, they work in chains.
Lesson 04 changes it again: tools match jobs by purpose, and purposes can
repeat (shared tools). Lesson 05 closes with gratitude and role-play —
the project's public performance.

## Session-by-session guidance

| Session | Watch for | If it wobbles |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (L1) | Learner names all four school helpers with tools | Re-run the tool sort with two helpers only, then add the others |
| 2 (Practice A) | Tool mismatches, especially bandage/bus | Keep the nurse and bus driver cards far apart; re-mime each job |
| 3 (Story C) | Retell order (sweep → wipe → mop → empty trash) | Re-read with the learner holding the tool mime for each step |
| 4 (Sort D) | Bus driver placement (school vs. street) | Accept either mat with an explanation — the reasoning is the learning |
| 5 (L2) | Picture-reading by uniform + tool, not by face | Cover the faces with sticky notes and re-ask: "Who is this, by tool?" |
| 6 (Practice B) | Confusing doctor and nurse | Workplace cue: nurse at school, doctor at clinic/hospital |
| 7 (L3) | Chain order (collect → sort → drive → deliver) | Walk the stations physically; remove one station and ask what breaks |
| 8 (Relay E) | Break-the-chain reasoning | Ask "what is missing?" before "can it still arrive?" |
| 9 (L4) | Shared-tool trap (one tool, one helper) | Re-run Worked example 2 with books, then thermometers |
| 10 (Wrong-tool F) | Learner laughs but cannot explain what goes wrong | Ask "what is the tool FOR?" — purpose first, then the mismatch |

| 12 (Song) | Participation, not pitch | Let the learner supply job lines for new verses |
| 13 (L5) | Interrupting busy helpers | Re-run the error-analysis round; rehearse waiting for a pause |

## Misconceptions and re-teaching

- **"Helpers are only people I like."** Every job counts, including jobs
  done when the learner is not watching (custodian after hours, sorter at
  night). Re-teach: Lesson 01, Worked example 2; Practice C (Ms. Rosa's
  invisible morning work).
- **"The helper I see does everything."** The visible helper is usually
  the last link. Re-teach: Lesson 03, acted journey; Practice E, break-the-
  chain round.
- **"One tool belongs to one helper."** Books (teacher, librarian) and
  thermometers (nurse, doctor) break this rule. Re-teach: Lesson 04,
  Worked example 2; Practice F, shared-tool round.
- **"Bigger tools mean more important jobs."** A bandage is small, a fire
  truck is big; both jobs matter. Re-teach: Lesson 04, extension; Lesson 05
  reflection ("which job surprised you?").
- **"I can thank any helper anywhere."** Safety first: in person only with
  a trusted adult, only school helpers the learner already knows, never
  by approaching strangers, never while a helper is driving or using a
  machine. Re-teach: Lesson 05, error-analysis round.
- **"Firefighters only fight fires."** They also teach fire safety and
  rescue. Keep the fire-safety rule at kindergarten level: get out, stay
  out, tell a grown-up. Never stage a fire or use matches.
- **"The nurse fixes everything."** The nurse helps at school and calls a
  grown-up; serious help comes from adults and doctors. Re-teach: Lesson
  03, second chain (nurse → grown-up → doctor).

## Readiness: what "ready for Unit 04" looks like

The learner names five or more helpers with jobs and tools, explains how at
least two helpers' work helps the community, orders the letter chain with
helpers named, names one city helper, and delivers a respectful thank-you
without interrupting busy work. The unit assessment total of 7/10 or more
is the operational check; the project rubric total of 8/12 or more is the
performance check. If either falls short, re-teach only the lowest
criterion — do not repeat the whole unit — using the matching lesson and
practice session, then re-check with the matching quiz question.

## Accessibility notes

- Every check accepts oral, pointing, drawing, or gesture responses.
  Never require a spoken sentence for a correct answer.
- Uniforms are never the only cue: every helper is named by job, tool,
  and workplace. The picture activities all have text-only alternatives.
- The letter chain is walked physically (floor-play variant) for learners
  who need movement; the paper strip is the seated equivalent.
- If a learner is frightened by fire content, keep Lesson 02's fire
  safety to one sentence — "if there is a fire, get out and tell a
  grown-up" — and spend the time on the mail carrier and grocer instead.
- Home-language words for helpers and tools are welcome throughout; the
  sentence frames in Lesson 05 carry the English structure while the
  learner supplies the job words in either language.
