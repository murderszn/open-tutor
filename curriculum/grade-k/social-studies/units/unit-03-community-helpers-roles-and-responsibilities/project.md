# Project — The Helper Fair and Thank-You Delivery

**Type:** dramatic-play investigation + real thank-you delivery
**Sessions:** setup in Session 8, Part 1 in Session 11, Part 2 in Session 14
**Adult role:** the adult sets up stations, plays visitors, keeps the
delivery safe, and scribes. The learner runs the fair.

## The question

How can we show what helpers do — and thank a real helper for doing it?

## Materials

- Helper stations (built by the learner with the adult): at least three of
  — mail station (shoebox mailbox, envelopes, mailbag), fire station
  (coiled rope hose, yellow raincoat or paper helmet), clinic (listening
  tube, bandages, white coat from an old shirt), grocery shop (basket,
  play apples, play cash register), school station (books, broom)
- A station sign for each station (adult-written): the helper's name and
  one job sentence
- Thank-you cards from Sessions 8 and 13 (drawings + adult-scribed
  "thank you for ___" — no names, addresses, school names, or photos)
- The generated illustration
  [`assets/community-helpers-street.png`](assets/community-helpers-street.png)
  as the fair's "street map" poster

## Steps

### Part 1 — Build the helper stations (Session 11)

1. The learner chooses three helpers for the fair (at least one school
   helper and at least one neighborhood helper).
2. For each chosen helper, the learner sets up a station: the right tool,
   the dress-up piece, and the sign. The adult asks for each station:
   "Who works here? What tool do they use? What do they do?"
3. The learner practices one job sentence per station ("I deliver mail!"
   "I put out fires!" "I check your heartbeat!") and one visitor question
   per station ("What tool is that?" "How does that help us?").
4. The learner draws a simple fair map: three stations in a row, like the
   picture street. The adult labels the stations.

### Part 2 — Run the fair and deliver the thanks (Session 14)

1. The adult (and any willing family members or classmates, with the
   adult present) visits each station as a "visitor." The learner, in
   role, demonstrates the tool, says the job sentence, and answers one
   visitor question per station.
2. Chain demo: the learner walks a visitor through the letter's journey
   using the mail station — collect, sort, drive, deliver — naming the
   helper at each step.
3. Thank-you delivery: the learner, with a trusted adult beside them,
   delivers the finished thank-you cards in person to real school helpers
   the learner already knows (for example, the custodian or teacher).
   The learner waits for a safe pause, says the three-part thank-you
   ("Thank you for ___. You help us by ___."), and hands over the card.
   Cards are never mailed to strangers, never posted, never photographed
   for sharing.
4. Reflection: the adult asks, "Which station was hardest to run? Which
   helper's job surprised you? What did the helper say when you said thank
   you?" The adult scribes the answers for private storage.

## Deliverables

1. Three (or more) helper stations, each with the right tool, a dress-up
   piece, and a job sentence.
2. A fair map drawing with the stations labeled.
3. One delivered thank-you card per real school helper visited (drawing +
   "thank you for ___" words; no personal details).
4. A short oral reflection (adult-scribed): one thing learned about a
   helper's job, and one thing the learner noticed about saying thank you.

## Safety rules for the fair

- Real tools are never used: the hose is a rope, the stethoscope is a
  paper tube, the "fire" is pretend. No water, no matches, no real
  medicine, no real bandages on real injuries.
- Delivery happens only with a trusted adult, only to school helpers the
  learner already knows, only at a safe pause in their work. If a helper
  is busy, driving, or using a machine, the learner waits or leaves the
  card with the adult to pass on later.
- No names, addresses, school names, or photos on any card or map. The
  fair map shows stations, not real places.

## Rubric

Scored by adult observation during Part 2 (and the Part 1 setup). Each
criterion is scored 2 (secure), 1 (developing), or 0 (not yet). A total of
8 or more out of 12 signals readiness on the project's objectives; below 8,
re-teach the lowest criterion with the matching lesson before Unit 04.

| Criterion | 2 — Secure | 1 — Developing | 0 — Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Helper and tool match (Obj. 1, 2) | All stations pair the right tool with the right helper | Most stations match; one mismatch, fixed with a hint | Tools do not match helpers, even with hints |
| Job explanation (Obj. 1, 3) | Says what each helper does AND how it helps, for all stations | Says what helpers do, but not how it helps (or vice versa) | Cannot say what a helper does |
| Helping chain (Obj. 4) | Walks the letter journey in order, naming helpers at ≥3 steps | Orders most steps, or names helpers at 1–2 steps | Cannot order the steps |
| Thank-you delivery (Obj. 6) | Waits for a safe pause, names the work, and says how it helps | Delivers the card with 1–2 of the three thank-you parts | Will not deliver, or interrupts busy/dangerous work |
| City helper awareness (Obj. 5) | Names one city helper and what they do for the whole city during the fair or reflection | Names a city helper but not what they do for the city | Cannot name a city helper |
| Reflection (Obj. 3, 6) | Says one thing learned about a helper's job AND one thing noticed about thanking | Says one of the two | Cannot reflect on either |

## Differentiation

- **Support:** run two stations instead of three; the adult supplies the
  job sentence and the learner repeats it; the thank-you sentence frame
  stays visible during delivery.
- **Extension:** add a fourth station for a city helper (road worker or
  trash collector) with its own tool and job sentence; the learner writes
  (or dictates) a second thank-you card for a neighborhood helper the
  family already knows, delivered by the adult, not the learner alone.
