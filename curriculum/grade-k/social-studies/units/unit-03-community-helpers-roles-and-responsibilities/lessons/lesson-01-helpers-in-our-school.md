# Lesson 01 — Helpers in Our School

**Student goal:** I can name helpers in our school, say what each one does,
and name one tool each helper uses.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U03](../README.md) · **Session 1 of 16**

## Materials

- 4 helper picture cards (drawn by the adult): teacher, custodian, school
  nurse, bus driver — each card shows the helper with one tool
- Toy stand-ins: a book, a broom or mop, a bandage, a small toy bus
- A large sheet of paper titled (by the adult) "Our School Helpers"

**Privacy note for the adult:** this lesson never asks the learner to say or
write their name, address, school name, or family details. Restate the Unit 01
safety rule at the start: "Names, addresses, and photos stay private."

## Readiness check (1 minute)

"Point to something in this room that helps you learn." (Books, crayons, a
chair — accept anything.) "People help you learn, too. Today we meet four
people whose job is helping in a school." If the learner is ready to listen
and point, begin.

## Teaching

A **community** is people who live and work together. Our school is a small
community. A **helper** is a person whose job is to help other people.
A **job** is the work a person does. Every helper has **tools** — the things
they use to do their job.

Here are four school helpers. For each one, we ask three questions:

1. **What does this helper do?**
2. **What tool does this helper use?**
3. **How does this helper's work help us?**

**Teacher.** The teacher teaches. The teacher reads stories, shows us new
things, and helps us learn. Tools: books, picture cards, crayons. How the
teacher helps us: we learn new things every day.

**Custodian.** The custodian keeps the school clean and safe. The custodian
sweeps floors, wipes tables, and picks up trash. Tools: a broom, a mop, a
bucket. How the custodian helps us: our rooms are clean, and nobody slips
on a messy floor.

**School nurse.** The nurse helps when someone is hurt or feels sick at
school. The nurse puts on a bandage, takes a temperature, and calls a
grown-up for help. Tools: bandages, a thermometer. How the nurse helps us:
we feel better, and grown-ups know what to do.

**Bus driver.** The bus driver drives children to school and home again,
safely. Tools: the bus, the stop sign on the bus that tells cars to stop.
How the bus driver helps us: we arrive safely, together.

(Gesture for each helper: point to yourself for "teacher — I teach"; mime
sweeping for "custodian"; pat your own arm for "nurse — I help you feel
better"; turn a pretend steering wheel for "bus driver.")

## Worked example 1 — the teacher, step by step (modeled)

Hold up the teacher card.

> "Who is this? A teacher. What does the teacher do? The teacher teaches
> us — she reads a story and shows us letters. What tool does she use?
> A book. How does that help us? We learn something new. Teacher, book,
> learning — that is one helper, one tool, one way of helping."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** we did not just name the helper. We said the
job, the tool, and the help — three pieces, every time.

## Worked example 2 — the custodian, and a wrong-tool check (modeled)

Hold up the custodian card and the broom.

> "Who is this? The custodian. What does the custodian do? Keeps our school
> clean and safe. What tool? A broom. How does that help us? Clean floors
> mean nobody slips."
>
> "Now — would the custodian use a stethoscope?" (Hold up the bandage as a
> distractor, or mime listening to a heartbeat.) "No! A stethoscope is the
> nurse's and doctor's tool, for listening to bodies. The custodian's tool
> is for cleaning. Tools match jobs."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** a tool is not just "something a helper holds."
It is the thing that lets that helper do that job.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds. Offer choices; never demand a spoken answer.

1. Hold up the nurse card. "Who is this? What does the nurse do? What tool
   do you see?" *(School nurse; helps when hurt or sick at school;
   bandage or thermometer.)*
2. Hold up the bus driver card. "What tool tells cars to stop?" *(The stop
   sign on the bus.)* "How does the bus driver help us?" *(Takes us to
   school and home safely.)*
3. Error-analysis round: the adult announces, very seriously: "The teacher
   sweeps the whole school every morning with a mop, and the custodian
   teaches reading!" Ask: "Is that right? Who does which job?" *(Expected:
   no — mixed up. The custodian cleans; the teacher teaches. Jobs belong
   to the helper who trained for them.)*
4. Tool sort: lay out the book, broom, bandage, and toy bus. "Give the
   teacher her tool. Give the custodian his tool." Continue until all four
   are placed. *(Book → teacher; broom → custodian; bandage → nurse;
   bus → bus driver.)*
5. "Which school helper would you call if you scraped your knee?"
   *(The school nurse — and the nurse calls a grown-up if it is serious.)*
6. If the learner is verbal: "Finish the sentence: the bus driver helps us
   by ___." *(Driving us to school and home safely.)*

## Independent practice

The learner draws one school helper and that helper's tool on a paper
divided in two halves: helper on the left, tool on the right. The adult
labels each half with the dictated words ("custodian" / "broom"). The adult
sits nearby and observes: did the learner pair a helper with a tool that
belongs to that job? When finished, the adult asks, "How does this helper
help us?" and scribes the answer for private storage.

*Alternate response mode:* the learner points to the helper card and then
to the matching toy tool, and mimes the job (sweeping, driving, reading).

## Application

"Tomorrow, notice one school helper at work. Watch quietly, then tell me:
who was it, and what tool were they using?" (In a one-on-one setting, the
learner reports back at the next session.) The learner practices the
three-question routine — job, tool, help — in a real setting.

## Exit check

The adult holds up two cards (for example, nurse and bus driver) and asks
for each: "Who is this? What do they do? What tool do they use?" The
learner should answer at least two of the three questions for each card
(words, pointing, or mime). The adult records: secure / developing /
not yet. A second probe: "Name one more school helper from today."
(Any of the four counts.)

## Supports

- **Language access:** teach each helper by action first, word second. Mime
  the job before saying the job word. Accept home-language words alongside
  English.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing, mime, sorting cards, drawing.
- **Floor-play variant:** lay the four cards on the floor; the learner
  "drives" the toy bus to the bus driver card and "sweeps" the custodian
  card with the toy broom.
- **Seated-table variant:** do the whole lesson at the table with the
  picture cards and toy tools.

## Extension

"Is there a school helper we did not meet today? Who keeps the lunchroom
running? Who helps in the library?" (Previews Lesson 02's neighborhood
helpers and the librarian. Accept any sensible answer — cafeteria worker,
librarian, principal — and add it to the helper chart.)

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) "helpers are only the people I like" — every
job in the school counts, including jobs the learner never sees done
(custodian work after hours); (2) "a tool is any object" — the wrong-tool
check teaches that tools match jobs; (3) "the nurse fixes everything" —
the nurse helps at school and calls a grown-up; serious help comes from
adults and doctors, which Lesson 02 and Lesson 03 build on.

**Answer-key reference:** [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-01) has the
expected responses for the error-analysis round and exit check.
