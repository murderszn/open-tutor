# Lesson 02 — Helpers in Our Neighborhood

**Student goal:** I can name helpers in our neighborhood, say what each one
does, and point to the tool each helper uses in a picture.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U03](../README.md) · **Session 5 of 16**

## Materials

- The generated illustration
  [`assets/community-helpers-street.png`](../assets/community-helpers-street.png)
  (printed large, or shown on a screen the adult controls)
- 4 helper picture cards: firefighter, mail carrier, grocer, doctor — each
  with one tool
- Toy stand-ins: a coiled rope (hose), an envelope and small bag, a basket
  with play apples, a paper-tube "listening tube" (stethoscope stand-in)

**Privacy note for the adult:** this lesson never asks the learner to say or
write their name, address, or where they live. The "neighborhood" in this
lesson is the picture street and the school's neighborhood in general, never
the learner's home address. Restate the Unit 01 safety rule at the start.

## Readiness check (1 minute)

"Yesterday we met school helpers. Show me the sweeping mime — who was that?"
(The custodian.) "Today we walk out the school door, into the neighborhood.
Who else helps out there?" If the learner is ready to name or guess, begin.

## Teaching

A neighborhood is the streets, shops, and homes near each other. Lots of
helpers work there. We ask the same three questions about each one: what
does this helper do, what tool does this helper use, and how does this
helper's work help us?

**Firefighter.** The firefighter puts out fires and teaches fire safety.
Tools: a hose that sprays water, a helmet that protects the head, a big
truck. How the firefighter helps us: keeps people and homes safe from fire.
Fire safety rule for us: if there is a fire, get out, stay out, and tell a
grown-up. Never hide, never go back inside, never touch matches.

**Mail carrier.** The mail carrier collects, sorts, and delivers letters and
packages to homes and shops. Tools: a mailbag, a mail truck, the mailbox.
How the mail carrier helps us: our letters and packages reach the right
people. (Lesson 03 follows one letter all the way.)

**Grocer.** The grocer sells food in a shop. Tools: baskets and shelves full
of food, a scale, a cash register. How the grocer helps us: families can buy
the food they eat.

**Doctor.** The doctor helps sick people feel better and checks that bodies
are healthy. Tools: a stethoscope for listening to a heartbeat and
breathing, a thermometer. How the doctor helps us: we get better, and
grown-ups learn how to keep us healthy.

Then look at the picture street together. Four helpers stand on one street:
a mail carrier, a firefighter, a doctor, and a grocer. Each one wears a
uniform — special clothes for the job — and holds a tool.

## Worked example 1 — reading the picture, helper by helper (modeled)

Point to each figure in turn, left to right, and narrate:

> "First, in blue, the mail carrier. What is he holding? A letter — and a
> mailbag over his shoulder. His job: deliver mail. Second, in yellow, the
> firefighter. What is she holding? A coiled hose. Her job: put out fires.
> Third, in a white coat, the doctor. What is around her neck? A
> stethoscope, for listening. Her job: help sick people feel better.
> Fourth, in a green apron, the grocer. What is she holding? A basket of
> apples. Her job: sell food."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** we did not guess from faces. We read the
uniform and the tool — blue uniform plus letter means mail carrier; yellow
helmet plus hose means firefighter. Tools and uniforms are clues.

## Worked example 2 — one helper, three questions, with a wrong answer (modeled)

> "Look at the grocer. What does the grocer do? Sell food. What tool?
> A basket of apples. How does that help us? We can buy food to eat.
> Now — does the grocer put out fires? No! The grocer sells food; the
> firefighter puts out fires. Each helper has their own job. If we mix up
> the jobs, nobody gets the right help."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** saying what a helper does NOT do is part of
knowing the job. The firefighter does not sell apples, and the grocer does
not spray water on a fire.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds. Offer choices; never demand a spoken answer.

1. Point to the firefighter in the picture. "What tool is she holding?
   What is it for?" *(A hose; it sprays water on a fire.)*
2. Point to the doctor. "What is around her neck? What does she listen
   to?" *(A stethoscope; a heartbeat and breathing.)*
3. Error-analysis round: the adult says, very seriously: "The mail carrier
   puts out fires with a mailbag!" Ask: "Is that right? Who puts out
   fires, and with what?" *(Expected: no — the firefighter puts out fires,
   with a hose. The mail carrier delivers mail.)*
4. "Point to the helper who sells food. What is in her basket?" *(The
   grocer; apples.)*
5. Tool match: lay out the rope-hose, envelope, play apples, and listening
   tube. "Give the firefighter her tool. Give the doctor her tool."
   Continue until all four match. *(Hose → firefighter; envelope →
   mail carrier; apples → grocer; listening tube → doctor.)*
6. Safety round: "If you smell smoke, what do you do?" *(Get out, stay
   out, tell a grown-up. Never hide.)*

## Independent practice

The learner draws one neighborhood helper and that helper's tool (helper on
the left half of the paper, tool on the right, as in Lesson 01). The adult
labels each half with the dictated words. The adult observes: did the
learner pair a helper with a tool that belongs to that job? Then the adult
asks, "How does this helper help us?" and scribes the answer for private
storage.

*Alternate response mode:* the learner points to a figure in the picture
and then to the matching toy tool, and mimes the job (spraying a hose,
carrying a mailbag, listening with the tube).

*Text-only alternative:* if the picture cannot be seen, use this text: "Four
helpers stand on a street. The mail carrier, in blue, holds a letter and a
mailbag. The firefighter, in yellow with a yellow helmet, holds a coiled
hose. The doctor, in a white coat, wears a stethoscope around her neck. The
grocer, in a green apron, holds a basket of apples." Every question in this
lesson can be answered from that text alone.

## Application

"On your next walk with a grown-up, notice one neighborhood helper at work
— from a safe distance, with your grown-up beside you. Tell me at our next
session: who was it, and what tool were they using?" The learner practices
the three-question routine in a real setting, with the safety rule that
helpers are observed, never approached alone.

## Exit check

The adult points to two figures in the picture (for example, the mail
carrier and the grocer) and asks for each: "Who is this? What do they do?
What tool do they use?" The learner should answer at least two of the
three questions for each figure (words, pointing, or mime). The adult
records: secure / developing / not yet. A second probe: "Which helper
would you want if there was a fire?" *(The firefighter.)*

## Supports

- **Language access:** teach each helper by action first, word second. Mime
  spraying a hose, carrying a mailbag, listening with the tube before
  saying the job word. Accept home-language words alongside English.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing, mime, sorting cards, drawing.
- **Floor-play variant:** lay the four cards on the floor as a "street";
  the learner walks a toy figure to each helper's "shop" and hands over the
  right tool.
- **Seated-table variant:** do the whole lesson at the table with the
  printed picture and the toy tools.

## Extension

"Which helper is missing from our picture street? Who helps us cross the
street? Who collects the trash? Who works in the library?" (Crossing guard
with a stop sign; trash collector with a truck; librarian with books.
Previews Lesson 05 and the city helpers in Lesson 03's extension.)

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) "the doctor and the nurse are the same job"
— both help sick people, but the nurse in Lesson 01 works at school and the
doctor works at a clinic or hospital; the jobs overlap but the workplaces
differ; (2) "firefighters only fight fires" — they also teach fire safety
and rescue; keep the fire-safety rule simple and never scary: get out,
tell a grown-up; (3) "helpers are strangers I can go up to" — correct
gently every time: we watch helpers at work with a trusted adult, and we
ask a trusted adult first if we need help.
