# Practice Sessions — Unit 03: Community Helpers

These sessions fill the non-lesson slots in the
[16-session pacing](README.md).
Every task is oral, pointing, drawing, manipulative, or adult-scribed. The
adult reads everything aloud. Answers are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md#practice-sessions).

## Session A — School helper and tool match

**Goal:** match the four school helpers to their tools.

1. Lay out the four school helper cards (teacher, custodian, school nurse,
   bus driver) and the four toy tools (book, broom, bandage, toy bus).
2. The learner matches each tool to its helper, saying the job sentence
   for each: "The teacher teaches with a book." "The custodian sweeps with
   a broom." "The nurse helps with a bandage." "The bus driver drives the
   bus."
3. Mix-up round: the adult deliberately places the bandage on the bus
   driver card. "Is that right? Who uses a bandage?" The learner fixes it
   and explains why. *(No — the nurse uses the bandage; the bus driver
   uses the bus.)*
4. Speed round (gentle, no timer pressure): the adult names a helper; the
   learner points to the tool. Then reverse: the adult holds up a tool;
   the learner names the helper.
5. Draw: the learner draws one school helper with their tool and dictates
   one sentence about how that helper helps. The adult scribes it.

## Session B — Neighborhood helper hunt

**Goal:** find helpers and tools in the generated picture and in a picture
walk.

1. Show the generated illustration
   [`assets/community-helpers-street.png`](assets/community-helpers-street.png).
   "Find the helper holding a letter." "Find the helper holding a hose."
   "Find the helper wearing a stethoscope." "Find the helper holding
   apples." The learner points to each figure.
2. Ask for each figure: "What is this helper's job? How does this helper
   help us?" Accept one-word or gestured answers.
3. Picture walk: the adult shows (or describes) four more "shops" — a
   library, a road being fixed, a street with a trash truck, a crossing.
   "Which helper works here?" *(Librarian; road worker; trash collector;
   crossing guard.)*
4. Odd-one-out: show the firefighter, mail carrier, and grocer cards plus
   the teacher card. "Which one is not a neighborhood helper from Lesson
   02? Where does that helper work instead?" *(The teacher — at school.)*
5. The learner chooses a favorite figure from the picture and draws them
   with their tool. The adult scribes the dictated job sentence.

*Text-only alternative for step 1:* "Four helpers stand on a street. The
mail carrier, in blue, holds a letter and a mailbag. The firefighter, in
yellow with a yellow helmet, holds a coiled hose. The doctor, in a white
coat, wears a stethoscope around her neck. The grocer, in a green apron,
holds a basket of apples." All four "find" prompts can be answered from
that text.

## Session C — Read-aloud: "A Day with Ms. Rosa"

**Goal:** hear a school helper's day as a story, then retell its jobs in
order.

*Original story, written for this unit. The adult reads it aloud with
expression; the learner listens, then draws and retells.*

> Ms. Rosa is the custodian at a school. In the morning, before the
> children arrive, Ms. Rosa sweeps the hallway with her broom. Swish,
> swish. Then she wipes the tables with a cloth so they are clean for
> lunch. At lunchtime, a cup tips over — splat! Ms. Rosa mops it up with
> her mop, and the floor is safe again. In the afternoon, she empties the
> trash bins so the rooms smell fresh. At the end of the day, Ms. Rosa
> looks around the clean school and smiles. "A clean school is a safe
> school," she says. "That is my job, and I am proud of it."

1. Listen: the adult reads the story once, slowly, showing the broom,
   cloth, and mop mime for each tool.
2. Retell in order: "What did Ms. Rosa do first? Next? Last?" The learner
   orders three picture cards: sweeping, wiping tables, emptying trash.
   *(Sweep hallway → wipe tables → mop the spill → empty trash bins;
   any order that keeps "sweep first, empty trash last" counts, with the
   spill placed where the learner heard it — lunchtime.)*
3. Tool talk: "Which tool did Ms. Rosa use for the floor? For the tables?"
   *(Broom and mop for floors; cloth for tables.)*
4. Draw: the learner draws Ms. Rosa with one of her tools and dictates
   one sentence: "Ms. Rosa helps us by ___." *(Keeping the school clean
   and safe.)*
5. Connect: "Is Ms. Rosa a school helper or a neighborhood helper?"
   *(School helper — she works at the school.)*

## Session D — Helper sort game

**Goal:** sort helpers by where they work, and retrieve the Unit 02 rule
idea.

1. Two labeled mats (adult-drawn): a school building and a street with
   shops. The learner sorts eight helper cards onto the mats: teacher,
   custodian, school nurse, bus driver → school; firefighter, mail carrier,
   grocer, doctor → neighborhood. *(The bus driver drives between school
   and homes — school mat counts; accept either mat if the learner
   explains "the bus goes to school.")*
2. Rule retrieval (Unit 02): "Helpers follow rules, too. What rule does a
   bus driver follow?" *(Drive safely; stop at the stop sign; wait for
   children to sit.)* "What rule does a firefighter follow?" *(Wear the
   helmet; stay safe near fire.)*
3. "Who helps in BOTH places?" Trick round — the answer is: nobody in
   this set works in both, but the teacher's and librarian's books appear
   in both places. The learner explains one shared tool if they can.
4. Sort again, faster, from memory. The adult records which helpers were
   placed without help.

## Session E — Pass-the-letter relay

**Goal:** act out the Lesson 03 chain in order, then set up the project.

1. Four stations across the room: mailbox, sorting table, truck, house.
   The learner carries the star envelope from station to station, naming
   the helper at each: "I collect!" "I sort!" "I drive!" "I deliver!"
2. Break-the-chain round: the adult removes the sorting station. "Can the
   letter still arrive? What is missing?" *(No — without sorting, the
   letter does not get to the right town. The chain needs every link.)*
3. Second chain, acted out: the learner plays the school nurse ("I help
   and call a grown-up"), then the doctor ("I check and help you feel
   better"). Two links, in order.
4. Project setup: the learner sketches a thank-you card (drawing only)
   for a school helper of their choice, and chooses which helper station
   they will run at the Helper Fair (Session 11). The card is finished in
   Lesson 05 and delivered in Session 14.

## Session F — Wrong-tool game

**Goal:** catch tool mismatches and explain what would go wrong.

1. The adult lays out six helper cards with tools — three correct, three
   deliberately wrong (firefighter + apples; grocer + hose; mail carrier +
   listening tube; plus three correct pairs of the learner's choice).
2. The learner inspects each pair: "Right tool or wrong tool? What would
   go wrong?" *(Firefighter + apples: apples cannot spray water. Grocer +
   hose: a hose cannot sell food. Mail carrier + listening tube: a tube
   cannot carry letters.)*
3. Fix-it round: the learner swaps the wrong tools to their right helpers
   and says each tool's purpose.
4. Shared-tool round: "Which tool do the teacher and the librarian share?"
   *(A book.)* "Which tool do the nurse and the doctor share?"
   *(A thermometer; also the listening tube for checking bodies.)*
5. Draw: the learner draws one "wrong tool" pair (for example, a
   firefighter with apples) and dictates what would go wrong. Humor is
   welcome; the explanation must name the tool's real purpose.

## Session G — Our helpers in action review

**Goal:** retrieve all six objectives before the formative quiz.

1. Rapid helper round: the adult flashes helper cards; the learner names
   the helper, one tool, and one way the helper helps. Aim for five or
   more helpers named without help.
2. Chain retell: the learner moves the star envelope through the four
   letter-journey stations in order, naming each helper.
3. City helper round: "Name one city helper and what they do for the whole
   city." *(Road worker, trash collector, librarian — any one counts.)*
4. Picture round: using the generated illustration, the learner points to
   each figure, names the helper and tool, and says one thank-you sentence
   for one of them.
5. The adult notes the highest-need objective (the one with the most
   misses) and re-teaches it briefly before the quiz. Then the learner
   takes the [formative quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md).

## The Helper Song (review, Session 12)

**Goal:** sing the unit's helpers, tools, and thanks to a familiar tune
(sung to "The Wheels on the Bus" — public-domain melody).

> The firefighter sprays the hose, sprays the hose, sprays the hose,
> the firefighter sprays the hose, and keeps our town safe.
>
> The mail carrier brings the mail, brings the mail, brings the mail,
> the mail carrier brings the mail, from town to town each day.
>
> The grocer sells the food we eat, food we eat, food we eat,
> the grocer sells the food we eat — thank you, helpers, thank you!
>
> The doctor listens, lub-dub-dub, lub-dub-dub, lub-dub-dub,
> the doctor listens, lub-dub-dub, and helps us feel okay.

Verses may be swapped for any helper in the unit (teacher, custodian,
nurse, bus driver, librarian, crossing guard, trash collector, road
worker) with the learner supplying the job line. The song is a memory aid,
not an assessment: participation, not pitch, is the goal.
