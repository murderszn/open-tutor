# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Rules, Fairness, and Cooperative Choices

**For the guiding adult.** Student materials are in [lessons/](lessons/),
[practice.md](practice.md), [project.md](project.md), and
[assessments/](assessments/). **Keep this guide and the
[answer key](answer-key.md) separate from the learner** — never place a key
beside a student question.

## How to run the unit

- **Session rhythm:** 4 sessions a week, 15–20 minutes each. Short and daily
  beats long and occasional. End while it's still fun.
- **Your role:** model first, then fade. In Week 1 you run the no-rules game
  while the learner watches; by Week 3 the learner runs the vote and the
  Turn-Captain Fair while you observe.
- **Scribe everything** the learner can't write yet. Their job is thinking;
  writing is yours. Date the back of every drawing for private storage.
- **The safety rule is sacred:** "names, addresses, and photos stay private."
  Say it at the start of every session.
- **The timer is the authority, not you.** When turns get tense, point at the
  sand: "What does the fair boss say?" This keeps you as the learner's ally
  instead of the rule enforcer.
- **Every vote must be real.** Never hold a pretend vote whose result you
  override. One overridden vote teaches that voting is theater; the unit's
  D4.8.K-2 goal dies there.
- **Repair is not punishment.** You repair your own real mistakes out loud
  (L5, Worked example 2). If you skip this, the learner learns repairs are
  for children only.

## Misconception table

| What you see | What it means | Response |
|---|---|---|
| "Fair means everyone gets the same thing right now" | Turn-taking not yet understood | "Fair means everyone gets a TURN. The timer decides the order." |
| Grabs the toy and calls it "sharing" | Taking confused with sharing | "Taking is not sharing. Sharing is when the giver chooses to pass." |
| "Rules are only for kids" | Authority asymmetry | Point at yourself: "I follow the curb rule too. Rules are for everyone." |
| "My vote should win because it's mine" | Voting = preference, not procedure | "One person, one vote. Whatever wins, we follow — that's the deal." |
| Changes vote after the count | Choosing vs. counting blurred | "Choosing ends when counting starts. Next vote is your next chance." |
| Fast "sorry!" with no fixing | Repair = evasion (from U01 too) | "Sorry is step 3 of 4. Fix first, then words, then try again." |
| "If nobody saw it, it doesn't count" | Repair serves an audience | "Repairs are for the promise, not for getting caught." |
| "Sorry erases it" | Steps 2 and 4 skipped | "Sorry doesn't un-knock the tower. Fixing and trying again do." |
| Waiting = doing nothing = boring | Waiting as passive suffering | Teach waiting as active: watch, plan your next move, feel the turn coming. |
| "The grown-up's turn doesn't count" | Adult exempt from rules | Pass visibly. Lose votes cheerfully. Repair your own mistakes aloud. |
| Points at the timer as "the boss who is mean" | Timer as punisher | "The timer isn't mean — it's fair. It gives everyone the same time." |
| Anxiety about street safety | Fear taking over | Matter-of-fact and empowering: the rule is the tool; practice the motions. |

## Re-teaching paths

- **Why rules exist (Obj. 1):** replay the no-rules/one-rule tower game for
  a week with different toys. Make the safe/fair sort a daily 2-minute game.
  The test must be automatic before the vote lesson.
- **Following our rules (Obj. 2):** shrink to ONE rule practiced excellently
  for three days (e.g., walking feet everywhere you go together). Catch the
  learner following it and name it: "You followed our rule!"
- **Fair turns (Obj. 3):** drop the coveted toy for a neutral one; shorten
  the timer. "Director mode" (learner directs your hands through pass/wait)
  works when impulse control is the blocker. Celebrate every clean pass like
  a goal.
- **Voting (Obj. 4):** run one silly vote a day for a week — low stakes,
  high reps. Always follow the result. Let the learner lose small votes
  often; graceful losing is a muscle.
- **Rules beyond the classroom (Obj. 5):** take the picture cards on a real
  walk; spot one rule and one helper per outing. Real reps beat drills.
- **Repair (Obj. 6):** never demand a repair as punishment — model it
  yourself on small real moments for a week ("I interrupted — stop, fix,
  rule, try again"). Let the learner watch ten repairs before doing one.

Use the track's flexible diagnostic/catch-up weeks for re-teaching — do not
compress Unit 03 to "catch up." A shaky Unit 02 makes Unit 03 (community
helpers and their rules) wobble: helpers enforce rules the learner must
already understand.

## Readiness for Unit 03

Move on when the learner: states the safe/fair reason for rules without
prompting; takes a timer turn cleanly (pass + calm wait); accepts a lost
vote without refusal; and sequences the four repair steps with the gestures.
"Developing" on one objective is fine — note it and pre-teach in Unit 03's
first retrieval warm-up. "Not yet" on two or more objectives means a
catch-up week first.
