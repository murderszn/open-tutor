# Practice, Story, and Review Sessions — Unit 02

Named sessions referenced in the [16-session pacing](README.md#16-session-pacing-4-weeks--4-sessions-1520-minutes-each).
Each runs 15–20 minutes. The adult reads aloud and scribes; the learner
responds orally, by pointing, drawing, or gesture. The safety rule ("names,
addresses, and photos stay private") opens every session.

---

## Session A — Rule detectives (Practice, Session 2)

**Goal:** spot rules in the real environment; test each with "safe or fair?"

1. Before the session, the adult hides 6 rule picture cards around the room
   (walking feet, quiet voices, clean up toys, gentle hands with blocks, one
   person talks at a time, walking feet by the "pool" — a blue blanket).
2. The learner is the detective: find each card, read the rule with the
   adult, and stamp it "SAFE" or "FAIR" (two stamp pads, or two sorting
   mats). Some cards are both — celebrate double stamps.
3. "Which rule keeps YOU safest?" The learner picks one and shows the rule
   in action (walks the hallway card's rule for real).
4. Plant one "wish" card among them: "everyone must wear purple socks." The
   detective must reject it: "Not a rule — not safe, not fair!"

**Watch for:** the learner stamping everything "safe" — ask "who does this
rule protect?" vs. "who does this rule make things fair for?" to separate
the two ideas.

## Session B — Turn-taking games (Practice, Session 6)

**Goal:** run fair turns fluently with the timer; practice "not yet."

**Game 1 — The coveted toy (three full rotations).** One toy, the sand
timer, two players (learner + adult or puppet). Three complete rotations:
each player gets the toy for one sand run, then passes. The adult narrates
the first rotation, whispers prompts the second, and stays silent the third.
End with: "Did the turn come back around every time?"

**Game 2 — One die, two travelers.** A simple drawn path (10 squares) and
one shared die. Players alternate rolls AND alternate moving a single shared
game piece — "we travel together." If the learner wants their own piece,
pause: "What would be fair with one die?" (Alternate rolls; the piece is
shared; the journey is the game.)

**Cool-down:** "Not yet" drill. The adult asks for the toy three times in
silly voices (a robot, a whisper, a giant). The learner answers "Not yet —
your turn is coming" each time, in a kind voice. Laugh together; the phrase
should feel friendly, not sharp.

**Watch for:** the learner "forgetting" to pass on the third rotation —
return to the fair boss: "What does the timer say?" Never accuse; the timer
is the authority, not the adult.

## Session C — Read-aloud: "Tessa and the One Red Ball" (Story, Session 3)

**Goal:** hear a story where rules save the play; connect it to the learner's
own turn-taking.

*An original story written for this unit. Read aloud with expression; pause at
the ★ marks for the learner to respond.*

---

Tessa loved the playground. She loved the swings and the slide, but most of
all she loved the big red ball. The trouble was, there was only ONE red ball.
★ *"If three children want one ball, what could go wrong?"*

One sunny morning, Tessa ran to the ball first. "Mine!" she shouted, and
hugged it tight. Marco reached for it. "My turn!" Priya reached for it too.
"MY turn!" The ball rolled away, and nobody was playing at all. ★ *"Was that
safe? Was that fair?"*

Just then, their teacher clapped twice — the listening signal. "Friends,"
she said, "we need a rule. What should it be?" Tessa thought hard. "How
about… one turn each! You count to ten, then you PASS." Marco nodded. "And no
grabbing." Priya added, "And the turn comes back around!"

They tried it. Tessa counted to ten — one, two, three… — and passed the ball.
Marco counted to ten and passed. Priya counted to ten and passed. The ball
went round and round, and everyone was laughing. ★ *"What made it fair this
time?"* (One turn each, counting, passing — the rule!)

But then a new problem: Tessa wanted to play kickball, and Marco wanted to
play catch. "We can't play both at once," said Priya. "Let's VOTE." ★ *"What
are the vote steps? Remember: hear the choices, choose, count, follow the
decision."*

They heard the choices: kickball or catch. They chose — one person, one
vote. They counted: two for catch, one for kickball. "Catch wins!" said
Priya. Tessa felt a little sad — she had wanted kickball. But she took a
breath and said, "Okay. Catch it is!" And you know what? Catch was fun too.
★ *"Tessa felt sad AND followed the decision. Have you ever done that?"*

At the end of the afternoon, Tessa hugged the red ball goodbye. "Rules helped
us play all day," she said. "One turn each… and whatever wins, we follow."

---

After the story: the learner draws "the rule that helped the most" — the
count-and-pass rule, the vote, or the no-grabbing rule. (No names on the
drawing.) "Tell me: what would have happened with no rules?" The adult
scribes one sentence on the back for private storage.

## Session D — Rule sort game (Review, Session 4)

**Goal:** distinguish real rules (safe/fair) from wishes; retrieve Unit 01.

1. Lay out 8 cards: 5 real rules (walking feet, one person talks at a time,
   clean up toys, gentle hands, stop-look-hold-hands at the curb) and 3
   wishes ("everyone must wear purple socks," "lunch must be pizza,"
   "bedtime is canceled"). The learner sorts into "REAL RULE" and "WISH"
   piles, testing each: "Safe or fair?"
2. For each real rule, the learner shows it in action (walks, mimes
   listening, pretends to clean up). Rules live in bodies, not just words.
3. **Unit 01 retrieval:** "Our kindness tools — show me all three." (Listening
   ears, gentle hands, kind words.) "Which kindness tool helps us follow
   rules?" (All three — listening hears the rule, gentle hands follow it,
   kind words repair it.)
4. Exit: "Tell me one rule and why it exists — safe or fair?" (Re-check of
   L1.)

**Watch for:** the learner calling a wish "a rule because I like it" — return
to the test: "Rules are about safe and fair, not about likes."

## Session E — Practice votes (Practice, Session 8)

**Goal:** run the four vote steps fluently; set up the project rule poster.

1. Three quick silly votes, full four steps each, learner leading more each
   time: "Which puppet is sillier — Pip or Squeaks?" / "Should we clap fast
   or stomp slow to end today?" / "Red block tower or blue block tower?"
2. Tie drill: engineer a 1–1 tie (adult + learner disagree). "It's a tie!
   What do we do?" Practice the tie rule: vote again, or the grown-up picks
   this time and we vote fresh next time.
3. Graceful-loss drill: the adult deliberately "loses" a vote and models the
   words: "I wanted the other one. I feel a little sad. But our class
   chose this — so we follow it." The learner practices the same sentence
   after losing one silly vote.
4. **Project setup:** sketch the class rule poster together — a large paper
   divided into 4 boxes (one per future rule). The learner draws a border
   and picks the poster colors. (The rules themselves are co-created in
   Session 11.)

**Watch for:** the learner changing their vote after the count — "Choosing
ends when counting starts. Next vote is your next chance."

## Session F — Neighborhood rule puppets (Practice, Session 10)

**Goal:** apply "safe or fair" beyond the classroom; meet the rule helpers.

Set up: the street-and-curb scene (strip of paper, book curb, toy car),
three helper cards (crossing guard, teacher, lifeguard), two puppets.

1. Scenario 1: a puppet runs toward the street without stopping. Freeze!
   The learner directs the repair: "Stop at the curb! Look both ways! Hold
   a hand!" Act the safe crossing.
2. Scenario 2: at the "park" (a green blanket), a puppet lets its (toy) dog
   run wild. "Which rule? Who is the helper here?" (Dogs on leashes; the
   rule keeps everyone safe — and fair, since the park belongs to all.)
3. Scenario 3: puppets finish playing and leave toys everywhere. "Whose rule
   is this? Safe or fair?" (Clean up — fair: the next children find it
   ready. Connect to Unit 01 helper jobs: the class runs on everyone.)
4. The learner invents Scenario 4: picks a rule card, stages the break, and
   directs the puppet repair. (Creativity + mastery.)
5. Helper salute: the learner picks a helper card and says, "Thank you,
   crossing guard, for keeping our crossing safe!" (Gratitude as civic
   practice — previews Unit 03's community helpers.)

**Watch for:** fear-based language about streets ("cars will kill you") —
redirect to the empowering frame: "The rule is our tool. Stop, look, hold
hands — then we are safe."

## Session G — Our rules in action review (Review, Session 15)

**Goal:** consolidate all six objectives through role-play; preview the quiz.

1. **Rule role-play stations** (3 minutes each): the learner rotates through
   three puppet scenarios and must (a) name the rule, (b) follow or repair
   it: the grabbed timer, the shouted-over circle, the un-cleaned blocks.
2. **Vote re-check:** hold a final silly vote with full steps, learner-led
   start to finish.
3. **Picture re-read:** show `fair-turns.png` again — "Find one NEW fair
   thing you didn't notice before." (Deepens observation; acceptable: the
   waiting child's calm face, each child's own stack, the middle pile shared.)
4. **Quiz preview:** "Next we do show-me time — like Unit 01. Same deal:
   speak, point, draw, or gesture. Stop anytime." (Lowers threat; the
   [formative quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md) follows.)
5. Exit: the learner teaches the four repair steps to a puppet, start to
   finish, with gestures. (Teaching = mastery check.)

**Watch for:** fatigue this late in the unit — keep stations snappy, end
while it's fun, and split the quiz to another day if needed.
