# Answer Key — Unit 02: Rules, Fairness, and Cooperative Choices

**For the guiding adult only.** Never place this beside a student question.
Each entry gives the expected response, acceptable variations, and what to do
when the learner's answer differs.

---

## Lesson 01

**Guided practice, round 3** ("Everyone must eat pickles for lunch!"):
Expected — "No, that's not a real rule. It doesn't keep anyone safe or make
anything fair." Accept: "That's a wish," "No — rules are safe and fair,"
or the learner sorting it to a "wishes" pile. If the learner says "but I
like pickles": "Liking it doesn't make it a rule. Test it: safe or fair?"

**Guided practice, round 5** (puppet: "Rules are only for little kids"):
Expected — "No — grown-ups follow rules too." Accept any example: stop
signs, quiet voices in the library, the curb rule. If the learner agrees
with the puppet, give one example and ask: "Do I stop at red lights? Then
rules are for me too."

**Exit check**, part 1 ("Why do we need rules?"):
Secure — states both reasons in child words: rules keep people safe AND keep
things fair (words, the shield + scale gestures, or pointing to both sides of
their drawing). Developing — names one reason, or needs the gesture prompt
to produce the second. Not yet — cannot name either after two tries (replay
the no-rules/one-rule game tomorrow).

**Exit check**, part 2 ("Is 'everyone must wear blue socks' a real rule?"):
Secure — "No — it's not safe or fair, it's a wish." Developing — "No" without
the reason (prompt: "Test it — safe or fair?"). Not yet — "Yes" (re-teach
the safe/fair test with two clear examples).

## Lesson 02

**Picture questions** (using `assets/fair-turns.png`):
- "Point to the child who is passing the turn. What are they passing?" —
  the middle child with the glasses and brown ponytail, handing the
  hourglass sand timer to the child on the left. Accept the learner pointing
  anywhere on that figure; the passed object is the sand timer.
- "What does the timer passing mean?" — the turn is changing hands; it is
  the next child's turn now. Accept: "It's the other kid's turn," "The turn
  passes."
- "Point to the child who is waiting fairly. How can you tell?" — the child
  on the right (black pigtails, pink shirt): hands folded, smiling calmly.
  Accept any one true observation: folded hands, calm face, not grabbing.
- "Is everyone getting to play?" — yes: each child has their own small stack
  of blocks. Accept pointing to the three stacks.
- Text-only alternative (for use without the picture): "Three children sit
  on a round rug with wooden blocks. The middle child, wearing glasses,
  hands a sand timer to the child on the left — that is how the turn passes.
  The child on the right waits with hands folded and a smile — that is
  waiting fairly. Each child has their own small stack of blocks."

**Guided practice, round 3** (adult grabs the toy: "My turn is more
important!"):
Expected — "Not fair! Nobody's turn is more important. Give it back / pass
it." Accept: "The timer decides," or the learner physically returning the
toy to the turn-holder. If the learner laughs and allows it: freeze and ask,
"Who decides whose turn it is?" (The timer.)

**Exit check**, part 1 ("Show me a fair turn with the timer"):
Secure — plays for one sand run, passes when the sand runs out without
grabbing or stalling, shows a calm waiting pose. Developing — passes but
needs one reminder, or waiting pose needs coaching. Not yet — refuses to
pass or grabs (return to Practice B Game 1 with a neutral toy).

**Exit check**, part 2 ("What does 'not yet' mean?"):
Secure — "My turn is coming" / "Not no, just wait." Developing — "Wait"
(prompt: "And then what happens?" → "My turn comes"). Not yet — "No" (re-teach:
"Not yet is a promise, not a no.").

## Lesson 03

**Guided practice, round 3** (puppet votes twice):
Expected — "That's cheating — one person, one vote! Take one back."
Accept: "Not fair, you voted two times," with the learner removing the extra
token and re-counting. If the learner doesn't notice, point: "Count the
tokens. Count the voters. Do they match?"

**Guided practice, round 4** (tie):
Expected procedure — acknowledge the tie, then either re-vote or apply the
agreed tie rule (grown-up picks this time, fresh vote next time). Accept
either as long as it is stated BEFORE acting and followed.

**Guided practice, round 5** (puppet refuses the losing song):
Expected — "You have to follow the decision. You can feel sad AND sing."
Accept the learner modeling the graceful-loss sentence from Worked example 2.
If the learner sides with the puppet ("let's just do what Pip wants"): "Then
why did we vote? The deal is: whatever wins, we follow."

**Exit check**, part 1 (four vote steps):
Secure — names or gestures all four in order: hear the choices, choose,
count, follow the decision. Developing — three of four, or needs the gesture
prompts. Not yet — fewer than three (run one silly vote a day for a week).

**Exit check**, part 2 ("What do we do if our choice loses?"):
Secure — "Follow the decision" (feelings allowed: "I can feel sad and
follow it"). Developing — "Follow it" without the feelings nuance (acceptable;
add: "And how may you feel inside?"). Not yet — "Vote again" or "Don't do
it" (re-teach the deal with the puppet's graceful loss).

## Lesson 04

**Guided practice, round 4** ("Everyone must splash as much as possible!"):
Expected — "No — that's not safe or fair. The real pool rule is about
staying safe in the water." Accept any true pool rule: walk don't run, a
grown-up stays with you, no pushing. If the learner thinks splashing IS the
rule: "Test it: does more splashing keep anyone safe? Does it keep things
fair for the calm swimmers?"

**Guided practice, round 5** (puppet: "I don't need the curb rule — I'm
fast!"):
Expected — "The rule is for everyone. Fast doesn't make cars slower. Stop,
look, hold hands." Accept any version containing: everyone follows it + the
three actions. If the learner agrees with the puppet: act out Scene 1 again
(no-rule crossing) and ask "was that safe because the figure was fast?"

**Exit check** (one school rule + one neighborhood rule, each with safe/fair):
Secure — names one of each AND gives a correct safe/fair reason for both
(e.g., "walking feet — safe, so nobody crashes"; "curb rule — safe, cars are
fast"). Developing — names both rules but reasons need prompting. Not yet —
cannot name one of each (take the cards on a real walk tomorrow; real reps).

## Lesson 05

**Guided practice, round 3** (puppet's fast "SORRY!" + grabs the toy):
Expected — "No — that wasn't a repair. The puppet skipped fixing (step 2)
and trying again (step 4). A fast sorry is just noise." Accept: "They didn't
fix it," "They skipped steps," with the learner naming at least one skipped
step. If the learner accepts the fast sorry: "Watch: did the puppet fix
anything? Did the puppet try again the rule way? Then was it a repair?"

**Guided practice, round 4** ("What if you CAN'T fix it?"):
Expected — "Fix what you CAN — pick up what's there, say the rule, say
sorry, try again with what's left. The repair still counts." Accept any
version of: do the fixable part + complete all four steps anyway.

**Guided practice, round 5** (puppet broke a rule unseen):
Expected — "Yes — repair anyway. Repairs are for the promise, not for
getting caught." Accept: "Yes, fix it and do better today." If the learner
says no: "Who was the rule protecting, even with nobody watching?"

**Exit check**, part 1 (four repair steps with puppets):
Secure — sequences all four in order with gestures: stop, fix what you can,
say the rule / say sorry and mean it, try again. Developing — four steps
with one prompt or out of order (accept out-of-order once, then re-sequence
together). Not yet — fewer than three steps (practice the gesture chain as
a chant for a week).

**Exit check**, part 2 ("Is a repair a punishment?"):
Secure — "No — it's how we keep the promise." Developing — "No" (prompt:
"No, it's how we keep the…?" → "promise"). Not yet — "Yes" (re-teach with
your own modeled repair: "Was I in trouble when I repaired talking over
you? No — I was keeping our promise.").

## Practice sessions

**Session A (Rule detectives):** the wish card ("purple socks") must be
rejected with the safe/fair test. If the learner stamps it as a rule, ask:
"Who does purple socks keep safe? Who does it make things fair for?" —
silence is the answer; move it to "not a rule."

**Session B (Turn-taking games):** Game 1 passes on clean rotation 3
(timer authority, no grabbing). Game 2's shared piece: if the learner
insists on separate pieces, allow it once, then ask "was that more fair or
less fair than traveling together?" — let the experience teach.

**Session C (Story):** ★ responses — accept any reasonable answer; the
targets: one ball + three children → grabbing/no play (unsafe, unfair);
the fix → count-and-pass rule (fair); the vote steps in order; Tessa's
sad-AND-following as graceful losing. The drawing: any of the three rules
depicted; the scribed sentence should name what would happen with no rules
("nobody would get to play").

**Session D (Rule sort):** the three wishes must land in the WISH pile with
the safe/fair test applied. U01 retrieval: all three kindness tools shown;
acceptable answer to "which tool helps us follow rules?" — all three, with
any reasonable mapping (listening hears, hands follow, words repair).

**Session E (Practice votes):** every vote follows the four steps; the tie
drill resolves by re-vote or the stated tie rule; the learner practices the
graceful-loss sentence aloud at least once.

**Session F (Neighborhood puppets):** Scenario 1 — stop/look/hold-hands
sequenced. Scenario 2 — leash rule named (safe + fair). Scenario 3 — cleanup
rule named (fair; Unit 01 helper-job link accepted as bonus). Scenario 4
(learner-invented) — any rule staged and repaired counts.

**Session G (Review):** the three role-play stations each resolve with the
rule named AND followed/repaired. The picture re-read should surface at
least one new observation beyond Lesson 02's. The puppet-taught repair steps
must include all four, in order, with gestures.

## Project — Rule Book and Turn-Captain Fair

**Part 1:** the 3–4 rules must be in the learner's words (clumsy wording
accepted and celebrated; the adult may tidy grammar only with the learner's
approval). Each rule needs a safe/fair test passed. The rule book page:
drawing shows the rule being followed (Secure), the action (Developing), or
unrelated (Emerging); scribed words must match the learner's dictation
verbatim.

**Part 2:** the Fair log needs three rotation entries; honest entries about
wobbles ("Pip kept the toy. I said 'not yet'") score HIGHER than suspiciously
perfect ones — honesty is the skill. The report frame: rules / captain
actions / hardest part / what-if-no-rules. The "what if no rules?" answer
must name a real consequence (grabbing, no turns, nobody has fun, someone
could get hurt).

**Rubric scoring:** 10–12 Secure · 7–9 Developing · 4–6 Emerging. Never share
numbers with the learner — only descriptive feedback.

## Formative quiz

**Q1** — Secure: both reasons (safe + fair) in any mode. Developing: one
reason. Not yet: neither after two tries.
**Q2** — "Walking feet": real rule, safe. "One person talks": real rule,
fair. "Purple socks": wish — neither. Score each of the three; 3/3 = Secure,
2/3 = Developing.
**Q3** — Secure: names all 3–4 rules unprompted (own words accepted).
Developing: names 2+ with the poster as prompt. Not yet: fewer than 2.
**Q4** — Secure: clean timer turn (play, pass on sand-out, calm wait) + "not
yet" = "your turn is coming." Developing: needs one reminder. Not yet:
refuses to pass.
**Q5** — Passing child: middle child with glasses handing the sand timer to
the left child; passing = the turn changing hands. Waiting child: right
child — folded hands, calm smile. Accept pointing anywhere on the correct
figures. Text alternative may be read aloud instead.
**Q6** — Four steps in order (words or gestures). Loss response: "follow the
decision" (feelings allowed). Secure: all four + graceful-loss answer.
Developing: three steps or needs the gesture chain. Not yet: fewer.
**Q7** — One school + one neighborhood rule, each with a correct safe/fair
reason. Secure: both with reasons. Developing: both rules, one reason.
Not yet: cannot name one of each.
**Q8** — Secure: all four repair steps sequenced with the puppet (stop, fix,
say rule/sorry, try again). Developing: three steps or one prompt. Not yet:
fewer.

**Overall:** 6+ Secure = on track · 3–5 = re-teach Developing objectives in
Session 16 · <3 = catch-up week before Unit 03.

## Unit assessment

**Task 1** — Each of the four rules: safe/fair + a WHY (Secure: all four
with reasons; Developing: sorting right, reasons need prompting). The wish:
rejected BECAUSE it is neither safe nor fair (not merely "I don't like
pickles").
**Task 2** — Secure: three clean rotations + kind, firm rule-holding when
the puppet tests ("not yet," timer restated, turn recovered). Developing:
rule held with one adult prompt. Emerging: gave in to the puppet or grabbed
back. Not yet: no rule-holding.
**Task 3** — (1) Timer-passer identified + meaning stated. (2) Waiting child
identified with body evidence. (3) Fairness explained FROM the image (turns
for everyone / everyone has blocks / calm waiting). Secure: all three with
evidence. Developing: two of three. Emerging: one.
**Task 4** — Secure: four steps run honestly AND the engineered loss accepted
gracefully (follows the winning choice, no re-vote demand, no refusal).
Developing: steps complete but loss accepted only with the graceful-loss
sentence prompted. Emerging: re-vote demanded or refusal. (Re-run with swapped
puppet votes if the learner wins the first round — the loss must be
practiced.)
**Task 5** — Secure: broken rule named in child words + all four repair steps
sequenced + one school and one neighborhood rule each with a correct safe/fair
reason. Developing: repair complete, rules named but one reason missing.
Emerging: partial repair or rules without reasons.

**Totals:** 13–15 Secure · 9–12 Developing · 5–8 Emerging · 0–4 Not yet, with
the next steps in the assessment file. This assessment is teacher-made, not
standardized or validated.
