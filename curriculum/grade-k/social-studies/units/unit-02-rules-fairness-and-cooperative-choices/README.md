# Unit 02 — Rules, Fairness, and Cooperative Choices

**Grade:** Kindergarten · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #9](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/9), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Rules tell us what to do so everyone stays safe and things stay fair. In this
unit the learner discovers the two reasons rules exist — **safety** and
**fairness** — co-creates the class's own rules in child words, practices fair
turns and sharing with a sand timer, and runs the year's first class vote:
hearing the choices, choosing, and accepting the group's decision. Then rules
leave the classroom: school rules, neighborhood rules, and what to do when a
rule gets broken — stop, fix what can be fixed, say the rule again, and try
again.

**Privacy by design:** rule work at this age must not collect personal
information. The class rule book carries drawings and rule pictures, never
names. The fair-turn log records actions ("we took 3 turns"), never who
"misbehaved." The adult keeps all records in private storage.

## Essential question

Why do we need rules — and who gets to help make them?

## Prerequisites

- Unit 01: identity, belonging, and classroom community (classroom routines
  and helper roles are referenced in Lessons 1 and 5; the safety rule "names,
  addresses, and photos stay private" is restated every session)
- Can sit for a 5–10 minute circle time with movement breaks
- Follows one-step directions; two-step directions emerging
- No reading or writing required. The adult reads everything aloud and scribes.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Explain in child words why rules exist: to keep people **safe** and to keep
   things **fair**.
2. Name the class's 3–4 co-created rules and follow them during play and
   routines (adult reminders allowed).
3. Take fair turns and share limited materials: wait, pass the turn, and accept
   "not yet" without grabbing or pushing.
4. Take part in a class vote: listen to the choices, make a choice, and accept
   the group's decision even when it is not their pick.
5. Name one rule from school and one rule from the neighborhood or community,
   and say what each rule keeps safe or fair.
6. When a rule is broken, name a repair step: stop, fix what can be fixed, say
   the rule again (or say sorry and mean it), and try again.

## Vocabulary (taught with pictures and actions first, word second)

rule · safe · fair · turn · wait / waiting · share · pass · timer · vote ·
choice · decide / decision · agree · repair · rule book

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md#8-safe-materials) for the full
safe-materials list): large paper, crayons or markers, picture cards (cut from
magazines or drawn), two puppets or stuffed animals for scenarios, a sand
timer or simple timer (about one minute), one "coveted" toy for turn practice
(a single coveted item the learner likes), wooden blocks, voting tokens (two
small baskets and one token per choice, or picture ballots), the generated
illustration [`assets/fair-turns.png`](assets/fair-turns.png). No sharp tools;
props checked for choking hazards; no food allergens used as counters or props
without checking.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, drawing, or gesture responses; the adult
scribes. Each lesson includes a seated-table and a floor-play variant, a
language-access support, an alternate response mode, and an extension. The
timer for turn-taking is visible (sand timer) as well as timed — no color-only
cues. Voting uses picture ballots or token baskets so no reading is needed.
The generated illustration ships with a text-only alternative carrying every
action the activity asks about.
