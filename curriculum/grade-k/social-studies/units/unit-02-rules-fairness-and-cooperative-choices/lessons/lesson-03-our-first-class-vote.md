# Lesson 03 — Our First Class Vote

**Student goal:** I can take part in a vote: hear the choices, choose, and
follow the group's decision.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U02](../README.md) · **Session 7 of 16**

## Materials

- Two small baskets (or two marked spots on the table) and one token per
  voter — or picture ballots the learner can point to
- Two real choices the class will actually follow (examples: which song to
  sing at the end of the session; which of two picture books to read
  tomorrow; which color paper to use for the rule poster)
- A puppet or stuffed animal "voter"

**Privacy note for the adult:** restate the safety rule at the start:
"Names, addresses, and photos stay private." Votes are counted, never tied
to names — a token in a basket carries no name.

## Readiness check (1 minute)

"Yesterday we learned fair turns. But what if the WHOLE class has to decide
ONE thing — like which song we sing? We can't all sing different songs at
once. Today we learn the fairest way for a group to decide: a vote." If the
learner is curious, begin.

## Teaching

A **vote** is how a group makes a **decision** together — fairly. Here are
the four vote steps. Say them with me, with a gesture for each:

1. **Hear the choices.** (Hands cupped behind ears.) Everyone listens to
   what we are choosing between. No choosing yet — just listening.
2. **Choose.** (One finger up.) Each person picks ONE. One person, one vote —
   you cannot vote twice, and nobody can vote for you.
3. **Count.** (Point and count together.) We count the votes out loud, where
   everyone can see.
4. **Follow the decision.** (Hands together, then open.) The choice with the
   most votes wins. Everyone follows it — even people who chose the other
   one. You can feel sad AND follow the decision at the same time.

**Agree** means the group goes with the decision together. We do not have to
all feel the same inside — we agree to follow the same decision outside.

## Worked example 1 — the year's first real vote (modeled)

Pick a real, low-stakes choice the class will truly follow — for example,
"Which song do we sing to end today: the Belonging Song (Unit 01) or the new
Fair Rules Song?" Present both choices clearly, one at a time.

> "Step 1: hear the choices. Choice A: the Belonging Song. Choice B: the Fair
> Rules Song. I will sing one line of each so you can hear them." *(Sing or
> chant one line of each.)*
>
> "Step 2: choose. I choose… the Fair Rules Song." The adult drops a token in
> the B basket. "Now you. Remember — one person, one vote." The learner
> votes. The puppet votes too (let the learner decide the puppet's vote, or
> have it choose the opposite to set up step 4).
>
> "Step 3: count." Count the tokens together, out loud, pointing at each one.
> "Two for the Fair Rules Song, one for the Belonging Song. The Fair Rules
> Song wins."
>
> "Step 4: follow the decision. We all sing the Fair Rules Song — even the
> puppet, who chose the other one. Watch." The puppet sings along gamely.

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the vote was fair because everyone heard the
same choices, everyone got one vote, and everyone could see the count. And
the decision is real — we actually sing the winning song.

## Worked example 2 — losing gracefully (modeled)

The puppet "loses" the vote above (or in a replay where the learner wins).

> The puppet droops. "I wanted the Belonging Song. I feel sad."
>
> The adult: "It is okay to feel sad. Watch what the puppet does next." The
> puppet takes a breath, sits up, and sings the winning song with the group
> — a little quietly at first, then fully.
>
> "See? The puppet felt sad AND followed the decision. Both can be true. The
> puppet's turn to choose will come — next vote, maybe the Belonging Song
> wins."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** a vote only works if losers accept the result.
That is the deal we all make in step 1: "whatever wins, we follow." This is
the hardest part of voting, and the most important.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds. Keep every vote real — the class follows each result.

1. Vote: "Which puppet runs the next round — Pip or Squeaks?" Full four
   steps, with the learner leading the count.
2. Vote: "Blocks or drawing for our 3-minute free choice?" Follow the result
   immediately (set the timer for 3 minutes of the winner).
3. Error-analysis round: the puppet drops TWO tokens in a basket and
   whispers, "Nobody saw." Freeze! "What did the puppet do? Why is it
   unfair?" *(Expected: voted twice — one person, one vote. Take one token
   back and re-count.)*
4. "What if it is a tie?" (With two voters this happens.) Teach the tie
   rule: "If it is a tie, we vote again — or the grown-up picks this time
   and we vote fresh next time." Practice a re-vote once.
5. Puppet round: the puppet loses a vote and shouts, "I am NOT singing that
   song!" Ask: "What should the puppet remember?" *(Expected: step 4 — we
   follow the decision, even when we feel sad. Feelings are allowed;
   refusing is not.)*
6. If the learner is verbal: "Tell me the four vote steps." *(Hear the
   choices, choose, count, follow the decision.)*

## Independent practice

The learner runs a full vote alone for the puppet audience: the learner
presents two choices (e.g., "red crayon or blue crayon for the rule poster
border"), collects the puppets' votes (the adult voices them, one per
puppet, no coaching), counts aloud, announces the winner, and enforces step
4 — including on a puppet that "loses." The adult observes: did the learner
keep one-person-one-vote? Count honestly? Hold the loser to the decision
kindly? The adult scribes one dictated sentence about the result for private
storage.

*Alternate response mode:* picture ballots — the learner points to the
chosen picture instead of using tokens; or the learner directs the adult's
hands through the four steps.

## Application

"At home, run a family vote: two choices for tonight's bedtime story, or
two choices for a weekend walk route. You are the vote leader — all four
steps." The learner practices the full procedure in a real setting and
reports the result next session.

## Exit check

The adult asks: "Show me the four vote steps." The learner should name or
gesture all four (hear choices, choose, count, follow the decision). Then:
"What do we do if our choice loses?" (Follow the decision — we can feel sad
and follow it.) The adult records: secure / developing / not yet, for both
parts.

## Supports

- **Language access:** teach "vote," "choice," and "decision" by doing first,
  naming second. The four gestures carry the sequence. Accept home-language
  words alongside English.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing to picture ballots, token baskets,
  directing the adult's hands.
- **Floor-play variant:** lay the two choice cards on the floor far apart;
  the learner "walks their vote" to a card and stands on it; count by
  walking the line of voters.
- **Shy learners:** let the learner vote by whispering the choice to a
  puppet, which drops the token; losing gracefully can be practiced through
  the puppet first.

## Extension

"Our class has THREE choices next time. How would we vote with three?"
(Previews ranked/multiple-choice thinking: still one person one vote; the
most votes wins; ties re-vote. Do not formalize — just play it.)

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) "voting means my choice should win" — the
puppet's graceful loss is the antidote; repeat the deal: "whatever wins, we
follow"; (2) "I can change my vote after the count" — no: choosing ends when
counting starts; (3) "the grown-up's vote counts double" — it does not; the
adult votes once, visibly, and loses cheerfully when it happens; (4) "a tie
means nobody wins and we do nothing" — teach the tie rule explicitly.
