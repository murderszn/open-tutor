# Answer Key — Unit 01: Identity, Belonging, and Classroom Community

**For the guiding adult only.** Never place this beside a student question.
Each entry gives the expected response, acceptable variations, and what to do
when the learner's answer differs.

---

## Lesson 01

**Error-analysis round** ("My favorite color is… my address!"):
Expected — "No, that breaks the safety rule. Say a favorite or a talent
instead." Accept: "No — addresses stay private," "Wrong — say a favorite,"
or the learner pointing to the "stays private" pile. If the learner laughs
but can't articulate: ask "Safe to share, or stays private?" and accept the
sort.

**Exit check**, part 1 ("Show me one thing that makes you unique"):
Secure — the learner names, draws attention to, or gestures a specific
favorite/talent/interest of their own (not copied from the adult's example).
Developing — copies the adult's favorite but can name a different one with
prompting. Not yet — no response or only repeats the adult's words.

**Exit check**, part 2 ("What is our safety rule?"):
Secure — indicates names/addresses/photos stay private (words, nod + point to
the no-name symbol, or sorts correctly). Developing — gets it right after the
adult starts the sentence ("Names stay…?"). Not yet — offers a name or
address as an answer (redirect warmly; re-teach tomorrow).

## Lesson 02

**Picture questions** (using `assets/belonging-circle.png`):
- "Point to someone giving a greeting" — the child in the blue hat with the
  sun shirt, hand raised in a wave, smiling toward the newcomer. Accept the
  learner pointing anywhere on that figure.
- "Point to the children who are sharing" — the two children with the
  building blocks (one handing a green block to the other; an orange block
  held nearby). Accept either figure.
- "How can you tell they feel welcome?" — smiles; the wave; the open space in
  the circle; the newcomer walking in. Accept any one true observation.
- Text-only alternative (for use without the picture): "Seven children sit in
  a circle on a colorful rug. A child in a blue hat waves hello to a newcomer
  with a backpack who is joining the circle. Two children share building
  blocks. A child in a wheelchair smiles with the group. A child with round
  glasses sits calmly with folded hands, listening. Every face is smiling."

**Error-analysis round** (adult mumbles "hi" at the floor, turned away):
Expected — "No — no eyes, no kind face. The words alone aren't enough."
Accept: "You didn't look," "No smile," or the learner demonstrating the
correct three-part greeting as the correction.

**Exit check**: "Point to one way these children help everyone belong" —
accept the wave/hello, the block-sharing, the open space for the newcomer, or
the listening child. "Show me a greeting with all three parts" — eyes toward
the adult, kind face, hello words or wave. All three present = Secure; two =
Developing; one or none = Not yet.

## Lesson 03

**Job descriptions** (accept words, gesture, or mime):
- Line leader — "walks first and keeps everyone together" / "shows the way."
- Door holder — "holds the door so everyone gets through safe."
- Materials keeper — "keeps the crayons/baskets ready."
- Plant waterer — "gives the plant a drink so it stays alive."
- Clean-up captain — "tidies the room so it's ready for tomorrow."

**"How did your job help?"** — accept any true causal statement: "The
crayons were ready when we drew" / "Nobody got lost" / "The plant is alive."
Sentence frame may be needed: "My job helped because ___."

**Error-analysis round** (adult runs ahead as line leader):
Expected — "No — a line leader stays where everyone can see and keeps the
group together." Accept: "You went too far," "Come back," or the learner
physically guiding the adult back to the front of an imaginary line.

**Exit check**: "Is the clean-up captain's job important? Why?" — Secure:
"Yes, because ___" with a true reason (the room is ready / everyone can find
things / it's fair that everyone helps). Developing: "Yes" with prompting for
the reason. Not yet: "No" or "only the leader matters" (re-teach the big
idea: the class runs on everyone).

## Lesson 04

**Tool check** — Secure: all three gestures (cup ears / fold hands /
hands-to-heart-then-out). Accept the learner naming instead of gesturing.

**Puppet scenario** ("I can't do this puzzle"):
Expected kind-words response — "Are you okay? I will help you" or "Can I
help?" or "Want to do it together?" Accept any offer of help in kind words.
Not acceptable: taking the puzzle away to "fix" it alone (that's not
listening first).

**Gentle-hands practice** — Secure: slow, soft stroking; can say "soft and
slow" when asked how they knew it was gentle.

**Error-analysis round** (Puppet B shouts "GIVE ME THAT!" and snatches):
Expected — "Puppet B forgot all three tools. Puppet B could say 'Can I play
too?' with gentle hands." Accept identification of any two missing tools plus
one kind-words alternative.

**Exit check**: "Your friend is sad. Which tool first, and what do you say?"
— Secure: listening ears + "Are you okay?" (or equivalent). Developing: right
tools in any order, or kind words without naming the tool. Not yet: "I don't
know" after two tries, or an unkind response (re-teach with the puppets).

## Lesson 05

**Guided practice scenario** (Puppet A grabs Puppet B's crayon) — walk all
four steps: 1) stop/freeze + breathe, 2) "I grabbed your crayon," 3) "I am
sorry I grabbed your crayon" (slow, eye contact or oriented toward), 4) "Next
time I will ask — 'Can I use it after you?'" Secure: all four, in order,
without rushing. Developing: all four with icon-card prompting. Not yet:
skips to a fast "sorry" (re-model; never accept the rushed version as done).

**"What is a good new plan for grabbing?"** — "Ask first" / "Use kind words:
can I play too?" / "Wait for a turn." Any plan that prevents the grab.

**Error-analysis round** (adult does a rushed "SORRY!"):
Expected — "No — that wasn't a real repair. It was too fast, no eye contact,
and there was no plan." Accept: "Too fast," "You didn't say what happened,"
"You need a plan."

**Exit check**: "Tell me the four repair steps" — Secure: stop, say what
happened, say sorry (and mean it), make a new plan — any order accepted if
all four are named; icon cards or gestures count. "You took a toy, your
friend is sad — what do you say?" — Secure: includes stopping, naming ("I
took your toy"), sorry, and a plan ("Next time I will ask").

## Practice sessions

- **A (favorite cards):** no wrong favorites. Key decision — "Can you be
  friends with someone who loves a card you don't?" Expected: yes.
- **B (greeting pairs):** the "shy visitor" adaptation — softer voice, wave
  first, wait. The "other language" adaptation — wave + smile works across
  languages.
- **C (Milo's First Morning):** ★ responses — tummy butterflies (any gesture
  of nervousness); the teacher's three greeting parts (eyes, kind face,
  hello); the girl "made room / patted the spot / invited him"; Milo feels
  happy/proud/brave (any positive feeling shown on the face).
- **D (favorites game):** safety sort — "favorite food" and "talent" go to
  safe-to-share; "my name," "my address," "my photo" go to stays-private.
- **E (puppet scenarios):** Scenario 2 fix — "You can play the next round" /
  "Here is a spot" / "Come play!" Scenario 3 (shy) — gentle voice, wave
  first, sit nearby, wait.
- **F (kindness detectives):** any true observed kind action counts as a
  "case." If the learner reports none, the adult models one first.
- **G (job chart):** all five jobs named from pictures; the three helper
  steps rehearsed (know it, do it, notice the help).

## Formative quiz

**Q1:** Secure — a specific favorite/talent/interest of the learner's own.
**Q2:** Secure — "favorite food" + "talent" in safe-to-share; "my name" +
"my address" + "my photo" in stays-private. One mis-sort = Developing.
**Q3:** Secure — all three greeting parts. **Q4:** Secure — one belonging
action pointed out AND gentle hands identified (the block-sharing children).
**Q5:** Secure — job named, action described, help explained. **Q6:** Secure
— all three tools shown; sensible tool + action for the dropped crayons
("gentle hands — pick them up"; "kind words — 'are you okay?'"). **Q7:**
Secure — all four repair steps named; a complete repair sentence. **Q8:**
Secure — "Yes" with a true reason (every job helps / the class runs on
everyone).

## Unit assessment

**Task 1:** Secure (3) — drawing/building shows one unique thing AND one
helping action; states the safety rule. Developing (2) — shows both with
prompting, or states the rule only when asked directly. Emerging (1) — shows
only one of the two. Not yet (0) — no response.
**Task 2:** Secure (3) — three-part greeting + including action + one
kindness tool, unprompted. Developing (2) — needs one reminder ("don't
forget your…"). Emerging (1) — only the greeting. Not yet (0) — no response.
**Task 3:** Secure (3) — two distinct belonging actions; newcomer identified
with evidence; "everyone matters" with reasoning. Developing (2) — two of the
three parts. Emerging (1) — one part. Not yet (0) — no response.
**Task 4:** Secure (3) — all four repair steps performed as Pip, slow and
honest, with a plan; receives the repair graciously. Developing (2) — needs
the icon card to sequence the steps. Emerging (1) — fast "sorry" only.
Not yet (0) — refuses or no response (never force; model it yourself and
score 0 with a re-teach plan).
**Task 5:** Secure (3) — three distinct community elements named; safety rule
explained in own words ("so everyone stays safe" / "private things stay
private"). Developing (2) — two elements, or the rule explained only with
prompting. Emerging (1) — one element. Not yet (0) — no response.

## Project — Classroom Helper Week (rubric scoring examples)

- **Secure (10–12):** log shows 3+ daily entries with the three-part frame;
  banner panel drawing clearly shows the job helping (e.g., water visibly
  pouring from can to plant, plant drawn healthy); report uses the full frame
  and answers "what if nobody did it?" ("The plant would die" / "We'd have
  no crayons").
- **Developing (7–9):** log has 2 entries or needs the frame each time;
  panel shows the tool but not the helping action; report covers job + what
  they did but not the help.
- **Emerging (4–6):** job done with daily reminders; log dictated in
  fragments; panel unrelated to the job. → Re-teach: shrink to one job, redo
  one excellent day, and re-score just that day.
