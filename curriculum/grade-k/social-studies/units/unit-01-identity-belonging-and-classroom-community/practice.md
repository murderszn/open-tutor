# Practice, Story, and Review Sessions — Unit 01

Named sessions referenced in the [16-session pacing](README.md#16-session-pacing-4-weeks--4-sessions-1520-minutes-each).
Each runs 15–20 minutes. The adult reads aloud and scribes; the learner
responds orally, by pointing, drawing, or gesture. The safety rule ("names,
addresses, and photos stay private") opens every session.

---

## Session A — Favorite-things picture cards (Practice, Session 2)

**Goal:** name favorites confidently; hear that different favorites are all
welcome.

1. Spread 8–10 picture cards (foods, animals, toys, activities). "Find a
   favorite. Hold it up."
2. Round 1 — foods: everyone points; the adult names each choice aloud:
   "Apple! Pizza! Noodles! Our favorites are different — that is what unique
   means."
3. Round 2 — animals; Round 3 — activities. Same celebration of difference.
4. "Find a card that is NOT your favorite. Can you still be friends with
   someone who loves it?" (Yes — different favorites, same community.
   Previews belonging.)
5. Sort: make two piles — "my favorites" and "not my favorites." Count the
   favorite pile together. (Light numeracy, ungraded.)

**Watch for:** the learner copying the adult's pick — cover your own card
until they choose, or pick last.

---

## Session B — Greeting practice pairs (Practice, Session 6)

**Goal:** perform the three-part greeting (eyes, kind face, hello) fluently.

1. Quick tool check: "Show me the three greeting parts."
2. The learner greets three "visitors" in a row: a puppet, a stuffed animal,
   and the adult — ball roll each time, all three parts each time.
3. Level up: the visitor is "shy" (puppet hides). "How do you greet someone
   shy?" (Softer voice, wave first, wait.) The learner adapts.
4. Level up: the visitor "speaks another language" (adult says "¡Hola!").
   The learner answers with a wave and a smile — greetings work across
   languages.
5. The learner teaches the greeting to the puppet: "Now YOU show the puppet
   how." (Teaching = mastery check.)

**Watch for:** words without eyes/face — keep coaching all three parts.

## Session C — Read-aloud: "Milo's First Morning" (Story, Session 3)

**Goal:** hear a story about belonging; connect it to the learner's own
morning.

*An original story written for this unit. Read aloud with expression; pause at
the ★ marks for the learner to respond.*

---

Milo woke up with wiggly toes. It was the first morning of school, and his
backpack was packed, and his shoes were by the door, and his tummy felt like
it had butterflies in it. ★ *"Have you ever felt butterflies in your tummy?
Show me."*

At school, the teacher opened the door wide and said, "Good morning! I am so
glad you are here." She looked right at Milo with kind eyes and a big smile.
★ *"What three parts did her greeting have?"* (eyes, kind face, hello)

Inside, the children sat in a circle on a big rug. There was one empty spot —
just Milo's size. "We saved you a place," said a girl with a yellow bow. She
patted the rug beside her. ★ *"What did she do to include Milo?"* (made room,
patted the spot, invited him)

Then it was sharing time. A boy held out a basket of blocks. "Want to build
with me?" Milo picked a red block. The tower wobbled… and did NOT fall down.
Everyone clapped. Milo's butterflies flew away. ★ *"How do you think Milo
feels now? Show me on your face."*

At the end of the morning, the teacher said, "Milo, you belong in our class."
Milo said, "I know. There was a spot just my size."

---

After the story: the learner draws "Milo's circle" — the rug, the empty spot,
Milo joining. (No names on the drawing.) "Tell me: what helped Milo belong?"
The adult scribes one sentence on the back for private storage.

## Session D — Share circle review (Review, Session 4)

**Goal:** revisit L1; practice the safety rule in a group-feeling activity.

1. The learner's L1 drawing (from private storage) comes out — the adult
   holds it, the learner describes it again. (Retrieval practice.)
2. The Favorites Guessing Game: the adult lays out 4 anonymous favorite
   cards drawn by "friends" (the adult drew all of them — there are no other
   children here, and that is fine; in a real classroom, classmates' cards
   would join the pile). "Which one do you think is mine?" The learner
   guesses; the adult reveals. Then reverse: "Which one is yours?" — the
   learner finds their own card in the pile.
3. Safety-rule drill as a game: the adult holds up cards saying "favorite,"
   "talent," "my name," "my address," "my photo." The learner sorts them into
   "safe to share" and "stays private" piles. Celebrate every correct sort.
4. Exit: "Show me one thing that makes you unique" (re-check of L1).

**Watch for:** the learner wanting to write their name on a card — warmly
redirect every time; the rule is the rule.

## Session E — Include-me puppet scenarios (Game, Session 7)

**Goal:** practice including others; recognize being left out and fix it.

Set up: two puppets are playing; a third puppet watches from the side.

1. Scenario 1: the watchers' puppet says nothing. "What should the players
   do?" The learner directs the player puppets: "Say 'come play!'" / "Make
   room!" Act it out.
2. Scenario 2: a player puppet says "No, go away." Freeze! "Was that
   including? What could they say instead?" ("You can play the next round" /
   "Here is a spot.")
3. Scenario 3: the new puppet is shy and hides. "How do we include someone
   shy?" (Gentle voice, wave first, sit nearby, wait.)
4. The learner runs Scenario 4 alone: picks the puppets, invents the
   including moment, narrates it. (Creativity + mastery.)
5. Real-world bridge: "Has anyone ever included YOU? What did they do?"
   (Accept "I don't remember" — no pressure.)

**Watch for:** the learner scripting exclusion as funny — pause and name it:
"Being left out hurts, even in pretend. Let's give the puppet a kind
ending."

## Session F — Kindness detectives review (Review, Session 8)

**Goal:** spot kindness in the wild; re-check L1–L2 exits.

1. "Kindness detectives notice kind actions. This week, who did you see being
   kind?" The learner reports (from the L4 application). The adult writes each
   sighting as a "case" on paper: "Case 1: Grandma used kind words."
2. Re-check L1: "Show me one thing that makes you unique."
3. Re-check L2: show the generated picture —
   "Point to one way these children help everyone belong."
4. Detective badge: the learner draws a magnifying glass badge (paper,
   crayons) — "Official Kindness Detective." Worn proudly; no name on it.
5. Preview: "Next week we get classroom JOBS. Every job helps. Start
   thinking: which job would you pick?"

---

## Session G — Helper-role dry run (Practice, Session 10)

**Goal:** learn every job card; practice the three helper steps.

1. Lay out all five job cards. The learner names each job from its picture.
2. Dry-run two jobs for real: water the plant (small, careful pour), tidy the
   crayon basket. For each: "What is your job? Do it. How did it help?"
3. Build the **job chart** together: a large paper grid — one row per job
   card (picture), one column per day of Helper Week. The chart shows PICTURES
   of jobs, not names of children. (The adult keeps a private note of who
   chose what.)
4. The learner chooses their Helper Week job and places their card's marker
   on the chart. (Choice honored; duplicates allowed — two materials keepers
   is fine.)
5. Rehearse the reporting sentence frame: "My job was ___. I ___. It helped
   because ___."

## Belonging song (Review, Session 12)

**Goal:** joyful retrieval of the unit's big ideas through music and movement.

Sung to the tune of "The Wheels on the Bus" (public-domain melody):

> *We all belong in our classroom, our classroom, our classroom —*
> *We all belong in our classroom, you and me!*
>
> *We greet our friends with hellos, hellos, hellos —*
> *Eyes and smiles and hellos, you and me!*
>
> *We help with jobs around the room, round the room, round the room —*
> *Every job helps, big and small, you and me!*
>
> *We're kind with ears and hands and words, hands and words, hands and words —*
> *Kindness grows our community!*

Motions: arms wide for "belong," wave for "hellos," mimed sweeping for
"jobs," cupped ears / folded hands / hands-to-heart for "kindness." Sing it
twice; let the learner lead the motions the second time. (Retrieval through
music; also usable as a transition song for the rest of the year.)
