# Lesson 05 — Fixing Hurt Feelings

**Student goal:** I can use words to fix hurt feelings: say what happened,
say sorry, and say what I will do differently.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U01](../README.md) · **Session 15 of 16**

## Materials

- The two puppets from Lesson 04
- A "repair steps" card for the adult (pictures only): 1) stop, 2) say what
  happened, 3) say sorry, 4) make a new plan — with simple icons (stop hand,
  talking mouth, heart, arrow forward)

## Readiness check (1 minute)

Safety rule: "What stays private?" Then the three tools: "Show me listening
ears, gentle hands, kind words." If the tools are there, they are ready —
repair is what we do when a tool slips.

## Teaching

Even in the kindest class, feelings get hurt. Someone grabs. Someone says a
sharp word. Someone forgets to include. That does not mean you are bad — it
means you are learning. What matters is the **repair**: fixing hurt feelings
with words.

Repair has four steps. Say them with me:

1. **Stop.** Freeze your body. Breathe.
2. **Say what happened.** "I grabbed your block."
3. **Say sorry — and mean it.** "I am sorry I grabbed your block."
4. **Make a new plan.** "Next time I will ask, 'Can I play too?'"

A fast "sorry!" to escape trouble is not a repair. A repair is slow, honest,
and it ends with a plan. Grown-ups do repairs too — I will show you.

## Worked example 1 — the puppet repair (modeled)

Puppet B knocks over Puppet A's tower (adult narrates the crash).

> Adult, thinking aloud as Puppet B: "Step 1 — stop. I freeze. I breathe.
> Step 2 — say what happened: 'I knocked over your tower.' Step 3 — sorry,
> and I mean it: 'I am sorry I knocked your tower. I can see you worked hard
> on it.' Step 4 — a new plan: 'Next time I will walk around your tower. Can
> I help you rebuild it?'"
> Then Puppet A answers: "Okay. Thank you for telling me." The adult notes:
> "Puppet A listened with listening ears — repair takes two people."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** each step has a job — stop calms the body, naming
shows honesty, sorry shows care, the plan shows learning. Skip a step and the
repair wobbles.

## Worked example 2 — the adult's real repair (modeled)

> "Yesterday I snapped at someone I love — I said a sharp word because I was
> tired. Here was my repair: I stopped. I said, 'I used a sharp voice with
> you.' I said, 'I am sorry — you did not deserve that.' And my plan: 'Next
> time I am tired, I will say I need a minute instead.' Grown-ups repair too.
> It never stops being useful."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** repair is a life skill, not a punishment. When
the adult models a real one, the learner sees it is safe to admit mistakes.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

1. Puppet scenario: Puppet A grabs Puppet B's crayon. "Walk Puppet B through
   the four steps." *(Hint: point to each icon on the repair card as they
   go.)*
2. The learner IS Puppet B; the adult is Puppet A receiving the repair.
   Coach the slow, honest pace — not the speedy "sorry!"
3. "What is a good new plan for grabbing?" *(Expected: "Ask first" / "Use
   kind words: can I use it after you?")*
4. Reverse: the adult plays a learner who rushes — "SORRY!" (too fast, no
   eye contact, no plan). "Was that a real repair? What was missing?"
   *(Expected: no — missing the naming, the meant-it, and the plan.)*
5. Feelings words check: "Show me on your face: hurt. Show me: forgiven and
   calm." (Naming feelings is part of repair.)
6. Real-life link (only if a real hurt happened this week — never invent
   one): "Remember when ___ happened? Let's do the four steps together now."

## Independent practice

The learner draws a two-panel "repair story": panel 1 shows the hurt moment
(blocks knocked over, sad face), panel 2 shows the repair (two figures
talking, smile). The adult observes silently: does the drawing show the
problem AND the fixing? The learner narrates; the adult scribes one sentence
per panel on the back for private storage.

*Alternate response mode:* act the repair with puppets instead of drawing;
sequence the four icon cards in order and describe each.

## Application

"If feelings get hurt this week — yours or someone else's — try the four
steps. You can ask a grown-up to help with the words. Tell me how it went."
Real repair, with a safety net.

## Exit check

"Tell me the four repair steps." (Accept in any order if all four are named;
gestures or the icon cards count.) Then the scenario: "You took a toy. Your
friend is sad. What do you say?" (Accept: stop + "I took your toy. I am
sorry. Next time I will ask.") The adult records: secure / developing / not
yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** the four steps taught with icons and gestures first.
  Home-language repairs count fully.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the icon cards in order; puppet play;
  one-word answers the adult expands.
- **Floor-play variant:** puppets repair on the floor; the learner moves the
  "stop" hand icon onto the scene.
- **Never force:** a repair is never demanded as punishment. If the learner
  is not ready, the adult models it ("I will do the repair, you watch") and
tries again another day.

## Extension

"Teach the four steps to your puppet, slowly, like a teacher." Or: "What is
the hardest step? Why?" (Metacognition — usually "the plan" or "saying what
happened.")

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) "sorry" erases what happened — no: sorry is
step 3 of 4; the plan is what prevents repeats; (2) the hurt person must
forgive instantly — no: "Puppet A can say 'I need a minute.' Forgiveness is
their choice"; (3) repair is only for big hurts — practice on small ones so
the steps are ready for big ones.
