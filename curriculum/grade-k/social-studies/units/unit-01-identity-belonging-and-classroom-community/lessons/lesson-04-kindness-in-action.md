# Lesson 04 — Kindness in Action

**Student goal:** I can show kindness with listening ears, gentle hands, and
kind words.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U01](../README.md) · **Session 13 of 16**

## Materials

- Two puppets or stuffed animals (one "kind," one learning)
- Picture cards: listening ears (ear), gentle hands (hands), kind words
  (speech bubble with a heart) — the three kindness tools
- The three kind-words sentence frames on a card for the adult: "Can I play
  too?" · "Are you okay?" · "Thank you."

## Readiness check (1 minute)

Safety rule: "What stays private?" Then: "Show me your hello — all three
parts." (Recalls L2.) If the greeting is there, they are ready — kindness is
what we do *after* hello.

## Teaching

Kindness is not just a feeling. Kindness is **action** — things we do with
our bodies and words. Our class has three kindness tools:

1. **Listening ears** — eyes watching, mouth quiet, body still while someone
   talks. Listening says "what you say matters."
2. **Gentle hands** — hands that touch softly, or stay in your lap. Gentle
   hands never hit, grab, or push.
3. **Kind words** — words that help, not hurt. "Can I play too?" "Are you
   okay?" "Thank you."

Say it with me, with the gestures: "Listening ears" *(cup ears)*, "gentle
hands" *(fold hands)*, "kind words" *(hands to heart, then out)*.

## Worked example 1 — the puppets (modeled)

The adult holds both puppets. Puppet A "talks" (adult narrates): "I built a
tower! It is SO tall!"

> Adult as Puppet B, thinking aloud: "Puppet B is using listening ears. Watch:
> eyes on Puppet A… mouth quiet… body still. Now Puppet B says, 'Wow! Tell me
> how you built it!' Those are kind words — they ask, and they celebrate."
> Then the adult replays it wrong: Puppet B grabs the tower and knocks it.
> "Was that gentle hands? No. Gentle hands would never knock someone's tower."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the same moment can go kind or unkind — the
difference is which tool you pick up. Listening ears, gentle hands, kind
words: choose all three.

## Worked example 2 — kind words that fix a small moment (modeled)

> "Puppet A is crying — well, pretend crying. Puppet B walks over. Watch the
> tools: listening ears first — 'What happened?' Puppet A says, 'I lost my
> red block.' Kind words next: 'Are you okay? I will help you look.' Gentle
> hands last: Puppet B pats Puppet A's shoulder softly. See? Three tools, one
> kind moment."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** kind words name what you notice ("are you
okay?") and offer help ("I will help"). Kindness starts by paying attention.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

1. "Show me listening ears." / "Show me gentle hands." / "Show me kind
   words." (The three gestures — quick tool check.)
2. Puppet scenario: Puppet A says, "I can't do this puzzle." Learner coaches
   Puppet B: "What should Puppet B say?" *(Hint: pick a kind-words frame:
   "Are you okay? I will help.")*
3. Gentle-hands practice: the learner strokes the stuffed animal "gently,
   gently" — then the adult asks, "Was that gentle? How could you tell?"
   (soft, slow, the animal "looks happy").
4. Listening game: the adult whispers a short sentence; the learner repeats
   it back. "Listening ears caught every word!"
5. Error-analysis round: Puppet B shouts "GIVE ME THAT!" and snatches a
   block. "Which tools did Puppet B forget? What could Puppet B say instead?"
   *(Expected: forgot all three; could say "Can I play too?" with gentle
   hands.)*
6. Real practice: the adult "drops" crayons; the learner has a genuine chance
   to help. Notice and name it: "You used kind words AND gentle hands!"

## Independent practice

The learner draws "kindness in action": a picture of one of the three tools
being used (big ears listening, gentle hands helping, a heart speech bubble).
The adult observes silently: can the learner pick one tool and show it in a
drawing? Then: "Tell me about your picture" — adult scribes one sentence on
the back for private storage.

*Alternate response mode:* act out one tool with a puppet instead of drawing;
point to the three tool cards to answer "which tool?"

## Application

"Today, catch someone being kind — at home or anywhere. Notice which tool
they used. Tell me about it tomorrow: who, what tool, what happened." The
learner becomes a kindness noticer in the real world.

## Exit check

"Show me the three kindness tools." (All three gestures.) Then a scenario:
"Your friend is sad. Which tool do you use first, and what do you say?"
(Accept: listening ears + "Are you okay?") The adult records: secure /
developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** tools taught through gesture first. Home-language kind
  words count ("¿estás bien?" is kind words).
- **Alternate response mode:** gestures only; puppet play; pointing to tool
  cards.
- **Floor-play variant:** puppets act on the floor; the learner crawls the
  "kind" puppet over to the "sad" puppet.
- **Big feelings:** if the learner is having a hard day, the lesson IS the
  practice — name their feeling, model the tools on them, and count it.

## Extension

"Teach the three tools to someone at home tonight." (Teaching cements
learning.) Or: sort picture cards into "kind" and "unkind" piles and explain
one card's placement. (Previews the Practice C sorting game.)

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) kindness = being quiet and still — no: kindness
is active (helping, asking, including); quiet compliance is not the goal;
(2) "sorry" said fast to escape trouble counts as kind words — it does not;
real repair is Lesson 05; (3) gentle hands means never touching — no: gentle
means *careful*, and helping touch (a pat, handing a block) is good.
