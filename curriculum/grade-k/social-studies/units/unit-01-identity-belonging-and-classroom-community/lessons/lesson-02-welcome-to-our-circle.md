# Lesson 02 — Welcome to Our Circle

**Student goal:** I can greet someone, and I can tell when someone is being
included or left out.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U01](../README.md) · **Session 5 of 16**

## Materials

- A soft ball or stuffed animal for the greeting circle
- The generated illustration
  [`assets/belonging-circle.png`](../assets/belonging-circle.png) (shown on a
  screen or printed large)

## Readiness check (1 minute)

Safety rule first: "What stays private?" (names, addresses, photos — the
learner indicates in any mode). Then: "Show me your best hello wave." If the
learner waves, smiles, or moves toward you, they are ready.

## Teaching

A **community** is a group of people who belong together. Our class is a
community. **Belong** means you are part of the group — there is a place for
you. A **greeting** is how we say "I see you, and I am glad you are here."

Watch: I greet you three ways. "Good morning!" *(wave)* "Hello, friend!"
*(smile and nod)* "Welcome!" *(open arms)*. A greeting has three parts: eyes
that look, a face that is kind, and words or a wave that say hello.

**Include** means to make room for someone — to invite them in. When someone
new comes, we make the circle bigger. Nobody stands outside the circle.

## Worked example 1 — the greeting circle (modeled)

The adult and learner sit facing each other. The adult rolls the soft ball to
the learner while saying, "Good morning! I am glad you are here." Then the
adult models the learner's turn: "Now you roll it back and greet me."

> Adult (as learner, thinking aloud): "I look at your eyes. I smile. I roll
> the ball and say, 'Good morning!' My greeting had all three parts: eyes, a
> kind face, and hello words."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** a greeting is not just the word "hello" — it is
eyes + face + words together that make someone feel welcome.

## Worked example 2 — reading the picture (modeled)

Show [`belonging-circle.png`](../assets/belonging-circle.png): "This is an
AI-made picture of a classroom circle — not a real photo. Let's read it like
detectives."

> "Look at the child with the blue hat. His hand is up — he is waving hello.
> His mouth is smiling. Waving + smiling = a greeting. He is saying 'I see
> you, I am glad you are here' to the child with the backpack who is joining
> the circle."
> "Now look at the two children with the blocks. One is handing a block to
> the other. Sharing is including — 'there is room for you in my game.'"
> "And the child with the round glasses, sitting with hands folded? Eyes
> closed, calm face — that is listening. Listening is kindness too."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** we can tell belonging is happening by what bodies
do — waves, shared toys, open space in the circle — not just by words. (A
text-only description of every child and action is in
[assets/README.md](../assets/README.md#text-only-alternative), so this
activity works without the picture.)

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

1. Point to the picture: "Point to someone giving a greeting." *(Hint: "Who
   has a hand up in the air?")*
2. "Point to the children who are sharing." *(Hint: "Who is handing a block?")*
3. "How can you tell they feel welcome? Look at faces." (smiles)
4. Greeting practice with the ball: learner rolls and greets; adult coaches
   the three parts — "eyes, kind face, hello."
5. Error-analysis round: the adult turns away, mumbles "hi" at the floor
   without looking. "Did I greet you well? What was missing?" *(Expected: no —
   no eyes, no kind face; the words alone were not enough.)*
6. "Show me 'include' with your arms." (Learner opens arms wide / makes the
   circle bigger.)

## Independent practice

The learner practices greeting a puppet "new student": look, smile, wave or
say hello, and scoot over to make room. The adult observes silently: all three
greeting parts? Did they make room? Then the learner draws the puppet joining
a circle (paper, crayons) — a drawing of belonging, no names.

*Alternate response mode:* the learner waves instead of speaking; makes room
by patting the floor beside them; or points to the picture to answer "who is
included?"

## Application

"At your next meal or playtime, greet someone with all three parts — eyes,
kind face, hello — and notice how they react. Tell me tomorrow what happened."
Real greetings, real feedback.

## Exit check

Show the picture. "Point to one way these children help everyone belong."
(Accept: the wave/hello, the sharing, the open space for the newcomer, the
listening.) Then: "Show me a greeting with all three parts." The adult
records: secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** teach "greet," "belong," "include" through the ball
  roll first, words second. Home-language greetings ("hola," "bonjour") count
  fully.
- **Alternate response mode:** wave-only greetings; patting the floor to
  invite; pointing at the picture instead of acting.
- **Floor-play variant:** the whole lesson happens sitting on the floor in a
  real circle; the learner physically scoots to widen it for the puppet.
- **Sensory:** the soft ball can be squeezed instead of rolled; eye contact is
  modeled but never forced — "look toward" counts.

## Extension

"Draw two circles: one where someone is left out, one where they are
included. What is different?" (Previews fairness, Unit 02.) Or learn a
hello in another language and teach it to the adult.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) greeting = saying the word "hi" — keep
insisting on the three parts; (2) including means giving up your toy — no:
including means making room; sharing is one way, not the only way; (3) the
child in the wheelchair "needs help" — correct gently: "She is part of the
circle, just like everyone. She belongs."
