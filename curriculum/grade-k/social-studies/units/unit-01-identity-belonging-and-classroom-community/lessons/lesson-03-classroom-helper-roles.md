# Lesson 03 — Classroom Helper Roles

**Student goal:** I can name a classroom helper job, do it, and explain how
it helps our community.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U01](../README.md) · **Session 9 of 16**

## Materials

- 4–5 job cards the adult draws or prints: **line leader** (arrow), **door
  holder** (door), **materials keeper** (crayon box), **plant waterer**
  (watering can), **clean-up captain** (broom) — pictures only, no words the
  learner must read
- Real props for at least two jobs (a small watering can or cup, a basket for
  materials)

## Readiness check (1 minute)

Safety rule: "What stays private?" Then: "In our circle, who waved hello?"
(recalls L2). If the learner remembers the greeting, they are ready — helper
roles are the next way we belong.

## Teaching

A **helper role** is a job that keeps our community working. Every job
matters — the line leader matters, and the clean-up captain matters just as
much. Nobody's job is too small.

Here is the big idea: **all people, not just leaders, play important roles in
a community.** A classroom does not run on one person. It runs on everyone
doing their part.

Watch how each job helps: the line leader keeps us together so nobody gets
lost. The door holder keeps hands safe and lets everyone through. The
materials keeper makes sure crayons are there when we need them. The plant
waterer keeps our plant alive. The clean-up captain makes the room ready for
tomorrow.

## Worked example 1 — the adult takes a job (modeled)

The adult picks the materials-keeper card, holds it up, and thinks aloud:

> "My job is materials keeper. First I check: are the crayons in the basket?
> Yes. Then I count the baskets — one for us. My job is done. How did I help?
> Now when we draw, the crayons are ready. My small job helped the whole
> class."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** every helper job has the same three steps: know
your job, do your job, notice how it helped.

## Worked example 2 — a job done badly, then fixed (modeled)

The adult picks the plant-waterer card and mimes pouring a whole flood of
water.

> "Oops — I poured too much! The plant is swimming. Did I do my job well?"
> *(No.)* "Let me fix it: a good helper does the job the right way — a
> little drink, not a flood." The adult mimes a small, careful pour. "Now the
> plant is happy. A helper's job is to help, not to hurry."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** doing a helper role well means doing it
carefully and completely — quality matters more than speed.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

1. Lay out the job cards. "Point to the job you want to try." (Choice is
   theirs; every job is honored.)
2. "What does this helper do?" The learner describes in words, gesture, or by
   pointing at the card's picture. *(Hint: "Look at the picture — what is the
   helper holding?")*
3. The learner does the job for real (waters the plant, tidies the crayon
   basket, holds a door, leads a line to the next room).
4. "How did your job help our class?" *(Sentence frame: "My job helped
   because ___." Accept a gesture or one word.)*
5. Error-analysis round: the adult takes the line-leader card and runs ahead
   out of sight. "Did I do the job well? What should a line leader do?"
   *(Expected: no — a line leader stays where everyone can see and keeps the
   group together.)*
6. Rotate: learner picks a second card and names what that helper does.

## Independent practice

The learner chooses one job card, completes the real job alone while the adult
observes silently (do they follow the three steps: know it, do it, notice the
help?), then draws themselves doing the job — a picture of a helper at work,
no name on it. The adult scribes the job title on the back for private
storage.

*Alternate response mode:* the learner points to the card and mimes the job;
for limited mobility, "director" mode — the learner tells the adult each step
and the adult's hands do it.

## Application

"At home tonight, take a helper job: set the table, feed a pet, or tidy one
shelf. Tomorrow tell me the three steps — what was your job, what did you do,
how did it help?" Helper roles transfer to the home community.

## Exit check

"Point to a helper job. What does this helper do? How does it help our
class?" Then: "Is the clean-up captain's job important? Why?" (Every job
matters; the class runs on everyone.) The adult records: secure / developing /
not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** job names taught with the real action first. Accept
  home-language job names.
- **Alternate response mode:** mime the job; point to cards; direct the
  adult's hands.
- **Floor-play variant:** jobs happen on the floor — the "line" is a masking-
  tape path to follow; the materials basket lives at floor level.
- **Choice support:** if choosing overwhelms, the adult picks two cards and
  the learner picks one of the two.

## Extension

"Invent a new helper job our class needs." (The learner names it, draws its
tool, and explains the help.) Or: rank the jobs — "which job would you do
every day?" — and give a reason. (Previews decision-making, Unit 02.)

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) only the leader's job matters — repeat the big
idea every session: the class runs on everyone; (2) helper = boss of others —
a helper serves the class, not commands it; redirect "line leader" power plays
immediately; (3) rushing through the job to finish first — praise careful over
fast.
