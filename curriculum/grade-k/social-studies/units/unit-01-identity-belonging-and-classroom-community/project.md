# Project — Classroom Helper Week

**Type:** investigation / community-action project · **Sessions:** 10 (setup),
11 (Part 1), 14 (Part 2) · **Unit:** [U01](README.md)

The learner runs a real classroom job for a week, tracks what they did, and
reports how their work helped the community. The class builds an anonymous
"We Help Our Class" banner showing every job and its help — drawings and job
pictures, never names.

## Driving question

"Can one person's job make the whole class work better? How will you prove
it?"

## Materials

- The five job cards and the job chart from [Session G](practice.md#session-g-helper-role-dry-run)
- Large paper for the banner; crayons or markers; tape
- A simple "helper log": one row per day with three picture prompts (What was
  my job? What did I do? How did it help?) — the adult scribes the learner's
  dictation

## Steps

### Part 1 — Take the jobs (Session 11, ~20 min, then daily through the week)

1. The learner does their chosen helper job for real, once a day for at least
   three days (Sessions 11–13 window). Same three steps every time: know your
   job, do your job carefully, notice how it helped.
2. After each day's job, the learner dictates one line to the helper log:
   "My job was ___. I ___. It helped because ___." The adult scribes; the
   learner may draw a small picture beside the words.
3. Mid-week check (Session 12 review): "Is your job going well? What is hard
   about it?" Adjust the job if needed — switching jobs is allowed once, and
   the learner explains why (metacognition, not failure).

### Part 2 — Helper reports and the banner (Session 14, ~20 min)

4. **Helper report:** the learner presents to the adult (and anyone available):
   "My job was ___. I did it ___ times. It helped our class because ___."
   The adult asks one follow-up: "What would happen if nobody did your job?"
   (The learner reasons about the community depending on every role.)
5. **The banner:** on large paper, the learner draws their job's tool in
   action (a watering can tipping over a plant, a hand holding a door) and
   the adult scribes the help underneath: "Watering keeps our plant alive."
   In a real classroom, every child's panel joins the same banner. **No names
   anywhere on the banner** — the panels are identified by job pictures only.
6. Hang the banner where the class gathers. Read it together: "Look at all
   the ways our class is helped. Every job matters."

## Deliverables

1. A completed helper log: at least three daily entries (dictated, adult-
   scribed, pictures optional). Kept in private storage.
2. One banner panel: a drawing of the job in action + one scribed sentence
   about the help it gives. Displayed anonymously.
3. An oral helper report using the three-part frame (job / what I did / how
   it helped), plus the "what if nobody did it?" reasoning.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Emerging (1) | Developing (2) | Secure (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does the job | Needed reminders each day; job partly done | Did the job most days with one reminder | Did the job independently all three+ days, carefully |
| Notices the help | Cannot say how the job helped | Names the help with the sentence frame | Explains the help AND answers "what if nobody did it?" |
| Report | One-word or gestured answers | Uses parts of the frame with prompting | Full three-part report, clear enough to teach another child |
| Banner panel | Drawing unrelated to the job | Drawing shows the job's tool or action | Drawing shows the job helping; scribed sentence matches the picture |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = Secure · 7–9 = Developing · 4–6 = Emerging (re-teach and
retry one job day). The rubric assesses the *work*, never the child; share
only the descriptive feedback with the learner ("your report taught me how
the plant job helps!"), not the numbers.

## Privacy

The banner is anonymous by design. The helper log stays in private storage.
If the adult photographs the banner for a portfolio, the photo shows the
banner only — no children, no names.
