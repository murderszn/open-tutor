# Resource Pack — Unit 04: Maps, Symbols, and Nearby Places

## Goal and age fit

Give the guiding adult background reading, printable activities, and
short videos that reinforce this unit's goals — the bird's-eye view,
symbols and legends, position words, bigger maps, and land and water
on a globe — for a kindergarten learner (ages 4–6). The adult previews
everything; videos supplement the lessons, never replace them. All
core learning is achievable with the free, no-account resources
below, plus the unit's own generated illustration and printable
symbol sheet.

## Videos (labeled search links — adult previews required)

Direct video verification was not available in this run, so these are
**clearly labeled preferred-channel search links**, not verified
individual videos. Open each, watch fully, and confirm age fit,
captions, and ad behavior before showing the learner. Prefer official
PBS KIDS, National Geographic Kids, and Sesame Workshop uploads.

1. **YouTube search — map symbols and legends for kids**
   <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=map+symbols+and+legends+for+kids>
   *Why:* Short explainers that show a legend decoding a map, symbol
   by symbol — the Lesson 02 routine in video form. Relevance:
   Lesson 02, Practice B. Account: none needed; ads vary — preview
   first.
2. **YouTube search — kindergarten classroom map song / map skills song**
   <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=kindergarten+map+skills+song>
   *Why:* Call-and-response songs that name map parts (symbols,
   legend, directions) — good warm-ups before Lessons 01 and 02.
   Relevance: whole unit (supplement). Preview lyrics: skip any song
   that teaches cardinal directions as a kindergarten requirement —
   this unit teaches left/right and near/far, not north/south.
3. **YouTube search — globe land and water song for kindergarten**
   <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=globe+land+and+water+song+kindergarten>
   *Why:* Songs that pair "blue is water, green/brown is land" with a
   spinning globe — the Lesson 05 color rule in song form.
   Relevance: Lesson 05, Practice E. Preview: skip any song that
   names oceans or continents as test content; names are not tested
   in this unit.
4. **YouTube search — treasure hunt map for kids**
   <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=treasure+hunt+map+for+kids+classroom>
   *Why:* Short clips of children following a picture map to a
   treasure — the project in video form, useful for showing what a
   "museum tour" looks like before Session 14. Relevance: project.
5. **PBS LearningMedia search — maps (PreK–K)**
   <https://www.pbslearningmedia.org/search/?q=maps>
   *Why:* Standards-tagged clips and lesson plans from member
   stations; search "maps," "globes," or "neighborhood" for PreK–K
   items (the WGBH "Map It" media gallery for grades K–1 is one such
   item). Relevance: whole unit (supplement). Access: free; the
   search page loads as an interactive app — the adult browses and
   previews items. A free educator account unlocks saving/favoriting.

## Web references (checked 2026-10-29 unless noted)

1. **National Geographic Education — "Places in the Park"** (activity)
   <https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/places-in-the-park>
   Opened and read 2026-10-29. A park-map activity whose steps match
   this unit's method: drawings of real things are called symbols, a
   map key tells what each symbol stands for, and learners practice
   location words (*near*, *far*, *next to*, *between*) on the map
   before drawing a map of a familiar place with at least three
   symbols and a key. The activity page itself cites D2.Geo.2.K-2
   and D2.Geo.3.K-2. **Adaptation for kindergarten:** use the
   symbols/key/location-words steps only. Skip this activity's
   cardinal-direction step (north/south/east/west) and its
   five-written-sentences step — neither is a kindergarten
   requirement in this unit. Its linked prior activity, "Mapping
   the Classroom," is the same method applied to a classroom.
   Relevance: teacher background for Lessons 02, 03, and the project.
2. **National Geographic Education blog — "Introducing Maps to Little
   Kids"** (educator spotlight, published 2016-01-19)
   <http://blog.education.nationalgeographic.org/2016/01/19/educator-spotlight-introducing-maps-to-little-kids/>
   Opened and read 2026-10-29 (page copy crawled 2026-10-25). A
   kindergarten teacher's account of mapping a classroom and school
   routes with a legend, including the "imagine you are a fly on the
   ceiling looking down" shift in perspective and translating between
   a projected map and the real room by walking to pointed-to spots.
   Dated (2016) but the method is the same one this unit uses, and
   the teacher's own note — that making their own maps was still a
   challenge for her students — is why this unit scaffolds map-making
   across five lessons instead of one session. Relevance: teacher
   background for Lessons 01 and 04, Practice D.
3. **U.S. Geological Survey — "Cartographic representation"**
   <https://www.USGS.gov/centers/cegis/science/cartographic-representation>
   Opened and read 2026-10-29 (page copy crawled 2026-10-25).
   Teacher background only (not assigned): symbolization is how
   information is displayed on a map; water has long been shown in
   blue; and symbol detail changes as you zoom in or out (called
   generalization) — the adult-level wording for this unit's
   bird's-eye view, blue = water rule, and zoom-out rule.
   Relevance: teacher background for Lessons 01, 04, 05.
4. **C3 Framework for Social Studies State Standards**
   <https://www.socialstudies.org/standards/c3>
   Landing page and K–2 indicator codes/descriptions verified
   2026-10-02 during the track audit (see the
   [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)). No state
   adoption or accreditation claimed. Relevance: standards notes in
   every lesson.
5. **National Geographic Homework Help — "Finding Your Way Around"**
   (map symbols explainer)
   <https://www.nationalgeographic.com/pdf/kids/photos/articles/Homework-Help/map_symbols.pdf>
   Found via search 2026-10-29 (search-result text read; PDF not
   opened in full this run — treat as a **teacher reference to
   preview**, not a verified assignment). Its stated idea matches
   this unit: maps use a language of symbols, and the key is the
   "secret decoder" for what each symbol means. Preview before any
   use: it covers scale and a compass rose, which are beyond this
   unit's kindergarten scope. Relevance: teacher background for
   Lesson 02 only.

## Safety and access notes (2026-10-29)

- All listed resources are free. PBS LearningMedia may invite a free
  educator account for saving items; nothing in this unit requires an
  account.
- YouTube links are **search links, not verified videos**: the adult
  must open and preview each candidate for age fit, captions,
  comments, and advertising before the learner watches.
- No resource above asks the learner to enter a name, photo, or
  personal information. Keep it that way: use printables offline
  where possible.
- The practice neighborhood map in Lesson 04 is invented. Do not
  substitute a real map of the learner's town, school, or home —
  real locations never go on a learner map in this track.
- External pages change: if a link breaks, re-run the focused queries
  above rather than substituting an unverified look-alike.
