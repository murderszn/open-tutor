# Practice Sessions — Unit 04: Maps, Symbols, and Nearby Places

All sessions are 15–20 minutes, adult-led, oral/pointing/drawing.
The adult reads everything aloud and scribes. Session letters match
the [unit pacing](README.md#16-session-pacing-4-weeks--4-sessions-1520-minutes-each).

## Session A: Bird's-eye detective

*(Session 2; follows Lesson 01.)*

**Goal:** tell a bird's-eye (from-above) view from a side view.

**Materials:** two adult drawings — (1) a table seen from above (a
circle) and (2) the same table seen from the side (legs and top);
a toy bird.

**Activity:**

1. The adult shows both drawings. "Which one did the bird draw? The
   bird flies above, so the bird draws what it sees from above."
2. The learner points to the from-above drawing (the circle).
3. Repeat with a rug: from above, a flat shape; from the side, a
   thin line on the floor. Then with a bookshelf: from above, a long
   rectangle; from the side, a tall rectangle with shelves.
4. Bird game: the adult holds the bird over a real object (a chair,
   a book, a cup) and the learner says or mimes what the bird sees
   ("a square," "a rectangle," "a circle").

**Success:** the learner picks the from-above view at least twice
out of three pairs, with or without the bird's help.

**Adult note:** if the learner keeps picking the side view, go back
to standing over the block room in Lesson 01 — the body experience
(looking down) fixes this faster than more drawings.

## Session B: Legend hunt

*(Session 6; follows Lesson 02; uses the generated illustration.)*

**Goal:** use the legend to find places on the picture map.

**Materials:** the generated illustration
[`assets/classroom-birdseye-map.png`](assets/classroom-birdseye-map.png),
the printable symbol sheet
[`assets/map-symbols-legend.svg`](assets/map-symbols-legend.svg), and
six small tokens (buttons or blocks).

**Activity:**

1. The adult reads a clue: "Find the place where we read books."
   The learner points to the reading corner (top-left), then checks
   the legend: bookshelf + cushions = reading corner.
2. Clues (in order): reading corner → rug → a table → the door →
   the plant → the teacher's desk → a window → the toy shelf.
3. Each time, the learner places a token on the spot and says (or
   points to) the legend line that proves it.
4. Speed round (optional, no pressure): the adult names a place and
   the learner points as fast as they can — then checks the legend
   to confirm.

**Success:** the learner finds at least six of the eight places,
checking the legend at least twice without prompting.

**Text alternative:** a learner who cannot see the picture uses the
text alternative in [`assets/README.md`](assets/README.md) — the
adult reads each place's description ("top-left: bookshelf and two
cushions") and the learner points to the matching spot on a blank
sheet divided into the same regions.

## Story: Milo the Sparrow Sees the Classroom

*(Session 3; follows Lesson 01. Original read-aloud, written for
this unit. The adult reads it aloud slowly, pausing at each
"what does Milo see?" moment for the learner to answer.)*

**Milo the Sparrow Sees the Classroom**

Milo was a small brown sparrow with a very big curiosity. Every
morning, Milo sat on the windowsill and watched the classroom wake
up. But Milo had a problem: from the windowsill, he could only see
one side of everything. He saw the fronts of the tables. He saw the
sides of the chairs. "I wonder what this room looks like from
above," Milo chirped.

So one quiet morning, before anyone arrived, Milo fluttered up,
up, up — until he was right above the room, looking straight down.
And what a surprise!

"Oh!" chirped Milo. "The rug is not a soft hill at all. From up
here, the rug is a big rainbow circle, flat on the floor!"

Milo flew a little further. "And the tables! From the side, they
looked like tall boxes with legs. From up here, each table is just
a circle — a plain, round circle, with little chairs tucked around
it like petals."

Milo flew to the corner. "The bookshelf is a long rectangle along
the wall, full of colors. And next to it, two soft cushions — a
yellow one and a pink one — waiting for readers."

Milo flew to the door. "The door is a gap in the wall — a doorway
where feet come in and out."

Then Milo laughed a sparrow laugh. "From above, everything looks
different — and simpler! I don't need to draw legs or faces. I just
need the shapes, and where they sit."

Milo flew back down to the windowsill, very pleased. "Tomorrow,"
he chirped, "I will draw what I saw. A picture of a place, seen
from above. I think I will call it… a map!"

**After the story — draw (5 minutes):** the learner draws what Milo
saw: a big circle for the rug, small circles for tables, a long
rectangle for the bookshelf. The adult labels each shape with the
learner's dictated words. This drawing is a first map — keep it for
the Session 11 museum if the learner wants it there.

**Success:** the learner names at least two things Milo saw from
above (rug as a circle, tables as circles, bookshelf as a
rectangle, door as a gap) and draws at least one from-above shape.

## Session C: Treasure hunt

*(Session 8; follows Lesson 03; also the project setup.)*

**Goal:** follow and give two-step routes using position words.

**Materials:** the generated illustration, a small toy ("treasure"),
and the two toys from Lesson 03.

**Activity:**

1. The adult hides the treasure on the picture map (for example,
   under the plant, or next to a table — the adult says where in
   legend words so the hiding is fair: "I hid it next to the plant").
2. The learner follows a two-step route spoken by the adult: "Start
   at the door. Go to the rug. Then go to the plant." The learner
   moves a toy along the route and finds the treasure.
3. Swap roles: the learner hides the treasure and gives the adult a
   two-step route ("Start at the rug. Go above the rug. Then go to
   the reading corner"). The adult follows exactly what is said —
   including wrong turns, with good humor — so the learner learns
   that directions must be exact.
4. Three rounds each way, or until the learner gives one complete
   two-step route without help.

**Success:** the learner follows at least one two-step route and
gives at least one two-step route that lands on the treasure.

**Project setup (last 5 minutes):** "Next week we build a Classroom
Map Museum. Which map will we show — our classroom map, a school
map, or both?" The learner chooses; the adult notes the choice for
Session 11. No wrong choice.

## Session D: Our school route

*(Session 10; follows Lesson 04.)*

**Goal:** trace a route on a school map, or walk one with an adult.

**Materials:** a simple school map drawn by the adult (classrooms as
rectangles, a hallway, a lunchroom, a playground — invented layout,
no real school name), or, for the walk version, the adult's approved
route and walking rules.

**Version 1 — adult-led walk (preferred when possible):**

1. Before leaving, agree the walking rules (Unit 02 retrieval): walk
   quietly, stay with the adult, wait your turn to point.
2. Walk a short approved route: classroom door → hallway → lunchroom
   door (or playground gate). At each stop, the adult asks: "What is
   next to us? What is behind us?"
3. Back inside, the learner traces the same route on the school map
   with a finger and retells it in two steps.

**Version 2 — tabletop route (always available):**

1. The learner traces routes on the school map with a toy: "Start at
   our classroom. Go along the hallway. Stop at the lunchroom."
2. The learner gives the adult a route to trace, then checks it:
   "Did you pass the hallway? Yes."

**Success:** the learner traces or walks at least one complete
route and names at least two places passed along it.

**Safety:** the walk never leaves adult-approved school grounds or
paths, never crosses a road, and never happens without a trusted
adult. If weather, mobility, or supervision rules block the walk,
use Version 2 with no penalty and no explanation owed to the learner
beyond "today we map at the table."

## Session E: Land or water?

*(Session 13; follows Lesson 05.)*

**Goal:** sort globe patches into water (blue) and land (other colors).

**Materials:** the globe or land-and-water ball, blue and green/brown
paper squares, and the "our town" dot.

**Activity:**

1. The adult points to ten patches on the globe, one at a time, in
   mixed order. The learner answers "water" (wave gesture) or "land"
   (pat gesture) and places a matching paper square in the water pile
   or the land pile.
2. Count the piles together: "Which pile is bigger? The water pile
   — there is more water than land on Earth."
3. The learner places the "our town" dot on the land pile and says:
   "Our town is on land."
4. Error round: the adult puts the dot on the water pile. "Fix it!"
   The learner moves it to land and explains why.

**Success:** the learner sorts at least eight of ten patches
correctly and places the town dot on land with a reason.

## Session F: Fix the mixed-up map

*(Session 13; follows Lesson 05; error-analysis game.)*

**Goal:** catch legend and position errors and fix them.

**Materials:** a deliberately mixed-up map drawn by the adult: a
classroom map whose legend says "circle = door" and "gap in the wall
= table" (swapped), and one place mislabeled by position ("the rug
is above the bookshelf" when it is below it).

**Activity:**

1. The adult presents the mixed-up map proudly: "I made a perfect
   map!" The learner checks each legend line against the picture.
2. The learner finds the two swapped legend lines and fixes them
   (with the adult scribing): "circle = table" and "gap = door."
3. The learner finds the position error: "The rug is below the
   bookshelf, not above it." (Or whatever the adult's drawing shows
   — the adult must know the true positions before playing.)
4. The learner re-reads the fixed map: "Now find the door using the
   fixed legend." Success is a correct find.

**Success:** the learner finds and fixes at least two of the three
planted errors.

**Adult note:** if the learner finds only one error, that is still
good error-hunting — praise the catch, fix the rest together, and
note which error type (legend swap vs. position) needs re-teaching
in the teacher guide.

## Session G: Midyear map games

*(Session 4, short version; Session 15, full version; cumulative
review of Units 01–04.)*

**Goal:** revisit community, rules, helpers, and maps in play, and
surface what needs re-teaching before Unit 05.

**Materials:** the generated illustration, the Unit 03 helper cards
(if kept), and a set of question cards written by the adult.

**Short version (Session 4):**

1. One round of "map or not a map?" (Lesson 01) with two new
   sketches.
2. Unit 02 retrieval: "Name one classroom rule about walking."
   (Walk quietly / stay with the adult / wait your turn.)
3. Unit 03 retrieval: "Name one helper and where that helper works."
   (Firefighter → fire station; librarian → library; grocer → store.)

**Full version (Session 15):**

1. **Map round:** Legend hunt speed round (Session B clues, new
   order) — at least four finds.
2. **Rules round:** role-play: the adult "breaks" a walking rule
   (runs ahead on the pretend walk); the learner names the rule and
   the fix.
3. **Helpers round:** "Which helper works at the fire station on
   our neighborhood map? What tool does that helper use?"
   (Firefighter; hose and helmet — Unit 03.)
4. **Community round:** "What makes our classroom community
   special?" (Any Unit 01 answer counts: kind words, helping,
   including others.)
5. **Build round:** the learner adds one new symbol to the museum
   map legend (Session 11's map) and explains it.

**Success:** the learner answers at least one question in each
round they play. Anything missed goes on the Session 16 re-teach
list in the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md).
