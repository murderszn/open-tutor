# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Maps, Symbols, and Nearby Places

Adult-only. Keep this guide and the [answer key](answer-key.md)
separate from the learner's materials.

## How this unit fits

Unit 04 is the track's first geography unit and its midyear
checkpoint. Weeks 15–17 teach the new content (bird's-eye view,
symbols and legends, position words, bigger maps, land and water);
Week 18 reviews Units 01–04 together. The unit assumes Units 01–03
(belonging and the privacy rule, walking rules and fair turns,
helpers and their workplaces) and retrieves each of them in a named
session — do not skip the retrievals, because the midyear review
depends on them.

## Session-by-session guidance

| Session | Watch for | If it wobbles |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (L1) | Learner confuses side view with from-above view | Go back to standing over the block room; the body experience fixes this faster than drawings |
| 2 (A) | Learner picks side views repeatedly | Repeat with real objects (chair, book, cup) before returning to drawings |
| 3 (story) | Learner cannot retell Milo's view | Re-read the ending (the rug as a rainbow circle) and draw it together |
| 4 (G short) | Walking-rule or helper retrieval fails | Replay the Unit 02/03 games briefly; do not re-teach whole units |
| 5 (L2) | Learner guesses instead of checking the legend | Cover the map, uncover the legend first, and require the "checking tap" before each answer |
| 6 (B) | Door or teacher's desk missed | Re-teach those two legend lines explicitly — they are the only non-obvious symbols, by design |
| 7 (L3) | Left/right flips when the learner turns | Keep the map in one orientation all lesson; say "the left side of the map," never just "your left" |
| 8 (C + setup) | Route has one step instead of two | Model a two-step route first, then ask for the learner's; accept "first… then…" phrasing in any words |
| 9 (L4) | Learner wants tables on the neighborhood map | Re-do the zoom-out gesture (hands spreading) and the rule: bigger place, fewer details |
| 10 (D) | Walk is not possible | Use the tabletop version with no penalty and no fuss; the learning goal is routes, not walking |
| 11 (project 1) | Map drawn from the side | Let the learner finish, then build a second small from-above map together; keep both — the comparison teaches |
| 12 (L5) | "Blue means sky" | Point to the sky (not on the globe) vs. the blue patches (water, on the globe); repeat the wave/pat gestures |
| 13 (E + F) | Land colors confuse (brown/yellow = land?) | Restate the rule as "blue = water, other colors = land" and sort again with the paper squares |
| 14 (project 2) | Visitor route fails | Treat the fix as part of the task; do not score the first failed try against the learner |
| 15 (G full + quiz) | Cumulative items missed | Note them for Session 16 re-teach; do not hold up Unit 05 for a single cumulative miss |
| 16 (assessment) | Anxiety about "a test" | Call it "show me what you can do," allow hints, and record hint use honestly — hints change the level to Developing, not to failure |

## Misconceptions (and the lesson that targets each)

1. **"A map is a picture of anything."** → Lesson 01's map-or-not-a-map
   check: a map must answer "where is it?"
2. **"I can just tell what a shape is."** → Lesson 02's brown-rectangle
   example: without a legend, a shape is only a shape.
3. **"Every map uses the same symbols."** → Lesson 02's closing warning:
   always read *that* map's legend.
4. **"Left and right never change."** → Lesson 03's turn-around
   demonstration: left/right belong to the viewer, so map talk fixes
   the map in place.
5. **"Above means up in the sky."** → Lesson 03's double wording, every
   time: "above — toward the top of the page."
6. **"Bigger maps show more detail."** → Lesson 04's zoom-out: bigger
   place, fewer details, or the map becomes unreadable.
7. **"The neighborhood map is real."** → Lesson 04's privacy note:
   the practice neighborhood is invented; real addresses never go on
   a map in this track.
8. **"Blue means sky" / "land is only green."** → Lesson 05's color
   rule: blue = water, other colors = land, on every globe in this unit.
9. **"Our town's dot is our real address."** → Lesson 05's privacy note:
   the dot stands for "a town on land," never for a real place.

## Re-teaching paths

- **Objective 1 (what a map is):** repeat Lesson 01 + Practice A.
  Use real objects and the bird before any new drawings.
- **Objective 2 (read a map with a legend):** repeat Lesson 02 +
  Practice B, with the legend uncovered for the first round and
  covered for the second. Focus extra time on the door and the
  teacher's desk — the two legend-dependent symbols.
- **Objective 3 (position words):** repeat Lesson 03 with the two
  toys in the real room first, then on the map. Keep the map
  orientation fixed. Accept gestures before words.
- **Objective 4 (routes):** repeat Practice C with the adult giving
  routes first, then swapping roles. Shorten to one-step routes,
  then build back to two steps.
- **Objective 5 (build a map; land and water):** for map-building,
  repeat project Part 1 with the adult drawing the rectangle and
  the learner placing shapes. For land/water, repeat Practice E
  with the paper-square sort before returning to the globe.
- **Cumulative (Units 01–03):** repeat the matching Practice G
  round only — rules, helpers, or community — not the whole earlier
  unit.

## Readiness: what "ready for Unit 05" looks like

The learner can say what a map is, find four places on the picture
map with the legend, use four position words correctly, follow and
give a two-step route, build a small map with a legend, and place
"our town" on land. Partial credit is real: a learner who does all
of this with occasional hints is Developing and ready, with the
matching re-teach path noted for the flexible weeks. A learner who
cannot yet do two or more of these should use a flexible week on
the matching practice sessions before Unit 05 — the track plan
expects this use of flexible weeks.

## Safety and privacy reminders

- No real addresses, school names, street names, or home locations
  on any map, ever. The neighborhood map is invented; "our town" is
  generic.
- School walks are adult-led, adult-approved, on school grounds or
  approved paths only, never across a road, with a tabletop
  alternative always available.
- Keep all learner maps, legends, and self-reviews in private
  storage. Never photograph or share them outside the learning
  setting.
- Globe work: the globe is not thrown or spun hard; the textured
  ball alternative is available for any learner who needs it.
- Materials check each session: no sharp tools, no small parts that
  are choking hazards for this learner, no food allergens used as
  counters or props without checking.
