### Text-only alternative (carries everything the activities ask about)

Imagine the classroom seen from directly above, inside a dark blue
border (the walls). The floor is light tan.

- **Center:** one big round rug made of rainbow rings — red on the
  outside, then orange, yellow, green, blue, and a purple center.
- **Top-left (reading corner):** a long bookshelf with many colorful
  book spines, and below it two soft cushions, one yellow and one pink.
- **Top-right:** one large plain brown rectangle — the teacher's desk.
- **Right side:** three round tables. Each table is a tan circle with
  four small chairs tucked around it in bright colors.
- **Left wall:** two blue windows, one above the other, each divided
  into four panes.
- **Bottom-left:** a low toy shelf: a rectangle holding two rows of
  small colorful squares (blocks and toys).
- **Bottom-right:** one potted plant with green leaves in a brown pot.
- **Bottom wall:** a white gap in the middle of the dark blue border —
  that gap is the door opening.

Questions the activities ask about this picture, with their answers
in the [answer key](../answer-key.md#generated-illustration): how many
round tables (three); how many chairs at each table (four); what is in
the center (the rainbow rug); where the reading corner is (top-left);
what the brown rectangle is (the teacher's desk, per the legend); where
the door is (the gap in the bottom wall); how many windows (two, on the
left wall); where the plant is (bottom-right corner).

### Known limitations

- The teacher's desk and the door are recognizable only with the
  legend; the adult always introduces them by name before asking
  questions about them.
- The picture is not to scale and is not a plan of any real classroom;
  learners' own maps will differ, and that is expected — maps show the
  same *kinds* of things in different rooms.
- Colors are bright but never the only cue: every item also has a
  distinct shape and place, and the text alternative above names each
  one.
