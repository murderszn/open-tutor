# Answer Key — Unit 04: Maps, Symbols, and Nearby Places

Adult-only. Keep this key separate from the learner's materials.
Every practice item, quiz question, assessment task, and project
criterion below has a worked answer or an acceptable range. Where an
answer depends on the adult's own drawing (Lesson 04, Practice D),
the key states the rule and the default layout instead of inventing
a fixed answer.

## Lesson 01

**Guided practice:**

1. The bird sees a rectangle or square (the top of a block) from above.
2. Flat, palm down — the bird sees the rug as a flat shape.
3. Error-analysis: no, that is a side view. The bird would see the
   seat from above — a small square or circle.
4. Floor things: the rug, the tables, the door. The ceiling is not
   on our map; a map shows the floor and the things on it.
5. "A map is a picture of a **place** seen from **above**."
6. A map shows where things are. A map does not show faces, stories,
   or sounds. Accept any sensible pair.

**Exit check:** the table-on-a-rug sketch is the map, because it
shows a place and where things are in it. The smiling face is a
picture, not a map. Second probe: a round table seen from above is a
circle.

## Lesson 02

**Guided practice:**

1. The rug: the rainbow circle in the middle; known from the legend.
2. The door: the white gap in the bottom wall. Without the legend,
   most learners would not know — that is the point of the question.
3. Error-analysis: no, mixed up. Rainbow circle = rug; tan circle
   with chairs = table.
4. Symbol sort: reading corner → top-left bookshelf and cushions;
   plant → bottom-right; door → bottom-wall gap; teacher's desk →
   top-right brown rectangle; rug → center rainbow circle; table →
   any tan circle with chairs.
5. Three tables; four chairs at each table (twelve chairs in all).
6. "When I don't know a symbol, I **check the legend**."

**Exit check:** plant = bottom-right green leaves in a pot; table =
a tan circle with chairs; door = the gap in the bottom wall. The way
we know is the legend. "What is a legend for?" — it tells what each
symbol means.

## Lesson 03

**Guided practice:**

1. Above the rug: the reading corner (bookshelf and cushions).
2. Below the rug: the toy shelf.
3. The teacher's desk is far from the toy shelf — opposite corners
   (top-right vs. bottom-left).
4. Error-analysis: no, swapped. The reading corner is above the rug;
   the toy shelf is below the rug; the plant is to the right and
   below the rug, near the bottom-right table.
5. Two-toy game: duck under the table = below the table; bear on the
   table = above the duck / on the table; bear next to the box, duck
   between the bear and the box — as placed.
6. Windows are on the **left** side of the map; the teacher's desk is
   on the **right** side.

**Exit check:** reading corner above the rug; toy shelf below the
rug; windows on the left; teacher's desk far from the toy shelf.
"Between" means in the middle of two things, with one on each side.
At least three of four correct (words, pointing, or gesture) is
Secure-level for this check.

## Lesson 04

**Default layout (draw this if you do not invent your own):**
school top-left, park top-right, store bottom-right, library
bottom-left, fire station center-right, with roads crossing between
them. All answers below follow this layout; if your drawing differs,
your drawing's answers are correct — the key's rule is "read your
own map," not "match this layout."

**Guided practice:**

1. Park is near the school (top-right next to top-left, across the
   top road).
2. Across the road from the store (bottom-right): the library side
   (bottom-left) lies across the central road. Accept any answer
   that correctly reads the adult's own drawing.
3. Error-analysis: no, it would not work. A neighborhood map shows
   buildings, not tables — tables are too small and the map would
   be unreadable.
4. Helper workplaces: fire station → firefighter; library →
   librarian; store → grocer.
5. Trace-a-route: school → park passes the top road (and the park
   edge on arrival). Accept the route as traced on the adult's
   drawing.
6. "A neighborhood is **the area around a school or home, with
   streets, houses, shops, and parks**." Accept any part of that.

**Exit check:** small rectangle = classroom; medium = school; large
= neighborhood. On a neighborhood map we draw buildings, not tables.

## Lesson 05

**Guided practice:**

1. Blue patch → water (wave gesture).
2. Green patch → land (pat gesture).
3. Error-analysis: no, a town cannot be in the middle of blue water.
   Move the dot to a land patch. Towns are on land.
4. More blue than land — more water than land on Earth.
5. Land; water; our town's dot is on land.
6. "On a globe, blue means **water**. Our town is on **land**."

**Exit check:** four patches in mixed order (blue, green, blue,
brown) → water, land, water, land. At least three of four correct,
plus "our town is on land," is Secure-level for this check.

## Practice sessions

**Session A — Bird's-eye detective:** from-above picks: table =
circle; rug = flat shape; bookshelf = long rectangle. Side-view
picks are incorrect for "what the bird draws."

**Session B — Legend hunt:** reading corner = top-left bookshelf
and cushions; rug = center rainbow circle; table = any tan circle
with chairs; door = bottom-wall gap; plant = bottom-right pot;
teacher's desk = top-right brown rectangle; window = left-wall blue
rectangle; toy shelf = bottom-left colorful squares. At least six
of eight found, with at least two unprompted legend checks, is
success.

**Story — Milo the Sparrow Sees the Classroom:** from above, Milo
sees the rug as a big rainbow circle, the tables as plain circles
with chairs tucked around them, the bookshelf as a long rectangle
along the wall, the two cushions (yellow and pink), and the door as
a gap in the wall. The learner names at least two of these and draws
at least one from-above shape.

**Session C — Treasure hunt:** example routes in the text: door →
rug → plant (finds treasure hidden next to the plant); rug → above
the rug → reading corner (learner-given example). Any two-step
route that lands on the treasure counts; a route that fails and is
then fixed counts as success with a fix, not as failure.

**Session D — Our school route:** answers follow the adult's school
map. The route must start at the classroom, pass the hallway, and
end at the lunchroom (walk version) or at the place named in the
learner's route (tabletop version). At least two places named along
the route is success.

**Session E — Land or water?:** ten patches sorted by the color
rule (blue = water; green/brown/yellow/tan = land). At least eight
of ten correct, and the town dot on the land pile with the reason
("towns are on land"), is success. The water pile is bigger.

**Session F — Fix the mixed-up map:** planted errors: (1) legend
says "circle = door" — fix to "circle = table"; (2) legend says
"gap in the wall = table" — fix to "gap = door"; (3) position claim
"the rug is above the bookshelf" — fix to "the rug is below the
bookshelf." Finding and fixing at least two of three is success.

**Session G — Midyear map games:** short version — a walking rule
(walk quietly / stay with the adult / wait your turn); a helper and
workplace (firefighter → fire station; librarian → library; grocer
→ store). Full version — map round: four legend-hunt finds; rules
round: name the broken rule (running ahead) and the fix (walk with
the adult); helpers round: firefighter, hose and helmet; community
round: any Unit 01 answer (kind words, helping, including others);
build round: one new legend line that matches its symbol. At least
one answer per round played is success.

## Formative quiz

1. A map is a picture of a place seen from above that shows where
   things are. Accept either half plus a prompt for the other half
   as Developing.
2. Rug = center rainbow circle; reading corner = top-left bookshelf
   and cushions; door = bottom-wall gap. All three = Secure; two =
   Developing.
3. The brown rectangle is the teacher's desk. How we know: the
   legend says so (checked after the first guess).
4. Reading corner above the rug; windows on the left; teacher's
   desk far from the toy shelf. All three = Secure; two = Developing.
5. Route: door → rug → next to the plant. Correct if the toy ends
   next to the plant.
6. Blue patch = water; green patch = land; our town is on land.
7. Any one walking rule (walk quietly, stay with the adult, wait
   your turn).
8. Any helper + workplace pair (firefighter → fire station;
   librarian → library; grocer → store; teacher → school; nurse →
   school; custodian → school; bus driver → bus/school).

## Unit assessment

Acceptable variations are in the assessment file itself; the notes
below resolve the borderline cases:

- **Task 1:** "a picture from above that shows where stuff is" is a
  full Secure answer. "A picture of a room" alone is Developing.
- **Task 2:** a learner who points to the rug, reading corner, and
  a table but calls the toy shelf "the door" is Developing (three
  of four with a near-miss); re-teach the door legend line only.
- **Task 3:** gestures count as uses if the adult names the word
  and the learner confirms ("Is the rug below the bookshelf?" —
  nod = one use). Four uses across three descriptions is Secure.
- **Task 4:** Part B counts if the adult's toy lands on the hidden
  toy on the second try after the learner's fix. A route with no
  position words ("go there, then there" with pointing) is
  Developing, not Secure — the words are the objective.
- **Task 5:** a map with three symbols and a legend in which one
  line mismatches is Developing; fix that line together and the
  task becomes Secure on re-check. Globe: blue = water, green =
  land, town on land — all three for Secure.

## Project rubric notes

- **Bird's-eye view:** side-view elements (chair legs, faces) do
  not fail the row alone if the layout is readable — score
  Developing and build a second from-above map together.
- **Symbols and legend:** the visitor must be able to find places
  from the legend alone for Secure. If the visitor needed the
  learner's spoken help for one place, score Developing.
- **Routes:** a route fixed by the learner (without adult help)
  still counts as Secure if it lands on the second try; a route
  fixed only with adult help is Developing.
- **School map (if chosen):** a school map that copies the
  classroom map's tables scores Not yet on that row — re-teach the
  zoom-out rule and redraw with rooms, not tables.
- **Globe question:** "our town is on land" with a point to land
  is Secure even if the learner cannot yet say why in a sentence;
  the point plus the word "land" is enough at this age.
