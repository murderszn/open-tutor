# Project — Classroom Map Museum

**Type:** investigation and construction project (runs across
Sessions 8, 11, and 14)
**Question:** Can our map guide someone else to a treasure?

## Overview

The learner builds a big classroom map (and, if they chose it in
Session 8, a school map) on large paper, writes its legend, hides a
treasure on it, and then runs a museum: a visitor (the adult, or a
family member the adult invites) follows the learner's map and
spoken two-step routes to find the treasure. The museum tests
everything the unit taught: the bird's-eye view, symbols and legends,
position words, routes, and — in the school-map version — zooming
out. It is the unit's applied task, and it doubles as the midyear
review's showpiece.

## Materials

- Large paper (at least one big sheet per map), crayons or markers
- The generated illustration
  [`assets/classroom-birdseye-map.png`](assets/classroom-birdseye-map.png)
  as a model (the learner's map will differ — that is expected)
- The printable symbol sheet
  [`assets/map-symbols-legend.svg`](assets/map-symbols-legend.svg)
- A small toy ("treasure") and a star sticker or drawn star
- The learner's Session 8 choice (classroom map only, or classroom
  plus school map)

## Steps

### Part 1 — Build the big maps (Session 11)

1. **Draw the room from above.** The learner draws their classroom
   (or the picture-map classroom, if the real room is hard to draw)
   as seen by the bird: a big rectangle for the walls, shapes inside
   for the rug, tables, reading corner, door, and at least one more
   place. The adult helps with the rectangle; the learner places the
   shapes.
2. **Write the legend.** For every symbol on the map, the learner
   dictates one legend line and the adult writes it: "circle = rug,"
   "brown rectangle = teacher's desk," and so on. At least three
   symbols and three legend lines are required; five is the goal.
3. **Add the treasure star.** The learner picks a hiding place on
   the map, draws a star there, and tells the adult where it is in
   legend words ("next to the plant") — but does not tell the
   visitor.
4. **School map (only if chosen in Session 8).** The learner draws a
   second, simpler map: rectangles for classrooms, a hallway, and
   one more school place, with its own short legend. This map shows
   buildings and rooms, not tables — the zoom-out rule from Lesson
   04, applied.

### Part 2 — Museum tours and treasure hunts (Session 14)

1. **Set up the museum.** The big map(s) go on a table or wall where
   the visitor can point at them. The learner stands beside their
   map like a museum guide.
2. **Tour 1 — the visitor follows the map.** The visitor points to
   three places on the map using only the legend ("Show me the
   reading corner — I checked the legend"). The learner confirms or
   corrects each find.
3. **Tour 2 — the treasure hunt.** The learner gives the visitor a
   two-step route from the door to the treasure star, using position
   words ("Start at the door. Go to the rug. Then go next to the
   plant."). The visitor follows exactly what is said. If the route
   fails, the learner fixes it and tries again — fixing a route is
   part of the task, not a failure.
4. **Tour 3 — the visitor asks.** The visitor asks two questions
   the learner must answer from the map: one "where is it?" question
   ("Where is the toy shelf?") and one land/water question ("Is our
   town on land or water? Show me on the globe"). The learner
   answers with the map or the globe.
5. **Close the museum.** The learner says one thing their map does
   well ("My legend tells what every shape means") and one thing
   they would add next time. The adult scribes both for private
   storage.

## Deliverables

1. One big classroom map with at least three symbols and a matching
   legend (five symbols is the goal).
2. A treasure star placed on the map, found by the visitor via the
   learner's two-step route.
3. (If chosen) one simpler school map with its own legend.
4. The learner's spoken answers to the visitor's two questions.
5. The learner's self-review: one strength, one next-time addition.

## Rubric

Score each row as **Secure (2)**, **Developing (1)**, or **Not yet
(0)**. A total of 8–10 is Secure overall; 5–7 is Developing (re-teach
the lowest row before Unit 05); 0–4 is Not yet (use the flexible
weeks and the teacher guide's re-teach paths).

| Criterion | Secure (2) | Developing (1) | Not yet (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bird's-eye view: map shows the place from above | Shapes are flat from-above shapes (circles, rectangles); no side-view legs or faces | Mix of from-above and side-view shapes, but the layout is readable | Map is drawn from the side, or shapes do not match the places named |
| Symbols and legend: at least 3 symbols, legend matches | 3+ symbols; every legend line matches its symbol; visitor can find places from the legend alone | 3 symbols, but 1 legend line mismatched, or visitor needed help beyond the legend | Fewer than 3 symbols, or no usable legend |
| Position words and routes: two-step route works | Route uses position words and lands on the treasure on the first or second try | Route lands on the treasure only after the learner fixes it with adult help | Route cannot be followed, or uses no position words |
| Bigger maps (school map, if chosen): zoom-out rule | School map shows rooms/buildings, not tables; learner can say it is "zoomed out" | School map mixes room and table details, but the idea of a bigger map is present | No school map, or school map is a copy of the classroom map |
| Globe question: land and water | Answers "our town is on land" and points to land on the globe (blue = water rule stated or gestured) | Answers correctly with a prompt, or points correctly but cannot say why | Cannot place the town on land, or calls blue "land" |

**Notes for the adult:** the museum is not a performance for an
audience. If no second visitor is available, the adult plays the
visitor — the task works exactly the same way. Never photograph or
share the learner's maps outside private storage. If the learner
wants to keep the museum up, keep it up — it is a fine review tool
for Unit 05's opening retrieval.
