# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Addition Stories Within 10

**Unit:** [U05](README.md) · **Track weeks:** 19–22 · **Sessions:** 16 ×
15–20 minutes, adult-led, oral/manipulative/drawing responses.

This guide is for the guiding adult. Learner-facing pages never link
here with answers; the [answer key](answer-key.md) is a separate file.

## Unit at a glance

| Lesson | Idea | Proof method taught |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Putting together | Show both parts, push together, count all |
| L2 | Adding to | Start group + newcomers; counting on, checked by counting all |
| L3 | Number sentences | Say `a + b = c`; adult writes, learner reads back pointing at parts |
| L4 | Solving stories to 10 | Four steps, learner's choice of tool; ten frame as the 10-check |
| L5 | Parts of 5 and 6 | Split one whole two ways; count-all check after every split |

Golden rule, all unit long: **show the parts before any answer, and
count all to check.** A total stated without shown parts is a guess,
even when it happens to be right.

## Session-by-session guidance

- **Sessions 1–2 (Week 19, part 1):** putting-together only. Keep the
  two groups physically apart until each is counted. Do not let
  number sentences sneak in yet — the story sentence ("4 red apples
  and 3 green apples — 7 apples altogether") is the whole goal.
- **Sessions 3–4 (Week 19, part 2):** adding-to only. Insist the
  learner says the start number out loud before newcomers move;
  that single habit prevents most counting-on errors. Session 4's
  retell warm-up matters: a learner who cannot retell a story
  cannot solve a new one.
- **Sessions 5–6 (Week 20, part 1):** sentences. The adult always
  writes; the learner always reads back pointing at each part. If
  the learner wants to write numerals, let them write the numerals
  only — symbols (`+`, `=`) are read, not yet written, at this
  grade.
- **Sessions 7–8 (Week 20, part 2):** mixed solving. Session 8's
  error hunt is the unit's most important lesson: a learner who can
  spot a skipped number in someone else's count will catch their
  own. Play it twice if Session 8 runs short.
- **Sessions 9–10 (Week 21, part 1):** parts. Two colors of counters
  help enormously. Require the count-all check after every split —
  dropped counters are the whole failure mode here.
- **Sessions 11–12 (Week 21, part 2):** the story book. Act out
  before drawing, every page. A page drawn from an un-acted story
  almost always has a wrong total; fix by re-acting, never by
  redrawing for the learner.
- **Sessions 13–14 (Week 22, part 1):** review games. Session 13's
  card game should reuse Practice C's sentence cards plus new story
  cards; Session 14's within-5 fluency work is the bridge to Unit
  06, where subtraction stories will lean on these same facts.
- **Sessions 15–16 (Week 22, part 2):** quiz, then assessment, then
  the book re-read as celebration. Never let the assessment be the
  last thing the learner remembers from the unit.

## Misconceptions and reteaching moves

| What you see | What it usually means | Reteach move |
|---|---|---|
| Learner answers with one part (e.g. "2" for 3 + 2) | The story was heard but never shown | No answer accepted until both parts are built: "show me the two parts first, then we count" |
| Counting-on starts from the wrong number (5 + 2 → 8) | Start number was never said aloud | Point at the start group: "how many are already here?" — learner says it, then newcomers move |
| Total is one too many / one too few | A number was skipped or an object counted twice | Line objects in a row; touch-and-say each count; recount slowly. Then replay the error hunt (Practice D, item 5) |
| "5 is 2 and 3, and that's it" | One split mistaken for the only split | Always ask "can you split it a different way?" before a split counts as finished |
| Split total stops matching the whole (6 split as 2 and 3) | A counter was dropped or borrowed mid-split | Count-all check after every split, no exceptions; recount the whole pile first |
| Sentence read as "the answer comes next" | `=` understood as a prompt, not "the same as" | Use "same as" language from Lesson 03 on; read `5 = 2 + 3` as "5 is the same as 2 and 3" |
| Guessed total, quickly, with no showing | Speed rewarded over proof somewhere earlier | Slow the game down: cover the joined pile, rebuild the parts, count together. Praise the careful count, not the fast answer |

## Readiness and next steps

- **Ready for Unit 06 (subtraction stories):** 5 of 6 assessment
  tasks passed, with parts shown and totals counted — see the
  assessment's readiness rule for per-task reteaching.
- **Not yet ready:** if Tasks 1–2 fail, replay Lessons 01–02 with
  totals at 5 or below for a week; if Task 3 fails, replay Lesson
  03's sentence-sort game; if Tasks 5–6 fail, replay Lesson 05 with
  two-color counters. Do not start Unit 06 on a shaky Task 1 — every
  subtraction story in Unit 06 assumes a fluent join story.
- **Ahead of pace:** totals of 9–10 from Session 7 on; two-wave
  stories (5, then 2, then 1 more); a first split of 7 as preview
  only — 7's splits are not assessed in this unit.

## Accessibility and materials notes

- All responses may be oral, pointing, drawing, manipulative, or
  adult-scribed; no reading or writing is required of the learner.
- The unit picture has a text-only alternative carrying every count
  (see [assets/README.md](assets/README.md)); every picture item
  in the quiz and assessment can be given as counters instead.
- Counters must be large enough to touch-count reliably and in two
  colors if possible; dried beans on a plain tray work as well as
  any bought manipulative. No materials in this unit are hazardous;
  supervise small counters as usual with young children.
