# Lesson 02 — Adding To: A Group Grows

**Goal (read aloud):** "I can act out an adding-to story and say how
many there are now."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** 20 counters, a tray, paper
and crayons

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

Adult: "Put 5 counters on the tray. Count them. Now push 2 more
counters onto the tray. Count them all. How many now?" (7.) This
revisits Lesson 01: the learner should already be able to join two
groups and count all. Today the story changes shape — the first group
is already there, and more *arrive*.

## Teaching (5 minutes)

An **adding-to** story is different from yesterday's story in one
way: the first group is already in place, and then more join it.
There is no second pile waiting beside it — the newcomers walk into
the group that is already there.

Say it slowly while you act it: "5 ducks swim on the pond. Then 2
more ducks waddle in and join them. How many ducks are on the pond
now?" First count the ducks that are already there — 5. Then bring
in the 2 newcomers, one at a time — "6… 7" — and say: "5 ducks, then
2 more ducks join — 7 ducks altogether."

That way of counting has a name: **counting on**. Instead of starting
all over at 1, you start from the first group's number (5) and count
forward for each newcomer: 6, 7. Counting on is allowed from today
on — but it is never required. Counting all from 1 is always a correct
check. If counting on feels wobbly, count all instead.

The question words change too. A putting-together story asks "how
many altogether?" An adding-to story often asks **"how many now?"**
Both questions want the same thing: the total after joining.

## Worked example 1 — Ducks on the pond

Adult acts it out: "5 ducks swim on the pond. 2 more ducks join them.
How many ducks are on the pond now?"

- Start group: count — 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. (5 ducks.)
- Newcomers arrive: bring in 1 duck — "6"; bring in 1 more duck —
  "7." (Counting on from 5.)
- Check by counting all, touching each duck — 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7.
  (7 ducks.)
- Say it: "5 ducks, then 2 more ducks join — 7 ducks altogether."

Point out: counting on (5… 6, 7) and counting all (1… 7) gave the same
answer. Two ways, one total. That agreement is the check.

## Worked example 2 — Cars in the driveway

Adult: "6 toy cars are parked in the driveway. 3 more cars drive in.
How many cars are there now?"

- Start group: count — 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. (6 cars.)
- Newcomers arrive, counting on: "7" (first new car), "8" (second),
  "9" (third).
- Check by counting all — 1 through 9. (9 cars.)
- Say it: "6 cars, then 3 more cars drive in — 9 cars now."

Point out: the start number (6) is the biggest number we already
know, so counting on from 6 is shorter than starting over at 1. But
if you forget the start number, start over at 1 — a slow correct
count beats a fast wrong one.

## Guided practice (adult leads; learner acts and counts)

The adult tells each story; the learner builds the start group, adds
the newcomers one at a time, and says the sentence. Counting on is
invited, counting all is the check. (Solutions are in the
[answer key](../answer-key.md).)

1. "3 children play in the sandbox. 1 more child joins. How many
   children are playing now?"
2. "4 cups are on the table. 4 more cups are brought to the table.
   How many cups are there now?"
3. "2 kittens nap on a blanket. 5 more kittens climb on. How many
   kittens are on the blanket now?"
4. "7 beads are on a string. 1 more bead is added. How many beads
   are on the string now?"

## Independent practice

1. Draw an adding-to story in two steps: first draw the start group,
   then draw the newcomers arriving (arrows help). Tell the adult
   the story and the total. (Adult scribes the words.)
2. Act out a story of your own with counters: start with a group of
   2–6, add 1–3 more, and say "___ then ___ more join — ___
   altogether."

## Application

Tidying-up story (real task): "There are 4 blocks in the box. You
find 3 more blocks on the floor and put them in the box. Act it out,
count, and say how many blocks are in the box now." The learner does
the real tidying and the real counting together.

## Exit check

Adult tells one new story, no hints: "5 apples are in a bag. 3 more
apples are put in the bag. How many apples are in the bag now?" The
learner must show the start group, add the newcomers, and say the
total sentence. This checks Objective 2.

## Supports and extension

- **Support:** keep the start group at 5 or below and add only 1 or
  2; always check by counting all, even if counting on was used.
- **Alternate response:** the learner may show the story silently
  with counters and hold up fingers for the total while the adult
  says the sentence.
- **Extension:** start with 8 and add 2 ("how many now?" — 10); or
  tell the story with the newcomers arriving in two waves (5, then
  2, then 1 more — 8 in all).

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: the learner counts on from the wrong start
number (e.g. starts at 6 when the start group is 5, giving 8 instead
of 7 for 5 + 2). Fix: point at the start group and ask "how many are
already here?" before any newcomer moves, and have the learner say
that number out loud first. Another trap: adding the newcomers but
forgetting to include them in the final count — the count-all check
catches it. Full guidance is in the [teacher guide](../teacher-guide.md).
