# Answer Key — Unit 05: Addition Stories Within 10

**Adult only.** Every item below was solved independently by
counting the parts and counting all; where an item is open-ended, a
sample correct response is given and any response with the same
parts and total is acceptable.

## Lesson 01 — Putting together

Guided practice: (1) 2 cars and 2 cars — 4 cars altogether
(`2 + 2 = 4`). (2) 4 strawberries and 1 strawberry — 5 strawberries
altogether (`4 + 1 = 5`). (3) 3 blocks and 3 blocks — 6 blocks
altogether (`3 + 3 = 6`). (4) 1 fish and 4 fish — 5 fish altogether
(`1 + 4 = 5`).
Independent practice: sample own story — "2 pens and 3 pens — 5 pens
altogether"; drawing sample — a group of 3 dots and a group of 2
dots circled together, "5 altogether." Any two groups of 1–4 with a
correctly counted total are correct.
Exit check: 5 buttons and 2 buttons — 7 buttons altogether
(`5 + 2 = 7`).

## Lesson 02 — Adding to

Guided practice: (1) 3 children, then 1 more joins — 4 children now
(`3 + 1 = 4`). (2) 4 cups, then 4 more — 8 cups now (`4 + 4 = 8`).
(3) 2 kittens, then 5 more — 7 kittens now (`2 + 5 = 7`). (4) 7 beads,
then 1 more — 8 beads now (`7 + 1 = 8`).
Independent practice: sample drawing — 4 dots, then 2 dots with an
arrow arriving, "6 altogether"; sample own story — "4 blocks, then
3 more join — 7 blocks altogether."
Exit check: 5 apples, then 3 more — 8 apples in the bag now
(`5 + 3 = 8`).

## Lesson 03 — Number sentences

Guided practice: (1) 3 stars and 2 more stars — 5 stars altogether;
sentence `3 + 2 = 5`. (2) 6 ants and 1 more ant — 7 ants now;
sentence `6 + 1 = 7`. (3) Sample story for `1 + 7 = 8`: "1 bird sits
on a branch. 7 more birds land beside it. 8 birds altogether."
(4) Sample story for `4 + 4 = 8`: "4 red cars and 4 blue cars park
together. 8 cars altogether."
Independent practice: sample — drawing of 2 dots and 3 dots, adult
writes `2 + 3 = 5`, learner reads it back. Sentence sort: the story
(3 counters, then 2 more) matches both `3 + 2 = 5` and `2 + 3 = 5`,
because the same two groups in either order give the same total.
Exit check: 4 beads and 3 more beads — 7 beads altogether;
sentence `4 + 3 = 7`.

## Lesson 04 — Solving stories to 10

Guided practice: (1) 3 birds, then 6 more — 9 birds now
(`3 + 6 = 9`). (2) 2 green frogs and 6 brown frogs — 8 frogs
altogether (`2 + 6 = 8`). (3) 9 stickers, then 1 more — 10 stickers
now (`9 + 1 = 10`); the ten frame is exactly full. (4) 4 yellow ducks
and 4 white ducks — 8 ducks altogether (`4 + 4 = 8`).
Independent practice: (1) 5 fish, then 4 more — 9 fish now
(`5 + 4 = 9`). (2) 3 apples and 3 apples — 6 apples altogether
(`3 + 3 = 6`). Error analysis: the acted count touched one counter twice and
reached 9, so the claimed total is wrong; careful recount gives 3
counters and 5 counters — 8 altogether (`3 + 5 = 8`). What went
wrong: counting one object twice makes the total too big even when
the groups are right.
Exit check: 6 spoons, then 3 more — 9 spoons now (`6 + 3 = 9`).

## Lesson 05 — Parts of 5 and 6

Guided practice: (1) Sample: 5 as 1 and 4 — sentence `5 = 1 + 4`
(any split except 2 and 3 is correct). (2) Sample: 5 as 2 and 3 —
sentence `5 = 2 + 3` (must differ from the learner's item-1 split).
(3) Doubles split of 6: 3 and 3 — sentence `6 = 3 + 3`. (4) Sample:
6 as 2 and 4 — sentence `6 = 2 + 4` (1 and 5, 5 and 1, or 4 and 2 are
also correct).
Independent practice: sample part-part story — "6 cookies: 5 with
chocolate chips and 1 plain. 5 and 1 — 6 cookies in all." Same-whole
game sample: whole 6, first part 2 → other part 4, sentence
`6 = 2 + 4`.
Exit check: any two distinct splits of 6, e.g. `6 = 3 + 3` and
`6 = 5 + 1`, each part counted and each sentence said.

## Practice A — Put-together stories

(1) 2 frogs and 3 frogs — 5 frogs altogether. (2) 4 shells and 2
shells — 6 shells altogether. (3) 1 star and 5 stars — 6 stars
altogether. (4) 3 red beads and 3 blue beads — 6 beads altogether.
(5) 5 balloons and 2 balloons — 7 balloons altogether.

## Practice B — Adding-to stories

(1) 4 ducks, then 2 more — 6 ducks now. (2) 3 cats, then 4 more — 7
cats now. (3) 6 fish, then 1 more — 7 fish now. (4) 2 dogs, then 5
more — 7 dogs now. (5) 5 birds, then 3 more — 8 birds now.

## Practice C — Sentence match game

Picture 1 (2 red dots, 4 blue dots) → `2 + 4 = 6`. Picture 2 (5 stars,
1 moon) → `5 + 1 = 6`. Picture 3 (3 fish, 5 fish) → `3 + 5 = 8`.
Picture 4 (1 cat, 4 cats) → `1 + 4 = 5`. Picture 5 (apple picture) →
`4 + 3 = 7`. Same total of 6: `2 + 4 = 6` and `5 + 1 = 6`. The apple
picture matches `4 + 3 = 7`.

## Practice D — Mixed stories and one counting error

(1) 3 birds, then 6 more — 9 birds now (`3 + 6 = 9`). (2) 2 green
frogs and 7 brown frogs — 9 frogs altogether (`2 + 7 = 9`). (3) 8
stickers, then 1 more — 9 stickers now (`8 + 1 = 9`). (4) 5 children,
then 5 more — 10 children now (`5 + 5 = 10`). (5) The claim of 8 is
wrong: the count skipped 5 ("1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8" touches only 7
pencils while ending on the word 8). Careful recount: 4 pencils and
3 pencils — 7 pencils altogether (`4 + 3 = 7`).

## Practice E — Part-part stories

(1) 5 as 2 and 3 — `5 = 2 + 3`. (2) 5 as 1 and 4 — `5 = 1 + 4`.
(3) 6 as 3 and 3 — `6 = 3 + 3`. (4) 6 as 2 and 4 — `6 = 2 + 4`.
Sample stories: for (1), "5 birds: 2 on the low branch and 3 on the
high branch." For (3), "6 cookies: 3 with chocolate chips and 3
plain." Any story whose parts match the sentence is correct.
Same-whole game: e.g. whole 5, first part 4 → other part 1
(`5 = 4 + 1`); whole 6, first part 1 → other part 5 (`6 = 1 + 5`).

## Practice F — Mixed review and fluency within 5

Warm-ups: 7 is more than 4. A ten frame with 8 dots needs 2 more dots
to fill it (10 in all).
Stories: (1) 2 apples and 2 apples — 4 apples altogether. (2) 4 bees,
then 1 more — 5 bees now. (3) 3 boats, then 2 more — 5 boats now.
(4) 1 kitten, then 3 more — 4 kittens now. (5) 2 red cars and 3 blue
cars — 5 cars altogether.

## Formative quiz (Week 22)

(1) 4 red apples and 3 green apples — 7 apples altogether
(`4 + 3 = 7`). (2) 5 ducks, then 2 more — 7 ducks now (`5 + 2 = 7`).
(3) Sample story for `3 + 2 = 5`: "3 apples on a tree and 2 apples
on the ground — 5 apples altogether"; acting it out and counting
all to 5 is required, wording may vary. (4) 6 spoons, then 3 more —
9 spoons now (`6 + 3 = 9`). (5) 1 red balloon and 7 blue balloons —
8 balloons in all (`1 + 7 = 8`). (6) Any two distinct splits of 5,
e.g. `5 = 2 + 3` and `5 = 4 + 1`, each said as a sentence. (7) Doubles
split of 6: `6 = 3 + 3`. (8) The claim of 7 is wrong: only 7 counts
were said for 8 pencils — one pencil was never touched. Careful
recount: 4 pencils and 4 pencils — 8 pencils altogether
(`4 + 4 = 8`).

## Unit assessment

Task 1: 3 yellow ducks and 5 white ducks — 8 ducks altogether
(`3 + 5 = 8`). Task 2: 7 beads, then 2 more — 9 beads now
(`7 + 2 = 9`). Task 3: 4 bees and 4 more bees — 8 bees altogether;
sentence `4 + 4 = 8`, read back pointing at each part. Task 4: 2 red
cars and 8 blue cars — 10 cars in all (`2 + 8 = 10`); the ten frame
is exactly full. Task 5: any two distinct splits of 5, e.g.
`5 = 2 + 3` and `5 = 1 + 4`, plus a sample story such as "5 frogs: 2
on a log and 3 on a rock." Task 6: any two distinct splits of 6,
e.g. `6 = 3 + 3` and `6 = 2 + 4`, plus a sample story such as "6
cookies: 3 chocolate-chip and 3 plain."

## Project — My Addition Story Book

A complete book has 3 pages (putting-together, adding-to, parts of 5
or 6), each with a drawing showing two countable parts, a dictated
story, and a matching sentence. Sample page set: Page 1 — "3 birds
and 2 birds — 5 birds altogether" (`3 + 2 = 5`); Page 2 — "4 ducks,
then 3 more join — 7 ducks now" (`4 + 3 = 7`); Page 3 — "6 is 3 and
3" (`6 = 3 + 3`) with a doubles story. Score with the rubric in
[project.md](project.md); "Strong" on all four rows is complete,
"On the way" on any row is a finished book with a named row for
Week 22 review.
