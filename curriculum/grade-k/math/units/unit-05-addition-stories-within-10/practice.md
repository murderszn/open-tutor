# Practice — Unit 05: Addition Stories Within 10

All practice is oral, manipulative, or drawing-based. The adult reads
every story aloud and scribes the learner's words and sentences.
Worked solutions are in the [answer key](answer-key.md), never on
this page.

## Practice A — Put-together stories (Session 2, after Lesson 01)

Build two groups, push them together, count all, say the sentence.

1. 2 frogs on a log and 3 frogs on a rock join together. How many
   frogs altogether?
2. 4 shells in a bucket and 2 shells on the sand join together. How
   many shells altogether?
3. 1 star in the sky and 5 stars beside it shine together. How many
   stars altogether?
4. 3 red beads and 3 blue beads go on one string. How many beads
   altogether?
5. 5 balloons rise and 2 more balloons rise with them. How many
   balloons altogether?

## Practice B — Adding-to stories (Session 4, after Lesson 02)

Build the start group, add the newcomers one at a time, say the
sentence. Count on if you can; always check by counting all.

1. 4 ducks swim on a pond. 2 more ducks join them. How many ducks
   are on the pond now?
2. 3 cats nap in the sun. 4 more cats join them. How many cats are
   napping now?
3. 6 fish swim in a tank. 1 more fish is added. How many fish are in
   the tank now?
4. 2 dogs play in a yard. 5 more dogs run in. How many dogs are
   playing now?
5. 5 birds sit on a wire. 3 more birds land on the wire. How many
   birds are on the wire now?

Warm-up before you start: retell yesterday's duck story (Lesson 02,
Worked example 1) in your own words, then solve it again.

## Practice C — Sentence match game (Session 6, after Lesson 03)

The adult writes these five sentences on cards: `2 + 4 = 6`,
`5 + 1 = 6`, `3 + 5 = 8`, `1 + 4 = 5`, `4 + 3 = 7`. The adult draws
five pictures (or builds five counter groups):

- Picture 1: 2 red dots and 4 blue dots.
- Picture 2: 5 stars and 1 moon.
- Picture 3: 3 fish and 5 fish.
- Picture 4: 1 cat and 4 cats.
- Picture 5: 4 red apples and 3 green apples (use the unit picture).

Match each sentence card to its picture. Read the matched sentence
aloud, pointing at each part. Then answer: which two sentences have
the same total (6)? Which sentence matches the apple picture?

## Practice D — Mixed stories and one counting error (Session 8, after Lesson 04)

The adult tells each story once. You choose counters, ten frame, or
drawing.

1. 3 birds sit on a fence. 6 more birds land on the fence. How many
   birds are on the fence now?
2. 2 green frogs and 7 brown frogs sit by the pond. How many frogs
   are there altogether?
3. 8 stickers are on a page. 1 more sticker is added. How many
   stickers are on the page now?
4. 5 children play ball. 5 more children join the game. How many
   children are playing now?
5. Error hunt: the adult acts out "4 pencils and 3 pencils" and
   counts "1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8" and claims the total is 8. Is that
   right? Count carefully yourself, find the mistake, and say the
   correct sentence.

## Practice E — Part-part stories (Session 10, after Lesson 05)

For each pair, build it with counters, say the sentence, and tell a
short story for it.

1. 5 as 2 and 3.
2. 5 as 1 and 4.
3. 6 as 3 and 3 (doubles).
4. 6 as 2 and 4.

Then play "same whole, new split": the adult says a whole (5 or 6)
and a first part; you find the other part and say the full sentence.
Three rounds.

## Practice F — Mixed review and fluency within 5 (Session 14, Week 22)

Warm-ups (Units 03–04): Which is more, 7 or 4? A ten frame holds 8
dots; how many more dots fill it? (2; the frame then holds 10.)

Then solve these five stories. For the within-5 stories, try to
answer without recounting from 1 — counting on is allowed.

1. 2 apples and 2 apples join together. How many apples altogether?
2. 4 bees buzz. 1 more bee joins. How many bees now?
3. 3 boats sail. 2 more boats sail out. How many boats now?
4. 1 kitten naps. 3 more kittens join. How many kittens now?
5. 2 red cars and 3 blue cars park together. How many cars
   altogether?
