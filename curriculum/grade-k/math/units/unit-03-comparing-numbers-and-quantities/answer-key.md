# Answer Key — Unit 03: Comparing Numbers and Quantities

**Teacher-only.** Every question in the lessons, practice sessions,
quiz, assessment, and project is solved here independently of the
lesson text; where a lesson's numbers and this key differ, trust this
key and fix the lesson. Accept any answer that states the same
comparison with a valid proof (matching, counting, or number-line
position).

## Lesson 01

- Worked example 1 (3 vs. 5): 5-group (blue) has more; 3-group (red) has
  fewer; 2 leftovers on the blue side. Check: 5 − 3 = 2.
- Worked example 2 (4 blocks vs. 4 beans): same/equal — no leftovers
  either way, despite different sizes and spacing.
- Guided practice: (1) 2 vs. 6 — 6 more, 2 fewer, 4 leftovers. (2) 5 vs.
  5 — same/equal, 0 leftovers. (3) 7 vs. 4 — 4-group fewer, 7-group more,
  3 leftovers. (4) 1 vs. 9 — 1 fewer, 9 more, 8 leftovers. (5) 0 vs. 3 —
  empty side fewer (zero is fewer than 3), 3 leftovers. (6) Learner
  builds more than 3 — any group of 4–10, verified by matching.
- Independent practice: two self-built groups (adult's secret numbers
  6 and 8) — 8-group more, 6-group fewer, 2 leftovers.
- Application (3 vs. 5 crackers): 5-plate has more; shared fairly, both
  plates end at 4 and 4 (move 1 cracker) — or any equal split the
  learner proves by matching.
- Exit check: 4 vs. 6 — 6 more; 8 vs. 8 — same; 2 vs. 10 — 10 more, 2
  fewer. Need 2 of 3 with leftover proof.

## Lesson 02

- Worked example 1 (6 vs. 4): 6 greater, 4 less; matching check leaves 2
  leftovers on the 6-side (6 − 4 = 2).
- Worked example 2 (5 spread vs. 8 bunched): 8-group has more (8 > 5),
  despite looking smaller; 3 more (8 − 5 = 3).
- Guided practice: (1) 3 vs. 9 — 9 greater, 3 less. (2) 7 vs. 7 — equal.
  (3) 10 vs. 6 — 6-group fewer, 10-group more. (4) Scattered 8 vs.
  lined-up 5 — 8 more, 5 fewer. (5) Hidden 4 vs. learner's 6 — learner's
  group more (6 > 4). (6) Less than 8 — any group of 1–7, verified by
  counting both.
- Independent practice cards: (2, 5) — 5 more; (9, 9) — equal; (10, 3) —
  10 more; (6, 7) — 7 more.
- Application: basket counts as counted; greater/fewer named with both
  numbers stated.
- Exit check: 5 vs. 8 — 8 more; 10 vs. 10 — equal; 3 vs. 1 — 3 more, 1
  fewer. Need 2 of 3 with both counts stated.

## Lesson 03

- Worked example 1 (4 vs. 9): 9 greater than 4; 4 less than 9; matching
  check: 5 leftovers on the 9-side (9 − 4 = 5).
- Worked example 2: 6 vs. 6 — equal. 7 vs. 8 — 8 greater than 7 by one
  step; 7 less than 8.
- Guided practice: (1) 2 vs. 10 — 10 greater, 2 less. (2) 5 vs. 5 —
  equal. (3) 9 vs. 6 — 6 less, 9 greater. (4) 3 vs. 4 — 4 greater, 3
  less. (5) 1 vs. 8 — "8 is greater than 1." (6) Learner-dealt pair —
  any pair, judged on the explanation (further right = greater).
- Independent practice cards: (6, 2) — 6 greater; (4, 4) — equal;
  (10, 7) — 10 greater; (1, 9) — 9 greater; (8, 8) — equal; (3, 6) — 6
  greater; (5, 10) — 10 greater; (7, 5) — 7 greater.
- Application (number detective): any number greater/less than the
  adult's number, verified on the line.
- Exit check: 7 vs. 2 — 7 greater; 9 vs. 9 — equal; 4 vs. 10 — 10
  greater; 6 vs. 5 — 6 greater. Need 3 of 4.

## Lesson 04

- Worked example 1 (start 6): one more 7; one fewer 5. Check: 6 + 1 = 7;
  6 − 1 = 5.
- Worked example 2: one more than 9 is 10; one more than 10 goes past
  this unit's line (11, taught in Unit 04 — accept 11 as extension);
  one fewer than 1 is 0.
- Guided practice: (1) Start 3 — one more 4, one fewer 2. (2) Start 8 —
  9 and 7. (3) Counters from 5 — 6 and 4. (4) Start 1 — one fewer 0, one
  more 2. (5) Start 10 — one fewer 9, one more past the line (11
  extension). (6) Reverse: landed on 7 by hopping right — started at
  6; landed on 4 by hopping left — started at 5.
- Independent practice hop cards: start 4 — one more 5, one fewer 3;
  start 9 — 10 and 8; start 2 — 3 and 1; start 7 — 8 and 6; start 10 —
  one fewer 9; start 1 — one fewer 0.
- Application: 6 crackers, eat one → 5 (one fewer); given one more →
  6 again.
- Exit check: start 4 — 5 and 3; start 7 — 8 and 6; start 1 — 2 and 0;
  start 10 — one fewer 9, one more past the line (or 11). Need 3 of 4
  starts fully correct.

## Lesson 05

- Worked example 1 (picture): top row 5 apples, bottom row 8 apples;
  8 > 5, so the bottom row has more (greater) and the top row has
  fewer (less).
- Worked example 2 (make equal): add 3 to the top row (5 → 8, 8 = 8) or
  take 3 from the bottom row (8 → 5, 5 = 5). Both moves move 3.
- Guided practice: (1) Picture again — 8 greater than 5. (2) Which row
  is less — top row (5 less than 8). (3) Add to reach 8 from 5 — 3
  (count on: 6, 7, 8). (4) 4 and 9 — add 5 to the 4-group → 9 = 9.
  (5) 7 and 7 — already equal, nothing to add or take. (6) Drawn 3 and
  6 — "6 is greater than 3. 3 is less than 6."
- Independent practice: (2, 2) — equal, add 0; (10, 4) — 10 greater, add
  6 to the 4-row to make equal; (6, 9) — 9 greater, add 3 to the 6-row.
- Application: 5 grapes vs. 8 crackers — crackers more; eat 3 crackers
  to make equal (8 − 3 = 5).
- Exit check: top 5, bottom 8, bottom greater, add 3 to the top row.
  All four parts required (re-count a counting slip together).

## Practice sessions

### Practice Session A

Pairs in order: (4, 7) — 7 more, 3 leftovers; (2, 2) — same; (9, 5) — 9
more, 4 leftovers; (1, 10) — 10 more, 9 leftovers; (6, 6) — same;
(8, 3) — 8 more, 5 leftovers. The adult's deliberate wrong guess in
round 4 is corrected by re-matching, not by authority.

### Practice Session B

Trays in order: (5, 8) — 8 greater; (3, 3) — equal; (10, 6) — 10
greater; (2, 9) — 9 greater. Mystery round: compare the announced
hidden number with the learner's counted number — greater/less
follows the numbers, whichever side wins.

### Practice Session C

No fixed answers — every round is decided by the flipped cards and the
number line. Secure play: both numerals named, line positions pointed
to, greater/less/equal called correctly, close-pair (differ by 1)
rounds recognized. Final card-count comparison uses the same skill:
whichever pile counts more wins.

### Practice Session D

Starts in order — one more / one fewer: 4 → 5 / 3; 9 → 10 / 8; 2 → 3 /
1; 7 → 8 / 6; 1 → 2 / 0; 10 → past the line (11 extension) / 9; 5 → 6 /
4; 3 → 4 / 2. Reverse riddles: landed on 8 by hopping right — started
at 7 (plus two more adult-chosen reverses, solved the same way:
subtract one for a right-hop landing, add one for a left-hop landing).

### Practice Session E

Station 1: 6 vs. 9 — 9 more, 3 leftovers. Station 2: 7 vs. 10 — 10
greater, 7 less. Station 3: 4 vs. 8 — 8 greater, 4 less. Station 4: 3
vs. 7 — add 4 to the 3-group (→ 7 = 7) or take 4 from the 7-group
(→ 3 = 3); both ways move 4.

### Practice Session F

No fixed answers — the correct wall follows the groups/cards shown
each round. Secure play: counts or matches completed before running;
equal groups → middle position, arms out, "the same!"

## Formative quiz

1. 3 vs. 6 — 6-group more, 3-group fewer, 3 leftovers.
2. 8 vs. 5 — 8 greater, 5 less.
3. 4 vs. 9 — 9 greater, 4 less.
4. 7 vs. 7 — equal.
5. Start 6 — one more 7, one fewer 5.
6. 2 vs. 5, add to smaller — add 3 → 5 and 5, equal.
7. Picture — top 5, bottom 8, bottom row greater, add 3 to the top row.
8. 10 bunched vs. 4 spread — 10-group more; valid proofs: counting
   (10 vs. 4) or matching (6 leftovers). "It looks bigger" is not a
   proof.

## Unit assessment

- Task 1: 4 vs. 9 — 9-group more, 4-group fewer, 5 leftovers.
- Task 2: 6 vs. 10 — 10 greater, 6 less.
- Task 3: (8, 3) — 8 greater, 3 less; (5, 5) — equal; (2, 7) — 7 greater,
  2 less.
- Task 4: start 8 — one more 9, one fewer 7; start 1 — one fewer 0;
  start 10 — one fewer 9.
- Task 5: top 5, bottom 8, bottom greater / top less, add 3.
- Task 6: 3 vs. 8 — add 5 (→ 8 = 8); take away 5 (→ 3 = 3).
- Task 7: 7 small beans vs. 4 big blocks — beans more; proof by
  counting (7 vs. 4) or matching (3 leftovers).
- Scoring totals: 12–14 secure; 8–11 developing; 0–7 not yet (see the
  assessment's scoring table).

## Project

No single fixed answer — collections are learner-chosen (1–10 each).
Secure work: all three comparison methods agree on the same winner;
the board's drawn dots match the collections' counts; make-equal way 1
adds the difference and way 2 takes away the same difference (e.g.,
for 4 vs. 7: 3 both ways); the presentation answers "how do you know?"
with matching, counting, or the number line. Rubric totals: 8–10
secure (no 0); 5–7 developing; 0–4 not yet.
