# Project — The Comparison Fair

**Type:** multi-session investigation and presentation project ·
**Sessions 13, 14**
**Unit:** [U03](README.md) · **Objectives addressed:** 1, 2, 3, 5
(matching, counting, numeral comparison, making equal)

The learner runs a "fair": two collections of real objects are built,
compared three ways (matching, counting, numerals), made equal, and
presented to a "judge" (the adult, a stuffed animal, or a family member
on a call). The finished deliverable is a **Comparison Fair board** —
one page (adult-scribed words, learner's marks) showing both
collections, both counts, the comparison sentence, and the
make-equal step — kept in private storage, never in this public repo.

## Adult directions (read before starting)

- This is a **two-part** project in review week. Part 1 (Session 13):
  build and compare. Part 2 (Session 14): make equal and present.
- The adult prepares: 20+ counters or small household objects in two
  kinds (e.g., buttons and beans, or red and blue blocks), two trays, a
  large sheet of paper for the board, numeral cards 1–10, crayons.
- **Choice matters:** let the learner pick the two collection kinds and
  (within 1–10) how many of each to start with — ownership drives
  care. If the learner picks equal starting numbers, celebrate it and
  ask them to change one collection by 2 so the comparison has a
  winner to find.
- The adult scribes all words on the board; the learner draws the
  dots/marks, places the numeral cards, and does all the comparing.
- No food allergens as counters without checking; no small parts for
  children under 3 in shared settings; adult supervises throughout.

## Part 1 — Build and compare three ways (Session 13, ~20 minutes)

1. **Build:** the learner builds Collection A (e.g., 4 buttons) and
   Collection B (e.g., 7 beans) in their trays.
2. **Match:** pair them one-to-one. The learner points to leftovers and
   says: "B has more. A has fewer." (Record on the board: "Matching
   says: ___ has more.")
3. **Count:** count each collection aloud; place the numeral card for
   each count beside its tray. The learner says: "___ is greater than
   ___." (Board: "Counting says: ___ and ___. ___ is greater.")
4. **Numerals alone:** cover both trays. Show only the two numeral
   cards. The learner compares them again using the number line and
   confirms: same answer, three ways. (Board: "Numerals alone say the
   same thing!")
5. **Draw:** the learner draws one dot per object for each collection
   on the board (two rows of dots), counts the dots back, and checks
   the drawing matches the collections.

## Part 2 — Make equal and present (Session 14, ~20 minutes)

1. **Make equal, way 1:** add counters to the smaller collection until
   it matches the bigger one. Count on aloud ("5… 6, 7") and record how
   many were added. (Board: "I added ___ to make them equal.")
2. **Make equal, way 2 (reset, then):** rebuild the original unequal
   collections; this time take counters away from the bigger collection
   until it matches the smaller one. Record how many were taken away.
3. **Compare the two ways:** "Did adding and taking away move the same
   number of counters?" (Yes, when starting from the same two
   collections — the difference is the difference, either direction.
   For 4 vs. 7: 3 both ways.)
4. **Present:** the learner presents the board to the judge: names both
   collections, both counts, the comparison sentence, and both
   make-equal steps. The judge asks one question: "How do you know?"
   The learner answers with matching, counting, or the number line.
5. **Celebrate and file:** the board goes into private storage. The
   learner picks a favorite comparison from the unit to retell at
   dinner ("I can prove which group has more!").

## Deliverables

- One Comparison Fair board (drawn dots, numeral cards placed or
  drawn, adult-scribed sentences) — see rubric.
- An oral presentation (2–3 minutes, adult-prompted) answering "how do
  you know?" with at least one proof method.

## Rubric (observable; adult scores after Part 2)

| Criterion | 2 — Secure | 1 — Developing | 0 — Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Matching comparison | Pairs one-to-one, names more/fewer correctly, points to leftovers | Pairs with help, or names correctly without pointing to proof | Cannot pair, or names the wrong side as more |
| Counting comparison | Counts both groups to 10 accurately, states both numbers, compares correctly | Counts with 1–2 slips (self-corrected or adult-prompted), compares correctly | Miscounts and compares from the wrong numbers |
| Numeral comparison | Compares two numerals 1–10 without objects, using the line, correctly | Compares correctly with counters as a check | Needs objects and still guesses, or reverses greater/less |
| Make equal | Makes groups equal both ways (add and take away) and states how many moved each way | Makes equal one way, or states the amount with help | Cannot make equal, or adds/takes the wrong amount |
| Presentation | Explains the comparison and answers "how do you know?" with a proof method | Explains with prompting, or proof is vague ("I just know") | Cannot explain the comparison |

**Secure overall:** 8–10 points, with no criterion at 0. **Developing:**
5–7 points, or any single 0 — re-teach that criterion with its lesson
before Unit 04. **Not yet:** 0–4 points — use a flexible catch-up week
([teacher guide](teacher-guide.md)) and re-run the project with smaller
numbers (5 and under).

**Note on the rubric:** a counting slip that the learner catches and
fixes is not a failure — self-correction is the skill. Score the
fixed answer.
