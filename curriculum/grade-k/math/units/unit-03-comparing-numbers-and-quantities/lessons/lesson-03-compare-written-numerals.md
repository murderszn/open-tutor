# Lesson 03 — Compare Written Numerals

**Student goal:** I can look at two number cards and tell which number
is greater, which is less, or whether they are equal — without counting
objects.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U03](../README.md) · **Session 9 of 16**
**Objectives:** 3 (numeral comparison)

## Materials

- Numeral cards 1–10 (two sets, or one set plus a number line 1–10)
- 10 counters (only for the check step, not the decision step)

## Readiness check (1 minute)

Hold up 7 and 3. "Count out 7 counters. Count out 3 counters. Which
group has more?" If the learner counts and compares (Lesson 02),
proceed. If numeral names wobble, re-run a Unit 02 numeral-naming round
first.

## Teaching

Now the objects go away. All we have are two **numerals** — written
marks like 4 and 9. Can we still compare? Yes! Because every numeral
has a home on the **number line**. Numbers later on the line (further
right) are greater. Numbers earlier (further left) are less. Same
numeral twice? Equal.

The number-line rule, said together: "Further right is greater. Further
left is less. Same spot is equal."

Try it: 4 and 9 on the line. 9 sits further right. So 9 is greater than
4, and 4 is less than 9. We did not need a single counter.

## Worked example 1 — 4 vs. 9 on the number line

> "Find 4 on the line. Find 9 on the line. Which is further right? 9!
> So **9 is greater than 4**, and **4 is less than 9**. Now check with
> counters if you like: build 9, build 4, match them — 5 leftovers on
> the 9-side. The line was right."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the number line is a map of size. Walking
right always means "more." That is why count-on (Unit 02) always moves
right.

## Worked example 2 — equal numerals, and a close pair

> "Here are two cards: 6 and 6. Same spot on the line — **equal**. Not
> greater, not less. Equal."
>
> "Now 7 and 8 — a close pair, easy to mix up. Find them on the line:
> 7, then 8 right after it. 8 is one step further right, so **8 is
> greater than 7** — just barely. Close numbers are still comparable:
> one step right is still greater."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** equal means the *same* number, not just
"close." 7 and 8 are close but not equal — one step matters.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds with shuffled cards. The learner points to each
numeral's line position, then decides — counters only to check.

1. 2 vs. 10 — "Which is greater? Which is less?" *Hint: "Find them on
   the line. Further right wins."*
2. 5 vs. 5 — "Greater, less, or equal?" *(Equal.)*
3. 9 vs. 6 — "Which is less?" (6)
4. 3 vs. 4 — close pair. "One step apart — which is greater?" (4)
5. 1 vs. 8 — "Say it in a full sentence: 8 is greater than 1." *(Error-
   watch: reading the pair backwards — "1 is greater" — re-point to
   the line.)*
6. Learner deals: the learner flips two cards and teaches the adult:
   "8 is greater than 3 because 8 is further right." (Any pair; the
   explanation is the point.)

## Independent practice

Eight numeral-pair cards (adult-scribed): (6, 2), (4, 4), (10, 7),
(1, 9), (8, 8), (3, 6), (5, 10), (7, 5). For each, the learner points
to the greater numeral — or claps twice if they are equal — and says
the comparison aloud ("6 is greater than 2"). The adult records
right/wrong without coaching mid-round.

## Application

"Number detective" at the number line on the floor: the adult stands on
a number (say, 6) and says "I am 6. Find a number greater than me —
stand on it. Now find a number less than me." Three rounds, then swap
roles. Movement makes "further right = greater" physical.

## Exit check

Four pairs, numerals only (no counters, number line allowed): 7 vs. 2;
9 vs. 9; 4 vs. 10; 6 vs. 5. The learner must call greater/less/equal
correctly on at least 3 of 4, naming which numeral is greater (or that
they are equal).

## Supports

- **Keep the line visible:** the number line stays out for the whole
  lesson — it is a tool, not a crutch to be confiscated.
- **Count-on check:** if the line confuses, count from 1 to each
  numeral aloud — the one you reach later is greater.
- **Sentence frames:** "___ is greater than ___." / "___ is less than
  ___." / "They are equal." The adult scribes the learner's sentences.
- **Big cards:** large, high-contrast numerals; avoid look-alike pairs
  (6/9) until the learner is confident.

## Extension

Order all ten numerals 1–10 from least to greatest, then greatest to
least, explaining each step ("5 comes after 4, so 5 is greater").
Or: "I am thinking of a number greater than 7 and less than 10. What
could it be?" (8 or 9 — both are right; discuss why two answers work.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: reading the pair in the wrong order (naming the
first card shown as "greater" regardless), and thinking close numbers
(7 vs. 8) are "the same." The line position + full sentence fixes
both. Note: do **not** introduce the > / < symbols yet — words only in
kindergarten; symbols come in later grades. Answer key:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-03).
