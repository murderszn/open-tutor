# Lesson 04 — One More, One Fewer

**Student goal:** I can find one more and one fewer than a number, and
show the step on the number line.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U03](../README.md) · **Session 11 of 16**
**Objectives:** 4 (one more / one fewer), 3 (review)

## Materials

- Number line 1–10 (floor or strip), numeral cards 1–10, 10 counters
- A toy frog or token that "hops" along the line (optional, beloved)

## Readiness check (1 minute)

"Stand on 5. Take one hop right — where are you? Take one hop left —
where are you?" If the learner hops to 6 then back to 5 (or names
them), proceed. If hops confuse, walk the line counting 1–10 together
first.

## Teaching

**One more** means: add one, take one step right on the number line.
**One fewer** means: take one away, take one step left.

- One more than 4 is 5. (4, hop right → 5.)
- One fewer than 4 is 3. (4, hop left → 3.)

The secret: one more is always **greater** than the number you started
with, and one fewer is always **less**. So "one more" and "one fewer"
are comparisons in disguise — comparing a number with its neighbors.

## Worked example 1 — one more and one fewer than 6

> "Start on 6. Hop right once: 7. **One more than 6 is 7.** Check with
> counters: build 6, add 1 — count: 7. Agrees!
>
> "Back to 6. Hop left once: 5. **One fewer than 6 is 5.** Check: build
> 6, take 1 away — count: 5. Agrees!
>
> "And notice: 7 is greater than 6, and 5 is less than 6. One more is
> greater; one fewer is less. Always."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** hopping right adds one; hopping left
takes one away. The direction tells you whether the answer is greater
or less — before you even count.

## Worked example 2 — the edges: 1 and 10

> "One more than 9 is 10 — hop right to the end of our line. What about
> one more than 10? Our line stops at 10, and that number (11) belongs
> to Unit 04's teen numbers — so today we say 'one more than 10 goes
> past our line; we will meet it soon.'"
>
> "One fewer than 1? Hop left from 1 and you fall off the line — into
> zero! **One fewer than 1 is 0** (none). Zero is less than 1. That one
> we *can* say, because zero is an old friend from Unit 02."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** edges are honest teaching moments, not
tricks. One fewer than 1 is 0; one more than 10 waits for the teen
unit. (Extension learners may name 11 — celebrate it, but do not
require it.)

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds. The learner hops (or points), then states both
answers in full sentences.

1. Start at 3 — "One more? One fewer?" (4; 2) *Hint: "Hop right for
   more, hop left for fewer."*
2. Start at 8 — "One more? One fewer?" (9; 7)
3. Start at 5, counters only (no line): "Build 5. Add one — how many?
   Take one away from 5 — how many?" (6; 4)
4. Start at 1 — "One fewer?" (0 — "none!") "One more?" (2)
5. Start at 10 — "One fewer?" (9) "One more?" ("past our line — 11
   comes in Unit 04")
6. Reverse round: "I hopped right and landed on 7. Where did I start?"
   (6 — one fewer than 7.) "I hopped left and landed on 4. Where did I
   start?" (5.)

## Independent practice

Six "hop cards" (adult-scribed): "Start at 4: one more ___ , one fewer
___"; "Start at 9…"; "Start at 2…"; "Start at 7…"; "Start at 10: one
fewer ___"; "Start at 1: one fewer ___". The learner hops on the line
(or counts counters) and fills each blank aloud; the adult scribes the
answers. All six should be right before moving on — re-hop any miss
immediately.

## Application

Snack hops: "You have 6 crackers. Eat one — how many now? (5 — one
fewer.) Now I give you one more — how many? (6 again.)" Real eating
makes one-more/one-fewer unforgettable. (Allergy check first, per the
track's safe-materials note; use counters if food is unsuitable.)

## Exit check

Four starts, number line allowed: 4, 7, 1, 10. For each, the learner
gives one more and one fewer (for 10, "one more" may be "past our
line" or 11; for 1, one fewer must be 0). At least 3 of 4 starts fully
correct.

## Supports

- **Hop physically:** whole-body hops on a floor line beat pointing
  for most kindergarteners.
- **Counters as backup:** build the start number, add/remove one
  counter, recount — the line and the counters must agree.
- **Direction chant:** "Right is more, left is fewer!" — say it before
  every round until it is automatic.
- **Zero reminder:** keep the 0 card visible at the left end of the
  line so "one fewer than 1 is 0" has a place to land.

## Extension

Two hops: "Two more than 5?" (7) "Two fewer than 8?" (6) — hop twice
and count on. Or: "What number is one more than 9 and one fewer than
11?" (10 — a two-clue riddle.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: hopping the wrong direction (left for more), and
answering "one more than 1 is 0"-style reversals. The direction chant
plus a counters check fixes both. Never accept "one more than 10 is
0" (wrap-around thinking) — correct it at once: numbers keep going
right. Answer key: [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-04).
