# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Teen Numbers and Ten Frames

**Unit:** [U04](README.md) · **Track weeks:** 15–18 · **Sessions:** 16 ×
15–20 minutes, adult-led, oral/manipulative/drawing responses.

This guide is for the guiding adult. Learner-facing pages never link
here with answers; the [answer key](answer-key.md) is a separate file.

## Unit at a glance

| Lesson | Idea | Proof method taught |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | A full ten frame | Frame shape: full always means ten |
| L2 | Eleven to fifteen | Full frame + extras; ten-and-some-more sentence |
| L3 | Sixteen to nineteen | Same pattern; order and missing teens |
| L4 | Writing teen numerals | Build first, ten first, then check against the build |
| L5 | Ten and four in a picture; partners that make 10 | Row grouping; empty boxes are the partner |

Golden rule, all unit long: **never teach a teen number as a bare
numeral.** Always show the full frame (or bundle) and the extras
beside it, and always say the sentence "ten and ___ more make ___."
A teen number a child can build is a teen number a child understands;
a teen number only memorized will flip into 41 at the first distraction.

## Session-by-session guidance

- **Sessions 1–4 (Week 15):** the frame only. Do not rush past "full
  = ten." If Session 1's exit check fails, replay Session A before
  Lesson 02 — everything later leans on instant recognition of a full
  frame. Session 4's count-to-20 warm-up is rote only; meaning arrives
  in Weeks 16–17.
- **Sessions 5–8 (Week 16):** 11–15. Insist on the sentence every
  time, even when the build is right — the sentence is the concept in
  words. Session 8 re-teaches whichever of the frame or 11–15 is
  weaker — check both exit checks from Lessons 01–02.
- **Sessions 9–12 (Week 17):** 16–19, writing, picture, partners. Keep
  numeral cards 11–19 visible and in order all week; removing them
  early creates guessing. Lesson 04's reversal work is normal and
  unhurried — correct with the build, never with a drill.
- **Sessions 13–14 (Week 18, part 1):** the Teen Market project.
  Part 1 must end with all four cards matching their builds; if they
  disagree, the build is the referee — rebuild or rewrite, do not
  average. Part 2's customer questions are the real assessment of
  understanding: a child who can explain a pack owns the idea.
- **Sessions 15–16 (Week 18, part 2):** midyear stations + formative
  quiz, then the unit assessment. Week 18 doubles as the track's
  midyear review (scope-and-sequence Weeks 15–18), so quiz items 9–10
  and station 2 deliberately retrieve Units 01–03. Quiz misses set
  the re-teach list for Session 16 planning; do not re-teach
  everything, only the missed pattern.

## Misconceptions and re-teaching moves

| Misconception | What you see | Re-teaching move |
|---|---|---|
| Filling boxes in any order | Bottom row started before the top row is full | Re-model Lesson 01, Worked example 1; guide the learner's hand for the first two counters |
| Counting one by one past 10 | Correct totals, but slow and error-prone; no use of the frame | After the one-by-one count, re-say it the fast way: "You counted 14. Look: ten (frame) and four more" |
| Eleven/twelve treated as unrelated words | "Eleven is just eleven" with no ten inside | Build them explicitly: frame + 1, frame + 2; say "eleven means ten and one" every time for a week |
| Reversal: 41 for 14 | Dictated "fourteen" written 41 (also 31 for 13, 21 for 12) | Point at the build, say "ten first," rewrite together; never drill or punish reversals |
| Sixteen heard as sixty | "Sixteen is sixty" or a huge group built | Correct at once: "Sixty is six tens; sixteen is one ten and six" — show one bundle vs. six bundles if needed |
| 19 is the biggest number | "Nothing comes after nineteen" | Show two full frames = 20; count on: "nineteen… twenty!" |
| Partner = counters in the frame | "7's partner is 7" (the filled count, not the empty boxes) | Point at the empty boxes and count them: "These are the missing part — 3. Seven and three make ten" |
| Equal means "looks alike" (carried from Unit 03) | Two teen builds called equal because both have a full frame | Count the extras: equal means the same total, frame plus extras together |

## Readiness criteria (ready for Unit 05)

The learner is ready when, without a build shown first, they can:
write 11–19 on dictation with at most one reversal (self-corrected
against a build); build any teen 11–19 and say its sentence; name the
make-10 partner for 1–9 with at most one error; and explain why 41 is
not fourteen. If any of these is missing, use a flexible week for the
targeted re-teach below — do not start Unit 05 addition stories on a
shaky teen foundation, since Unit 05's stories will immediately use
ten-and-some-more thinking.

## Catch-up plan (by missed pattern)

| Missed pattern | Re-teach (one 15–20 minute session each) |
|---|---|
| Full frame not instant | Session A again (fresh numbers), then Lesson 01 exit check |
| 11–15 sentences shaky | Session B with 11, 13, 15 only; sentence first, build second, then together |
| 16–19 order or names | Lesson 03 guided practice rounds 5–6; number-line walk saying each teen's sentence |
| Reversals in writing | Lesson 04 guided practice rounds 1, 3, 5; build → write → check, every round |
| Partners that make 10 | Lesson 05, Worked example 2, replayed with 4, 6, 8, and 9 counters |
| Midyear retrieval (quiz 9–10) | Unit 03 Practice Session A (matching) or Unit 01 counting, as needed — a retrieval miss is a Unit 01–03 gap, not a Unit 04 failure |

## Accessibility and adult notes

- Every check is oral, pointing, drawing, or manipulative; the adult
  scribes all words and numerals the learner dictates but cannot yet
  write.
- Keep numeral cards large and high-contrast; use textured counters
  for low-vision learners; every lesson has a floor-play variant
  (frames on the floor, learner steps into boxes) as well as a table
  variant.
- The generated picture ships with a text-only alternative in
  [assets/README.md](assets/README.md); color is never the only cue
  (rows are counted, not color-matched).
- Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades
  in private storage — never in this public repository.
