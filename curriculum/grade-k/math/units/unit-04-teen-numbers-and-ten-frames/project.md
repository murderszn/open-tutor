# Project — The Teen Market

**Unit:** [U04](README.md) · **Sessions 13–14 of 16** · **Type:**
investigation and presentation project (adult-supervised, no real
money, no real shopping).

## The idea

The learner runs a pretend market stall. Every item on the stall is
sold in teen-numbered packs: a pack always holds a group of ten plus
some more ones. The learner stocks the stall, prices each pack with its
teen number and its ten-and-some-more sentence, and presents the stall
to a "customer" (the guiding adult, a stuffed animal, or a family
member who is present) — explaining every pack without counting one
by one.

## Materials

- Two ten-frame mats, 20+ counters in several colors (or play food,
  blocks, buttons)
- A tray or table corner as the "stall"; paper price cards; crayons
- Numeral cards 11–19; the generated picture
  [ten and four apples](assets/ten-and-four-apples.png) as the stall's
  "apple pack" poster (14 = 10 + 4)

## Part 1 — Stock the stall (Session 13)

1. Choose four packs to sell. Each pack must be a different teen
   number from 11 to 19, and at least one pack must be 11–14 and at
   least one must be 15–19.
2. For each pack, build it with a full frame (or bundle) + extras.
   Count only the extras; say the sentence: "Ten and ___ more make
   ___."
3. Make a price card for each pack: draw the frame + extras (dots are
   fine) and write the numeral and the equation, for example
   "14 = 10 + 4." The adult scribes any words the learner dictates.
4. Arrange the four packs on the stall in order from smallest to
   largest teen number.

**Checkpoint (end of Part 1):** all four packs built correctly, cards
match the builds, stall ordered correctly. If a card and its build
disagree, the build is the referee — rebuild or rewrite before Part 2.

## Part 2 — Price, bundle, and present (Session 14)

1. Add one "special offer" pack: a full frame of 10 with a partner
   challenge — "This pack has 7 in the frame. How many more to fill
   it?" (Answer: 3 — 7 + 3 = 10.) The learner makes a card for the
   partner pair too.
2. Present the stall to the customer: for each pack, point at the
   build and say its sentence ("Ten and six more make sixteen").
   The customer asks two questions: "Which pack has the most?" and
   "Which pack would you buy if you needed exactly 13?" The learner
   answers by pointing at the builds, not by guessing.
3. Pack up: sort all counters back into tens (full frames) and extras,
   and say how many full frames the whole stall made.

## Deliverables

1. Four price cards (drawing + numeral + equation each), matching
   their builds.
2. One partner card (a frame build with its make-10 partner).
3. An oral presentation: the sentence for each pack, plus answers to
   the customer's two questions.
4. (Optional, fun) A drawing of the stall with the packs in order.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Beginning (1) | Developing (2) | Secure (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teen builds | 0–1 packs correct as ten + extras | 2–3 packs correct | All 4 packs correct as ten + extras |
| Numerals and equations | 0–1 cards match their builds | 2–3 cards match | All 4 cards match (numeral + equation) |
| Ordering | Packs not in order | Order correct with adult help | Packs ordered smallest → largest alone |
| Partner card | Partner wrong or missing | Partner right with hints | Partner right (e.g., 7 + 3 = 10) and explained with empty boxes |
| Presentation | Sentences missing or guessed | Most sentences said, some counting one by one | Every pack explained with its ten-and-some-more sentence; both customer questions answered from the builds |

**How to use the rubric:** the adult scores privately after Session
14; the learner is told what they did well and one thing to practice
next — never a bare score. A total of 12–15 with no criterion at 1
means ready for Unit 05. Any criterion at 1 becomes a Session 16
re-teach item (see the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md)).
