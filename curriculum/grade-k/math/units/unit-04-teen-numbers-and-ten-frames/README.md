# Unit 04 — Teen Numbers and Ten Frames

**Grade:** Kindergarten · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #6](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/6), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Units 01–03 taught the learner to count sets to 10, read and write the
numerals 0–10, and compare groups and numerals. This unit answers the
next question: **what happens after 10?** The learner meets the teen
numbers 11–19 and discovers the pattern that makes them easy: every teen
number is **ten ones and some more ones**. A ten frame — two rows of
five boxes, filled left to right, top row first — makes the ten visible,
so 14 is not a mystery string of sounds but "a full frame and 4 more."
By the end, the learner can build, name, write, and take apart every
teen number, and can find the partner that makes 10.

## Essential question

How can seeing one full group of ten make the numbers 11–19 easy to
count, say, and write?

## Prerequisites

- Units 01–02: touch-count sets to 10 with one-to-one correspondence;
  state the total when asked "how many?"; count out a requested number
  1–10; name, write, and match numerals 0–10
- Unit 03: compare groups to 10 by matching and counting; one more and
  one fewer within 10
- Rote count sequence to at least 20, in order (counting past 10 by
  rote is enough to start — this unit gives those number names meaning)
- Can point, move counters, and draw simple marks (adult scribes words)

No teen-number knowledge is assumed. The words *eleven* and *twelve*
do not sound like "ten and one" or "ten and two," so this unit teaches
them as special names for 10 + 1 and 10 + 2, explicitly.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Fill a ten frame to 10, say "a full frame is ten," and count out a
   set of up to 20 objects arranged in ten frames (a full frame plus a
   partly filled frame), stating the total.
2. Compose and decompose the numbers **11–15** into ten ones and some
   more ones using a ten frame, a bundle of ten, or counters, and record
   each as a drawing or an equation (for example, 13 = 10 + 3).
3. Compose and decompose the numbers **16–19** the same way, name teen
   numbers in order from 11 to 19, and find a missing teen number in a
   sequence.
4. Read and write the numerals **11–19** and match each numeral to its
   ten-frame or bundle representation — writing the ten first (the 1
   on the left), never reversed (for example, 14, not 41).
5. Find the **partner that makes 10** for any number from 1 to 9 (for
   example, 7 needs 3, because 7 + 3 = 10), shown on a ten frame with
   empty boxes counted as the missing part.

## Vocabulary (taught with objects first, word second)

ten frame · full frame · ten ones · some more ones · teen number ·
bundle of ten · partner (that makes 10) · empty box · eleven · twelve ·
thirteen · fourteen · fifteen · sixteen · seventeen · eighteen ·
nineteen · equation (as "a number sentence with + and =")

Note for the adult: at this grade, "10 + 4 = 14" is read aloud as "ten
and four more make fourteen." The symbols are introduced gently; the
idea — one ten plus some ones — is the point, not symbol fluency.

## Materials (whole unit)

- Two ten-frame mats (drawn 2 rows × 5 boxes on paper or card; or an
  egg carton cut to 10 cups) and 20 identical counters (buttons, beans,
  or blocks) in one color, plus 10 in a second color for partner work
- A bundle of ten: 10 sticks or straws held with a rubber band, plus
  loose sticks for the "some more ones"
- Numeral cards 0–20 (adult-made, large, dark marker) and a number line
  0–20 on the floor or a strip
- Paper, crayons/markers, dot stickers (optional)
- The unit's generated picture:
  [ten and four apples](assets/ten-and-four-apples.png) — see
  [assets/README.md](assets/README.md) for its caption, alt text,
  and text-only alternative
- A small basket or tray for the project market stall (optional, fun)

All tasks are oral, pointing, drawing, or manipulative; the adult
scribes any words. Sessions are 15–20 minutes with a movement break
built in.

## For the guiding adult

Teen numbers are the first place counting stops being "just say the
next word" and starts being place value — so go slowly and keep the
ten visible at all times. Never teach a teen number as a bare numeral
to memorize: always show the full frame (or bundle) and the extra ones
beside it, and always say the sentence "ten and ___ more make ___."
Watch for the two classic traps: writing 41 for fourteen (the ear hears
"four" first, but the ten is written first) and calling 11 "ten-one"
as if it were two numbers — eleven is one number, made of ten and one.
If a session wobbles, the fix is almost always the same: back to the
frame, fill it together, count the extras. Keep completed learner work,
names, photos, schedules, and grades in private storage — never in
this public repository.
