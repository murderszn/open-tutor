# Unit Assessment — Unit 04: Teen Numbers and Ten Frames

**Unit:** [U04](../README.md) · **Session 16 of 16** · **Format:**
performance tasks, adult-observed, 15–20 minutes (split across two
sittings if the learner tires). The learner answers by pointing,
moving counters, drawing, or speaking; the adult scribes any words.
**This page contains student tasks only — answers and scoring are in
the separate [answer key](../answer-key.md).**

**How to score (adult only):** each task is scored 0 (not yet), 1
(with a hint or one error), or 2 (correct and explained with the
ten-and-some-more sentence or the frame). Total 12. 10–12 = secure;
7–9 = re-teach the missed objective in the next flexible week; 0–6 =
replay the missed lessons before Unit 05. Do not report a bare score
to the learner — tell them what they did well and one thing to
practice next.

## Task 1 — Build three teens (Objectives 1, 2, 3)

The adult says, one at a time: 12, 17, 15. For each, the learner builds
it with a full frame + extras and says the sentence ("Ten and ___
more make ___").

## Task 2 — Read and write (Objective 4)

(a) The adult shows numeral cards 19, 13, and 16 (one at a time). The
learner reads each numeral and builds it.
(b) Dictation, no build shown first: the learner writes 14, 18, and
11.

## Task 3 — Take apart (Objectives 2, 3)

The adult builds a full frame + 6 extras and asks: "What number is
this? Write the equation." Then the adult builds a full frame + 2
extras and asks the same.

## Task 4 — Partners that make 10 (Objective 5)

The adult shows three ten frames: one with 4 counters, one with 8
counters, one with 5 counters. For each, the learner says how many
empty boxes there are and the partner sentence ("4 and 6 make 10,"
and so on).

## Task 5 — Picture (Objectives 1, 3)

Show [ten and four apples](../assets/ten-and-four-apples.png).
"How many apples are there? Tell me how you counted them. If we added
one more row of 5 apples, how many would there be then?"

## Task 6 — Explain your reasoning (Objectives 3, 4)

The adult writes 41 on paper and says: "My friend says this is
fourteen. Is my friend right? Explain — use a build if you want."
Then: "Is 20 a teen number? Why or why not?"
