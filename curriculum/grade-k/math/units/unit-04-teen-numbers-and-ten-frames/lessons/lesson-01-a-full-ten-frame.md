# Lesson 01 — A Full Ten Frame

**Student goal:** I can fill a ten frame and say "a full frame is ten."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U04](../README.md) · **Session 1 of 16**
**Objectives:** 1 (ten frame to 10; count out sets to 20 in frames)

## Materials

- One ten-frame mat (2 rows of 5 boxes) and 20 counters
- Numeral card 10

## Readiness check (1 minute)

Put out 7 counters. "Touch-count them. How many?" Then 10 counters in
a scattered pile: "How many?" If the learner counts one-to-one to 10
(Units 01–02), proceed. If counting wobbles, spend this session
re-touching Unit 01 counting and try the frame tomorrow.

## Teaching

Today we meet a tool: the **ten frame**. It is a box with two rows and
five little boxes in each row. Count the boxes with the learner:
5 on top, 5 underneath — 10 boxes in all.

The rule of the ten frame: **fill the top row first, left to right,
then the bottom row, left to right.** Never skip a box, never start in
the middle. When every box has a counter, the frame is **full** — and a
full frame is always ten. You do not have to count it one by one ever
again: full frame means ten, just like that.

Say it together, pointing at a full frame: "Full frame — ten!"

Why does this help? Because after 10, counting gets long. If you can
see a full frame at a glance, you can count big groups fast: "ten…
and some more." That is the whole secret of the teen numbers, and it
starts here.

## Worked example 1 — filling a frame to 10

The adult places counters one at a time, and the learner counts aloud,
filling top row first: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 — top row full — then 6, 7, 8, 9,
10 — bottom row full.

> "Top row: five. Bottom row: five. Five and five make ten. The frame
> is full. From now on, when you see a frame like this, all filled in,
> you can just say 'ten' — no counting needed. Try it: cover the frame
> with your hand, lift it — what do you see? A full frame. How many?
> Ten!"

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the frame's shape does the counting for
us. Full always means the same number: ten.

## Worked example 2 — a frame that is NOT full

The adult fills only 7 boxes (top row 5, bottom row 2) and asks "Is the
frame full? How do you know?"

> "Top row full — five. Bottom row: two counters, and look — three
> boxes are empty. Empty boxes mean not full yet. How many are here?
> Five and two more make seven. How many empty boxes? Three. Seven
> counters and three empty boxes — seven and three make ten. The frame
> needs 3 more to be full."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** empty boxes are information, not nothing.
Counting the empty boxes tells us how many more we need — this idea
returns in Lesson 05 as partners that make 10.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds. Vary the counts; always fill top row first.

1. Fill to 4 — "Top row only, partway. How many? How many empty boxes
   in the whole frame?" *(4 counters; 6 empty boxes.)*
2. Fill to 8 — "Top row full, bottom row 3. How many? How many more to
   fill it?" *(8 counters; 2 more.)*
3. Fill to 10 — "Full frame! How many — and did you count one by one?"
   *(10; praise a "just ten" answer.)*
4. Fill to 6, then add 1 at a time to 10 — "Say the count each time:
   six… seven… eight… nine… ten — full!"
5. Start from a full frame, take away 2 — "Now how many? How many empty
   boxes?" *(8 counters; 2 empty.)*
6. Two frames: one full, one with 3 — "How many in all? Ten… and three
   more… thirteen?" *(Accept counting on from 10; the teen name is
   previewed, not required, today.)*

## Independent practice (adult watches, no hints)

The learner fills a frame from a pile of 20 counters to match a number
the adult says: 5, then 9, then 10, then 2. For each, the learner says
"full" or "not full" and, if not full, how many empty boxes there are.

## Application

"Packing lunch": the adult says a ten frame is an egg box with 10
spaces. "We have 6 eggs. Pack them in the frame, top row first. How
many spaces are still empty? How many more eggs do we need to fill the
box?" (4 empty; 4 more eggs.)

## Exit check

Show a full frame. The learner says "ten" without counting one by one.
Then show a frame with 7 counters and ask: "How many? How many more to
fill it?" (7; 3 more.) Both answers right = ready for Lesson 02.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** use larger counters and a large frame; let the learner
  place counters while the adult counts aloud; color the top row of
  the mat a different shade so "top row first" is visible.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the filled boxes and hold up
  fingers for the count; adult scribes the number.
- **Extension:** two full frames — "How many in all? Ten and ten make
  twenty." (Counting by tens preview; do not require it.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: filling boxes in any order (for example, the
bottom row first) — the count still comes out right, but the visual
pattern that makes "full = ten" instant is lost. Gently re-fill top
row first every time this week; the habit pays off in Lessons 02–03.
