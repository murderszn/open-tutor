# Lesson 02 — Eleven to Fifteen: Ten and Some More

**Student goal:** I can show 11, 12, 13, 14, and 15 as ten and some more.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U04](../README.md) · **Session 5 of 16**
**Objectives:** 2 (compose/decompose 11–15)

## Materials

- Two ten-frame mats, 20 counters, a bundle of ten sticks
- Numeral cards 11–15

## Readiness check (1 minute)

Show a full frame. "How many?" (Ten — no counting.) Show a frame with
6 counters. "How many? How many empty boxes?" If the learner answers
both (Lesson 01), proceed. If "full = ten" is not yet instant, replay
Lesson 01's worked example 1 before continuing.

## Teaching

Yesterday a full frame meant ten. Today we go past ten — and the frame
is still our friend.

Take a full frame: ten. Now put **one more** counter beside it, outside
the frame. Count: ten… and one more… **eleven**. Eleven is a funny
name — it does not sound like "ten and one" — but that is exactly what
it is: **ten ones and one more one**. Say the sentence together:
"Ten and one more make eleven."

Every teen number works this way:

- 11 = ten and 1 more
- 12 = ten and 2 more
- 13 = ten and 3 more
- 14 = ten and 4 more
- 15 = ten and 5 more

The bundle of ten shows the same idea another way: one bundle (ten
sticks tied together) plus loose sticks. Ten and some more — same
number, different picture. Whenever you are unsure, go back to the
frame: full frame first, then count the extras.

## Worked example 1 — thirteen

The adult fills a frame (ten), then places 3 counters beside it.

> "Full frame — ten. Now the extras: one, two, three. Ten and three
> more make… thirteen! Say it with me: 'Ten and three more make
> thirteen.' And we can write it as a number sentence: 13 = 10 + 3.
> The 13 is the whole group; the 10 is the full frame; the 3 is the
> extras."

Then the adult covers the extras: "What is left? Just the frame — ten."
Uncovers them: "And now? Thirteen."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** we never counted all 13 one by one. We
trusted the full frame as ten and only counted the small extra group.

## Worked example 2 — twelve, two ways

The adult shows 12 first as a full frame + 2 counters, then as a bundle
of ten sticks + 2 loose sticks.

> "Frame way: ten and two more make twelve. Bundle way: one bundle is
> ten sticks, plus two loose sticks — ten and two more make twelve.
> Same number! Twelve is a special name, like eleven: it does not say
> 'ten-two,' but it means ten and two."

The adult asks: "Which picture do you like better — the frame or the
bundle?" Either answer is fine; both show ten and some more.

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the representation can change while the
number stays the same. Ten-and-some-more is the idea, not the picture.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds, always in the order: build it, say the sentence,
show the numeral card.

1. Build 11 — "Full frame, one extra. Ten and one more make…?" *(11.)*
2. Build 14 — "Ten and four more make…?" *(14.)*
3. Build 15 — "Ten and five more make…?" *(15.)*
4. The adult builds 13; the learner names it and counts only the
   extras to prove it. *(13 = 10 + 3.)*
5. "Show me 12" with the bundle: learner makes a bundle of ten (or
   takes the ready bundle) and adds 2 loose sticks. *(12.)*
6. Error-watch round: the adult "accidentally" puts 4 extras with a
   frame that has only 9 counters (one box empty) and says "fourteen!"
   The learner checks: "Wait — the frame is not full! It is nine and
   four more — thirteen." *(9 + 4 = 13. Praise the catch loudly.)*

## Independent practice (adult watches, no hints)

The adult says a number — 11, 15, 13, 12, 14, in mixed order. For each,
the learner builds it with a frame + extras and says the "ten and ___
more make ___" sentence. Then the learner draws one of them (frame
boxes + extra dots) while the adult scribes the sentence.

## Application

"Party cups": "We need 14 cups for a party. A pack holds 10 cups. How
many packs, and how many loose cups?" The learner shows one full frame
(a pack) + 4 extras and says: "One pack of ten and 4 more make 14."

## Exit check

The adult builds 15 (frame + 5) and asks: "What number? How do you
know?" Then: "Build 11 for me." Both right, with the ten-and-some-more
sentence, = ready for Lesson 03.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** keep the extras in a different color from the frame
  counters so "ten" and "some more" stay visually separate; say the
  sentence for the learner first, then together, then alone.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the frame ("ten") then to each
  extra while counting; adult scribes the equation.
- **Extension:** "What if we had a full frame and 6 more?" (Sixteen —
  a preview of Lesson 03; accept "ten and six more" as a full answer.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: counting the whole group one by one from 1 every
time, which works but hides the ten structure. Do not forbid it — but
after the one-by-one count, always re-say it the fast way: "You counted
13. Look: ten (frame) and three more — thirteen." The structure, not
speed, is the goal.
