# Lesson 03 — Sixteen to Nineteen

**Student goal:** I can show 16, 17, 18, and 19 as ten and some more,
and put the teen numbers in order.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U04](../README.md) · **Session 9 of 16**
**Objectives:** 2, 3 (compose/decompose 16–19; order teen numbers)

## Materials

- Two ten-frame mats, 20 counters
- Numeral cards 11–19, number line 0–20

## Readiness check (1 minute)

"Build 14." The learner shows a full frame + 4 extras and says "ten
and four more make fourteen." Then: "Build 12." If both are quick
(Lesson 02), proceed. If 11–15 still wobbles, replay Lesson 02's guided
practice for 11, 13, and 15 before continuing.

## Teaching

Yesterday we learned 11–15. Today we finish the teens: 16, 17, 18, 19.
The pattern does not change — only the extras get bigger:

- 16 = ten and 6 more
- 17 = ten and 7 more
- 18 = ten and 8 more
- 19 = ten and 9 more

Listen to their names: six-**teen**, seven-**teen**, eight-**teen**,
nine-**teen**. Can you hear it? "Teen" means "ten and some more"! The
first part tells you the extras (six, seven, eight, nine), and "teen"
tells you there is a ten hiding underneath. Sixteen is not "sixty" —
sixty is much bigger, and it is not in our unit. Sixteen is ten and six.

Now the whole teen family, in order: 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19.
Say them while pointing at the number line. Notice: every one starts
with a 1 — because every one has one ten. The second digit counts the
extras. That is why the 1 comes first when we write them.

One more idea: what comes after 19? Ten and ten — two full frames —
twenty! Twenty is not a teen number (it has two tens, no extras), but
it is the number right after the teens. We will meet it properly later.

## Worked example 1 — eighteen

The adult fills a frame (ten), then places 8 counters beside it in a
row.

> "Full frame — ten. Extras: one, two, three, four, five, six, seven,
> eight. Ten and eight more make eighteen! 18 = 10 + 8. Look at the
> name: eight-teen — eight extras and a ten. And look at the numeral:
> 1 first (one ten), then 8 (eight extras)."

The adult then asks: "How many more extras would make a second full
frame?" (2 more — 8 extras + 2 = 10. Preview of partner thinking.)

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the second digit of a teen numeral is not
random — it is the count of the extras. Reading the numeral tells you
the build.

## Worked example 2 — find the missing teen

The adult lays out numeral cards 11, 12, 14, 15, 16 in order, leaving a
gap, and asks: "Which teen is hiding?"

> "Eleven, twelve… then this card says fourteen. What comes between
> twelve and fourteen? Thirteen! Ten and three more. Put 13 in the
> gap: 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16. The teens go in order, and each step adds
> exactly one more extra."

Then a second gap: 15, 16, ___, 18. "Seventeen — ten and seven more."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** order is a check, not a chant. If a
number is missing, the numbers on either side tell you what it must be.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds.

1. Build 17 — "Ten and seven more make…?" *(17.)*
2. Build 19 — "Ten and nine more make…? How many extras?" *(19; 9.)*
3. Build 16, then add one extra — "Now what number?" *(17. One more
   extra = next teen.)*
4. The adult builds 18; the learner names it, then says the equation:
   "18 = 10 + 8." *(Hint: "How many in the frame? How many extras?")*
5. Order cards: shuffle 11–19; the learner lays them in order on the
   number line, saying each teen's ten-and-some-more sentence for 16
   and 19 only (to keep the session short). *(11, 12, …, 19.)*
6. Missing teen: lay out 13, 14, 15, 17, 18. "Which teen is hiding
   between 15 and 17?" *(16.)*

## Independent practice (adult watches, no hints)

The adult says a number — 18, 16, 19, 17, in mixed order. For each, the
learner builds it and says the sentence. Then the learner draws 19 as
a frame + extras (adult scribes "19 = 10 + 9").

## Application

"Two hands and toes": "Hold up ten fingers — that is one ten. Now we
need 17 fingers and toes altogether. How many toes do we need after
the ten fingers?" The learner counts: ten fingers, then 7 toes (or
counters for toes) — "Ten and seven more make seventeen." (Movement
break built in: wiggle the 7 toes.)

## Exit check

"Build 18. What number is it? What is missing to reach 19?" (18;
one more extra.) Then: "Say the teens from 15 to 19 in order."
(15, 16, 17, 18, 19.) Both right = ready for Lesson 04.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** keep numeral cards 11–19 visible in order during
  building; for 16–19, count extras in two groups (5 + the rest) to
  avoid losing count.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the frame, then touch each
  extra while the adult counts aloud; learner says only the final
  number name.
- **Extension:** "Build 20 with two full frames. Is 20 a teen? Why
  not?" (Two tens, no extras — it ends the teen family, it does not
  join it.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: (1) hearing "sixteen" as "sixty" — correct at
once ("sixty is six tens; sixteen is one ten and six"); (2) thinking
19 is the biggest number there is — show two full frames = 20 to prove
numbers keep going. Do not introduce tens-and-ones place-value
language ("tens place") formally yet; "one ten and some ones" is
enough at this grade.
