# Answer Key — Unit 04: Teen Numbers and Ten Frames

**Unit:** [U04](README.md) · **Teacher-only.** Never show this file
beside a student question. Every answer below was solved independently
of the lesson text and reconciled with it; where a learner's valid
alternative exists (frame vs. bundle picture, "more" vs. "greater"),
it is noted as acceptable.

## Lesson 01 — A full ten frame

- Worked example 1: a full frame is 10 (5 + 5). "Full frame — ten,"
  no one-by-one counting needed.
- Worked example 2: 7 counters in the frame (top row 5, bottom row 2);
  3 empty boxes; 7 + 3 = 10.
- Guided practice: (1) 4 counters, 6 empty boxes. (2) 8 counters,
  2 more to fill. (3) 10 — full frame. (4) 6 → 7 → 8 → 9 → 10, full.
  (5) Full frame minus 2 = 8 counters, 2 empty boxes. (6) One full
  frame (10) + 3 = 13.
- Independent practice: 5 → "not full," 5 empty boxes. 9 → "not
  full," 1 empty box. 10 → "full." 2 → "not full," 8 empty boxes.
- Application (packing lunch): 6 eggs packed, 4 spaces empty, 4 more
  eggs needed to fill the box.
- Exit check: full frame = 10 (no counting). Frame with 7 counters =
  7, and 3 more to fill it.

## Lesson 02 — Eleven to fifteen

- Worked example 1: 13 = 10 + 3 ("ten and three more make thirteen").
  Covering the extras leaves just the frame: 10.
- Worked example 2: 12 = 10 + 2, as a frame + 2 counters and as a
  bundle of ten + 2 loose sticks. Both pictures show the same number.
- Guided practice: (1) 11 = 10 + 1. (2) 14 = 10 + 4. (3) 15 = 10 + 5.
  (4) The adult's build is 13 = 10 + 3. (5) Bundle of ten + 2 loose
  sticks = 12. (6) Error-watch: the frame has only 9 counters (one box
  empty), plus 4 extras = 13, not 14 — the adult's "fourteen" is wrong.
- Independent practice: 11 = 10 + 1; 15 = 10 + 5; 13 = 10 + 3;
  12 = 10 + 2; 14 = 10 + 4. Drawn frame + extras must match the
  equation the adult scribes.
- Application (party cups): one pack of ten + 4 loose cups = 14 cups.
- Exit check: the build (frame + 5) is 15; the learner's build of 11
  is a full frame + 1 extra.

## Lesson 03 — Sixteen to nineteen

- Worked example 1: 18 = 10 + 8 ("ten and eight more make eighteen").
  Two more extras would make a second full frame (8 + 2 = 10).
- Worked example 2: missing teen between 12 and 14 is 13; missing
  teen between 15 and 17 is 16 (order: 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18,
  19).
- Guided practice: (1) 17 = 10 + 7. (2) 19 = 10 + 9. (3) 16 + 1 extra
  = 17. (4) The adult's build is 18 = 10 + 8. (5) Ordered cards:
  11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19. (6) Missing teen between 15 and
  17 is 16.
- Independent practice: 18 = 10 + 8; 16 = 10 + 6; 19 = 10 + 9;
  17 = 10 + 7. Drawn 19 = frame + 9 extras, scribed "19 = 10 + 9."
- Application (hands and toes): ten fingers + 7 toes = 17.
- Exit check: the build is 18; one more extra reaches 19. Teens from
  15 to 19 in order: 15, 16, 17, 18, 19.

## Lesson 04 — Writing teen numerals

- Worked example 1: the adult's 41 is forty-one (four tens and one),
  not fourteen. Correct writing: 14 — 1 (the ten) first, then 4 (the
  extras). "Ten and four more make fourteen."
- Worked example 2: build of 17 written as 1, then 7 → 17. Written 12
  built as a frame + 2 extras ("ten and two more make twelve").
- Guided practice: (1) 13. (2) 19. (3) 11 ("eleven" = ten and one).
  (4) Written 15 built as frame + 5 extras. (5) Written 41 is
  forty-one, not fourteen; fourteen is 14. (6) Dictated "eighteen"
  written 18, checked with a frame + 8 build.
- Independent practice: numerals in order 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17,
  18, 19. Circled numeral for frame + 3 extras: 13.
- Application (mailbox numbers): house numbers 17 and 14 written
  correctly; 17 has more extras (7) than 14 (4); both have one ten.
- Exit check: dictation — 16, 12, 19 — all written ten-first; the
  build of 16 (frame + 6) proves the first.

## Lesson 05 — Ten and four in a picture

- Picture counts (verified by direct inspection): top row 5 apples,
  middle row 5 apples, bottom row 4 apples. Top two rows: 5 + 5 = 10.
  Total: 10 + 4 = 14 apples.
- Worked example 1: top row 5, middle row 5, "five and five make
  ten"; bottom row 4; "ten and four more make fourteen" — 14 apples.
- Worked example 2: frame with 6 counters → 4 empty boxes;
  6 + 4 = 10. Frame with 8 counters → 2 empty boxes; 8 + 2 = 10.
- Guided practice: (1) Top two rows 10; bottom row 4; total 14.
  (2) Eating the bottom row of 4 leaves 10 apples. (3) Frame with 4
  counters → 6 empty boxes; 4 + 6 = 10. (4) Frame with 9 counters → 1
  empty box; 9 + 1 = 10. (5) Frame with 5 counters → 5 empty boxes;
  5 + 5 = 10 (its own partner). (6) Error-watch: the adult's one-by-one
  count of 13 is wrong (a middle-row apple skipped); row counting
  gives 5 + 5 + 4 = 14.
- Independent practice: top row 5, middle row 5, bottom row 4; "ten
  and four more make 14"; frame with 3 counters → partner 7
  (3 + 7 = 10).
- Application (apple boxes): two rows of 5 (ten apples) + a third row
  of 4 = 14 apples; written 14.
- Exit check: picture total 14, explained as "five and five make ten,
  and four more make fourteen." Frame with 7 counters → partner 3
  (7 + 3 = 10).

## Practice sessions

- Session A: rounds 5 (5 empty), 10 (full), 3 (7 empty), 8 (2 empty),
  0 (10 empty), 7 (3 empty), 10 (full), 2 (8 empty). Success: top row
  first and empty-box counts right in at least 6 of 8 rounds.
- Session B: 12 = 10 + 2; 15 = 10 + 5; 11 = 10 + 1; 14 = 10 + 4;
  13 = 10 + 3. Success: at least 5 of 7 builds correct with the
  sentence.
- Session C: builds 18 = 10 + 8; 16 = 10 + 6; 19 = 10 + 9; 17 = 10 + 7.
  Ordered strip 11–19; the removed card in the example is 16.
- Session D: build → write: 14, 17, 12, 19, 11. Write → build: 15
  (frame + 5), 13 (frame + 3), 18 (frame + 8), 16 (frame + 6). Success:
  at least 7 of 9 correct, ten first.
- Session E (midyear stations): (1) 17 = frame + 7 extras. (2) 9
  buttons have more, 7 have fewer (matching or counting accepted).
  (3) Dictation: 13, 18, 11, 16. (4) Partners: frame with 2 → 8;
  with 8 → 2; with 5 → 5; with 6 → 4.
- Session F: any 6 numbers 0–10; success is correct frames, top row
  first, in at least 4 of 6 rounds (speed never counts).
- Session G: example bags — 11, 13 sorted "few extras" (11–14); 15,
  17, 19 sorted "many extras" (15–19). Success: at least 6 of 8 bags
  correct.
- Review Session 4: frames 10, 6, 9, 4, 10, 1; count-to-20 warm-up,
  then 10 → 20 counting on. Review Session 8: re-administered Lesson
  01–02 exit checks (full frame = 10; 7-counter frame = 7 with 3 more
  to fill; build 15 = frame + 5; build 11 = frame + 1).

## Project — Teen Market

- Part 1: four different teen packs, at least one from 11–14 and one
  from 15–19, ordered smallest → largest. Example stall: 12, 14, 16,
  19 — cards "12 = 10 + 2," "14 = 10 + 4," "16 = 10 + 6," "19 = 10 + 9."
  Any four-pack set meeting the rules is acceptable; cards must match
  their builds.
- Part 2: special-offer pack — frame with 7 counters, 3 empty boxes,
  partner card "7 + 3 = 10." Customer questions: most = the largest
  pack on the stall (19 in the example); exactly 13 = the 13 pack if
  stocked, otherwise "we don't have a 13 pack — the closest are 12
  and 14" (any honest build-based answer is acceptable).
- Rubric: see [project.md](project.md). Total 12–15 with no criterion
  at 1 = ready for Unit 05.

## Formative quiz (week-04_quiz.md)

1. Full frame = 10, named without one-by-one counting.
2. 13 = frame + 3 extras; "ten and three more make thirteen."
3. Frame + 8 extras = 18 = 10 + 8.
4. "Seventeen" written 17 (ten first).
5. Frame with 7 counters → 3 empty boxes; 7 + 3 = 10.
6. Ordered cards: 11, 12, 15, 17, 19.
7. Picture: 5 + 5 = 10 (top two rows), + 4 = 14 apples in all.
8. Missing teen between 16 and 18 is 17 (order: 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19).
9. Retrieval: 9 buttons have more, 7 have fewer (matching or counting
   as proof).
10. Retrieval: 8 counters + 6 more = 14 counters in all.

## Unit assessment (unit-assessment.md)

Scoring: each task 0–2 (0 not yet, 1 with a hint or one error, 2
correct and explained). Total 12. 10–12 secure; 7–9 re-teach the missed
objective; 0–6 replay the missed lessons before Unit 05.

- Task 1: 12 = 10 + 2; 17 = 10 + 7; 15 = 10 + 5 — each built as a full
  frame + extras, with its sentence.
- Task 2: (a) Cards read and built: 19 = 10 + 9; 13 = 10 + 3;
  16 = 10 + 6. (b) Dictation written: 14, 18, 11.
- Task 3: Frame + 6 extras = 16, equation "16 = 10 + 6." Frame + 2
  extras = 12, equation "12 = 10 + 2."
- Task 4: Frame with 4 → 6 empty boxes, "4 and 6 make 10." Frame with
  8 → 2 empty boxes, "8 and 2 make 10." Frame with 5 → 5 empty boxes,
  "5 and 5 make 10."
- Task 5: Picture total 14 ("five and five make ten, and four more
  make fourteen"). Adding a fourth row of 5: 14 + 5 = 19 (ten and nine
  more).
- Task 6: 41 is not fourteen — 41 is forty-one (four tens and one);
  fourteen is 14 (one ten and four). 20 is not a teen number — it is
  two tens (two full frames) with no extras; the teens end at 19.
