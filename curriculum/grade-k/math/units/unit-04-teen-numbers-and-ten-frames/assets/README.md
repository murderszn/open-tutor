# Assets — Unit 04: Teen Numbers and Ten Frames

## ten-and-four-apples.png

- **File:** `ten-and-four-apples.png` (PNG, raster, 1920 × 1280)
- **Prompt (verbatim, third and kept generation):** "A flat
  educational illustration on a plain white background for a
  kindergarten math lesson about teen numbers. Three straight
  horizontal rows of identical red apples with green leaves, all
  apples the same size, evenly spaced, clearly separated. Top row:
  exactly 5 apples. Middle row: exactly 5 apples. Bottom row: exactly
  4 apples. Simple clean style, bright high-contrast red apples, plain
  white background, no text, no numbers, no letters, no people, no
  baskets, no clutter. Count carefully before finishing: 5 apples in
  the top row, 5 apples in the middle row, 4 apples in the bottom row,
  14 apples in total."
- **Tool:** Muse built-in image generation (`media.generate_image`),
  via the structured media pipeline.
- **Date generated:** 2026-10-28
- **Generation notes:** two earlier generations were rejected after
  inspection and deleted, not shipped: the first showed 11 apples in
  its top row and 4 below (15 in all, not 10 + 4); the second showed
  9 above and 5 below (14 in all, but not 10 + 4). The third
  generation satisfied the prompt exactly and was kept; no further
  regeneration was needed.
- **Learning purpose:** Used in
  [Lesson 05](../lessons/lesson-05-ten-and-four-picture.md)
  (Worked example 1, guided practice, exit check), in the
  [formative quiz](../assessments/week-04_quiz.md) (Item 7), in the
  [unit assessment](../assessments/unit-assessment.md) (Task 5), and
  as the Teen Market project's "apple pack" poster
  ([project.md](../project.md)). The learner counts the rows the
  smart way: the top two rows together make ten (5 + 5), the bottom
  row adds 4 more, and ten and four more make fourteen (14 = 10 + 4).
- **Factual review (2026-10-28, inspected before linking):** counts
  verified by direct visual inspection — top row: 5 red apples ✓;
  middle row: 5 red apples ✓; bottom row: 4 red apples ✓; total 14 ✓.
  Every apple is clearly separated and individually countable; no
  text, numerals, letters, or people in the image. All apples are
  drawn the same size, so size is not a distractor here — grouping
  into a ten is the lesson.
- **Caption:** "An AI-generated picture of red apples in rows. Count
  the rows the smart way: how many make a ten, and how many more are
  there?"
- **Alt text:** "Three rows of red apples on a white background:
  5 apples in the top row, 5 apples in the middle row, and 4 apples
  in the bottom row. The top two rows together make ten apples, and
  the bottom row adds four more — fourteen apples in all."
- **Text-only alternative** (carries every count the activities need,
  so the activity works without the image): "Top row: 5 red apples.
  Middle row: 5 red apples. Bottom row: 4 red apples. The top two
  rows together make 10 apples. The bottom row adds 4 more. Ten and
  four more make 14 apples in all."
- **Known limitations:** AI illustration, not a photograph — stylized
  flat shapes. Do not use this image as evidence of real observations;
  counts in learner activities come from the learner's own
  manipulatives and from counting this picture together with the
  adult.
