# Project — The Counting Museum

**Type:** investigation / collection project · **Sessions:** 11, 14 (build),
15 (tours) · **Unit:** [U01](README.md)

The learner becomes a museum curator: gather three small collections, sort
each one, count each one, label the counts, arrange the collections from
fewest to most, and give a guided tour — pointing and counting each exhibit.

## Driving question

"Can you build a museum where every exhibit is sorted, counted, and labeled,
so a visitor knows exactly how many things are in it?"

## Materials

- 3 small collections of 3–10 objects each (crayons, blocks, buttons, shells,
  toy cars — safe household items; no small parts for under-3s in shared
  settings without adult supervision)
- 3 trays, boxes, or paper "exhibit frames"
- Paper strips for labels; crayons; tape
- The adult as scribe and visitor

## Steps

### Part 1 — Gather and sort (Session 11, ~20 min)

1. The learner hunts around the home (with the adult) for three kinds of
   things: "Find some crayons. Find some blocks. Find some buttons."
2. Each collection goes in its own tray — **one kind per tray** (the sorting
   rule is "kind of object").
3. "Check: does everything in each tray belong? Anything in the wrong tray?"
   Fix as needed.

### Part 2 — Count, label, arrange (Session 14, ~20 min)

4. Count each collection with one-to-one touch counting. Recount to check.
5. Make a label for each tray: the learner draws the objects (e.g., 5 small
   crayon drawings) and the adult scribes the count: "5 crayons." The learner
   may also trace or copy the numeral — optional, not assessed.
6. Arrange the three trays in order from **fewest to most**. "Which exhibit
   has the fewest? Which has the most? How do you know?" (The counts on the
   labels are the evidence.)
7. Rehearse the tour: point to each tray, say what it is, count it aloud,
   read the label.

### Part 3 — Museum tours (Session 15, ~10 min of the session)

8. The learner gives a tour to the adult (and anyone else available): walks
   from fewest to most, points, counts each exhibit, reads each label.
9. The visitor asks two questions: "Which exhibit has the most?" and "How many
   are in the middle exhibit?" The learner answers from the labels/counts.

## Deliverables

1. Three sorted, labeled exhibit trays (physical; kept at home, not in the
   repo).
2. A drawn "museum map" on paper: three boxes labeled with each collection's
   name and count (adult scribes words; learner draws and counts). The map is
   the portable record of the project.
3. The oral tour (observed by the adult).

## Rubric

Observable criteria, scored per exhibit and overall. Levels: **Getting
started** · **Getting there** · **Secure**.

| Criterion | Getting started | Getting there | Secure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sorting** (one kind per tray) | Mixed trays; rule not kept | Most objects sorted; 1–2 strays | Every object in the right tray; can name the rule |
| **Counting** (one-to-one to total) | Skips or double-counts; total unreliable | Counts with 1–2 errors, self-corrects on recount | One touch per object, accurate total, recount matches |
| **Cardinality** ("how many?") | Recounts from 1 or guesses | States the total after a prompt ("what was your last word?") | Answers "how many?" with the last count word unprompted |
| **Comparing** (fewest → most) | Cannot order the trays | Orders with adult help or 1 error | Orders fewest→most independently and justifies with counts |
| **Tour communication** | Points silently or names only | Names exhibits and counts with prompting | Leads the tour: names, counts, reads labels, answers visitor questions |

**Overall:** Secure = Secure on at least 3 criteria and no "Getting started."
Developing = mix of Getting there. Re-teach = 2+ "Getting started" (see the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md#re-teaching-paths)).

## Adult directions

- Let the collections be the learner's choice — ownership drives care.
- Scribe everything written; never correct the learner's drawings, only the
  counts (recount together).
- If a collection is too big to count (>10), celebrate it and split it into
  two exhibits — this is a feature, not a failure.
- Photograph the museum for private family records only — never in the repo.

## Accessibility

- Collections can be large, high-contrast objects (e.g., 5 big balls).
- The tour can be given by pointing while the adult counts aloud, then the
  learner confirms.
- Labels can be dot stickers (one sticker per object) instead of drawn
  pictures.
