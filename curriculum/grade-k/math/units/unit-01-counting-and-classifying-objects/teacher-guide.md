# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Counting and Classifying Objects

**For the guiding adult.** Student materials are in [lessons/](lessons/),
[practice.md](practice.md), [project.md](project.md), and
[assessments/](assessments/). **Keep this guide and the
[answer key](answer-key.md) separate from the learner** — never place a key
beside a student question.

## How to run the unit

- **Session rhythm:** 4 sessions a week, 15–20 minutes each. Short and daily
  beats long and occasional. End while it's still fun.
- **Your role:** model first, then fade. In Week 1 your hand may guide the
  learner's; by Week 3 you should be silent during independent practice.
- **Scribe everything** the learner can't write yet. Their job is thinking;
  writing is yours.
- **Every session** ends with "how many?" asked at least once — the
  cardinality habit is the unit's spine.

## Misconception table

| What you see | What it means | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Words faster than touches | One-to-one not yet stable | "Slow finger." Move counted items to a done bowl. |
| Touches one object twice | No start/stop discipline | Line objects up; always move left to right. |
| Skips an object | Tracking breakdown | Fewer objects; touch-and-say together, hand over hand. |
| Answers "how many?" by recounting | Cardinality not yet formed | "What was your last word? That's your answer." Chant it. |
| Sorts by color, then by size | Rule not held in memory | Restate the rule aloud before every placement. |
| Says the spread-out pile "has more" | Trusts eyes over count | Count both; "eyes can be tricked; counts cannot." |
| Counts out too many | No stop signal | Bowl/plate boundary; practice "freeze and check." |
| "Five!" after counting to 6 | Last-word habit missing | Error-analysis rounds (built into every lesson). |

## Re-teaching paths

- **One-to-one (Obj. 1):** replay Practice A/B at 3–5 objects for a week.
  Drop the picture rounds until touches are stable.
- **Cardinality (Obj. 2):** after every count this week, ask "how many?"
  and wait. Accept a pointed finger at the last object as an answer.
- **Count out (Obj. 3):** shrink requests to 1–5; make the bowl smaller so
  "full" is visible; let the learner say "stop" while you move objects.
- **Sorting (Obj. 4):** sort together, you placing 3 then the learner placing
  3; reduce to 2 colors with high contrast (red/yellow).
- **Comparing (Obj. 5):** match pairs physically (one red with one blue)
  before comparing counts; keep differences large (5 vs 2, not 5 vs 4).

Use the track's flexible diagnostic/catch-up weeks for re-teaching — do not
compress Unit 02 to "catch up." A shaky Unit 01 makes Unit 02 (numerals) twice
as hard.

## Readiness for Unit 02

The learner is ready when the [unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md)
shows Secure on 4 of 5 tasks with no "Not yet." Specifically: stable
one-to-one to 10, "how many?" answered with the last word, and count-out to
10. Unit 02 adds written numerals to these same skills — the counting itself
must be solid first.

## Differentiation

- **Need more time:** repeat any week as a "week 3.5" using the practice
  sessions; the 36-week track has slack built in.
- **Need more challenge:** extensions are in every lesson (scattered sets,
  three-group sorts, "one more than", drawing rounds). Do not introduce
  numerals early as a reward — Unit 02 owns them.
- **Language:** objects first, words second; home-language counting is
  counting. Visual word wall: count, sort, more, fewer, same.
- **Mobility/vision:** pointing, eye-gaze + adult touch, large high-contrast
  objects, textured counters; every check has an alternate response mode.

## Safety

Household manipulatives only; supervise small parts; check food-allergen
safety before edible counters; outdoor tasks are adult-supervised with
observation alternatives. See the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md#8-safe-materials).
