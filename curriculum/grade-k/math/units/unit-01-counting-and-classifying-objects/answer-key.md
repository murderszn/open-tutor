# Answer Key — Unit 01: Counting and Classifying Objects

**For the guiding adult only.** Every practice, quiz, assessment, and project
item is solved or scored here. Solutions were worked independently from the
student materials and reconciled with them on 2026-10-01.

## Lesson 01

- **Guided 1–4:** 2 counters → "two"; 3 → "three"; 4 → "four"; 5 → "five."
  One touch per object; same total on recount.
- **Guided 5 (error analysis):** The adult touched one of 4 counters twice and
  said "five." Correct response: "You touched that one twice — there are
  **four**." Accept: "four, you counted one twice."
- **Guided 6 (scattered 3):** "three" — accept slower counting and moving
  counted objects aside.
- **Independent:** 5 lined up → "1, 2, 3, 4, 5 — five"; recount also five.
- **Application:** 5 crackers → "five crackers."
- **Exit check:** 4 fingers → touches each once, counts "1, 2, 3, 4,"
  answers **"four."**

## Lesson 02

- **Guided 1–2:** depends on the mix you prepared — count it yourself first.
  Example: 5 red + 3 blue crayons → "color — reds here, blues here"; "five
  reds, three blues."
- **Guided 3–4:** 3 big + 3 little blocks → "size"; "three big, three
  little."
- **Guided 5 (error analysis):** "No — that blue button goes with the blues.
  The rule is color." Learner moves it.
- **Guided 6 (picture round):** the box with 4 things is the **bottom-left**
  box — **4 blue balls**. (Text alternative: "bottom-left: 4 blue balls.")
- **Independent:** e.g., 4 red + 6 blue → "I sorted by color. Four reds, six
  blues." Verify each object is in exactly one tray.
- **Application:** rule "owner" or "color"; counts as prepared.
- **Exit check:** 3 yellow + 1 green → rule **"color"**; **"three yellow, one
  green."**

## Lesson 03

- **Guided 1:** 6 → "six." **Guided 2:** 8 → "eight."
- **Guided 3:** 7 in a circle → learner marks a start, counts, answers
  **"seven."**
- **Guided 4:** 10-cube train → **"ten."**
- **Guided 5 (error analysis):** The adult counted to 6 but said "five."
  Correct: "The last word was **six**, so it is **six**."
- **Guided 6:** 9 in a circle → **"nine."**
- **Independent:** same 10 cubes as train → "ten"; as circle → "ten."
  (Arrangement doesn't change the total.)
- **Application:** 10 steps to the door → "ten steps"; back → "ten steps"
  (accept a careful recount if the learner miscounts).
- **Exit check:** 8 counters → **"eight."**

## Lesson 04

- **Guided 1:** 2 counters. **Guided 2:** 5. **Guided 3:** 3. **Guided 4:** 7.
- **Guided 5 (error analysis):** Asked for 4, adult moved 6. Correct: "No —
  that's 6. Take 2 back." Bowl then holds **4**.
- **Guided 6:** 5 drawn stars; check-count → "five."
- **Independent (picnic plates):** Plate 1 → 3; Plate 2 → 6; Plate 3 → 4.
  Each plate's check-count matches.
- **Application:** 4 forks → "four."
- **Exit check:** "Give me 6" → 6 moved, stops, recounts bowl → **"six."**

## Lesson 05

- **Guided 1:** 6 red / 4 blue → "six reds, four blues — **reds have more**,
  blues have fewer."
- **Guided 2:** 3 big / 5 little → "three big, five little — **little ones
  have more**."
- **Guided 3:** 4 yellow / 4 green → "four and four — **same number**."
- **Guided 4 (error analysis):** Spread-out 3 vs. tight 5: "No — count them.
  **Five is more than three.** The tight pile has more."
- **Guided 5:** 5 drawn circles, 2 triangles → "I drew more **circles**."
- **Independent:** e.g., 5 of one color + 4 of another → "Five [color], four
  [color]. The [first color] have more."
- **Application:** 5 grapes vs. 3 → "this plate has more"; make them same →
  remove/eat 2 from the 5-plate → 3 and 3.
- **Exit check:** 6 vs. 4 → counts "six" and "four"; "**this one** (6) **has
  more, this one** (4) **has fewer**."

## Practice sessions

- **A:** Example mix 7 red / 5 blue / 4 yellow → counts 7, 5, 4; most = red
  tray. (Your mix will differ — count it yourself first.)
- **B:** lines: 4 → "four"; 7 → "seven"; 10 → "ten." Circles: 6 → "six";
  8 → "eight"; 9 → "nine." Eyes-closed round: 10 − 3 = **8**.
- **C:** no fixed answers. Note the stable range (often breaks at 7, 9, or
  11–15) for the teacher guide's records.
- **D:** (1) 4 apples → "four." (2) 3 stars, 2 moons → **stars have more.**
  (3) 6 dots in a circle → "six." (4) tray drawing → 5 apples, 3 lemons
  (approximate drawing accepted; counts are the point). (5) 7 balls + 2 more
  = **9**.
- **E:** orders filled exactly — 2, 5, 4, 7, 6, 8, 10, 1, 9. Wrong-order
  round: asked 4, adult gave 6 → learner removes **2**, leaving **4**.

## Formative quiz

- **Q1:** 6 in a line → **"six."**
- **Q2:** 7 in a circle → start marked, **"seven."**
- **Q3:** rule = **"color"** (reds together, blues together).
- **Q4:** **4 reds, 3 blues — reds have more.**
- **Q5:** bowl holds **5**; check-count → "five."
- **Q6:** record the stable range (e.g., "stable to 16, wobbles at 17").

## Unit assessment

- **Task 1:** 8 → **"eight."** After removing 2: recount → **6**.
  "It changed because you took 2 away — I counted again."
- **Task 2:** **5 yellow, 3 green — yellow has more, green has fewer.**
  Rule: "color."
- **Task 3:** cup holds **9**; check-count → "nine." (A self-corrected
  overshoot still earns Secure.)
- **Task 4:** **4 big circles, 6 little circles — "I drew more little
  circles."**
- **Task 5:** (a) **top-left box — 5 red apples** — the most.
  (b) **None** — every box holds a different kind ("they are all different").
  Accept "none" or equivalent.
  (c) pears (2) + lemons (3) = **5**. Accept finger-counting or recounting
  the two boxes.

## Project

**Sample museum map (worked):** collections of 6 crayons, 4 blocks, 8
buttons. Labels: "6 crayons", "4 blocks", "8 buttons". Order fewest → most:
**blocks (4), crayons (6), buttons (8).**

**Sample tour responses:** "This is my crayon exhibit — 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 —
six crayons." Visitor Q: "Which exhibit has the most?" → "The buttons — eight,
that's the most." Visitor Q: "How many in the middle exhibit?" → "Six
crayons."

**Scoring examples:**
- *Secure counting:* learner counts 8 buttons 1–8, recounts 1–8. ✓
- *Getting-there counting:* learner says "1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8" then, on
  recount with finger guidance, "1...8 — eight." (Self-corrects → Getting
  there.)
- *Getting-started sorting:* red buttons in the crayon tray and the learner
  can't say why → re-teach sorting (Practice A) before scoring again.
- *Secure comparing:* "Buttons have the most because 8 is more than 6 and
  more than 4." ✓
