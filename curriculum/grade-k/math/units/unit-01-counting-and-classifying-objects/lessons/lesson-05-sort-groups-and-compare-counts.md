# Lesson 05 — Sort Groups and Compare Counts

**Student goal:** I can sort objects, count each group, and tell which group
has more, which has fewer, or that they are the same.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U01](../README.md) · **Session 13 of 16**
(second half)

## Materials

- 10 mixed counters in 2 colors (e.g., 6 red, 4 blue)
- 2 trays
- Paper and crayon

## Readiness check (1 minute)

Quick sort: "Sort these 6 mixed buttons by color. Count each group." (Lessons
02–03 re-check.) This lesson adds the comparison words.

## Teaching

After we sort and count, we can **compare** the groups. **More** means a bigger
count. **Fewer** means a smaller count. **Same number** means the counts match.
Big piles can fool your eyes — a pile that is spread out looks bigger but
might have fewer. We trust the **count**, not the look.

## Worked example 1 — 5 reds and 3 blues: which has more?

The adult sorts 5 red and 3 blue buttons into trays.

> "Reds: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 — five reds. Blues: 1, 2, 3 — three blues. Five is more
> than three, so the **reds have more**. Three is fewer than five, so the
> **blues have fewer**."
> "Check by matching: I pair one red with one blue, one red with one blue..."
> *(lines up 3 pairs)* "...and 2 reds have no partner. The reds have 2 extra —
> more reds. The count and the matching agree."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** two ways to compare — compare the counts, or
match pairs and see what's left over. Both give the same answer.

## Worked example 2 — 4 big and 4 little blocks: same number

The adult sorts 4 big and 4 little blocks.

> "Bigs: 1, 2, 3, 4 — four. Littles: 1, 2, 3, 4 — four. The counts are the
> same: **same number**. Watch the trick: I spread the big blocks far apart."
> *(spreads them)* "Which looks like more now? The bigs look bigger — but
> count: still 4 and 4. **Same number.** Eyes can be tricked; counts cannot."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** spreading changes the look, not the count. This
is why we count instead of guessing from the look.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

1. Sort 6 red / 4 blue buttons. Count each. "Which group has more? Which has
   fewer?" *(Expected: "reds have more; blues have fewer.")*
2. Sort 3 big / 5 little blocks. Count each. "Which has more?"
   *Hint: "Say both counts first, then decide."*
3. Sort 4 yellow / 4 green crayons. "More? Fewer? Or...?" *(Expected: "same
   number.")*
4. Error analysis: the adult looks at a spread-out pile of 3 and a tight pile
   of 5 and says "the spread-out one has more." Ask: "Am I right? How do we
   know for sure?" *(Expected: "no — count them; 5 is more than 3.")*
5. Draw it: draw 5 circles and 2 triangles. Count each. "Which did you draw
   more of?"

## Independent practice

The learner gets 9 mixed counters (5 of one color, 4 of another), sorts,
counts, and reports: "I sorted by color. Five [color], four [color]. The
[color] have more." Then the adult asks: "How many more?" — accept "one more"
with objects, or "I don't know yet" (that's Unit 03's work; not required).

*Alternate response mode:* the learner points to the bigger-count tray when
asked "which has more?" instead of saying the sentence.

## Application

Snack comparison: two small plates — 5 grapes on one, 3 on another (allergen
check; or use crackers). "Which plate has more? Which has fewer? Are any
plates the same?" Then the best part: "Make them the same number." (The
learner eats or moves 2 — subtraction preview through play.)

## Exit check

The adult presents two pre-sorted trays: 6 of one kind, 4 of another. "Count
each group. Which has more? Which has fewer?" Expected: counts "6" and "4,"
answers "this one has more, this one has fewer." Record: secure / developing /
not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** teach more/fewer/same with gestures (arms wide for
  more, pinched fingers for fewer, level hands for same) before requiring the
  words.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the answer tray; hold up the written
  word cards "more / fewer / same."
- **Floor-play variant:** sort beanbags by color into two hoops, count each
  hoop, stand in the hoop that has more.

## Extension

Three groups: sort by color into 3 trays, count all three, and order the
trays from fewest to most ("first, second, third"). Or: "How could you make
the two groups the same number?"

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) judging by spread instead of count — always
return to "count, don't guess from the look"; (2) "more" meaning "bigger
objects" — compare counts of the same-kind groups first; (3) thinking "fewer"
is a trick word — pair it with "more" every time until both are solid.
