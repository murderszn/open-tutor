# Lesson 04 — Count Out a Requested Number

**Student goal:** I can take exactly the number of objects someone asks for —
no more, no fewer.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U01](../README.md) · **Session 13 of 16**
(first half)

## Materials

- A pile of 12–15 counters (more than the learner will be asked for)
- A small bowl or "my plate" mat
- Paper and crayon

## Readiness check (1 minute)

"Count these 8 blocks. How many?" (Lesson 03 re-check.) Then hold up 3
fingers: "Give me this many blocks from the pile." This previews the lesson.

## Teaching

Sometimes someone doesn't ask "how many?" — they tell you a number: **"Give me
4."** Then you count objects **out of** a bigger pile and **stop** when you
reach the number. Move one, count one. When your count matches the asked
number, your hand stops.

## Worked example 1 — "Give me 4 blocks"

The adult has a pile of 12 blocks and an empty bowl.

> "You asked for 4. I move one block to the bowl: one. Another: two. Another:
> three. Another: four. **Stop** — four is the number. Let me check my bowl:
> 1, 2, 3, 4. Four blocks, exactly."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the bowl keeps the counted ones separate from the
pile, so my eyes can see when to stop. Checking the bowl proves I stopped at
the right number.

## Worked example 2 — "Draw 6 dots"

The adult draws on paper:

> "Draw 6 dots. One dot *(draws)*, two, three, four, five, six — stop. Now
> count the dots to check: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. Six dots. If I had drawn 7, I
> would cross one out — too many."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** drawing works the same as moving objects — one
mark, one word, stop at the number. Checking catches "too many," and crossing
out fixes it.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

1. "Give me 2 counters." (easy win)
2. "Give me 5 counters." *Hint: "Count into the bowl. Stop at five."*
3. "Give me 3 counters."
4. "Give me 7 counters." (first bigger request)
   *Hint if the learner overshoots: "Freeze! Count your bowl. What number did
   I ask for?"*
5. Error analysis: the adult is asked for 4, moves 6 into the bowl, and says
   "four!" Ask: "Did I give the right number? Fix it." *(Expected: "no — take
   2 back; 4 means stop at 4.")*
6. "Draw 5 stars on your paper. Count them to check."

## Independent practice

"Picnic plates": the learner sets out 3 paper plates. The adult calls orders:
"Plate 1 gets 3 crackers. Plate 2 gets 6 crackers. Plate 3 gets 4 crackers."
(Use real crackers with allergen check, or counters.) The learner fills each
plate, then counts each plate to check. The adult observes: does the hand
stop at the number?

*Alternate response mode:* the learner points to each object as the adult
moves it, saying "stop!" at the right moment — the stop decision is the skill.

## Application

Setting the table: "We need 4 forks — one for each person. Count them out."
Real requests with real consequences: too few means someone has no fork.

## Exit check

"Give me 6 counters from the pile." Expected: moves 6, one at a time, stops,
and can recount the bowl to 6. Record: secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** act out "give me" with an open hand before using the
  words; accept the home language for the request and the count.
- **Alternate response mode:** learner says "stop" while the adult moves
  objects; or the learner slides counted objects across a line on the table.
- **Floor-play variant:** "Bring me 5 beanbags from across the room — one
  trip per beanbag, count as you drop each in the basket."

## Extension

"Give me 9." Or two-step: "Give me 4 reds and 3 blues" (sort + count out).
Or: "Give me one more than 5" (previews Unit 03's "one more" reasoning —
accept "6" with objects, don't require the word yet).

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) overshooting — the hand keeps moving after the
mouth stops; the bowl/plate boundary makes "stop" visible; (2) counting the
pile instead of the moved set — always count what you moved; (3) treating the
request as "about that many" — insist on exact, then check.
