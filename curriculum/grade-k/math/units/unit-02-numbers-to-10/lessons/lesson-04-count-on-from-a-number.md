# Lesson 04 — Count On from a Number

**Student goal:** I can start counting from a number other than 1 (for
example, start at 4 and count to 9).
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U02](../README.md) · **Session 13 of 16**
(combined with Lesson 05)

## Materials

- Numeral cards 0–10
- 10 counters in a row
- Floor tape or chalk for a 0–10 number line on the floor (adult-made before
  the session)

## Readiness check (1 minute)

The learner reads the 0–10 line in order. If the order is secure, proceed.
Counting on needs the order to be automatic — otherwise the learner is
fighting two battles at once.

## Teaching

Until now we always started counting at 1. But mathematicians do not always
start over. If I already know there are 4, and I add more, I **count on**
from 4: "4... 5, 6, 7."

The adult covers the first 4 counters of a row of 9 with a cloth. "Under the
cloth there are 4 — I counted them already. Now I count the rest, starting
from 4." The adult points to the 5th counter and says "5," then 6, 7, 8, 9.

> "I did NOT say 1, 2, 3, 4 again. I started at 4 and kept going. That is
> called **counting on**. It is faster, and it is how we add later."

The adult walks the floor number line: stands on 4, then steps to 5, 6, 7,
saying each numeral. "I started at 4. I did not walk back to 1."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the number I start from is already counted — it
stays in the total. Counting on keeps what we know and adds what is new.

## Worked example 1 — count on with covered counters

The adult lines up 8 counters and covers the first 3.

> "Under the cloth: 3. I know that already. Now the rest: *(points to the
> 4th counter)* four, five, six, seven, eight. **Eight** in all."
> "Check: let me uncover and count all of them from 1." *(counts 1–8)*
> "Same answer — eight. Counting on gives the same total as starting over."

The learner tries with 5 covered and 4 showing: "Under the cloth: 5. Count
on: 6, 7, 8, 9."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the check (counting all from 1) proves counting
on is honest — same total, less work.

## Worked example 2 — count on on the number line

The adult stands the learner on the floor numeral **2**.

> "You are on 2. Take three steps forward. Say each numeral as you land:
> 3... 4... 5. Where did you land? **5**."
> "Now start at 6. Take two steps: 7... 8. You landed on **8**."

Then the adult asks: "Start at 7. How many steps to reach 10?" The learner
steps 8, 9, 10 — "three steps."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the number line shows that counting on is
moving forward from where you are — you never walk back to the start.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds.

1. 6 counters, first 2 covered — "Under the cloth: 2. Count on."
   (3, 4, 5, 6 → "six")
   *Hint: "Say the covered number first, then keep going."*
2. Start at 5, count to 8 — no counters, just the words. "5... 6, 7, 8."
3. Floor line: stand on 3, take 4 steps. "Where did you land?" (7)
4. The adult counts on WRONG: covers 4, then says "4, 6, 7, 8." Ask: "Did I
   count on right? What did I skip?"
   *(Error-analysis: expected — "you skipped 5.")*
5. "Start at 8. Count to 10." (8, 9, 10)
6. Reverse check: "I counted on from 3 and landed on 7. How many did I
   count?" *(Harder — acceptable answers: "4 more" or the learner re-walks
   it: 4, 5, 6, 7 — four steps.)*

## Independent practice

The learner covers part of a 10-counter row with the cloth, announces the
covered number, and counts on aloud to the total. Three rounds, different
splits. The adult observes: does the learner restart at 1? (Note it — that
is the exact habit this lesson breaks.)

*Alternate response mode:* the adult moves a toy car along the floor number
line while the learner calls the numerals; or the learner points along a
table number line. For limited mobility, the adult walks while the learner
directs "step... step."

## Application

Snack count-on: "You already ate 3 crackers" (show 3 empty spots on the
plate). "Here are more — count on from 3." The learner counts the new
crackers 4, 5, 6... Counting on answers "how many in all?" without
recounting the eaten ones.

## Exit check

"Start at 4. Count to 9." Then: "Start at 7. Count to 10." The learner
should say the sequences without restarting at 1. The adult records:
secure (both correct, no restart) / developing (one restart, self-corrected)
 / not yet (restarts at 1 both times).

## Supports

- **Language access:** "count on" is taught by doing it first; the adult may
  say "keep going from ___" in the home language.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing along a number line, directing the
  adult's steps, or sliding a marker.
- **Floor-play variant:** the whole lesson can live on the floor number
  line — big steps, big numerals.

## Extension

Count on from 9 to 12 (previews teen numbers, Unit 04). Or count on
*backwards*: start at 10, count back to 7 — "10, 9, 8, 7" (previews
subtraction thinking, Unit 06).

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) restarting at 1 every time — the cloth (hiding
the already-counted part) is the cure; remove it only after the habit
breaks; (2) skipping the starting number ("start at 4" → "5, 6..." is
correct to *say* 4 first, then continue — saying just "5, 6" drops the 4
from the total); (3) treating the covered number as unknown — always
announce it: "under the cloth: 4."
