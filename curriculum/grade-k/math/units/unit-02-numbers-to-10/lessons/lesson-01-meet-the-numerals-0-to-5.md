# Lesson 01 — Meet the Numerals 0–5

**Student goal:** I can name the numerals 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and match each one
to a group with that many objects.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U02](../README.md) · **Session 1 of 16**

## Materials

- Numeral cards 0–5 (adult-made: one large numeral per index card, dark
  marker, at least 3 inches tall)
- 5 identical counters and a small empty bowl (for zero)
- Tray or placemat

## Readiness check (1 minute)

Touch-count 5 counters together, then ask "how many?" If the learner counts
one-to-one and answers "five" (Unit 01), proceed. If not, spend this session
re-touching Unit 01 counting and try numerals tomorrow.

## Teaching

We already know how to count things and say how many. Now we meet the
**written marks** that stand for those counts. A written mark for a number is
called a **numeral**. The numeral is not the things — it is a short way to
write "how many."

Hold up the cards one at a time, in order, and name each with its number
word: "This mark is called **one**. It means •." Place one counter under it.
"This mark is **two**. It means ••." Place two counters. Continue to 5.

Now the special one: hold up **0**. "This mark is called **zero**. It means
**none** — an empty bowl." Show the empty bowl. "Zero counters. We write 0."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** every numeral answers "how many?" without
needing to see the things. The mark 3 means ••• even when the counters are
put away.

## Worked example 1 — match the numeral to the set

The adult lays out three small groups: 2 buttons, 4 buttons, 1 button.
The adult holds up the **3** card.

> "I am looking for three. This group — touch, count — one, two. That is
> two, not three. This group — one, two, three, four. That is four. This
> group — one. That is one. Hmm, none of these is three. I need to build
> three." *(adult counts out 3 buttons)* "One, two, three — three buttons.
> The **3** card goes here."

Then the adult hands the learner the **2** card: "Your turn. Find or build
the group that matches 2."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the numeral tells me what to look for; counting
checks whether I found it.

## Worked example 2 — zero means none

The adult shows the **0** card, then three bowls: one with 3 beans, one with
1 bean, one empty.

> "Zero means none. This bowl has beans — not zero. This bowl has one bean —
> not zero. This bowl is empty — **zero** beans. The 0 card goes with the
> empty bowl."

The adult then asks: "Show me zero fingers." *(learner makes a fist)*
"How many fingers are up? Zero — none!"

**Reasoning to name aloud:** zero is a real answer. "None" counts as
information — it tells us the bowl is empty.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds. Shuffle the 0–5 cards between rounds so order is not a crutch.

1. Hold up **1** — "What numeral is this? Build it." (1 counter)
   *Hint: "The mark with one tall line."*
2. Hold up **4** — "Name it. Build it."
3. Show 3 counters — "How many? Find the numeral card that matches."
4. Show the empty bowl — "How many beans? Find the numeral." *(Error-watch:
   the learner may say "nothing" — accept it, then name it: "Nothing — and
   the numeral for nothing is zero.")*
5. Hold up **5**, then **2** — quick-fire naming, no building.
6. The adult "reads" a card wrong on purpose: holds up **3** and says
   "five!" Ask: "Did I read it right? What does this numeral say?"
   *(Error-analysis: expected answer "no — that says three.")*

## Independent practice

The learner takes the 0–5 cards, lays them in a row, and places the matching
number of counters under each card (an empty space under 0). The adult
observes silently: correct matches? Any numeral named wrong?

*Alternate response mode:* the learner points to the matching card instead
of building sets; or the adult builds two sets and the learner places the
card on the right one. For limited mobility, eye-gaze plus adult placement
confirmed by the learner's "yes/next" works.

## Application

Numeral hunt: the adult hides the six cards around the room (in sight, not
tricky). "Find a card, bring it back, and tell me its name." Each found card
is named before the next hunt begins. Numerals now live in the real world,
not just on the table.

## Exit check

Shuffle the 0–5 cards. Hold each up once: "What numeral is this?" The
learner should name all six (order not required). The adult records:
secure (6/6) / developing (4–5) / not yet (fewer than 4).

## Supports

- **Language access:** teach "numeral" by pointing at the mark while saying
  the number word. Accept home-language number words alongside English.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing, eye-gaze with adult placement, or
  matching with a second identical set of cards.
- **Floor-play variant:** lay the cards in a big row on the floor; the
  learner hops to the card the adult names.

## Extension

Add the 6–10 cards face-down as a "mystery row" — the learner may look but
naming is not required yet (previews Lesson 03). Or match numerals 0–5 to
groups the learner draws (••• under 3).

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) naming the numeral by position in a memorized
row rather than by shape — always shuffle; (2) treating 0 as "nothing to
learn" and skipping it — 0 is the hardest and most important card; (3)
confusing the numeral's shape with the count — remind: "the mark *stands
for* the things."
