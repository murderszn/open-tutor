# Lesson 05 — Counting Scattered Groups

**Student goal:** I can count a messy, scattered group of up to 10 objects
without missing any or counting any twice, by moving or marking each one I
count.
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Unit:** [U02](../README.md) · **Session 13 of 16**
(combined with Lesson 04)

## Materials

- 10 small counters (beans, buttons)
- A "done" bowl
- Paper and crayon (for the marking strategy)
- Numeral cards 0–10

## Readiness check (1 minute)

Touch-count 6 counters in a neat row. If one-to-one is secure in a row
(Unit 01), proceed — today's challenge is keeping track when there IS no
row.

## Teaching

In a row, your finger knows where it has been. In a messy pile, it does
not — so we give the finger a helper. There are two keep-track tricks:

**Trick 1 — move it:** touch an object, say its number word, then MOVE it
into the "done" bowl. Counted things live in the bowl; uncounted things stay
on the table. You can never count one twice, because it is in the bowl.

**Trick 2 — mark it:** for drawings, touch a drawn object, say its number
word, then make a small dot or X on it. Marked things are counted.

The adult scatters 7 beans. "Watch. One *(moves a bean to the bowl)*, two
*(moves another)*, three, four, five, six, seven. The table is empty and the
bowl has them all. **Seven** beans."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** moving or marking turns a messy pile into two
piles — "not yet" and "done." The rule is simple: nothing is counted until it
moves, and nothing moves twice.

## Worked example 1 — move-and-count 8 scattered buttons

The adult scatters 8 buttons, places the empty "done" bowl to the side.

> "One." *(moves a button to the bowl)* "Two." *(moves another)* "Three,
> four, five, six, seven, eight." *(each word moves one button)*
> "Table empty. Bowl full. **Eight** buttons."
> "Check: pour the bowl out in a row and count again — 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7,
> 8. Same answer."

The learner tries with 6 scattered beans while the adult watches the hands.

**Reasoning to name aloud:** the recount check proves the trick works — the
messy count and the neat count agree.

## Worked example 2 — mark-and-count a drawing

The adult draws 9 stars scattered on paper (quick, rough stars).

> "I cannot move drawings into a bowl — so I mark them. One *(dots the first
> star)*, two *(dots the second)*... nine." *(every star gets one dot)*
> "Nine stars. See? Every star has exactly one dot — none missed, none
> double-dotted."

Then the adult draws 5 circles and marks one circle twice on purpose: "One,
two, three, three — wait." Ask the learner: "What went wrong?" *(Expected:
"you dotted that one two times.")*

**Reasoning to name aloud:** one mark per object, just like one touch per
object — the same one-to-one rule, in a new costume.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds, alternating the two tricks.

1. 5 scattered beans — move-and-count into the bowl.
   *Hint: "Touch, say, move — every time."*
2. 7 scattered buttons — move-and-count.
3. A drawing of 6 scattered apples (adult-drawn) — mark-and-count with dots.
4. 10 scattered counters — move-and-count. "This is the biggest one — slow
   wins."
   *Hint if the learner rushes: "Freeze after each move."*
5. The adult move-counts 6 beans but "forgets" to move one — counts it where
   it sits, then counts it again later: "...five, six, seven — seven!"
   Ask: "Did I count right? What rule did I break?"
   *(Error-analysis: expected — "you counted that bean twice; it never went
   in the bowl.")*
6. Missing-numeral game: the adult lays 0–10 in order, removes one card
   while the learner's eyes are closed — "which numeral is missing?" (any)

## Independent practice

The learner gets a scattered pile of 9 counters and the done bowl, counts
independently, and places the matching numeral card beside the bowl. Then a
second round with a scattered drawing to mark. The adult observes silently:
every object moved/marked once? total correct?

*Alternate response mode:* the learner directs the adult's hand ("move that
one — say three"); or uses a pointer stick to touch while the adult moves.
For limited mobility, eye-gaze selection with adult moving works.

## Application

Toy cleanup count: scatter 10 toys (or laundry items) on the floor. "Put
each toy in the basket and count as you go — one, two..." The learner counts
the cleanup. Keeping track makes a chore into math.

## Exit check

Scatter 8 counters. "Count these and tell me how many — use the bowl." The
learner should move-count to 8 and answer "eight." The adult records:
secure (8/8, one move each) / developing (correct total, one slip
self-corrected) / not yet (misses or double-counts without noticing).

## Supports

- **Language access:** "done bowl" can be named in the home language; the
  action (move = counted) carries the meaning.
- **Alternate response mode:** directing the adult's hand, pointer-stick
  touching, or sorting into two trays ("not yet" / "done").
- **Floor-play variant:** scatter large soft toys; the learner carries each
  counted toy to a blanket ("the done blanket").

## Extension

Count 10 scattered objects WITHOUT moving — only pointing — then check by
move-counting. (Shows why the trick matters.) Or play the missing-numeral
game with two cards removed.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) moving without saying the word (or saying
without moving) — the word and the move must happen together, like the touch
and the word in Unit 01; (2) "saving" a favorite object for last and losing
count — the bowl does not care about favorites; (3) believing scattered is
"harder math" rather than the same math with a tool — celebrate: "same
counting, smarter tracking."
