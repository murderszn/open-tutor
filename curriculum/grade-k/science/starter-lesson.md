# Starter Lesson — Observe a plant

**Status:** draft supplement. **Time:** three 15–20 minute sessions, with optional brief daily observations. **Goal:** Describe a visible feature of a plant, make a question, and compare what is seen at two times.

## Adult preparation and readiness

Choose a safe potted plant, a fallen leaf, or an adult-provided photo. Gather paper and crayons. Ask: “What do scientists do when they look closely?” Accept any observation or question. Do not have the learner taste or handle unknown plants; wash hands after plant contact. A photo or adult description is the accessible alternative.

## Teach and model

Explain: “An observation is something we notice with our senses. A guess can be a good question, but we need evidence before we call it a fact.” Model “I see four leaves” as observation and “the plant is thirsty” as an idea to investigate.

**Worked example 1:** At the first look, a plant has four green leaves. Record “four leaves” and draw four ovals.

**Worked example 2:** Day 1 drawing: four leaves. Day 2 drawing: five leaves, including one small leaf. Compare: “I see five leaves today; yesterday I counted four. One new leaf appeared.” This is a modeled example, not a promised plant result.

**Pacing and roles:** Session 1: adult models observation versus guess and guides task 1. Session 2: revisit the same plant (or compare the two text descriptions below), then learner draws/dictates. Session 3: compare records and complete error check together; learner shares the exit response.

## Guided and independent tasks

1. **Adult-guided:** Look for one color and one shape; state or point to each.
2. **Guided, then independent:** Draw the plant or leaf using two observable details.
3. **Independent oral/drawing response:** Finish “I notice ___.” Adult records exact words if needed.
4. **Independent oral/drawing response:** Finish “I wonder ___.” Keep the question separate from the observation.
5. **Optional modeled comparison, no supplied image needed:** Adult draws the text-described plant twice: Day 1 has four leaves; Day 2 has five leaves, one small new leaf. Learner names one similarity and one difference. Or revisit the same plant at two times.
6. **Adult-guided error check:** Adult says, “I see the plant wants water.” Ask whether this is seen directly or is a guess.

**Answers/checks:** Responses in tasks 1–2 vary but must refer to visible details. “I see the plant wants water” is a guess; a visible feature might be drooping leaves or dry soil. In the modeled comparison, both observations show a plant with leaves; Day 2 has one more small leaf. A real plant may show no change.

## Exit check and product

Learner shares one observation and one wonder. Product: a labeled drawing or adult-scribed notice/wonder card. Do not photograph or post the learner or private home setting.

## Review rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Observation | Names a visible feature | Names feature with choices | More modeling needed |
| Question | Offers a wonder distinct from what is seen | Asks after prompt | Adult models a question |
| Compare | Identifies one accurate change/similarity | Accurate with adult pointing | Revisit two clear images |

Use large images, tactile plant-safe alternatives, oral dictation, or pointing. No outdoor access is necessary. Avoid allergens or unsafe plants and supervise all handling. Results vary; one observation cannot establish why a plant changed.
