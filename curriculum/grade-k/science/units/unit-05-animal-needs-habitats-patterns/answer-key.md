# Answer Key — Unit 05

**For the guiding adult only.** Do not place this file beside student
materials. Every lesson item, practice item, quiz item, assessment
task, and project criterion is solved here; acceptable variations are
noted. Where a lesson says "adult scribes," the scribed answer counts
as the learner's answer.

## Lesson 01

- Readiness: any animal; any food it eats.
- Guided 1: food = apple/leaves card; water = cup card; shelter =
  house/nest card; air = wavy-lines card.
- Independent 2: the four needs — food, water, shelter, air (any
  order).
- Independent 3: rabbit eats grass/leaves/carrots; drinks water (puddle,
  stream, or bowl for a pet); shelters in a burrow (hutch for a pet).
- Independent 4: any need card placed beside any animal card is
  accepted if the learner names the need aloud.
- Independent 5 (error-analysis): "No — animals need **air**, not a
  bicycle."
- Application 6: learner's own food, water, shelter; air is the air
  around us. Accept any true examples.
- Exit: all four needs named unprompted + one animal example for one
  need.

## Lesson 02

- Readiness: food, water, shelter, air (re-show cards if needed).
- Guided 1: fish = orange fish under the water; pond gives it water to
  swim in and food in the water. Turtle = green turtle on the brown
  log; the log gives it a resting place (shelter) out of the water.
- Independent 2: duck is in the water, swimming.
- Independent 3: any of — duck: water/food/shelter; fish: water/food;
  frog: lily-pad shelter, flies for food; turtle: log shelter, water.
  One need per animal is enough.
- Independent 4: four animals in the picture (duck, fish, frog,
  turtle); all four need water.
- Independent 5 (error-analysis): "No — the turtle is on a log by the
  pond. The pond is its home."
- Application 6: pond — yes (water, food, shelter); desert with no
  water — no (no water to swim in); forest — no for a duck (no pond;
  other animals live there); bathtub — no (not a real home; no food
  or shelter).
- Exit: two pond animals + one correct need each, unprompted.

## Lesson 03

- Readiness: deer eats leaves/buds/grass; learner's own food (any).
- Guided 1: deer–leaves/buds; rabbit–grass; frog–flies; bear–berries
  and fish.
- Independent 2: deer–leaves, rabbit–grass, frog–flies, bear–berries
  and fish. The fox card is the distractor in this task (its food is
  introduced in the extension).
- Independent 3: No, animals do not all eat the same food. What is the
  same: every animal needs food, and every animal needs water.
- Independent 4: No — plants do not take in food like animals do;
  plants need water and light.
- Independent 5 (error-analysis): "No — a rabbit eats grass. A bear
  eats fish."
- Application 6: deer → leaves ("Deer eat leaves"); rabbit → grass;
  frog → flies; bear → berries and fish.
- Exit: two animals matched to foods unprompted + the pattern sentence
  "Different animals eat different food."
- Extension: plant-eaters — deer, rabbit; animal-eaters — frog, fox;
  both — bear.

## Lesson 04

- Readiness: duck lives in a pond (or water); learner lives in a home.
- Guided 1: duck → pond mat (water to swim in, food in the water);
  frog → pond mat (water, flies for food, lily pad for resting).
- Independent 2: pond — duck, fish, frog, turtle; forest — deer, fox,
  bear; burrow — rabbit. Accept bear on forest; accept fox on forest.
- Independent 3: one need-based reason per animal (food, water, or
  shelter found there).
- Independent 4: people live in a home (house/apartment); reasons:
  food in the kitchen, water from taps, shelter from weather, air all
  around.
- Independent 5 (error-analysis): "No — a fish needs water. A fish
  lives in the pond."
- Application 6: fish → pond; deer → forest; rabbit → burrow, each
  with a need reason ("The deer needs trees for food, so the deer goes
  to the forest").
- Exit: two animals on correct mats + one need-based reason each,
  unprompted.

## Lesson 05

- Readiness: squirrel eats nuts/seeds; keeps food by burying it in the
  ground.
- Guided 1 (tree roots): changed — the flat sidewalk now has cracks
  and bumps; need — the tree needs room (and water) to grow; evidence
  — "I see the cracks and the roots in them."
- Independent 2 (garden bed): changed — bare ground became a bed with
  soil and seeds; need — food (people grow food to eat); evidence —
  "I see the bed, the soil, and the seeds."
- Independent 3 (change or no change): duck swims — no change (using
  the pond); squirrel buries — change, food; frog sits — no change
  (resting); bird builds — change, shelter.
- Independent 4: a completed "I know because I saw…" sentence for one
  change of the learner's choice.
- Independent 5 (error-analysis): "No — it buries the nut to save
  **food** for later."
- Application 6: any real observed change (dug hole, nest, garden bed,
  worn path) with change + need + evidence; or a card-based argument
  if no walk was possible.
- Exit: one change card argued fully without help — change, need, and
  "I know because I saw…".

## Lesson 06

- Readiness: food, water, shelter, air; any correct animal–home pair.
- Modeled: frog/pond (flies, lily pad); deer/forest (buds and leaves).
- Guided 1: rabbit, burrow — grass grows around the burrow; the hole
  is its shelter.
- Independent 2: duck/pond ("swims in water, eats plants and tiny
  things, rests in tall grass"); fish/pond ("swims under the water,
  never leaves the water"); bear/forest ("eats berries and fish,
  walks among trees").
- Independent 3: learner's hidden animal + two need clues; adult
  guesses; learner confirms/corrects with evidence.
- Independent 4: the turtle lost water (and the food and shelter the
  pond gave). Safe help: leave wild turtles alone, tell an adult; for
  a pet turtle, an adult gives clean water and food.
- Independent 5 (error-analysis): "No — bears eat berries and fish.
  It is a **frog**, in the **pond**."
- Application 6: learner teaches one clue set and explains the answer
  with a need and evidence (adult-scribed).
- Exit: new clue set solved unprompted — turtle, pond (rests on a log,
  swims in the pond, eats pond plants).

## Practice sessions

- **A (needs sort):** apple → food; leaves → food; cup → water; nest
  → shelter; house → shelter; wavy lines → air; toy car and ball →
  "not a need" pile. Check: all four needs named + one example each.
- **B (pond picture match):** duck–water (or food/shelter); fish–water
  (or food); frog–shelter (lily pad) or food (flies); turtle–shelter
  (log) or water. Challenge: flies → frog; under the water → fish;
  on a log → turtle; on top of the water → duck.
- **C (who eats what):** deer–leaves, rabbit–grass, frog–flies,
  bear–berries and fish, fox–small animals. Groups: plant-eaters —
  deer, rabbit; animal-eaters — frog, fox; both — bear. Check: four of
  five matched + pattern sentence.
- **D (habitat sort):** pond — duck, fish, frog, turtle; forest —
  deer, fox, bear; burrow — rabbit. Reasons: pond — "they need water";
  forest — "they eat leaves and berries from trees"; burrow — "the
  rabbit hides and rests underground." Check: six of eight correct +
  one reason per mat.
- **E (change detectives):** squirrel — hole with a nut / food / saw
  the digging and the nut going in; bird — new nest in tree / shelter
  / saw the twigs and nest shape; roots — cracked sidewalk / room and
  water to grow / saw roots in the cracks; garden — bed with seeds /
  food / saw the bed, soil, and seeds.
- **F (guessing game, full):** two adult clue sets solved + one
  learner-made solvable clue set.

## Formative quiz

1. Food, water, shelter, air (any order).
2. Frog; lives in the pond, on a lily pad; the pond gives it water,
   flies for food, and the lily pad for shelter (any one is enough).
3. Deer eats buds and leaves; lives in the forest; because the forest
   has the trees whose buds and leaves it eats (and shelter).
4. Duck: water (to swim in), food, or shelter among grass/cattails;
   fish: water (to swim in) or food in the water. One need each.
5. Changed: the ground now has a hole with a hidden nut. Need: food
   (saved for later). Evidence: "I saw the squirrel dig, and I saw the
   nut go into the hole" (any observed detail counts).
6. Missing need: shelter (a resting place out of the water). Safe
   help: leave a wild turtle alone and tell an adult; for a pet
   turtle, an adult gives clean water, food, and a resting place.
   Accept "shelter" or "a safe resting place"; accept water if the
   learner explains the log was the turtle's dry resting place (the
   need named in the item is shelter, so guide back to it with one
   prompt if needed).

## Unit assessment

- **Task 1:** apple → food; leaves → food; cup → water; pond → water;
  nest → shelter; burrow → shelter; wavy lines → air; ball → not a
  need. Ready: six of eight correct + all four needs named.
- **Task 2:** any two of duck–water/food/shelter, fish–water/food,
  frog–shelter/food, turtle–shelter/water.
- **Task 3:** food part, water part, shelter part pointed to; top open
  for air (or room to move). At most one prompt.
- **Task 4:** squirrel — hole + hidden nut / food / "I saw the dig
  and the nut going in"; bird — new nest in tree / shelter / "I saw
  the twigs and the nest shape." Ready: one card fully argued (all
  three parts) and the other with two of three parts.
- **Task 5:** missing — water. Pet help — an adult puts clean water
  (and food) in the bowl. Wild duck — no, do not take it home; wild
  animals are left alone, watched from far away, and an adult is told
  if one looks hurt.

## Project rubric (summary scoring)

- **Needs shown:** Showing — food, water, shelter clearly shown, open
  top for air. Getting there — two needs clear, third after one
  prompt. With help — one or fewer without adult placement.
- **Habitat match:** Showing — habitat matches the animal and the
  learner says why. Getting there — match correct, reason needs one
  prompt. With help — mismatch or no reason.
- **Model explanation:** Showing — each part explained unprompted.
  Getting there — two needs unprompted, rest with prompts. With help
  — parts named but needs not explained.
- **Care and safety:** Showing — pretend materials only; learner
  states the wildlife rule. Getting there — rule stated after one
  prompt. With help — learner wants a real animal or pond water;
  adult redirects and re-teaches.
- Project complete when every criterion is at least "Getting there"
  and the model shows all four needs.
