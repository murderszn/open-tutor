# Practice Sessions — Unit 05

All sessions are 15–20 minutes, adult-supervised, and accept oral,
pointing, drawing, acting-out, or adult-scribed responses. Sessions A,
B, and D are in the unit pacing; Sessions C, E, and F are alternates
the adult can swap in for re-teaching, extension, or a rainy day.

## Session A: Needs sort game

**Use in pacing:** Session 2 (after Lesson 01).

**Materials:** the four need cards (food, water, shelter, air) and
picture cards or small objects for: an apple, leaves, a cup of water,
a nest, a house, wavy air lines, a toy car (distractor), a ball
(distractor).

**Play:**

1. Lay the four need cards in a row. Name each together: food, water,
   shelter, air.
2. The learner picks one picture card at a time and places it under the
   need it shows, saying the need aloud. (Apple → food; leaves → food;
   cup → water; nest → shelter; house → shelter; wavy lines → air.)
3. When the learner picks the toy car or the ball, the learner says:
   "That is not a need — it is a toy. Animals can live without it."
   The distractor cards go in a "not a need" pile.
4. **Check:** The learner names all four needs and points to one
   example under each, without help.

**Adult note:** If the learner puts the house under "food" or another
wrong column, ask "Do we eat a house, or do we sleep in it?" and let
the learner move it. Moving a card after thinking is learning, not
failure.

## Session B: Pond picture match

**Use in pacing:** Session 7 (after Lesson 02).

**Materials:** the pond illustration
[`assets/pond-habitat-scene.png`](assets/pond-habitat-scene.png), the
four need cards.

**Play:**

1. Look at the picture together. Name the four animals: duck, fish,
   frog, turtle.
2. For each animal, the learner places one need card beside the picture
   and says a sentence: "The pond gives the duck water." "The pond
   gives the frog a lily pad for shelter." "The pond gives the fish
   water." "The pond gives the turtle a log for shelter."
3. **Challenge round:** "Which animal eats flies?" (Frog.) "Which
   animal swims under the water?" (Fish.) "Which animal rests on a
   log?" (Turtle.) "Which animal swims on top of the water?" (Duck.)
4. **Check:** The learner matches all four animals to at least one need
   each, without help.

## Session C: Who eats what? (alternate)

**Swap in for:** Lesson 03 re-teaching or extension.

**Materials:** animal cards (deer, rabbit, frog, bear, fox) and food
cards (leaves, grass, flies, berries, fish, small animals).

**Play:**

1. Deal the animal cards in a row. Deal the food cards in a shuffled
   pile.
2. The learner matches each animal to its food(s): deer–leaves,
   rabbit–grass, frog–flies, bear–berries and fish, fox–small animals.
3. **Sort round:** Sort the animals into three groups and name each
   group: "eats plants" (deer, rabbit), "eats animals" (frog, fox),
   "eats both" (bear).
4. **Check:** The learner matches four of the five animals correctly
   and says the pattern: "Different animals eat different food."

## Session D: Habitat sort

**Use in pacing:** Session 11 (after Lesson 04).

**Materials:** habitat mats (pond, forest, burrow) and animal cards
(duck, fish, frog, turtle, deer, fox, bear, rabbit).

**Play:**

1. Lay the three mats in a row. Name each: pond, forest, burrow.
2. The learner places each animal card on its home mat: pond — duck,
   fish, frog, turtle; forest — deer, fox, bear; burrow — rabbit.
3. For each mat, the learner says one reason the animals belong there:
   pond — "they need water"; forest — "they eat leaves and berries
   from trees"; burrow — "the rabbit hides and rests underground."
4. **Check:** The learner places at least six of the eight animals
   correctly and gives one reason per mat.

## Session E: Change detectives (alternate)

**Swap in for:** Lesson 05 re-teaching or extension.

**Materials:** change cards (squirrel burying a nut, bird building a
nest, tree roots cracking a sidewalk, person building a garden bed).

**Play:**

1. The learner picks a card and plays detective: "What changed? What
   need does it meet? What did I see that shows it?"
2. Record each argument in the journal as three short lines:
   Changed / Need / I saw.
   - Squirrel: ground has a hole with a nut / food / the squirrel
     digging and the nut going in.
   - Bird: tree holds a new nest / shelter / twigs and the nest shape.
   - Roots: sidewalk cracked / room and water to grow / roots in the
     cracks.
   - Garden: bare ground became a bed with seeds / food / the bed,
     soil, and seeds.
3. **Check:** The learner completes one full three-part argument
   without help.

## Session F: Habitat guessing game, full version (alternate)

**Swap in for:** Lesson 06 extension or a review day.

**Materials:** animal cards, habitat mats, need cards.

**Play:**

1. The adult reads clue sets (food + water + shelter clues for one
   animal). The learner guesses the animal and its home, with a reason.
2. Then the learner writes (draws or dictates) two clue sets of their
   own, and the adult guesses. The learner judges the adult's guess
   with evidence.
3. **Check:** The learner solves two adult clue sets and makes one
   solvable clue set of their own.

## Animal-watch notes

**Use in pacing:** Session 3 (and any fair-weather session).

Watch birds or squirrels from a window, a porch, or a park bench — with
the adult, from a safe distance, for 5–10 minutes. Never approach,
touch, feed, or follow an animal. The adult scribes what the learner
notices: what the animal does, what it might be eating, where it
shelters. If no animal appears, use the pond picture and the animal
cards instead and record that the observation was picture-based — a
picture observation still counts as an observation at kindergarten,
and the journal must say which kind it was.
