# Lesson 01 — What do animals need?

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you learn what every animal
needs to stay alive. Animals need **food**, **water**, **shelter**, and
**air**. You will name the four needs and show one example of each."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** four need cards (drawn or
printed by the adult: food — an apple and leaves; water — a cup;
shelter — a small house or nest; air — wavy lines for breath), animal
cards (duck, deer, rabbit, fish), the science journal.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Name an animal you know." Accept any animal. "What does it eat?"
Accept any food the learner names. The adult names the pattern: "Every
animal eats something. Eating is one thing animals need. Today we find
all four needs."

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *An animal is a living thing that moves and
eats. Every animal needs four things to stay alive: **food** to eat,
**water** to drink, **shelter** — a safe place to live and rest — and
**air** to breathe. Plants are different: plants need water and light,
but plants do not eat food the way animals do. That is one pattern you
already know from watching plants grow: plants and animals both need
water, but only animals need food.*

**Modeled example 1 — a deer.** Adult holds up the deer card: "I
**notice** a deer. A deer eats buds and leaves. That is its **food**. A
deer drinks **water** from a stream. A deer rests in the forest, under
trees — that is its **shelter**. A deer breathes **air**. Food, water,
shelter, air — four needs."

**Modeled example 2 — a pet dog (or the learner's own pet, if any).**
Adult says: "A dog eats food from a bowl. A dog drinks water from a
bowl. A dog sleeps in a house or a bed — its shelter. A dog breathes
air. Same four needs, different animal. That is the pattern: the needs
stay the same, even when the animal changes."

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** Lay out the four need cards. "Point to **food**." (Apple
   and leaves card.) "Point to **water**." (Cup card.) "Point to
   **shelter**." (House/nest card.) "Point to **air**." (Wavy-lines
   card.) Repeat until the learner points to all four without help.
   Prompt with "Which one do you drink?" if the learner is stuck.

## Independent practice

2. **Independent naming:** "Name the four things animals need." Accept
   any order; the adult scribes the learner's words. (Food, water,
   shelter, air.)
3. **Independent matching:** Hold up the rabbit card. "What does a
   rabbit eat?" (Grass, leaves, carrots.) "Where does a rabbit drink?"
   (Water — a puddle, a stream, a bowl for a pet rabbit.) "Where does
   a rabbit shelter?" (A burrow, a hutch for a pet rabbit.)
4. **Independent sorting:** Mix the four need cards with two animal
   cards. "Put each need card next to the animal it helps." Any pairing
   is fine if the learner can say the need aloud; the point is naming,
   not matching one card to one animal.
5. **Error-analysis (guided):** The adult says, "Animals need food,
   water, shelter — and a bicycle!" The learner corrects: "No! Animals
   need **air**, not a bicycle." Celebrate the correction.

## Application

6. **My needs, too:** "You are an animal, too — a person! What is your
   food? Your water? Your shelter?" (Food: dinner; water: a drink;
   shelter: home.) "What air do you breathe?" (The air around us.)
   The adult scribes: "People need the same four things."

## Exit check

The learner names all four needs (food, water, shelter, air) without the
adult naming them first, and gives one animal example for one need. The
adult records the four words and the example.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** Need words are taught with a picture and
  a gesture first, the word second (mime eating, drinking, sleeping
  under a roof, breathing). Home-language need words are welcomed
  alongside the English words.
- **Alternate response mode:** Pointing to a need card counts fully;
  the adult scribes the learner's words. A learner may answer entirely
  by showing.
- **Extension:** "Does a fish need air? A fish lives in water and
  breathes there — water gives the fish what it needs to breathe. What
  else does water give a fish?" (A place to swim, food in the water.)
- **Table and floor variants:** Seated at a table with the cards in a
  row, or on the floor with the cards spread on a blanket; both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "Animals need toys / clothes / a TV."
Redirect: "Those are things people like. Needs are what keeps an animal
alive: food, water, shelter, air. A toy is fun, but an animal can live
without it."

**Common misconception:** "Shelter means a house with a door." Redirect:
"A shelter is any safe place to rest: a nest, a burrow, a log, the shade
of a tree. A duck's shelter can be tall grass by the pond."
