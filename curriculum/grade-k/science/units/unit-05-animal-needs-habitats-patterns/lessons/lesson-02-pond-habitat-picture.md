# Lesson 02 — What does the pond give its animals?

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you look closely at a pond
picture. You will find four animals and tell what the pond gives each
one: food, water, shelter, or a place to live."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** the illustration
[`../assets/pond-habitat-scene.png`](../assets/pond-habitat-scene.png),
the four need cards from Lesson 01, the science journal.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Name the four things animals need." Accept food, water, shelter, air
in any order; re-show the need cards if the learner stalls. "Today we
check: does one place — a pond — give animals all four?"

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *A **habitat** is the place an animal lives.
A pond is a habitat. The pond gives its animals water to swim in and
drink, food to eat, and safe places to rest — tall grass, a lily pad, a
log. The air is all around the pond. One place can meet many needs at
once. That is why the animal lives there.*

**Modeled example 1 — the duck.** Adult points to the duck in the
picture: "I **notice** a brown duck swimming on the blue water. The pond
gives the duck **water** to swim in. The duck eats plants and tiny
things in the water — that is its **food**. The tall grass at the edge
is a safe resting place — its **shelter**. Water, food, shelter: three
needs from one pond."

**Modeled example 2 — the frog.** Adult points to the frog on the lily
pad: "I **notice** a green frog sitting on a round green leaf floating
on the water. That leaf is a lily pad. The lily pad is the frog's
resting place — its **shelter**. The frog eats flies that come to the
pond — its **food**. The pond water is all around it — its **water**.
Same pond, different animal, same four needs."

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** "Show me the **fish** in the picture." (Orange fish
   under the water.) "What does the pond give the fish?" (Water to swim
   in; food in the water.) "Show me the **turtle**." (Green turtle on
   the brown log.) "What does the log give the turtle?" (A resting
   place — shelter — out of the water, in the sun.)

## Independent practice

2. **Independent pointing:** "Find the **duck**. Is the duck in the
   water or on the grass?" (In the water, swimming.)
3. **Independent need-naming:** "Point to one animal. Tell me one need
   the pond gives it." Repeat for all four animals (duck, fish, frog,
   turtle); the adult scribes each answer.
4. **Independent counting:** "How many animals live in this pond
   picture?" (Four: duck, fish, frog, turtle.) "How many need water?"
   (All four.)
5. **Error-analysis (guided):** The adult says, "The turtle lives in
   the sky!" The learner corrects: "No — the turtle is on a log by the
   pond. The pond is its home." Celebrate the correction.

## Application

6. **Pond or not a pond?** The adult names places: "A pond. A desert
   with no water. A forest. A bathtub." For each, the learner says
   whether a duck could live there and why. (Pond: yes — water, food,
   shelter. Desert: no — no water to swim in. Forest: no pond for a
   duck, though other animals live there. Bathtub: no — it is not a
   real home, and it has no food or shelter.)

## Exit check

The learner points to two pond animals and names one need the pond
meets for each, without the adult naming it first. The adult records
both animals and needs.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** Animal names are taught by pointing in
  the picture first, the word second. Home-language animal words are
  welcomed alongside the English words.
- **Alternate response mode:** Pointing to the animal and its need card
  counts fully; the adult scribes the learner's words. A learner may
  answer entirely by showing.
- **Extension:** "The sun is in the picture. Does the sun give the pond
  animals anything?" (Warmth and light; plants in the pond need light
  to grow, and pond animals eat those plants.) "What would happen if
  the pond dried up?" (The animals would lose water, food, and shelter
  — they would have to find a new home, or they could not live.)
- **Table and floor variants:** Seated at a table with the printed
  picture, or on the floor with the picture on a blanket; both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "The pond is only water." Redirect: point to
the grass, the cattails, the log, the lily pad — "A habitat is the
whole place: water, plants, land at the edge, and air above it. All of
it together is the animal's home."

**Common misconception:** "Fish drink water like we do." Redirect: "Fish
live in water and breathe there. The water is their whole home — they
do not drink from a cup. At kindergarten we say: fish need water to
live in."

**Answer-key reference:** [`../answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md#lesson-02) —
four animals: duck, fish, frog, turtle; duck: water/food/shelter;
frog: lily-pad shelter, flies for food; fish: water/food; turtle:
log shelter; pond or not: pond yes, desert no, forest no-for-duck,
bathtub no.
