# Lesson 05 — Animals change their world

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you become a change detective.
Animals change the world around them to get what they need. You will
tell what changed, what need it meets, and what you saw that shows it."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** change cards or drawings (a
squirrel burying a nut; a bird building a nest; tree roots cracking a
sidewalk; a person building a garden bed), the science journal.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"What does a squirrel eat?" Accept nuts, seeds. "Where does a squirrel
keep food for later?" Accept "in the ground" or "it buries it." The
adult names the question: "When a squirrel buries a nut, the ground
changes — there is a hole, and a nut is hidden in it. Today we ask:
what changed, and what need does the change meet?"

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *Animals do not only use their habitat —
they **change** it to meet a need. A squirrel digs in the ground to
hide its food. The ground changes: now it has a hole with a nut in it.
The need is food — food saved for later. A bird builds a nest from
twigs and grass. The tree changes: now it holds a nest. The need is
shelter — a safe place for eggs and baby birds. When we say an animal
changed something, we must say our **evidence** — what we saw that
shows it. "I know because I saw…" is how a scientist argues.*

**Modeled example 1 — the squirrel.** Adult holds up the squirrel
card: "I **notice** a squirrel digging with its paws, and a nut going
into the ground. What changed? The ground now has a hole and a hidden
nut. What need does it meet? **Food** — the squirrel can dig the nut
up later, when food is hard to find. My evidence is: I saw the squirrel
dig, and I saw the nut go into the hole. I know because I saw it."

**Modeled example 2 — the bird.** Adult holds up the nest card: "I
**notice** a bird carrying a twig to a tree, and a round nest made of
twigs and grass. What changed? The tree now holds a nest that was not
there before. What need does it meet? **Shelter** — a safe place for
the bird's eggs. My evidence is: I saw the twigs, and I saw the nest
shape in the tree."

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** Show the tree-roots card. "What do you notice?" (Roots
   pushing up through the sidewalk; cracks.) "What changed?" (The flat
   sidewalk now has cracks and bumps.) "What need does it meet?" (The
   tree grows and needs room — its roots push to find water and space
   to grow. Accept "the tree needs room/water to grow.") "What is your
   evidence?" (I see the cracks and the roots in them.)

## Independent practice

2. **Independent argument:** Show the garden-bed card (a person
   building a raised bed and planting seeds). "What changed?" (Bare
   ground became a garden bed with soil and seeds.) "What need does it
   meet?" (Food — people grow food to eat.) "What is your evidence?"
   (I see the bed, the soil, and the seeds.)
3. **Independent sorting — change or no change:** The adult names four
   scenes: "A duck swims in the pond. A squirrel buries a nut. A frog
   sits on a lily pad. A bird builds a nest." For each, the learner
   says "change" or "no change" and, for changes, the need it meets.
   (Swims: no change — the duck is using the pond. Buries: change —
   food. Sits: no change — the frog is resting. Builds: change —
   shelter.)
4. **Independent evidence sentence:** The learner finishes, for one
   change of their choice: "I know because I saw…" The adult scribes
   the full sentence.
5. **Error-analysis (guided):** The adult says, "A squirrel buries a
   nut because it likes digging!" The learner corrects: "No — it
   buries the nut to save **food** for later." Celebrate the
   correction.

## Application

6. **Change detective walk:** On a short walk with the adult (or
   looking out a window), the learner finds one real change an animal
   or person made: a dug hole, a nest, a garden bed, a path worn in
   grass. The learner says what changed, what need it meets, and the
   evidence. If no walk is possible, the learner uses the change cards
   instead. Never touch nests, holes, or animals; look only, from a
   safe distance.

## Exit check

The learner picks one change card and completes the argument without
help: what changed, what need it meets, and "I know because I saw…"
The adult records all three parts.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** "Change" is taught with a before/after
  gesture (flat hands, then a bump) first, the word second.
  Home-language words are welcomed alongside the English words.
- **Alternate response mode:** Pointing to the change in the picture
  and to the need card counts fully; the adult scribes the learner's
  argument. A learner may answer entirely by showing.
- **Extension:** "Can a change help one animal and hurt another?" (A
  tree's roots crack a sidewalk — good for the tree, but people must
  fix the path. A beaver dam makes a pond — good for the beaver, but
  it changes the stream for other animals. Changes have effects; that
  is why we look carefully before deciding whether a change is good.)
- **Table and floor variants:** Seated at a table with the cards in a
  row, or on the floor with the cards spread on a blanket; both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "The animal changed the world on purpose to
be naughty / to break things." Redirect: "The animal is meeting a
need — food or shelter. Breaking is not the goal; growing, eating, and
staying safe are the goals."

**Common misconception:** "Evidence means guessing." Redirect: "Evidence
is what you saw. 'I know because I saw the nut go into the hole' is
evidence. 'I think so' without seeing is a guess. Scientists show their
evidence."

squirrel: hole + hidden nut, food; bird: new nest in tree, shelter;
roots: cracked sidewalk, room/water to grow; garden: bed + seeds,
