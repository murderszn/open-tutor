# Lesson 06 — Habitat guessing game

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you play the habitat guessing
game. You will hear clues about what an animal needs, guess the animal
and its home, and explain your guess with evidence."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** animal cards, habitat mats
(pond, forest, burrow), the four need cards, the science journal.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Name the four things animals need." Accept food, water, shelter, air.
"Name one animal and its home." Accept any correct pair (duck–pond,
deer–forest, rabbit–burrow). The adult names the game: "I give clues
about needs. You guess the animal and its home — and tell me why."

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *To guess well, use the needs as clues. Ask:
what food does it eat? Where is its water? Where is its shelter? If
the clues point to water and a lily pad, the animal is a pond animal.
If the clues point to trees and leaves, the animal is a forest animal.
Guessing with clues is what scientists do — they use what they already
know to figure out something new.*

**Modeled example 1 — the pond animal.** Adult reads the clues slowly:
"I eat flies. I rest on a floating leaf. I live in water." Then models
the reasoning aloud: "Flies come to the pond. A floating leaf is a lily
pad. Water plus lily pad means pond. The animal is a **frog**, and its
home is the **pond**. My evidence: frogs eat flies, and the lily pad is
in the pond picture."

**Modeled example 2 — the forest animal.** Adult reads: "I eat buds
and leaves. I rest under trees. I need a forest to find my food." Then
models: "Buds and leaves grow on trees. Trees grow in a forest. The
animal is a **deer**, and its home is the **forest**. My evidence:
deer eat buds and leaves, and buds and leaves are in the forest."

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** Adult reads: "I eat grass. I hide in a hole in the
   ground." The learner guesses (rabbit, burrow) with the reason:
   "Grass grows around the burrow, and the hole is its shelter."
   Prompt with "What food clue did you hear?" if the learner is stuck.

## Independent practice

2. **Independent guessing:** The adult reads three more clue sets; the
   learner guesses animal + home + one reason for each:
   - "I swim in water. I eat plants and tiny things in the water. I
     rest in tall grass at the edge." (Duck, pond.)
   - "I swim under the water. I never leave the water." (Fish, pond.)
   - "I eat berries and fish. I walk among trees." (Bear, forest.)
3. **Independent clue-making:** The learner picks one animal card (kept
   hidden) and gives two need clues; the adult guesses. The learner
   confirms or corrects, using evidence: "Yes, because…" or "No,
   because my animal eats…"
4. **Independent missing-need round:** The adult says: "A turtle's
   pond dried up. What did the turtle lose?" (Water, and the food and
   shelter the pond gave.) "What can a person do?" (Leave wild turtles
   alone and tell an adult; for a pet turtle, an adult gives clean
   water and food.) The adult scribes the learner's answer.
5. **Error-analysis (guided):** The adult guesses wrong on purpose:
   "Clues: eats flies, rests on a lily pad — it must be a bear!" The
   learner corrects: "No — bears eat berries and fish. It is a
   **frog**, in the **pond**." Celebrate the correction.

## Application

6. **Teach-back:** The learner teaches the game to a family member or
   stuffed animal: read one clue set, wait for the guess, and explain
   the answer with a need and evidence. The adult scribes the
   learner's explanation.

## Exit check

The learner solves one new clue set (animal + home + one reason)
without the adult naming the answer first. Suggested new set: "I rest
on a log in the sun. I swim in the pond. I eat pond plants." (Turtle,
pond.) The adult records the animal, home, and reason.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** Clues are read slowly, one at a time,
  with the need cards shown. Home-language animal words are welcomed
  alongside the English words.
- **Alternate response mode:** Placing the animal card on the habitat
  mat counts fully; the adult scribes the learner's reason. A learner
  may answer entirely by showing.
- **Extension:** "Make a hard clue set with three clues — food, water,
  and shelter — for an animal we did not use today." (For example, a
  squirrel: eats nuts, drinks from puddles, shelters in a tree hole.)
  "Trade clues with the adult and solve each other's."
- **Table and floor variants:** Seated at a table with mats and cards,
  or on the floor with mats spread on a blanket; both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "Guessing means saying any animal." Redirect:
"A good guess uses clues. Say the clue back: 'It eats flies, so it is
not a deer — deer eat leaves.' Clues rule some animals out."

**Common misconception:** "The home is wherever the animal is right
now." Redirect: "A duck visiting a puddle still lives in the pond. A
home is where the animal finds its food, water, and shelter every day,
not where it happens to be standing."
