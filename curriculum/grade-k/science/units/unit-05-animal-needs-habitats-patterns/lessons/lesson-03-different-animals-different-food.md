# Lesson 03 — Different animals, different food

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you find a pattern: different
animals eat different food. Some animals eat plants. Some eat other
animals. Some eat both. You will match four animals to their food."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** animal cards (deer, rabbit,
frog, bear, fox), food cards or drawings (leaves, grass, flies, berries,
fish), the science journal.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"What does a deer eat?" Accept leaves, buds, grass. "What do you eat?"
Accept any food. The adult names the pattern to test: "People and deer
do not eat the same food. Today we check whether animals all eat the
same food — or different food."

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *Here is a pattern: **different animals need
different kinds of food**. A deer eats buds and leaves from trees. A
rabbit eats grass. A frog eats flies. A bear eats berries — and fish,
too. Some animals eat plants, some eat other animals, and some eat
both. But every animal needs food, and every animal needs water. The
food changes; the need for food does not.*

**Modeled example 1 — deer and rabbit.** Adult holds up the deer and
rabbit cards: "A deer eats buds and leaves. A rabbit eats grass. Both
eat plants, but not the same plant food. Deer food is up in the trees;
rabbit food is down on the ground. Different animals, different food —
that is the pattern."

**Modeled example 2 — frog and bear.** Adult holds up the frog and
bear cards: "A frog eats flies — tiny animals that fly to the pond. A
bear eats berries from bushes, and a bear can catch fish, too. The frog
eats animals; the bear eats plants and animals. Different again. If all
animals ate the same food, there would not be enough to go around —
different foods help different animals live in the same world."

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** Lay out the food cards. "Which food does a **deer**
   eat?" (Leaves/buds.) "Which food does a **rabbit** eat?" (Grass.)
   "Which food does a **frog** eat?" (Flies.) "Which foods does a
   **bear** eat?" (Berries and fish.) Prompt with "Does a deer eat
   flies?" if the learner is stuck — the learner answers "No!"

## Independent practice

2. **Independent matching:** Mix the five animal cards and five food
   cards. "Match each animal to its food." (Deer–leaves, rabbit–grass,
   frog–flies, bear–berries and fish; fox card is the distractor —
   see Task 4.)
3. **Independent pattern talk:** "Do all animals eat the same food?"
   (No.) "What is the same for every animal?" (Every animal needs
   food, and every animal needs water.)
4. **Independent plant contrast:** "Does a plant eat food like an
   animal does?" (No — plants need water and light, not food to eat.
   That is a pattern from the plant unit: animals take in food, plants
   do not.)
5. **Error-analysis (guided):** The adult says, "A rabbit eats fish!"
   The learner corrects: "No — a rabbit eats grass. A bear eats fish."
   Celebrate the correction.

## Application

6. **Feed the animals game:** The adult pretends to be each animal in
   turn ("I am a deer — I am hungry!"). The learner offers the right
   food card and says the pattern: "Deer eat leaves." Repeat for
   rabbit, frog, bear.

## Exit check

The learner matches two animals to their foods without the adult naming
the food first, and says the pattern sentence: "Different animals eat
different food." The adult records both matches and the sentence.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** Food words are taught with a picture
  first, the word second. Home-language food words are welcomed
  alongside the English words.
- **Alternate response mode:** Placing the food card next to the animal
  card counts fully; the adult scribes the learner's words. A learner
  may answer entirely by showing.
- **Extension:** "What does a **fox** eat?" (Small animals, like
  rabbits and mice.) "A fox eats other animals. A deer eats plants. A
  bear eats both. Can you sort the five animals into plant-eaters,
  animal-eaters, and both-eaters?" (Plant-eaters: deer, rabbit.
  Animal-eaters: frog, fox. Both: bear.)
- **Table and floor variants:** Seated at a table with cards in rows,
  or on the floor with cards spread on a blanket; both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "All animals eat meat / all animals eat
plants." Redirect with the cards: "Look — the deer eats leaves, the
frog eats flies. Different animals, different food. That is the
pattern we observed."

**Common misconception:** "A bear only eats berries" (or "only eats
fish"). Redirect: "A bear eats both — berries and fish. 'Both' is a
real answer. Some animals eat plants and animals."
