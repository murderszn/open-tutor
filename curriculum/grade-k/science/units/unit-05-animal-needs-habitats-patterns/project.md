# Project — Build a Habitat

**Driving question:** Can you build a home that gives your animal
everything it needs?

**Time:** three sessions (Sessions 6, 10, 13), 15–20 minutes each,
plus a showcase in Session 13. **Materials:** a shoebox or tray, blue
paper or cloth, green paper, modeling clay or play dough, twigs and
pebbles collected by the adult, cotton balls, crayons, glue sticks,
animal picture cards, the science journal. The adult cuts cardboard
and handles scissors; the learner builds, places, and explains.

## Safety

Build with pretend materials only. No real animals, no animal food, no
pond water, no soil from a place where animals live. Wildlife is
watched from a safe distance only. If the learner's animal is a pet,
the model shows the pet's real care (bowl, bed, water) — the adult
handles all real pet care.

## Part 1 — Choose and plan (Session 6)

1. The learner chooses one animal: duck, frog, turtle, deer, or rabbit.
   (Any of the five works; the adult steers a learner who is stuck
   toward the duck or frog, whose pond needs are freshest from
   Lesson 02.)
2. Together, fill in the planning card (adult scribes):

   - My animal: ________
   - Its home (habitat): ________ (pond / forest / burrow)
   - Its food in my model: ________
   - Its water in my model: ________
   - Its shelter in my model: ________
   - Air and space: the model is open at the top so air can reach the
     animal; the animal has room to move.
3. The learner draws a quick sketch of the model: where the water goes,
   where the food goes, where the shelter goes.

**Check (end of Part 1):** the planning card names a habitat and one
food, one water, and one shelter for the chosen animal.

## Part 2 — Build (Session 10)

1. Build the model from the plan: blue paper or cloth for water (pond
   animals), green paper for grass and leaves, clay for the animal,
   twigs for a nest or log, cotton balls for a burrow lining or clouds
   (clouds are decoration only — they are not a need).
2. Place the animal in the model. Check each need out loud, touching
   each part: "Food — here. Water — here. Shelter — here. Air — the
   top is open."
3. If a need is missing, add it now. A model with a missing need is not
   finished — that is the science, not a mistake: the learner is
   testing the needs–place relationship.

**Check (end of Part 2):** the model shows food, water, and shelter,
and the learner can point to each.

## Part 3 — Finish, check, and showcase (Session 13)

1. Finish any missing part. The learner completes the "my animal
   needs" card (adult scribes):

   > "My animal is the ________. It lives in the ________. It eats
   > ________. It drinks ________. Its shelter is ________. My model
   > shows all four needs: food, water, shelter, and air."
2. **Showcase:** The learner presents the model to the adult (or
   family) in 2–3 minutes: name the animal, name its habitat, point to
   each need in the model, and answer one question ("What would happen
   if the water dried up?" — the animal would lose water, food, and
   shelter, and would need a new home).
3. The adult scores with the rubric below and records one strength and
   one next step in the journal.

## Deliverables

1. The habitat model (kept or photographed by the adult for private
   storage — no learner photos go in this repository).
2. The planning card (Part 1) and the "my animal needs" card (Part 3),
   kept in the science journal.
3. The showcase explanation (adult-scribed notes of what the learner
   said, including the answer to the "water dried up" question).

## Rubric

Score each criterion as **Showing** (does it independently), **Getting
there** (does it with one prompt), or **With help** (needs the adult to
do or say most of it). The project is complete when every criterion is
at least "Getting there" and the model shows all four needs.

| Criterion | Showing | Getting there | With help |
|---|---|---|---|
| Needs shown | Model clearly shows food, water, and shelter, with an open top for air | Two needs are clear; the third is added after one prompt | One or fewer needs shown without the adult placing them |
| Habitat match | The habitat matches the animal (pond for duck/frog/turtle, forest for deer, burrow for rabbit) and the learner says why | Habitat matches, but the reason needs one prompt | Habitat does not match, or the learner cannot say why |
| Model explanation | Learner points to each part and explains the need it meets, unprompted | Learner explains two needs unprompted and the rest with prompts | Learner names parts but cannot say which need each meets |
| Care and safety | Model uses pretend materials only; learner states that real wildlife is watched, not touched | Learner states the safety rule after one prompt | Learner wants to use a real animal or real pond water; adult redirects and re-teaches |

**Scoring note for the adult:** "Showing" on all four is excellent, not
required. A learner at "Getting there" on every criterion has met the
unit's modeling objective (K-ESS3-1). If a criterion is still "With
help" after Part 3, re-teach with Practice Session D (habitat sort)
before the unit assessment, and note the re-teach in the teacher guide
log.
