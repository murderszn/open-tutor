# Teacher Guide — Unit 05

**For the guiding adult.** This guide holds session guidance,
misconceptions, and re-teaching moves. It is not a student page: keep
it separate from the learner's materials, and do not read the answer
key aloud as a lesson. Full solutions are in the
[answer key](answer-key.md).

## Session guidance

- **Sessions 1–4 (Week 19):** Teach the four needs as a chant with
  gestures (eat, drink, sleep-under-a-roof, breathe) before any card
  work. The chant is the memory hook for the whole unit; repeat it at
  the start of every session in Weeks 19–20. Session 3's animal watch
  is observation, not a field trip: 5–10 minutes, safe distance,
  window watching counts fully.
- **Sessions 5–8 (Week 20):** Lesson 02 is the unit's anchor — keep the
  pond picture visible all week. Project Part 1 should end with a
  complete planning card; a vague plan ("my animal eats stuff") is
  sent back for one more clue, not accepted. Session 8 (Lesson 03)
  introduces the food pattern; do not let it become a diet quiz — the
  pattern sentence ("Different animals eat different food") matters
  more than any single animal's diet.
- **Sessions 9–12 (Week 21):** Lesson 04's mats stay out through the
  project build. If the learner's model animal is the deer, the forest
  mat is the plan; if it is the rabbit, the burrow mat is the plan.
  Lesson 05's evidence sentence ("I know because I saw…") is the
  unit's hardest language demand — model it twice before asking for
  it, and accept a pointed-to picture as the "I saw" part.
- **Sessions 13–16 (Week 22):** The showcase (Session 13) is a
  celebration, not an exam; score the rubric during the talk, not
  after. Lesson 06 (Session 14) doubles as quiz preparation. If the
  quiz (Session 15) shows an objective unmet, use Session 16's
  re-teach time before the assessment tasks, and record the swap in
  the journal — the pacing is a guide, and the track's flexible weeks
  exist for exactly this.

## Likely misconceptions (and the redirect)

- **"Needs are things animals like"** (toys, TV, clothes): "Needs keep
  an animal alive. Likes are extra. A toy is fun, but an animal can
  live without it." (Lesson 01.)
- **"Shelter means a house"**: "A shelter is any safe resting place:
  nest, burrow, log, tall grass, shade." Show, don't just tell — point
  to the lily pad and the log in the pond picture. (Lessons 01–02.)
- **"All animals eat the same food"**: return to the cards — deer
  eats leaves, frog eats flies. The pattern is difference, not
  sameness. (Lesson 03.)
- **"Animals live anywhere"**: "An animal lives where its food, water,
  and shelter are, every day." The wrong-home game (Lesson 04) makes
  the mismatch visible. (Lesson 04.)
- **"Evidence means guessing"**: "Evidence is what you saw. 'I know
  because I saw the nut go into the hole' is evidence." (Lesson 05.)
- **"A wild animal needs rescuing"**: "Wild animals are watched from
  far away. We help pets with an adult; we leave wildlife alone and
  tell an adult if one looks hurt." Never let the learner approach,
  feed, or handle wildlife. (Lessons 05–06, Task 5.)

## Differentiation

- **Support:** need cards stay visible all unit; the adult reads every
  clue twice; pointing counts as answering; the model may be built
  with the adult's hands helping and the learner's directions leading.
- **Extension:** the fox/both-eaters sort (Lesson 03), the two-habitat
  animal drawing (Lesson 04), the "can a change help one animal and
  hurt another" discussion (Lesson 05), and learner-made clue sets
  (Lesson 06). None of these is required for readiness.
- **Language access:** teach every new word with a picture, gesture,
  or object first; welcome home-language animal, food, and habitat
  words; accept mixed-language answers and scribe the English word
  beside the learner's word.

## Readiness criteria (end of unit)

The learner is ready for Unit 06 when the unit assessment shows: Tasks
1–3 ready, and at least one of Tasks 4–5 ready. A learner who is ready
on Tasks 1–3 but not 4–5 should still move on — evidence language
keeps growing in Units 06–08 — with a note in the journal to revisit
"I know because I saw…" in the midyear review. A learner not ready on
Task 1 or 2 needs Lessons 01–02 re-taught before Unit 06; a learner
not ready on Task 3 needs the project re-run with Practice Session D.

## Safety log (adult completes)

- Animal watches: date, place, distance kept, animals seen (or
  "picture-based observation" if none seen).
- Model materials: confirm pretend materials only; no real animals,
  animal food, or pond water used.
- Any wildlife encounter: record that the learner watched from a safe
  distance and did not approach, feed, or touch.
