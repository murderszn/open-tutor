# Unit 05 — Animal Needs, Habitats, and Patterns

**Grade:** Kindergarten · **Subject:** Science · **Track weeks:** 19–22
**Backlog:** [issue #7](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/7), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

What does an animal need — and why does it live where it lives? In this
unit the learner names the four things animals need (food, water,
shelter, air), finds patterns in what different animals eat, studies a
pond picture to see what one habitat gives its animals, builds a tabletop
habitat model for one animal, and argues with evidence that animals
(and people) change their surroundings to meet a need — a squirrel
burying nuts, a bird building a nest. Every response may be spoken,
pointed to, acted out, drawn, or adult-scribed. No learner is asked to
touch, feed, catch, or approach wildlife: all animal watching is from a
safe distance, or from pictures, books, and video.

## Essential question

What do animals need to survive, and how does where an animal lives
give it what it needs?

## Prerequisites

- Unit 01: "I notice / I wonder" language, describing what is seen,
  sorting by an observable property, and keeping a science journal with
  adult scribing (used in every lesson).
- Unit 02: plan and carry out a simple observation and repeat it the
  same way (used in the animal-watch sessions).
- Unit 03: define what a good solution must do and compare two tested
  things (used when the learner judges whether a habitat model gives its
  animal everything it needs).
- The plant-needs pattern from the track's plant unit (delivered as a
  validated draft in a separate draft PR; named here in prose only):
  plants need water and light and do not eat food the way animals do.
  Lesson 01 recaps that pattern, so this unit also works for a learner
  meeting the plant pattern here for the first time.
- Can use scissors only with the adult cutting; the adult cuts all
  cardboard and handles all glue that is not a glue stick. The learner
  never handles real animals, animal food, or water from a pond.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. **Name what animals need** — food, water, shelter, and air — and give
   one example of each need from an observation, a picture, or a book.
2. **Describe patterns in animal needs** — different animals need
   different kinds of food; all animals need water; every animal needs
   a safe shelter — using at least two animal examples.
3. **Use a model to show the needs–place relationship** — build or draw a
   habitat model (pond, forest, or burrow) and explain, part by part,
   how it gives its animal food, water, shelter, and air or space.
4. **Argue with evidence that living things change their surroundings
   to meet a need** — for example, a squirrel burying nuts to store
   food, or a bird building a nest for shelter — saying what changed
   and what need it meets.
5. **Tell what is missing when a need is unmet, and what a person can
   do to help safely** — for a pet (food, water, shelter from an adult)
   or for wildlife (leave it alone, watch from far away, tell an adult).

## Vocabulary (taught with the picture or object first, word second)

animal · need · food · water · shelter · air · habitat ("the place an
animal lives") · pond · forest · burrow · pattern · change · evidence
("what I saw that shows it") · model · observe · safe distance

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS),
kindergarten performance expectations**, as published by Achieve, Inc.
(2013). The three codes below and their descriptions match the track
audit's crosswalk (verified 2026-10-01) and were re-confirmed
2026-11-04: K-LS1-1 and K-ESS2-2 against the NGSS kindergarten standards
document, and K-ESS3-1 against the NSTA-hosted display of that standard
(opened and read 2026-11-04), including its clarification examples
(deer eat buds and leaves, so they usually live in forested areas;
grasses need sunlight, so they often grow in meadows). No state
adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification is claimed.

| Code | Description |
|---|---|
| K-LS1-1 | Use observations to describe patterns of what plants and animals (including humans) need to survive. |
| K-ESS3-1 | Use a model to represent the relationship between the needs of different plants and animals (including humans) and the places they live. |
| K-ESS2-2 | Construct an argument supported by evidence for how plants and animals (including humans) can change the environment to meet their needs. |

| Unit objective | NGSS connection |
|---|---|
| 1 | K-LS1-1 — use observations (picture, book, animal watch) to name the four needs |
| 2 | K-LS1-1 — patterns: different foods for different animals; all living things need water; animals need to take in food but plants do not |
| 3 | K-ESS3-1 — the habitat model represents the needs–place relationship (deer/forest example from the clarification statement is Lesson 04's model) |
| 4 | K-ESS2-2 — squirrel-buries-food and bird-builds-nest arguments, each with stated evidence |
| 5 | K-LS1-1 and K-ESS3-1 — a missing need breaks the needs–place relationship; helping means restoring the need safely |

Crosscutting concept (K expression): **Patterns** — the same four needs
show up for every animal, while the foods and shelters differ.
Practice emphasis: asking questions, developing and using models (the
habitat model), and constructing arguments from evidence ("I know
because I saw…"). Teacher background: the NSTA position statement on
early childhood science (adult-guided observation and talk about what
was done and seen) and the repo's
`resources/biology_fundamentals.md` (adult background only; never
learner-facing at K). Assessment boundary honored: no food-chain,
ecosystem-energy, or adaptation-mechanism explanations are taught or
tested; "some animals eat plants, some eat other animals, some eat
both" is a pattern to observe, not a classification test.

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items, all large enough to handle safely: the
generated illustration
[`assets/pond-habitat-scene.png`](assets/pond-habitat-scene.png),
picture cards or drawings of a duck, fish, frog, turtle, deer, rabbit,
fox, bear, squirrel, and bird (adult draws or prints them; the cards
are reused across lessons), a shoebox or tray per habitat model,
modeling clay or play dough, blue paper or cloth for water, green paper
for grass and leaves, twigs and pebbles collected by the adult, cotton
balls, crayons, paper, glue sticks, the science journal. No glass, no
sharp tools for the learner, no small parts that could be a choking
hazard; the adult cuts cardboard and handles scissors. All sessions
are adult-supervised; wildlife is watched from a safe distance only,
never touched, fed, or followed. See the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) §8 for the full
safe-materials list.

## 16-session pacing (4 weeks × 4 sessions, 15–20 minutes each)

Sessions rotate as: observation/research lesson → model-building
session → evidence-talk circle → share-and-review, matching the track
session model. The project (Build a Habitat) runs across Sessions 6,
10, and 13. Practice Sessions C, E, and F are alternates the adult can
swap in for re-teaching or extension.

| Session | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lesson | [L1 — What do animals need?](lessons/lesson-01-what-animals-need.md) — the four needs |
| 2 | Practice | [Needs sort game](practice.md#session-a-needs-sort-game) — sort cards into food, water, shelter, air |
| 3 | Play | [Animal watch](practice.md#animal-watch-notes) — watch birds or squirrels from a safe distance (or watch the pond picture) |
| 4 | Review | Share-and-review circle; L1 exit re-checks |
| 5 | Lesson | [L2 — What does the pond give its animals?](lessons/lesson-02-pond-habitat-picture.md) — the generated illustration |
| 6 | Project | [Build a Habitat](project.md), Part 1 — choose an animal, plan what its home must have |
| 7 | Practice | [Pond picture match](practice.md#session-b-pond-picture-match) — match each pond animal to a need the pond meets |
| 8 | Lesson | [L3 — Different animals, different food](lessons/lesson-03-different-animals-different-food.md) — food patterns |
| 9 | Lesson | [L4 — Where animals live](lessons/lesson-04-where-animals-live.md) — pond, forest, burrow, home |
| 10 | Project | [Build a Habitat](project.md), Part 2 — build the model |
| 11 | Practice | [Habitat sort](practice.md#session-d-habitat-sort) — sort animals into pond, forest, burrow |
| 12 | Lesson | [L5 — Animals change their world](lessons/lesson-05-animals-change-their-world.md) — evidence arguments |
| 13 | Project | [Build a Habitat](project.md), Part 3 — finish, check every need, showcase |

Weeks 19–21 introduce and practice; week 22 (track week 22) is the
review week: the habitat guessing game, the model showcase, the
formative check, and the culminating assessment. If the learner is not
yet secure on an objective after Session 16, use the diagnostic/catch-up
guidance in the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) before starting
Unit 06 — the track's flexible weeks exist for exactly this.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, acting-out, or drawing responses; the
adult scribes. "Food / water / shelter / air" are shown with picture
cards and real objects (a cup, a toy house, a breath), not words alone.
Each lesson includes a seated-table and a floor variant, a
language-access support, an alternate response mode, and an extension.
Home-language animal and habitat words are welcomed. The generated
illustration ships with a text-only alternative containing every detail
the activity needs. A learner who cannot go outside uses the pond
picture and the animal cards for every observation task.
