# Lesson 01 — What does a plant need?

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you are a plant detective. You
will look closely at real plants and find out what plants need to live
and grow. A **need** is something a living thing must have. You will
tell me four plant needs."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** two healthy classroom plants
the learner may touch gently, a small cup of water, a dry sponge or dry
soil in a cup, the science journal, crayons.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Point to something in this room that is alive." Accept a plant, a
person, or a pet picture. "Point to something that is not alive."
Accept a chair, a book, a toy. The adult names the pattern: "Living
things need things to stay alive. Today we find out what *plants*
need."

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *A plant is alive, like you. It does not eat
food the way you do, and it does not walk to find water. It stays in one
place, so what it needs must come to it, or we must give it. Plants
need four things: **water**, **light** from the sun or a bright window,
**air**, and **soil** with **room to grow**. Water goes into the soil.
Light shines on the leaves. Air is all around, even though we cannot
see it. Soil holds the plant up and holds water for its roots.*

**Modeled example 1 — the thirsty plant.** Adult shows a plant whose
soil is dry (or says, "Pretend this plant has not had water for days"
if all plants are watered) and touches the soil: "I **notice** the soil
feels dry, like dust. The leaves droop down. I **wonder** if this plant
needs water. Let me give it a little water and watch. That is one
pattern: plants need **water**." (Modeled reasoning: notice, wonder,
test with water, name the need.)

**Modeled example 2 — the plant by the window.** Adult points to a
plant near light: "I **notice** this plant leans toward the window.
Its leaves face the bright light. I **wonder** if it is reaching for
the light. That is a second pattern: plants need **light**." Then the
adult lifts a plant gently (or points to its pot): "And it lives in
**soil**, in this pot, with **room to grow** — and **air** is all
around it, even though we cannot see it. Four needs: water, light,
air, soil and room."

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** "Show me a plant that has water." The learner points to
   damp soil or to the watering cup. "Show me where the light comes
   from." The learner points to the window. Prompt with "Touch the
   soil — wet or dry?" if the learner is stuck.
2. **Guided:** The adult names a need; the learner shows it: water
   (cup), light (window), soil (pot), air (waves a hand through the
   air). Repeat until all four are shown without help.

## Independent practice

3. **Independent naming:** "Tell me the four things a plant needs."
   (Water, light, air, soil with room to grow. Any order counts; the
   adult scribes.)
4. **Independent sorting:** The adult lays out four picture cards or
   objects: a cup of water, a sun picture, a pot of soil, a toy car.
   "Which three does a plant need? Which one does it not need?" (Needs:
   water, sun/light, soil. Not a need: the toy car.)
5. **Independent observation:** "Look at our plant. Tell me one thing
   you notice that shows it has what it needs." (Green leaves, standing
   up straight, damp soil, new leaves growing. Any one observation
   tied to a need counts.)
6. **Error-analysis (guided):** The adult says, "Plants need shoes to
   grow!" The learner corrects: "No — plants need water, light, air,
   and soil. Shoes are not a plant need." Celebrate the correction.

## Application

7. **Room hunt:** "Find one place in this room where a plant would get
   light, and one place where it would get almost no light." (Light: by
   the window. Almost no light: inside a closed cupboard — the adult
   opens it for a peek; the learner never climbs in.) "Which place
   would be better for a plant? Why?" (The window, because plants
   need light.)

## Exit check

The learner names at least three of the four plant needs without the
adult naming them first, and points to one piece of evidence for one
need (damp soil, window light, a pot of soil). The adult records the
needs named and the evidence pointed to.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** Need words are taught by touching the
  real thing first (damp soil, window light), the word second.
  Home-language words for water, light, and soil are welcomed.
- **Alternate response mode:** Pointing, acting out (arms up like a
  plant reaching for light), or drawing counts fully; the adult scribes.
- **Extension:** "Do people need the same things as plants? What is
  different?" (People need water and air too, but people need food and
  plants do not eat food the way people do. Accept any answer that
  names one same need and one different need.)
- **Table and floor variants:** Seated at a table with the plants in
  front, or on the floor beside a big plant; both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "Plants eat soil" or "plants need food like
I do." Redirect: "Soil holds the plant and holds water. Plants do not
eat food the way you do — that is one way plants and animals are
different." (This is exactly the K-LS1-1 clarification pattern; do not
go further into how plants use light.)

**Common misconception:** "Air is nothing, so plants do not need it."
Redirect: wave a hand, feel moving air, watch a leaf flutter: "Air is
there even when we cannot see it — like wind you can feel."
