# Lesson 03 — Sprout watch

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you watch your bean plant grow.
You will look at a picture of a plant growing in four stages, put the
stages in order, and measure your own plant with cubes. Plants grow in
a pattern: seed, then sprout, then stem and leaves, then a taller
plant."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** the illustration
[`../assets/bean-growth-stages.png`](../assets/bean-growth-stages.png),
the learner's planted cup, counting cubes or blocks, the science
journal, crayons, a hand lens.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Show me with your hands: how tall was your plant when we planted it?
How tall is it now?" Accept "tiny / no plant yet" for a just-planted
seed, or a hand height for a sprouted plant. The adult names the
pattern: "We watch and measure, because growing takes time and we want
to see the pattern."

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *A **sprout** is the first green shoot that
comes out of the seed. After the sprout, a **stem** grows up and
**leaves** open. The plant gets **taller** — more stem, more leaves —
as long as it has water, light, air, and soil. We **measure** how tall
it is by stacking cubes beside it and counting them. Measuring the same
way every time is how we know it really grew, and not just that we
looked from a different place.*

**Modeled example 1 — ordering the picture.** Adult points to the
illustration left to right: "Pot one: a brown bean seed on dark soil.
Pot two: a tiny green sprout with two little leaves. Pot three: a
taller stem with three leaves. Pot four: a tall plant with many big
leaves. The order is seed, sprout, taller stem, tall plant. That is the
pattern." (Modeled reasoning: describe each pot, then name the order.)

**Modeled example 2 — measuring with cubes (practice example).**
"This is a practice example, not your plant's real measurement. A
practice plant measured 2 cubes tall on Monday. On Friday it measured
5 cubes tall. 5 is more than 2, so the practice plant grew 3 cubes
taller. We count the cubes from the soil to the tallest leaf." The
adult stacks 2 cubes, then 5, so the learner sees the difference.

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** "Point to the pot with only a seed. Point to the pot
   with the tiny sprout. Point to the tallest plant." The learner
   points left to right: seed pot, sprout pot, tall plant pot. Prompt
   with "Which one has the most leaves?" if the learner is stuck.
2. **Guided:** Together, the adult and learner measure the learner's
   own plant: stack cubes from the soil line to the tallest leaf and
   count aloud. The adult scribes: "My plant is ___ cubes tall today."

## Independent practice

3. **Independent ordering:** The adult covers the picture's pots one at
   a time (or the learner closes their eyes while the adult points) and
   asks: "What comes after the sprout?" (A taller stem with leaves.)
   "What comes first, the seed or the sprout?" (The seed.)
4. **Independent journal drawing:** The learner draws today's plant:
   cup, soil, stem, and however many leaves it has today. The adult
   scribes the cube count beside the drawing.
5. **Independent pattern sentence:** "Tell me the growth pattern in
   your own words." (Seed, then sprout, then stem and leaves, then a
   taller plant. Shorter versions count if the order is right.)
6. **Error-analysis (guided):** The adult points to the tall plant pot
   and says, "This pot comes first!" The learner corrects: "No — the
   seed comes first. The tall plant comes last."

## Application

7. **Match to my plant:** "Which pot in the picture looks most like
   your plant today?" The learner points to the matching stage and
   says why (for example, "Mine has a tiny sprout, like pot two").
   If the learner's seed has not sprouted yet, the answer is pot one —
   and that is a real observation, not a failure.

## Exit check

The learner puts the four stages in order (seed, sprout, taller stem,
tall plant) using the picture or words, and measures the learner's own
plant in cubes with the adult's help, recording the count in the
journal. The adult records the order given and the cube count.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** Stage words are taught by pointing at
  the picture first, the word second. Home-language words for seed,
  sprout, and leaf are welcomed.
- **Alternate response mode:** Pointing to pots in order, acting out
  growing (curled small, then standing tall with arms as leaves), or
  drawing the stages counts fully; the adult scribes.
- **Extension:** "What do you think your plant will look like next
  week? Draw your guess, and we will check it next week." (A
  prediction, kept in the journal and revisited — right or wrong, it
  is science.)
- **Table and floor variants:** Seated at a table with the picture
  printed and the plant beside it, or on the floor with the plant on
  the tray; both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "The plant grows all at once." Redirect to
the picture order: "First the seed, then a tiny sprout — a little at a
time, day after day. That is why we draw it again and again."

**Common misconception:** "Measuring means guessing how tall it looks."
Redirect: "We stack the cubes from the soil every time, and we count
them. Same way, every time — that is how we know it grew."

**If nothing has sprouted yet:** Say so plainly — "Our seed is still
under the soil, getting ready. We keep watering and waiting, and we
draw pot one today." Never substitute a store plant and call it the
learner's bean, and never dig the seed up to check; digging breaks the
roots.

**Answer-key reference:** [`../answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md#lesson-03) —
order: seed pot, sprout pot, taller-stem pot, tall-plant pot; practice
example: 2 cubes → 5 cubes = 3 cubes taller.
