# Teacher guide — Unit 04: Plant Needs and Growth Observations

For the guiding adult. Learner-facing pages are the lessons, practice,
project, and assessments; this guide and the
[answer key](answer-key.md) stay with the adult.

## How this unit works

This unit is the track's first long observation: a plant that takes
weeks to change, tended a little at a time. The arc is deliberate —
needs first (Lesson 01), planting second (Lesson 02), watching and
measuring third (Lesson 03), one fair test fourth (Lesson 04), the
test's result fifth (Lesson 05), and the parts-and-care model last
(Lesson 06 and the showcase). Do not rush the fair test: Lesson 05
must wait until the two test plants actually look different, or until
enough days have passed that "almost the same so far" is an honest
result to record and revisit. Sessions are 15–20 minutes; stop while
the learner is still engaged rather than finishing a page. Tending
sessions are the unit's backbone — a skipped week of watering is a
lost plant, so protect the tending routine even when a lesson runs
short.

## Session-by-session guidance

| Session | Watch for | If it goes sideways |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (L1) | "Plants eat food" | "Plants do not eat the way you do. Water, light, air, soil — that is the plant list." |
| 2 (Practice A) | Sandwich sorted as a plant need | Praise the distinction: "Sandwiches are people food. Plants do not eat food like people do." |
| 3 (Practice B) | Picking leaves outdoors | "We look, we do not pick. The plant needs its leaves." Redirect to the hand lens. |
| 4 (Review) | Only one need remembered | Re-run the four-point tour: cup, window, pot, wave for air. |
| 5 (L2 + Project 1) | Flooding the cup | "Damp, not flooded." Tip out standing water with the adult's help and re-check. |
| 6 (Practice C) | Tasting a bean | Stop, remove beans from reach, restate: "Seeds and soil are not food." Wash hands. |
| 7 (L3) | "My plant is broken" (not sprouted) | "Not sprouted yet is a real result. We draw pot one today and keep waiting." Never dig it up. |
| 8 (Practice D) | Measuring from the table, not the soil | "Start the cubes at the soil every time. Same way, every time." |
| 9 (L4) | Guessing treated as pass/fail | "A prediction is a guess before looking. We will see what the plants do." |
| 10 (Project 2) | Dark plant left out all day | Put it back at once; note the extra light in the journal honestly. |
| 11 (L5) | "Taller means better" | Point to the pale bending stem: "Strong means green and standing up, not just tall." |
| 12 (Practice E) | "Water every day, lots" | "Check first: dry means water, damp means wait." |
| 13 (L6) | Leaves labeled as roots | Re-point on the real plant: roots down in soil, leaves up in light. |
| 14 (Project 3) | Invented garden story | "Tell it from the journal only. What did we actually draw?" |
| 15 (Quiz) | Quiz anxiety | Call it "show me what you can do"; allow a second sitting. |
| 16 (Assessment) | New seed feels unfair | Remind: "Scientists plant new seeds — that is the job. You already know the order." |

## Misconceptions to expect (and the fix that works)

- **"Plants eat soil / need food like people."** Fix: the Lesson 01
  distinction, repeated in Practice A (sandwich) — plants need water,
  light, air, soil; animals (including people) need food.
- **"More water is always better."** Fix: the damp-vs-flooded touch
  test (Practice E) and the finger rule: dry = water, damp = wait.
- **"A fair test means everything must be equal at the end."** Fix:
  fair means the same at the START except one thing (Lesson 04); a
  difference at the end is the finding.
- **"Taller = stronger."** Fix: compare the real test plants stem to
  stem — pale, bending, and tall is weak; green, straight, and tall is
  strong.
- **"My prediction was wrong, so I failed."** Fix: say it out loud —
  "Finding out you guessed differently IS the discovery."
- **"The seed is dead because nothing shows."** Fix: growth underground
  comes first; keep tending, keep drawing pot one, and wait at least
  two weeks before re-planting a backup seed alongside (never
  replacing the first seed's journal).

## Observation-only / simulation alternatives

- **Cannot do hands-on soil work** (allergy, setting, or access): the
  adult plants and tends; the learner observes, draws, measures with
  cubes beside the adult's plant, and judges the fair test from the
  adult's plants. All objectives can be met by observation.
- **No bright window:** use the brightest indoor spot available and
  note it in the journal; the fair test still works if the "light"
  plant gets clearly more light than the cupboard plant.
- **No outdoor access:** Practice B runs entirely indoors with
  houseplants; the illustration covers the growth stages.
- **Seeds fail to sprout:** plant a backup seed, keep the first cup's
  honest journal, and use the illustration plus Practice C's soaked
  bean for stage-order work until a sprout appears.

## Readiness: what "ready to move on" looks like

The learner names the four needs with one observed piece of evidence,
plants in order (soil, seed, water), orders the growth stages and
measures in cubes, names light as the fair test's only difference with
an observed reason, and names the three plant parts with jobs plus a
care routine (water when dry, light, gentle hands). If any piece is
missing, re-teach that lesson's activity once and re-check in one
sitting before Unit 05 (animal needs build directly on the needs
pattern: food, water, shelter, air — and on the distinction that
animals, unlike plants, need food).

## Safety checklist (adult, before Session 1)

- Seed, soil, and pollen allergies checked; hands washed after soil work.
- Bean seeds supervised at all times; no tasting; seeds out of reach
  of children under 3 in shared settings.
- Adult pours from the jug; learner uses only the small cup; water
  stays over the tray.
- Dark cupboard is for the plant only; the learner never enters it.
- Never look at the sun; outdoor time short, with sun protection.
- Wildlife observed in place only — never picked, captured, or brought
  indoors.
