# Answer key — Unit 04: Plant Needs and Growth Observations

For the guiding adult only. Keep this file away from the learner-facing
pages. Every answer below was solved independently from the lesson text
and checked against it; where an answer depends on the learner's own
plants or predictions, the acceptable range is stated instead of a
single value.

## Lesson 01

1. **Guided:** water → the watering cup or damp soil; light → the
   window; soil → the pot; air → waving a hand through the air.
2. **Guided (name-and-show):** water, light, soil, air — each shown as
   in item 1.
3. **Independent naming:** water, light, air, soil with room to grow,
   in any order.
4. **Independent sorting:** needs — cup of water, sun picture, pot of
   soil; not a need — toy car.
5. **Independent observation:** any one observation tied to a need —
   for example, green leaves (light/water), standing up straight
   (healthy growth), damp soil (water), new leaves (growing).
6. **Error-analysis:** "Plants need shoes" is wrong; plants need water,
   light, air, and soil. Shoes are not a plant need.
7. **Application:** light place — by the window; almost-no-light place
   — inside a closed cupboard. The window is better because plants
   need light.
**Exit check:** at least three of the four needs named without the
adult naming them first, plus one piece of evidence pointed to (damp
soil, window light, or a pot of soil).

## Lesson 02

1. **Guided:** first in the cup — soil (filled most of the way).
2. **Guided:** seed pushed in gently, covered, about a fingertip joint
   deep.
3. **Independent watering:** small cup poured slowly; soil damp and
   darker, no standing puddle.
4. **Independent ordering:** soil, seed, water.
5. **Independent journal drawing:** cup, soil, and the scribed note
   "My bean seed is planted. I gave it soil, a seed, and water," with
   the date.
6. **Error-analysis:** planting in an empty cup with no soil is wrong;
   a seed needs soil. Order: soil, seed, water.
7. **Application (care plan):** lives by the window for light; check
   the soil daily and water only when it feels dry; look and draw
   changes.
**Exit check:** three steps in order (soil, seed, water) without the
adult ordering them; cup left in a bright place with damp, not
flooded, soil.

## Lesson 03

1. **Guided:** seed-only pot = first pot (left); tiny-sprout pot =
   second pot; tallest plant = fourth pot (right).
2. **Guided (measure together):** count depends on the learner's real
   plant; the adult records whatever count is stacked from soil to
   tallest leaf. "Not sprouted yet" (0 cubes above soil) is a valid
   record.
3. **Independent ordering:** after the sprout comes a taller stem with
   leaves; the seed comes first, before the sprout.
4. **Independent journal drawing:** today's plant — cup, soil, stem,
   and however many leaves it has today — with the cube count scribed
   beside it.
5. **Independent pattern sentence:** seed, then sprout, then stem and
   leaves, then a taller plant (shorter versions count if the order
   is right).
6. **Error-analysis:** the tall plant pot does not come first; the
   seed comes first and the tall plant comes last.
7. **Application (match to my plant):** the pot matching the learner's
   plant today (pot one if not sprouted; pot two for a tiny sprout;
   pot three for a taller stem; pot four for a tall leafy plant), with
   a reason tied to what is seen.
**Exit check:** four stages in order (seed, sprout, taller stem, tall
plant) plus the learner's plant measured in cubes and recorded.
**Practice example (from the lesson):** 2 cubes → 5 cubes = 3 cubes
taller.

## Lesson 04

1. **Guided:** same — seed, soil, cup, water. Different — light only
   (window vs. cupboard).
2. **Guided (prediction):** any prediction about the two plants and
   light, scribed verbatim (for example, "the light plant will grow
   greener and stronger"). There is no wrong prediction before the
   test.
3. **Independent setup:** Light plant by the window; Dark plant handed
   to the adult for the cupboard; labels matched to the right plants.
4. **Independent journal drawing:** both test plants drawn side by
   side before the test starts.
5. **Independent fair-or-not:** (a) same plants, same water, light vs.
   dark — fair. (b) different water AND different light — not fair
   (two things differ).
6. **Error-analysis:** watering the light plant daily and the dark
   plant never is not fair; same water for both, or the test cannot
   tell whether light or water caused the difference.
7. **Application (care routine):** the adult waters both the same
   small amount on the same days; the learner checks and draws; the
   dark plant comes out only briefly for looking, then goes straight
   back.
**Exit check:** prediction stated in the learner's own words; same and
different named; both plants placed correctly (window, cupboard).

## Lesson 05

1. **Guided:** greener leaves → the light plant (in the usual outcome;
   if the real plants differ, the greener plant is whichever the
   learner points to with the leaves in front of them). Straighter stem
   → the light plant (same note).
2. **Guided (measure together):** both real plants measured in cubes
   from soil to tallest leaf; counts recorded as measured.
3. **Independent comparison sentence:** any one observed difference —
   for example, "The light plant is greener and stands up straight,
   and the dark plant is pale and bendy."
4. **Independent journal drawing:** both test plants drawn side by
   side today, with cube counts scribed beside them.
5. **Independent prediction check:** right, partly right, or different
   — any of the three counts, with one observed reason (for example,
   "Partly right: the dark plant still grew, but it is pale").
6. **Error-analysis:** "The dark plant is taller, so it is better" is
   wrong when the dark plant is pale and bending; strong means green
   and standing up, not just tall. If the real dark plant is taller
   AND green and straight, the adult records that honestly and extends
   the test — see the teacher guide.
7. **Application (pattern statement):** plants with light grow greener
   and stronger; plants without light grow pale and weak — in the
   learner's own words, scribed verbatim.
**Exit check:** at least one observed difference named (color,
strength, or cube-measured height) plus the light pattern stated.
**Practice example (from the lesson):** light plant 6 cubes, dark
plant 4 cubes = light plant 2 cubes taller; tall-but-pale-and-bending
is not stronger.

## Lesson 06

1. **Guided:** model with roots in soil, stem, and leaves; labels
   scribed as the learner points: "roots — water," "stem — holds up,"
   "leaves — light"; place added (soil at bottom, sun/window at top,
   air all around).
2. **Guided (place):** see item 1 — soil, light source, and air.
3. **Independent part naming (mixed order):** roots — in soil, take in
   water; stem — holds the plant up; leaves — catch light.
4. **Independent care routine:** look; touch the soil; water a little
   only if dry; keep in light; gentle hands. Four of five steps counts.
5. **Independent journal walk:** story in order — first the seed, then
   a sprout, then leaves, then the tall plant (exact words do not
   matter; order does).
6. **Error-analysis:** roots in the soil are not leaves; leaves go up
   in the light, roots go down in the soil.
7. **Application (care card):** one correct place (bright window) and
   one correct care step (water when the soil feels dry).
**Exit check:** model shows roots in soil, stem, leaves, each named
with its job; care routine includes water-when-dry and light.

## Practice

### Session A: Needs sort game

1. Needs pile: water, sun/light, soil. Not-need pile: toy car, shoe,
   book. Sandwich: people food — not a plant need (praise the
   distinction).
2. Hardest need to see: air (invisible; felt by waving a hand).
3. Speed round: each named need matched to its card/object.
4. Error hunt: the shoe is not a plant need; plants need water, light,
   air, and soil.

### Session B: Plant observation walk

1. One "I notice…" per plant, scribed (green leaves, tall stem, soil in
   a pot, or any correct observation).
2. Light → window or sun; water → pot or rain/soil; soil → pot or
   ground — pointed to for each plant.
3. Hand-lens new detail: leaf lines, tiny soil crumbs, a small root at
   the soil edge, or any new detail actually seen.
4. Plant with everything it needs: green, standing up, damp soil — any
   one piece of evidence tied to a need.

### Session C: Soak and look

1. Dry bean: hard, smooth, small. Soaked bean: bigger, softer, wrinkly
   skin, possibly a tiny split.
2. Change: the bean got bigger and softer; water went in.
3. Split (if visible): where the sprout will push out. If no split is
   visible, "skin looser and wrinkly" counts.
4. Damp soil, not dry: so the seed can take in water and start growing.
5. Journal drawing: dry bean beside soaked bean, scribed "Water made
   the bean bigger and softer."

### Session D: Tending and measuring

1. Care check in order: look; touch soil (dry or damp); water a little
   only if dry; check light.
2. Measure: cubes from soil to tallest leaf, counted aloud; growth =
   new count minus old count, counted out with cubes.
3. Journal drawing: today's plant with cube count and one "I notice…"
   sentence.
4. Prediction drawing: a small guess drawing for next time, revisited
   next session (right or wrong, it counts as a prediction).

### Session E: Water detective

1. Dry soil: dusty, light, falls apart. Damp soil: darker, cool, holds
   together.
2. Eyes-closed touch test: dry vs. damp named by touch alone.
3. Better soil for a plant: damp — plants need water, and damp soil
   has water in it.
4. Watering demo: dry soil turns darker and damp and holds together;
   pouring and pouring floods it — too much water is not better.
5. Rule, scribed in the learner's words: "Water when the soil feels
   dry. Wait when it feels damp."

### Session F: Garden story retell

1. Journal walk story in order: planted, sprouted, grew leaves, light
   test, caring.
2. Picture match: the pot matching the plant today (as in Lesson 03,
   item 7).
3. Needs chant: water, light, air, soil and room — each answered.
4. One pattern for a friend: needs, growth order, light pattern, or
   care rule — any one correct pattern.

## Project — Our Bean Garden

Scored with the rubric in [`project.md`](project.md): planting order
(soil, seed, water); three or more dated drawings with cube counts (or
honest "not sprouted yet"); fair-test record (prediction, side-by-side
drawings, pattern statement); labeled model (roots, stem, leaves);
garden story in order from the journal. The plant's actual growth is
never scored.

## Formative quiz

1. **Name the needs:** water, light, air, soil with room to grow
   (any order; three of four is the minimum pass for this item, with
   full marks for all four).
2. **Sort a need:** needs — water, sun, soil; not a need — toy car.
3. **Order the planting steps:** soil, seed, water.
4. **Order the growth stages:** first — seed pot; last — tall-plant
   pot; after the sprout — a taller stem with leaves.
5. **Judge the fair test:** the greener, stronger plant (the light
   plant in the usual outcome) with one observed reason — greener
   leaves, straighter stem, or a larger cube count. If the real test
   plants differ from the usual outcome, the learner's observed reason
   governs, not this key's expected plant.
6. **Care decision:** dry soil → water a little; damp soil → wait
   (do not water).

## Unit assessment

- **Task 1:** four needs named, with one observation tied to one need
  (for example, drooping leaves + dry soil → needs water).
- **Task 2:** planting done in order (soil, seed, water) and told back
  in order, or picture cards ordered soil, seed, water.
- **Task 3:** (a) stages in order — seed, sprout, taller stem, tall
  plant; (b) cube measure from soil to tallest leaf, counted aloud;
  (c) growth pattern told in the learner's own words in the right
  order.
- **Task 4:** same — seed, soil, cup, water; different — light only;
  conclusion — the light plant grew stronger and greener (usual
  outcome; otherwise the learner's observed conclusion governs),
  supported by at least one observation (color, strength, or cube
  count).
- **Task 5:** roots (in soil, water), stem (holds up), leaves (light);
  care advice — bright place for light, water only when the soil feels
  dry, gentle hands.
- **Ready to move on:** all five tasks show their core evidence (see
  the assessment's Scoring section). Re-teach per task from the
  teacher guide otherwise; a slow or unsprouted plant is not a failed
  task, and a prediction that turned out wrong is not penalized.
