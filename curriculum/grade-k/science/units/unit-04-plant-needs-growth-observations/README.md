# Unit 04 — Plant Needs and Growth Observations

**Grade:** Kindergarten · **Subject:** Science · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #7](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/7), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

What does a plant need — and how do we know? In this unit the learner
watches real plants, plants bean seeds, tends them for weeks, draws what
changes, measures how much taller they grow, and runs one fair test: two
bean plants that are the same in every way except that one gets light
and one does not. Every response may be spoken, pointed to, acted out,
drawn, or adult-scribed. Nothing in this unit asks the learner to explain
*how* plants use light inside their leaves — at kindergarten, the work
is observing the pattern: plants with water and light live and grow;
plants without them do not grow well.

## Essential question

What do plants need to live and grow, and what do we see when a plant
gets what it needs — or does not?

## Prerequisites

- Unit 01: "I notice / I wonder" language, describing what is seen,
  sorting by an observable property, and keeping a science journal with
  adult scribing (used in every lesson).
- Unit 02: plan and carry out a simple test and repeat it the same way
  (used in Lesson 04's fair test).
- Unit 03: define what a good solution must do and compare two tested
  things (used when the learner judges which test plant grew better).
- Can pour a small cup of water only with the adult holding the jug;
  the adult pours all water from the jug, and the learner waters with a
  small cup the adult hands over.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. **Name what plants need** — water, light, air, and soil with room to
   grow — by pointing to or naming evidence from observing real plants
   (for example: dry soil after days without water, a plant leaning
   toward the window, roots in soil).
2. **Plant a bean seed in the right order** — soil in the cup, seed in
   the soil, a little water — and tell the steps back in order with
   words, pictures, or actions.
3. **Observe and record growth over time** — draw the plant in the
   journal at least three times across the unit and measure its height
   with cubes or a paper strip, then describe the pattern: seed, then
   sprout, then stem and leaves, then a taller plant.
4. **Compare two plants tested fairly** — same kind of seed, same soil,
   same water, same cup, different light only — and tell which plant
   grew stronger and greener, using observations from the test, not
   from guessing.
5. **Use a simple model** (a drawing or tabletop model with adult-scribed
   labels) to show how a plant's parts match its needs and where it
   lives — roots in soil for water, stem holding the plant up, leaves
   in the light — and tell one way to care for a plant every day.

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS),
kindergarten performance expectations**, as published by Achieve, Inc.
(2013). The two codes below and their descriptions were verified
against the official framework text reproduced in state and framework
sources (re-checked 2026-10-28; the track audit listed these codes
2026-10-01). No state adoption, accreditation, or alignment
certification is claimed.

| Code | Description |
|---|---|
| K-LS1-1 | Use observations to describe patterns of what plants and animals (including humans) need to survive. Clarification: examples of patterns include that animals need to take in food but plants do not, the requirement of plants to have light, and that all living things need water. Assessment boundary: assessment is limited to observations, not how plants use light (photosynthesis). |
| K-ESS3-1 | Use a model to represent the relationship between the needs of different plants and animals (including humans) and the places they live. |

| Unit objective | NGSS connection |
|---|---|
| 1 | K-LS1-1 — observe real plants and describe the pattern of what plants need |
| 2 | K-LS1-1 — planting is the investigation setup; the steps are observed and recorded, not memorized from a book |
| 3 | K-LS1-1 — observations over time are the data; the growth pattern is described from the journal |
| 4 | K-LS1-1 — the fair test compares observations of two plants that differ in light only |
| 5 | K-ESS3-1 — the labeled drawing/model shows needs matched to parts and to the plant's place (sunny windowsill, soil, room to grow) |

Crosscutting concept (K expression): **Patterns** — the same needs show
up for plant after plant, and the same growth stages show up in the
same order. Practice emphasis: planning and carrying out investigations
(the fair test), analyzing and interpreting data (journal drawings and
cube measures), and developing and using models (the labeled plant
drawing in Lesson 06).

Assessment notes the unit honors: at K, "plants need light" means "the
plant in the light grew greener and stronger than the plant in the dark
in our test" — it is a test result, not a claim about every plant
everywhere, and it is never tested by explaining photosynthesis. A
plant in the dark may still grow tall and pale for a while as it reaches
for light; the teacher guide says exactly what to say when that happens,
so the learner is not told a result the plants did not show. Growth
takes weeks: if a seed is slow, the unit waits — the journal, not the
calendar, decides when Lesson 05 happens.

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items, all large enough to handle safely:
clear plastic cups (so roots can be seen), potting soil, dried bean
seeds (large lima or kidney beans; the adult checks the packet and any
allergy notes first), a small watering cup, a tray, a ruler or paper
strip, counting cubes or blocks for measuring, paper and crayons, the
science journal, a hand lens, two classroom plants the learner may
observe (any healthy houseplants), the generated illustration
[`assets/bean-growth-stages.png`](assets/bean-growth-stages.png).
No glass, no sharp tools for the learner, no tasting of seeds or soil;
the adult handles soil bags and scissors. All sessions are
adult-supervised; water stays over the tray, away from books and
electronics. Wash hands after soil work. Check for seed, soil, and
pollen allergies before planting or outdoor work. See the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) §8 for the full
safe-materials list.

## 16-session pacing (4 weeks × 4 sessions, 15–20 minutes each)

Sessions rotate as: investigation lesson → plant-tending and journal
session → measurement/counting or observation play → share-and-review,
matching the track session model. The project (Our Bean Garden) runs
across Sessions 5, 10, and 14; the bean plants are tended on every
tending session in between.

| Session | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lesson | [L1 — What does a plant need?](lessons/lesson-01-what-plants-need.md) — observing real plants, naming needs |
| 2 | Practice | [Needs sort game](practice.md#session-a-needs-sort-game) — sort need cards: plant needs vs. not needs |
| 3 | Play | [Plant observation walk](practice.md#session-b-plant-observation-walk) — looking closely at plants indoors and outside (in place, never picked) |
| 4 | Review | Share-and-review circle; L1 exit re-checks |
| 5 | Lesson + project | [L2 — Plant a bean seed](lessons/lesson-02-plant-a-bean-seed.md) and [Our Bean Garden](project.md), Part 1 — plant the class beans, first journal drawing |
| 6 | Practice | [Soak and look](practice.md#session-c-soak-and-look) — dry bean vs. bean soaked overnight (adult-prepared), hand lens |
| 7 | Lesson | [L3 — Sprout watch](lessons/lesson-03-sprout-watch.md) — ordering the growth stages with the generated illustration, first measuring |
| 8 | Practice | [Tending and measuring](practice.md#session-d-tending-and-measuring) — water, draw, measure with cubes |
| 9 | Lesson | [L4 — Does light matter?](lessons/lesson-04-does-light-matter.md) — predict, then set up the fair test (light plant vs. dark-cupboard plant) |

Weeks 15–17 introduce, plant, tend, and test; week 18 (track week 18)
is the midyear review week: the garden showcase, the formative check,
and the culminating assessment, plus a cumulative observation game
revisiting Units 01–04. If the bean plants are not yet sprouted by
Session 7, repeat Session 8's tending routine and use the generated
illustration for Lesson 03's ordering work until sprouts appear — do
not fake a sprout. If the learner is not yet secure on an objective
after Session 16, use the diagnostic/catch-up guidance in the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) before starting Unit 05 — the track's
flexible weeks exist for exactly this.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, acting-out, or drawing responses;
the adult scribes. "Taller" is shown with cubes stacked beside the
plant, not numbers alone; "dry soil" and "damp soil" are shown by touch,
not words alone. Each lesson includes a seated-table and a floor or
outdoor variant, a language-access support, an alternate response mode,
and an extension. Home-language plant and need words are welcomed. The
generated illustration ships with a text-only alternative containing
every detail the activity needs. A learner who cannot do hands-on soil
work uses the observation-only alternative in the teacher guide
(watching the adult's plant and drawing from the illustration).
