# Lesson 05 — I notice, I wonder

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you will learn two special science
sentences. 'I notice…' is for something you observe. 'I wonder…' is for a
question. Scientists keep what they see and what they guess in two different
places."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** one familiar object (a leaf, a shell,
a toy car); paper with two columns the adult draws ("I notice" / "I wonder"),
crayons.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

Adult: "What is an observation?" (Something noticed with the senses.) "What
is a guess?" Accept: "something we think but did not see." Today we sort
sentences, not objects.

## Teach and model

Explain: *An **observation** is something your senses tell you — "I notice
the leaf is green." A **guess** is something you think might be true but did
not observe — "The leaf is thirsty." Guesses are not bad! A guess becomes a
**question** we can explore: "I wonder if the leaf needs water." Scientists
write observations and questions separately so they do not mix them up.*

**Modeled example 1 — sort the sentences.** Adult holds the shell and says
three sentences, placing each in a column: "'I notice the shell is pink
inside' — I saw that, so it goes under I NOTICE. 'I notice it feels bumpy'
— I felt that, so I NOTICE. 'I wonder what animal lived in it' — I did not
see that, so it goes under I WONDER." (Modeled reasoning: check each
sentence against the senses before filing it.)

**Modeled example 2 — turning a guess into a question.** Adult: "'The shell
is old.' Did I see 'old'? No — old is a guess. I can turn it into a
question: 'I wonder how old this shell is.' Now it is a wonder, and wonders
are allowed in the question column. Some wonders we can explore — 'I wonder
if it floats' — and some we cannot — 'I wonder if it dreams.' Both are fine
questions; scientists notice the difference."

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** The adult gives four sentences about the object; the learner
   points to the "I notice" or "I wonder" column for each. Use two clear
   observations ("I notice it is brown", "I notice it has a hole") and two
   clear guesses ("It is sleepy", "I wonder if it is from the beach").

## Independent practice

2. **Independent oral:** The learner makes one "I notice…" sentence about
   the object. It must name something the adult can check with their senses.
3. **Independent oral:** The learner makes one "I wonder…" question about the
   object. It must be a question, not a statement.
4. **Independent oral — error analysis:** The adult says, "I notice this leaf
   wants to be a kite." The learner explains why that sentence is wrong for
   the "I notice" column. (Accept: "You can't see 'wants'"; "That is a guess/
   a story"; then help rephrase as "I wonder what the leaf would do in the
   wind.")

## Application

5. **Two-column journal page:** The learner dictates (or draws) one notice
   and one wonder about a different object; the adult files each in its
   column. This page becomes the model for the project journal entries.

## Exit check

The learner produces one checkable "I notice…" and one real "I wonder…",
and correctly files two adult-given sentences into the columns.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** The two sentence starters are written large
  with a simple icon (an eye for "I notice", a question mark for "I wonder")
  — the child can point to a starter and then speak. Home-language sentence
  starters are welcomed.
- **Alternate response mode:** Filing can be done by placing sentence cards
  (adult-written) into two labeled trays; no speaking required beyond the
  exit check.
- **Extension:** "Which of your wonders could we explore by looking or
  testing? Which could we not? How could we find out?" (Sets up the idea
  that questions lead to investigations — Unit 02 and beyond.)
- **Table and floor variants:** Table: two-column paper. Floor: two labeled
  trays; the learner drops object cards or speaks into each.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** Learners often treat "I wonder" as a second kind of
observation ("I wonder it is green"). Keep the boundary crisp: "wonder" is
always followed by a question. If the child says "I wonder it is green,"
recast together: "I wonder IF it is green — now it is a question we could
check."

**Answer-key reference:** [`../answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md#lesson-05) —
a notice counts only if the adult can verify it with their senses; a wonder
counts only if it is phrased as a question.
