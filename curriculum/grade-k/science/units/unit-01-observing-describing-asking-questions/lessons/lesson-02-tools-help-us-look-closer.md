# Lesson 02 — Tools help us look closer

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you will use a tool called a hand lens
to look closer. A hand lens makes small things look bigger. Scientists use
tools to notice details they cannot see with their eyes alone."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** a hand lens (magnifier); one leaf
(fallen or from a safe houseplant); paper, crayons. **Safety:** The learner
never looks at the sun through the lens, or at all. The adult holds and hands
over the lens; no lens play near eyes of others.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

Adult asks: "What does it mean to observe?" Accept: "to notice with your
senses." Then: "Show me your eyes. What can your eyes do?" (See things.)
"Today we get helper eyes."

## Teach and model

Explain: *Our eyes are good, but some details are too small to see well. A
**hand lens** is a science tool — a curved piece of glass that makes things
look bigger when you hold it close to them. Using a tool to observe is still
observing; the tool does not change what is there, it just shows more of it.*

**Modeled example 1 — without and with.** Adult holds up the leaf: "With my
eyes alone, I observe a green leaf. I notice it is smooth and has one pointy
end." Then adult holds the hand lens over the leaf and thinks aloud: "Now I
look through the lens. I **notice** tiny lines on the leaf — veins — that I
could not see before. That is one new detail. The tool helped me observe
more." Draw both: a plain leaf, then a leaf with vein lines.

**Modeled example 2 — a second detail.** Adult moves the lens to the leaf's
edge: "I notice the edge has tiny bumps — it is not smooth like I thought.
My first look said 'smooth'; my closer look says 'bumpy edge'. Looking closer
can change what we describe." (Modeled reasoning: compare the two
observations and keep the more careful one.)

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** The learner holds the leaf and the adult steadies the lens
   over it (or the learner holds the lens if able). "Look with your eyes
   first. Tell me one thing. Now look through the lens. Tell me one NEW
   thing you could not see before." The adult scribes both statements.

## Independent practice

2. **Independent oral/drawing:** "Draw the leaf two times: what you saw with
   your eyes, and what you saw through the lens. Make the second drawing show
   the new detail." Accept any true-to-leaf detail (veins, bumps, spots,
   fuzz).
3. **Independent oral:** Finish: "Without the lens I saw ___. With the lens I
   saw ___." The adult writes the exact words.
4. **Independent oral:** "Name one other thing you would like to look at
   through the lens." (No wrong answer — curiosity is the point.)

## Application

5. **Tool test:** Offer two more familiar objects (a fingertip, a fabric
   scrap, a coin). The learner picks one, looks through the lens, and reports
   one new detail. Key idea: tools help everywhere, not just on leaves.

## Exit check

The learner names one detail seen only through the lens and finishes the
"Without the lens… / With the lens…" pair. The adult records the words.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** The words "lens" and "detail" are taught while
  holding the tool, not from a word list. Home-language words for "look
  closely" are welcomed.
- **Alternate response mode:** If holding the lens is hard, the adult holds
  it steady and the learner only looks; pointing plus one dictated word
  counts. A learner with low vision may use touch for the "closer look"
  (feel the veins) — the objective is noticing a new detail, not the lens
  itself.
- **Extension:** "Look at the same leaf edge with the lens tomorrow. Is it
  the same? How do you know?" or "Draw what you think is INSIDE the leaf"
  (a labeled guess — see Lesson 05 for why it stays a guess).
- **Table and floor variants:** Table: lens on a stand or adult-held over a
  tray. Floor: big cushions, lens passed hand to hand with the adult guiding.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** Learners may say the lens "makes things" — e.g.,
"the lens made lines on the leaf." Correct gently: "The lines were already
there. The lens helped your eyes find them." This is the seed of
distinguishing the tool from the object, revisited in later units.

**Answer-key reference:** [`../answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md#lesson-02) —
any real new detail counts; the key lists likely leaf details (veins, edge
bumps, spots, fuzz) and what does not count (details invented or copied from
the adult).
