# Lesson 01 — Use your five senses

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you will be a scientist. You will use
your senses to notice things in a picture. Senses are the ways our bodies
learn about the world: we see with our eyes, hear with our ears, touch with
our hands, smell with our nose, and taste with our tongue."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** the illustration
[`../assets/observation-scene.png`](../assets/observation-scene.png), paper,
crayons.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

Adult asks: "Point to your eyes. What do your eyes help you do?" (See.)
Repeat for ears, hands, nose, tongue. Accept any correct pairing. This lesson
uses sight; Lessons 02–03 add touch, hearing, and smell.

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *To **observe** means to notice things with your
senses. Scientists observe carefully and then **describe** what they notice —
they tell or draw what is really there, not what they wish were there.*

**Modeled example 1 — noticing with sight.** Adult looks at the illustration
and thinks aloud: "I am using my eyes. I **observe** a big green leaf. I
**notice** a small red bug with black dots sitting on the leaf. I can
**describe** it: it is red, round, and has black dots. That is one
observation." (Modeled reasoning: the adult names the sense used, says what is
noticed, and gives two details.)

**Modeled example 2 — a second observation.** Adult: "I am still using my
eyes. I notice a blue butterfly on a yellow flower. I can describe it: the
butterfly's wings are blue and the flower has yellow petals. That is a second
observation — scientists make more than one." Emphasize: both statements say
what is *in the picture*, not a story about it.

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** Adult points to the butterfly. "I will say one thing I notice.
   Now you say or point to one thing you notice in the picture." Prompt with
   "What do you see near the leaf?" only if the learner is stuck.

## Independent practice

2. **Independent oral/pointing/drawing:** "Say or point to **three** things you
   notice in the picture." The learner may name the ladybug, butterfly,
   snail, bird, sun, clouds, flower, stone, fence — anything actually in the
   picture (see the text-only alternative below). The adult scribes the three
   observations on paper.
3. **Independent drawing:** "Draw one thing you noticed, using at least two
   details." (e.g., the ladybug with red body *and* black dots; the snail
   with shell *and* stone.)
4. **Independent oral:** "Finish this sentence: 'I notice ___' about the
   picture." The adult writes the exact words the child dictates.

## Application

5. **Real-object transfer:** Hand the learner a familiar object (an apple, a
   shell, a block). "Use your senses. What do you notice?" Accept touch and
   smell observations too ("It feels smooth", "It smells like nothing").

## Exit check

The learner says or points to three noticed details from the picture *or* the
object and finishes one "I notice ___" sentence. The adult records the words.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** Vocabulary is taught with the picture first,
  the word second. Home-language labels for the animals are welcomed
  alongside the English words.
- **Alternate response mode:** Pointing to a detail in the picture counts
  fully; the adult scribes everything. A learner may answer entirely by
  drawing.
- **Extension:** "Find one thing in the picture that is the same as something
  in this room" (e.g., a leaf like a plant at home), or "Notice one more
  thing — that is four observations."
- **Table and floor variants:** Seated at a table with the printed picture, or
  on the floor with the picture propped up; both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** Learners often report a guess or a story as an
observation ("the ladybug is lost"). Gently redirect: "That might be a good
story. What do your eyes *see*? I see a red bug with black dots." Lesson 05
teaches observation vs. guess explicitly.

**Answer-key reference:** [`../answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md#lesson-01) —
acceptable observations must name something actually in the picture (see the
text-only alternative below) and may be given by pointing.
