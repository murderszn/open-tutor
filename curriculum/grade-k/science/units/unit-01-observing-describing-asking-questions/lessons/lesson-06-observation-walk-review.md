# Lesson 06 — Observation walk (put it all together)

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you are a scientist on a walk. You
will observe with your senses, look closer at one thing, describe it, and
ask one question. Everything you learned this unit goes on the walk with you."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** science journal, crayons, hand lens,
paper bag for collected *fallen* items only (leaves, pebbles — never pick
living plants or touch unknown objects). **Safety:** Stay with the adult; do
not touch or taste unknown plants, mushrooms, insects, or litter; if anything
stings, bites, or looks unsafe, observe from a distance. Indoor alternative:
an "observation walk" through the rooms of the home with the same steps.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

Adult: "Name our two science sentences." ("I notice…", "I wonder…") "What
tool helps us look closer?" (Hand lens.) "What must we never do on a science
walk?" (Touch/taste unknown things; go alone.)

## Teach and model

Explain: *A walk is an investigation with your feet. The plan is simple:
**stop, look, notice, describe, wonder**. At each stop we use our senses
first, the lens second, and our questions last.*

**Modeled example 1 — a full stop.** Adult stops at a tree (or a houseplant
for the indoor version) and thinks aloud: "Stop. I observe with my eyes: I
notice rough brown bark. I look closer with the lens: I notice tiny cracks.
I describe it: it is brown, rough, and has thin cracks. I wonder: I wonder
how old this tree is." (Modeled reasoning: senses → tool → description →
question, in order.)

**Modeled example 2 — a non-visual observation.** Adult closes eyes: "I
observe with my ears: I notice birds chirping. I cannot see them, but hearing
is observing too. I wonder what the birds are saying to each other." This
models that observation is not only sight — important for the accessibility of
the whole track.

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** At the first stop, the adult leads the full sequence and the
   learner contributes one notice; the adult scribes it in the journal.

## Independent practice

2. **Independent (three stops):** At each of three stops, the learner:
   - says or points to **two things noticed** (any sense),
   - looks closer at **one** thing (lens or careful looking) and names **one
     new detail**,
   - dictates **one "I notice"** and **one "I wonder"** for the journal.
   The adult scribes; the learner draws one stop's observation.
3. **Independent oral:** "Which stop had the most interesting detail? What
   made it interesting?" (Any defensible answer — the reasoning is the work.)

## Application

4. **Sort the walk:** Back home, the learner sorts the three stops' notices
   into "things I saw", "things I heard/felt", and "questions I have" —
   reusing Lesson 04's sorting and Lesson 05's columns on real data.

## Exit check

The learner completes one full stop independently: two notices, one closer
look with a new detail, one "I notice", one "I wonder" — recorded in the
journal.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** The stop sequence is on a card with icons:
  eye → lens → mouth (describe) → question mark. The child points through the
  sequence as they work.
- **Alternate response mode:** The whole walk can be done from a window or a
  single bench ("sit-spot observing"); pointing and drawing replace walking.
  Sounds and smells count double here.
- **Extension:** "Draw a map of your walk with one observation at each stop"
  (early modeling — a K expression of Practice 2, Developing and using
  models) or "Pick one wonder and tell me how we could explore it."
- **Table and floor variants:** This lesson is the floor/outdoor variant by
  design; the table variant is a photo walk — the adult shows 4–5 printed
  photos (backyard, park, kitchen) and the learner does the same sequence on
  each.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** On walks, learners often rush to the "interesting"
thing and skip observing. The stop sequence is the fix — practice stopping
before noticing. If the learner names only animals ("a dog!"), prompt senses:
"What do you hear? What does the ground feel like?"

each stop counts if it contains two checkable notices, one tool-assisted new
detail, and one filed notice/wonder pair.
