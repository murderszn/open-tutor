# Lesson 03 — Describing what you see

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you will describe what you observe so
that someone else can picture it. To **describe** means to tell the details:
the colors, the shapes, the sizes. A good description lets another person
find the same thing."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** two familiar objects that differ
clearly (e.g., a red apple and a green apple; a smooth stone and a rough
sponge); paper, crayons; the science journal.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

Adult: "What is an observation?" (Something we notice with our senses.)
"Yesterday we used a tool. What did it help us do?" (See small details.)

## Teach and model

Explain: *Observing finds the details; **describing** shares them. A
description has details a listener can check: colors, shapes, sizes, textures
(how it feels). "It is nice" is not a description a scientist can use —
"nice" is a feeling, not something seen.*

**Modeled example 1 — a checkable description.** Adult holds the red apple and
thinks aloud: "I observe with my eyes and hands. I will describe it: it is
round, it is red, it has a brown stem, and it feels smooth. Four details — a
friend could find this apple from my words." Then test it: the adult asks the
learner to pick the apple out of the two objects using only the description.
It works.

**Modeled example 2 — a weak description, fixed.** Adult holds the stone:
"'It is a rock' — is that a good description?" (The learner will likely say
yes.) "Hmm — but this sponge could also be called 'a thing'. Let me try
again: it is gray, flat, cold, and rough. Now could you find it?" (Yes.) The
modeled fix: *name details someone can check with their own senses.*

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** Adult picks one object; the learner describes it while the
   adult scribes. Prompt with: "What color? What shape? How does it feel?
   How big — bigger than your hand or smaller?" Aim for two details together;
   celebrate a third.

## Independent practice

2. **Independent oral/drawing:** The learner chooses the other object and
   describes it alone: at least two checkable details. The adult scribes the
   exact words.
3. **Independent drawing:** "Draw the object so someone else could recognize
   it. Put in the details you said." (Two details in the drawing count.)
4. **Independent oral:** "Which of these two sentences is a description a
   scientist could use: 'It is pretty' or 'It is yellow and bumpy'? Why?"
   (Accept any reasoning that mentions checkable details vs. feelings.)

## Application

5. **Description game:** The learner describes an object in the room without
   naming it; the adult tries to find it. Swap roles once.

## Exit check

The learner describes a new object (adult's choice) with at least two
checkable details, and the adult can identify it from the description alone.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** Sentence frames are allowed and encouraged:
  "It is ___. It has ___. It feels ___." Frames are a support, not a
  requirement.
- **Alternate response mode:** Drawing-only descriptions count when they show
  two recognizable details; the adult labels what the child dictates.
- **Extension:** "Describe the same object to a stuffed animal that cannot
  see — what would you say first?" or describe two objects and say one way
  they are the same and one way they are different (preview of Lesson 04).
- **Table and floor variants:** Table: objects on trays, drawing side by
  side. Floor: objects in a circle, descriptions spoken to the group.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "Describe" gets confused with "like/dislike" ("it
is yummy", "it is ugly"). Keep the redirect playful and consistent: "Is
'yummy' something your eyes see? What do they see instead?" Taste words are
fine only for known safe foods being observed with all senses.

**Answer-key reference:** [`../answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md#lesson-03) —
a detail counts if the adult can verify it with their own senses; feelings,
stories, and names ("it is an apple") alone do not count as descriptions.
