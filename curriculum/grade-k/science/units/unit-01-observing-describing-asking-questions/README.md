# Unit 01 — Observing, Describing, and Asking Questions

**Grade:** Kindergarten · **Subject:** Science · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #7](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/7), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Scientists learn about the world by looking closely, noticing details, and
asking questions they can explore. In this unit the learner uses the five
senses to observe familiar objects, looks closer with a simple tool (a hand
lens), describes observations with words and drawings, sorts objects by an
observable property, and practices finishing "I notice…" with something seen
and "I wonder…" with a question. Every response may be spoken, pointed to,
drawn, or adult-scribed — the science is in the noticing, not the writing.

## Essential question

What can we learn by looking closely, and how do good questions help us
learn more?

## Prerequisites

- Diagnostic weeks 1–2: willing to touch, look, and try; emerging "I notice"
  language when prompted; can draw recognizably (see the track
  [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
- No reading, writing, counting beyond whole-number tallies, or tool use is
  assumed; the unit teaches each explicitly.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Use the five senses to observe a familiar object and name or point to at
   least **three things noticed** (words, drawing, pointing, or adult-scribed
   dictation all count).
2. Use a hand lens (or careful close looking when no lens is available) to
   notice and describe **one detail not seen without it**.
3. Sort a small set of familiar objects by an observable property and **name
   the property** used (color, texture, size, shape, or another visible
   difference).
4. Describe an observation with words or a drawing that shows at least **two
   visible details** (an adult may scribe labels the child dictates).
5. Complete "I notice…" with something actually seen and "I wonder…" with a
   question — and, with prompting, tell whether a statement is something seen
   or a guess.

## Vocabulary (taught with objects first, word second)

observe · senses: sight, hearing, touch, smell, taste *(taste only for known
safe foods; never taste found or unknown objects)* · notice · describe ·
wonder · question · guess · compare · same · different · sort · hand lens ·
journal · "I notice…" / "I wonder…"

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS), Science and
Engineering Practices**, as defined in **NGSS Appendix F (April 2013 release)**.
The eight practices were verified 2026-10-04 against the published Appendix F
text (mirrors of the official release); the audit verified the K performance
expectations against [nextgenscience.org/search-standards](https://www.nextgenscience.org/search-standards)
on 2026-10-01. This unit is foundational — it teaches the practices that
support every later K performance expectation. No state adoption,
accreditation, or alignment certification is claimed.

| Unit objective | NGSS practice (K-band expression) |
|---|---|
| 1, 2 | Practice 3: Planning and carrying out investigations — make direct
observations (including with tools) to collect information |
| 3, 4 | Practice 4: Analyzing and interpreting data — at K, describe patterns
and sort observations; Practice 8: Obtaining, evaluating, and communicating
information — communicate observations with drawings and words |
| 5 | Practice 1: Asking questions (for science) — ask questions that can be
explored by looking, touching, or testing |

Crosscutting concept (K expression): **Patterns** — noticing that things can
be alike and different, and that the same close look can reveal new details.
This unit also follows the guidance of the **NSTA Early Childhood Science
Education position statement** (adopted January 2014): young children have
the capacity to observe, explore, and discover; adults focus observations and
provide time to talk about what was done and seen. Statement text verified
2026-10-04.

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials
list): a hand lens (magnifier), paper, crayons or markers, a science journal
(stapled paper or a notebook — adult scribes), sorting trays or shoebox lids,
familiar objects for sorting (buttons, shells, blocks, fabric scraps, leaves),
a mystery bag (paper bag), the generated illustration
[`assets/observation-scene.png`](assets/observation-scene.png). No sharp
tools; no small parts for children under 3 in shared settings; check allergies
before handling soil, plants, or food; never taste unknown or found objects;
wash hands after outdoor or soil contact. All sessions are adult-supervised.

## 16-session pacing (4 weeks × 4 sessions, 15–20 minutes each)

Sessions rotate as: lesson → practice → game/play → review, matching the
track session model. The project (observation journal) runs across Sessions
6, 10, and 14.

| Session | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lesson | [L1 — Use your five senses](lessons/lesson-01-five-senses-noticing.md) — observe the generated garden illustration |
| 2 | Practice | [Journal drawing practice](practice.md#session-a-journal-drawing-practice) — draw one observed object |
| 3 | Game | [Mystery-bag senses game](practice.md#session-b-mystery-bag-senses-game) — guess by touch, then check by sight |
| 4 | Review | Share-and-review circle; L1 exit re-checks |
| 5 | Lesson | [L2 — Tools help us look closer](lessons/lesson-02-tools-help-us-look-closer.md) — hand lens on a leaf |
| 6 | Project | [My Observation Journal](project.md), Part 1 — choose an object, first journal entry |
| 7 | Play | Lens exploration: revisit three familiar objects with and without the lens; compare drawings |
| 8 | Review | Share circle; L1–L2 exit re-checks |
| 9 | Lesson | [L3 — Describing what you see](lessons/lesson-03-describing-what-you-see.md) — words and drawings that show details |
| 10 | Project | [My Observation Journal](project.md), Part 2 — second entry + one "I wonder" question |
| 11 | Lesson | [L4 — Sorting by what you notice](lessons/lesson-04-sorting-by-what-you-notice.md) |
| 12 | Lesson | [L5 — I notice, I wonder](lessons/lesson-05-i-notice-i-wonder.md) — observation vs. guess; questions we can explore |

Weeks 1–3 introduce and practice; week 4 (track week 6) is the review week:
the observation walk, the journal showcase, the formative check, and the
culminating assessment. If the learner is not yet secure on an objective after
Session 16, use the diagnostic/catch-up guidance in the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) before starting Unit 02 — the track's
flexible weeks exist for exactly this.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, or drawing responses; the adult scribes.
Observations can be made by touch, smell, and listening — not only sight;
each lesson names a non-visual observation option. Color is never the only
cue (the illustration's animals differ in shape and size as well as color;
sorting tasks accept texture, size, or shape rules). Each lesson includes a
seated-table and a floor/outdoor-play variant, a language-access support, an
alternate response mode, and an extension. The generated illustration ships
with a text-only alternative containing every detail the activity needs.
