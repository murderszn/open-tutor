# Teacher guide — Unit 02: Pushes, Pulls, and Moving Objects

For the guiding adult. Learner-facing pages are the lessons, practice,
project, and assessments; this guide and the
[answer key](answer-key.md) stay with the adult.

## How this unit works

This unit is the track's first **causal** science: pushes and pulls *cause*
changes in motion. It builds on Unit 01's observation language — every
lesson asks for "I notice" statements about what happened — and it
introduces two big scientific moves: **comparing one changed thing**
(strength or direction, never both at once) and **repeating a test**
(three times, everything else the same). Lesson 05 adds the engineering
loop: define the problem, build, test, judge, improve. Sessions are 15–20
minutes; stop while the learner is still engaged rather than finishing a
page.

## Session-by-session guidance

| Session | Watch for | If it goes sideways |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (L1) | Push/pull mixed up | Return to the body motions: push air away, pull sleeve close. Act, then name. |
| 2 (Practice A) | "Everything is a push" | Slow the hunt: one object, "away or closer?" each time. |
| 3 (Practice B) | Can't stop the ball | Widen the lane; use a slower roll; stopping with two hands is fine. |
| 4 (Review) | Shyness about sharing | The adult shares first, imperfectly ("my push went… somewhere"). |
| 5 (L2) | Aiming strong pushes at people/things | Set the test lane first; "strong" is still controlled. |
| 6 (Project 1) | No plan — random building | Ask "point to where the ball will go" before any building. |
| 7 (Play) | Marks everywhere, no comparison | Two colors of tape: one for gentle, one for strong. |
| 8 (Review) | Forgetting Lesson 01 words | Re-run the L1 body-motion check as the warm-up. |
| 9 (L3) | "Pull" only means "toward me" | Pull from three different spots; the puller moves. |
| 10 (Project 2) | Bumper placed with no prediction | "Tell me what the ball will do BEFORE you roll." |
| 11 (L4) | Changing two things at once | "Hands off everything except the push." Name the one change. |
| 12 (L5) | "It didn't work" = giving up | The Lesson 05 story: the adult's flat bumper failed first too. "Not yet" is data. |
| 13 (L6) | Doing without naming | Narrate alongside: "You just *pulled* it — say it with me." |
| 14 (Project 3) | Reluctance to share | Audience of one (a stuffed animal) counts. |
| 15 (Quiz) | Quiz anxiety | Call it "show me what you can do"; allow a second sitting. |
| 16 (Assessment) | New setups feel unfair | Remind: "Scientists test NEW things — that is the job." |

## Misconceptions to expect (and the fix that works)

1. **"Push means hard and fast."** (Lesson 01–02) — Gentle pushes get
   filed as "not a real push." Fix: the Lesson 02 feather-touch on the
   adult's hand — "that was a push too."
2. **"The push wore off."** (Lessons 02, 04) — When the ball stops, the
   push is imagined as a fuel that ran out. K-level fix: "Something
   *stopped* it — the floor, the cushion, your hand. Stopping is also a
   push." (Do not teach friction as a mechanism; the assessment boundary
   excludes it.)
3. **"Pulls only come toward me."** (Lesson 03) — Fix: move the puller;
   the direction follows the puller, not the child.
4. **"Fair means being nice."** (Lesson 04) — Fix: "A *fair test* means
   only one thing changes. Unfair tests trick us."
5. **"It didn't work, so I failed."** (Lesson 05, Project) — Fix: the
   modeled flat-bumper failure; "not yet" is data; engineers expect the
   first test to teach them.
6. **"Bigger/stronger is always better."** (Lessons 02, 05) — Fix: the
   cushion stops the ball (soft beats hard for stopping); Session C's
   "stop before the line" game rewards gentle.

## Readiness and reteaching

**Ready to move on** looks like: push vs. pull named correctly on 5 of 6
actions (assessment Task 1 at score 2), strong-beats-gentle predicted and
shown (Task 2 at 2), a pull directed to two named targets (Task 3 at 2),
same-vs.-changed named for the ramp test (Task 4 at 2), and a bumper built,
tested, and judged (Task 5 at 2). The unit assessment's 9–10 band is the
formal bar.

**Reteach moves (use before repeating a whole lesson):**
- Objective 1 weak → one more scavenger hunt (Practice A) + Lesson 01
  body motions with the picture.
- Objective 2 weak → Session C with three gentles and three strongs in one
  sitting; let the learner be the "strength teacher" for a stuffed animal.
- Objective 3 weak → Lesson 03's two-target game daily for a week, 5
  minutes; the adult pulls from a new spot each day.
- Objective 4 weak → Lesson 04's error-analysis "tests" (different ball,
  raised ramp) as a game: the learner is the "fairness detective."
- Objective 5 weak → Lesson 05's guided practice with the adult building
  first (learner judges "worked / not yet") before the learner builds.

**When to use flexible time:** a 0–5 on the unit assessment, or two
objectives still at 0 after Session 16, means using the track's flexible
catch-up time before Unit 03. One shaky objective (score 1) can travel into
Unit 03 with a weekly 5-minute warm-up — pushes and pulls will reappear
naturally in the materials-design unit.

## Safety and accessibility reminders

- All investigations are adult-supervised; the full boundaries are in the
  track [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) §8.
- Ramp tests run in a marked "test lane" away from faces, windows, and
  fragile things. No throwing balls at bumpers or people.
- The adult builds the ramp, ties knots, and steadies tall stacks; the
  learner designs and places.
- Every check accepts oral, pointing, acting-out, or drawing responses;
  the adult scribes. "Farther" is tape marks, never numbers.
- Home-language push/pull words are welcomed; visual word walls with the
  body motions support language access.
