# Lesson 01 — Push or pull?

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you learn two science words:
**push** and **pull**. A **push** moves something *away* from you. A
**pull** brings something *closer* to you. You will find pushes and pulls
in this picture."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** the illustration
[`../assets/push-pull-play-scene.png`](../assets/push-pull-play-scene.png),
a toy car, a short piece of string.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Stand up. Push the air away from your face with both hands. Now pull your
sleeve toward your chin." The adult names each move as the learner does it:
"*Push* — away from you. *Pull* — closer to you." Body first, words second.

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *A **push** is a move that sends something away
from you. A **pull** is a move that brings something closer to you.
Pushes and pulls can **start** something moving, **stop** something moving,
or **change** how it moves.*

**Modeled example 1 — a push in the picture.** Adult points to the child
with the red car: "I **notice** a child pushing a red car with both hands.
The car moves *away* from the child's hands, toward the cardboard ramp.
The car went *away* — that is a **push**." (Modeled reasoning: name what is
seen, say which way it moves, name the word.)

**Modeled example 2 — a pull in the picture.** Adult points to the hand
with the string: "I **notice** a hand pulling a string tied to the blue
truck. The truck moves *closer* to the hand. The truck came *closer* —
that is a **pull**."

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** "Show me the **push** in the picture." (Points to the child
   with the red car.) "Show me the **pull**." (Points to the hand and
   string.) Prompt with "Does it go away or come closer?" if the learner is
   stuck.

## Independent practice

2. **Independent pointing:** "Find one more **push** and one more **pull**
   you could do in this room." (Push: a door, a chair, a toy. Pull: a
   drawer, a wagon, a sleeve.) Accept pointing or acting it out.
3. **Independent acting:** With the toy car — "Push the car. Now pull the
   car back to you with one finger." The learner says "push" and "pull" as
   they move.
4. **Independent oral:** "The child in the picture pushed the car. What
   happened after the push?" (It started moving toward the ramp.) "What made
   it move?" (The push.)
5. **Error-analysis (guided):** The adult pulls the car with the string and
   says, "That was a push!" The learner corrects: "No — it came *closer*,
   so it is a **pull**." Celebrate the correction.

## Application

6. **Real-object transfer:** "Look at the yellow ball in the picture. It
   rolled down and bumped the soft gray cushion — and it stopped. What
   stopped the ball?" Accept: "The cushion." Then: "The cushion *pushed
   back* on the ball. A push can also **stop** a moving object." (Previews
   Lesson 05's bumpers.)

## Exit check

The learner names one **push** and one **pull** — from the picture, the
room, or their own body — and acts each one out. The adult records the
two examples.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** Push and pull are taught with the body
  first, the word second. Home-language words for push/pull are welcomed
  alongside the English words; the adult pairs them with the hand motions.
- **Alternate response mode:** Acting out or pointing counts fully; the
  adult scribes the learner's words. A learner may answer entirely by
  showing.
- **Extension:** "Can one toy be pushed AND pulled?" (Push the truck, then
  pull its string.) "The string helps the pull — why?" (Accept any
  reasonable idea: it lets the hand stay far while pulling.)
- **Table and floor variants:** Seated at a table with the printed picture
  and a small car, or on the floor re-creating the scene with real toys;
  both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "Push means hard and fast." Redirect: "A gentle
push is still a push — the car moves away a little bit." Lesson 02 tests
gentle vs. strong directly.

**Common misconception:** "Pulls need a rope." Redirect: "Opening a drawer
and lifting a toy are pulls too — anything that comes closer to you."

**Answer-key reference:** [`../answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md#lesson-01) —
the push is the child-and-red-car (moves away); the pull is the hand,
string, and blue truck (moves closer). Pointing counts.
