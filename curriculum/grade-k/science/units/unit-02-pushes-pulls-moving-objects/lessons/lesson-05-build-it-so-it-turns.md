# Lesson 05 — Build it so it turns

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you are an engineer. Build a turn
so a rolling ball goes a different way. Test it: did it do what you wanted?
Then make it better."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** the cardboard ramp, one ball, books
or blocks for walls, a cushion or block to use as a **bumper**, tape.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Roll the ball straight down the ramp." Then: "Here is the problem:
the ball must **turn left** at the bottom. How could you make it turn?"
Pose the problem before showing any solution — the defining move of
engineering.

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *An **engineer** solves a problem by building
something, **testing** it, and **improving** it. Our problem: make the
rolling ball turn. Our solution: a **bumper** — something the ball bumps
into that sends it a new way. Test means try it and watch. Improve means
change it and try again.*

**Modeled example 1 — a bumper that works.** Adult leans a block at an
angle at the bottom of the ramp: "My plan: the ball bumps the block and
turns left." Rolls the ball. "I **notice** the ball hit the bumper and
turned left! My solution **works** — it did what I wanted." (Modeled
reasoning: state the plan, test, compare the result to the plan.)

**Modeled example 2 — a bumper that needs improving.** Adult lays the
bumper flat across the path: "My plan: the ball turns left." Rolls — the
ball stops. "It stopped. That is **not** what I wanted — I wanted a turn.
So I **improve**: I will tilt the bumper." Adult tilts it and rolls again.
"Now it turns! Improving means changing it and testing again. A test that
'fails' still teaches us." (Models K-PS2-2: judge whether the solution
works as intended, then revise.)

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** The learner places their bumper; the adult rolls the ball.
   "Did it go the way you wanted — yes, or not yet?" If not yet: "What
   will you change?" (One change at a time.)

## Independent practice

2. **Independent build and test:** "Build a turn. Roll the ball. Tell me:
   did it work the first time?" The adult scribes the yes-or-not-yet.
3. **Independent improve:** "Change one thing and test again. Is it better
   now?" (Accept: moved the bumper, tilted it, used a different bumper.)
4. **Independent compare:** "Try the soft cushion as the bumper. Does the
   cushion **turn** the ball or **stop** it?" (Stops it — connect to the
   Lesson 01 picture: the yellow ball and the gray cushion. Soft things
   stop; hard tilted things turn.)
5. **Independent record:** In the science journal: draw or point to the
   path the ball took (straight line, then a turn); the adult scribes the
   learner's description: "It went straight, hit the bumper, and turned."

## Application

6. **Real-world engineers:** "Engineers test and improve everything built.
   Think of the slide at the park — what do you think the builders tested?"
   (That kids land safely; that it is fun; that it works in rain.) "What
   would you improve about our slide?" (Accept any reasoned idea — this is
   engineering talk, not a right answer.)

## Exit check

The learner tells the adult: "Did your turn work the first time? What did
you change?" The adult records the answer (works / improved / still working
on it — all honest engineering).

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** "Bumper," "works," and "improve" are taught
  with the objects first. Home-language words for "turn" are welcomed.
- **Alternate response mode:** Pointing to the bumper and the turned path
  counts as the explanation; the adult scribes. The journal drawing may be
  the whole record.
- **Extension:** "Build a turn to the **right**." Or: "Make the ball turn
  and then **stop** in the box — two jobs, one course." (Previews the
  project's target box.)
- **Table and floor variants:** Table-top ramp with books as walls, or the
  full floor course; both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "It didn't work, so I failed." Redirect with the
Lesson 05 story: "The adult's flat bumper failed too — then the tilt worked.
Engineers *expect* the first test to teach them. 'Not yet' is data."

**Common misconception:** "Change everything at once." Redirect: "Engineers
change *one* thing, then test. We already know that move from Lesson 04's
fair tests."

**Safety:** Keep rolling balls in the test lane; no throwing the ball at
the bumper. Cushions are soft — still, no aiming at faces.

**Answer-key reference:** [`../answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md#lesson-05) —
a working turn = ball changes direction at the bumper as planned; the
cushion stops rather than turns; improvement = one deliberate change
followed by a re-test.
