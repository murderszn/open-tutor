# Lesson 04 — Same test, three times

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Scientists test the same way three times.
We keep the ramp the same, the ball the same, and the push the same kind —
then we can trust what we see."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** the cardboard ramp from Lesson 02
(same setup, same height), one ball, tape for marks, the learner's science
journal.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Last time: gentle push or strong push — which sent the car farther? Show
me both pushes." Quick re-demonstration; then: "Today we test like
scientists — the *same* test, three times."

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *Scientists **repeat** tests. Repeat means do it
again the same way. If we change the ball or the ramp in the middle, we
won't know whether the push or the new ball caused what happened. Keep
everything the same except the one thing you are testing.*

**Modeled example 1 — the same test three times.** Adult sets up: "Same
ramp. Same ball. Same kind of push — gentle." Rolls three times, marking
each stop. "Look at my three marks — they are close together. The test
worked the same way each time. I can trust it: a gentle push sends the ball
about *this* far." (Modeled reasoning: name what stayed the same, show the
three results, state the conclusion.)

**Modeled example 2 — an unfair test.** Adult swaps in a different, heavier
ball mid-"test": "Same ramp, same push — but a *different ball*. If it goes
farther, was it the push or the ball? I can't tell! The ball changed, so
this is **not** the same test." Adult puts the first ball back. (Models
K-PS2-1's discipline: compare strengths *or* directions — one change at a
time, everything else the same.)

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** The learner runs the gentle-push ramp test three times; the
   adult marks each stop. "Point to your three marks. Are they close
   together?" (Yes — the test was the same.)

## Independent practice

2. **Independent three strong pushes:** "Now do the **strong**-push test
   three times. Same ramp, same ball — only the push changed." Mark and
   compare: "Which set of marks went farther — the gentle set or the strong
   set?"
3. **Independent oral — what stayed the same:** "Tell me two things that
   stayed the same in your test." (Same ball, same ramp. Accept: "same
   hill," "same push kind.") The adult scribes.
4. **Independent oral — what changed:** "Tell me one thing that changed."
   (The push — gentle became strong.)
5. **Error-analysis:** The adult "runs a test" where the ramp is lifted
   higher for one trial and says, "Look — farther! The strong push did it."
   The learner spots the unfair change: "You moved the ramp! The ramp
   changed, not just the push." (The core fair-test move: find what
   changed.)

## Application

6. **Home-test planning:** "Scientists test the same way three times so
   they know it was the push, not luck. Tell me one thing you could test
   three times at home — and what you would keep the same." (Accept any
   reasonable plan: rolling a ball down the couch cushion three times;
   "same ball, same couch, same push.")

## Exit check

The learner runs one ramp test three times with the adult, then says what
was kept the same (ball, ramp) and what was changed (the push). The adult
records the answer.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** "Same" and "changed" are the working words;
  hold up the same ball each time ("same ball? yes") and a different ball
  ("same ball? no"). Home-language equivalents are welcomed.
- **Alternate response mode:** Pointing to the two same-things (ball, ramp)
  counts as naming them; the adult scribes. Counting the three marks on
  fingers ("one, two, three tests") supports the repeat idea.
- **Extension:** "Test a *different* ball with the *same* gentle push three
  times. Does the pattern hold?" Or: "Which trial was the farthest of your
  three? Why might one be a little different?" (Even same tests wiggle a
  little — a gentle preview of real-world variation.)
- **Table and floor variants:** Table ramp with a marble and sticker marks,
  or the full floor ramp; both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "Three times is just for fun." Redirect: "The fun
part is real — but the *reason* is trust. One roll could be lucky. Three
rolls that agree tell us the push did it."

**Common misconception:** "Fair means being nice." Redirect: "In science,
*fair test* means only one thing changes. Unfair tests trick us."

**Common misconception:** Learners may change two things at once (strong
push AND higher ramp) and not notice. Slow down: "Name one thing to change.
Everything else — hands off."

**Answer-key reference:** [`../answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md#lesson-04) —
the same test = same ramp, same ball, same kind of push, three trials; the
unfair changes to spot are the different ball (modeled) and the raised ramp
(error analysis).
