# Lesson 06 — Push-pull obstacle course (review)

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Show what you know. We run the
push-and-pull obstacle course: push it, pull it, turn it, stop it. You are
the scientist AND the engineer today."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** toy cars, a ball, the string toy,
the ramp, one bumper, two targets, tape for the course stations.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

Retrieval warm-up from Unit 01: look at the course setup together. "Finish
this: 'I **notice** ___.' Good. Now finish: 'I **wonder** ___.'" (Example:
"I wonder if the bumper works the second time.") One notice, one wonder —
then we play.

## Teach and model

Brief review only — this lesson is mostly doing. Quick word check with
motions: "Push?" (hands push air away). "Pull?" (pull toward). "Gentle?"
(feather touch). "Strong?" (firm). "Direction?" (point two ways). "Turn?"
(curve a hand). "Stop?" (flat hand halts).

**Modeled example 1 — the course, narrated.** Adult walks the course
slowly, narrating each station: "Station 1: I **push** the car — it goes
*away* toward the ramp. Station 2: I roll the ball down and it hits the
**bumper** — it *turns*. Station 3: I **pull** the truck with the string —
it comes *closer* to me. Station 4: I **stop** the ball with my flat hand —
a push can stop motion. I **notice** every move changed how something
moved." (Modeled reasoning: every action is named as push/pull and its
effect is stated.)

**Modeled example 2 — catch my mistake.** Adult pulls the truck's string
and announces, "That was a **push**!" Pause. The learners correct: "No! It
came *closer* — it was a **pull**!" Celebrate: "You caught it. Scientists
check each other's work." (Error analysis, playful.)

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided course run:** The learner walks the four stations with the
   adult, naming each move aloud: "push! … turn! … pull! … stop!"

## Independent practice

2. **Independent course run:** The learner runs the full course alone,
   naming each move. The adult only watches and scribes the four names.
3. **Independent strength control:** "Push the ball so it **stops before**
   the tape line." (Control: a gentle push on purpose — strength is a tool,
   not just "more.")
4. **Independent direction choice:** "Pull the truck to the target YOU
   choose. Tell me which target and why." (Accept any target + reason:
   "the red box, because it is closer.")
5. **Independent sort-from-memory:** The adult describes three actions
   from the Lesson 01 picture: "The child moves the red car toward the
   ramp" (push); "The hand moves the truck toward itself" (pull); "The
   ball bumps the cushion and stops" (a push stopped it). The learner
   names push or pull for each.

## Application

6. **Design one station:** "Add ONE new station to our course. What is the
   move — push, pull, turn, or stop?" The learner builds it and
   demonstrates. (Engineering creativity inside the unit's vocabulary.)

## Exit check

"Name **two** things you can do to a rolling ball to change how it moves."
(Accept any two: push it harder/softer, pull it, change its direction with
a bumper, stop it.) The adult records the two moves. This is the unit's
final review check before the quiz.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** Every station has its motion (push/pull)
  acted out first; home-language labels are welcomed. The course can be
  "run" entirely by pointing at stations in order.
- **Alternate response mode:** Naming the moves by pointing to picture
  cards (adult-drawn push/pull icons) counts; the adult scribes everything.
- **Extension:** Time-free challenge: "Can you run the course using ONLY
  pulls?" (Pull the car with the string, pull the ball with… a problem to
  solve! Accept creative attempts.) Or: teach the course to a stuffed
  animal, narrating every move.
- **Table and floor variants:** Table-top mini course with a marble, or the
  full floor course; both work.

## Teacher note

**Watch for:** Learners who can *do* every station but name none of the
moves — the doing is real science, but the words are the unit's objective.
Narrate alongside them ("you just *pulled* it — say it with me").

**Watch for:** The stop station being skipped — stopping is the quiet hero
of K-PS2-1 (a push can stop motion). Insist on the stop.

**Answer-key reference:** [`../answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md#lesson-06) —
acceptable move pairs for the exit check: any two of push harder/softer
(strength), pull, change direction (bumper/turn), stop. The sort-from-memory
answers are push / pull / push-stopped-it.
