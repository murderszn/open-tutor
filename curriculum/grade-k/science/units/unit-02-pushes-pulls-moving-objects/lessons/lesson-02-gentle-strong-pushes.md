# Lesson 02 — Gentle and strong pushes

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you test gentle and strong pushes.
A **gentle** push moves a thing a little. A **strong** push moves it a
lot. You will find out which push sends your car farther."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** a toy car, a cardboard ramp (a
board or book leaning on a low stack of books), masking tape or crayon
marks, a smooth floor.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Push my hand gently." (Feather touch.) "Now push my hand strongly."
(Firmer — keep it safe and friendly.) "Gentle is small and soft. Strong is
big and firm. Both are pushes."

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *Pushes can be **gentle** or **strong** — same
car, same floor, but the push is different. A stronger push usually moves
the thing **farther** (a longer way). Scientists keep everything the same
and change only the push, so they know the push caused what happened.*

**Modeled example 1 — gentle push.** Adult puts the car at the start line
and pushes it **gently** on the flat floor. "I pushed gently. Look — it
stopped *here*." Adult marks the spot with tape. "Gentle push — a short
trip." (Modeled reasoning: same car, same floor, gentle push, mark the
result.)

**Modeled example 2 — strong push.** Same start line, same car: "Now I
push **strongly**." The car rolls farther. Adult marks the new stop.
"I **notice** this mark is farther from the start. Strong push — a longer
trip. I changed only the push, so the push made the difference."

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** Adult pushes gently; the learner pushes strongly from the
   same start line. "Whose mark is farther? Why?" (The strong push's mark;
   the push was stronger.)

## Independent practice

2. **Independent three-trial test:** "Push the car **gently** three times
   from the start line. Mark each stop." The learner points to the three
   marks: "They are all close together — gentle pushes go a short way."
3. **Independent prediction:** "Before you push: will a **strong** push go
   farther than your gentle marks? Tell me your guess, then test it."
   (Oral prediction + test — previews Lesson 04's three-times testing.)
4. **Independent oral:** "Roll the ball down the ramp with a gentle push,
   then a strong push. Which one went farther? What did you change?"
   (Only the push changed; the ball and ramp stayed the same.)
5. **Error-analysis:** The adult does a very strong push on a bumpy rug
   and says, "My strong push went a short way — so strong pushes go less
   far!" The learner explains the unfair part: "The floor changed! The rug
   is bumpy." (Accept: "you changed the floor." — sets up fair-test
   thinking for Lesson 04.)

## Application

6. **Real-life transfer:** "When do you want a **gentle** push in real
   life?" (Passing a glass of water, pushing a swing for a baby.) "When do
   you want a **strong** push?" (Pushing a heavy door, kicking a ball far.)
   The adult scribes one example of each.

## Exit check

The learner shows a gentle push and a strong push with the car from the
same start line, and says which mark went farther and why. The adult
records the words.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** "Gentle" and "strong" are taught with the
  hand-feel first; pair with home-language words. Marks on the floor make
  "farther" visible without numbers.
- **Alternate response mode:** Pointing to the farther mark counts as the
  comparison; the adult scribes the reason. A learner may draw the two
  trips instead of marking.
- **Extension:** "Try a *medium* push. Where does its mark land?" (Between
  the two.) Or: "Push the *ball* gently and strongly — does the pattern
  hold for a different object?"
- **Table and floor variants:** Table-top track with a small car and tape
  marks, or the full floor ramp; both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "The car went far because I am strong" (person,
not push). Redirect: "The *push* was strong. Let's try: same you, gentle
push — see?"

**Common misconception:** "Farther is always better." Redirect: "Sometimes
we want a short trip — like putting a puzzle piece in place. Gentle pushes
have a job too."

**Safety:** Keep strong pushes aimed away from faces, windows, and fragile
things; set the "test lane" before pushing.

**Answer-key reference:** [`../answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md#lesson-02) —
strong pushes send the car farther than gentle pushes from the same start;
the ramp and car stay the same in the comparison.
