# Project — My Motion Machine

**Type:** multi-session investigation (runs across Sessions 6, 10, and 14
of the unit pacing). **Time:** three 15–20-minute sessions plus sharing.
**Product:** a working ramp course — drawn and adult-scribed — that sends a
ball from a start line to a target box, with a turn, tested three times.

## Driving question

"Can you build a course that sends the ball exactly where you want it to
go?"

## Materials

Cardboard ramp, one ball, books/blocks for walls, one bumper (block) and
one soft cushion, a target box, tape, paper and crayons, the science
journal. The adult builds the ramp base and ties any knots; the learner
designs and places.

## Steps

### Part 1 — Plan and first roll (Session 6)

1. The adult states the challenge: "The ball starts at the top of the
   ramp. It must land in the target box."
2. **Plan:** the learner points to or draws the path the ball should take
   (down the ramp, across the floor, into the box). The adult scribes the
   plan in the journal: "The ball will go ___."
3. **Build:** the learner places the ramp and the box; the adult steadies.
4. **Test once:** roll the ball. "What happened?" The adult scribes the
   result next to the plan: "It went ___."
5. Adult checks: does the result match the plan? Either answer is fine —
   Part 1 is about planning and observing, not succeeding.

### Part 2 — Add the turn (Session 10)

1. New challenge: "Now the ball must **turn** before it reaches the box."
   (Harder problem — K-PS2-2 territory.)
2. **Plan:** "Where will you put the bumper so the ball turns?" The learner
   places the bumper and says the plan: "The ball will hit the bumper and
   turn."
3. **Test twice.** After each test: "Did it work as intended — yes or not
   yet?" The adult scribes both answers.
4. **Improve once:** change one thing (tilt the bumper, move it, swap the
   block for the cushion) and test again. "Is it better now?"
5. Journal: draw the path with the turn; the adult scribes "it worked /
   not yet" and what was changed.

### Part 3 — Final test and share (Session 14)

1. **Same test, three times** (Lesson 04 in action): the learner runs the
   full course three times without changing anything. "Three tests, same
   course — do you trust it?"
2. **Share:** the learner shows the machine to the adult (or family) and
   explains: "I push the ball here. It rolls down, hits the bumper, turns,
   and lands in the box." The adult asks one follow-up: "What did you
   change to make it work?"
3. The learner picks the machine's "best moment" (the turn, the landing)
   and the adult scribes it as the project's closing line.

## Deliverables

- Journal pages: the path plan (Part 1), the turn plan with test results
  and the improvement (Part 2), the three-test record (Part 3).
- A 1–2-minute share: where the ball goes, what the bumper does, and what
  was changed to make it work.

## Rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plan and carry out | Drew/pointed a plan, built it, and tested (Parts 1–2) | Built and tested with heavy adult direction; plan unclear | No plan; random building |
| Compare strength | Chose a push strength on purpose and said why (e.g., "gentle so it doesn't fly past") | Used different strengths but can't say why | No attention to push strength |
| Change direction | Placed a bumper that changes the ball's direction | Bumper placed; direction change unclear or accidental | No turn attempted |
| Test as intended + improve | Tested, said "worked / not yet," changed one thing, re-tested | Tested but did not judge or change | No test-and-improve cycle |
| Communicate | Explains the path, the bumper, and the change in the share | Shares with heavy prompting | Does not share |

**Scoring:** 8–10 = ready to move on; 5–7 = secure with the week's review
sessions, then move on; 0–4 = use the track's flexible catch-up time to
re-teach Lessons 04 and 05 before Unit 03 (see the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md)).

## Accessibility and safety

- All text may be adult-scribed; pointing to the path or acting out the
  roll may replace any spoken part.
- If no ramp materials are available, the "machine" may be a floor path
  with pushed/rolled balls and hand-drawn arrows — the objectives (plan,
  test, turn, improve) are the same.
- Keep the ball in the test lane; no throwing. The adult handles all
  stacking and knots.
