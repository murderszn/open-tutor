# Unit 02 — Pushes, Pulls, and Moving Objects

**Grade:** Kindergarten · **Subject:** Science · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #7](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/7), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Pushes and pulls are how the world gets moving — and how it stops. In this
unit the learner names pushes and pulls with their body first and words
second, tests gentle vs. strong pushes on the same car, changes where a toy
goes by pulling in different directions, runs the same rolling test three
times (a first fair test), and builds a bumper that turns a rolling ball —
testing and improving it like an engineer. Every response may be spoken,
pointed to, acted out, drawn, or adult-scribed.

## Essential question

How can we make things move, change direction, or stop — and how do we know
our way works?

## Prerequisites

- Unit 01: "I notice / I wonder" language, describing what is seen, sorting
  by an observable property (used in Lessons 03 and 06).
- Can roll a ball, push a toy, and walk safely around a floor course.
- No reading, writing, or counting beyond whole-number marks is assumed.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Tell or show a **push** vs. a **pull** on familiar objects, and name what
   the push or pull made the object do (start moving, stop, move farther).
2. Compare a **gentle** push with a **strong** push on the same object and
   describe the difference in what happened (the strong push sends it
   farther).
3. Show with a string-pull that pulling in a **different direction** sends
   an object a different way, and tell whether a push or pull changed an
   object's direction.
4. With adult help, plan and carry out the **same simple rolling test three
   times** — keeping the ramp and ball the same and changing only the push —
   and say what changed and what stayed the same.
5. **Build a simple solution** (a ramp turn or bumper) that changes the
   speed or direction of a rolling ball, test it, tell whether it worked as
   intended, and improve it.

## Vocabulary (taught with the body first, word second)

push · pull · motion · start · stop · gentle · strong · direction · ramp ·
roll · bumper · turn · test · same · change · works ("did it work?") ·
improve · engineer

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS),
kindergarten performance expectations**, as published by Achieve, Inc.
(2013). The two codes below and their descriptions were verified against
the official standards search at
[nextgenscience.org/search-standards](https://www.nextgenscience.org/search-standards)
(re-confirmed reachable and matching 2026-10-15; the track audit verified
them 2026-10-01). The clarification statements and assessment boundaries
summarized below are paraphrased from the standards' published framework
notes (cross-checked against a published K curriculum correlation,
2026-10-15). No state adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification
is claimed.

| Code | Description |
|---|---|
| K-PS2-1 | Plan and conduct an investigation to compare the effects of different strengths or different directions of pushes and pulls on the motion of an object. |
| K-PS2-2 | Analyze data to determine if a design solution works as intended to change the speed or direction of an object with a push or a pull. |

Assessment notes the unit honors (paraphrased): at K, assessment compares
different strengths **or** different directions — not both at once — and
does not include non-contact pushes/pulls (e.g., magnets) or friction as
the mechanism for speed change. This unit never tests strength and
direction in the same task, uses only contact pushes/pulls, and treats
"the ball slowed down" without naming friction.

| Unit objective | NGSS connection |
|---|---|
| 1, 2, 3 | K-PS2-1 — compare effects of different strengths or different directions of pushes and pulls |
| 4 | K-PS2-1 — plan and carry out the investigation (same-test-three-times) |
| 5 | K-PS2-2 — test whether the built solution changes motion as intended; improve it |

Crosscutting concept (K expression): **Cause and effect** — pushes and
pulls *cause* changes in how things move. Practice emphasis: planning and
carrying out investigations (repeat the same test), analyzing data (marks
close together vs. farther), and constructing simple solutions (the
bumper). Teacher background: the Generation Genius K–2 pushes-and-pulls
teacher notes (common misconceptions) and the repo's
`resources/physics_fundamentals.md` (adult background only).

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items: a toy car and a ball that roll well, a
short piece of string or yarn tied to a toy truck, a cardboard ramp (a
folded box lid or a board leaning on books — the adult builds it), books or
blocks for walls, a cushion and a block to use as bumpers, two targets
(shoeboxes, cushions, or taped floor squares), masking tape or crayons for
marks, paper and crayons, the science journal, the generated illustration
[`assets/push-pull-play-scene.png`](assets/push-pull-play-scene.png). No
sharp tools; keep rolling balls in the test lane, aimed away from faces and
fragile things; the adult builds the ramp and ties knots. All sessions are
adult-supervised. See the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) §8 for the full
safe-materials list.

## 16-session pacing (4 weeks × 4 sessions, 15–20 minutes each)

Sessions rotate as: investigation lesson → test-and-measure play →
design-and-build session → share-and-review, matching the track session
model. The project (My Motion Machine) runs across Sessions 6, 10, and 14.

| Session | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lesson | [L1 — Push or pull?](lessons/lesson-01-push-or-pull.md) — vocabulary with the generated play-scene illustration |
| 2 | Practice | [Push/pull scavenger hunt](practice.md#session-a-pushpull-scavenger-hunt) — find pushes and pulls in the room |
| 3 | Play | [Start-and-stop game](practice.md#session-b-start-and-stop-game) — a push starts it, a push stops it |
| 4 | Review | Share-and-review circle; L1 exit re-checks |
| 5 | Lesson | [L2 — Gentle and strong pushes](lessons/lesson-02-gentle-strong-pushes.md) — same car, different push |
| 6 | Project | [My Motion Machine](project.md), Part 1 — plan the path, build the ramp, first roll |
| 7 | Play | [Test-and-measure](practice.md#session-c-test-and-measure) — gentle vs. strong, mark how far |
| 8 | Review | Share circle; L1–L2 exit re-checks |
| 9 | Lesson | [L3 — Pulls in different directions](lessons/lesson-03-pulls-different-directions.md) — string pulls to two targets |
| 10 | Project | [My Motion Machine](project.md), Part 2 — add the turn; "did it work as intended?" |
| 11 | Lesson | [L4 — Same test, three times](lessons/lesson-04-same-test-three-times.md) — the first fair test |
| 12 | Lesson | [L5 — Build it so it turns](lessons/lesson-05-build-it-so-it-turns.md) — bumper design, test, improve |
| 13 | Lesson | [L6 — Push-pull obstacle course](lessons/lesson-06-push-pull-obstacle-course.md) — review games |
| 14 | Project | [My Motion Machine](project.md), Part 3 — final three-test run, share with the adult |
| 15 | Check | [Formative quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md) (oral/observed, adult-scribed) |
| 16 | Assessment | [Unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md) (performance tasks) + re-teach planning |

Weeks 7–9 introduce and practice; week 10 (track week 10) is the review
week: the obstacle course, the machine showcase, the formative check, and
the culminating assessment. If the learner is not yet secure on an objective
after Session 16, use the diagnostic/catch-up guidance in the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) before starting Unit 03 — the track's
flexible weeks exist for exactly this.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, acting-out, or drawing responses; the
adult scribes. "Farther" is shown with tape marks, not numbers; "same" and
"changed" are shown with objects, not words alone. Each lesson includes a
seated-table and a floor variant, a language-access support, an alternate
response mode, and an extension. Home-language push/pull words are welcomed.
The generated illustration ships with a text-only alternative containing
every detail the activity needs.
