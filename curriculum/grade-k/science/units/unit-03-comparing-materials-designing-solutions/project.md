# Project — Keep Teddy Dry

**Type:** multi-session investigation (runs across Sessions 6, 10, and 14
of the unit pacing). **Time:** three 15–20-minute sessions plus sharing.
**Product:** two tested rain covers for a toy bear, a comparison record,
and one improved final cover — drawn and adult-scribed — that keeps
Teddy dry under the same "rain."

## Driving question

"Can you design a cover that keeps Teddy dry in the rain — and prove it
is better than another cover?"

## Materials

The toy teddy, cardboard, a plastic bag, foil, tape, paper and crayons,
the watering can or spray bottle (adult pours only), the tub, a towel,
the science journal. The adult cuts cardboard and foil and handles all
scissors and tape dispensers as needed; the learner designs and places.

## Steps

### Part 1 — Define, sketch, build cover 1 (Session 6)

1. The adult states the problem: "Teddy gets wet in the rain. We want
   him to stay dry."
2. **Define:** the learner dictates the three "must do" rules (Lesson 04)
   while the adult scribes them in the journal: cover all of Teddy, keep
   water out, stay up by itself.
3. **Sketch:** the learner draws cover 1, showing its shape and where
   water will run off (Lesson 05). The adult scribes any labels.
4. **Build:** the learner builds cover 1 from the sketch (cardboard roof
   is the usual first choice). The adult steadies and tapes on request.
5. **First rain (no judging yet):** the adult pours three spoonfuls over
   cover 1. "What happened?" The adult scribes the result next to the
   sketch: "Teddy was ___ (dry / a little wet / very wet)."
6. Adult checks: does the result match the sketch's promise? Either
   answer is fine — Part 1 is about defining, sketching, and observing,
   not succeeding.

### Part 2 — Build cover 2, test both, compare (Session 10)

1. New challenge: "Build a **second** cover from a **different**
   material or a **different** shape." (Sloped plastic-bag cover is the
   usual second choice.)
2. **Sketch** cover 2 the same way: shape plus the water's path.
3. **Test both fairly:** the adult pours the same three spoonfuls over
   each cover, one at a time, on the same Teddy. After each test the
   learner checks Teddy's fur and the adult scribes: "Cover 1: ___.
   Cover 2: ___."
4. **Compare:** "Which cover was better? What is your evidence?" The
   learner names the winner and the evidence (Teddy drier), plus one
   strength and one weakness for each cover.
5. Journal: draw both covers side by side (adult-scribed labels) with
   their results underneath.

### Part 3 — Improve the winner, final test, share (Session 14)

1. **Improve once:** change one thing about the winning cover (tape the
   edges, add side walls, steepen the slope). "What did you change, and
   what do you think it will do?"
2. **Final test, three times:** the learner runs the same three-spoonful
   rain over the improved cover three times without changing anything
   (Lesson 02's repeat-the-test move, applied to design). After each
   run: "Dry, a little wet, or very wet?" The adult scribes all three.
3. **Judge:** "Did the change help? Is the improved cover better than
   the winner was before?" The learner judges from the three results.
4. **Share:** the learner shows both covers and the winner to the adult
   (or family) and explains: "The problem was ___. My covers were ___.
   The better one was ___ because ___. I changed ___ and it ___."
5. The learner picks the project's "best moment" (the first dry Teddy,
   the winning comparison) and the adult scribes it as the closing line.

## Deliverables

- Journal pages: the "must do" rules, cover 1 sketch with first-rain
  result, cover 2 sketch, the side-by-side comparison record, the
  improvement and three-test record.
- A 1–2-minute share: the problem, both covers, the winner and its
  evidence, and the improvement.

## Rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Define the problem | States the problem and at least two "must do" rules | States the problem with heavy prompting; rules unclear | No problem stated; builds at random |
| Sketch the shape | Sketch shows the cover shape and where water runs off | Sketch shows a cover but not the water's path | No sketch, or a picture unrelated to the cover |
| Test fairly and compare | Tests both covers in the same rain and picks the winner with evidence (Teddy drier) | Tests both but comparison is a guess, or rains differ | No comparison, or only one cover tested |
| Improve and re-test | Changes one thing, re-tests, and judges whether it helped | Changes something but does not re-test or judge | No improvement attempted |
| Communicate | Explains the problem, both covers, the winner's evidence, and the change in the share | Shares with heavy prompting | Does not share |

**Scoring:** 8–10 = ready to move on; 5–7 = secure with the week's review
sessions, then move on; 0–4 = use the track's flexible catch-up time to
re-teach Lessons 03 and 06 before Unit 04 (see the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md)).

## Accessibility and safety

- All text may be adult-scribed; pointing to the sketch or acting out
  the rain path may replace any spoken part.
- If no cardboard or plastic bag is available, covers may be built from
  paper, cloth, and foil — the objectives (define, sketch, compare,
  improve) are the same, and the comparison will simply have a different
  winner.
- The adult pours all water; keep the test over the tub; dry the floor
  at once. No cover is ever tested on a real pet or person.
