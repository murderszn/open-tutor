# Teacher guide — Unit 03: Comparing Materials and Designing Simple Solutions

For the guiding adult. Learner-facing pages are the lessons, practice,
project, and assessments; this guide and the
[answer key](answer-key.md) stay with the adult.

## How this unit works

This unit is the track's first **engineering** unit: it turns Unit 01's
observation language and Unit 02's test-and-improve loop toward a
designed object. The arc is deliberate — properties first (Lessons 01–03),
problem second (Lesson 04), sketch third (Lesson 05), comparison and
improvement last (Lesson 06 and the project). Do not let the building
start before Lesson 04: a learner who builds first will defend the first
thing built instead of judging it. Sessions are 15–20 minutes; stop while
the learner is still engaged rather than finishing a page.

## Session-by-session guidance

| Session | Watch for | If it goes sideways |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (L1) | Material named by color | Return to touch: "Color tells us how it looks. What does it feel like?" |
| 2 (Practice A) | Sorting by size, not material | Remove size cues: one big and one small item of the same material. |
| 3 (Practice B) | "Rough" used for everything bumpy | Contrast pairs: sponge (rough) vs. foil (smooth) side by side. |
| 4 (Review) | Shyness about sharing | The adult shares first, imperfectly ("I thought foil was paper"). |
| 5 (L2) | Sponge argued into the wrong pile | Accept either pile with a reason — squashy-and-springy is a real observation. |
| 6 (Project 1) | Building before defining | Stop and re-read the three rules aloud before any building. |
| 7 (Play) | Splashing instead of testing | One item at a time, adult holds the tub; "dip, lift, look underneath." |
| 8 (Review) | Forgetting Lesson 01 words | Re-run the L1 picture sort as the warm-up. |
| 9 (L3) | "Wet on top means failed" | Point underneath: "The question is what is *under* it." |
| 10 (Project 2) | Different rains for the two covers | Count the spoonfuls aloud together: "Same rain: one, two, three." |
| 11 (L4) | "The problem is we need a cover" | Re-run the too-small and falling-down demos; the problem comes first. |
| 12 (L5) | Pretty picture, no shape | Ask only: "Show me where the water goes." Redraw if there is no path. |
| 13 (L6) | "Better means perfect" | Name the winner's remaining flaw (tape, slipping) out loud. |
| 14 (Project 3) | Changing three things at once | "Engineers change one thing. Pick your best one." |
| 15 (Quiz) | Quiz anxiety | Call it "show me what you can do"; allow a second sitting. |
| 16 (Assessment) | New covers feel unfair | Remind: "Scientists test NEW things — that is the job." |

## Misconceptions to expect (and the fix that works)

1. **"Material means color."** (Lessons 01–02) — Fix: two red things of
   different materials (red cloth, red plastic cup if handy); touch
   decides, not color.
2. **"Soft means bendy, hard means stiff."** (Lesson 02) — Fix: the
   sponge — soft, squashy, springs back, not bendy like cloth.
3. **"Waterproof means always dry."** (Lessons 03, 06) — Fix: the
   hole-in-the-foil demo; a cover's gaps matter as much as its material.
4. **"The problem is that we need a cover."** (Lesson 04) — Fix: the
   problem is Teddy getting wet; the cover is the solution.
5. **"A sketch must be pretty."** (Lesson 05) — Fix: "Can someone else
   see the shape and where the water goes?" That is the whole test.
6. **"One test proves it."** (Lesson 06, Project) — Fix: the three-test
   final run; one test tells us about this rain, this time.
7. **"Bigger is always better."** (Lessons 05–06) — Fix: a huge flat
   cover still pools water; shape beats size.

## Readiness and reteaching

**Ready to move on** looks like: four materials named with a correct
property each (assessment Task 1 at 2), the problem plus two rules
(Task 2 at 2), a sketch showing shape and water path (Task 3 at 2), the
drier cover picked with evidence (Task 4 at 2), and one change made and
judged from a re-test (Task 5 at 2). The unit assessment's 9–10 band is
the formal bar.

**Reteach moves (use before repeating a whole lesson):**
- Objective 1 weak → Practice A sort game daily for a week, 5 minutes,
  with the mystery bag.
- Objective 2 weak → Lesson 04's two failing demos (too small,
  falls down) as a game: the learner is the "rule checker."
- Objective 3 weak → Lesson 05 with the adult sketching first (learner
  traces the water's path) before the learner sketches.
- Objective 4 weak → Lesson 06's comparison with the adult judging
  first ("I say this one — here is my evidence") before the learner
  judges.
- Objective 5 weak → Project Part 3's improvement with the adult choosing
  the change first (learner judges "helped / did not help") before the
  learner chooses.

**When to use flexible time:** a 0–5 on the unit assessment, or two
objectives still at 0 after Session 16, means using the track's flexible
catch-up time before Unit 04. One shaky objective (score 1) can travel
into Unit 04 with a weekly 5-minute warm-up — materials and design
language will reappear naturally in the plant-growth observations
(containers, covers, and watering).

## Safety and accessibility reminders

- All investigations are adult-supervised; the full boundaries are in
  the track [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) §8.
- The adult pours all water and handles all scissors and cutting. Water
  tests run over the tub or a towel, away from books and electronics;
  dry drips at once.
- No glass, no small parts that could be a choking hazard, no testing on
  real pets or people.
- Every check accepts oral, pointing, acting-out, or drawing responses;
  the adult scribes. "Dry / a little wet / very wet" is shown with the
  toy and towel, never numbers.
- Home-language material and property words are welcomed; visual word
  cards with the hand motions (rub, bend, pour, check underneath)
  support language access.
