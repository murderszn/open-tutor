# Answer key — Unit 03: Comparing Materials and Designing Simple Solutions

For the guiding adult only. Keep this file away from the learner-facing
pages. Every answer below was solved independently from the lesson text
and checked against it; where an answer depends on the learner's own
test results, the acceptable range is stated instead of a single value.

## Lesson 01

1. **Guided:** cloth = the folded red cloth in the left tray; sponge =
   the yellow sponge in the left tray; paper = the white sheet in the
   right tray.
2. **Independent pointing:** foil = the shiny silver sheet in the right
   tray; it is shiny, not dull.
3. **Independent sorting:** left tray — wood (block), cloth (red cloth),
   sponge (yellow sponge); right tray — foil (sheet), plastic (bag),
   paper (white sheet).
4. **Independent real-object transfer:** block → wood; cloth → cloth;
   sponge → sponge; foil → foil (metal sheet); bag → plastic; paper →
   paper. Accept "soft stuff" style answers only if the learner then
   names the material with the adult's prompt.
5. **Error-analysis:** the sponge is not wood; it is soft, squishy, and
   full of holes. Wood is the hard block.
6. **Application:** wood — chair leg, shelf, block; cloth — shirt,
   curtain, towel. Any correct pair counts.
**Exit check:** two pictured materials + one real material, named
without the adult naming them first.

## Lesson 02

1. **Guided mystery feel:** answers depend on the hidden item; the adult
   confirms from the revealed object (block: smooth, stiff; cloth:
   smooth or slightly rough, bendy; sponge: rough, squashy; foil:
   smooth, bendy; bag: smooth, bendy; paper: smooth, bendy).
2. **Independent sorting:** bendy — cloth, paper, paper towel, plastic
   bag, foil; stiff — wooden block, cardboard. Sponge: either pile
   accepted with a reason (squashes and springs back).
3. **Independent describing:** any two correct properties per object
   (for example, block: smooth, stiff; cloth: smooth, bendy; foil:
   smooth, bendy; sponge: rough, squashy).
4. **Independent oral:** paper towel — bendy (folds easily). Block —
   smooth on its flat side (accept "a little rough" for an unfinished
   edge, with the reason).
5. **Error-analysis:** paper bends, so it is bendy, not stiff; the
   learner demonstrates the bend.
6. **Application:** bendy — foil or plastic bag, because it must fold
   around the sandwich. A stiff wrap cannot fold around it.
**Exit check:** two correct properties for one unseen item, or the same
for two seen items.

## Lesson 03

1. **Guided:** foil, flat and hole-free, one spoonful — cloth underneath
   stays dry; the foil kept the water out. If water runs off the edge,
   re-center the pour; an edge spill is not a foil failure.
2. **Independent predicting:** dry cloth — soaks up; after the pour the
   cloth underneath is wet, confirming the prediction.
3. **Independent sorting:** soaks up — paper towel, cloth, paper, sponge
   (and cotton ball); keeps water out — plastic bag, flat hole-free foil.
4. **Independent oral:** plastic bag — it kept water out in the test;
   the paper towel soaked water through.
5. **Error-analysis:** foil with a hole — wet underneath; the water went
   through the hole. The material kept water out, but the hole let it
   through, so the cover fails.
6. **Application:** raincoat — more like the plastic bag (keeps water
   out). With a big hole, water gets through the hole.
**Exit check:** cotton ball — soaks up (wet underneath); cardboard —
soaks up over time (damp underneath; accept "soaks up slowly" as the
best answer).

## Lesson 04

1. **Guided:** cardboard roof shape — covers the whole body: yes; keeps
   water out: only for a little while (cardboard soaks up over time);
   stays up by itself: yes. Best answer names all three honestly.
2. **Independent judging:** plastic bag held open as a tent — covers:
   yes if held wide; keeps water out: yes; stays up by itself: no,
   unless propped or taped. The learner should spot the stay-up gap.
3. **Independent oral:** problem — Teddy gets wet in the rain; we want
   him to stay dry. One rule — any of: cover all of Teddy, keep water
   out, stay up by itself.
4. **Independent listing:** the three rules as scribed: 1. Cover all of
   Teddy. 2. Keep water out. 3. Stay up by itself.
5. **Error-analysis:** the problem is not "we do not have a teddy bear"
   — Teddy is present; the problem is that Teddy gets wet in the rain.
6. **Application:** carrying books in the rain — a bag must hold the
   books without tearing, keep water out, and be easy to carry. Any two
   sensible rules count.
**Exit check:** problem in the learner's own words + at least two of
the three rules.

## Lesson 05

1. **Guided:** the learner's sketch must show where water goes — traced
   off the drawing (down a slope, off the sides, or around a wrap). A
   sketch with no water path is sent back for the path.
2. **Independent sketching:** second shape — tent (slopes shed water),
   box (walls block sideways rain), bag-house (wraps and keeps water
   out), dome (water runs off all sides). Any shape with a stated
   helping mechanism counts.
3. **Independent choosing:** either sketch, with a reason tied to shape
   ("water runs off") or to a Lesson 03 result ("plastic kept water
   out").
4. **Independent building:** build matches the sketch in shape and
   material, or the learner names what is different. Differences are
   expected and are not errors.
5. **Error-analysis:** a pretty picture of Teddy with flowers and sun is
   not a cover sketch — a sketch must show the cover shape and how it
   works.
6. **Application:** cup — hollow shape holds water; shoe sole — shape
   grips; umbrella — dome sheds rain (best answer).
**Exit check:** sketch shown + shape named + how the shape helps stated.

## Lesson 06

1. **Guided:** better cover — the sloped plastic cover; evidence: Teddy
   was dry under it, a little wet under the flat cardboard cover.
2. **Independent comparing:** flat cardboard — strength: covers and
   stays up; weakness: soaks through. Sloped plastic — strength: sheds
   water and keeps it out; weakness: can slip or needs tape to stay up.
3. **Independent improving:** one change only (tape edges, add side
   walls, steeper slope); re-tested with the same three spoonfuls.
4. **Independent judging:** "drier / same / wetter" judged from the
   re-test; the adult scribes the before/after and whether the change
   helped. The correct judgment is whatever the re-test shows — not a
   predetermined "helped."
5. **Error-analysis:** three spoonfuls vs. ten spoonfuls is not a fair
   comparison — the rains differ, so the drier result proves nothing.
   Re-test with the same rain.
6. **Application:** plastic bag keeps lunch drier; fair test = same rain
   on both bags, then check inside each.
**Exit check:** better cover picked + evidence (dry vs. a little wet)
+ one improvement named.

## Practice

### Session A — Materials sort game

1. By material: wood — block; cloth — red cloth; sponge — sponge; foil
   — foil sheet; plastic — bag; paper — white paper and paper towel;
   cardboard — cardboard piece.
2. By property: bendy — cloth, paper, paper towel, bag, foil; stiff —
   block, cardboard; sponge either pile with a reason.
3. Harder sort: any reason tied to looking vs. testing (the property
   sort needs touch and bending, not just looking).
4. Speed round: one-word material names as in Lesson 01.

### Session B — Property detective

1. Mystery bag: properties as in Lesson 02, confirmed from the revealed
   object.
2. Room hunt: rough — brick, bark, rough sponge; smooth — tabletop,
   window; bendy — ribbon, cloth; stiff — book, board. Any correct set
   counts.
3. Spoon — stiff (must not fold while stirring); blanket — bendy and
   soft (must fold around you).
4. Error hunt: cotton ball — soft, squashy, not stiff.

### Session C — Water tub tests

1. Soak or keep out: soaks — sponge, cloth, paper towel, paper, cotton
   ball; keeps out — plastic bag, foil.
2. Sink or float: floats — plastic bag, foil sheet on the surface,
   paper briefly, sponge, cotton ball, wooden block (wood floats). A
   waterlogged paper towel or a sponge pressed under sinks — that
   soaking-changes-floating observation is a discovery, not a mistake.
3. Warm or cool (if a metal item is handy): metal feels cool, cloth
   feels warmer. Skipped without penalty if no metal item is handy.
4. Mop up a spill — sponge or paper towel (soak up). Cover food in the
   rain — plastic bag or foil (keep water out).

### Session D — Materials market

1. Buys must name a fitting property (foil for covering food: keeps
   water out).
2. Refusals must name the failing property (paper towel for Teddy's
   rain cover: soaks up water).
3. Re-checks: Lesson 01 — two materials; Lesson 02 — two properties of
   one item; Lesson 03 (Session 8) — soak/keep-out prediction for one
   item.
4. Favorite material: any material with a one-sentence reason tied to a
   property or test.

## Project — Keep Teddy Dry

Rubric scoring is in [`project.md`](project.md#rubric). Acceptable
evidence per criterion:

- **Define the problem (2):** problem stated + at least two "must do"
  rules, adult-scribed.
- **Sketch the shape (2):** sketch shows cover shape + water path.
- **Test fairly and compare (2):** same three-spoonful rain on both
  covers; winner picked with Teddy-dryness evidence.
- **Improve and re-test (2):** one change, re-tested, judged from the
  result.
- **Communicate (2):** share names the problem, both covers, the
  winner's evidence, and the change.
**Bands:** 8–10 ready to move on; 5–7 secure with review, then move on;
0–4 flexible catch-up, re-teach Lessons 03 and 06.

## Formative quiz

1. Block → wood; cloth → cloth; bag → plastic.
2. Sponge — rough (bumpy, holey) and squashy/springy (accept "bendy"
   with the squash reason, or "soft"); soaks up water.
3. Paper towel — prediction: soaks up; after one spoonful the cloth
   underneath is wet, confirming it.
4. Problem — Teddy gets wet in the rain; we want him to stay dry.
5. Cover leaving feet out — does not work; it fails "cover all of
   Teddy," so feet get wet.
6. Better cover — cover B (Teddy dry) over cover A (a little wet);
   evidence is Teddy's fur after the same rain.
