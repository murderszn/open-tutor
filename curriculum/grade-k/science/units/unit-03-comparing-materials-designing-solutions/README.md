# Unit 03 — Comparing Materials and Designing Simple Solutions

**Grade:** Kindergarten · **Subject:** Science · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #7](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/7), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Some things soak up water. Some things keep water out. Some things bend,
and some things stay stiff. In this unit the learner sorts everyday
materials by what they can actually do — not by what they look like —
tests which materials keep a toy dry, and designs, builds, tests, and
improves a rain cover for a toy bear. Every response may be spoken,
pointed to, acted out, drawn, or adult-scribed.

## Essential question

How do we choose the right material for the job — and how do we know our
design works?

## Prerequisites

- Unit 01: "I notice / I wonder" language, describing what is seen, sorting
  by an observable property (used in Lessons 01–02).
- Unit 02: plan and carry out a simple test, repeat the same test, judge
  "did it work as intended?", and improve a design (used in Lessons 05–06
  and the project).
- Can handle a spray bottle or watering can only with the adult pouring;
  the adult pours all water in this unit.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. **Describe materials by observable properties** — color, rough/smooth
   feel, bendy/stiff, and what water does on them — for at least four
   everyday materials, using touch and looking, not color alone.
2. **Define a simple problem** people want solved (a toy gets wet in the
   rain) and say what a good solution **must do**: cover the whole toy,
   keep water out, and stay up on its own.
3. **Draw or build a model** that shows how the **shape** of an object
   helps it solve the problem — for example, a sloped roof shape that lets
   water run off instead of pooling.
4. **Build and test two solutions** to the same problem under the same
   "rain," compare their strengths and weaknesses from the test results,
   and tell which worked better and why, using observations.
5. **Improve a solution** after testing — change one thing, re-test under
   the same "rain," and say whether the change helped.

## Vocabulary (taught with the hands first, word second)

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS),
K–2 engineering design performance expectations**, as published by
Achieve, Inc. (2013). The three codes below and their descriptions were
verified against the official NSTA-hosted NGSS document for K–2
engineering design (opened and read 2026-10-22; the track audit listed
these codes 2026-10-01). No state adoption, accreditation, or alignment
certification is claimed.

| Code | Description |
|---|---|
| K-2-ETS1-1 | Ask questions, make observations, and gather information about a situation people want to change to define a simple problem that can be solved through the development of a new or improved object or tool. |
| K-2-ETS1-2 | Develop a simple sketch, drawing, or physical model to illustrate how the shape of an object helps it function as needed to solve a given problem. |
| K-2-ETS1-3 | Analyze data from tests of two objects designed to solve the same problem to compare the strengths and weaknesses of how each performs. |

| Unit objective | NGSS connection |
|---|---|
| 1 | K-2-ETS1-1 — make observations and gather information about materials before defining the problem |
| 2 | K-2-ETS1-1 — define the simple problem (keep the toy dry) and what a solution must do |
| 3 | K-2-ETS1-2 — sketch or model how the cover's shape helps it work |
| 4 | K-2-ETS1-3 — test two covers, compare strengths and weaknesses from the results |
| 5 | K-2-ETS1-3 — because there is always more than one possible solution, it is useful to compare and test designs; improving and re-testing is the optimization step |

Crosscutting concept (K expression): **Structure and function** — the
shape and stability of a designed object are related to what it does.
Practice emphasis: asking questions and defining problems, developing
and using models (sketches and built covers), and analyzing data from
tests (dry / a little wet / very wet). Teacher background: the NSTA
position statement on early childhood science (adult-guided observation
and talk about what was done and seen) and the repo's
`resources/chemistry_fundamentals.md` (adult background only, on what
materials are; never learner-facing at K).

Assessment notes the unit honors: at K, "waterproof" is never a property
a material has in the abstract — it is a test result ("this cover kept
the toy dry under this rain, this time"). A material with a hole, a gap,
or an uncovered edge can fail even if the material itself keeps water
out; Lessons 03 and 06 teach exactly that. No claim is made that a
winning cover would work in a real storm.

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items, all large enough to handle safely: a
wooden block, a folded red cloth, a yellow sponge, a sheet of kitchen
foil, a clear plastic bag, a sheet of white paper, a paper towel, a piece
of cardboard, cotton balls, two shallow trays or shoebox lids, a small
toy (a teddy bear is used in the lessons; any washable toy works), a
watering can or spray bottle (adult pours only), a shallow tub, a towel,
masking tape, paper and crayons, the science journal, the generated
illustration
[`assets/materials-sort-scene.png`](assets/materials-sort-scene.png).
No glass, no sharp tools for the learner, no small parts that could be a
choking hazard; the adult cuts cardboard and foil and handles all
scissors. All sessions are adult-supervised; water stays over the tub or
a towel, away from books and electronics. See the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) §8 for the full
safe-materials list.

## 16-session pacing (4 weeks × 4 sessions, 15–20 minutes each)

Sessions rotate as: property-exploration lesson → sketch/planning
session → build-and-test session → share-and-review, matching the track
session model. The project (Keep Teddy Dry) runs across Sessions 6, 10,
and 14.

| Session | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lesson | [L1 — What is it made of?](lessons/lesson-01-what-is-it-made-of.md) — sorting by material with the generated illustration |
| 2 | Practice | [Materials sort game](practice.md#session-a-materials-sort-game) — sort the real kit by material |
| 3 | Play | [Property detective](practice.md#session-b-property-detective) — rough/smooth, bendy/stiff hunt |
| 4 | Review | Share-and-review circle; L1 exit re-checks |
| 5 | Lesson | [L2 — Rough, smooth, bendy, stiff](lessons/lesson-02-rough-smooth-bendy-stiff.md) — property words by touch |
| 6 | Project | [Keep Teddy Dry](project.md), Part 1 — define the problem, sketch, build cover 1 |
| 7 | Play | [Water tub tests](practice.md#session-c-water-tub-tests) — soak up, keep out, sink, float |
| 8 | Review | Share circle; L1–L2 exit re-checks |
| 9 | Lesson | [L3 — Does water get through?](lessons/lesson-03-does-water-get-through.md) — the fair water test |
| 10 | Project | [Keep Teddy Dry](project.md), Part 2 — build cover 2, test both, compare |
| 11 | Lesson | [L4 — What is the problem?](lessons/lesson-04-what-is-the-problem.md) — what a good cover must do |
| 12 | Lesson | [L5 — Draw it, then build it](lessons/lesson-05-draw-it-then-build-it.md) — shape helps it work |
| 13 | Lesson | [L6 — Test two covers, pick the better one, improve it](lessons/lesson-06-test-two-covers-improve.md) — compare and improve |
| 14 | Project | [Keep Teddy Dry](project.md), Part 3 — improve the winner, final test, showcase |

Weeks 11–13 introduce and practice; week 14 (track week 14) is the review
week: the materials market, the design showcase, the formative check,
and the culminating assessment. If the learner is not yet secure on an
objective after Session 16, use the diagnostic/catch-up guidance in the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) before starting Unit 04 — the track's
flexible weeks exist for exactly this.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, acting-out, or drawing responses; the
adult scribes. "Dry / a little wet / very wet" is shown with the toy and
a towel, not numbers; "bendy" and "stiff" are shown with hands, not words
alone. Each lesson includes a seated-table and a floor variant, a
language-access support, an alternate response mode, and an extension.
Home-language material and property words are welcomed. The generated
illustration ships with a text-only alternative containing every detail
the activity needs.
