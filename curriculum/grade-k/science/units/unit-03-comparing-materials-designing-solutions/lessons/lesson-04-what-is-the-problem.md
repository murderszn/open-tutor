# Lesson 04 — What is the problem?

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you are an engineer. An engineer
starts with a **problem** — something people want to change. Our
problem: Teddy gets wet in the rain. You will tell what a good cover
must do."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** the toy teddy, the watering can
or spray bottle (adult holds it), the picture from Lesson 01
([`../assets/materials-sort-scene.png`](../assets/materials-sort-scene.png))
showing Teddy under his cardboard roof, paper and crayons for the adult
to scribe the "must do" list.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Look at Teddy. Pretend it is raining." The adult mimes rain with fingers
(no water yet). "What is the problem?" Accept: "Teddy gets wet." The
adult repeats it as a problem sentence: "Teddy gets wet in the rain, and
we want him to stay dry."

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *A **problem** is a situation people want to
change. Engineers do not start by building. They start by asking: what
is the problem, and what must a good solution do? A good solution for
Teddy must do three things: cover his whole body, keep water out, and
stay up by itself. If it does only one of those, it is not finished yet.*

**Modeled example 1 — a cover that is too small.** Adult holds a small
piece of paper over Teddy's head only: "I **notice** his head is covered
but his feet stick out. Rain would still wet his feet. Does it cover his
whole body? No. So it fails rule one — even though paper is a material
we tested." (Modeled reasoning: check each rule, name the failed rule.)

**Modeled example 2 — a cover that falls down.** Adult drapes the cloth
over Teddy with nothing holding it: "I **notice** it covers him, but
when I let go, it slides off. Does it stay up by itself? No. So it fails
rule three. A good cover must stay up."

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** The adult shows the cardboard roof shape from the picture
   (or a folded cardboard piece): "Check the three rules with me. Does it
   cover his whole body? Does it keep water out? Does it stay up?"
   (Cardboard: covers, stays up — but from Lesson 03, cardboard soaks
   up water over time, so "keeps water out" is shaky. Accept "covers and
   stays up; keeps water out only for a little while" as the best
   answer.)

## Independent practice

2. **Independent judging:** The adult shows a plastic bag held open over
   Teddy like a tent (not wrapped tight): "Check the three rules." (Covers
   whole body: yes, if held wide. Keeps water out: yes. Stays up by
   itself: no, unless it is propped or taped — the learner should spot
   this gap.)
3. **Independent oral:** "In your own words, what is our problem?" (Teddy
   gets wet in the rain; we want him to stay dry.) "Name one thing a good
   cover must do." (Any of the three rules.)
4. **Independent listing:** The learner dictates the "must do" list while
   the adult scribes: "1. Cover all of Teddy. 2. Keep water out. 3. Stay
   up by itself." The learner points to each rule as it is read back.
5. **Error-analysis (guided):** The adult says, "The problem is that we
   do not have a teddy bear!" The learner corrects: "No — we have Teddy.
   The problem is that **Teddy gets wet in the rain**."

## Application

6. **New problem:** "What if the problem was carrying heavy books in the
   rain — what must a bag do?" (Hold the books without tearing, keep
   water out, be easy to carry. Accept any two sensible rules.)

## Exit check

The learner states the problem in their own words and names at least two
of the three "must do" rules. The adult records them.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** The three rules are shown with actions
  (spread arms wide for "cover all," hands as a roof for "stay up"),
  not words alone. Home-language problem words are welcomed.
- **Alternate response mode:** Pointing to the failing part (feet
  sticking out, cover sliding off) counts fully; the adult scribes.
- **Extension:** "What if Teddy were bigger — a real dog? Would the same
  cover work?" (No — a bigger cover, stronger supports. The rules stay
  the same; the size changes.)
- **Table and floor variants:** Seated with Teddy on the table, or on the
  floor with Teddy on a towel; both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "The problem is that we need a cover."
Redirect: "A cover is a *solution*. The problem is what happens first:
Teddy gets wet. If we name the wrong problem, we build the wrong thing."

**Common misconception:** "One rule is enough." Redirect: run the
too-small and falling-down demos again — each fails exactly one rule,
and each failure still gets Teddy wet.

**Answer-key reference:** [`../answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md#lesson-04) —
problem: Teddy gets wet in the rain; rules: cover whole body, keep water
out, stay up by itself; cardboard roof: covers + stays up, keeps water
out only briefly.
