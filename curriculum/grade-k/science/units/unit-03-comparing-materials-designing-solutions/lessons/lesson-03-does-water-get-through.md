# Lesson 03 — Does water get through?

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you test materials with water.
Some materials **soak up** water. Some materials **keep water out**.
You will watch what the water does and tell which is which."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** a paper towel, a clear plastic
bag laid flat, a sheet of foil laid flat, a dry cloth or towel
underneath each test item as the "what is underneath" check, a small cup
or spoon for the adult to pour with, the tub and a towel for drips. The
adult pours all water.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Yesterday we felt materials. Today we test them. What do you think will
happen if I pour water on a paper towel?" Take one prediction, then:
"Scientists do not guess and stop — they pour and look. We will pour
the same amount on each material and look underneath."

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *Some materials **soak up** water — the water
goes into them, like a sponge drinking. Some materials **keep water
out** — the water sits on top or runs off, and what is underneath stays
dry. A material that keeps water out is called **waterproof** — but only
for the test we did. If it has a hole or a gap, water can still get
through the hole. So we test, and we look underneath.*

**Modeled example 1 — the paper towel soaks it up.** Adult pours one
spoonful onto the paper towel: "I **notice** the towel gets dark and
wet. I lift it — the cloth underneath is wet too. The towel **soaked
up** the water, and the water went through. Not waterproof." (Modeled
reasoning: pour the same amount, look underneath, name the result.)

**Modeled example 2 — the plastic bag keeps it out.** Adult pours one
spoonful onto the flat plastic bag: "I **notice** the water sits on top
in little drops. I lift the bag — the cloth underneath is dry. The bag
**kept the water out**. For this test, it is waterproof."

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** The adult pours one spoonful onto the foil sheet. "Look
   underneath. Dry or wet?" (Dry — the foil kept the water out, as long
   as the sheet has no holes and the water does not run off the edge.)
   Prompt with "What does the cloth underneath tell us?" if stuck.

## Independent practice

2. **Independent predicting:** Before the next pour, "This is a dry
   cloth. Will it soak up water or keep water out? Tell me before I
   pour." (Soak up.) The adult pours; the learner checks underneath and
   confirms or corrects the prediction.
3. **Independent sorting:** "Put the materials we tested into two piles:
   **soaks up** and **keeps water out**." (Soaks up: paper towel, cloth,
   paper, sponge. Keeps out: plastic bag, foil — if flat and hole-free.)
4. **Independent oral:** "If you wanted to keep a book dry in the rain,
   would you wrap it in a paper towel or a plastic bag? Why?" (Plastic
   bag — it kept the water out in our test.)
5. **Error-analysis (guided):** The adult pours water on a foil sheet
   with a small hole poked in it (adult prepares it) and says, "Foil is
   waterproof, so underneath must be dry!" The learner checks: "No —
   it is wet! The water went through the **hole**." The adult confirms:
   "The material kept water out, but the hole let it through. That is
   why covers must have no gaps."

## Application

6. **Real-object transfer:** "Your raincoat keeps you dry. Is a raincoat
   more like the paper towel or more like the plastic bag?" (Plastic
   bag — it keeps water out.) "What would happen if your raincoat had a
   big hole in it?" (Water would get through the hole.)

## Exit check

The learner predicts and then checks one new item (a cotton ball or a
piece of cardboard): soaks up or keeps out, judged by looking
underneath. The adult records the prediction and the result.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** "Soak up" is shown with the darkening
  towel; "keeps out" is shown with the dry cloth underneath. Home-language
  water words are welcomed.
- **Alternate response mode:** Pointing to the dry/wet cloth, or holding
  up the "soaks up" / "keeps out" pile card, counts fully; the adult
  scribes.
- **Extension:** "Pour the same spoonful on the sponge. Does it soak up
  more or less than the paper towel?" (More — it holds a whole spoonful
  and still looks dry underneath at first; with a second spoonful it
  drips. Two spoonfuls is a new test, not the same test.)
- **Table and floor variants:** Seated at a table over the tub, or on the
  floor on a towel with the tub beside; both work, as long as the adult
  pours.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "Waterproof means always dry, forever."
Redirect: "Waterproof is a test result, not a magic word. This bag kept
water out under one spoonful. A hole, a gap, or a flood can still beat
it — that is why Lesson 06 tests the whole cover, not just the
material."

**Common misconception:** "If it got wet on top, it failed." Redirect:
"Wet on top is fine — the question is what is *underneath*. The plastic
bag is wet on top and dry underneath. That is a pass."

**Safety note:** The adult pours; the learner never pours near their own
face or clothes. Mop drips at once so the floor is not slippery.
