# Lesson 06 — Test two covers, pick the better one, improve it

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you test *two* covers in the same
rain, compare them, pick the **better** one, and **improve** it. Better
means drier Teddy — and you must show your evidence."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** two built covers from the project
(or two quick builds: a flat cardboard cover and a sloped plastic-bag
cover), the toy teddy, the watering can or spray bottle (adult pours),
a dry cloth to check Teddy after each test, the journal for the
dry / a little wet / very wet record. The adult pours all water.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Remind me of the three rules a good cover must do." (Cover all of
Teddy, keep water out, stay up by itself.) "Today we judge covers by
those rules — and by Teddy's fur after the rain."

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *A fair comparison tests two covers in the
**same** rain: the same number of pours, from the same height, on the
same Teddy. After each test we check Teddy: **dry**, **a little wet**,
or **very wet**. The cover that leaves Teddy drier is **better** — but
better does not mean perfect. Then we **improve** the winner: change one
thing, and test it again. If Teddy is drier, the change helped.*

**Modeled example 1 — the flat cardboard cover.** Adult pours three
spoonfuls over the flat cardboard cover: "I **notice** water sat on top,
then soaked through near the middle. I lift the cover — Teddy's back is
a little wet. Record: **a little wet**. It covered him and stayed up,
but it did not fully keep water out." (Modeled reasoning: same rain,
check Teddy, name the evidence, judge by the rules.)

**Modeled example 2 — the sloped plastic cover.** Adult pours the same
three spoonfuls over the sloped plastic-bag cover: "I **notice** water
ran off the sides. I lift the cover — Teddy is dry. Record: **dry**.
This cover is **better** because Teddy is drier — and I can say why:
the slope ran the water off, and the plastic kept water out."

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** "Which cover was better? Point to it and tell me your
   evidence." (The sloped plastic cover — Teddy was dry, the other left
   him a little wet.) Prompt with "What did Teddy's fur tell us?" if
   stuck.

## Independent practice

2. **Independent comparing:** The learner states one strength and one
   weakness for each cover. (Flat cardboard: strength — covers and stays
   up; weakness — soaks through. Sloped plastic: strength — sheds water,
   keeps out; weakness — can slip or need tape to stay up.)
3. **Independent improving:** "Change ONE thing about the winner to make
   it even better." (Tape its edges down, add side walls, make the slope
   steeper.) The learner makes the change; the adult re-tests with the
   same three spoonfuls.
4. **Independent judging:** After the re-test, "Is Teddy drier, the same,
   or wetter than before?" The learner judges whether the change helped,
   and the adult scribes: "We changed ___. Teddy was ___ before and ___
   now. The change helped / did not help."
5. **Error-analysis (guided):** The adult tests cover A with three
   spoonfuls and cover B with ten spoonfuls and says, "Cover A is better
   — Teddy was drier!" The learner corrects: "No — that was not the
   **same rain**! Cover B got more water. That test was not fair."

## Application

6. **New comparison:** "Two lunch bags: one paper, one plastic. It rains
   on both. Which keeps lunch drier? How would you test it fairly?"
   (Plastic — and the fair test is the same rain on both, then check
   inside each bag.)

## Exit check

The learner picks the better of two tested covers, gives the evidence
(Teddy's dry / a little wet / very wet result), and names one
improvement. The adult records all three.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** "Dry / a little wet / very wet" is shown
  by touching Teddy's fur and the towel, not by numbers. Home-language
  comparison words are welcomed.
- **Alternate response mode:** Pointing to the drier cover, or placing
  the dry/wet markers, counts fully; the adult scribes the reasoning.
- **Extension:** "What if it rained twice as long? Would the winner still
  win?" (Maybe not — cardboard would soak more; plastic might still win
  but could slip. A longer rain is a new test, and engineers test again.)
- **Table and floor variants:** Seated over the tub on a table, or on the
  floor on towels; both work, as long as the adult pours.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "Better means perfect." Redirect: "The sloped
plastic cover won, but it still needed tape to stay up. Better means
drier than the other cover — and still improvable."

**Common misconception:** "One test proves it forever." Redirect: "One
test tells us about *this* rain, *this* time. That is why engineers test
again after every change — and why the project re-tests the final cover
three times."

**Safety note:** The adult pours; keep Teddy's test over the tub. Dry the
floor at once.

**Answer-key reference:** [`../answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md#lesson-06) —
flat cardboard: a little wet (soaks through); sloped plastic: dry
(winner); fair comparison requires the same rain; improvements must
change one thing and be re-tested.
