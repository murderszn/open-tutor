# Lesson 02 — Rough, smooth, bendy, stiff

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you learn four feeling words:
**rough**, **smooth**, **bendy**, and **stiff**. You will close your
eyes, feel a material, and tell which word fits it."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** the real materials kit (wooden
block, red cloth, sponge, foil sheet, plastic bag, white paper, paper
towel, cotton balls), a cloth bag or pillowcase for the mystery-feel
game.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Rub your hands together fast. That bumpy feeling is close to **rough**.
Now rub the table. That flat, easy feeling is close to **smooth**."
The adult says each word as the learner feels it: body first, word
second.

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *A **property** is something you can notice
about a material — how it feels, how it bends, what it does. **Rough**
feels bumpy. **Smooth** feels flat and easy. **Bendy** folds and bends
without breaking. **Stiff** stays straight and does not bend easily.
The same material can be rough in one place and smooth in another — you
have to test it, not guess it.*

**Modeled example 1 — bendy vs. stiff.** Adult bends the red cloth: "I
bend it, and it folds easily. It does not snap. That is **bendy**." Then
the adult tries to bend the wooden block: "I push and push, and it stays
straight. That is **stiff**." (Modeled reasoning: try the bend, say what
happened, name the word.)

**Modeled example 2 — rough vs. smooth.** Adult rubs the sponge: "It
feels bumpy, full of little holes. That is **rough**." Then rubs the
foil sheet flat: "It feels flat and slippery under my hand. That is
**smooth**."

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided mystery feel:** The adult hides one kit item in the cloth bag.
   The learner reaches in (no peeking), feels it, and says "rough or
   smooth? bendy or stiff?" The adult confirms with the revealed object.
   Repeat for two items.

## Independent practice

2. **Independent sorting:** "Put all the **bendy** things in one pile
   and all the **stiff** things in another." (Bendy: cloth, paper,
   paper towel, plastic bag, foil. Stiff: wooden block, cardboard.)
   Accept the sponge as either with a reason — it squashes but springs
   back; the adult notes the learner's reasoning.
3. **Independent describing:** "Pick one thing. Tell me two properties:
   rough or smooth, bendy or stiff." Repeat for two objects.
4. **Independent oral:** "Is the paper towel bendy or stiff? How do you
   know?" (Bendy — it folds easily.) "Is the block rough or smooth?
   How do you know?" (Smooth on its flat side — it feels flat; accept
   "a little rough" for an unfinished edge, with the reason.)
5. **Error-analysis (guided):** The adult bends the paper and says, "This
   paper is stiff — it will not bend!" The learner corrects by bending
   it: "No — look, it bends. It is **bendy**."

## Application

6. **Choose-for-a-job:** "We need something to wrap a cold sandwich so
   it stays covered. Should it be bendy or stiff?" (Bendy — it must fold
   around the sandwich. Accept foil or plastic bag, with the reason
   "it bends around.")

## Exit check

The learner feels one unseen item and names two correct properties
(rough/smooth and bendy/stiff), or describes the same two properties for
two seen items. The adult records the words used.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** Property words are always paired with the
  hand motion (rub for rough/smooth, bend for bendy/stiff). Home-language
  feeling words are welcomed.
- **Alternate response mode:** Sorting into piles, nodding, or acting out
  the bend counts fully; the adult scribes.
- **Extension:** "Which is heavier to hold — the block or the cotton
  balls? Is heavy a property we can feel?" (Yes — heavy/light is another
  property. Hold both, one in each hand.)
- **Table and floor variants:** Seated with the kit in trays, or on the
  floor with the mystery bag; both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "Soft means bendy and hard means stiff."
Redirect: "A sponge is soft and squashes, but it is not quite bendy like
cloth — it springs back. Test it: bend it, squash it, and describe what
actually happened."

**Common misconception:** "One word is enough." Redirect: "The foil is
smooth AND bendy. Materials usually have more than one property — that
is why we test two."

**Answer-key reference:** [`../answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md#lesson-02) —
bendy: cloth, paper, paper towel, plastic bag, foil; stiff: wooden block,
cardboard; sponge: squashy, springs back (either pile accepted with a
reason).
