# Lesson 05 — Draw it, then build it

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you draw your cover *before* you
build it. Engineers draw a **sketch** — a quick drawing that shows the
**shape** and how the shape helps. A roof shape helps because water
runs off it."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** paper and crayons, the toy teddy,
cardboard, a plastic bag, tape, the Lesson 04 "must do" list (adult-scribed).

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Point to the roof shape in the Lesson 01 picture." (The cardboard roof
over Teddy.) "Why is it pointy on top instead of flat?" Take any idea,
then: "Today your drawing must show a shape that helps."

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *A **sketch** is a quick drawing of your idea.
It does not have to be pretty. It has to show the **shape** — and the
shape has to help solve the problem. A flat roof lets water sit and soak
in. A sloped roof lets water run off the sides, away from Teddy. A bag
wrapped tight around Teddy keeps water out, but Teddy cannot sit under
it like a roof. The shape is part of the design, not decoration.*

**Modeled example 1 — a sloped roof sketch.** Adult draws a quick
triangle roof over a teddy shape, with arrows running down the slopes
(adult draws the arrows): "I **notice** my roof is high in the middle
and low at the sides. Rain lands on top and runs *down and away*. The
shape helps because water runs off instead of sitting." (Modeled
reasoning: name the shape, say what water does on it, say why it helps.)

**Modeled example 2 — a flat roof sketch.** Adult draws a flat line over
a teddy shape with a puddle blob on top: "This roof is flat. Rain lands
and *sits* in a puddle. The puddle soaks through. The shape does not
help — it hurts. So I would change the shape, not just the material."

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** The learner draws (adult scribes labels if wanted) a cover
   for Teddy. The adult asks only: "Show me where the water goes." The
   learner traces the water's path off the drawing. If the drawing has no
   path, the adult asks: "Where will the rain run? Draw it running off."

## Independent practice

2. **Independent sketching:** "Draw a second cover with a *different*
   shape — not a roof. Maybe a tent, a box, or a bag-house." The learner
   draws it and says how its shape helps (tent: slopes shed water; box:
   walls block sideways rain; bag-house: wraps and keeps water out).
3. **Independent choosing:** "Which of your two sketches would you build
   first? Why?" (Any reason tied to shape or to a Lesson 03 test result
   counts — for example, "the roof, because water runs off" or "the
   bag-house, because plastic kept water out.")
4. **Independent building:** Build the chosen sketch quickly from
   cardboard and/or the plastic bag, with tape. The adult helps with
   cutting and taping; the learner places the pieces. Compare the build
   to the sketch: "Does it look like your drawing? What is different?"
5. **Error-analysis (guided):** The adult shows a pretty drawing of Teddy
   with flowers and a sun and says, "This is a great cover sketch!" The
   learner corrects: "No — it does not show a cover or a shape. A sketch
   must show **how the cover works**."

## Application

6. **Shape hunt:** "Find one thing in this room whose shape helps it do
   its job." (A cup's hollow shape holds water; a shoe's sole shape
   grips; an umbrella's dome sheds rain — an umbrella is the best
   answer if one is handy.)

## Exit check

The learner shows a sketch, names its shape, and says how the shape
helps keep Teddy dry. The adult records the shape and the reason.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** Sketching is drawing, not writing; labels
  are adult-scribed on request. Home-language shape words are welcomed.
- **Alternate response mode:** Building the model without a drawn sketch
  counts as the model half of the standard — the learner then points to
  the shape on the built model and says how it helps.
- **Extension:** "Draw the same cover for a *taller* toy. What changes
   about the shape?" (Taller walls or a higher roof; the slope idea stays
   the same.)
- **Table and floor variants:** Seated sketching at a table, or floor
  building with big cardboard pieces; both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "A sketch must be pretty." Redirect: "Engineers
sketch fast and messy. The only question is: can someone else see the
shape and where the water goes?"

**Common misconception:** "Material is all that matters." Redirect:
"Lesson 03's foil kept water out flat — but a flat foil sheet over Teddy
with no slope and open sides still lets rain in from the sides. Shape
and material work together."

**Answer-key reference:** [`../answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md#lesson-05) —
sloped roof: water runs off; flat roof: water sits and soaks; acceptable
second shapes: tent, box, bag-house, dome; sketch must show the cover
shape and the water's path.
