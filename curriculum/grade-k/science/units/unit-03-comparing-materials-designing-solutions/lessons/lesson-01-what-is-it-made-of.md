# Lesson 01 — What is it made of?

**Goal (read to the learner):** "Today you learn a science word:
**material**. A **material** is what something is made of. Wood is a
material. Cloth is a material. You will look at a picture and tell what
each thing is made of."

**Time:** 15–20 minutes. **Materials:** the illustration
[`../assets/materials-sort-scene.png`](../assets/materials-sort-scene.png),
the real materials kit (wooden block, red cloth, sponge, foil sheet,
plastic bag, white paper) for handling after the picture work.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Touch your shirt. What is it made of?" Accept "cloth" or "soft stuff."
"Touch the table. What is it made of?" Accept "wood" or "hard stuff."
The adult names the pattern: "Everything is made of *something*. That
something is its **material**."

## Teach and model

Explain in your own words: *A **material** is what a thing is made of.
A block can be made of wood. A bag can be made of plastic. Two things
can look alike but be made of different materials — and two things made
of the same material can look different. To know the material, you look
closely and you touch.*

**Modeled example 1 — wood in the picture.** Adult points to the left
tray: "I **notice** a brown block with lines in it. It looks hard, and it
feels hard when I tap it. Blocks like this are made of **wood**. The
material is wood." (Modeled reasoning: name what is seen, say how it
feels, name the material.)

**Modeled example 2 — plastic in the picture.** Adult points to the
right tray: "I **notice** a see-through bag. I can see through it, and it
is smooth and a little crinkly. Bags like this are made of **plastic**.
The material is plastic."

## Guided practice (adult-guided)

1. **Guided:** "Show me the **cloth** in the picture." (Points to the
   folded red cloth.) "Show me the **sponge**." (Points to the yellow
   sponge.) "Show me the **paper**." (Points to the white sheet.)
   Prompt with "What does it feel like?" if the learner is stuck.

## Independent practice

2. **Independent pointing:** "Find the **foil** in the picture. What
   color is it? Is it shiny or dull?" (Shiny silver foil sheet.)
3. **Independent sorting:** "The child in the picture sorted things into
   two trays. Point to each tray and tell me one material in it." (Left:
   wood, cloth, sponge. Right: foil, plastic, paper.)
4. **Independent real-object transfer:** With the real kit on the table —
   "Pick up one thing. Tell me what it is made of." Repeat for three
   objects; the adult scribes the learner's words.
5. **Error-analysis (guided):** The adult holds up the sponge and says,
   "This sponge is made of wood!" The learner corrects: "No — it is soft
   and squishy. It is a **sponge**." Celebrate the correction.

## Application

6. **Room hunt:** "Find one thing in this room made of **wood** and one
   thing made of **cloth**." (Wood: a chair leg, a shelf, a block. Cloth:
   a shirt, a curtain, a towel.) The learner points or brings the object.

## Exit check

The learner names the material of two pictured objects and one real
object, without the adult naming it first. The adult records the three
materials.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** Material words are taught by touching the
  real object first, the word second. Home-language material words are
  welcomed alongside the English words.
- **Alternate response mode:** Pointing or holding up the object counts
  fully; the adult scribes the learner's words. A learner may answer
  entirely by showing.
- **Extension:** "Can one thing be made of *two* materials?" (A shoe:
  cloth and rubber. A book: paper and cardboard.) "What is your shirt
  made of — and what are its buttons made of?"
- **Table and floor variants:** Seated at a table with the printed picture
  and the kit in trays, or on the floor with the kit spread on a blanket;
  both work.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** "Material means color." Redirect: "Red things
can be cloth, plastic, or paper. Color tells us what it *looks* like;
touching tells us what it is *made of*." Lesson 02 builds the touch
vocabulary (rough, smooth, bendy, stiff).

**Common misconception:** "The tray is the material." Redirect: point to
the tray, then to what is *in* it — "The tray holds the materials. The
block is wood; the tray is something else."
