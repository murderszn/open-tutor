# Unit 04 — Blending, Segmenting, and Regular CVC Words

**Grade:** Kindergarten · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #8](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/8), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

In Unit 03 the learner finished the alphabet and attached a sound to
every letter. Now those sounds start working together. This unit
teaches the first real reading move: hearing three separate sounds and
squishing them into one word — /c/ /a/ /t/ becomes *cat* — and the
reverse move: hearing *cat* and pulling it apart into /c/ /a/ /t/.
By the end, the learner can blend sounds to read regular
consonant-vowel-consonant (CVC) words, take CVC words apart sound by
sound, change one sound to make a new word, read four high-frequency
words by sight, tell look-alike words apart by the letter that differs,
and spell simple CVC words the way they sound. That is the bridge Unit
05 will walk across into shared reading and retelling.

## Essential question

How do three sounds join to make a word — and how can taking a word
apart help us read it, change it, and write it?

## Prerequisites

- Names all 26 uppercase and lowercase letters and says the primary
  sound for each consonant and the short sound for each vowel
  (Unit 03)
- Can hear rhymes and syllables and blend spoken word parts (Unit 02:
  "pen-cil" → *pencil*; /c/ + /at/ → *cat*)
- Follows talk-circle rules: listen, take turns, stay on the topic
  (Units 01–02)
- Holds a crayon or pencil and writes letters for most consonant and
  short-vowel sounds (Unit 03)

No sentence reading is required at the start of this unit. The adult
reads every instruction and models every sound; the learner's job is
listening, blending, segmenting, pointing, moving chips or tiles, and
writing single words.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed;
all responses may be oral, pointing, or drawing, adult-scribed, except
for the spelling tasks in objective 6, which the learner attempts
independently with a crayon or pencil):

1. Blend three spoken sounds into a regular CVC word (for example,
   /m/ /a/ /t/ → *mat*, /p/ /i/ /g/ → *pig*), reading at least 8 of
   10 words the adult sounds out, with no final /l/, /r/, or /x/
   sounds in the test words.
2. Isolate and say the first, middle-vowel, and last sounds of a
   spoken CVC word (for example, *dog* → /d/ … /o/ … /g/), getting
   at least 5 of 6 asked positions right.
3. Segment a spoken CVC word into its three sounds, in order, moving
   one chip or tile per sound (for example, *sun* → /s/ /u/ /n/,
   three chips), for at least 5 of 6 words.
4. Change exactly one sound in a spoken CVC word to make a new real
   word (for example, *cat* → *cap* by changing the last sound,
   *pin* → *pan* by changing the middle sound), completing at least
   4 of 5 chains correctly.
5. Read the high-frequency words *the, of, to, you* by sight, and
   distinguish similarly spelled CVC words by naming the letter
   whose sound differs (for example, *cat* and *cap* differ in the
   last letter; *pin* and *pan* differ in the middle letter), for at
   least 4 of 5 pairs.
6. Spell simple CVC words phonetically, writing one letter per sound
   in order (for example, hearing *mop* and writing M O P), for at
   least 4 of 5 dictated words, with recognizable letters (reversals
   of b/d still count if the letter order is right).

**Sound notes the adult must keep straight (see the teacher guide for
detail):** every CVC word in this unit has exactly three sounds and
three letters, a short vowel in the middle, and no letter that changes
its sound — no silent *e*, no digraphs (*sh, ch, th*), no *r*-controlled
vowels, and no words ending in /l/, /r/, or /x/ (so *ball, car, box*
are out, even though the learner may know them). The four sight words
are deliberately **not** sounded out: *the* uses *th*, *of* is
pronounced /uv/, *to* has an *o* that says /oo/, and *you* starts
with /y/ + /oo/ — the learner memorizes these four by sight and the
adult says so plainly. Short vowels only in CVC words: A says /a/ as
in *apple*, E says /e/ as in *egg*, I says /i/ as in *igloo*, O says
/o/ as in *octopus*, U says /u/ as in *umbrella*.

Weeks 15–17 introduce and practice; Week 18 (track week 18) is the
midyear-review week in the scope-and-sequence, and this unit's Session

highest-need objective, and the formative check of Units 01–04. If
the learner is not yet secure on an objective after Session 16, use

share one session by design — L4 teaches change-one-sound chains the
learner already partly knows from rhyming word families, and L5 adds
only four sight-word cards and one look-alike sort; the full sight-word
and look-alike work lands in Session 14's project pages and Session

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, or drawing responses; the adult
scribes dictated words. Sound boxes and chips make every sound visible
and countable without print; letter tiles are large print, high
contrast, and color is never the only cue (position — first, middle,
last — is always spoken as well as shown). Each lesson includes a
seated-table and a floor-play variant, a language-access support, an
alternate response mode, and an extension. The generated illustration
ships with a text-only alternative containing every picture name and
sound the activity needs. Mirror work and mouth-shape cues supplement
auditory discrimination for every sound lesson. Spelling attempts are
judged on sound order, not handwriting perfection; reversals are
noted as watch items, not failures.
