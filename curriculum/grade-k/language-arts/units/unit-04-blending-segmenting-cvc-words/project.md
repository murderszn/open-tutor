# Project — My CVC Flip Book

**Type:** creation project across Sessions 11, 14, and 15.
**Driving question:** "Can I build a book that makes new words when
I flip a sound — and read it to someone else?"

The learner authors a flip book: five word-family pages, each with a
flippable first letter (or last letter, on one page) that changes one
sound to make a new word. Each page shows the word family, the words
the learner built, a drawn picture for one word, and a spelled word
written by the learner. A final sight-word page shows *the, of, to,
you* in short phrases with CVC words. The book is then read aloud to
an audience (adult, family member, or stuffed-animal audience voiced
by the adult) and shared in a talk circle.

## Materials

8–10 large sheets of paper or card, crayons and markers, a stapler or
binder clips (adult use), letter tiles or cards for flipping, the
generated illustration
[`assets/cvc-blending-pictures.png`](assets/cvc-blending-pictures.png)
as a model for the animal pages, the talking stick.

## Part 1 — Word-family pages (Session 11)

1. Make three family pages: **-at**, **-an**, **-ig**.
   - On each page, write the family ending big at the right (AT,
     AN, IG — the adult writes; the learner traces or copies).
   - Attach a small flap or a loose tile at the left that the
     learner can swap: for -at, the flippable letters are C, H, M,
     B, S, R (cat, hat, mat, bat, sat, rat); for -an, F, P, C, V, M
     (fan, pan, can, van, man); for -ig, P, B, F, D, W (pig, big,
     fig, dig, wig).
2. For each page, the learner flips through at least three first
   letters, blends each new word, and the adult scribes the words
   built on the page (at least three per page).
3. The learner draws one picture per page: a cat for -at, a fan for
   -an, a pig for -ig (the pig may copy the generated illustration's
   pink pig).
4. The learner writes one word per page independently (phonetic
   spelling counts; the adult notes the sound order, not the
   handwriting): suggested words — *cat*, *fan*, *pig*.

## Part 2 — More families, sight-word page, cover (Session 14)

1. Add two more family pages: **-op** (flippable M, T, H, P: mop,
   top, hop, pop) and **-un** (flippable S, B, R, F: sun, bun, run,
   fun), following the same steps as Part 1 (at least three words
   scribed per page, one drawn picture per page, one word written
   by the learner per page — suggested: *mop*, *sun*).
2. Add a sight-word page: the four sight-word cards (the, of, to,
   you) pasted or written big, plus three short sentences the adult
   writes and the learner reads (sight words by sight, CVC words by
   blending): "The cat sat." "The pig ran." "The dog sat."
3. Make the front cover: title "My CVC Flip Book," the learner's
   first-name initial (or a drawn self-portrait — no full names or
   photos in the shared book), and a favorite picture from the
   generated illustration (cat, pig, or sun).
4. Make the back cover: the learner's hardest word family, drawn big
   with its words — "I am still learning -ig: pig, big, fig" is a
   proud back cover, not a confession.
5. Bind the book (adult staples or clips); the learner puts the
   pages in order, front to back: cover, -at, -an, -ig, -op, -un,
   sight-word page, back cover.

## Part 3 — Share (Session 15, with the formative quiz)

Talk-circle share: the learner presents the book (holds it correctly,
flips one family to build two new words aloud, reads the sight-word
page's three sentences, and shows the back-cover family), then takes
two questions from the audience (adult, family member, or
stuffed-animal "audience" voiced by the adult), following talk rules.
The adult scribes one audience question and the learner's answer onto
the back cover as a souvenir.

## Deliverables

1. The flip book (cover, five family pages with at least three
   scribed words each, one drawn picture and one learner-written
   word per family page, sight-word page with three sentences, back
   cover).
2. A flip-and-read demonstration (two new words built live by
   flipping).
3. A talk-circle share with two answered questions.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (3) | Developing (2) | Starting (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Word building | Builds 3+ real words on each of the five family pages by changing exactly one sound | Builds 3+ words on most pages, or one page has a two-sound change or a non-word | Builds fewer than 3 words per page, or changes are random |
| Reading the built words | Blends and reads 8+ of the 10 words the adult asks for from the book | Reads 5–7 words, with prompting on the rest | Reads fewer than 5, or guesses from pictures without blending |
| Spelling | Writes one word per family page with all three sounds in order (5/5 pages) | Writes 3–4 pages' words with all sounds in order | Writes fewer than 3 words with all sounds in order, or writes first letters only |
| Sight-word page | Reads all four sight words mixed up and reads the three sentences' CVC words by blending | Reads 2–3 sight words, or needs help on one sentence | Reads fewer than 2 sight words, or guesses sentences from pictures |
| Sharing | Flips to build two new words live, reads the sight-word page, answers two questions, follows talk rules | Presents with heavy prompting or answers one question | Cannot present without the adult reading for them |

**Scoring:** 13–15 = ready for Unit 05; 9–12 = ready with a
Session 12-style re-teach of the weakest criterion; 5–8 = repeat the
project's weakest pages in a flexible week before Unit 05. The rubric
is observational, not a grade; the adult records evidence, not
scores, in private storage.
