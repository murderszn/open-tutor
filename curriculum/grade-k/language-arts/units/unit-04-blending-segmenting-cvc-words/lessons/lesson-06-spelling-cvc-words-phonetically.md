# Lesson 06 — Spelling CVC Words the Way They Sound

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can hear the sounds in a word and write
a letter for each sound, in order."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** paper, pencil or crayon,
letter tiles (for checking), three sound boxes.
**Standards:** L.K.2.d (spell simple words phonetically, drawing on
knowledge of sound-letter relationships); RF.K.3.a.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Segment *mop* with chips: /m/ /o/ /p/. Say: "Three sounds, three
chips. Today each chip becomes a letter. That is spelling: sounds
turned into letters, in the same order you hear them."

## Explicit teaching

**Phonetic spelling** means writing what you hear: one letter for
each sound, in order. It has the same three steps as segmenting,
plus one:

1. **Say the word slowly** and stretch it: /m/…/o/…/p/.
2. **Say each sound** and find its letter: /m/ → M, /o/ → O,
   /p/ → P. (Unit 03 taught every letter–sound pair you need.)
3. **Write the letters in order**, left to right: M O P.
4. **Read it back** by blending: /m/ /o/ /p/ → *mop*. If the
   read-back word matches the word you heard, the spelling worked.

Three promises for kindergarten spelling:

- **Sound order matters more than perfect handwriting.** A wobbly
  M in the right place counts; a beautiful P in the wrong place does
  not.
- **Reversals are watch items, not failures.** Writing d for b
  (or b for d) is normal at five; note it, model the right shape,
  and keep going.
- **Every sound gets a letter.** A spelling with a missing middle
  vowel (*mp* for *mop*) is the most common miss — stretch the
  middle sound longest and write it second.

### Modeled example 1 — spelling *mop* from dictation

*Adult thinks aloud, writing slowly:*

"The word is *mop*. Slowly: /m/…/o/…/p/. First sound /m/ — the
letter M." *(Writes M.)* "Middle sound /o/ — the letter O."
*(Writes O.)* "Last sound /p/ — the letter P." *(Writes P.)*
"Read it back: /m/ /o/ /p/… *mop*. It matches! M O P spells
*mop*."

### Modeled example 2 — spelling *bed*, with a self-check

*Adult thinks aloud:*

"The word is *bed*. Slowly: /b/…/e/…/d/. First sound /b/ — B.
Middle sound /e/ — E. Last sound /d/ — D. B E D. Read it back:
/b/ /e/ /d/… *bed*. Matches!"

"Now check a wrong one: suppose I wrote B D. Read it back: /b/
 /d/… *bd* — that is not a word, and the middle sound is missing.
Fix it: put E in the middle. B E D. Always read back — your ears
catch what your eyes miss."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Spell *cat* from dictation with the adult saying each sound:
   C A T.
2. Spell *sun* from dictation: S U N.
3. Spell *pig* from dictation, the learner saying the sounds first:
   P I G.
4. The adult writes *ht* and asks: "I tried to spell *hat*. What is
   wrong, and what fixes it?" (The middle vowel A is missing;
   H A T.)
5. The adult writes *mpo* and asks: "I tried to spell *mop*. What
   is wrong?" (The letters are out of order — O and P swapped;
   M O P.)

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

The learner writes alone; the adult observes and notes sound order.

1. Spell *dog* from dictation: D O G.
2. Spell *pen* from dictation: P E N.
3. Spell *cup* from dictation: C U P.
4. Spell *sit* from dictation: S I T.
5. Spell *bug* from dictation: B U G. Then read it back by blending.

## Application

"Word shop." The learner runs a pretend shop: the adult "buys" a word
("I would like a *hat*, please"); the learner writes the word on a
paper label, reads it back, and hands it over. Three labels: hat,
mop, sun. The adult scribes any sound the learner asks for help
with, and notes which sounds the learner found alone.

## Exit check

1. Spell *cap* from dictation: C A P.
2. Spell *red* from dictation: R E D.
3. "What do you do after you write a word?" (Read it back by
   blending, to check it matches.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** dictate words the learner already knows and
  likes (a pet's name pattern, a favorite food word from the bank);
  accept home-language CVC-like words as extra dictation.
- **Alternate response mode:** build the word with letter tiles
  instead of writing it; the adult scribes the tile order.
- **Seated-table variant:** sound boxes on paper; one letter per
  box, left to right.
- **Floor-play variant:** the learner writes each letter on a
  separate card and lays the cards in order on the floor.
- **Extension:** the learner writes a two-word label with a sight
  word: "the cat" (sight word *the* + spelled CVC *cat*), with the
  adult modeling *the*.

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Writing the first letter only:* *m* for *mop* means segmenting
  stopped early. Return to chips: three sounds, three letters, no
  exceptions in this unit.
- *Missing middle vowel:* *mp* for *mop* is the classic miss. The
  fix is always the same: stretch the middle sound and write it
  second.
- *Letters in sound order but reversed shapes:* b/d or p/q swaps
  are developmental. Record them as watch items (Unit 03's tricky
  pairs) and model the belly-and-stick rule again; do not mark the
  spelling wrong for shape alone if the order is right.
