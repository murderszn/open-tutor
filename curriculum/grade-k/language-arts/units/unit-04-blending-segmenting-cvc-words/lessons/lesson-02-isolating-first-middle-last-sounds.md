# Lesson 02 — First, Middle, and Last Sounds

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can hear the first sound, the middle
sound, and the last sound in a word."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** picture cards (dog, bed,
cup, hen, mop), three chips, a small mirror.
**Standards:** RF.K.2.d (isolate initial, medial-vowel, and final
sounds in CVC words; no /l/, /r/, or /x/ endings).

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Blend one word together: /d/ /o/ /g/ → *dog*. Then say: "We squished
three sounds into *dog*. Today we do the detective work: which sound
is first, which is in the middle, and which is last?"

## Explicit teaching

Every CVC word has three jobs, in order:

1. **First sound** — the sound the word *starts* with. In *dog*, the
   first sound is /d/. Your mouth starts closed-ish and pops open.
2. **Middle sound** — the vowel, the belly of the word. In *dog*,
   the middle sound is /o/. It is the loudest sound, and your mouth
   is open widest for it.
3. **Last sound** — the sound the word *ends* with. In *dog*, the
   last sound is /g/. It is the sound you hear just before you stop
   talking.

A good detective question for each job: "What sound do I hear
*first*?" (Say the word slowly and catch the very first noise.)
"What sound is in the *middle*?" (Stretch the word and listen for
the loud vowel.) "What sound do I hear *last*?" (Say the word and
notice what your mouth does at the very end.)

Watch the mirror: /d/ starts with the tongue behind the teeth; /o/
rounds the lips; /g/ lifts the back of the tongue. Mouth shapes are
clues, not the answer — the ear decides.

### Modeled example 1 — taking *bed* apart by position

*Adult thinks aloud, holding up one finger at a time:*

"Say *bed* slowly: /b/…/e/…/d/. First sound: /b/ — my lips pop.
Middle sound: /e/ — the loud vowel, like *egg*. Last sound: /d/ —
my tongue taps behind my teeth. So: first /b/, middle /e/,
last /d/."

"Now you check me: I will say a sound — /e/ — and you tell me
where it lives in *bed*." *(Middle.)*

### Modeled example 2 — taking *cup* apart by position

*Adult thinks aloud:*

"*Cup*: /c/…/u/…/p/. First: /c/ — a little cough in the back of
my throat. Middle: /u/ — the *umbrella* sound, mouth open. Last:
/p/ — my lips pop shut. First /c/, middle /u/, last /p/."

"One trap: *cup* ends with /p/, not /b/. Say them both: *cup*,
*cub*. Feel the difference? /p/ is a quiet pop; /b/ buzzes. In
*cup* the pop is quiet, so the last sound is /p/."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. *Dog*: first sound? (/d/.) Middle sound? (/o/.) Last sound? (/g/.)
2. *Hen*: the adult asks for the middle sound only. (/e/.)
3. *Mop*: the adult asks for the first and last sounds. (/m/, /p/.)
4. The adult says a sound; the learner says where it lives in *pin*:
   /p/ (first), /i/ (middle), /n/ (last).
5. Odd-one-out by first sound: *sun, sit, mop* — which word starts
   with a different sound? (*Mop* — /m/ — while *sun* and *sit*
   start with /s/.)

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

1. *Hat*: first sound? (/h/.)
2. *Pig*: middle sound? (/i/.)
3. *Ten*: last sound? (/n/.)
4. *Bug*: first, middle, and last sounds, in order. (/b/, /u/, /g/.)
5. *Red*: the adult asks: "Which sound is /e/ in *red* — first,
   middle, or last?" (Middle.)

## Application

"Sound detective badge." The adult names a mystery sound (for
example, /o/); the learner hunts through five picture cards (dog,
mop, cat, pig, sun) and collects every card whose word has /o/ in
it — anywhere: *dog* (middle), *mop* (middle). The learner earns the
badge by saying each word's /o/ position aloud. (Only *dog* and
*mop* qualify; *cat* has /a/, *pig* has /i/, *sun* has /u/.)

## Exit check

1. *Fan*: middle sound? (/a/.)
2. *Jet*: last sound? (/t/.)
3. *Kit*: first sound? (/k/.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** use the learner's own name sounds where
  possible ("Your name starts with /m/ — like *mop*!") and accept
  home-language example words for position practice.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to chip 1, 2, or 3 (first,
  middle, last) instead of saying the sound; the adult says it and
  the learner confirms.
- **Seated-table variant:** chips in three boxes; the learner taps
  the box for the asked-for position.
- **Floor-play variant:** three floor spots labeled first, middle,
  last; the learner stands on the spot for the asked-for sound.
- **Extension:** the learner gives the adult a word and asks for a
  position ("What is the last sound in *rug*?" — /g/).

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Naming the letter instead of the sound:* "The first sound in
  *dog* is D" (the letter name "dee") is not /d/. Answer with the
  sound: "The sound is /d/ — the pop, not the name."
- *Middle sound confused with the whole word:* a learner who answers
  "dog" for the middle sound is repeating, not isolating. Stretch
  only the vowel: "/doooog/ — hear the loud /o/ in the middle?"
- *Last sound guessed from spelling habits:* *cup* → /b/ is a voicing
  mix-up, not carelessness. Contrast the pair (*cup*/*cub*) aloud
  and let the quiet pop decide.
