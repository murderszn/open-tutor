# Lesson 01 — Blend Sounds to Read CVC Words

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can hear three sounds and squish them
together into a word."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** lowercase letter tiles or
cards (c, a, t, d, o, g, p, i, s, u, n), three small chips, the
talking stick (optional).
**Standards:** RF.K.2.c (onset-rime blending, extended here to
three sounds); RF.K.3.a (letter–sound correspondences).

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Say a word in two parts, the way Unit 02 did: "/c/ … /at/." Ask:
"What word is that?" (cat.) Then "/d/ … /og/." (dog.) Say: "You
already squish two parts into a word. Today we squish three sounds —
one sound at a time."

## Explicit teaching

A **sound** is one small noise in a word. A **CVC word** is a word
with three sounds: a **consonant** sound, a **vowel** sound, and a
**consonant** sound. *Cat* is a CVC word: /c/ (consonant), /a/
(vowel), /t/ (consonant). Three sounds, three letters.

**Blending** means saying the sounds one at a time, then saying them
faster and faster until they squish into one word. The trick is to
never stop between the sounds for long — stretch them like a rubber
band, then let go: "/c/…/a/…/t/… cat!"

Blending has three steps, every time:

1. **Hear** each sound: the adult says them slowly, one chip per
   sound, left to right.
2. **Stretch** them: say them again, a little faster, sliding from
   one sound into the next.
3. **Say the word:** say the whole word at normal speed, like you
   would in a sentence.

The vowel in the middle is the loudest, longest sound — it is the
belly of the word. If you lose the word, listen for the middle sound
first: /a/ in *cat*, /o/ in *dog*, /i/ in *pig*, /u/ in *sun*.

### Modeled example 1 — blending /c/ /a/ /t/ into *cat*

*Adult lays out three chips, left to right, and thinks aloud:*

"Three chips, three sounds. First chip: /c/. Second chip: /a/.
Third chip: /t/. Now stretch: /c/…/a/…/t/… Now fast: /caaat/…
*cat!* The word is *cat*." *(Learner echoes the stretch and the word.)*

"Now with letters." *(Adult swaps each chip for its tile: C, A, T.)*
"C says /c/. A says /a/. T says /t/. Blend: /c/ /a/ /t/… *cat*."

### Modeled example 2 — blending /d/ /o/ /g/ into *dog*

*Adult lays out three new chips and thinks aloud:*

"First sound: /d/. Middle sound — the belly: /o/. Last sound: /g/.
Stretch: /d/…/o/…/g/… Fast: *dog!*"

"Check it: does *dog* have three sounds? Count them on your fingers:
 /d/ (one), /o/ (two), /g/ (three). Yes — a CVC word. If I said
/d/ /o/ /g/ slowly and you heard *dog*, you blended."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Blend with the adult, chips first, then tiles: /p/ /i/ /g/ → *pig*.
2. Blend with the adult: /s/ /u/ /n/ → *sun*.
3. Blend with the adult, the learner pushing one chip per sound:
   /m/ /o/ /p/ → *mop*.
4. The adult says the sounds slowly; the learner says the word alone:
   /h/ /e/ /n/ → *hen*; /b/ /e/ /d/ → *bed*.
5. The learner picks any three tiles that make a real word from the
   unit word bank, lays them in order, and blends them for the adult
   (for example, H A T → *hat*).

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

The learner works with the chips and tiles visible; the adult watches
and notes which blends are secure.

1. Blend: /h/ /a/ /t/ → *hat*.
2. Blend: /f/ /a/ /n/ → *fan*.
3. Blend: /t/ /e/ /n/ → *ten*.
4. Blend: /l/ /o/ /g/ → *log*.
5. Blend: /b/ /u/ /g/ → *bug*.

## Application

"Word squisher." The adult whispers three sounds for a word from the
word bank (for example, /r/ /u/ /n/); the learner squishes them and
acts the word out (running in place for *run*), then tells the adult
one thing about it ("Running is fast!"). The adult scribes the word
the learner blended.

## Exit check

1. Blend: /c/ /a/ /p/ → *cap*.
2. Blend: /s/ /i/ /t/ → *sit*.
3. "How many sounds are in *cat*?" (Three: /c/ /a/ /t/.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** pair each blended word with a picture or an
  action (act out *run*, point to a picture for *pig*). Home-language
  words with three clear sounds are welcome as extra blends.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the picture of the blended
  word instead of saying it; the adult says the word and the learner
  confirms with a nod or thumbs-up.
- **Seated-table variant:** all work at the table; chips move into
  three drawn boxes.
- **Floor-play variant:** "sound hop" — three floor spots, one per
  sound; the learner hops spot to spot saying each sound, then jumps
  forward saying the whole word.
- **Extension:** the learner blends a word, then names a rhyming word
  from the same family (*cat* → *hat*; *pig* → *big*).

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Saying the letter names instead of the sounds:* "C-A-T" spelled
  out is not blending. If you hear "cee-ay-tee," restart with the
  sounds: "The sounds are /c/ /a/ /t/ — squish the sounds."
- *Dropping the middle vowel:* *cat* blended as "ct" means the vowel
  got lost. Stretch the middle sound longest: "/c/…/aaaa/…/t/."
- *Blending only the first two sounds:* "ca…" and stopping means the
  learner needs the third chip pushed before the word is said. Keep
  all three chips down until the whole word is spoken.
