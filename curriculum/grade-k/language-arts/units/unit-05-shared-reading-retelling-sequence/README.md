# Unit 05 — Shared Reading, Retelling, and Sequence

**Grade:** Kindergarten · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 19–22
**Backlog:** [issue #8](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/8), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

In Units 01–04 the learner learned how books work, how sounds work, and
how letters and sounds join into words. Now the learner uses all of
that to do what readers do: listen to a story, hold it in mind, and
tell it back in order, with the parts that matter. This unit is shared
reading — the adult reads aloud, the learner listens, looks, talks,
and retells. No decoding is required of the learner; the reading work
here is listening, remembering, ordering, and telling.

By the end, the learner can hear a short story twice and retell it
beginning, middle, and end, naming who was in it, where it happened,
and what happened in order; can say what a picture shows about the
story; can name the author and the illustrator and what each one
does; and can compare what happened to the characters in two
familiar stories. The unit's stories are original, written for this
unit: *Milo and the Lost Mitten*, *Ana Plants a Seed*, and *Sam's
Rainy-Day Picnic*. A fourth short original text, *How Seeds Grow*,
supplies information the learner recalls to answer a question.

## Essential question

How can I listen to a story, remember what mattered most, and tell it
back so someone else can follow it from beginning to end?

## Prerequisites

- Follows talk-circle rules: listen, take turns, stay on the topic
  (Units 01–02)
- Listens to a 10-minute read-aloud and answers simple questions
  about it (Units 01–02)
- Names letters and says letter sounds (Unit 03) — used only to read
  the word cards *first*, *next*, *last* aloud with the adult; the
  learner is never asked to decode story text
- Blends and segments sounds in CVC words (Unit 04, delivered as a
  validated draft in draft PR #284, unmerged) — used only for the
  playful word work in Lesson 01's question game, not for reading
  the stories

No reading is required at the start of this unit. The adult reads
every story aloud. The learner's job is listening, talking, pointing,
drawing, acting, and retelling, with the adult scribing dictated
words.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed;
all responses may be oral, pointing, drawing, or acting, adult-scribed
where noted):

1. Ask and answer questions about key details in a story read aloud
   — at least 4 of 5 questions correct across two stories, using
   *who*, *what*, *where*, *when*, *why*, or *how* questions.
2. Retell a familiar story, including key details — naming the
   characters and setting and at least 2 of the 3 major events, in
   order, without the adult telling the story first in that retelling.
3. Identify characters, settings, and major events in a story, and
   put 3 major events in order using *first*, *next*, and *last*.
4. Name the author and the illustrator of a story and define the
   role of each (author writes the words; illustrator draws the
   pictures), and describe what an illustration shows about the
   story — naming the moment it depicts and one detail visible in it.
5. Compare the adventures of characters in two familiar stories —
   naming at least one way they are the same and one way they are
   different — and recall information from a provided source to
   answer a question (for example, what a seed needs to grow).

## The unit's stories (original texts, written for this unit)

The adult reads each story aloud, slowly, twice when the lesson says
so. The full texts are printed in the lessons that first use them
and in the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md#the-units-stories) for
easy re-reading. Nothing here is copied from a published book.

- ***Milo and the Lost Mitten*** (Lessons 01, 03, 04; Practice
  Sessions A, C, D; project option; assessment comparison story).
  Milo walks through snow to Grandma's house, loses a blue mitten,
  and his dog Biscuit brings it back.
- ***Ana Plants a Seed*** (Lessons 02, 06; Practice Sessions B, C;
  project option). Ana plants a seed, waters it, watches it sprout,
  and picks a sunflower with her friend Ben.
- ***Sam's Rainy-Day Picnic*** (Lesson 05 draw-and-tell; formative
  quiz; culminating assessment story). Sam plans a picnic, rain
  changes the plan, and the picnic moves inside — and is still fun.
- ***How Seeds Grow*** (Lesson 06, information recall). Three
  sentences of original informational text: a seed needs soil,
  water, and sun; it sprouts a root, then a stem and leaves.

| 1 | SL.K.2 | Confirm understanding of a text read aloud or information presented orally or through other media by asking and answering questions about key details and requesting clarification if something is not understood. |

**A note on "key details" for the adult:** a key detail is a detail
the story cannot be told without — who the story is about, where it
happens, the problem, and what fixes it. The color of Milo's coat
(red) is a helpful picture detail, but the retelling still works
without it; the lost mitten, Biscuit finding it, and Milo saying
thank you are key. Lessons teach this difference explicitly, because
young retellers often list every detail or only the last thing that
happened.

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials
list): the three story texts (printed in the lessons and teacher
guide), the generated illustration
[`assets/milo-lost-mitten.png`](assets/milo-lost-mitten.png) shown on
a screen or printed large, three sequence cards per story (adult-made
before the session: simple drawings or cut-out pictures — Milo
walking in snow; the mitten on the ground; Biscuit bringing the
mitten; and matching sets for Ana and Sam), a stuffed animal or toy
dog for prop retells, a blue mitten or glove (or a paper mitten cut
from blue paper), crayons, pencils, drawing paper folded into a
3-page book, a small plant or seed packet for Lesson 06's
information talk (optional; a picture of a seedling works), the
talking stick from Unit 01, and any familiar picture book from home
for the author/illustrator lesson (any book with a cover naming an
author and illustrator, or one the adult names). No sharp tools;
adult supervises small parts in shared settings.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, drawing, or acting responses; the
adult scribes dictated words. Sequence cards carry a picture and a
spoken label, never a written word alone. Color is never the only
cue (the blue mitten is named aloud and shown, not identified by
color alone). Each lesson includes a seated-table and a floor-play
variant, a language-access support, an alternate response mode, and
an extension. The generated illustration ships with a text-only
alternative containing every detail the picture-talk questions need.
Props and acting give the retelling a body, not just words, for
learners who remember by moving.
