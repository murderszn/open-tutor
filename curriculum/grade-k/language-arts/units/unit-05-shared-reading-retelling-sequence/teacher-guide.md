# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Shared Reading, Retelling, and Sequence

**For the guiding adult.** This guide holds the session logic, the
full story texts for re-reading, the misconceptions to watch for,
and the re-teach triggers. It is not shown to the learner. Answers
live in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## Session logic

- **Sessions 1–2 (Lesson 01 + Question game):** the unit opens with
  listening and questioning, not retelling. Learners who can answer
  questions about a story are halfway to retelling it; do not skip
  to retelling if questions are still shaky.
- **Sessions 3–4 (Lesson 02 + Sort):** characters and setting are
  the vocabulary of every later retelling. The sort's two piles
  (*who* vs. *where/things*) should stay visible through Session 8.
- **Sessions 5–6 (Lesson 03 + Sequence race):** order before
  retelling. A learner who cannot order three cards will not
  retell in order, and no amount of prompting fixes that — the
  cards do.
- **Sessions 7–8 (Lesson 04 + Prop retell):** the first unaided
  retellings. Keep them short and praised; the assessment's retell
  is a new story (Sam), so transfer — not memorization of Milo —
  is the goal.
- **Sessions 9–10 (Lesson 05 + Draw-and-tell):** picture reading is
  taught explicitly here because the assessment's picture task
  (Task 3) depends on the shown/guessed/not-shown language. Use
  those three verbs every time.
- **Sessions 11–12 (Lesson 06 + Project Part 1):** comparison and
  information recall are the unit's newest thinking; keep them in
  one session as designed, but split them across two days if the
  learner tires — comparison first, *How Seeds Grow* second.
- **Sessions 13–14 (Review + Project Part 2):** Session 13 is
  diagnostic, not filler: run the game that matches the weakest
  objective (see re-teach triggers), then let the project share
  carry the celebration.
- **Sessions 15–16 (Quiz + Assessment):** the quiz is formative and
  low-stakes; say so. The assessment's Sam story is new on purpose
  — it tests whether retelling transfers, and a learner who retells
  Milo perfectly but cannot retell Sam needs more varied stories,
  not more Milo drills.

## The unit's stories

Original texts, written for this unit. Re-read from here whenever a
lesson needs the text again.

### *Milo and the Lost Mitten*

> Milo put on his red coat, his red hat, and his two blue mittens.
> He was walking to Grandma's house in the snow. His dog Biscuit
> walked beside him, wagging her tail.
>
> On the snowy path, one blue mitten slipped off Milo's hand. Milo
> did not see it fall. He kept walking. Biscuit stopped. She sniffed
> the snow. She picked up the blue mitten in her mouth.
>
> Biscuit ran to Milo and dropped the mitten at his feet. Milo
> laughed. "Thank you, Biscuit!" he said. He put the mitten back on.
> Then Milo and Biscuit walked on together to Grandma's house.

Key details: Milo, Biscuit, snowy path to Grandma's house, mitten
falls off, Biscuit finds and brings it back, Milo says thank you.
Non-key: hat color, coat color, tail wagging.

### *Ana Plants a Seed*

> Ana had a packet of sunflower seeds. She went out to her garden
> with her friend Ben. "Let's plant a seed," said Ana.
>
> Ana dug a little hole in the soil. She dropped one seed in. Ben
> poured water from a blue watering can. "Grow, little seed,
> grow," said Ana.
>
> Every day, Ana came back to look. One morning she saw a tiny green
> sprout! The sprout grew taller and taller. At the end of summer,
> a big yellow sunflower stood in the garden. Ana and Ben smiled
> up at it. "We grew that," said Ben.

Key details: Ana, Ben, garden, planting the seed, watering, sprout,
sunflower. Non-key: the watering can's color, the packet.

### *Sam's Rainy-Day Picnic*

> Sam packed a picnic basket with sandwiches, apples, and juice.
> He was going to eat outside under the big tree with his sister
> June. "Perfect picnic day!" said Sam.
>
> Then dark clouds came. Rain pattered down on the grass. "Oh no,"
> said Sam. "Our picnic is getting wet!"
>
> June laughed. "Let's have the picnic inside!" They spread a
> blanket on the living-room floor. They ate the sandwiches, apples,
> and juice right there. "Best picnic ever," said Sam. "Rain or
> no rain!"

Key details: Sam, June, big tree / outside, then inside on a
blanket; basket foods; rain changes the plan; picnic still fun.
Non-key: the exact foods (any two of the three suffice in a retell).

### *How Seeds Grow* (information text)

> A seed needs soil, water, and sun. First, the seed sprouts a root
> under the soil. Then it grows a stem and leaves above the soil.
> With soil, water, and sun, a small seed can grow into a big plant.

## Misconceptions, collected

- **Favorite detail vs. key detail:** learners report the detail
  they liked (red hat) instead of the detail the story needs. Fix:
  the "take it out, does the story still work?" test, every time.
- **Character = everything named:** the sunflower, the mitten, the
  basket are not characters. Fix: "Does it talk, choose, or do
  things on purpose?"
- **Order by preference:** the favorite event goes first. Fix: the
  "can this happen before that?" test.
- **Picture = whole story:** learners narrate events the picture
  does not show as if visible. Fix: the three verbs — shows,
  remember, guess — kept distinct.
- **Comparison = two facts:** "Milo has a red coat and Ana has a
  garden" is not a comparison. Fix: the frame "In Milo's story ___,
  and in Ana's story ___" with the same slot on both sides.
- **Information from imagination:** "seeds need love" is sweet but
  not fetched from the text. Fix: re-read, praise the fetch.

## Re-teach triggers

| If you see... | Re-teach with... | Before moving on |
|---|---|---|
| Questions answered with the last thing heard | Lesson 01's key-detail test; Practice Session A, items 1–3 | 4 of 5 questions correct across two stories |
| Characters confused with things | Lesson 02 sort; Practice Session B | Sort correct without help, sunflower correctly excluded |
| Events out of order | Lesson 03 cards; Practice Session C | 3-card order correct for both Milo and Ana |
| Retell lists names, not events | Lesson 04 four-finger frame; Practice Session D | Retell with 2+ events in order, unaided |
| Picture read as whole story | Lesson 05 three questions; Practice Session E | Moment correct + one shown / one guessed / one not-shown distinction |
| Comparison gives two facts | Lesson 06 table, spoken aloud | One true same and one true different, same slot both sides |
| Information answers invented | Re-read *How Seeds Grow* across two short sessions | Soil, water, sun named after one hearing |

## Readiness criteria

The learner is ready for Unit 06 (decodable reading) when they can:
retell a familiar story with characters, setting, and at least 2 of
3 major events in order; order 3 events with *first / next / last*;
name author and illustrator roles; and answer 4 of 5 key-detail
questions about a story heard twice. The Unit 06 work — decoding
short sentences — builds on this listening comprehension; it does
not replace it. If retelling is not yet secure, use a flexible week
for story games (Session F) rather than pushing into decoding with
a shaky retell.

## Accessibility notes

All responses may be oral, pointing, drawing, or acting. Sequence
cards always carry a picture plus a spoken label. The generated
illustration's text alternative (in [assets/README.md](assets/README.md))
contains every detail the picture questions need, so a learner who
cannot see the picture clearly can still answer from the spoken
alternative. Never use color as the only cue: the blue mitten is
always named and shown, and the sequence cards differ by picture,
not by color.
