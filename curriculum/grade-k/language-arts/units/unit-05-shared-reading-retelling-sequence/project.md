# Project — My Retell Book

**Sessions:** 12 (Part 1) and 14 (Part 2, with share) · **Time:**
15–20 minutes each · **Standards:** RL.K.2, RL.K.3, RL.K.7, SL.K.4,
SL.K.5, W.K.8 (dictated retell as gathered information about the
story)

## What the learner makes

A 3-page book — one page for the beginning, one for the middle, one
for the end — of a story the learner chooses: *Milo and the Lost
Mitten* or *Ana Plants a Seed*. Each page has a drawing of that
part of the story. Under each drawing, the adult scribes the
learner's dictated sentence for that part. The cover names the
story title (adult writes it, learner may copy letters) and the
learner as the reteller: "Retold by ___." The learner then tells
the whole book, page by page, to a listener.

## Materials

Three sheets of drawing paper (or one sheet folded into a book with
a cover), crayons, the chosen story (adult re-reads it in Part 1),
the sequence cards for the chosen story, the blue mitten prop or
seed picture for reference, a stapler or tape (adult handles it).

## Steps

### Part 1 (Session 12) — Draw the three parts

1. The learner chooses Milo or Ana. The adult re-reads the chosen
   story once, slowly.
2. With the sequence cards in order as a guide, the learner draws:
   - Page 1 — **Beginning:** Milo and Biscuit walking in the snow,
     *or* Ana and Ben in the garden with the seed packet.
   - Page 2 — **Middle:** the mitten on the ground with Biscuit
     finding it, *or* Ana planting and watering the seed and the
     sprout coming up.
   - Page 3 — **End:** Biscuit bringing the mitten back and Milo
     saying thank you, *or* the tall sunflower with Ana and Ben
     smiling.
3. The learner tells the adult one sentence per page, in order.
   The adult scribes each sentence under its drawing, exactly as
   the learner says it (fixing nothing except spelling the scribed
   words correctly).
4. The adult reads the three scribed sentences back. The learner
   checks: "Is that what I said? Is it in order?" Corrections are
   welcome — this is the learner's book.

### Part 2 (Session 14) — Practice, share, finish

1. The learner practices telling the book: cover first ("This book
   is ___; I retold it"), then page 1, 2, 3 in order, pointing to
   each drawing as they tell it.
2. The learner shares the book with a listener — the adult, a
   family member, a stuffed animal audience, or a recorded telling
   for a family member (adult handles any recording; no recording
   is kept in the repository).
3. The listener asks one question about the book ("Who helped?" /
   "What happened in the middle?"). The learner answers from the
   story.
4. The adult staples or tapes the book, adds the cover credit
   "Retold by ___," and puts the book where the learner can
   re-read it (by retelling) again later. Re-reading one's own
   retell book counts as review in later weeks.

## Deliverables

1. A 3-page book with a drawing on each page (beginning, middle, end).
2. One dictated sentence scribed under each drawing (adult-scribed).
3. A cover naming the story and the learner as reteller.
4. An oral telling of the whole book, page by page, to a listener.

## Rubric

Score each row 2 (secure), 1 (nearly there), or 0 (not yet). A total
of 6–8 is secure; 4–5 is nearly there (revisit the missed row in
Session 13-style games); 3 or fewer means re-teach with the
sequence cards before Unit 06.

| Criterion | 2 — Secure | 1 — Nearly there | 0 — Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Order | Pages run beginning → middle → end, and the telling follows the pages | Two pages in order, one out of place, or the telling skips a page | Pages or telling out of order, or only one part told |
| Key details | Each page's sentence names a key detail: characters and setting on page 1, the problem or job on page 2, the ending on page 3 | Two pages carry key details; one page is a minor detail or unclear | Sentences list things or colors without events, or no sentences |
| Pictures match words | Each drawing shows the moment its sentence tells | Two drawings match; one is generic or mismatched | Drawings do not match the sentences |
| Telling | Tells the whole book to a listener, in order, without the adult telling it first | Tells with one or two adult prompts ("And then?") | Cannot tell the book without the adult telling it first |

## Differentiation

- **Support:** the learner draws only, and dictates single words
  ("walking," "mitten," "thank you") that the adult builds into
  sentences with the learner's approval; sequence cards stay face
  up throughout.
- **Extension:** the learner adds a fourth page — "What happened
  after the end?" — clearly labeled as their own invention, not
  part of the story (for example, "Milo and Biscuit drank cocoa at
  Grandma's"), and tells which page is invented and which pages
  are the story.
