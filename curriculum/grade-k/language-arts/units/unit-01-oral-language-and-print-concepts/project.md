# Project — My First Big Book

**Type:** investigation / creation project across Sessions 11, 14, and 15.
**Driving question:** "Can I make a book that someone else can read the way
books are supposed to be read?"

The learner authors a 4-page big book (drawings + dictated sentences the
adult writes in large print), then proves it works by reading it with
correct book handling, tracking, and word-touching — and by answering and
asking questions about it in the share circle.

## Materials

4 large sheets of paper (or folded chart paper), crayons/markers, a stapler
or binder clips (adult use), word cards from Lesson 03 for sentence
planning, the talking stick.

## Part 1 — Plan and draft (Session 11)

1. Choose a topic the learner knows well (my family, my pet, the sun —
   keep it to something drawable). Adult scribes the topic on a planning
   strip.
2. Plan 4 pages: "What will happen on page 1? 2? 3? 4?" One dictated
   sentence per page (3–6 words each, e.g., "The dog runs fast.").
3. Draw one picture per page that shows the sentence. The adult writes each
   dictated sentence in large print under the picture, leaving clear word
   spaces — modeling print concepts in real time ("I am leaving spaces so
   we can see the words").
4. Number the pages 1–4 together (adult writes; learner points and counts).

## Part 2 — Assemble and rehearse (Session 14)

1. Put the pages in order, front to back. Adult binds them; the learner
   makes the front cover (title + picture) and the back cover.
2. Rehearsal: the learner "reads" the book to a stuffed animal — tracking
   left to right with the pointer, touching one word per spoken word,
   turning pages one at a time, starting each page at the top left.
3. The adult asks one **who/what/where** question per page; the learner
   answers. Then the learner asks the adult one question about the book.

## Part 3 — Share (Session 15, with the formative quiz)

Talk-circle share: the learner presents the book (holds it correctly,
reads/tracks all four pages), then takes two questions from the audience
(adult, family member, or stuffed-animal "audience" voiced by the adult),
following talk rules. The adult scribes one audience question and the
learner's answer onto the back cover as a souvenir.

## Deliverables

1. The 4-page big book (cover, numbered pages, pictures, large-print
   dictated sentences with clear word spaces).
2. A tracked reading (observed): correct handling, left-to-right tracking,
   one touch per word, page turns.
3. One answered question and one asked question about the book (scribed).

## Rubric

| Criterion | Getting started (1) | Developing (2) | Secure (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Book handling** | Needs hand-over-hand help to hold/turn | Holds correctly with reminders; turns one page at a time most times | Front/back, right-side up, one page at a time, independently |
| **Tracking** | Pointer parks or moves randomly | Tracks left to right with shadowing help; line sweeps need cues | Independent left-to-right tracking, line sweeps, page restarts |
| **Word matching** | Touches do not match spoken words | Matches most words; self-corrects some mismatches | One touch per spoken word across all four pages |
| **Talking about the book** | Does not ask or answer | Answers questions OR asks one question | Answers questions about key details AND asks a genuine question |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = secure — ready for Unit 02. 7–9 = developing — note
the lowest criterion and re-teach it in Session 12/16 before moving on.
Below 7 = re-teach the matching lessons during a flexible week; the track's
diagnostic/catch-up weeks exist for this.

## Adult notes

- The sentences are the learner's dictation, scribed verbatim — do not
  "fix" grammar; invented content is the point.
- If the learner cannot yet draw recognizably, any marks with a dictated
  label ("this is the dog") fully count.
- Keep the book afterward: Unit 05 reuses learner-made books for retelling
  practice.
