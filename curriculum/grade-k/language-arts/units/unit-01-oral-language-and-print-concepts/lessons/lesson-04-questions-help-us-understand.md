# Lesson 04 — Questions Help Us Understand

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can ask a question about a story and answer
questions about what happened in it."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** a short picture book with clear
key details (who did what), the talking stick from Lesson 01.
**Standards:** SL.K.2, SL.K.3 (with SL.K.1/SL.K.1.a talk rules revisited).

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

"Yesterday we learned talk rules: listen, take turns. Today we use them
for a new job — asking questions. What is something you wonder about?"
Accept anything; curiosity is the skill.

## Explicit teaching

Good listeners do not just sit quietly — they **wonder out loud**.
A **question** asks for something you want to know. An **answer** gives
what someone asked for. During a read-aloud we ask questions about the
**key details**: who is in the story, what happened, and where.

Question words are our tools: **who** (a person or animal), **what** (a
thing or action), **where** (a place). You can ask a question *before* we
read ("I wonder what the dog will do"), *during* ("Why is she hiding?"),
or *after* ("Where did they go?").

### Modeled example 1 — adult asks, learner answers

*Read two pages of the picture book, then stop. Adult thinks aloud:*
"I have a question: **who** is carrying the basket?" *Waits.* "You said
the girl — that answers my who-question. A who-question wants a person."
*Reads on, stops again:* "New question: **what** is in the basket? ...
Apples — that answers my what-question."

### Modeled example 2 — learner asks, adult answers (and models "I don't know yet")

*Adult:* "Now it is your turn to wonder. Look at this picture — what do
you want to know?" *If the learner asks, e.g., "Where is the dog going?",
the adult answers from the book if possible, or says honestly:* "I don't
know yet — let's keep reading to find out." *If the learner offers a
statement instead of a question:* "That is a good noticing! Can we turn it
into a question? 'The dog is running' becomes '**Where** is the dog
running?'"

## Guided practice (talk circle with the stick; 4 tasks)

1. Read one page. Adult asks a **who** question; the learner answers with
   the stick. ("Who is in the tree?" → "The cat.")
2. Read the next page. Adult asks a **what** question; the learner
   answers. ("What did she find?" → "A key.")
3. "Your turn to wonder." Learner asks any question about the picture;
   the adult answers or says "let's find out." Coach statements into
   questions gently.
4. "Ask me for help with a word." Learner practices SL.K.3 directly: "What
   does ___ mean?" about an unknown word on the page.

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 4 tasks)

1. After the read-aloud: "Ask me one question about the story." (Any
   genuine question counts — who/what/where or other.)
2. Adult asks: "What happened first — what happened at the very
   beginning?" (One key detail from the start.)
3. "Tell me one thing you did not understand, or one thing you want to
   know more about." (Requesting clarification — SL.K.2.)
4. Talking-stick round: learner answers a partner/toy's question about the
   story, following turn-taking.

## Application

"Wonder wall": the learner dictates 2–3 questions about the book ("I
wonder why... / I wonder where..."). The adult writes each on a strip and
posts them. After the unit's later read-alouds, revisit: "Did we find the
answer to your question?" Circle answered ones.

## Exit check

1. "What is a question?" (Asking something you want to know — any
   reasonable phrasing.)
2. "Ask me a question about our book." (A real question, not a statement.)
3. "Answer my question: who was the story about?" (A key-detail answer
   from the book.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** question words taught with gestures (who → point to
  people; where → look around; what → hold up an object). Home-language
  question words welcomed and mapped to the English ones.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may ask by pointing at a picture
  detail with a questioning face and tone — the adult voices the question
  shape ("You're asking who that is?").
- **Seated-table variant:** book on the table, wonder strips in front of
  the learner.
- **Floor-play variant:** "question hop" — hop onto a who/what/where mat
  square, then ask that kind of question.
- **Extension:** sort questions into "the book answered it" vs. "we still
  wonder" — the beginning of distinguishing answered and open questions.

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Statements dressed as questions:* "The dog ran away?" with rising
  intonation is a fine beginning — accept it, then model the question-word
  form once: "**Where** did the dog run?"
- *Answer = any talking:* children sometimes answer a question by talking
  about something else. Gently re-anchor: "That is interesting — and my
  question was *who* is carrying the basket."
- *Fear of wrong questions:* there are no wrong wonderings at this stage.
  Praise the asking every time; correct only the question *shape* when you
  are teaching it, never the curiosity.
