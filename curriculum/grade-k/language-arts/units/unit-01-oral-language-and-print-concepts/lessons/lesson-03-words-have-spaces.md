# Lesson 03 — Words Have Spaces

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can touch one word for each word I hear, and I
can find the spaces between words."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** word cards or a pocket chart with
the line "The sun is hot." as separate cards, the generated illustration
from Lesson 02, a pointer.
**Standards:** RF.K.1, RF.K.1.b, RF.K.1.c.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Say slowly: "The — sun — is — hot." Clap once per spoken word. "How many
claps? How many words did you hear?" (Four.) Today we find those four words
on the page.

## Explicit teaching

Every word you say is one clump of letters on the page. One spoken word =
one printed word. And printed words do not touch each other — they leave
**spaces** between them, like fences between yards. The spaces tell your
eyes where one word ends and the next begins.

A **word** is a group of letters with spaces on both sides (except the
first and last words on a line — they have a space on one side and the edge
of the line on the other). Letters inside a word touch; words do not.

### Modeled example 1 — matching speech to print

*Four word cards on the table, spaced apart: The | sun | is | hot.*
*Adult says the sentence slowly, touching one card per spoken word:* "The
*(touch)* sun *(touch)* is *(touch)* hot *(touch).* Four spoken words, four
touches, four cards." *Adult scrambles the cards and re-lays them with no
gaps:* "Now the words are touching — can you see where one word ends?
*(No.)* The spaces were doing a job. Watch me put the fences back."
*Adult re-spaces the cards.*

### Modeled example 2 — finding spaces in the illustration

*Display `big-book-tracking.png`:* "Let's be space detectives. Put your
finger in a space — not on a word, *between* words." *Adult demonstrates:*
"Here is a space between 'The' and 'sun'. Here is one between 'sun' and
'is'." *(On the second line:)* "And between 'is' and 'hot.'."
"Count the spaces on line one with me: one ... two. Two words on line one
have one space between them."

## Guided practice (adult and learner together; 4 tasks)

1. Adult says "The sun is hot." slowly; learner touches one word card per
   spoken word. Repeat twice, learner-led the second time.
2. "Cover one word with your hand. Now say the sentence — what is
   missing?" (Any word; the sentence sounds wrong without it.)
3. Space hunt on the illustration: learner points to each space between
   words and counts them. (Line 1: 1 space; line 2: 1 space.)
4. Adult reads the line at normal speed while the learner tracks with the
   pointer, one touch per word — no rushing, no skipping.

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 4 tasks)

1. Lay out the four word cards spaced. Learner says the sentence and
   touches each card once. (One touch per word; no double taps.)
2. "How many words?" Learner counts the cards. (Four.)
3. Adult removes the spaces (pushes cards together). "What happened to the
   spaces? Can you still see four words?" Learner re-spaces the cards.
4. New line, same skill: cards "I | see | a | cat." Learner touches and
   counts. (Four words.)

## Application

"Word builder": the learner arranges the four cards in order (adult may
read the target sentence aloud as a model), spaces them, and "reads" the
line to a stuffed animal by touching each word. The adult scribes the
learner's dictated sentence if the learner wants to keep it.

## Exit check

1. Adult says "The sun is hot." — "Touch one word for each word you
   hear." (Four touches, one per card/printed word.)
2. "Point to a space." (Finger lands between two words, not on a letter.)
3. "Are 'sun' and 'is' one word or two words? How do you know?" (Two —
   "there is a space between them.")

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** *space* is an abstract word — teach it as "the empty
  place" and let the learner put a finger in it. Home-language word for
  "empty/gap" welcomed.
- **Alternate response mode:** touching, covering, and re-spacing cards are
  all valid answers; speech optional.
- **Seated-table variant:** cards on the table or in a pocket chart.
- **Floor-play variant:** giant word cards on the floor; learner hops from
  word to word, landing in the spaces between hops to "feel" them.
- **Extension:** two-word vs. three-word lines — "Which line has more
  words? How many spaces does it have?" (Words = spaces + 1 on a line.)

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Letters are words:* children often point to a single letter and call it
  a word. Restate the rule: a word is the whole clump between spaces.
  Trace around the whole word with the pointer.
- *The period belongs to the word:* "hot." — the dot is punctuation stuck
  to the word, not a separate word. Say so plainly; do not drill it.
- *Fast reciters:* a child who has memorized the sentence may chant it
  without matching touches. Slow them down: "One touch, one word — let
  your finger wait for each word."
