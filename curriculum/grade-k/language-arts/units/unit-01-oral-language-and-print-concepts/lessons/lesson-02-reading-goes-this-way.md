# Lesson 02 — Reading Goes This Way

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can point under words from left to right, go
down to the next line, and turn the page to keep going."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** the generated illustration
[big-book-tracking.png](../assets/big-book-tracking.png), any big book or
large-print page, a pointer (finger puppet or craft stick).
**Standards:** RF.K.1, RF.K.1.a.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Open any book to a page with two or three lines of large print. "Point to
where we would start reading this page." Accept any top-left attempt;
today's lesson teaches the rest.

## Explicit teaching

Words on a page have a road, and readers drive the road the same way every
time. The road rules:

1. Start at the **top** of the page, at the **left**.
2. Move **left to right** under the words as they are read.
3. At the end of the line, **sweep back** to the left and **down** to the
   next line.
4. At the bottom of the page, **turn the page** and start again at the top
   left.

Look at our picture. The pointing hand starts on the first word, "The",
and the green arrow sweeps from left to right — that is the road.

### Modeled example 1 — with the illustration

*Display `big-book-tracking.png`. Adult points with the pointer, reading
slowly:* "The *(touch "The")* sun *(touch "sun")* is *(touch "is")*
hot. *(touch "hot.")* I started at the top left. I moved left to right.
Then I swept down to the next line — 'is' — and kept going left to right.
The green arrow shows my road."

*Text-only alternative (same information, no image needed):* "The page has
two lines of large words. Line one: 'The sun'. Line two: 'is hot.'. A hand
points at 'The', and a green arrow sweeps left to right under the words."

**Question for the learner (with image or alternative):** "Which word is
the pointing hand touching?" → *The.* "Which word comes right after 'The'
on the road?" → *sun.*

### Modeled example 2 — page-by-page in a real book

*Open a big book to a full page of large print. Adult reads two lines with
the pointer, deliberately sweeping down to the second line, then turns the
page:* "I finished the bottom line — so I turn the page, and look: I start
again at the top left of the new page. Every page, same road."

## Guided practice (adult and learner together; 4 tasks)

1. With the illustration: the adult reads "The sun is hot." and the
   learner moves the pointer under each word while the adult says the
   words. Adult's hand shadows the learner's to keep the sweep.
2. "Where do we start on a new page?" (Top left — point to it on the
   image.)
3. "Show me with your finger: left to right." The learner traces the
   green arrow's direction in the air.
4. In the big book: the adult reads one line; the learner sweeps the
   pointer back to the left edge and down to the next line on cue: "sweep!"

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 4 tasks)

1. Learner "reads" the illustration alone: points to each of the four
   words left to right, top line then bottom line, while saying any sounds
   or the words if known. (Correct pointing matters, not correct reading.)
2. "Point to the last word on the page." (Accepts "hot.")
3. "Point to the first word on the second line." (Accepts "is.")
4. In a real book: learner turns three pages, each time placing the
   pointer at the top-left starting spot.

## Application

The learner is the "reading driver" for a stuffed animal: they hold the
pointer, read (or pretend-read) one page of a real book, moving left to
right, sweeping down, turning the page — and narrate the road rules to the
toy: "Start here. Go this way. Sweep down. Turn the page."

## Exit check

1. "Show me with the pointer: where do we start on a page?" (Top left.)
2. "Which way do we go?" (Left to right — gesture or words.)
3. "What do we do at the end of a line?" (Sweep back to the left and go
   down to the next line.)
4. "What do we do at the bottom of the page?" (Turn the page; start again
   at the top left.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** teach *left/right* on the learner's own hands first
  (a sticker or mark on the "start" hand), then transfer to the page.
- **Alternate response mode:** all checks may be pointing and gesturing;
  the learner never has to say the words "left" and "right" if the finger
  shows the road.
- **Seated-table variant:** illustration and book flat on the table.
- **Floor-play variant:** tape a long paper "road" on the floor with word
  cards; the learner walks the road left to right, sweeping down at each
  line end.
- **Extension:** two-page spread — "the road keeps going across both
  pages, then back to the left page's top?" (No: each page restarts at its
  own top left. Let the learner discover the turn.)

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Pointing without moving:* children often park the pointer on one word
  while the adult reads on. Gently move their hand with yours and say,
  "Your pointer rides along with the words."
- *Right-to-left starts:* a child who starts at the right is not being
  difficult — many home languages read right to left. Honor that knowledge
  ("In Arabic the road goes the other way! In English books it goes this
  way") and practice the English road explicitly.
- *Skipping the sweep:* children jump straight down instead of sweeping
  back to the left. Exaggerate the return sweep — "all the way back to the
  start line!"
