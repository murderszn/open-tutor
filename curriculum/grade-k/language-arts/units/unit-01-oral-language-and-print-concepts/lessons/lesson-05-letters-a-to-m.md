# Lesson 05 — Letters A to M

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can name the uppercase letters A, B, C, D, E,
F, G, H, I, J, K, L, M."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** uppercase letter cards A–M (large
print, high contrast), an alphabet strip or arc mat, a small mirror.
**Standards:** RF.K.1.d (uppercase A–M; N–Z and lowercase complete in Unit
03).

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Spread the A–M cards face up, out of order. "Point to any letter you
already know the name of." Note which are known — start instruction at the
unknown ones, not at A.

## Explicit teaching

Letters are the building pieces of words. Each letter has a **name** we
say ("ay", "bee", "cee") — the name is not the same as the sound it makes
in words; sounds come in Unit 03. Today we learn the *names* of the first
thirteen letters, A through M.

Meet them in small families so they stick:

- **Straight-line family:** A, E, F, H, I, K, L, M (made of straight
  lines)
- **Curvy family:** B, C, D, G, J (have curves)
- Say each name slowly, trace its shape in the air, and have the learner
  echo: "A — ay. Trace it. Your turn."

Order matters for finding letters later (the alphabet song is a finding
tool), but knowing a letter means naming it **out of order** too — so we
practice both ways.

### Modeled example 1 — in order, with the song as a tool

*Lay A–M in order on the arc mat. Adult points and sings slowly to M:*
"A, B, C, D, E, F, G — H, I, J, K, L, M!" *Then, covering all but three:*
"The song told me where to look. Now without the song: *(points to E)*
this is E — 'ee'. *(Points to K)* this is K — 'kay'."

### Modeled example 2 — out of order, with a mix-up fixed aloud

*Cards shuffled. Adult draws C:* "This one is ... C — 'cee'. I know
because of the big curve, like a moon." *Draws M:* "M — 'em'. Straight
lines with the two humps." *Draws B:* "Careful — B or D? B has two
bellies; this is B — 'bee'." *Thinking aloud about confusions is the
teaching.*

## Guided practice (adult and learner together; 4 tasks)

1. Echo round: adult shows each card in order A–M; learner says the name
   and air-traces. (Slow; repeat unknown ones immediately.)
2. "Find E." Learner scans the spread and points. Repeat with 3–4 target
   letters the learner missed in the readiness check.
3. Mirror check: learner says "M — em" and "W is not here yet" while
   watching their mouth in the mirror (names, not sounds — just playful
   mouth awareness for later sound work).
4. Sort the cards into the two families (straight-line / curvy) with the
   adult, naming each letter as it is placed.

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 4 tasks)

1. Cards face up, shuffled. "Name this one." Draw 5 cards; learner names
   each. (Adult notes hits/misses for the answer key log.)
2. "Find all the letters in your name that are between A and M." (Point
   and name each; skip letters outside A–M without penalty.)
3. Alphabet arc: learner places A–M in order on the mat from a shuffled
   pile, saying names as they go.
4. "Which two letters look most alike?" (Any defensible pair — B/D, E/F,
   M/N-not-yet — with the learner's reason.)

## Application

"Letter mail": the learner picks 3 letter cards, "mails" each to a stuffed
animal by naming it ("Dear Bear, here is K — 'kay'"), and the adult
scribes a one-line dictated note per letter. The notes go on the wonder
wall next to Lesson 04's questions.

## Exit check

1. Adult holds up 5 shuffled cards (mix of known and new): learner names
   each. (3 of 5 = secure for this lesson; fewer → re-teach list.)
2. "Point to M." / "Point to B." (Out-of-order recognition.)
3. "Say the letters from A to M." (Order recall — the song may be used.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** letter names are new vocabulary — pair each with a
  familiar anchor word ("B — 'bee', like the buzzing bee") and a picture
  if helpful.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing to named letters ("point to K")
  counts fully; naming aloud is not required for the check to pass.
- **Seated-table variant:** cards on the table; arc mat as the workspace.
- **Floor-play variant:** giant floor letters; learner jumps to the named
  letter.
- **Extension:** find A–M in the wild — on the book cover, in the room
  (exit signs, labels); or preview N–Z names without mastery pressure.

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Song ≠ knowledge:* a child who sings to M perfectly may still not name
  a shuffled K. Always test out of order; the song is a tool, not the
  goal.
- *B/D, E/F, M/W confusion:* expected and normal. Name the distinguishing
  feature every time ("B has two bellies; D has one big belly") rather
  than just saying "no, that's D."
- *Name/sound blending:* children will offer sounds ("buh!") — delight in
  it ("You know its sound already! Today we practice its *name*: 'bee'"),
  and park sound work for Unit 03.
