# Lesson 01 — How Books Work

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can show the front and back of a book, hold it
right-side up, turn one page at a time, and follow our talk rules."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** a picture book, letter-free
familiarity — no reading needed; a toy microphone or talking stick.
**Standards:** RF.K.1, SL.K.1, SL.K.1.a.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Hold up the picture book closed. "Show me how you would pick up a book to
start reading." Watch, don't correct yet. Note who goes for the front, who
opens from the back, who holds it upside down — that is your starting map.

## Explicit teaching

Books have a plan, and readers follow the plan. The **front** is where the
book begins — it usually shows the **title** (the book's name) and a
picture. The **back** is where the book ends. When we read, the front faces
us and the words sit **right-side up** — not upside down, not sideways.

Pages are read in order, one at a time, from the **front** of the book to
the **back**. We turn one page gently, like this — not two, not a whole
handful.

Talking about books has a plan too. Our talk-circle rules:

1. **Listen** while someone else is talking (eyes and ears on the speaker).
2. **Take turns** — one voice at a time; the talking stick shows whose turn
   it is.
3. **Stay on the book** — our words are about this story.

### Modeled example 1 — front, back, right-side up

*Adult holds the book upside down and backwards, then thinks aloud:*
"Hmm. This picture is upside down — the dog is standing on the sky! And
this cover has no title, just the back blurb. This must be the **back**.
*(Flips the book over.)* Here is the title and the big picture — this is
the **front**. Now the words sit right-side up." *Adult opens to the first
page:* "The first page comes right after the front cover. That is where we
start."

### Modeled example 2 — talk rules in action

*Adult with the talking stick:* "I am holding the stick, so it is my turn.
I will say one thing about the cover: I see a dog. Now I pass the stick to
you — it is your turn. You say one thing you see. *(Learner responds.)* You
listened while I talked, and I listened while you talked. That is rule 1
and rule 2 working."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 4 tasks)

1. Hand the learner the book closed. "Show me the front." (Prompt with
   "where does the book begin?" if needed.)
2. "Now turn it to the back — where does the book end?"
3. "Hold it so the words would be right-side up." Gently adjust; name what
   you see: "Now the dog is standing on the ground, not the sky."
4. Pass the talking stick. "It is your turn — tell me one thing you see on
   the front cover." Model listening visibly: eyes on the learner, nod.

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 4 tasks)

1. Place the book upside down and backwards on the table. "Fix the book so
   it is ready to read." (Front facing learner, right-side up.)
2. "Open to where we would start reading." (First page after the front
   cover — any early page is acceptable.)
3. Turn three pages, one at a time, front to back. Count aloud together.
4. Talking stick in the learner's hands: "Tell me one more thing about the
   cover." The learner holds the turn; the adult practices rule 1.

## Application

"Be the teacher." The learner teaches a stuffed animal how to hold the
book and states one talk rule in their own words ("You have to listen").
The adult writes down (scribes) the rule exactly as the learner said it.

## Exit check

1. "Show me the front of the book." (Points/holds correctly.)
2. "Show me how the pages go — which way do we turn?" (Front to back,
   one at a time — gesture or words accepted.)
3. "What is one talk rule?" (Any of listen / take turns / stay on the
   book, in the learner's own words.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** teach *front/back, top/bottom* with the learner's
  body first ("touch your front — your tummy; your back is behind you"),
  then the book.
- **Alternate response mode:** learner may answer every check by pointing,
  gesturing, or demonstrating — no speech required.
- **Seated-table variant:** all work at the table with the book flat.
- **Floor-play variant:** "book walk" — carry the closed book across the
  room held correctly, then sit and open.
- **Extension:** name the **author** ("writes the words") and
  **illustrator** ("draws the pictures") on the title page; find where each
  name is printed.

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Opening from the back:* many children open a book from whichever side
  is nearest. Name it plainly: "You opened the back — books begin at the
  front. Watch me find the front." Do not treat it as misbehavior.
- *Talk rules as silence rules:* children may think "listen" means "never
  talk." Restate: listening is waiting for your turn, and turns come to
  everyone.
- *Upside-down tolerance:* some children genuinely cannot see that print is
  upside down yet. Use the picture ("the dog is standing on the sky") as
  the cue, not the letters.
