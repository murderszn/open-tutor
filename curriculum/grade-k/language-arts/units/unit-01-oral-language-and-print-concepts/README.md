# Unit 01 — Oral Language Listening and Print Concepts

**Grade:** Kindergarten · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #8](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/8), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Before children can read words, they learn how books work and how talking
about books works. In this unit the learner handles books on purpose (front,
back, top, bottom), tracks print left to right and top to bottom while an
adult reads, discovers that each spoken word matches one clump of letters and
that spaces separate words, practices asking and answering questions during
read-alouds, follows talk-circle rules, and names the uppercase letters A
through M.

## Essential question

How do books and words carry the ideas people talk about, and how do we
join a conversation about a book?

## Prerequisites

- Talks in multi-word sentences; follows two-step oral directions (from
  diagnostic weeks 1–2)
- Can listen to a 10-minute read-aloud with pictures
- Fine-motor readiness: holds a crayon, traces lines

No letter knowledge or reading is required — Unit 01 assumes none.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed; all
responses may be oral, pointing, or drawing, adult-scribed):

1. Handle a book with purpose: show its front and back, hold it right-side
   up, and turn pages one at a time front to back.
2. Track print in the reading direction: point under each word left to
   right, move top line to bottom line, and move page to page while an adult
   reads aloud.
3. Show that spoken words match printed words and that spaces separate
   words: touch one word (one letter-clump) for each spoken word in a short
   repeated line.
4. Name the uppercase letters A through M in order and out of order.
5. Join a talk circle about a read-aloud: follow discussion rules (listen,
   take turns), and ask or answer at least one question about key details.

## Vocabulary (taught with objects and books first, word second)

front · back · cover · page · top · bottom · title · author (writes the
words) · illustrator (draws the pictures) · word · letter · space · left ·
right · line · question · answer · listen · take turns · uppercase ·
*A–M letter names*

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials
list): 2–3 picture books (library or homemade), a big book or a book with
large print, uppercase letter cards A–M (large print, high contrast),
a toy microphone or talking stick for the talk circle, crayons and drawing
paper, a pointer (finger puppet, clean unsharpened pencil, or craft stick),
the generated illustration
[`assets/big-book-tracking.png`](assets/big-book-tracking.png). No sharp
tools; adult supervises small parts in shared settings. Read-aloud books are
previewed by the adult for age suitability.

## 16-session pacing (4 weeks × 4 sessions, 15–20 minutes each)

Sessions rotate as: read-aloud/lesson → pointing/talk practice → game →
review, matching the track session model. The project runs across Sessions
11 and 14, with the share in Session 15.

| Session | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lesson | [L1 — How books work](lessons/lesson-01-how-books-work.md): front/back, top/bottom, page turning, talk-circle rules |
| 2 | Practice | [Read-aloud pointing practice](practice.md#session-a-read-aloud-pointing) — adult reads, learner tracks pictures and words |
| 3 | Game | [Book doctor game](practice.md#session-b-book-doctor-game) — fix an upside-down or backwards book |
| 4 | Review | [Talk-circle review](practice.md#session-d-talk-circle-review) + L1 exit re-checks |
| 5 | Lesson | [L2 — Reading goes this way](lessons/lesson-02-reading-goes-this-way.md) (uses the generated illustration): left-to-right, top-to-bottom, page-by-page tracking |
| 6 | Practice | [Pointer reading practice](practice.md#session-a-read-aloud-pointing) — learner moves the pointer while the adult reads |
| 7 | Game | [Reading train game](practice.md#session-c-reading-train-game) — bodies move left-to-right through a line of word cards |
| 8 | Review | [Book and talk review](practice.md#session-d-talk-circle-review) + L1–L2 exit re-checks |
| 9 | Lesson | [L3 — Words have spaces](lessons/lesson-03-words-have-spaces.md): one spoken word = one letter-clump; spaces separate words |
| 10 | Practice | [Word-touch practice](practice.md#session-e-word-touch-practice) — touch one word per spoken word on repeated lines |
| 11 | Project | [My First Big Book](project.md), Part 1 — draw pictures, dictate sentences |
| 12 | Review | Re-teach highest-need objective (see [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md#re-teach-triggers)) |
| 13 | Lesson | [L4 — Questions help us understand](lessons/lesson-04-questions-help-us-understand.md) and [L5 — Letters A to M](lessons/lesson-05-letters-a-to-m.md) (combined core session) |
| 14 | Project | [My First Big Book](project.md), Part 2 — assemble, practice reading and tracking |
| 15 | Check | [Formative quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md) (oral/observed) + project share |
| 16 | Assessment | [Unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md) (performance tasks) + re-teach planning |

Weeks 1–3 of the unit introduce and practice; Week 4 (track week 6) is the
review week: games, re-teaching, the formative check, and the culminating
assessment. If the learner is not yet secure on an objective after Session
16, use the diagnostic/catch-up guidance in the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md)
before starting Unit 02 — the track's flexible weeks exist for exactly this.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, or drawing responses; the adult scribes.
Color is never the only cue (the direction arrow also has a distinct shape
and position; letter cards are large print, high contrast). Each lesson
includes a seated-table and a floor-play variant, a language-access support,
an alternate response mode, and an extension. The generated illustration
ships with a text-only alternative containing every word the activity needs.
