# Project — My Rhyme Book

**Type:** investigation / creation project across Sessions 11, 14, and 15.
**Driving question:** "Can I make a book of rhyming words that someone
else can read and play with?"

The learner authors a 4-page rhyme book: each page shows one rhyme
family (a drawn picture pair plus the dictated rhyming words, scribed by
the adult). Then the learner proves the book works — reading the rhymes
aloud, clapping the syllables in the words, taking one word apart into
beginning + rest — and shares it in a talk circle.

## Materials

4 large sheets of paper (or folded chart paper), crayons/markers, a
stapler or binder clips (adult use), the rhyme picture cards from the
lessons as planning aids, the talking stick.

## Part 1 — Plan and draft (Session 11)

1. Choose 4 rhyme families the learner knows well (suggestions: -at,
   -og, -un, -ee; the learner may invent their own).
2. For each page: draw the two pictures (e.g., a cat and a hat), then
   dictate the rhyming pair. The adult writes the pair in large print
   under the pictures (e.g., "cat — hat"), modeling one-to-one
   correspondence as in Unit 01.
3. Under each pair, the adult and learner mark the syllable beats
   together: the learner claps while the adult draws one dot per
   syllable under each word (all of these words are one syllable — one
   dot each; noticing this is part of the learning).
4. Number the pages 1–4 together (adult writes; learner points and
   counts).

## Part 2 — Assemble and rehearse (Session 14)

1. Put the pages in order, front to back. Adult binds them; the learner
   makes the front cover (title "My Rhyme Book" + picture) and the back
   cover.
2. Rehearsal: the learner "reads" the book to a stuffed animal —
   saying each rhyming pair, clapping the syllables in each word, and
   taking one word apart into beginning + rest (e.g., "cat: /c/ ... -at").
3. The adult asks one question per page: "Do these two rhyme? How do you
   know?" and one challenge: "Can you make one more rhyme for this
   family?" The learner answers; then the learner asks the adult one
   question about the book.

## Part 3 — Share (Session 15, with the formative quiz)

Talk-circle share: the learner presents the book (holds it correctly,
reads all four rhyme families, claps one word's syllables, takes one
word apart), then takes two questions from the audience (adult, family
member, or stuffed-animal "audience" voiced by the adult), following
talk rules. The adult scribes one audience question and the learner's
answer onto the back cover as a souvenir.

## Deliverables

1. The 4-page rhyme book (cover, numbered pages, pictures, large-print
   dictated rhyming pairs with syllable dots).
2. An observed performance: reads the pairs, claps syllables in at
   least two words, takes at least one word apart into beginning + rest.
3. One answered question and one asked question about the book
   (scribed).

## Rubric

| Criterion | Getting started (1) | Developing (2) | Secure (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rhyme recognition** | Cannot reliably tell rhyming from non-rhyming pairs | Tells rhyme from non-rhyme with the ending stretched for them | Independently judges pairs and names the shared ending |
| **Rhyme production** | Repeats the given word or offers a non-rhyme | Produces a rhyme with a prompt ("keep -at...") | Produces rhymes for new words, including silly words |
| **Syllable & word-part skills** | Claps do not match parts; cannot blend or split | Claps syllables accurately; blends with the "say it fast" cue | Claps, blends, and takes apart (syllables and beginning/rest) independently |
| **Talking about the book** | Does not ask or answer | Answers questions OR asks one question | Answers questions about the rhymes AND asks a genuine question |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = secure — ready for Unit 03. 7–9 = developing — note
the lowest criterion and re-teach it in Session 12/16 before moving on.
Below 7 = re-teach the matching lessons during a flexible week; the
track's diagnostic/catch-up weeks exist for this.
