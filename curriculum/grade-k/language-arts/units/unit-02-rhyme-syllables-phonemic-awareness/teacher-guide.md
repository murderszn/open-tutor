# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Rhyme, Syllables, and Phonemic Awareness

Adult-facing. Learner materials never link here with answers visible; keep
this file and [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) out of the learner's reach.

## How this unit runs

Four 15–20-minute sessions per week. Every session opens with the same
30-second ritual — "ears on!" (learner cups hands behind ears, adult
models one slow word pair) — and closes with one sentence of specific
praise tied to the objective ("You caught that rhyme all by yourself").
This unit is **all ears**: no reading is required of the learner at any
point, which makes it the right place to build confidence before Unit 03's
letters arrive.

**Pacing judgment:** the [16-session pacing](README.md#16-session-pacing-4-weeks--4-sessions-15-20-minutes-each)
puts Lesson 06 and Project Part 2 together in Session 14. That works only
if Lesson 06 is trimmed to its core: the two block take-aparts (pencil,
window) plus the exit check. If the learner is tired, move Project Part 2
to the Session 12 re-teach slot and use Session 14 for the lesson alone —
the unit's design survives the swap.

## Session-by-session guidance

- **Sessions 1–4 (rhymes):** stretch every ending yourself for the first
  week. Children learn rhyme by hearing the exaggeration, not by hearing
  the rule. If the learner can already rhyme, move faster to production
  (Lesson 02) — recognition may need only one session.
- **Sessions 5–6 (rhyme families):** keep the generated illustration
  visible as the model family. The most common stall is forcing foils
  into families ("cow goes with dog — they're animals!"); redirect to
  sound every time, warmly.
- **Sessions 7–10 (syllables):** the jaw-drop cue (Lesson 03) is your
  best diagnostic — if claps and jaw drops disagree, trust the jaw and
  re-clap. Keep robot pauses to about one second; longer pauses break
  the blend.
- **Session 11/14 (project):** scribe the learner's dictated rhyming
  pairs verbatim, large print. The syllable dots go under each word as
  the learner claps — your pen follows their hands, not the other way
  around.
- **Session 13 (onset-rime):** always use **sounds**, never letter names
  ("/m/", not "em"). If you catch yourself saying a letter name,
  correct it aloud — the learner is watching.
- **Sessions 15–16 (check + assess):** do the quiz in the morning slot
  and the project share after a break. Never quiz a tired kindergartner
  and trust the result.

## Misconceptions — quick reference

| You see | It usually means | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| Calls *sun/cup* a rhyme | Matching beginnings, not endings | "Rhymes match at the **end**. Listen to the ends." |
| Offers *dog → dig* as a rhyme | Changed the ending, not the beginning | "The ending is the boss — keep -og. Change the beginning." |
| Claps 4 times for *puppy* | Counting sounds, not syllables | "Big parts, not tiny sounds: *pup-py* — two." |
| Echoes "ta ... ble" without blending | Good hearing, missing the melt | "Now say it fast, like one word — *table!*" |
| Says the letter name ("bee + at") | Letter-name habit | Model the sound /b/ aloud; correct yourself visibly |
| Splits *frog* and freezes | Two-sound onset confusion | Accept /f/ + -rog or /fr/ + -og; move on — phonemes are Unit 04 |
| Forces every card into a family | Sorting by meaning, not sound | "We sort by **sound** today — what do your ears say?" |
| One-clap words feel "wrong" | Assumes words need many parts | Celebrate one-clap words: "*Cat* is a strong one-clap word!" |

## Re-teach triggers

- **Rhyme recognition below 3 of 4** on the quiz → replay Practice
  Session B (Rhyme detective) across two days, stretching every ending.
- **No produced rhyme** → return to Lesson 02's recipe with heavy
  modeling: you keep the ending aloud, the learner supplies only the new
  beginning sound.
- **Syllable claps mismatch parts** → Lesson 03's jaw-drop cue for three
  sessions; drop to the learner's own name and 1–2 syllable words only.
- **Cannot blend** → shorten the robot pause; blend with the blocks
  pushed together as a visual; try known compound words (*cupcake*).
- **Cannot segment onset-rime** → stay at the syllable level one more
  week; onset-rime is the hardest skill here and may legitimately need
  the flexible week.

## Readiness for Unit 03

The learner is ready for Unit 03 (letter recognition and sound
correspondences) when: they recognize rhymes reliably (quiz item 1
secure), clap syllables in 1–3 syllable words (quiz item 3 secure), and
blend spoken syllables (quiz item 4 secure). Onset-rime segmenting may
still be developing — Unit 04 revisits word-part skills at the phoneme
level, and the flexible weeks exist for exactly this. Do not hold a
learner back from letters over onset-rime alone; do hold back if rhyme
recognition itself is not yet secure, since Unit 03's word families
(-at, -og) assume it.
