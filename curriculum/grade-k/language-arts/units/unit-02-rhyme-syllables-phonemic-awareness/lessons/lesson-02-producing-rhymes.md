# Lesson 02 — Rhyme Families and Picture Sorts

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can make up a word that rhymes, and I can
sort pictures into rhyme families."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** rhyme picture cards (cat, hat,
bat, dog, frog, log, pig, wig, sun, fun, run, bee, tree, plus foils: moon,
cow, fish); the generated illustration
[`assets/rhyme-family-cards.png`](../assets/rhyme-family-cards.png);
pocket chart or tabletop sorting mat.
**Standards:** RF.K.2, RF.K.2.a; SL.K.1.b.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Hold up the generated illustration. "Look at these three cards. The
pictures are a cat, a hat, and a bat. Do those words rhyme? How do you
know?" (Yes — same at the end, "-at.") This re-checks Lesson 01 and
introduces the cards as today's tools.

## Explicit teaching

Hearing rhymes is step one. Step two is **making** rhymes — thinking of a
word that sounds the same at the end. Watch how I do it.

I start with *dog*. I keep the ending "-og" in my ears and I try new
beginnings: *b-og ... bog!* That rhymes! *l-og ... log!* That rhymes too!
*Dog, bog, log* — a **rhyme family**. They all live in the same house
because their ends match.

Making a rhyme is like a recipe: **keep the ending, change the
beginning.** The ending is the boss — it must stay the same.

Sometimes the rhyme I make is a silly made-up word, like *zog*. That is
allowed! Rhyming is about **sound**, and *zog* sounds the same at the end
as *dog*. Real word or silly word — if the end matches, it rhymes.

### Modeled example 1 — making a rhyme

*Adult thinks aloud:* "My word is *pig*. Keep the ending '-ig' ... change
the beginning. *B-ig ... big!* Pig and big rhyme!" *(Points to the
illustration's -at family.)* "These cards are the -at family: cat, hat,
bat. Watch me add one: *s-at ... sat!* Cat, hat, bat, **sat** — the family
grows!"

### Modeled example 2 — sorting into families

*Adult lays out three 'house' cards — dog, sun, bee — and thinks aloud
while sorting:* "Here is *frog*. Does it belong with *dog*? *Frogg ...*
*dogg* — same at the end! Frog joins the -og family." *(Places frog with
dog.)* "Here is *moon*. With *sun*? *Moonnn ... sunnn* — different ends.
With *bee*? *Moonnn ... beee* — different. Moon does not belong in any
house today — it waits on the side. That is fine; not every word has a
family here."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. "My word is *cat*. Make a rhyme — keep '-at', change the beginning."
   (Accept: hat, bat, sat, mat, rat, or silly *zat*.)
2. "My word is *fun*. Make a rhyme." (Accept: sun, run, bun, or silly
   *dun*.)
3. Sort: adult holds up *log*. "Which family — dog, sun, or bee?" (Dog —
   '-og' family.)
4. Sort: adult holds up *tree*. "Which family?" (Bee — '-ee' family.)
5. Sort: adult holds up *cow*. "Which family?" (None — it waits on the
   side; praise the careful no-match.)

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

The learner works with the picture cards and the sorting mat; pointing
and placing are full answers.

1. Make a rhyme for *bed*. (Accept: red, fed, said-as-/sed/, or silly
   *med*.)
2. Make a rhyme for *cake*. (Accept: snake, bake, lake, take, or silly
   *zake*.)
3. Sort *wig* — which family: pig or sun? (Pig.)
4. Sort *fish* — which family: pig, sun, or bee? (None — waits on the
   side. Note: *fish/dish* is a real rhyme family, but *dish* is not on
   the mat today, so "none here" is the secure answer.)
5. **Challenge:** make a rhyme for *mop*. (Accept: top, hop, pop, cop, or
   silly *lop*.)

## Application

"Be the teacher." The learner builds one rhyme family of three or more
cards on the mat, names the family ("the -at family!"), and teaches a
stuffed animal the recipe: "Keep the ending, change the beginning." The
adult scribes the family words.

## Exit check

1. "Make a rhyme for *ball*." (Accept: tall, fall, call, wall, small
   pronounced to rhyme, or a silly rhyme.)
2. Point to *frog*: "Which family does it join — dog, sun, or bee?"
   (Dog.)
3. "What is the rhyme recipe?" (Keep the ending, change the beginning —
   any wording.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** teach *family/house* with a real picture of a
  house first; "the -at family lives in the -at house." Home-language
  rhymes welcome.
- **Alternate response mode:** learner may answer by placing cards, by
  pointing, or by saying only the new beginning sound ("buh!") while the
  adult supplies the ending.
- **Seated-table variant:** sort on the tabletop mat; the illustration
  stays visible as the model family.
- **Floor-play variant:** "family train" — the learner carries each card
  across the room to its family's 'house' (a hoop or taped square).
- **Extension:** the learner invents a two-line rhyming poem and dictates
  it ("The dog / sat on a log"); sort by **middle** sounds as a
  challenge ("Which family: *pin* or *pan* with *pig*?").

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Changing the ending instead of the beginning:* a child says *dog →
  dig*. Affirm the effort, then re-teach the recipe: "The ending is the
  boss — '-og' must stay. Change the **beginning**: d-og, l-og."
- *Only real words count:* children may insist silly words "don't count."
  State the rule explicitly: rhyming is about sound; silly words are
  legal and often the most fun.
- *Forcing every card into a family:* some children will place *cow*
  with *dog* "because they are animals." Praise the thinking, then
  redirect: "We are sorting by **sound**, not by what things are."
- *The illustration as the only family:* remind the learner the -at cards
  are one example family — today's mat builds new ones.
