# Lesson 03 — Clapping Syllables

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can clap the beats in a word — one clap for
each word part."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** hand drum or wooden spoon on a
pot; name cards for the learner and 2–3 family members/friends (adult
writes them).
**Standards:** RF.K.2, RF.K.2.b.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Clap a steady beat and say the learner's name in syllables: "Em-ma" (clap,
clap). "How many claps did your name get?" Count together. Try one more
name. You are mapping the idea that words have countable beats.

## Explicit teaching

Words are made of **beats** — small word parts called **syllables**. We
can hear the beats if we slow down and clap them.

Say *pencil* slowly: *pen ... cil.* Two beats! Clap them: *pen* (clap),
*cil* (clap). Two claps — two syllables.

Say *cat*: *cat.* One beat, one clap. Say *banana*: *ba-na-na.* Three
beats — three claps!

The rule: **one clap for each word part.** We clap the word the way we
say it — not too fast, not too slow. If your claps and your word parts do
not match, slow down and try again.

Names are great for practice because you already know how to say them.
*Josh-u-a* gets three claps. (Use the learner's real name and the names
on the cards.)

### Modeled example 1 — clapping a two-syllable word

*Adult says slowly and claps, thinking aloud:* "The word is *table*.
Slow down: *ta ... ble.* I hear two parts. Watch: *ta* (clap), *ble*
(clap). Two parts, two claps. *Table* has **two syllables**."

### Modeled example 2 — clapping a three-syllable word

*Adult:* "Now *elephant*. Slow: *el ... e ... phant.* Three parts!
*El* (clap), *e* (clap), *phant* (clap). Three claps — three syllables.
My jaw dropped three times — watch." *(Adult exaggerates the jaw drops;
the learner watches the mouth as a second cue.)*

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

Clap together first, then the learner leads.

1. *dog* — one clap. (1 syllable)
2. *puppy* — *pup-py*, two claps. (2)
3. *window* — *win-dow*, two claps. (2)
4. The learner's own name, slowed down. (Count together; accept the
   natural spoken syllables.)
5. *butterfly* — *but-ter-fly*, three claps. (3)

Cue the jaw-drop trick whenever claps and parts mismatch: "Watch my chin
— how many drops?"

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

The learner claps alone; the adult counts silently and notes the number.

1. *sun* (1)
2. *apple* — *ap-ple* (2)
3. *tiger* — *ti-ger* (2)
4. *banana* — *ba-na-na* (3)
5. *basket* — *bas-ket* (2)

## Application

"Be the teacher." The learner chooses any word (a toy's name, a food, an
animal), teaches a stuffed animal to clap its syllables, and announces
the count: "*Di-no-saur* — three!" The adult scribes the word with
hyphens between the parts exactly as the learner clapped it.

## Exit check

1. "Clap the syllables in *monkey*." (*mon-key*, 2 claps.)
2. "Clap the syllables in *egg*." (1 clap.)
3. "How many syllables in *tomato*?" (*to-ma-to*, 3 — claps or number
   accepted.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** teach *beat* with the drum first (one hit = one
  beat), then move the beat to hands. Home-language names and words
  welcome — syllables work in every language.
- **Alternate response mode:** stomp, tap the table, nod the head, or
  hold up fingers — any one-beat-per-part movement counts; no speech
  required.
- **Seated-table variant:** tap the table or tap knees; drum keeps the
  beat.
- **Floor-play variant:** "syllable stomp" — stomp one step per syllable
  across the room saying the word slowly.
- **Extension:** sort words by syllable count (1-clap, 2-clap, 3-clap
  piles); try a four-syllable word (*el-e-va-tor* — accept 4 or the
  natural 4); clap the beats in a whole short sentence.

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Clapping sounds instead of syllables:* a child claps 4 times for
  *puppy* (hearing p-u-p-y). Re-teach the unit of counting: "We clap
  **word parts**, not tiny sounds. *Pup-py* — two big parts." The tiny
  sounds come in Unit 04.
- *Clapping on rhythm, not parts:* fast chanting produces extra claps.
  Slow the word to a crawl: "Say it like a sleepy turtle."
- *Merging parts in fast speech:* *banana* becomes "b'nana" (2 beats).
  Accept the natural pronunciation first, then model the careful 3-beat
  version: "Say it slowly with me: ba-na-na."
- *One-syllable doubt:* children sometimes think every word must have
  more than one part. Celebrate one-clap words: "*Cat* is a strong
  one-clap word!"
