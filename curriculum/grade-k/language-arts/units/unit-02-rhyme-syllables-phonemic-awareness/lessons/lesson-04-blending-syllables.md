# Lesson 04 — Robot Talk: Putting Syllables Together

**Goal (learner hears):** "When I hear word parts said slowly, I can push
them together into the whole word."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** a toy robot, robot picture, or
the adult's best robot voice; 4–6 familiar objects or pictures
(pencil, table, apple, basket, turtle, window).
**Standards:** RF.K.2, RF.K.2.b.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

In a robot voice, say the learner's name in parts: "Em ... ma." Pause.
"What word did the robot say?" If the learner blends it, celebrate:
"Your ears pushed the parts together!" If not, model it once — today's
whole job is this skill.

## Explicit teaching

Yesterday we took words apart into beats. Today we do the opposite: we
**blend** — we push the parts back together into the whole word.

Robots talk in parts: "pen ... cil." Our job is to be the un-robot: we
hear the parts and we say the word fast and smooth: *pencil!*

Here is the trick: **say the parts, then say them fast.** *Ta ... ble.
Ta-ble. Table!* The parts melt together like ice melting into water —
you cannot see where one part ends anymore.

Blending is what your brain will do later when you read: you will see
parts, and your brain will push them into words. Today we practice with
our ears.

### Modeled example 1 — blending two parts

*Adult in robot voice, then thinks aloud in a normal voice:* "Robot says:
'win ... dow.' Hmm. Say the parts: win-dow. Say them fast: window!
*Window!*" *(Adult points to the window.)* "My ears pushed 'win' and
'dow' together and found the word hiding inside."

### Modeled example 2 — blending three parts

*Adult:* "Robot says: 'el ... e ... phant.' Three parts! Say them: el-e-phant.
Say them fast: elephant!" *(Adult trumpets an arm like a trunk.)* "Even
with three parts, we can push them together. The trick is the same: say
the parts, then say them fast."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

Adult does robot voice; learner blends. Point to the object or picture
after each success.

1. "ap ... ple" → *apple*
2. "bas ... ket" → *basket*
3. "tur ... tle" → *turtle*
4. "pup ... py" → *puppy*
5. "ba ... na ... na" → *banana* (three parts — cue "say the parts,
   then say them fast")

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

The learner hears the robot and says the whole word. Pointing to the
matching object or picture counts as a full answer.

1. "ta ... ble" → *table*
2. "mon ... key" → *monkey*
3. "rab ... bit" → *rabbit*
4. "ti ... ger" → *tiger*
5. "sock ... et" → *socket* (accept "sock-it" pronounced as a word; if
   unknown, show the picture and name it first)

## Application

"Be the teacher." The learner becomes the robot: picks an object or
picture, says it in parts ("cup ... cake"), and the stuffed animal
(voiced by the adult, deliberately slow at first) blends it. Then they
swap — the adult is the robot and the learner blends. The adult scribes
one robot-said word with hyphens and the blended word beside it.

## Exit check

1. Robot: "win ... dow." What word? (*Window.*)
2. Robot: "el ... e ... phant." What word? (*Elephant.*)
3. "What is the blending trick?" (Say the parts, then say them fast —
   any wording.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** demonstrate *push together* with hands first —
  two fists apart, then pushed together — before applying it to sounds.
  Robot talk works in any home language.
- **Alternate response mode:** learner may point to the matching object
  or picture instead of saying the word; may whisper the blend.
- **Seated-table variant:** robot voice at the table; objects lined up
  for pointing.
- **Floor-play variant:** "blend and find" — the robot says the parts
  and the learner runs to touch the matching object across the room.
- **Extension:** learner blends without the pause ("pencil" said
  half-split: "peh-ncil"); blend compound words the learner knows
  (*cup-cake*, *sun-shine*); the learner segments AND blends back ("say
  *table* in parts... now push them back").

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Repeating the parts instead of blending:* the child echoes "ta ...
  ble" proudly. That is good hearing! Now cue the melt: "Now say it
  fast, like one word — *table!*"
- *Blending only the last part:* "…ble? *Bleh!*" The child drops the
  first part. Re-say both parts with a hand sweep joining them, and
  have the child sweep too.
- *Long pauses break the blend:* if the robot waits too long between
  parts, the word falls apart. Keep parts about one second apart.
- *Unknown vocabulary blocks blending:* a child cannot blend *socket*
  if they have never heard it. Pre-teach unfamiliar words with the
  picture first — blending tests hearing, not vocabulary.
