# Lesson 06 — Two-Syllable Take-Apart

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can take a two-syllable word apart into its
two parts — and put it back together."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** two blocks, two beanbags, or two
cushions (one per syllable); picture cards: pencil, table, puppy, window,
basket, turtle, monkey, rabbit.
**Standards:** RF.K.2, RF.K.2.b.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

"Clap *pencil* with me: *pen-cil* — two claps!" Then: "Now say it the
way the robot would: *pen ... cil.* What word?" (*Pencil.*) You are
re-checking Lessons 03–04; today adds the take-apart direction for
two-syllable words.

## Explicit teaching

We can clap syllables (Lesson 03) and we can blend them (Lesson 04).
Today we learn the third move: **take apart** — hear a whole word and
split it into its syllables.

Listen: *window*. Take it apart: *win ... dow.* Two parts! I can show
the parts with my blocks — one block for *win*, one block for *dow*.
Push them apart: taken apart. Push them together: *window* — blended
back!

So now we have the whole toolbox:

- **Count** the parts (clap them) — Lesson 03
- **Blend** the parts into the word — Lesson 04
- **Take apart** the word into its parts — today

Watch out for sneaky words: *turtle* sounds like it might be one part,
but slow down — *tur ... tle* — two! And *basket*: *bas ... ket* — two.
Slow and turtle-slow always wins.

### Modeled example 1 — taking apart

*Adult places two blocks together, then thinks aloud:* "The word is
*puppy*. Take it apart: *pup ... py.*" *(Slides the blocks apart, one
per part.)* "Two parts! Block one says *pup*, block two says *py.*
And I can put it back: *pup-py* ... *puppy!*" *(Slides blocks together.)*

### Modeled example 2 — the sneaky one

*Adult:* "Now *turtle*. Hmm — is it one part or two? Slow down:
*tuuurr ... tle.* I hear two! *Tur* (block one), *tle* (block two).
*Turtle* has two syllables — it was hiding the second one!" *(Adult
checks by blending back: "tur-tle ... turtle — yes!")*

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

Adult and learner move the blocks together.

1. *table* → *ta ... ble* (2 parts)
2. *monkey* → *mon ... key* (2 parts)
3. *rabbit* → *rab ... bit* (2 parts)
4. *basket* → *bas ... ket* (2 parts)
5. *apple* → *ap ... ple* (2 parts)

After each take-apart, blend it back together to prove the parts are
right.

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes; 5 tasks)

The learner moves the blocks alone and says the parts.

1. *pencil* → *pen ... cil*
2. *window* → *win ... dow*
3. *turtle* → *tur ... tle*
4. *candle* → *can ... dle* (accept *can-dle*; pre-teach the word with a
   picture if unknown)
5. **Challenge:** *banana* → *ba ... na ... na* (3 parts — "even the
   toolbox works for three!")

## Application

"Be the teacher." The learner picks a picture card, takes the word
apart with the blocks for a stuffed animal, then asks the animal to
blend it back. If the animal (adult-voiced) blends it wrong ("*pen-sill?*"),
the learner corrects with the blocks: "No — *pen-cil ... pencil!*" The
adult scribes one take-apart with hyphens (pen-cil) exactly as the
learner split it.

## Exit check

1. "Take apart *puppy*." (*Pup ... py.*)
2. "Take apart *turtle*." (*Tur ... tle.*)
3. "What are our three syllable moves?" (Count/clap, blend together,
   take apart — any wording.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** teach *take apart / put together* with the blocks
  first (a tower), then with words. Every home language has syllables —
  practice in both.
- **Alternate response mode:** learner moves the blocks and points
  without speaking; the adult says the parts aloud for confirmation.
- **Seated-table variant:** blocks on the table; cards lined up.
- **Floor-play variant:** "syllable steps" — take one big step per part
  while saying it, then jump back together for the blend.
- **Extension:** take apart three-syllable words (*el-e-phant*,
  *to-ma-to*); find the **one**-syllable words hiding in a list
  ("Which of these has only one part: *sun, pencil, dog*?"); sort cards
  into 1-, 2-, and 3-syllable piles.

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Splitting into sounds instead of syllables:* the child says
  *p-e-n-c-i-l*. Redirect to the bigger parts: "We want the **big**
  parts — *pen ... cil.* The tiny sounds come in Unit 04."
- *Hearing one part in a two-syllable word:* *turtle* → "turtle" (one).
  Slow to turtle-speed and use the jaw-drop cue from Lesson 03 — two
  drops, two parts.
- *Adding a phantom syllable:* *window* → "win-dow-uh" (3). This comes
  from over-careful speech. Say the word naturally first, then split
  what you actually said.
- *Forgetting to blend back:* taking apart without re-blending leaves
  the word in pieces. Always close the loop: "Now put it back —
  *pencil!*"
