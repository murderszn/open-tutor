# Lesson 05 — The Beginning and the Rest

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can split a word into its beginning sound
and the rest — and I can put them back together."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** two hand puppets, stuffed
animals, or simply the adult's two hands (one = beginning, one = rest);
picture cards: cat, dog, mop, sun, fish, pig.
**Standards:** RF.K.2, RF.K.2.c.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

"Say *cat* slowly: *cccaaat.* What sound does it **start** with?" (/c/.)
"And what is the **rest** — the part after the beginning?" (*at.*) You
are checking whether the learner can already feel the split; most will
need today's teaching, and that is expected.

## Explicit teaching

Every short word has two parts hiding inside: the **beginning sound**
(called the *onset*) and **the rest** (called the *rime*).

Take *dog*. The beginning sound is /d/ — just the first puff of sound.
The rest is *-og*. /d/ ... *-og.* Beginning and rest.

We can do two jobs with these parts:

1. **Put together (blend):** hear "/m/ ... *-op*" and say *mop!*
2. **Take apart (segment):** hear *sun* and say "/s/ ... *-un.*"

The beginning is usually one sound. The rest is the vowel and everything
after it. Watch my mouth: for the beginning I barely open it (*/m/* —
lips together); for the rest my mouth opens wide (*-op*).

Why does this matter? The rest — the rime — is the same part that makes
rhymes! *Cat, hat, bat* all share the rime *-at*. When you can hear the
rime, you can hear rhymes AND you are one step closer to reading.

### Modeled example 1 — blending beginning + rest

*Adult holds up one hand for the beginning, one for the rest, then
thinks aloud:* "Beginning: /f/. Rest: *-ish.* Put them together: /f/ ...
*-ish* ... *fish!*" *(Hands clap together on the blend.)* "One more:
beginning /p/, rest *-ig*: /p/ ... *-ig* ... *pig!*"

### Modeled example 2 — segmenting into beginning + rest

*Adult holds up the sun card:* "The word is *sun*. Take it apart. The
beginning sound is /s/." *(One hand lifts.)* "The rest is *-un.*"
*(Other hand lifts.)* "/s/ ... *-un.* Beginning and rest — I took it
apart!" *(Adult reverses:)* "And I can put it back: /s/ + *-un* = *sun.*"

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 6 tasks)

Blend jobs first (adult gives parts, learner blends), then take-apart
jobs.

1. Blend: "/d/ + *-og*" → *dog*
2. Blend: "/m/ + *-op*" → *mop*
3. Blend: "/c/ + *-at*" → *cat*
4. Take apart: *pig* → "/p/ + *-ig*"
5. Take apart: *fish* → "/f/ + *-ish*"
6. Take apart: *sun* → "/s/ + *-un*"

Cue with the two hands every time; fade the hands as the learner gets
it.

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 6 tasks)

The learner may use the hand gestures, point to cards, or just speak.

1. Blend: "/b/ + *-at*" → *bat*
2. Blend: "/l/ + *-og*" → *log*
3. Blend: "/t/ + *-op*" → *top*
4. Take apart: *dog* → "/d/ + *-og*"
5. Take apart: *mop* → "/m/ + *-op*"
6. **Challenge:** take apart *frog* → "/f/ + *-rog*" — accept "/fr/ +
   *-og*" too (both are defensible; note which the learner hears)

## Application

"Be the teacher." The learner picks a picture card, shows it to a
stuffed animal, takes the word apart ("*/c/ ... -at*"), and then the
animal (adult-voiced) tries to blend it — getting it wrong once on
purpose ("*/c/ + -at = ... cut?*") so the learner can correct: "No —
*cat!* The rest is *-at*!" The adult scribes the split (c + at) and the
correction.

## Exit check

1. "Blend: /s/ + *-un*." (*Sun.*)
2. "Take apart: *fish*." (*/f/ + -ish.*)
3. "Which part makes the rhyme in *cat, hat, bat*?" (The rest — *-at*;
   "the ending" accepted.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** teach *beginning/rest* with a real object first
  (the beginning of a crayon vs. the rest of it), then move to sounds.
- **Alternate response mode:** learner shows the split with two hands
  (or two blocks pushed apart/together) without speaking; pointing to
  the correct card counts for blends.
- **Seated-table variant:** cards on the table; hands do the splitting.
- **Floor-play variant:** "sound jump" — jump once for the beginning,
  land-and-say for the rest ("*/m/* ... *-op* — MOP!").
- **Extension:** change the beginning to make rhymes ("/c/ + -at/ →
  *cat*; now /h/ + *-at* → ?"); sort cards by shared rime; try a word
  with no onset (*at* — "no beginning, just rest!").

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Saying the letter name instead of the sound:* "buh + at = *bat*"
  becomes "bee + at." Model the **sound** /b/, not the letter name —
  this unit is all ears, no letters. (Letter names return in Unit 03.)
- *Splitting into syllables instead of onset-rime:* the child says
  "*cat*" is one part and stops. Affirm ("Yes — one syllable!"), then
  teach the smaller split: "Now split it even smaller: the very first
  sound, then the rest."
- *Over-stretching the beginning:* "/mmmmoooop/." Teach the beginning
  as a quick puff, then the rest stretched — the contrast is the point.
- *Frog-type blends:* /fr/ is two sounds acting as one beginning. Accept
  either split and move on — do not turn this into a phonics lesson;
  individual phonemes are Unit 04's work.
