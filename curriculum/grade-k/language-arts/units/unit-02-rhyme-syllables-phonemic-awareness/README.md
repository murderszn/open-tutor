# Unit 02 — Rhyme, Syllables, and Phonemic Awareness

**Grade:** Kindergarten · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #8](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/8), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Before children connect letters to sounds, they learn to *hear* the sounds
inside spoken words. In this unit the learner plays with the music of
language: recognizing and producing rhyming words in songs, poems, and
picture sorts; clapping and counting syllables in names and words; blending
spoken syllables back into words ("pen-cil" → *pencil*); and splitting and
joining the beginning sound and the rest of a word (/c/ + /at/ → *cat*).
Every skill is oral, physical, and playful — ears and mouths first, eyes on
print only as a support.

## Essential question

How do the sounds inside spoken words fit together, and how can playing
with those sounds help us become readers?

## Prerequisites

- Talks in multi-word sentences; follows two-step oral directions
  (diagnostic weeks 1–2, Unit 01)
- Can listen to a 10-minute read-aloud and join a talk circle with the
  Unit 01 discussion rules (listen, take turns, stay on the topic)
- Fine-motor readiness: holds a crayon, claps hands on beat

No letter knowledge or reading is required — this unit is entirely oral
and aural. Print appears only on picture cards the adult reads.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed; all
responses may be oral, pointing, gesturing, clapping, or drawing,
adult-scribed):

1. Recognize rhyming words: tell whether two spoken words rhyme, and pick
   the word that does not rhyme from a set of three.
2. Produce a rhyming word for a given word (a real word or a sensible
   made-up word is accepted), using picture cards or word play.
3. Count syllables in spoken words — clap, stomp, or tap one beat per
   syllable — for one-, two-, and three-syllable words, including the
   learner's own name.
4. Blend spoken syllables into a word (e.g., hears "ta-ble" and says
   *table*).
5. Blend and segment onsets and rimes of single-syllable spoken words
   (e.g., hears /d/ + /og/ and says *dog*; says *sun* as /s/ + /un/);
   segment two-syllable words into their syllables (e.g., "pup-py").
6. Join a talk circle about sound play: follow discussion rules, take
   turns, and share one rhyme or word-part discovery.

## Vocabulary (taught with voices, bodies, and pictures first, word second)

clap · beat · blend (put together) · take apart · onset (beginning sound) ·
rime (the rest of the word) · song · poem · sort · same · different ·

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials
list): rhyme picture cards (homemade drawings or cutouts — cat, hat, bat,
dog, frog, log, pig, wig, sun, fun, run, bee, tree, plus 6–8 foils such as
moon, cow, fish), a pocket chart or tabletop sorting mat, hand drum or
wooden spoon on a pot (beat-keeping), the talking stick from Unit 01,
crayons and drawing paper, 4 large sheets of paper for the project, the
generated illustration
[`assets/rhyme-family-cards.png`](assets/rhyme-family-cards.png) shown on a
screen or printed large. No sharp tools; adult supervises small parts in
shared settings. Songs and poems are previewed by the adult for age
suitability.

Sessions rotate as: lesson → song/practice → game → review, matching the

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, gesturing, clapping, or drawing
responses; the adult scribes. All core skills are auditory — no reading is
required of the learner, which removes the print barrier entirely. Color is
never the only cue (rhyme families also differ by picture content and
position; the illustration's words are large, high-contrast print). Each
lesson includes a seated-table and a floor-play variant, a language-access
support, an alternate response mode, and an extension. Hearing support:
face the learner when modeling sounds; exaggerate mouth shapes; pair every
listening task with a visual or movement cue (clap, card, gesture). The
generated illustration ships with a text-only alternative containing every
word the activity needs.
