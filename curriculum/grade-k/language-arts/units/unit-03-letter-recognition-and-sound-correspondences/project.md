# Project — My Alphabet Sound Book

**Type:** creation project across Sessions 11, 14, and 15.
**Driving question:** "Can I make a book that shows every letter, its
sound, and a word that starts with that sound — and read it to
someone else?"

The learner authors a personal alphabet book: one page per letter
group, each page showing the letters (upper- and lowercase), a drawn
picture of an example word, and the letter's sound written as the
adult scribes the learner's dictation ("B says /b/ like ball"). The
book is then read aloud to an audience (adult, family member, or
stuffed-animal audience voiced by the adult) and shared in a talk
circle.

## Materials

8–10 large sheets of paper (or folded chart paper), crayons and
markers, a stapler or binder clips (adult use), the letter cards from
the lessons, the generated illustration
[`assets/sound-sort-animals.png`](assets/sound-sort-animals.png) as a
model for the animal pages, the talking stick.

## Part 1 — Consonant pages (Session 11)

1. Choose the page groups: (1) A–D, (2) E–H, (3) I–M, (4) N–Q,
   (5) R–U, (6) V–Z. Six pages keep the book finishable; one letter
   per line on each page is fine — the learner does not need a
   separate page per letter.
2. For each letter on a page, the learner writes or traces the
   uppercase and lowercase forms (adult models the formation; the
   learner's attempt counts, not perfection) and draws one picture
   of an example word. Suggested example words (the learner may
   substitute any sensible word with the right first sound):
   A — apple (vowel page, but included here as the alphabet's first
   letter), B — ball, C — cat, D — dog, E — egg, F — fish, G —
   goat, H — hat, I — igloo, J — jam, K — kite, L — lion, M —
   moon, N — nose, O — octopus, P — pig, Q — queen, R — rabbit,
   S — sun, T — turtle, U — umbrella, V — violin, W — web, X —
   fox (drawn at the end of the word — the adult notes "X is at the
   end of fox"), Y — yes (drawn as a nodding head or a "yes" sign),
   Z — zebra.
3. The adult scribes under each picture: the letter name, the sound,
   and the word — for example, "B says /b/ like ball."
4. Number the pages 1–6 together (adult writes; learner points and
   counts).

## Part 2 — Vowel pages and cover (Session 14)

1. Add a seventh page: "The Five Vowels." For each vowel, the
   learner draws the short-sound picture (apple, egg, igloo,
   octopus, umbrella) and the long-sound picture (cake, bee, bike,
   rope, unicorn) — ten small drawings, or five pairs if time is
   short. The adult scribes "A: short /a/ apple, long /ā/ cake"
   and so on for each vowel.
2. Make the front cover: title "My Alphabet Sound Book," the
   learner's first-name initial (or a drawn self-portrait — no
   full names or photos in the shared book), and a favorite animal
   from the generated illustration.
3. Make the back cover: the learner's hardest letter, drawn big,
   with its sound and word — "I am still learning Q, /kw/, queen"
   is a proud back cover, not a confession.
4. Bind the book (adult staples or clips); the learner puts the
   pages in order, front to back.

## Part 3 — Share (Session 15, with the formative quiz)

Talk-circle share: the learner presents the book (holds it correctly,
reads the letter names and sounds for three pages of the adult's
choosing, and reads the vowel page), then takes two questions from
the audience (adult, family member, or stuffed-animal "audience"
voiced by the adult), following talk rules. The adult scribes one
audience question and the learner's answer onto the back cover as a
souvenir.

## Deliverables

1. The alphabet sound book (cover, numbered pages, upper- and
   lowercase letters, drawn pictures, scribed name/sound/word lines,
   vowel page, back-cover hardest letter).
2. An observed performance: reads letter names and sounds for three
   pages and the vowel page, and answers two audience questions
   following talk rules.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (3) | Developing (2) | Starting (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Letter naming | Names 20+ of the 26 letters shown, in and out of order | Names 13–19 letters, mostly in order | Names fewer than 13, or only in song order |
| Sound matching | Says the correct primary sound for 20+ letters, including all five vowels' short sounds | Says the correct sound for 13–19 letters | Says sounds for fewer than 13, or gives names instead of sounds |
| Pictures and words | Every page has a picture whose word starts with the page's letter (X excepted: fox ends with /ks/) | Most pages match; 1–3 mismatches | Several pictures do not match their letters |
| Book care and order | Pages numbered and in order, cover and back cover complete | Pages mostly in order; one cover element missing | Pages unordered or covers missing |
| Sharing | Reads three pages and the vowel page, answers two questions, follows talk rules | Reads with heavy prompting or answers one question | Cannot present without the adult reading for them |

**Scoring:** 13–15 = ready for Unit 04; 9–12 = ready with a
Session 12-style re-teach of the weakest criterion; 5–8 = repeat the
project's weakest pages in a flexible week before Unit 04. The rubric
is observational, not a grade; the adult records evidence, not
scores, in private storage.
