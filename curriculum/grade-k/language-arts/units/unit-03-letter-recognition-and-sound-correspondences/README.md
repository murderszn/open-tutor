# Unit 03 — Letter Recognition and Sound Correspondences

**Grade:** Kindergarten · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #8](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/8), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

In Unit 01 the learner met the uppercase letters A through M. In Unit 02
the learner trained ears, not eyes: rhymes, syllables, and word parts.
Now ears and eyes meet. This unit finishes the alphabet — every
uppercase letter and every lowercase letter, named in order and out of
order — and attaches a sound to each letter: the primary sound of every
consonant, and the long and short sounds of the five major vowels. By
the end, the learner can hear a sound and find its letter, see a letter
and say its sound, and write letters for most consonant and short-vowel
sounds. That is the bridge Unit 04 will walk across into blending and
reading CVC words.

## Essential question

How does each letter help us hear, say, and write a sound — and how can
knowing all the letters and their sounds help us become readers?

## Prerequisites

- Names uppercase letters A–M in order and out of order (Unit 01)
- Follows talk-circle rules: listen, take turns, stay on the topic
  (Units 01–02)
- Can hear rhymes and syllables and blend spoken word parts
  (Unit 02: "pen-cil" → *pencil*; /c/ + /at/ → *cat*)
- Holds a crayon or pencil and traces lines and letter-like marks
  (diagnostic weeks 1–2, Unit 01)

No reading is required at the start of this unit. Print appears on
letter cards the adult reads and models; the learner's job is naming
letters, saying sounds, matching them, and forming them.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed; all
responses may be oral, pointing, or drawing, adult-scribed, except for
the letter-formation tasks in objective 5, which the learner attempts
independently with a crayon or pencil):

1. Name all 26 uppercase letters, A–Z, in alphabet order and in mixed
   order, from large-print cards shown one at a time.
2. Name all 26 lowercase letters, a–z, in alphabet order and in mixed
   order, from large-print cards shown one at a time.
3. Say the primary sound for each consonant when shown its letter
   (for example, B says /b/, M says /m/, S says /s/), and say the
   letter name and the sound together ("B, /b/").
4. Say the long and short sounds of the five major vowels (A, E, I, O,
   U) and match each sound to a spoken example word (short: *apple*,
   *egg*, *igloo*, *octopus*, *umbrella*; long: *cake*, *bee*, *bike*,
   *rope*, *unicorn*).
5. Match letters to sounds in any order — hear a sound and point to
   its letter, and see a letter and say its sound — and write a letter
   (upper- or lowercase) for most consonant and short-vowel sounds the
   adult dictates (at least 15 of the 21 consonants and at least 3 of
   the 5 short vowels, formed recognizably).

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for English Language
Arts**, kindergarten, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/)
(descriptions verified against the official kindergarten text during the
track audit, 2026-10-02; no state adoption or accreditation is claimed).

| Unit objective | Standard | Official description (kindergarten) |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 2 | RF.K.1.d | Recognize and name all upper- and lowercase letters of the alphabet. (Unit 01 covered uppercase A–M; this unit completes A–Z and all lowercase letters.) |
| 3 | RF.K.3.a | Demonstrate basic knowledge of one-to-one letter-sound correspondences by producing the primary sound or many of the most frequent sounds for each consonant. |
| 4 | RF.K.3.b | Associate the long and short sounds with the common spellings (graphemes) for the five major vowels. |
| 5 (matching) | RF.K.3.a, RF.K.3.b | As above; matching in both directions (sound → letter, letter → sound) is the unit's check that the correspondence is known, not just recited. |
| 5 (writing) | L.K.2.c | Write a letter or letters for most consonant and short-vowel sounds (phonemes). |
| All (talk) | SL.K.1 / SL.K.1.a | Participate in collaborative conversations ... Follow agreed-upon rules for discussions (e.g., listening to others and taking turns speaking). |

**Sound notes the adult must keep straight (see the teacher guide for
detail):** the "primary" consonant sounds taught here are the most
frequent ones: C says /k/ and G says /g/ in this unit; their other
frequent sounds (C as /s/ in *cent*, G as /j/ in *gem*) are named as
"later sounds," not tested. Q is taught with U as /kw/ (*quack*). X
says /ks/ (*box*, *fox*). Y is taught as a consonant /y/ (*yes*) in
this unit; its vowel jobs come later. Long-vowel spellings are kept to
the K level: the vowel "says its name," including the
vowel-consonant-*e* pattern (*cake*, *bike*, *rope*) and, for E, the
*ee* pattern (*bee*).

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials
list): uppercase letter cards A–Z and lowercase letter cards a–z
(large print, high contrast — make one set per case, or one double-sided
set), a pocket chart or tabletop alphabet arc (letters in order along a
curve the learner can walk or point along), magnetic letters or letter
tiles, a small mirror (mouth shapes for sounds), crayons, pencils, and
drawing paper, a simple picture-word set for the sound sorts (cat, dog,
pig, frog, sun, mop, bed, fish, and the generated illustration below),
the talking stick from Unit 01, and the generated illustration
[`assets/sound-sort-animals.png`](assets/sound-sort-animals.png) shown on
a screen or printed large. No sharp tools; adult supervises small parts
in shared settings. Songs and alphabet books are previewed by the adult
for age suitability and advertising-free access.

## 16-session pacing (4 weeks × 4 sessions, 15–20 minutes each)

Sessions rotate as: lesson → practice → game → review, matching the
track session model. The project runs across Sessions 11 and 14, with
the share in Session 15.

| Session | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lesson | [L1 — The whole uppercase alphabet](lessons/lesson-01-uppercase-alphabet.md): A–Z in order, then mixed |
| 2 | Practice | [Alphabet arc practice](practice.md#session-a-alphabet-arc-practice) — walk or point the arc, naming letters |
| 3 | Game | [Letter hunt game](practice.md#session-b-letter-hunt-game) — find a named letter in a mixed pile |
| 4 | Review | [Uppercase review](practice.md#session-c-uppercase-review) + L1 exit re-checks |
| 5 | Lesson | [L2 — The lowercase alphabet](lessons/lesson-02-lowercase-alphabet.md): a–z, matching upper/lower pairs, tricky pairs b/d and p/q |
| 6 | Practice | [Pair-match practice](practice.md#session-d-pair-match-practice) — match each lowercase card to its uppercase partner |
| 7 | Game | [Tricky-pair game](practice.md#session-e-tricky-pair-game) — tell b from d, p from q, by belly and stick |
| 8 | Review | [Case review](practice.md#session-f-case-review) + L1–L2 exit re-checks |
| 9 | Lesson | [L3 — Consonant sounds, A to M](lessons/lesson-03-consonant-sounds-a-to-m.md): primary sounds for B, C, D, F, G, H, J, K, L, M |
| 10 | Practice | [Sound-sort practice](practice.md#session-g-sound-sort-practice) — uses the generated illustration: sort cat, dog, pig, frog by first sound |
| 11 | Project | [My Alphabet Sound Book](project.md), Part 1 — consonant pages A–M |
| 12 | Review | Re-teach highest-need objective (see [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md#re-teach-triggers)) |
| 13 | Lesson | [L4 — Consonant sounds, N to Z](lessons/lesson-04-consonant-sounds-n-to-z.md): primary sounds for N, P, Q, R, S, T, V, W, X, Y, Z, plus [L5 — Short vowel sounds](lessons/lesson-05-short-vowel-sounds.md) (combined core session: L4 core + L5 short-vowel chant and sort) |
| 14 | Lesson + Project | [L6 — Long vowel sounds and letter–sound match](lessons/lesson-06-long-vowel-sounds-and-letter-sound-match.md) + [My Alphabet Sound Book](project.md), Part 2 — vowel pages and cover |
| 15 | Check | [Formative quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md) (oral/pointing/writing, adult-scribed where noted) + project share |
| 16 | Assessment | [Unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md) (performance tasks) + re-teach planning |

Weeks 11–13 introduce and practice; Week 14 (track week 14) is the
review week: sound games, re-teaching, the formative check, and the
culminating assessment. If the learner is not yet secure on an
objective after Session 16, use the diagnostic/catch-up guidance in the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) before starting Unit 04 — the track's
flexible weeks exist for exactly this. Note on Session 13: L4 and L5
share one session by design — L4 teaches the second-half consonant
sounds briskly (the routine is already familiar from L3) and L5 adds
only the five short-vowel chants and one sort; the full vowel work
lands in Session 14 (L6) and the project. If the learner tires, move
L5's sort to the Session 12 re-teach slot and keep Session 13 to L4
alone; the unit's design survives the swap.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, or drawing responses; the adult
scribes dictated words. Letter cards are large print, high contrast,
and color is never the only cue (the tricky-pair lessons use belly
position and stick direction, not color alone). Each lesson includes a
seated-table and a floor-play variant, a language-access support, an
alternate response mode, and an extension. The generated illustration
ships with a text-only alternative containing every animal name and
first sound the activity needs. Mirror work and mouth-shape cues
supplement auditory discrimination for every sound lesson.
