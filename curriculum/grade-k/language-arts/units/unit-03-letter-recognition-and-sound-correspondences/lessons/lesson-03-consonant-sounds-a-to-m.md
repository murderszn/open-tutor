# Lesson 03 — Consonant Sounds, A to M

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can say the sound for each consonant
letter from A to M — and say the letter name and the sound together."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** letter cards for B, C, D, F,
G, H, J, K, L, M (upper- and lowercase), a small mirror, picture cards
or objects for: ball, cat, dog, fish, goat, hat, jam, kite, lion, moon.
**Standards:** RF.K.3.a; SL.K.1.a (talk rules).

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Point to a picture of a cat. Ask: "What sound does *cat* start with?"
Stretch it: "/k/ ... cat." Then: "Which letter says /k/?" Accept any
guess, then show C and K together and say: "Two letters can say /k/.
Today we learn the first half of the consonant sounds — and C is one
of them."

## Explicit teaching

Letters that are not vowels are called **consonants**. Most letters are
consonants. Every consonant has a **sound** — the noise it makes at the
start of a word. The sound is not the same as the name: the name of B
is "bee," but the sound of B is /b/, like the start of *ball*.

Here are the primary sounds for the consonants in the first half of the
alphabet. Say each one as **name, sound, word**: "B, /b/, ball." The
sound is stretched or repeated so the ear can catch it: /b/ is a short
pop; /m/ and /s/ can hum or hiss for a long time.

| Letter | Primary sound | Example word | How to help the mouth |
|---|---|---|---|
| B | /b/ | *ball* | Lips pop open |
| C | /k/ | *cat* | Back of the tongue coughs softly |
| D | /d/ | *dog* | Tongue taps behind the teeth |
| F | /f/ | *fish* | Top teeth on bottom lip, blow out |
| G | /g/ | *goat* | Back of the tongue, like /k/ but with voice |
| H | /h/ | *hat* | A puff of air, like a sigh |
| J | /j/ | *jam* | Like /d/ with a buzz: "juh" |
| K | /k/ | *kite* | Same sound as C in *cat* |
| L | /l/ | *lion* | Tongue tip up behind the teeth, voice on |
| M | /m/ | *moon* | Lips closed, hum |

Two honest notes, kept short. First, **C and G have later sounds**:
C also says /s/ in *cent* and G also says /j/ in *gem* — we name them
as "later sounds" and do not test them in this unit. Second, **A, E,
I, O, U are vowels**, not consonants; they get their own lessons
(Lessons 05 and 06). The letters in this lesson are the consonants
whose names fall between A and M.

Say the sound, not the letter name, when you play sound games. "B says
/bee/" is a name; "B says /b/" is a sound. If you catch yourself
saying the name, correct it aloud — the learner is watching.

### Modeled example 1 — name, sound, word with the mirror

*Adult holds up M, looks in the mirror with the learner, and thinks
aloud:*

"M. The name is 'em.' The sound is /m/ — watch my lips: closed, and
the hum comes through my nose. /mmmm/ ... moon. M, /m/, moon." *(Learner
copies the mirror work and repeats: "M, /m/, moon.")*

"Now F. The name is 'eff.' The sound is /f/ — top teeth on my bottom
lip, and I blow: /fffff/ ... fish. F, /f/, fish." *(Learner copies.)*

### Modeled example 2 — hearing the sound in a word

*Adult says three words slowly and thinks aloud:*

"Listen: *dog, duck, door.* What sound starts all three? /d/, /d/,
/d/ — the tongue taps behind my teeth. Which letter says /d/? D!
D, /d/, dog."

"Now listen: *hat, hen, hop.* What sound starts all three? /h/ — a
puff of air. Which letter says /h/? H! H, /h/, hat."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Name–sound–word chant for B, C, D, F together with the adult:
   "B, /b/, ball. C, /k/, cat. D, /d/, dog. F, /f/, fish."
2. Name–sound–word chant for G, H, J, K together: "G, /g/, goat.
   H, /h/, hat. J, /j/, jam. K, /k/, kite."
3. Name–sound–word chant for L, M together, then all ten in order.
4. Sound detective: the adult says a sound — /l/, /g/, /j/, /m/,
   /k/ — and the learner points to the letter that says it.
5. Word detective: the adult says *fish, moon, ball, goat, kite* one
   at a time; the learner says the first sound and then the letter
   ("*fish* — /f/ — F").

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

1. The learner holds up each card B, C, D, F, G in turn and says
   name, sound, and example word for each.
2. The learner holds up each card H, J, K, L, M in turn and says
   name, sound, and example word for each.
3. The adult shows cards mixed up — K, B, M, D, J — and the learner
   says the sound for each (names optional).
4. The adult says sounds mixed up — /f/, /l/, /g/, /h/, /k/ — and the
   learner points to the right letter each time.
5. The learner sorts five picture cards (ball, cat, fish, lion, moon)
   under the right letter cards (B, C, F, L, M).

## Application

"Sound shop." The learner is the shopkeeper. The adult "buys" an item
by saying its first sound ("I would like something that starts with
/m/"). The learner hands over the matching picture card (moon) and the
letter card (M) and says, "M, /m/, moon." Swap roles for two rounds.

## Exit check

1. Say the sound for each letter: B, G, K, M, F. (4–5 correct =
   secure; 3 = nearly there, re-check in Session 12.)
2. "Which letter says /j/?" (J.) "Which letter says /l/?" (L.)
3. "What is the first sound in *dog*? Which letter says it?"
   (/d/ — D.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** anchor every sound to a picture and an action
  (pop for /b/, hiss for /s/ later, hum for /m/) before naming the
  letter; home-language example words for the same sounds are welcome
  alongside the English ones.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the letter for a spoken sound
  instead of saying the sound; use the mirror to watch mouth shapes
  rather than relying on hearing alone.
- **Seated-table variant:** all card work at the table; the mirror
  sits on the table.
- **Floor-play variant:** letter cards in a line on the floor; the
  learner jumps on the letter that says the sound the adult calls.
- **Extension:** the learner names a second example word for three
  letters (any sensible word with the right first sound counts).

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Saying the letter name instead of the sound:* "bee" for /b/ is the
  most common error in the unit. Model the correction every time:
  "That is the name. The sound is /b/ — a little pop." Do not accept
  the name as the sound on the exit check.
- *Voiced/voiceless confusion:* /d/ and /t/, /g/ and /k/ feel similar
  in the mouth. Use the throat check: fingers on the throat buzz for
  /d/ and /g/, stay still for /t/ and /k/ (the /t/ check lands in
  Lesson 04).
- *C and K sharing /k/:* children may insist only one letter can own
  a sound. Say plainly: "Sounds can have more than one letter. C and
  K are both /k/ letters."
