# Lesson 04 — Consonant Sounds, N to Z

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can say the sound for each consonant
letter from N to Z — and say the letter name and the sound together."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** letter cards for N, P, Q, R,
S, T, V, W, X, Y, Z (upper- and lowercase), a small mirror, picture
cards or objects for: nose, pig, queen, rabbit, sun, turtle, violin
(or van), web, fox, yes, zebra.
**Standards:** RF.K.3.a; SL.K.1.a (talk rules).

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Quick-fire review of Lesson 03: the adult shows B, F, K, M, G and the
learner says each sound. Then say: "Those were the first-half
consonants. Today we finish the consonants — N to Z — and then you
will know a sound for every consonant letter."

## Explicit teaching

Here are the primary sounds for the consonants in the second half of
the alphabet, again as **name, sound, word**. Three of these letters
need a special sentence, which the adult says word for word the first
time:

- **Q** never travels alone in words: it comes with U, and together
  they say /kw/, like *queen*. In this unit we say "Q, /kw/, queen."
- **X** says /ks/ — two sounds squeezed into one letter — like the
  end of *fox*. Say it slowly: "/k/ ... /s/ ... /ks/."
- **Y** is a consonant here: it says /y/, like *yes*. (Later, Y does
  vowel jobs too — in *happy* and *my* — but those are not this
  unit's work.)

| Letter | Primary sound | Example word | How to help the mouth |
|---|---|---|---|
| N | /n/ | *nose* | Tongue tip up, hum through the nose |
| P | /p/ | *pig* | Lips pop, no voice (compare B, which pops with voice) |
| Q (+U) | /kw/ | *queen* | /k/ then /w/, run together |
| R | /r/ | *rabbit* | Tongue pulled back, voice on (some children say /w/ at first — that is normal) |
| S | /s/ | *sun* | Teeth close together, hiss like a snake |
| T | /t/ | *turtle* | Tongue taps behind the teeth, no voice (compare D, which taps with voice) |
| V | /v/ | *violin* | Bottom lip under top teeth, buzz with voice (compare F, which blows without voice) |
| W | /w/ | *web* | Lips round and push out, like blowing a kiss |
| X | /ks/ | *fox* | /k/ then /s/, at the end of the word |
| Y | /y/ | *yes* | Tongue high, a quick "yuh" glide |
| Z | /z/ | *zebra* | Like /s/ but with voice — a buzzing bee |

**Voiced and voiceless pairs** are the secret of this lesson. Some
sounds buzz in the throat (voiced: B, D, G, V, Z) and their partners
do not (voiceless: P, T, K, F, S). Put two fingers lightly on the
throat: buzz means voiced, still means voiceless. The adult models
this check for P/B and T/D; the learner tries it for V/F and S/Z.

### Modeled example 1 — the throat check

*Adult holds up P and B and thinks aloud:*

"P. The name is 'pee.' The sound is /p/ — lips pop, and my throat is
still. Feel — no buzz. Now B. The name is 'bee.' The sound is /b/ —
lips pop, and my throat buzzes. Feel — buzz! P and B are partners:
same pop, different throat. P, /p/, pig. B, /b/, ball."

*Learner copies the throat check for T/D: "T, /t/, turtle — still.
D, /d/, dog — buzz."*

### Modeled example 2 — the three special letters

*Adult holds up Q, X, Y in turn and thinks aloud:*

"Q. Q never travels alone — it brings U, and together they say
/kw/. Q, /kw/, queen. Say it with me: 'kwuh-een.'"

"X. X is a two-sound letter: /k/ and /s/ squeezed together. Fox —
listen to the end: /ks/. X, /ks/, fox."

"Y. Y is a consonant today: /y/, like *yes*. Y, /y/, yes. Later Y
will do vowel jobs, but today it is a consonant, and that is enough."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Name–sound–word chant for N, P, Q, R together: "N, /n/, nose.
   P, /p/, pig. Q, /kw/, queen. R, /r/, rabbit."
2. Name–sound–word chant for S, T, V, W together: "S, /s/, sun.
   T, /t/, turtle. V, /v/, violin. W, /w/, web."
3. Name–sound–word chant for X, Y, Z together, then all eleven in
   order.
4. Throat-check pairs: the adult says /p/ or /b/, /t/ or /d/, /s/ or
   /z/; the learner says whether the throat buzzed and names the
   letter.
5. Word detective: the adult says *sun, zebra, web, nose, turtle* one
   at a time; the learner says the first sound and then the letter.

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

1. The learner holds up each card N, P, Q, R, S in turn and says
   name, sound, and example word for each.
2. The learner holds up each card T, V, W, X, Y, Z in turn and says
   name, sound, and example word for each.
3. The adult shows cards mixed up — Z, Q, S, V, R — and the learner
   says the sound for each.
4. The adult says sounds mixed up — /w/, /ks/, /n/, /y/, /t/ — and
   the learner points to the right letter each time.
5. The learner sorts five picture cards (pig, sun, fox, zebra, web)
   under the right letter cards (P, S, X, Z, W). (Note: *fox* goes
   under X even though X is at the **end** of *fox* — the adult
   points this out if the learner hesitates.)

## Application

"Sound mail." The learner "posts" picture cards into letter
mailboxes (envelopes or boxes labeled with a letter). The adult calls
out a sound; the learner finds a picture that starts with it (or, for
X, ends with it) and posts it in the right mailbox, saying name,
sound, and word. Five pieces of mail, then swap roles.

## Exit check

1. Say the sound for each letter: S, Q, Z, N, X. (4–5 correct =
   secure; 3 = nearly there, re-check in Session 14.)
2. "Which letter says /kw/?" (Q, with U.) "Which letter says /r/?"
   (R.)
3. "What is the first sound in *web*? Which letter says it?"
   (/w/ — W.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** anchor /r/ and /w/ with actions (a rabbit hop
  for /r/, a blowing kiss for /w/) since these two are the hardest
  to hear apart; accept a /w/-like /r/ at this stage and model the
  adult form back without correcting the child into silence.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the letter for a spoken sound;
  use the throat check (touch, not hearing) to tell voiced from
  voiceless pairs.
- **Seated-table variant:** all card work at the table; mailboxes
  are envelopes on the table.
- **Floor-play variant:** letter cards in a line on the floor; the
  learner hops to the letter that says the sound the adult calls.
- **Extension:** the learner finds one word in a picture book for
  each of three letters and says its first sound.

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *X at the start:* children may hunt for a word starting with /ks/
  and stall. Teach X as an **ending** sound first (*fox*, *box*);
  X-initial words (*xylophone*, pronounced /z/) are not K material
  and are not tested.
- *R pronounced as W:* developmental and common at age five. Accept
  it, model /r/ clearly, and never make the child repeat /r/ until
  frustrated. Note it for the teacher guide; it does not block Unit
  04, where the letter, not perfect /r/, carries the blending.
- *Q without U in the alphabet line:* as in Lesson 01, Q's name is
  Q alone; Q-plus-U is a spelling pattern, not a letter name.
