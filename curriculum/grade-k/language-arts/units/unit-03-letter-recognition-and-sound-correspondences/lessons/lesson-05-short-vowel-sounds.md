# Lesson 05 — Short Vowel Sounds

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can say the short sound for each vowel —
a, e, i, o, u — and match it to a word."
**Time:** 15–20 minutes · **Materials:** vowel cards A, E, I, O, U
(upper- and lowercase), picture cards or objects for: apple, egg,
igloo, octopus, umbrella, plus ant, pen, pin, mop, cup for the sort.
**Standards:** RF.K.3.b (short sounds); SL.K.1.a (talk rules).

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Sing or chant the five vowel names with the learner: "A, E, I, O, U."
Then say: "Those are the vowel names. But vowels have another job:
each one has a **short sound** — the sound it makes in the middle of
short words. Today we learn the five short sounds."

## Explicit teaching

A **vowel** is a letter whose sound comes out with an open mouth and
voice — no lips, teeth, or tongue blocking the air. There are five
major vowels: **A, E, I, O, U**. (Y sometimes acts as a vowel, but in
this unit Y is a consonant; its vowel jobs come later.)

Each vowel has a **short sound**: the sound it makes in words like
*cat*, *bed*, *pin*, *hot*, *cup*. Learn each short sound with a
picture word you can act out:

| Vowel | Short sound | Picture word | Action cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | /a/ | *apple* | Pretend to bite a big apple: "ah!" |
| E | /e/ | *egg* | Pretend an egg cracks: "eh!" |
| I | /i/ | *igloo* | Shiver and say "ih-ih-ih" like you are cold |
| O | /o/ | *octopus* | Make a surprised round mouth: "ah!" — short and quick |
| U | /u/ | *umbrella* | Open an imaginary umbrella with a grunt: "uh!" |

Say each one as **name, short sound, word**: "A, /a/, apple." The
adult exaggerates the middle sound: "a-pple — hear /a/ in the
middle? The vowel is the middle of the word."

Two cautions. First, short **o** (/o/, *octopus*) and short **a**
(/a/, *apple*) sound close to each other; the mouth shape tells them
apart — short o has a rounder, more open mouth. If the learner mixes
them, accept the attempt, model both mouths in the mirror, and move
on; the distinction firms up in Unit 04's CVC work. Second, do not
teach the short sounds as letter names: "A says /ay/" is the name,
not the short sound. The short sound of A is /a/.

Short vowels are the engine of Unit 04: every CVC word the learner
will blend (*cat*, *bed*, *pin*, *hot*, *cup*) has a short vowel in
its middle. Learning them now makes blending possible later.

### Modeled example 1 — name, short sound, word with actions

*Adult holds up A, bites the imaginary apple, and thinks aloud:*

"A. The name is 'ay.' The short sound is /a/ — bite the apple:
'ah!' A, /a/, apple." *(Learner copies the bite and repeats.)*

"Now U. The name is 'yoo.' The short sound is /u/ — open the
umbrella: 'uh!' U, /u/, umbrella." *(Learner copies.)*

"Now I. The name is 'eye.' The short sound is /i/ — shiver: 'ih!'
I, /i/, igloo."

### Modeled example 2 — hearing the short vowel in the middle

*Adult says three words slowly and thinks aloud:*

"Listen: *bed, red, hen.* What sound is in the middle of all three?
/e/, /e/, /e/ — the egg sound! Which vowel says /e/? E! E, /e/, egg."

"Now listen: *cup, bus, sun.* What sound is in the middle? /u/ —
the umbrella grunt! Which vowel says /u/? U! U, /u/, umbrella."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Action chant for all five, with the adult: "A, /a/, apple.
   E, /e/, egg. I, /i/, igloo. O, /o/, octopus. U, /u/, umbrella."
2. The adult says a short sound — /a/, /o/, /u/, /e/, /i/ — and the
   learner points to the vowel that says it.
3. The adult says a word — *ant, pen, pin, mop, cup* — and the
   learner says the middle (vowel) sound and then the vowel letter.
4. Picture sort: the learner sorts five picture cards (apple, egg,
   igloo, octopus, umbrella) under the right vowel cards.
5. The adult starts a word and the learner finishes the vowel sound:
   "c-__-t" (learner says /a/); "b-__-d" (/e/); "p-__-n" (/i/);
   "h-__-t" (/o/); "c-__-p" (/u/).

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

1. The learner holds up each vowel card A, E, I, O, U in turn and
   says name, short sound, and picture word for each.
2. The adult shows vowel cards mixed up — O, A, U, E, I — and the
   learner says the short sound for each.
3. The adult says words — *hat, bed, sit, dog, bug* — and the learner
   says the middle sound for each.
4. The learner sorts five new picture cards (ant, pen, pin, mop, cup)
   under the right vowel cards (A, E, I, O, U).
5. The learner picks any vowel, draws its picture word, and says
   name, short sound, and word for it.

## Application

"Vowel doctor." The learner is the doctor. The adult presents a
"sick" word with its vowel missing — "c_t needs its middle sound!"
— and says the whole word (*cat*). The learner diagnoses the vowel
(A), writes or points to it, and says, "A, /a/, apple — all better!"
Five patients: *cat* (A), *bed* (E), *pig* (I), *dog* (O), *sun* (U).

## Exit check

1. Say the short sound for each vowel: A, E, I, O, U.
   (4–5 correct = secure; 3 = nearly there, re-check in Session 14.)
2. "What is the middle sound in *bed*? Which vowel says it?"
   (/e/ — E.)
3. "What is the middle sound in *cup*? Which vowel says it?"
   (/u/ — U.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** every short sound has a picture and an action
  before it has a letter; home-language words with the same middle
  sounds are welcome as extra anchors.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the vowel for a spoken sound
  instead of saying it; use the mirror to compare mouth shapes for
  /a/ and /o/.
- **Seated-table variant:** all card work at the table; picture
  cards in a row.
- **Floor-play variant:** vowel cards on the floor; the learner
  jumps on the vowel that says the sound the adult calls.
- **Extension:** the learner thinks of one more short-vowel word for
  two vowels (any sensible word with the right middle sound counts).

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Saying the letter name instead of the short sound:* "A says /ay/"
  is the most common error. Correct it the same way every time:
  "That is the name. The short sound is /a/ — bite the apple."
- *Short a vs. short o:* these two merge in many children's ears.
  Use the mirror: short o has a rounder mouth. Do not drill the
  pair into frustration; Unit 04's word-building gives the ear more
  chances.
- *Long sounds sneaking in:* a child may answer /ā/ ("ay") for A out
  of habit from the alphabet song. Praise the name knowledge, then
  redirect: "That is A's name-song. Today we want A's short sound:
  /a/, apple."
