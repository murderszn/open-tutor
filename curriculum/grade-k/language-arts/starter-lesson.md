# Starter Lesson — Hear sounds and connect them to letters

**Status:** draft supplement; not a complete phonics unit. **Time:** three 10–15 minute sessions. **Goal:** Hear the first sound in a spoken word, blend three spoken sounds, and connect a small explicitly taught set of letters to their sounds.

## Adult preparation and readiness

Use letter cards **m, s, a, t**, three counters, and paper. Readiness: ask the learner to tell whether *sun* begins like *sock* or *map*. Accept a point or home-language response; do not require letter knowledge to begin oral sound play. Teach only the four named correspondences here: **m** /m/, **s** /s/, short **a** /a/ as in *map*, **t** /t/. This is not a full phonics sequence.

## Teach and model

Say: “Words can be pulled apart into sounds. I can say /m/ /a/ /p/ slowly, then blend them: *map*.” Model moving one counter per sound. Point to letters for sounds already taught; letter names and sounds are different.

**Worked example 1:** *sat* begins /s/. The word has sounds /s/ /a/ /t/. Three counters represent the three sounds.

**Worked example 2:** Blend /m/ /a/ /t/: move three counters, say each sound, then say *mat*. The first sound changes from *sat* to *mat*; the last two stay the same.

**Pacing and roles:** Session 1: adult models the two examples and guides task 1. Session 2: guide tasks 2–4, then learner independently matches a taught letter card to its sound. Session 3: do error check 5, complete task 6 with adult support, then exit check.

## Guided and independent tasks

1. **Adult-guided oral task:** Adult says *sun*, *sock*, *map*. Learner says or gestures which two begin with the same sound. If useful, adult draws three simple picture choices; print decoding is not required.
2. **Adult-guided:** Stretch *sat*: learner taps one counter for /s/, /a/, /t/.
3. **Guided, then independent oral response:** Blend adult-spoken sounds /m/ /a/ /t/ to say *mat*.
4. **Guided:** Match printed **s** to /s/ and **m** to /m/; adult models **a** and **t** if unfamiliar.
5. **Adult-guided error check:** Adult says *sat* but moves two counters. Ask learner to show the number of sounds.
6. **Independent application with adult review:** Find or draw one familiar thing beginning with a taught sound (adult checks sound, not spelling).

**Answers/checks:** *sun* and *sock* begin /s/; *map* does not. *sat* has three sounds; /m/ /a/ /t/ is *mat*; two counters are insufficient because *sat* has three phonemes. Accept a picture/dictated example such as *sun* for /s/.

## Exit check and product

Ask learner to blend /s/ /a/ /t/ and identify the first sound in *mat*. Expected: *sat* and /m/. Product: three sound boxes with counters, letters, or an adult note of oral answers.

## Review rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| First sound | Correctly identifies /s/ or /m/ | Correct with two choices | Not yet consistent |
| Blending | Blends three phonemes after a slow model | Blends with repeated adult cue | Continue oral sound play |
| Letter link | Matches at least two taught letters to sounds | Matches with adult prompt | Revisit one letter at a time |

Use picture choices, mirror/mouth cues, gestures, repeated oral models, and home-language comparison when helpful. The learner may point or move counters instead of speaking/writing. If sound access or speech production differs, assess recognition by matching rather than articulation. Avoid small parts for children who mouth objects. Stop before frustration and retry briefly another day.
