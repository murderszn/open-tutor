# Unit 04 — Answer Key (adult only)

Every student question in Lessons 1–5, `practice.md`, the Week 16
quiz, and the unit assessment is solved here. Open responses give a
sample answer and acceptable variation. If a learner's answer differs
but shows the same reasoning, accept it and note the variation.

## Lesson 1 — 1920s prosperity and its limits

**G1.** $5 down + 10 × $10 = $5 + $100 = $105 total. Extra cost:
$105 − $90 = $15. The monthly figure hides the total and the
interest-like extra cost.
**G2.** $100 of stock, $10 own money, $90 borrowed. A 20% fall leaves
$80; selling and repaying $90 gives −$10: the buyer loses the whole
$10 and owes $10 more — a loss of 200% of the money put down.
**G3.** Any two, for example: Immigration Acts (1921/1924) targeted
southern/eastern Europeans and Asians; the Ku Klux Klan targeted
Black Americans, Catholics, Jews, and immigrants; Prohibition was
enforced most harshly in poor and immigrant neighborhoods.

**I1.** Sample: "The 1920s economy was healthy for urban consumers
and manufacturers — cheaper cars and appliances, rising output —
but unhealthy for farmers facing falling crop prices and for
households carrying installment debt. Judged for the whole country,
it was a boom built on unequal footing: healthy for many city
dwellers, fragile for farmers and debtors." Accept any paragraph
with one "yes" fact, one "no" fact, and a qualified judgment.
**I2.** $2,000 ÷ $2 = 1,000 bushels; $2,000 ÷ $1 = 2,000 bushels.
Deflation doubles the real burden of a fixed debt: the same loan
costs twice as much produce to repay.
**I3.** Errors: (1) "all women" — Black women in the South still
faced poll taxes, literacy tests, and violence, and many Native
American women were not yet citizens; (2) "inequality ended" — the
vote removed one barrier, not unequal pay, work, or legal status.
**I4.** Sample: "If one earner loses a job, the household's
installment payments continue while its income falls, so spending
on everything else collapses and missed payments add penalties —
one layoff can cascade into default."

**Applied task / exit check:** accept any diary entry or sentence
naming one boom mechanism (assembly line, credit, radio/movies,
jazz/Harlem culture) and one excluded/burdened group (farmers,
Black Americans, immigrants, women facing unequal work), labeled
as an original composite.

## Lesson 2 — Crash and causes

**G1.** "Almost half" of 381.17 ≈ 381.17 × 0.5 = 190.59, about 191.
**G2.** $200 stock falls 10% to $180; sell and repay $180 = $0 left.
The buyer loses the entire $20 put down = 100% of their own money.
**G3.** Sample pairings: overproduction → factory workers and
farmers (layoffs, falling prices); fragile credit → margin buyers
and installment debtors (margin calls, defaults); bank fragility →
depositors and small borrowers (lost savings, no credit); gold
standard → indebted countries and the Fed's constrained choices;
global links → exporters and debtor nations (trade collapse).

**I1.** 1929→1933: 24.9 − 3.2 = 21.7 percentage points. 1937→1938:
19.0 − 14.3 = 4.7 percentage points. Percentage points measure the
arithmetic difference between two percentages; "percent" would mean
a relative change (21.7 ÷ 3.2 ≈ 678%), which answers a different
question and misleads here.
**I2.** Sample (44 words): "Black Tuesday triggered panic selling,
but overproduction, margin debt, and a banking system with no
deposit insurance turned the crash into a depression when about
9,000 banks failed between 1930 and 1933." Accept any rewrite with
one trigger, two causes, and the banking collapse.
**I3.** Open: accept any defended pair, for example fastest =
deposit insurance (a law can be passed in months — and was, June
1933); longest = overproduction/wage structure (requires years of
income and industrial change). The key must show reasoning about
what each fix requires.
**I4.** Sample: "Under gold, dollars were convertible to gold at a
fixed rate. When Britain left gold in 1931, gold flowed out of the
U.S., so the Fed raised rates to defend gold — tightening credit
during a depression instead of expanding it to rescue banks."

**Applied task / exit check:** accept any explainer naming margin
calls, one other Part 2 cause, and one reason non-stockholders were
affected (bank failures, layoffs, falling spending); exit sentence
must name the crash as trigger and one cause/condition.

## Lesson 3 — Human cost and Dust Bowl

**G1.** 2,000 × 24.9% = 498 workers. Hidden: dependents, lost
savings, evictions, and lost customers for local shops.
**G2.** Sample observations: dust wall on the horizon; farmhouse
and windmill; loaded wagon and family; cracked earth; wire fence.
Each observation is what is shown; an inference adds a conclusion
(for example, "the family is leaving" infers intent from the loaded
wagon — plausible, but still an inference).
**G3.** Human causes: deep plowing that removed native grasses;
wheat expansion during/after WWI. Natural cause: multiyear drought
from the early 1930s. Interaction: plowing exposed topsoil, drought
dried it, and wind carried it away — no single factor alone makes
a "black blizzard."

**I1.** Sample: "The dispersal damaged Hoover because federal
troops with tanks and tear gas drove veterans — men who had served
in WWI — from the capital after Congress refused their bonus. It
became a symbol of force against veterans while jobless families
got no direct federal relief. Limit: it proves one confrontation's
choice, not Hoover's entire record (for example, the RFC)."
**I2.** Correction: deep plowing removed anchoring grasses, and
the wheat boom (WWI demand, then 1920s expansion with tractors)
pushed cultivation onto fragile land; drought then turned exposed
soil into dust storms.
**I3.** Sample: "A statistic shows scale (one worker in four) but
hides individual experience; a photograph shows individual
experience vividly but cannot show scale or typicality, and a
government-commissioned photograph also carries its maker's purpose."
**I4.** Errors: (1) it is not a photograph — it is an AI-generated
illustration; (2) it does not depict a specific real family or day.
Correct caption: "A farm family faces a dust storm, Great Plains,
about 1935 (AI illustration, not a photograph)."

**Applied task / exit check:** accept any composite/drawing with
one Dust Bowl cause, one family decision, and a sentence stating
what it illustrates vs. cannot prove; exit sentence must name one
mechanism (bank failure, layoffs, falling prices, lost customers).

## Lesson 4 — First New Deal

**G1.** FERA: primary relief (secondary: none strong — direct aid).
CCC: primary relief (secondary: recovery/conservation). TVA:
primary recovery/reform (secondary: relief through jobs). FDIC:
primary reform (secondary: recovery through restored confidence).
**G2.** Closing banks alone changes nothing about solvency; trust
returned because the government inspected banks, let only solvent
ones reopen under supervision, and explained the plan publicly —
depositors believed reopened banks were safe.
**G3.** Helped: landowners — AAA payments followed ownership and
raised farm income. Harmed: sharecroppers/tenant farmers —
payments went to owners, and land taken out of production displaced
the people who worked it.

**I1.** Sample: "The NRA aimed to raise wages and set fair codes
(recovery/reform) and did establish codes and end child labor in
covered industries, but enforcement was weak and the largest firms
often wrote their own codes; the Supreme Court then struck the
whole NIRA down in 1935."
**I2.** Errors: (1) timing — the Hundred Days were 1933, while
unemployment was 21.7% in 1934 and 20.1% in 1935; (2) evidence —
"fixed" would require near-normal employment, which did not arrive
until wartime mobilization.
**I3.** Open: accept a defended comparison, for example CCC gave
wages plus skills, housing, and conservation work (more dignity and
training) but cost more per person and served only young men, while
FERA reached more people faster with cash/food but built nothing
lasting.
**I4.** Support: the TVA was a federal corporation planning a whole
region's power and development — an unprecedented government role.
Complication: it built dams, power, and flood control that served
farmers and towns, operating more like a public utility than a
takeover of private industry.

**Applied task / exit check:** accept any editorial with one
program to expand and one to redesign, each with a lesson-based
reason; exit check: relief = FERA or CCC; recovery = PWA or TVA;
reform = FDIC (or TVA) — accept any correct program per R with a
one-phrase description.

## Lesson 5 — Second New Deal, courts, evaluation

**G1.** Wagner Act: guaranteed the right to organize and bargain
collectively; created the National Labor Relations Board; changed
industrial workers' bargaining position most (union membership
surged).
**G2.** Agricultural labor and domestic service were left out of
old-age insurance; about 65% of Black workers were excluded.
**G3.** Chronological order: *Schechter* (1935), Social Security
Act (August 14, 1935), *Butler* (1936), court-packing plan (February
1937), Fair Labor Standards Act (1938).

**I1.** Sample: "Roosevelt proposed adding justices after the Court
struck down the NIRA and AAA, hoping for a Court that would uphold
his laws. Congress refused — even many Democrats saw it as a threat
to judicial independence. The episode shows checks and balances in
action: a president and Congress can disagree even within one party,
and the Court can block both."
**I2.** Error: unemployment did not fall every year after 1933 — it
rose from 14.3% (1937) to 19.0% (1938). Corrected sentence: "Unemployment
fell from 24.9% in 1933 to 14.3% in 1937, then rose again to 19.0% in
1938."
**I3.** Accept any answer with one lasting institution (Social
Security, Wagner Act labor law, FDIC, SEC) and the 1940 figure
(14.6%), plus a weighed preference with a reason.
**I4.** Supporting "betrayed": Social Security's original exclusions
left about 65% of Black workers outside old-age insurance, and AAA
payments displaced Black sharecroppers. Supporting "helped more than
before": WPA/CCC jobs, relief, and later coverage expansions reached
Black workers at a scale no prior federal program had. Both can be
partly true because the New Deal was large and uneven: it helped
many Black Americans absolutely while distributing help unequally.

**Applied task / exit check:** accept any verdict brief with
measure, program fact, excluded group, and limitation; exit sentence
must name a measure and a Lessons 4–5 fact.

## Practice sets

**A1.** 50,000 × 0.249 = 12,450 workers.
**A2.** 1933→1937: 24.9 − 14.3 = 10.6 percentage points.
1937→1938: 19.0 − 14.3 = 4.7 percentage points. The first move is larger.
**A3.** (41.22 − 381.17) ÷ 381.17 = −0.8918 ≈ −89%. The error: 10.8%
is what remained (41.22 ÷ 381.17), not what was lost.
**A4.** 1929: 4,000 × 3.2% = 128. 1932: 4,000 × 23.6% = 944.
Increase: 944 − 128 = 816.
**A5.** September 1929 → November 1954 = 25 years and about 2 months.
A returning index does not restore investors who sold at the bottom
or were wiped out on margin — their losses were locked in.
**A6.** (24.9 − 14.3) ÷ 24.9 = 10.6 ÷ 24.9 = 0.4257 ≈ 42.6%, so "about
42%" is correct as a *relative* change. Use percentage points
(10.6 pp) for the absolute difference between two percentages and
percent for the change relative to the starting value.

**B1.** Primary aims: CCC relief (secondary: recovery/conservation);
FERA relief; TVA recovery/reform (secondary: relief); FDIC reform
(secondary: recovery via confidence); WPA relief (secondary:
recovery/infrastructure); Wagner Act reform (secondary: recovery via
wages/bargaining power).
**B2.** AAA: aim — raise farm prices/income by reducing production.
Benefited: landowners, via payments for acreage taken out of
production. Harmed: sharecroppers/tenant farmers, displaced when
that land left production. Verdict (sample, measure stated): "Judged
by landowners' income it worked; judged by all farm people's welfare
it failed many of the poorest."
**B3.** Position 1 appeals to bold action/security: supported by the
bank holiday's restoration of deposits and the CCC/WPA's direct
wages; weakened by the 1937–38 relapse and by exclusions. Position 2
appeals to limited government/market recovery: supported by NRA
enforcement problems and business uncertainty; weakened by the
banking collapse's scale (private charity and local relief had not
stopped it) and by the bank holiday's success.
**B4.** Also left out: government employees, nonprofit employees,
and the self-employed (including farm proprietors). The exclusions
mattered more for Black workers because a far larger share of Black
workers were farm laborers or domestic workers — about 65% excluded
overall, vs. about 27% of white workers in the commonly cited
comparison.
**B5.** Chronological order: bank holiday (March 6, 1933);
Emergency Banking Act (March 9, 1933); CCC (March 31, 1933); AAA
(May 12, 1933); TVA (May 18, 1933); NIRA (June 16, 1933); FDIC /
Glass-Steagall (June 16, 1933); *Schechter* (May 1935); Wagner Act
(July 5, 1935); Social Security Act (August 14, 1935); court-packing
plan (February 1937); Fair Labor Standards Act (June 1938).
**B6.** TVA: a federal development corporation is not "socialism" in
the sense of abolishing private property — it built public power and
flood control alongside private farms and firms. FDIC: insuring
deposits is not "capitalism" as laissez-faire — it is government
regulation of private banks. Both labels describe the programs'
politics, not their mechanisms.

**C1/C2.** Score on structure (see project rubric): C1 must state a
measure, cite the bank holiday/FDIC, one jobs program, and Social
Security, name an excluded group, and state a limitation. C2 must
present a genuine counterargument using a fact C1 omitted, plus one
sentence on what evidence would change the learner's mind.

## Unit assessment

**Part A (2 points each: 1 for position, 1 for year).** Order:
19th Amendment (1920); Immigration Act (1924); Black Tuesday
(1929); bank holiday (1933); TVA created (1933); *Schechter*
decision (1935); Wagner Act (1935); Social Security Act (1935);
court-packing plan proposed (1937); Fair Labor Standards Act
(1938). Accept bank holiday/TVA order within 1933 only if months
are correct (March before May).

**Part B.** 1. Position 1: bold federal action/experimentation —
supported by the bank holiday's deposit return or WPA wages.
Position 2: limited federal role/self-reliance — supported by NRA
enforcement problems or business uncertainty under codes. 2. Each
weakened: Position 1 by the 1937–38 relapse or by exclusions;
Position 2 by the banking collapse's scale and the failure of
voluntary/local relief to stop it. 3. Accept either position if
defended: for Position 1, unemployment fell 24.9% → 14.3% by 1937;
for Position 2, it never reached normal levels and relapsed to
19.0% in 1938. Limitation (required): unemployment alone does not
measure lasting institutions, excluded groups, or relief's human
effects.

**Part C (30 points).** Score: measure stated (5); bank holiday/FDIC
discussed (5); one jobs program discussed (5); Social Security
discussed (5); excluded group + mechanism (5); Court paragraph
(struck-down law + court-packing failure) (3); stated limitation
(2). Accept any defensible verdict meeting the structure.

**Part D.** 1. 24.9 − 3.2 = 21.7 percentage points. 2. (41.22 −
381.17) ÷ 381.17 ≈ −89%. 3. Counterexample: 1937 (14.3%) → 1938
(19.0%) rose; the claim "fell every year" is false. 4. 1937: 14.3%;
1940: 14.6% — still about one worker in seven, far from full
employment, so "ended unemployment by 1937" fails. 5. Sample: the
statistic does not measure who programs excluded (for example,
farm/domestic workers outside Social Security), what lasting
institutions were created (FDIC, labor law, Social Security
itself), or the human experience of relief vs. destitution; it also
rests on later estimates, not contemporary measurements.
