# Unit 04 — Culminating Assessment (Week 18, S5)

Answer all parts. Solutions and scoring guidance are in
`answer-key.md` (adult only). This assessment is not standardized or
validated; it measures this unit's objectives only.

## Part A — Timeline and terms (20 points, 2 points each)

Place these ten events in chronological order and give the year of
each: Social Security Act signed; bank holiday declared; Black
Tuesday; 19th Amendment ratified; *Schechter* decision; Immigration
Act (1924); Fair Labor Standards Act; TVA created; Wagner Act;
court-packing plan proposed.

## Part B — Source analysis (20 points)

Read these two short paraphrased positions (adapted from
public-domain-era arguments; no invented quotations):

- **Position 1 (1933, administration supporter):** "The government
  must act boldly and try programs. Fear itself is the enemy, and
  experiment beats inaction while millions are jobless."
- **Position 2 (1932, Hoover-era voluntarism):** "Prosperity must
  come from private enterprise and local responsibility. Federal
  handouts weaken self-reliance and delay real recovery."

1. For each position, name the underlying principle it appeals to
   (bold federal action vs. limited federal role / self-reliance)
   and one piece of evidence from Lessons 3–5 that supports it.
   (8 points)
2. For each position, name one piece of evidence from Lessons 3–5
   that weakens it. (8 points)
3. Which position better fits the unemployment record 1933–1938
   (24.9% → 14.3% → 19.0%)? Defend your answer in two sentences,
   naming one limit of using unemployment alone as the measure.
   (4 points)

## Part C — Program evaluation essay (30 points)

"Did the New Deal remake American government?" Write 300–400 words
that: state your measure; discuss the bank holiday/FDIC, one jobs
program, and Social Security; name one excluded group and its
mechanism; discuss the Court's role in one paragraph (a struck-down
law and the court-packing plan's failure); and end with one stated
limitation of your evidence. See the Lesson 5, Worked example 3
structure: measure, evidence, exclusion, limitation.

## Part D — Data task (30 points)

Use Lesson 2's unemployment table (1929–1940: 3.2, 8.9, 15.9, 23.6,
24.9, 21.7, 20.1, 17.0, 14.3, 19.0, 17.2, 14.6) and the Dow figures
(381.17 in September 1929; 41.22 in July 1932).

1. Compute the unemployment change in percentage points from 1929
   to 1933. (5 points)
2. Compute the Dow's percentage fall, peak to trough. (5 points)
3. A speaker claims "unemployment fell every year after 1933."
   Using the table, show the claim is false with one counterexample
   year pair. (5 points)
4. A speaker claims "the New Deal ended unemployment by 1937."
   Using the table, give the 1937 and 1940 figures and explain why
   the claim fails. (5 points)
5. Explain in three sentences why unemployment statistics alone
   cannot settle whether the New Deal "worked": name two things
   the statistic does not measure (for example, who was excluded
   from programs, or what lasting institutions were created).
   (10 points)
