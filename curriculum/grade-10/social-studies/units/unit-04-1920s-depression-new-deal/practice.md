# Unit 04 — Practice Sets

Work Sets A and B in Week 16–17 sessions; Set C is the Week 18
writing rehearsal for the assessment. Starred (★) items are the
core load; unstarred items are extensions. Solutions are in
`answer-key.md` (adult only).

## Set A — Data: unemployment and the market (Week 16, S3)

Use Lesson 2's table: unemployment 1929–1940 = 3.2, 8.9, 15.9, 23.6,
24.9, 21.7, 20.1, 17.0, 14.3, 19.0, 17.2, 14.6 (% of labor force).
Dow: 381.17 (September 3, 1929) → 41.22 (July 8, 1932).

**A1. ★** In a city with 50,000 workers, about how many were
unemployed in 1933 (24.9%)? Show the multiplication.

**A2. ★** Compute the unemployment change in percentage points
from 1933 (24.9%) to 1937 (14.3%), and from 1937 to 1938 (19.0%).
Which move is larger?

**A3. ★** Compute the Dow's percentage fall from 381.17 to 41.22
(Lesson 2, Worked example 1). If a learner says "the Dow fell
10.8%," explain the error in one sentence.

**A4.** A town's labor force is 4,000. In 1929 (3.2%) about how many
were unemployed? In 1932 (23.6%)? What is the increase?

**A5.** The Dow did not return to 381.17 until November 1954.
Compute how many years that is after September 1929, and explain
in one sentence why a price index returning to a peak does not
mean every investor recovered (hint: people who sold in 1932).

**A6.** Error analysis: a chart shows unemployment "falling 42%
from 1933 to 1937" (24.9% → 14.3%). Is "42%" correct as a *relative*
change? Compute (24.9 − 14.3) ÷ 24.9 and explain when to use
percentage points vs. percent.

## Set B — Sorting programs and comparing sources (Week 17, S3)

**B1. ★** Sort into relief / recovery / reform (primary aim only,
then one secondary aim): CCC, FERA, TVA, FDIC, WPA, Wagner Act.
Present your answer as a table.

**B2. ★** For the AAA, fill in: aim (one phrase); who benefited
(one group + mechanism); who was harmed (one group + mechanism);
verdict (one sentence stating its measure).

**B3. ★** Read these two paraphrased positions (adapted from
public-domain-era arguments; no invented quotations):
- Position 1 (1933, administration supporter): "The government
  must act boldly and try programs; fear itself is the enemy, and
  experiment beats inaction."
- Position 2 (1933, business critic): "Government codes and spending
  frighten business, raise costs, and delay the natural recovery."
For each: name the value it appeals to (action/security vs.
limited government/market recovery) and one fact from Lessons 4–5
that supports it and one fact that weakens it.

**B4.** The Social Security Act left out agricultural and domestic
workers. Name two other groups left out of its original old-age
insurance (see Lesson 5, Part 2 and the unit README), and explain
in one sentence why the exclusions mattered more for Black workers
than for white workers on average.

**B5.** Sequence and date: bank holiday, Emergency Banking Act,
CCC created, AAA passed, TVA created, NIRA passed, FDIC created,
Wagner Act, Social Security Act, *Schechter* decision, court-packing plan,
Fair Labor Standards Act. (Twelve events; years suffice except
for the March–June 1933 cluster, where month order matters.)

**B6.** A learner argues: "The TVA proves the New Deal was
socialist; the FDIC proves it was capitalist." Explain why both
labels mislead, using what each program actually did (one sentence
each).

## Set C — Argument rehearsal (Week 18, S4)

**C1. ★** Write a 200-word argument answering: "Did the New Deal
remake American government?" Your argument must: state a measure;
cite the bank holiday/FDIC, one jobs program, and Social Security;
name one excluded group; and state one limitation of your evidence.

**C2.** Counterargument drill: take your C1 position and write the
strongest 100-word counterargument against it, using one fact your
C1 did not use. Then write one sentence stating what evidence
would change your mind.
