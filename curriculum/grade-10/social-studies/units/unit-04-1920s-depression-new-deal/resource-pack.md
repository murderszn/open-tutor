## Web references (verified 2026-11-03)

1. **Federal Reserve History — "Stock Market Crash of 1929"**
   https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/stock-market-crash-of-1929
   Dow six-fold rise (63 in August 1921 to 381 in September 1929);
   peak 381.17 (September 3, 1929); Black Monday −13%, Black Tuesday
   −12%; trough 41.22 (July 8, 1932), −89%; return to peak November
   1954. Supports Objectives 1–2; Lessons 1–2; Practice Set A.
   Free, no account.

2. **U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — "Compensation from before
   World War I through the Great Depression" (historical series)**
   https://api.bls.gov/opub/mlr/cwc/compensation-from-before-world-war-i-through-the-great-depression.pdf
   Unemployment estimates: 3.2% (1929) to 24.9% (1933), 14.3% (1937),
   19.0% (1938), 14.6% (1940); stock market "nearly 90 percent"
   decline 1929–1932. Supports Objective 2; Lesson 2; Practice Set A;
   assessment Part D. Free, no account (PDF).

3. **Encyclopaedia Britannica — "Dust Bowl"**
   https://www.britannica.com/place/Dust-Bowl
   Six hardest-hit states; Black Sunday (April 14, 1935); about 2.5
   million people left the Plains states, over 200,000 reaching
   California; dust pneumonia deaths estimated at 7,000. Supports
   Objective 3; Lesson 3; Investigation A (Dust Bowl-adjacent
   context). Free article, no account.

4. **Social Security Administration — "Social Security History"**
   https://www.ssa.gov/history/50ed.html
   Act signed August 14, 1935; old-age benefits and unemployment
   insurance structure; groups not covered at first, including
   agricultural and domestic workers, the self-employed, and
   nonprofit employees. Supports Objective 5; Lesson 5; project
   (Social Security option). Free, no account.

5. **Roosevelt Institute — "What Is the Social Security Act?"**
   https://rooseveltinstitute.org/blog/what-is-the-social-security-act/
   Signed August 14, 1935; exclusions left about 65% of Black workers
   and about half the labor force outside old-age insurance;
   coverage extended to farm and domestic workers in 1950.
   Supports Objective 5; Lesson 5; Practice Set B. Free, no account.
   Note: this source states the Southern-politics motive for the
   exclusions more firmly than the SSA's own historian does — use
   it alongside reference 4 to show the debate (Lesson 5, Part 2).

6. **Library of Congress — Farm Security Administration / Office of
   War Information photograph collection (search landing page)**
   https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/fsa/
   Public-domain Depression-era photographs (including Dorothea
   Lange's and Arthur Rothstein's Dust Bowl work) with captions and
   dates. Supports Objective 3; Lesson 3's evidence discussion and
   Lesson 5's evidence lab. Free, no account. Adult previews every
   photograph before showing it; government commissioning is
   discussed as a purpose, not a neutral fact. Note: an automated
   fetch on 2026-11-03 returned LOC's human-verification challenge
   rather than page content, so the adult should open this link in
   a browser before assigning it.
