# Investigation A — New Deal Program Evaluation Brief and Town Hall

**Investigation A runs across Week 17, S4 and Week 18, S4.**
It is the unit's project: one program, evaluated honestly, defended
in public.

## The task

Choose one program: CCC, FERA, PWA, TVA, AAA, NRA, FDIC, WPA,
Wagner Act, or Social Security (old-age insurance). Research it
with this unit's lessons and at least one Resource Pack reference
(you must name the reference). Produce:

1. **A 400–600-word evaluation brief** with four labeled sections:
   - **Aims:** what the program was for (relief, recovery, reform
     — primary and secondary, Lesson 4's method).
   - **Results:** what it did, with at least two sourced facts
     (numbers, dates, or documented outcomes — not general praise).
   - **Who it left out:** at least one group excluded, harmed, or
     underserved, with the mechanism (Lesson 5's method).
   - **Recommendation:** expand, redesign, or retire — with one
     reason and one stated limitation of your evidence.
2. **A 2-minute town-hall statement** presenting your recommendation
   to classmates (or to the guiding adult), followed by answering
   one challenging question about your program's weakest point.

## Materials

This unit's lessons, the Resource Pack, a notebook or document,
and (optional) the unemployment chart for programs judged on jobs.
No purchases; library and free online sources only. All sources
must be free and no-account, or the adult supplies an alternative.

## Steps

1. **Week 17, S4 (session 1):** choose a program; list its aims
   and one question you cannot yet answer; begin research.
2. **Between sessions:** draft the brief's Aims and Results sections;
   find your excluded group and its mechanism.
3. **Week 18, S2 (evidence lab):** test your evidence — is each
   fact a statistic, a document, testimony, or an illustration?
   What can each kind prove, and what can it not?
4. **Week 18, S4 (session 2):** deliver the town-hall statement;
   answer one challenging question; revise the brief's
   Recommendation in light of the question.

## Deliverables

- The 400–600-word brief (four labeled sections; sources named).
- The town-hall statement (written notes or a recording, plus the
  challenging question and your answer, written down).

## Rubric (20 points; adult-scored)

| Criterion | 4 points | 2–3 points | 0–1 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aims sorted correctly (relief/recovery/reform, primary + secondary) | Correct primary aim with a defended secondary aim | Primary aim correct; secondary missing or undefended | Aims missing or mis-sorted without reasoning |
| Results with sourced facts | Two or more specific, sourced facts (numbers/dates/outcomes) | One specific fact, or facts without sources | General claims only ("it helped a lot") |
| Exclusion analyzed | Excluded group named with the mechanism that excluded them | Group named without mechanism | No excluded group discussed |
| Recommendation + limitation | Clear recommendation with a reason and a stated evidence limitation | Recommendation with a reason but no limitation, or vice versa | No recommendation, or a recommendation with neither |
| Town-hall defense | Answers the challenging question directly, conceding a real weakness | Answers partly or evades the weakness | No answer, or an answer unrelated to the question |

**Scoring guidance:** 17–20 = strong evaluation; 12–16 = solid with
one section to strengthen; below 12 = revise one section (the
adult names which) before the unit assessment. The rubric measures
this unit's objectives only; it is not a standardized score.
