# Unit 05 — Culminating Assessment

**100 points · about 60 minutes · closed book except the
aircraft-production table in Part B.** Covers Lessons 1–5. Write in
your notebook; the adult scores against `answer-key.md`. This is a
draft classroom assessment, not a standardized or validated test.

## Part B — Data interpretation (20 points)

Use this table (U.S. aircraft production, all types; Lesson 2):

| Year | Aircraft produced |
|---|---:|
| 1940 | 6,086 |
| 1941 | 19,433 |
| 1942 | 47,836 |
| 1943 | 85,898 |
| 1944 | 96,318 |

1. (5 pts) In which year was the largest *absolute* increase over the
   previous year, and by how many aircraft? Show the subtraction.
2. (5 pts) By what factor did production grow from 1940 to 1944?
   Show the division, rounded to one decimal place.
3. (5 pts) Women went from 27% of the workforce in 1940 to 37% in
   1945, and from about 12 million workers to over 19 million.
   Explain in 2–3 sentences what changed and what the numbers do
   *not* show about women's postwar lives.
4. (5 pts) A poster claims "American production won the war." In
   3–4 sentences, explain what the aircraft table supports in that
   claim and what it cannot prove on its own.

## Part C — Source analysis (25 points, 5 each)

Read this excerpt from Executive Order 9066 (February 19, 1942;
public domain, U.S. government):

> "I hereby authorize and direct the Secretary of War, and the
> Military Commanders whom he may from time to time designate,
> whenever he or any designated Commander deems such action necessary
> or desirable, to prescribe military areas in such places and of such
> extent as he or the appropriate Military Commander may determine,
> from which any or all persons may be excluded..."

1. What power does the order grant, and to whom?
2. What words are *missing* from the order that its application
   depended on — and why does that absence matter?
3. How does the *Korematsu* majority's reasoning relate to this text?
4. How does Justice Jackson's dissent — the decision would lie "like
   a loaded weapon, ready for the hand of any authority that can
   bring forward a plausible claim of an urgent need" — relate to
   this text?
5. What does *Ex parte Endo*, decided the same day as *Korematsu*,
   add to the picture?

## Part D — Argument essay (25 points)

**Prompt:** Did World War II resolve America's prewar crises, or
relocate them? Write 300–400 words.

- Use at least three pieces of evidence from at least two lessons.
- State a clear claim (resolve / relocate / a mix — and say which
  crises you mean).
- Name the strongest counter-consideration to your claim.
- State one limitation of your evidence.

## Part E — Evidence and its limits (10 points, 5 each)

1. A historian finds a 1943 War Department report claiming military
   necessity for the exclusion of Japanese Americans. A 1983
   commission finds the claim was based on no evidence of individual
   disloyalty, and that the government suppressed a contradictory
   report. Which source should the historian trust more about 1942,
   and why? Answer in 3–4 sentences.
2. The generated illustration in Lesson 2 shows women building a
   bomber in a 1940s factory. Can it prove that women built bombers
   in 1943? Why or why not? Answer in 2–3 sentences.
