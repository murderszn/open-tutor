# Unit 05 — Practice Sets

Work in your notebook. Starred (★) items are the core items — answer
those first if time is short; the rest deepen the same skills. All
figures are from the lessons; check your arithmetic before checking
the key.

## Set A — Timeline and causation (Lessons 1, 5)

1. Place these six events in order on a timeline, with dates: (a)
   Germany invades Poland; (b) Japan invades Manchuria; (c)
   Lend-Lease Act; (d) attack on Pearl Harbor; (e) Atlantic Charter;
   (f) destroyers-for-bases deal.
2. For each of the six events, label it a long-term cause, a step
   toward the Allies, or a trigger of American entry — and write one
   sentence justifying the label.
3. ★ A classmate says, "The United States was neutral until Pearl
   Harbor." Use two facts from Lesson 1 to correct the claim.
4. Explain in 4–5 sentences why appeasement failed to prevent war,
   using the Munich example.
5. Roosevelt called the United States the "Arsenal of Democracy."
   What did he mean, and what did the phrase leave out?
6. ★ The oil embargo of July 1941 is sometimes described as the
   United States "provoking" Japan. Evaluate that description: what
   did the embargo do, what choices did Japan still have, and what
   does "provoking" imply that the facts do not support? Write 5–6
   sentences.
7. ★ Error analysis: "Japan attacked Pearl Harbor because the United
   States cut off its oil, so the embargo caused the war." What is
   right and what is wrong with that sentence? Write 4–5 sentences.
8. ★ Was the United States' entry into World War II inevitable by
   1939? Write a short paragraph with two pieces of evidence and one
   counter-consideration.

## Set B — Data: production and work (Lesson 2)

Use the aircraft-production table (Lesson 2, Part 1) and the
women-in-the-workforce figures (12 million, 27%, in 1940; over 19
million, 37%, in 1945).

| Year | Aircraft produced |
|---|---:|
| 1940 | 6,086 |
| 1941 | 19,433 |
| 1942 | 47,836 |
| 1943 | 85,898 |
| 1944 | 96,318 |

1. ★ (a) In which year was the largest *absolute* increase over the
   previous year? Show the subtraction. (b) In which year was the
   largest *percentage* increase over the previous year? Show the
   division.
2. ★ By what factor did aircraft production grow from 1940 to 1944?
   Show the division, and round to one decimal place.
3. How many times greater was 1942 production than 1940 production?
   Show the division, and round to one decimal place.
4. Women went from 27% of the workforce in 1940 to 37% in 1945. (a)
   How many percentage points did the share rise? (b) If the total
   workforce were 50 million in both years, how many more women would
   be working in 1945 than in 1940? Show the arithmetic. (c) The real
   increase was larger than your answer to (b). Why? (Hint: what else
   changed besides the share?)
5. ★ A classmate says, "Aircraft production rose every year, so the
   war effort got steadily easier." Explain the error in 2–3
   sentences.
6. ★ If production had kept growing at the 1941 percentage rate, how
   many aircraft would 1942 have produced? Show the calculation, and
   explain in 2–3 sentences why that extrapolation is unreliable.

## Set C — Rights and sources (Lessons 3–4)

1. In your own words, what did Executive Order 9066 authorize, and
   to whom was it applied in practice? (2–3 sentences.)
2. ★ A classmate says, "The Supreme Court approved the camps, so they
   must have been legal." Use *Ex parte Endo* and the 1983 findings
   to correct that sentence in 3–4 sentences.
3. Why did the loyalty questionnaire put incarcerated Nisei in an
   impossible position? Name one way a "yes" answer and one way a
   "no" answer could each be held against the person answering.
4. The 1983 commission named three causes of the removal: race
   prejudice, war hysteria, and a failure of political leadership.
   For each cause, write one sentence of evidence from Lesson 3.
5. Explain in 4–5 sentences why the Civil Liberties Act of 1988 is
   both a meaningful reckoning and an incomplete one.
6. ★ Match each term to its definition (write the letter and the
   number):

   Terms: (a) Nisei; (b) Issei; (c) *Korematsu v. United States*;
   (d) *Ex parte Endo*; (e) War Relocation Authority; (f) Civil
   Liberties Act of 1988.

   Definitions: (1) The 1944 Supreme Court decision, 6–3, upholding
   the exclusion orders; (2) the federal agency that ran the ten
   relocation camps; (3) American-born Japanese Americans, U.S.
   citizens by birth; (4) the 1988 law apologizing for the
   incarceration and paying $20,000 to each surviving internee; (5)
   the unanimous 1944 Supreme Court decision that a loyal citizen
   could not be detained; (6) first-generation Japanese immigrants,
   barred by law from becoming U.S. citizens.
7. ★ "The government hid evidence from the Court." If that is true,
   what does it imply about the Korematsu majority's reasoning — and
   what does it *not* prove about what the justices knew in 1944?
   Write 5–6 sentences.
8. ★ Match each person or order to its role (write the letter and the
   number):

   People/orders: (a) James G. Thompson; (b) A. Philip Randolph; (c)
   Executive Order 8802; (d) Fair Employment Practice Committee.

   Roles: (1) threatened a March on Washington in 1941 to protest
   defense-industry discrimination; (2) the body created to hear
   complaints of discrimination in defense work; (3) wrote the
   January 1942 letter proposing the "double V"; (4) banned
   discrimination in defense industries and government work.

## Set D — Argument (Lessons 2, 4, 5)

1. ★ "Did the war change American women's lives permanently?" Write
   a paragraph answering that question with two pieces of evidence
   and one qualification.
2. ★ "Did World War II resolve or relocate America's prewar crises?"
   Write a paragraph answering that question with two pieces of
   evidence and one stated limitation.
3. ★ Error analysis: "The atomic bomb ended the war, so the bomb
   decision needs no further debate." What is wrong with that
   sentence? Write 4–5 sentences.
4. ★ Whose rights were most at risk during the war, and what
   protected them — or failed to? Write a 5-sentence paragraph naming
   two groups, one protection that worked, and one that failed.
