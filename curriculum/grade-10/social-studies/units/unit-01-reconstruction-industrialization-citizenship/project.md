# Unit 01 Investigation & Project

Two linked investigations. Investigation A runs across Weeks 3–4 (three
project sessions); Investigation B is a single Week 5 session. Both feed
the Week 6 argument workshop and the culminating assessment.

## Investigation A — *The Amendment on Trial*

**Driving question:** *Did the Reconstruction Amendments deliver on their
promises — and for whom?*

### Materials

- The three amendment texts (Lessons 1–2) and your audit table.
- Resource pack (`resource-pack.md`): National Archives, Library of
  Congress, and Gilder Lehrman links (checked 2026-10-14).
- One case file per learner, chosen from the list below (adult assigns
  or learner chooses; each case gets at least one investigator).

### Case files (each is a real historical episode)

1. **South Carolina, 1868:** Black-majority legislature writes the
   state's first public-school system. *Question:* What did political
   power build?
2. **Mississippi, 1865–66:** Black Codes criminalize Black unemployment;
   Congress responds with the Civil Rights Act of 1866. *Question:* How
   did evasion provoke federal counter-moves?
3. **Louisiana, 1873:** The Colfax Massacre and the *Cruikshank*
   decision (1876). *Question:* What happened when violence met the
   14th Amendment in court?
4. **The disputed election of 1876 and the Compromise of 1877.**
   *Question:* What was traded, and who paid?
5. **Mississippi, 1890:** the new state constitution's literacy test
   and poll tax. *Question:* How did a state defeat the 15th Amendment
   without naming race?
6. **Plessy v. Ferguson (1896).** *Question:* How did the Court read
   "equal protection" to permit segregation?

### Steps

1. **Week 3, S4 — Research plan (30 min).** Read your case file prompt.
   Write: (a) the amendment(s) your case tests, (b) three research
   questions, (c) the two sources you will consult first (from the
   resource pack), (d) one thing you expect to find and one thing that
   would surprise you.
2. **Week 4, S3 — Evidence draft (40 min).** Write a 300–400 word case
   brief: what happened (5 W's), which amendment promise was tested,
   what the outcome was, and your judgment — promise kept, broken, or
   partial — with two cited facts.
3. **Week 5, S3 — Peer review (30 min).** Exchange briefs with another
   investigator. Use the rubric's *Evidence* and *Reasoning* rows to
   give two specific strengths and two actionable revisions. Revise.
4. **Week 6, S3 — Assembly.** Combine your revised brief with a
   one-page "verdict" answering the driving question for *your* case:
   did the amendments deliver here? Add your case to the class
   evidence wall (physical or digital) under **Kept / Broken /
   Partial**.

### Deliverables

- Research plan (1 page).
- Case brief, 300–400 words, with at least two cited facts.
- Peer-review sheet (given and received).
- One-page verdict + evidence-wall placement with a 2-sentence
  justification.

## Investigation B — *Where Did the Railroad Go?* (Week 5, S4)

**Driving question:** *Who decided where the rails went — and who lived
with the consequences?*

### Materials

- Blank U.S. outline map; railroad-mileage figures (Lesson 4, Set C).
- Resource pack: Library of Congress railroad maps (public domain).

### Steps

1. Mark the first transcontinental route (1869), plus Chicago,
   Pittsburgh, and Birmingham (from Set C5).
2. Research **one** of the following and add it to your map with a
   3-sentence annotation: (a) a railroad land grant and the Native
   land it crossed; (b) a "railroad town" that boomed (e.g., Omaha,
   Cheyenne); (c) Chinese labor camps along the Central Pacific route.
3. Write a 150-word analysis: *one* group the railroad's routing
   helped, *one* group it harmed, and the mechanism in each case
   (land, jobs, access, displacement).

### Deliverable

- Annotated map + 150-word analysis.

## Rubric — Investigation A (also adapted for B's written analysis)

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Historical accuracy** | All facts correct and dated; no anachronism; sources cited | Facts correct; minor dating imprecision; sources cited | One factual error or vague dating; sources listed but not cited | Multiple errors or invented facts |
| **Evidence** | 3+ specific facts tied to claims; distinguishes primary/secondary | 2 specific facts tied to claims | Facts present but loosely tied to claims | Claims without factual support |
| **Reasoning** | Verdict follows from evidence; counterargument addressed; text-vs-practice distinction used | Verdict follows from evidence; counterargument named | Verdict asserted more than argued | No discernible argument |
| **Use of amendment text** | Quotes or precisely paraphrases relevant clause; explains its limits | References correct amendment and clause | Names amendment without textual grounding | Amendment misidentified |
| **Revision** | Peer feedback visibly incorporated; brief improved | Some revision evident | Feedback received but not used | No peer review completed |

**Scoring:** 17–20 = exemplary; 13–16 = proficient; 9–12 = developing;
5–8 = beginning. Below proficient on *Historical accuracy* triggers
re-teach before the unit assessment, regardless of total.

## Safety and access notes

- All episodes involve racial violence or exploitation; the adult
  previews each case file and adjusts detail to the learner.
- Map work may be done digitally or on paper; the 150-word analysis
  may be dictated to the adult if writing stamina is the barrier —
  historical thinking is what's assessed.
