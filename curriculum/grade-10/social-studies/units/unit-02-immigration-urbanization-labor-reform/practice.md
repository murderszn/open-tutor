# Unit 02 Practice Sets

Session-mapped practice for Weeks 7–10. Worked solutions are in
`answer-key.md` — do not open it until you have attempted every item.
Each set: 6–8 meaningful tasks. The adult assigns all or a selection;
the error log from each set feeds the next week's re-teach.

## Set A — Migration reasoning and data (Week 7, S2–S3)

A1. The urban share of the U.S. population was 25.7% (1870), 39.7%
    (1900), and 51.2% (1920). Compute the change in *percentage
    points* for 1870→1900 and for 1900→1920. Which 30-year span saw
    faster urbanization? Show your subtraction.

A2. Define "urban" as the Census did in 1920 (places of 2,500+). A
    classmate claims the 51.2% figure proves most Americans lived in
    *big cities*. Explain the error in two sentences.

A3. Classify each as push, pull, or both, with one evidence phrase
    each: (a) famine in southern Italy, 1890s; (b) a Pittsburgh steel
    mill hiring at $1.50/day; (c) pogroms in the Russian Empire;
    (d) a brother's prepaid steamship ticket from New York.

A4. Explain in 4 sentences why "new" immigrants settled in cities
    rather than on farms. Cite one fact about American land and one
    about immigrant origins.

A5. Compare Ellis Island and Angel Island: write three sentences on
    scale (numbers), process (inspection), and law (why the
    difference).

A6. **Explain-your-reasoning.** "America needed workers, so it
    welcomed all immigrants." Identify two flaws: which immigrants
    did the law *not* welcome, and what was the inspection system
    *for* at Angel Island?

A7. Timeline task: order 1882, 1892, 1907, 1910, 1920 and write one
    significance sentence for each. (All dates from Lessons 1–3.)

## Set B — City life and exclusion (Week 8, S2–S3)

B1. From the tenement-street illustration's text alternative (Lesson
    2), list four observable features and label each as evidence of
    *density*, *work*, or *community*.

B2. For the same illustration, write one observation and one
    inference, and explain in two sentences why they must be labeled
    differently. Add one sentence: what can an AI illustration *not*
    prove about 1900?

B3. Write a 4-sentence contrast: what would a reformer's report and
    an immigrant's memoir each emphasize about the *same* tenement
    building? Name each account's likely blind spot.

B4. Name the three mechanisms of the Chinese Exclusion Act (1882)
    and, in one sentence each, state which population each affected.

B5. Explain in 3 sentences how Angel Island's interrogation rooms
    followed *logically* from the Exclusion Act's enforcement
    system.

B6. **Error analysis.** "The Exclusion Act ended all Asian
    immigration." Correct the overstatement: whom did it bar, what
    happened to other Asian groups, and how long did the law last?
    (Repealed 1943.)

B7. Analyze one nativist cartoon (adult-selected): (a) describe what
    you see; (b) state the cartoonist's argument; (c) name the
    intended audience; (d) explain what the cartoon reveals about
    the era's prejudices.

## Set C — Labor conflict (Week 9, S2–S3)

C1. Define in one sentence each: strike, lockout, boycott,
    injunction, yellow-dog contract. Each definition must name who
    acts and against whom.

C2. Compare the Knights of Labor and the AFL: who could join, what
    strategy each used, and what happened to each. Three sentences
    per organization.

C3. For Haymarket (1886), Homestead (1892), and Pullman (1894):
    state the trigger in one sentence and the outcome's lesson in
    one sentence each.

C4. The 1900 census recorded about 1.7 million working children aged
    10–15. Write 3 sentences on what this number proves and 2 on
    what it cannot prove.

C5. Explain in 4 sentences why employers usually won strikes in this
    era. Cite the law, force, and money — one example each.

C6. **Explain-your-reasoning.** "The Triangle fire proved factories
    were unreformable." Identify the flaw (the fire *produced* about
    30 safety laws) and rewrite the claim using the
    conditions-plus-publicity-plus-organization formula.

C7. A steelworker in 1890 is unskilled and Black. Which union would
    admit him, and what does that reveal about each union's theory?
    4 sentences.

## Set D — Reform evaluation (Week 10, S2–S3)

D1. Match each muckraker to target and result: Ida Tarbell, Lincoln
    Steffens, Upton Sinclair → Standard Oil, city machines,
    meatpacking → antitrust climate, municipal reform, the 1906 food
    laws. For each, write one sentence on the mechanism
    (exposure → pressure → law).

D2. Explain the "aim/result gap" in *The Jungle*: what Sinclair
    wanted vs. what the public demanded vs. what Congress passed.
    4 sentences.

D3. Trace the 19th Amendment's path in 5 steps: Seneca Falls (1848)
    → 15th Amendment disappointment → state vs. federal strategy →
    congressional passage (1919) → Tennessee (August 18, 1920). One
    sentence per step.

D4. Rewrite accurately: "The 19th Amendment gave all women the
    vote." Name the specific barriers that remained (Southern Black
    women; citizenship bars) in 3 sentences.

D5. Define initiative, referendum, and recall: one sentence each,
    each naming who acts.

D6. **Argument prep.** List three pieces of evidence that Progressive
    reform was about *justice* and three that it was about
    *control*. You will use this list in the Week 10 deliberation
    and the assessment essay.

D7. **Explain-your-reasoning.** "Progressives fixed industrial
    America." Qualify the claim in 4 sentences: name one real gain,
    one group left out, and one way the statement needs hedging.
