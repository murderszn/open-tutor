# Unit 02 — Culminating Assessment (Week 10, S5)

**Coverage:** all five unit objectives. **Time:** 60 minutes (the adult
may split across two sessions). **Materials:** assessment only — no
lessons, no key. The learner may use their Investigation A dossier and
timeline.

## Part I — Foundational knowledge (20 points, ~15 min)

Answer each in 1–3 sentences with specific facts.

1. (4) Define *push* and *pull* factors and give one documented
   example of each from this unit.
2. (4) State the years of operation and approximate scale of Ellis
   Island and Angel Island, and name the law that made the Angel
   Island experience different.
3. (4) Name **two** of the three strikes studied (Haymarket 1886,
   Homestead 1892, Pullman 1894) and, for each, state the trigger
   and the outcome in one sentence.
4. (4) State the date and death toll of the Triangle Shirtwaist
   fire, and name **two** specific safety failures that caused the
   deaths.
5. (4) State the ratification date of the 19th Amendment and name
   one group of women for whom it did not deliver practical voting
   rights — and why.

## Part II — Source analysis (15 points, ~15 min)

Read the two excerpts, then answer. (Both are classroom excerpts
modeled on period arguments; treat them as primary-source-style
evidence for analysis practice.)

> **Source A** — A labor organizer's speech, 1894 (modeled on
> Debs-era arguments): "The Pullman worker pays the company's rent
> with the company's wage cut. He is free to leave the town — and
> free to starve outside it. This is not liberty; it is a company
> that owns the worker's home, his store, and his church, and calls
> the arrangement freedom."
>
> **Source B** — A company statement, 1894 (modeled on Pullman-era
> management arguments): "The town of Pullman is a model of comfort
> and order. Wages were reduced because business fell; rents were
> kept because the town's costs did not fall. No man is compelled to
> remain. The boycott is an attack on property and on every man's
> right to work unhindered."

6. (5) For **each** source, identify the creator's position, the
   intended audience, and the purpose. (3 points: one per element
   across both sources, done well.)
7. (5) Where do the sources **agree** on facts (name one shared
   fact) and where do they **differ** (name one difference)? For
   the difference, explain whether it comes from *disputed facts*
   or *different values*.
8. (5) Which source's argument is stronger on the question "was the
   Pullman system fair?" — defend your choice with one piece of
   evidence from the sources and one fact from Lesson 4. (Either
   answer is defensible if argued with evidence.)

## Part III — Argument essay (15 points, ~25 min)

9. (15) Answer the unit's essential question in 250–350 words:
   *"Millions of people came to an industrializing America between
   1865 and 1920, built its cities, and fought over its future —
   who paid the price of that growth, and who got to decide what
   'reform' meant?"*

   Your essay must:
   - State a clear thesis in the first paragraph.
   - Cite **at least four specific facts** from at least three
     different lessons (names, dates, laws, figures).
   - Address **both** halves of the question (who paid the price;
     who defined reform).
   - Include **one counterclaim** and your response to it.
   - End with one sentence on what this era suggests about
     immigration or labor debates today — phrased as a *question
     for further inquiry*, not a policy position.

**Scoring:** Part I: 20 · Part II: 15 · Part III: 15 (thesis 3,
evidence 5, both halves 3, counterclaim 2, closing question 2).
**Total: 50 points.** Readiness rule: 35+ with no zero on any part →
proceed to Unit 03. See `answer-key.md` for scoring guidance.
