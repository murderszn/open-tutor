# Unit 02 Investigations & Project

Two linked investigations. Investigation A runs across Weeks 7–9
(three project sessions); Investigation B is a single Week 10 session.
Both feed the Week 10 deliberation and the culminating assessment.

## Investigation A — *The Journey Dossier*

**Driving question:** *What did migration actually cost — and build —
for one immigrant group and one real individual?*

### Materials

- Lessons 1–4 and the resource pack (`resource-pack.md`): Ellis
  Island, Angel Island, Census, and Library of Congress links
  (checked 2026-10-21).
- One group file per learner, chosen from the list below (adult
  assigns or learner chooses; every group gets at least one
  investigator). The *individual story* must be a real, documented
  person — found via the resource pack, not invented.

### Group files (each is a real historical migration stream)

1. **Southern Italians to New York / Chicago, 1880–1920:** push
   (land hunger, taxes), pull (industrial and construction work),
   settlement (Little Italies), return migration (many went back).
   *Question:* Why did so many Italians *return* — and what does
   that say about "the American dream"?
2. **Eastern European Jews to New York, 1880–1920:** push
   (pogroms, conscription, legal restrictions), pull (garment work,
   religious freedom), settlement (Lower East Side), institutions
   (synagogues, unions, Yiddish press). *Question:* How did a
   community build its own institutions *before* reformers arrived?
3. **Chinese to the American West, 1850–1940:** railroad and mine
   labor, then exclusion (1882), then the Angel Island decades.
   *Question:* How did a community survive a law designed to make it
   disappear?
4. **Mexicans to the Southwest, 1900–1920:** railroad and farm
   labor, revolution-era displacement (1910–1920), circular
   migration. *Question:* How did the border region's labor needs
   shape a migration the law barely regulated — yet?
5. **African American migrants to Northern cities, 1910–1920:**
   internal migrants (not immigrants) pushed by Jim Crow and pulled
   by wartime factory jobs — the Great Migration's first wave.
   *Question:* How does internal migration compare with
   immigration — same cities, different doors?

### Session plan

- **Session 1 (Week 7, S3) — Launch.** Choose the group file.
  Build a push/pull table: at least three push and three pull
  factors, each with a source note (resource-pack link or lesson
  citation). Draft the dossier's timeline skeleton: home →
  journey → arrival → work → community. *Deliverable:* the
  push/pull table and skeleton.
- **Session 2 (Week 9, S3) — Build.** Fill the timeline with dated,
  sourced facts: one arrival/inspection detail, one work detail
  (job, hours, wage where documented), one community detail
  (institution, newspaper, mutual-aid society). Find one real
  individual's story (a memoir excerpt, an oral history, an Angel
  Island poem, a documented biography) and summarize it in 5
  sentences with a source citation. *Deliverable:* the filled
  timeline and the individual-story summary.
- **Session 3 (Week 10, S3) — Present.** Produce the final dossier:
  a 2-page written report or a 5-minute presentation covering (a)
  the group's push/pull profile, (b) the journey and arrival,
  (c) work and community, (d) the individual story, and (e) a
  one-paragraph answer to the driving question. Present to the
  adult (or family); the adult scores with the rubric below.
  *Deliverable:* the dossier + presentation.

### Investigation A rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | Excellent (9–10) | Adequate (6–8) | Needs work (0–5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Push/pull analysis | 3+ each, sourced, distinguished correctly | Factors listed, mostly correct | Confused or unsourced |
| Timeline accuracy | Dated, sourced facts; arrival, work, community all covered | Facts present, some unsourced or undated | Invented or missing sections |
| Individual story | Real person, documented, cited; 5-sentence summary accurate | Real person, thin citation | Fictional or uncited |
| Driving-question answer | Paragraph answers *cost* and *build* with two specifics | Answers one side only | Vague or missing |
*Total 40. Readiness: 28+ with no zeros → the research habits are solid.*

## Investigation B — *The Reform Report Card* (single session, Week 10, S3)

**Driving question:** *Did one Progressive reform do what its
supporters promised?*

### Materials

- Lessons 4–5, the practice Set D evidence lists, and one
  resource-pack reference on the chosen reform.

### Steps

1. Choose one reform: the 1906 food laws, New York's post-Triangle
   safety laws, the 19th Amendment, or initiative/referendum/recall
   in one state.
2. State the reform's **promise** in one sentence (what supporters
   said it would do), citing a lesson or source.
3. Find **two pieces of evidence** about its effects — one showing
   it worked, one showing its limits (or an unintended outcome).
4. Assign a **grade** (A–F) with a 5-sentence justification that
   cites both pieces of evidence and answers: *for whom* did it
   work, and *whom* did it leave out?
5. *Deliverable:* a one-page report card (promise, evidence,
   grade, justification). The adult scores with the rubric below.

### Investigation B rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | Excellent (5) | Adequate (3–4) | Needs work (0–2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Promise stated | Accurate, cited | Roughly right | Missing/invented |
| Evidence (works) | Specific, sourced | General | Absent |
| Evidence (limits) | Specific, sourced | General | Absent |
| Grade justified | Grade follows from both evidences; names who was left out | Grade asserted, thin link | Grade without evidence |
*Total 20. Readiness: 14+ → ready for the assessment essay.*

## Safety, boundaries, and adult role

- All sources are documentary and textual; no graphic imagery. The
  adult previews every primary source and every nativist cartoon
  before the learner sees it.
- The individual story in Investigation A must be a *real,
  documented person* — never invent a biography and present it as
  history. If no individual story can be documented for the group,
  the learner writes about the group's documented institutions
  instead and notes the gap honestly.
- No learner names, photos, or personal information appear in any
  dossier — the repository rule. Dossiers live in private storage.
