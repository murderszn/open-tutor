# Unit 03 — Practice Sets

Session-mapped sets. Solutions are in `answer-key.md` (adult only).
Do not write answers here.

## Set A — Week 11: Continental expansion (Lessons: Lesson 1; S2–S4 sessions)

A1. Define *allotment* and *assimilation* in one sentence each, using
the Dawes Act as your example.
A2. The Great Sioux Reservation: about 60 million acres (1868) →
about 21.7 million (1877) → about 12.7 million (after 1887). Compute
the total acreage lost between 1868 and 1887, and state in one
sentence what caused each of the two drops.
A3. A student says, "Railroads only helped settlers." Give two
counterexamples from Lesson 1 (one about soldiers/hunters, one about
land policy) that complicate that claim.
A4. Why do historians report Wounded Knee deaths as a range
(about 150–300) rather than a single number? Give two reasons from
the lesson.
A5. Short response: Was the Homestead Act's promise — 160 acres for
five years' work — fair to Native nations? Answer in three sentences
with one piece of evidence and one acknowledgment of a competing
view.
A6. Error analysis: "The Dawes Act protected tribal land by giving
each family its own farm." Identify the error and correct it with
the surplus-sale mechanism and the 138-million-to-48-million-acre
figures.

## Set B — Week 12: Empire debate (Lessons: Lessons 2–3; S2–S4 sessions)

B1. State the Treaty of Paris's four territorial outcomes and the
$20 million term. (One sentence per outcome.)
B2. Compare an anti-imperialist argument and an expansionist
argument from Lesson 2. For each, give one supporting and one
weakening piece of evidence.
B3. Classify: Puerto Rico (1905), the Philippines (1905), Cuba
(1905), Panama Canal Zone (1914) — territory, colony, protectorate,
or canal zone? One mechanism of U.S. control for each.
B4. The Roosevelt Corollary: state what it claimed, then name two
interventions carried out under it and one interest each served.
B5. Short response: Did the Open Door policy help China? Answer in
three sentences distinguishing the policy's stated aim from its
effect during the Boxer Rebellion.
B6. Error analysis: "Cuba was fully independent after 1902 because
the Teller Amendment promised it." Find the error (Platt Amendment)
and correct it with one intervention the U.S. actually carried out.

## Set C — Week 13: WWI causes and entry (Lesson: Lesson 4; S2–S4 sessions)

C1. Sort into long-term cause, trigger, or context for U.S. entry:
alliance system; Sarajevo assassination; U.S. trade with
Britain/France; Zimmermann Telegram; unrestricted submarine warfare
(resumed 1917). One reason per item.
C2. Timeline: place these eight events in order and mark each C
(cause), T (trigger), or X (context): July Crisis (1914); *Lusitania*
(May 7, 1915); Sussex pledge (1916); unrestricted submarine warfare
resumed (Feb 1, 1917); Zimmermann Telegram published (Mar 1, 1917);
Wilson's war message (Apr 2, 1917); declaration of war (Apr 6, 1917);
Armistice (Nov 11, 1918).
C3. Why did the U.S. not enter the war in May 1915? Give two reasons
from Lesson 4 (pledges; divided opinion) and one reason it entered in
April 1917.
C4. Wilson called the U.S. an "associated" power, not an ally. Explain
in two sentences what that wording preserved for the peace talks.
C5. Short response: Was American neutrality (1914–1917) genuinely
neutral? Give one argument that it was and one that it was not,
then a one-sentence judgment.
C6. Error analysis: "Germany's alliance with Mexico caused WWI."
Identify two errors (the telegram was an offer, not an alliance; it
concerned U.S. entry, not the war's outbreak) and correct them.

## Set D — Week 14: War at home and the failed peace (Lesson: Lesson 5; S2–S4 sessions)

D1. Name the three Senate factions in the League fight, what each
wanted, and how each voted on November 19, 1919 (with and without
reservations).
D2. Explain the "clear and present danger" test and why it did not
protect Eugene V. Debs. Two sentences each part.
D3. Compare the war's death tolls: 53,402 battle deaths; 63,114
non-battle deaths; about 675,000 influenza deaths. In three
sentences, explain what the comparison shows about counting war's
cost.
D4. Short response: Did World War I expand or contract American
democracy? Give one expansion and one contraction, then a
one-sentence judgment.
D5. The Senate failed to ratify the Treaty of Versailles on
November 19, 1919 (twice) and March 19, 1920. Name one explanation
that blames Wilson, one that blames the Senate, and one that blames
the treaty itself.
D6. Retrieval round (Weeks 11–13): (a) What did the Dawes Act do to
tribal land? (b) What did the U.S. pay Spain for the Philippines?
(c) What did the Zimmermann Telegram offer Mexico?
