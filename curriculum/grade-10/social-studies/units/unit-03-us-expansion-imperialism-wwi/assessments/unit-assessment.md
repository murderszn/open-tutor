# Unit 03 — Culminating Assessment (Week 14, S5)

Answer all parts. Solutions and scoring guidance are in
`answer-key.md` (adult only). This assessment is not standardized or
validated; it measures this unit's objectives only.

## Part A — Timeline and terms (20 points, 2 points each)

Place these ten events in chronological order and give the year of
each: Treaty of Versailles signed; Wounded Knee; *Lusitania* sunk;
Platt Amendment; Dawes Act; Armistice; Treaty of Paris (1898);
Zimmermann Telegram published; Panama Canal opens; U.S. declares war
on Germany.

## Part B — Source analysis (20 points)

Read these two short paraphrased positions (both adapted from
public-domain-era arguments; no invented quotations):

- **Position 1 (expansionist, 1899):** "America cannot abandon the
  Philippines. We have a duty to govern them until they can govern
  themselves, and our commerce and navy need Pacific bases."
- **Position 2 (anti-imperialist, 1899):** "A republic cannot rule a
  people without their consent. The Philippines did not ask to be
  governed by us, and holding them betrays the principles of 1776."

1. For each position, name the underlying principle it appeals to
   (duty/benevolence vs. consent) and one piece of evidence from
   Lessons 2–3 that supports it. (8 points)
2. For each position, name one piece of evidence from Lessons 2–3
   that weakens it. (8 points)
3. In two sentences, state which position is stronger on the
   evidence and why. (4 points)

## Part C — Causal essay (30 points)

*Why did the United States enter World War I in April 1917 rather
than in 1914 or 1915?* Write 250–400 words that:

- distinguish long-term causes (alliances, nationalism, militarism,
  imperial rivalry) from triggers and context (10 points);
- use at least three dated pieces of evidence (the *Lusitania*,
  submarine warfare's resumption, the Zimmermann Telegram) (10 points);
- address the strongest counterargument (the case for staying out)
  in at least two sentences (5 points);
- state a clear thesis in the first paragraph (5 points).

## Part D — The peace (20 points)

1. Name the three Senate factions in the League fight and what each
   wanted. (6 points)
2. State the outcome of the November 19, 1919 votes and the
   March 19, 1920 vote, with the vote counts. (8 points)
3. In three sentences, explain one structural reason (about the
   Constitution or the Senate's role) why a president's peace plan
   can fail at home even after a war is won. (6 points)

## Part E — Reflection (10 points)

In 3–5 sentences, answer the unit's essential question: *Did
expansion between 1865 and 1920 make America more democratic, or did
it put democracy's promises to their hardest test?* Use at least two
pieces of evidence from different weeks of the unit. There is no
single correct verdict; the score reflects evidence use, reasoning,
and acknowledgment of a counterargument.

**Total: 100 points.** 90–100: ready for Unit 04. 75–89: review the
missed objectives with practice Sets A–D and re-check. Below 75:
re-teach the weakest objective with the adult before starting
Unit 04.
