# Unit 03 — Teacher Guide (adult only)

## How to teach this unit

**Pacing:** five 50-minute sessions per week for four weeks (Weeks
11–14). Lessons anchor S1 (Week 12 carries two lessons); S2 is close
reading, S3 investigation, S4 writing/deliberation, S5 review and
check. If a week runs short, protect S1 and S4; move S5's retrieval
round into the next week's opening.

**Sensitive content:** conquest, massacre, colonial war, wartime
repression, racial violence, pandemic. All sources are documentary
and textual; no graphic imagery is used. Preview every primary source
yourself. Period cartoons and posters contain ethnic stereotypes:
frame them as evidence of the era's prejudices, never as neutral
humor. The Wounded Knee and Philippine-war death tolls are ranges —
insist the learner state the range and its reason, never a false
precise number.

**Reading load:** 20–40-line public-domain excerpts in paraphrase,
with short attributed quotations (Wilson's war message, the
Zimmermann Telegram text, Holmes's "clear and present danger").
Statutory and treaty language is paraphrased; the adult may substitute
the full public-domain text from the resource pack where stamina
allows.

## Week-by-week notes

**Week 11 (Lesson 1):** The hard idea is on-paper vs. in-practice.
Use the Fort Laramie acreage sequence (60M → 21.7M → 12.7M) as the
anchor. The image study's point is method: observation vs.
inference vs. what an illustration cannot prove. Do not let the
illustration be cited as evidence of anything historical.

**Week 12 (Lessons 2–3):** Two lessons in one week — Lesson 2 (S1)
and Lesson 3 (S2). Keep the debate structure visible: both sides
mixed principle with interest. The four-forms table (territory,
colony, protectorate, canal zone, Open Door) is the week's key
product; Investigation A session 1 builds on it.

**Week 13 (Lesson 4):** The hard idea is causal vocabulary:
long-term cause vs. trigger vs. context. Insist the learner classify
before arguing. The timeline investigation (S3) should produce a
labeled timeline, not a list of dates.

**Week 14 (Lesson 5):** The hard idea is the three-faction Senate
split. Students collapse it into "Wilson vs. the Senate"; correct
that every time. The Versailles memo (Investigation B) is the week's
key product. The assessment's Part C essay is the unit's best
evidence of Objective 4; read it for thesis, dated evidence, and
counterargument, not for length.

## Differentiation

- **Reading access:** pre-teach vocabulary before each lesson (the
  lesson lists name the terms); pair-read Parts 2–3 of longer lessons.
- **Alternate response modes:** tables, timelines, charts, and oral
  answers are accepted wherever a lesson says so; never penalize a
  mode change.
- **Extensions:** each lesson carries one; use them only after the
  core practice is complete.
- **Re-teaching triggers:** if the Week 12 quiz shows misses on
  treaty terms or the four forms, re-teach with Lesson 2, Part 1 and
  Lesson 3, Part 1 before starting Week 13. If the assessment's Part C
  lacks a counterargument, re-teach with Lesson 4's Part 3 before
  Unit 04.

## Answer key and scoring

Every practice, quiz, assessment, and project item is solved in
`answer-key.md`. Open responses carry model reasoning and acceptable
variation — accept any answer that uses the same evidence and
reasoning, even if worded differently. Project rubrics are in
`project.md`; grade the evidence and reasoning, not the verdict.

## Evidence of readiness for Unit 04

The learner can: sequence 1865–1920 events with causes attached;
state treaty terms and vote outcomes accurately; classify forms of
U.S. control; distinguish cause, trigger, and context; and write a
claim supported by two pieces of evidence with a counterargument
acknowledged. Assessment score 75+ with Part C's counterargument
present indicates readiness; below 75, re-teach the weakest
objective first.
