# Grade 10 Social Studies

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are
delivered; the eight units are planned, not yet written. See
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained
for one grade-10 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 10 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-10 **U.S. history after Reconstruction**
course, circa 1865 to the present, built on the C3 Framework's inquiry arc:
Reconstruction, industrialization, and contested citizenship; immigration,
urbanization, labor, and reform; U.S. expansion, imperialism, and World War I;
the 1920s, Great Depression, and New Deal; World War II's home front, rights
struggles, and global role; the Cold War, the civil rights movement, and
social movements; late-twentieth-century politics, economy, and culture; and a
recent-U.S.-history capstone using evidence, media literacy, and historical
inquiry.

Each of the eight units runs four weeks at about five 50-minute sessions per
week: a concept launch, a close-reading and skills session, a source
investigation or application session, an evidence-based writing or deliberation
session, and a review-and-check session. Learners work with public-domain
primary sources (amendment texts, executive orders, government-archived
speeches), maps and datasets, structured deliberations, and argument writing.
Two diagnostic weeks open the year, with midyear and final review weeks.

This is a proposed pathway, not a universal requirement: high-school
social-studies course order varies by system. A guiding adult checks placement
before starting and previews every primary source and topic for mature themes
— racial violence, conquest, war, incarceration, and movement repression are
central to this course and are handled with age-appropriate, adult-supervised
inquiry (see the audit's source and safety notes).

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. **Analyze Reconstruction and contested citizenship:** explain the 13th,
   14th, and 15th Amendments, federal Reconstruction policies, Black political
   participation and its violent suppression, and how the Compromise of 1877
   reshaped citizenship and rights. (C3 D2.His.1, D2.His.2, D2.His.6.9-12;
   D2.Civ.12.9-12)
2. **Explain industrialization's transformation of work and daily life:**
   describe how railroads, factories, and corporations changed production,
   labor, and cities; analyze labor's responses and reform movements.
   (D2.His.1, D2.His.14.9-12)
3. **Analyze immigration and urbanization, 1865–1920:** explain push/pull
   factors and settlement patterns, describe the new industrial city, analyze
   nativism and exclusion, and evaluate Progressive-era reforms. (D2.His.1,
   D2.His.4.9-12)
4. **Evaluate U.S. expansion, imperialism, and World War I:** explain
   continental expansion's consequences, debate overseas empire, and analyze
   WWI's multiple causes and domestic effects. (D2.His.4, D2.His.8,
   D2.His.14.9-12)
5. **Explain the 1920s and the Great Depression:** analyze the Depression's
   causes, describe its human cost, and evaluate New Deal programs — the
   expanded federal role and the people the programs left out.
   (D2.His.14.9-12; D2.Civ.5, D2.Civ.13.9-12)
6. **Evaluate World War II's domestic transformation:** explain mobilization
   and the home front; analyze rights under pressure (Japanese American
   incarceration, the Double V campaign); assess America's emerging global
   role. (D2.His.4, D2.His.10, D2.His.12.9-12)
7. **Analyze the Cold War and the civil rights movement:** explain Cold War
   rivalry at home and abroad; evaluate the movement's strategies,
   achievements, and unfinished work; compare with other social movements.
   (D2.His.3, D2.His.5.9-12; D2.Civ.2, D2.Civ.14.9-12)
8. **Explain late-twentieth-century change:** analyze the 1970s crises, the
   conservative turn, deindustrialization, demographic and cultural change,
   and the Cold War's end. (D2.His.2, D2.His.5, D2.His.14.9-12)
9. **Investigate recent U.S. history with evidence and media literacy:** frame
   historical questions about recent decades, evaluate how the same events are
   narrated across sources and time, and defend a reasoned interpretation.
   (D1.1–D1.5, D2.His.6, D2.His.16.9-12)
10. **Work with sources, argue from evidence, and reason civically:** gather
    and evaluate sources across viewpoints, integrate evidence into reasoned
    arguments, critique arguments for credibility and missing perspectives,
    present conclusions, and assess options for informed action.
    (D3.1–D3.4, D4.1–D4.4, D4.6, D4.7.9-12)

Standards references are to the College, Career, and Civic Life (C3) Framework
for Social Studies State Standards (National Council for the Social Studies),
grades 9–12 band. Codes were verified during the audit (see §4 of the
scope-and-sequence); descriptions there are brief paraphrases — check the
authoritative framework for exact wording. **No state adoption, accreditation,
or alignment certification is claimed.**

## Existing materials and their status

The audit (2026-10-08, against `main`) found **no existing files** in this
track's folder — it is planned from scratch. Reusable material inventoried
elsewhere in the repository (full table in
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md) §1):

- **Adult-side references:** `resources/government_basics.md` (branches,
  federalism, checks and balances), `resources/united_states_understanding_and_principles.md`
  (constitutional principles, amendment mechanism), `resources/black_excellence_figures.md`
  (adult pre-selects figures for U01/U06), `resources/black-panther-party-rise-1970s.md`
  (U06/U07), `resources/wars_fundamentals.md` (U03/U05/U06 concept vocabulary),
  `resources/supply_and_demand_economics.md` (U02/U04/U07 economics background).
- **Dataset candidates:** `resources/us_presidents.csv` for U01–U08 timeline
  tasks (note: `fact` strings are templated boilerplate — usable for
  sequencing, not content); `resources/us_states.csv` for U02 migration/map
  tasks (note: figures are approximations).
- **Enrichment (optional):** the `assignments/social-studies/history-of-war`
  assignment — optional reference for U03/U05; the track's war content is
  authored from primary sources.
- **Gaps to build:** all lessons, teacher guides, separate answer keys,
  quizzes, assessments, diagnostics, resource packs, generated images, and the
  R00 review package — authored in U01–U08 and R00.
