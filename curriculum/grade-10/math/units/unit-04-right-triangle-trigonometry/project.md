# Project — Clinometer Height Investigation

**Unit 04 · Sessions 14–15 (studios), Session 17 (gallery) · Weeks 17–18**

## Driving question

*How tall is something you cannot climb — and how do you know your
answer is trustworthy?*

## Materials

A protractor, a drinking straw, string, a small weight (a washer or
a paperclip), tape, a measuring tape (at least 30 m) or a trundle
wheel, a notebook, a calculator, and the unit's tree-height
illustration (`assets/tree-height-measurement-illustration.png`) as
the setup reference. A second observer is needed for the baseline
measurement.

**Safety.** Work outdoors only with adult supervision. Do not climb
the object being measured, do not stand in a road or driveway to
sight it, and do not point the sighting straw at the sun. If no
suitable tall object is safely accessible, use the simulation
alternative below — the mathematics is identical.

---

## Part 1 — Build the clinometer and measure (Session 14, ~50 min)

1. **Build.** Tape the straw along the protractor's straight edge
   so you can sight through it. Tie the string to the protractor's
   center mark (or tape it at the center) with the weight hanging
   freely, so the string marks the vertical. When you tilt the
   straw upward by an angle, the string hangs at
   90° − (that angle) on the protractor scale — read carefully and
   record how your instrument converts string position to sight
   angle. Calibrate: sight a horizontal target and confirm the
   reading is 0°.
2. **Choose the object.** A tree, a flagpole, or a building — tall,
   vertical, with flat ground between it and your observing point
   and a clear line of sight to its top.
3. **Measure.** One person sights the top through the straw while
   the other reads the angle. Measure: (a) the angle of elevation
   to the top; (b) the horizontal baseline from the object to the
   observer's feet (tape along the ground, not sloped); (c) the
   observer's eye height. Take each measurement twice and record
   both readings; if the two angle readings differ by more than
   2°, take a third and note the spread.
4. **Sketch and label.** Draw the triangle: baseline, eye height,
   sight line, and the vertical side above eye level. Mark which
   side is opposite the measured angle and which is adjacent.

**Simulation alternative.** In GeoGebra (free, no account), place a
vertical segment of unknown height, an observer point at a known
horizontal distance, and measure the angle with the angle tool; or
use the Lesson 02 worked-example numbers as a dry run and then
re-derive them. The adult previews GeoGebra for advertising.

## Part 2 — Compute two ways and report (Session 15, ~50 min)

1. **Method 1 (tangent).** Height above eye level =
   baseline × tan(angle). Total height = that value + eye height.
   Show every step, including degree-mode confirmation.
2. **Method 2 (second observation).** Move to a second observing
   point a measured distance closer to (or farther from) the object
   along the same baseline, measure the new angle, and solve the
   two-observation system from Lesson 05's applied task
   (two tangent equations, two unknowns). Compare with Method 1.
3. **Uncertainty note.** Your two angle readings differ by some
   amount; recompute Method 1 with the angle 1° higher and 1° lower
   and report the resulting height range. State the height to the
   precision this range justifies (usually 2–3 significant figures
   — never six calculator digits).
4. **Report (one page).** Driving question, labeled sketch, both
   methods with work, the uncertainty range, a reasonableness
   check (is the height plausible against the baseline and angle?
   does Method 2 agree with Method 1?), and one sentence on what
   would improve the measurement (longer baseline, steadier
   instrument, repeated readings).

## Deliverables

- Labeled sketch with all three measurements (both readings each).
- Method 1 computation with degree-mode note and eye height added.
- Method 2 computation and comparison with Method 1.
- Uncertainty range and correctly rounded final height.
- One-page report addressing the driving question.

## Rubric (24 points)

| Criterion | 4 points | 2–3 points | 0–1 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instrument and measurements (4) | Clinometer calibrated (0° check recorded); baseline, angle (twice), eye height all recorded with units | One element missing or uncalibrated, but measurements usable | Measurements missing, unlabelled, or without units |
| Method 1 (4) | Correct ratio chosen, work shown, eye height added, degree mode noted | Correct ratio but eye height missing, or work incomplete | Wrong ratio or no work shown |
| Method 2 (4) | Second observation taken; two-equation system set up and solved correctly | Second observation taken but system set up or solved with errors | No second method, or method unrelated to the problem |
| Comparison and uncertainty (4) | Methods compared numerically; ±1° range computed; final height rounded to justified precision | Comparison or range attempted but incomplete | No comparison and no uncertainty discussion |
| Reasonableness and report (4) | Plausibility check against baseline/angle; report answers the driving question; one improvement named | Check or improvement missing; report incomplete | No check; report does not address the question |
| Communication (4) | Sketch labeled (H/O/A relative to the angle); steps readable in order; units throughout | Sketch partly labeled or steps out of order | Sketch unlabelled; work unreadable or unitless |

**Total: 24 points.** 16+ meets the project standard; below 16,
revisit the clinometer calibration and the eye-height step before
the unit assessment.
