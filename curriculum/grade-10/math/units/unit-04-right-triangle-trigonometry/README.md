# Unit 04 — Right-Triangle Trigonometry and Applications

**Grade 10 · Math · Geometry pathway · Weeks 15–18 of the course year**
(draft, delivered 2026-11-02 for issue #46; not educator-reviewed)

## Overview

Unit 03 taught the learner that similar figures have proportional sides.
This unit cashes that fact in. Take any right triangle with a 38° acute
angle: no matter how big or small the triangle is, the ratio of the
side opposite that angle to the hypotenuse is always the same number.
That number has a name — the **sine of 38°** — and the same is true of
every acute angle and of the cosine and tangent ratios. Trigonometry is
not a new bag of tricks; it is similarity, named and tabulated.

The unit proceeds in three movements. First, the learner *defines* the
ratios from similarity (Lesson 01), proves the complementary-angle
relationship, and earns the exact values for 30°, 45°, and 60° from the
two special triangles. Second, the learner *solves* right triangles and
applies them: angles of elevation and depression, multi-step problems
that combine trigonometry with the Pythagorean Theorem (Lesson 02).
Third, the learner *generalizes*: the laws of sines and cosines, derived
from an altitude, solve triangles that are not right (Lessons 03–04),
including the ambiguous SSA case, the area formula
(1/2)·a·b·sin C, and surveying-style applications. Lesson 05 is a
synthesis and error-analysis lesson that also serves as the midyear
review bridge: it reconnects trigonometry to Units 01–03 (proof,
similarity, coordinates) and names the errors that most often sink an
otherwise correct solution.

The essential question running through all four weeks is: **when a
triangle is only partly known, which relationship — a ratio, a law, or
the Pythagorean Theorem — earns the missing piece, and how do you know
your answer is reasonable?**

## Prerequisites

- Unit 03 (all of it): similarity defined through rigid motions and
  dilations, the AA criterion, proportional sides, the Pythagorean
  Theorem proved by similarity. Without AA, the Lesson 01 definition
  ("the ratio depends only on the angle") has no justification.
- Unit 01: precise definitions, two-column and paragraph proof with a
  stated reason for every step, and the parallel-lines/transversal
  theorems (used in the Lesson 03 altitude derivations).
- Solving linear and quadratic equations in one variable; simplifying
  radicals (√2, √3, and rationalizing denominators where it clarifies).
- The Pythagorean Theorem in computational form (a² + b² = c²) and its
  converse, from Unit 03 and the Weeks 1–2 diagnostic.
- Calculator fluency: degree mode, sin/cos/tan and their inverses, and
  the habit of checking the mode before trusting a display. No prior
  trigonometry is assumed — the ratios are defined here from the
  ground up.

The Weeks 1–2 diagnostic already verified equation solving and radical
simplification; this unit re-teaches them in context where needed.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Define sine, cosine, and tangent for an acute angle as ratios in a
   right triangle; justify from similarity (AA) that each ratio depends
   only on the angle, not on the triangle's size; and state and use the
   complementary-angle relationship sin θ = cos(90° − θ) (and its
   cosine/tangent counterparts). (G-SRT.6, G-SRT.7)
2. Give exact values of sin, cos, and tan for 30°, 45°, and 60°,
   derived from the 45–45–90 and 30–60–90 triangles, and use them
   without a calculator where they apply. (G-SRT.6, G-SRT.7)
3. Solve right triangles — find unknown sides and angles — using
   trigonometric ratios and the Pythagorean Theorem, including angles
   of elevation and depression and multi-step problems; report answers
   with accuracy appropriate to the given measurements. (G-SRT.8;
   N-Q.1, N-Q.3)
4. Derive the laws of sines and cosines from an altitude and the area
   formula A = (1/2)·a·b·sin C; use them to solve general triangles,
   including the ambiguous SSA case (recognizing zero, one, or two
   solutions); and choose correctly between a ratio, the law of sines,
   the law of cosines, and the Pythagorean Theorem for a given set of
   known parts. (G-SRT.9 (+), G-SRT.10 (+), G-SRT.11 (+))
5. Model a physical situation with a triangle (height, distance, or
   force), carry out the solution with units and a reasonableness
   check, and explain in writing why a computed answer must be
   checked against the diagram (wrong side identified, wrong mode,
   missing eye height, or an impossible triangle). (G-SRT.8,
   G-SRT.11 (+); G-MG.1, N-Q.1, N-Q.3)

Objectives 7–8 of the track objectives (see the track README) are the
ones in play in this unit; objectives 1–6 are revisited in Week 18.

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, High
School Geometry — Similarity, Right Triangles, and Trigonometry domain
(descriptions verified against the published standards text on
2026-11-02; wording here is paraphrase). This unit addresses:

- **G-SRT.6** — by similarity, side ratios in right triangles are
  properties of the angles, giving the definitions of the
  trigonometric ratios for acute angles (Lesson 01).
- **G-SRT.7** — explain and use the relationship between the sine and
  cosine of complementary angles (Lesson 01; revisited in Lesson 05).
- **G-SRT.8** — use trigonometric ratios and the Pythagorean Theorem
  to solve right triangles in applied problems (Lessons 02, 04, 05;
  project).
- **G-SRT.9 (+)** — derive the area formula A = (1/2)·a·b·sin C with
  an auxiliary altitude (Lesson 03).
- **G-SRT.10 (+)** — prove the laws of sines and cosines and use them
  to solve problems (Lesson 03).
- **G-SRT.11 (+)** — understand and apply the laws of sines and
  cosines to find unknown measurements in right and non-right
  triangles, e.g., surveying problems and resultant forces
  (Lessons 03–04; project extension).
- **N-Q.1, N-Q.3** — units guide multi-step solutions; accuracy is
  chosen appropriate to measurement limits (Lessons 02, 04, 05).
- **G-MG.1** — geometric shapes and measures model physical objects
  (Lessons 02, 04; project).

The (+) standards are the advanced (+) content of the CCSS high-school

## Accessibility and supports

- Every triangle task has a text description of the diagram (which
  angle is marked, which sides are given) so the work does not depend
  on reading a picture alone; the generated illustration in Lesson 02
  carries a full text-only alternative.
- Calculator-free exact-value routes are taught alongside calculator
  routes; a learner who cannot use a calculator still completes the
  special-triangle and 3–4–5 work.
- Ratio tables (sin/cos/tan for whole degrees) may be used as an
  alternative to a calculator; the adult checks the table's degree
  column, not the learner's memory.
- Proof skeletons (statement column pre-filled) for the Lesson 03
  derivations; paragraph-proof option always allowed.
- Oral explanation accepted for exit checks; the adult scribes if
  needed.
- Extension: the ambiguous-case construction proof, the
  two-observation height method, and the resultant-force vector
  argument are marked in the teacher guide.
