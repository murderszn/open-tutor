# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Right-Triangle Trigonometry and Applications

For the guiding adult. Student materials never contain answer keys; all
solutions are in `answer-key.md`. This guide covers pacing, per-lesson
teaching moves, the misconception table, project and assessment
administration, and reteach triggers.

## Unit at a glance

- **Time:** 20 sessions × ~50 minutes (course Weeks 15–18). Do not
  compress the similarity-ratio lab (S2), the ambiguous-case lab
  (S7), or the two project studios (S14–S15) — ratio meaning and
  the supplement test are built there, not in the lessons.
- **Non-negotiables:** (1) sides are named relative to the chosen
  angle, before any ratio is written; (2) degree mode is checked
  before the first calculator computation of every session; (3)
  every applied solution ends with an eye-height sentence and a
  reasonableness check; (4) every SSA solution contains the
  supplement test as a written step.
- **What counts as done:** the project rubric (≥16/24) *and* the
  unit assessment (≥35/50) — see the readiness bands below. The
  Week-18 quiz is formative; scoring below 20 postpones the
  assessment.

## Per-lesson teaching notes

**Lesson 01 (S1).** The similarity justification is the lesson, not
a preface — spend the time on it. Learners who memorize SOH–CAH–TOA
without the AA argument will name sides relative to the wrong angle
within a week. The special triangles are worth deriving slowly:
the 30–60–90 triangle from halving an equilateral triangle is the
one derivation learners actually remember.

**Lesson 02 (S3).** Demo the eye-height trap physically before the
worked examples: hold a book at eye level and ask what the triangle's
vertical side measures. If time is short, cut independent-practice
problem 6 (the 55° computation), not the applied task — the
illustration task is where the eye-height habit forms. The
two-observation extension is solved in the teacher notes below.

**Lesson 03 (S6).** Derive, don't dictate: the altitude derivation
of both laws in one lesson is ambitious, so let the learner do the
law-of-sines algebra (two expressions for one altitude) and guide
the law-of-cosines substitution line by line. The ambiguous case
needs the compass construction, not just the arithmetic — swing the
arc and count intersections.

**Lesson 04 (S8).** Grade the sentence "I know ___, so I use ___"
before grading the computation. A learner who chooses the tool from
the unknown ("I need a side, so law of sines") will fail SSA and
SAS problems even with perfect arithmetic.

**Lesson 05 (S11).** This is also the midyear synthesis lesson.
Run the Units 01–04 chain as a retrieval exercise first (cover the
lesson text, ask for the four lines), then the error repairs. The
extension (two-observation system) is the project Method 2 in
miniature — teach it here so the studios run themselves.

**Two-observation solution (Lesson 05 applied task, project
Method 2).** With *h* above eye level, *d* the nearer distance,
and gap *g*: tan α = *h*/(*d* + *g*) and tan β = *h*/*d*. For the
lesson's numbers (α = 30°, β = 45°, *g* = 80 m): tan 45° = 1 gives
*h* = *d*; then *h* = (*h* + 80)·tan 30°, so
*h* = 80·tan 30° ÷ (1 − tan 30°) ≈ 46.19 ÷ 0.4226 ≈ 109.3 m above
eye level. Add eye height for the total. One observation alone
gives one equation in two unknowns (*h*, *d*) — unsolvable — which
is the conceptual point.

## Misconception table

| Misconception | Symptom | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Sides named by size, not by angle | sin/cos swapped; sin² + cos² ≠ 1 | Label H/O/A relative to θ before computing |
| Radian mode | sin 30 ≈ −0.988; negative ratios for acute angles | Check DEG display; sanity: acute-angle sines are in (0, 1) |
| Eye height dropped | Elevation answers short by ~1.5 m | "Plus eye height" sentence required |
| Supplement untested (SSA) | One triangle reported where two exist | Supplement test as a written step |
| Law chosen by the unknown | Law of sines applied to SAS | "I know ___, so I use ___" sentence first |
| Middle rounding, late precision | 23.397 m from a tape measure | Carry full precision; round once, at the end |
| Diagram trusted over theorem | "The sketch looks isosceles, so…" | Sketches are not to scale; theorems outrank pictures |

## Project administration

- **Studios (S14–S15):** check clinometer calibration (0° on a
  horizontal sight) before any measurement counts. Reject baselines
  measured on a slope — re-measure along the ground.
- **Gallery (S17):** each report is critiqued on the rubric's
  comparison-and-uncertainty row first; a report without an
  uncertainty range is returned for revision before scoring.
- **Sample (for the adult's calibration, not the learner's):**
  baseline 30 m, angle 36°, eye height 1.6 m → height above eye
  level 30 × tan 36° ≈ 21.81 m, total ≈ 23.4 m, reported as 23 m
  (tape-measure precision). A ±1° angle spread (35°–37°) gives
  about 22.6–24.2 m — the range the learner's uncertainty note should
  resemble in form, not in numbers.

## Readiness bands and reteach triggers

- **Quiz (30):** 20+ proceeds to the assessment. 15–19: reteach
  Lesson 02 (ratios and eye height) with Practice Set B, then
  re-quiz with new numbers. Below 15: reteach Lessons 01–02 fully.
- **Assessment (50):** 35+ meets the unit standard. 28–34: targeted
  reteach on the missed part (A = ratios/laws, B = applications,
  C = proof/explanation) using the corresponding practice set,
  then a fresh parallel problem set. Below 28: revisit Lessons
  01–03 before Unit 05; do not let a shaky ratio foundation carry
  into quadrilaterals and coordinate proofs.
- **Project (24):** 16+ meets the standard. Below 16, the usual
  cause is the eye-height step or the missing uncertainty range —
  fix those two rows and resubmit the report, not the measurements.

## Extensions (for learners who finish early)

- Circumdiameter: prove *a* ÷ sin *A* = 2*R* using a diameter
  through one vertex (Lesson 03 extension).
- Vector resultant derivation from the supplementary angle
  (Lesson 04 extension).
- The two-observation method with three observations and a
  least-squares-style discussion of why averaging helps (project
  extension; no statistics background assumed beyond "more
  measurements, less error").

## Midyear review (S16)

Use the Lesson 05 chain as the retrieval backbone: definitions and
proof (U01), rigid-motion congruence (U02), similarity and
Pythagoras (U03), ratios and laws (U04). The Week-18 quiz then
tests U04; the final-review weeks (35–36) will test the full year.
Record the S16 retrieval gaps in the notebook — they are the
Unit 05 starting point, not a grade.
