# Unit Assessment — Unit 04: Right-Triangle Trigonometry and Applications

**Unit 04 · Week 18, Session 19 · ~50 minutes · 50 points**

**Instructions:** Show a labeled diagram and the sentence "I know
___, so I use ___" for every computational problem. Calculator in
degree mode. Round only at the end. Solutions are in
`../answer-key.md` (adults only). Readiness: 35/50 meets the unit
standard; 28–34 calls for targeted reteach (see the teacher guide);
below 28, revisit Lessons 01–03 before moving to Unit 05.

## Part A — Core skills (5 × 4 points = 20)

1. A right triangle has legs 9 and 12 and hypotenuse 15. For the
   acute angle opposite the leg of length 9, give sin, cos, and tan
   as fractions, and verify sin² + cos² = 1.
2. Give sin 30°, cos 45°, and tan 60° exactly, and in one sentence
   explain why sin 60° = cos 30°.
3. An observer 35 m from a building measures an angle of elevation
   of 41° to its top from eye level 1.6 m above the ground. How
   tall is the building?
4. A triangle has sides 8, 11, and 13. Find its largest angle to the
   nearest tenth of a degree.
5. SSA: *a* = 12, *b* = 20, *A* = 25°. How many triangles are
   there? Justify with the supplement test and give both candidate
   values of *B*.

## Part B — Applied problems (3 × 6 points = 18)

6. Two survey stations are 100 m apart. The angles to a distant
   tower from the stations are 44° and 67°. Find the tower's
   distance from the first station.
7. Two forces of 40 N and 60 N act at a point with a 75° angle
   between them. Find the magnitude of their resultant, and run
   the range check (|60 − 40| to 60 + 40).
8. An observer stands 24 m from a tree and measures an angle of
   elevation of 52° to its top from eye level 1.7 m. (a) How tall
   is the tree? (b) If the observer moves 10 m closer to the tree
   (now 14 m away), what angle of elevation to the same treetop
   should the observer now measure? (Use the height from part (a)
   above eye level.)

## Part C — Proof and explanation (2 × 6 points = 12)

9. Prove that sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 for any acute angle θ, starting
   from the definitions of sine and cosine and the Pythagorean
   Theorem.
10. Explain, in a short paragraph, why SSA does not determine a
    unique triangle — using both the sine-supplement fact and the
    geometric picture of a swinging side — and why this is the same
    warning Unit 02 gave about SSA as a congruence criterion.
