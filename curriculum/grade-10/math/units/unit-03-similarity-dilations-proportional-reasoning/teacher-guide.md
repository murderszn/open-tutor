# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Similarity, Dilations, and Proportional Reasoning

**Audience:** the guiding adult. **Status:** draft, not
educator-reviewed. This guide does not contain student answers
in the lessons' order — solutions live in `answer-key.md`. Read
the lesson's teacher note before teaching each lesson.

## Pacing notes

- **Week 11 (Lessons 01–02):** dilation fluency is the gate.
  Do not move to Lesson 02 until center-elsewhere dilations are
  automatic: the "subtract, scale, add back" step is the
  single most common error in the whole unit. The S2 lab (tracing
  paper or GeoGebra) is not optional enrichment — it is where
  G-SRT.1.a/b stop being slogans.
- **Week 12 (Lesson 03):** the vector proof of the
  side-splitter theorem is the unit's hardest proof. If the
  learner is not ready for vectors, use the area proof (see the
  Lesson 03 teacher note) and return to the vector version as
  extension. The AA criterion, by contrast, should feel easy —
  that contrast is worth naming: one hard proof buys many easy
  arguments.
- **Week 13 (Lesson 04 + project):** protect S12–S14. The project
  is where accuracy, conditions, and honesty about precision are
  learned; a rushed project teaches "plug into the proportion"
  and nothing else. If weather cancels outdoor work, use the
  simulation alternative in `project.md` rather than skipping
  the project.
- **Week 14 (Lesson 05 + close):** Lesson 05 is the bridge to
  Unit 04. Spend the S16 session on the slope-constancy proof
  itself, not just on computing slopes — Unit 04's definition of
  trigonometric ratios is the same argument with triangles
  instead of lines.

## Lesson-by-lesson guidance

### Lesson 01 — Dilations

- **Likely misconception:** applying the origin rule to a
  non-origin center (Guided G4). **Response:** one
  center-elsewhere example at the start of every practice
  session; the "subtract, scale, add back" card stays on the
  desk until the learner no longer needs it.
- **Likely misconception:** "dilation changes angles."
  **Response:** measure one angle before and after in the lab;
  the angle is preserved because the dilation scales both
  legs of the angle by the same factor (similar triangles in
  miniature — preview of Lesson 02).
- **Reteach trigger:** if Practice Set A scores below 6/8,
  re-teach the center-elsewhere rule with tracing paper before
  Lesson 02.

### Lesson 02 — Similarity defined

- **Likely misconception:** "same type of figure means similar"
  (all rectangles, all rhombuses, all isosceles triangles).
  **Response:** the Lesson 02 rectangle non-example (4×6 vs
  5×8) and Practice B2/B8, repeated until the learner checks
  ratios unprompted.
- **Likely misconception:** writing a similarity statement
  without checking the order (Guided G3). **Response:** require
  the ratio table with matched pairs before any "~" statement
  is accepted.
- **Reteach trigger:** if the learner cannot state the
  correspondence for a pair of triangles without prompting,
  return to Lesson 02's Worked Example 1 and re-do it with the
  triangles drawn in different orientations.

### Lesson 03 — AA, side-splitter, Pythagoras by similarity

- **Likely misconception:** "a line cutting two sides is
  parallel" (dropping the equal-ratio condition — Guided G3).
  **Response:** Practice C4 (unequal ratios, not parallel) as
  the counterexample; make the learner state the ratio equality
  before claiming parallelism, every time.
- **Likely misconception:** using AA with only one angle pair,
  or with non-corresponding angles. **Response:** the
  two-column "angle | reason" list before concluding.
- **Reteach trigger:** if the learner cannot reproduce the
  altitude-to-hypotenuse argument with the diagram in front of
  them, re-teach it as a three-step recipe: (1) name the two
  smaller triangles, (2) match each to the whole by AA,
  (3) write the two leg² products and add.

### Lesson 04 — Applications

- **Likely misconception:** reporting calculator precision
  (e.g., 9.027777… m). **Response:** the project rubric's
  accuracy row — an unrounded answer loses the accuracy mark,
  by design. Make the learner state the measuring precision
  before computing.
- **Likely misconception:** applying the shadow proportion to
  measurements taken at different times, or on sloped ground.
  **Response:** Guided G4 and Practice D8; the method's
  conditions are examinable content, not fine print.
- **Reteach trigger:** if Practice Set D's area/volume items
  (D5, D6) are wrong while the length items are right, re-teach
  the "length k, area k², volume k³" rule with the rectangle
  derivation (ka by kb), not as a chant.

### Lesson 05 — Slope and midsegment

- **Likely misconception:** rise/run swap, and "rise 0 means
  undefined slope" (Guided G4). **Response:** the labeled
  rise-and-run triangle sketch before every slope computation.
- **Likely misconception:** applying the negative-reciprocal
  rule to a horizontal/vertical pair. **Response:** state the
  edge case explicitly every time the rule is used, until the
  learner volunteers it.
- **Reteach trigger:** if the learner can compute slopes but
  cannot reproduce the similar-triangles argument for slope
  constancy, re-teach Lesson 05's proof with two drawn
  triangles (3, 6) and (6, 12) before Unit 04.

## Assessment guidance

- **Quiz (Week 13, S18):** 27–30 ready for Lesson 05; 21–26
  review the missed lesson's guided practice; below 21 reteach
  the weakest lesson first. The most common weak question is
  Q6 (the converse) — reteach with Practice C3/C4.
- **Unit assessment (S19):** 44–50 ready for Unit 04; 35–43
  review the missed part's lesson; below 35 reteach the weakest
  objective with the practice set and the project report.
  Part B is the proof part: award method marks for a correct
  AA or side-splitter setup even when the final arithmetic
  slips.
- **Project (S13–S14):** score with the rubric in `project.md`.
  The accuracy row is the discriminating row: a project with
  correct heights but no error discussion scores at most
  "approaching" overall, by design.

## Readiness and next steps

- **Ready for Unit 04 if:** the learner can (1) dilate from any
  center, (2) prove two triangles similar by AA and state the
  correspondence, (3) use the side-splitter theorem and its
  converse, (4) set up an indirect measurement with its
  conditions, and (5) prove slope constancy and the midsegment
  theorem. These are the five objectives, and the assessment
  tests each one.
- **Not ready if:** dilations from a non-origin center still
  fail, or similarity statements are written without
  correspondence. Both are Unit 04 prerequisites (similar right
  triangles are Unit 04's first lesson).
- **What Unit 04 adds:** trigonometric ratios defined by
  similar right triangles, complementary-angle relationships,
  applied right triangles, and (as advanced content) the laws
  of sines and cosines. Nothing in Unit 04 re-teaches dilations
  or AA — they are assumed fluent.
