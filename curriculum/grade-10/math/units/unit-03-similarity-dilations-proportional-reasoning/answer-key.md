## Lesson 03 — Guided practice, application, exit check

**G1.** First triangle's third angle: 180 − 50 − 60 = 70°.
Second triangle's third angle: 180 − 60 − 70 = 50°. Both have
angle set {50°, 60°, 70°}, so AA applies (match 50° to 50° and
60° to 60°): similar.

**G2.** AD/DB = AE/EC: 2/3 = 4/EC, so EC = 6.

**G3.** The first half is the converse, correctly stated — but
only when the *same* ratio holds on both sides. The second half
is false: a line cutting the sides in *different* ratios is not
parallel to the third side (see Practice C4).

**G4.** Leg² = segment · hypotenuse: 5² = 25 = segment · 13, so
the adjacent segment is 25/13. The other segment is
13 − 25/13 = 144/13. The other leg² = (144/13) · 13 = 144, so
the leg is 12. (This is the similarity proof, run from a leg.)

**Application.** AD/AB = AE/AC: 20/60 = AE/45, so AE = 15 m from
A, and EC = 45 − 15 = 30 m from C. Ratios: 20:40 = 15:30 = 1:2.

**Exit check.** AA: two matching angle pairs imply similarity.
Side-splitter: a line parallel to one side divides the other
two proportionally. EC: 5/10 = 6/EC, so EC = 12.

## Practice Set E

**E1.** (0, 0) → (6, 3): slope 3/6 = 1/2. (6, 3) → (10, 5):
slope 2/4 = 1/2. The rise-and-run triangles are (6, 3) and
(4, 2) — the second is the first scaled by 2/3, hence similar,

**E2.** Parallel: −2/3. Perpendicular: 3/2 (negative reciprocal
of −2/3).

**E3.** Slope AB = 0; slope DC = (2 − 2)/(1 − 6) = 0 — equal, so
AB ∥ DC. Slope AD = (2 − 0)/(1 − 0) = 2; slope BC =
(2 − 0)/(6 − 5) = 2 — equal, so AD ∥ BC. Both pairs of opposite
sides parallel: a parallelogram.

**E4.** Midpoints: AB → (5, 0); AC → (2, 3). Midsegment from
(5, 0) to (2, 3): length √(3² + 3²) = √18 = 3√2 ≈ 4.24; slope
(3 − 0)/(2 − 5) = −1. Third side BC from (10, 0) to (4, 6):
length √(6² + 6²) = √72 = 6√2 ≈ 8.49 = 2 · 3√2; slope
(6 − 0)/(4 − 10) = −1. Half the length, equal slope — the
midsegment theorem verified numerically.

**E5.** Accept any answer that: draws the rise-and-run right
triangles for two point pairs; notes both have a right angle
and share the angle the line makes with the horizontal (via
parallel horizontals cut by the line as transversal); applies
AA to get similarity; and concludes rise/run is equal for both
pairs.

**E6.** The negative-reciprocal rule needs a finite slope m to
form −1/m; for a horizontal line m = 0, −1/0 is undefined, and
a vertical line has no slope at all. The pair is still
perpendicular geometrically (a horizontal line meets a vertical
line at 90°), but it must be stated as the separate edge case,
not derived from the rule.

**E7.** y = 3 + 2 · (4 − 1) = 3 + 6 = 9.

**E8.** The sign is missing: perpendicular slopes are *negative*
reciprocals, so 2 · (1/2) = 1 ≠ −1 — the lines are not
perpendicular. The correct perpendicular slope to slope 2 is
−1/2.
