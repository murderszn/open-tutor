# Unit Assessment — Similarity: Dilations and Proportional Reasoning

**Time:** ~50 minutes. Calculator allowed for arithmetic only.
Show all proportions and all reasons. Total 50 marks. This
assessment covers all five unit objectives; it is not a
standardized or validated test.

## Part A — Dilations and similarity (18 marks)

**A1 (4).** Triangle ABC has A(1, 1), B(5, 1), C(1, 4). Dilate it
with center the origin and k = 2. Give the image vertices and
verify one side length against k · (original length).

**A2 (4).** A dilation with center C(3, 1) and k = 1/2 sends P to
P′(5, 3). Find P. Show the "subtract, scale, add back" steps
(or the inverse steps, clearly labeled).

**A3 (5).** Triangles ΔABC and ΔDEF have AB = 7, BC = 9, CA = 11
and DE = 14, EF = 18, FD = 22. Their angles are: ΔABC: A = 50°,
B = 60°, C = 70°; ΔDEF: D = 70°, E = 50°, F = 60°. A learner
claims they are similar "because all the sides doubled." (a) Is
the learner's conclusion correct? (b) What is missing from the
learner's justification, and what must be checked for triangles
to be declared similar by the definition? (c) State the
correspondence and scale factor.

**A4 (5).** Rectangles measure 6 by 10 and 9 by 16. Are they
similar? Justify with a ratio table, and explain in one sentence
why "both are rectangles" is not enough.

## Part B — Criteria and proofs (18 marks)

**B1 (6).** In ΔABC, angle A = 52° and angle B = 90°. In ΔPQR,
angle P = 52° and angle R = 90°. (a) Prove the triangles similar,
naming the criterion and the correspondence. (b) The hypotenuse
of ΔABC is 13 and the hypotenuse of ΔPQR is 26. The legs of
ΔABC are 8 and 10.24 (rounded; exact values are not needed
here). Find the legs of ΔPQR.

*Note: 8² + 10.24² = 64 + 104.8576 = 168.8576 ≈ 169 = 13², so
the given legs are consistent with a 13 hypotenuse up to the
stated rounding. Your answer should use the scale factor, not
re-derive the legs.*

**B2 (6).** In ΔABC, D is on AB with AD = 8, DB = 12, and a line
through D parallel to BC meets AC at E with AE = 10. (a) Find
EC. (b) Prove, in two or three sentences, why the line through
D parallel to BC must divide AC in this ratio (name the
theorem; a full vector proof is not required here, but the
reasoning must be more than "the theorem says so" — state what
the parallel line forces about the division).

**B3 (6).** A right triangle has hypotenuse 25 and one leg 15.
(a) Use the altitude-to-hypotenuse similarity argument to find
the two segments of the hypotenuse. (b) Find the other leg from
your segments (not from a² + b² = c² directly). (c) Find the
altitude's length.

## Part C — Applications, slope, midsegment (14 marks)

**C1 (5).** A 1.8 m pole casts a 2.7 m shadow. At the same time, a
building casts a 31.5 m shadow. (a) How tall is the building?
(b) Name two conditions the shadow method requires, and explain
in one sentence each what goes wrong if that condition fails.

**C2 (4).** A solid is scaled by k = 2. By what factors do its
surface area and its volume change? A model boat 30 cm long
represents a real boat 15 m long. What is the scale factor from
the model to the real boat, and by what factor is the real
boat's volume larger than the model's?

**C3 (5).** Triangle ABC has A(0, 0), B(8, 0), C(4, 6). (a) Find
the midpoints of AB and AC and the midsegment joining them.
(b) Show the midsegment is parallel to BC and half its length,
using slopes and lengths. (c) State the theorem you verified.
