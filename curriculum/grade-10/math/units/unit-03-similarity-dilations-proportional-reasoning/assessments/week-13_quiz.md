# Formative Quiz — Week 13 (covers Weeks 11–13: Lessons 01–04)

**Time:** ~30 minutes. Calculator allowed for arithmetic only.
Show proportions, not just answers. Each question is worth the
marks shown; total 30.

**Q1 (3).** Dilate the point (2, 5) with center the origin and
k = 3. Then dilate the result with center (1, 1) and k = 1/2.
Give both images.

**Q2 (3).** A dilation has center C(2, 3) and k = 2. A line ℓ
passes through C. What is the image of ℓ? A second line m does
not pass through C. What can you say about the image of m?
(One sentence each, with the standard cited.)

**Q3 (3).** Triangles have sides 6, 8, 10 and 9, 12, 15. Both are
right triangles. Are they similar? Justify with both checks
(angles and sides) and state the scale factor and correspondence
rule you used.

**Q4 (3).** Two triangles have angles 45° and 70° (first) and
65° and 70° (second). Are they similar? Explain with AA.

**Q5 (3).** In ΔABC, DE ∥ BC with AD = 6, DB = 9, AE = 8. Find
EC. Then state the theorem you used.

**Q6 (3).** In ΔABC, D is on AB with AD = 4, DB = 6, and E is on
AC with AE = 6, EC = 10. Is DE ∥ BC? Justify with the converse
or explain why not.

**Q7 (3).** A 1.4 m stick casts a 2.1 m shadow. At the same time,
a pole casts a 12.6 m shadow. How tall is the pole? State one
condition the method requires.

**Q8 (3).** A map has scale 1:20,000. A road measures 7.5 cm on
the map. How long is the road in kilometres?

**Q9 (3).** A rectangle 2 by 3 is scaled by k = 5. Find the new
dimensions and area, and state the area scale factor.

**Q10 (3).** A learner says: "All rectangles are similar because
they all have four right angles." Explain the error with a
concrete counterexample (two rectangles with numbers).

**Scoring guide (for the adult):** 27–30: ready for Lesson 05;

Lesson 05; below 21: reteach the weakest lesson (usually the
side-splitter or the area factor) with the practice set before
