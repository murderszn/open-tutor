# Practice — Unit 03: Similarity, Dilations, and Proportional Reasoning

Work in your notebook. For every similarity claim, state the
correspondence and show the ratio table. For every indirect
measurement, state the method's conditions and report the answer
to a sensible precision. Solutions are in `answer-key.md` — try
each set before reading them.

## Practice Set A — Dilations (Lesson 01)

**A1.** Dilate the point (3, 1) with center the origin and scale
factor k = 4.

**A2.** Segment PQ joins P(1, 2) to Q(1, 6). Dilate it with center
the origin and k = 1.5. Find P′ and Q′ and the image length, and
check it against k · PQ.

**A3.** Find the image of P(5, 4) under the dilation with center
C(3, 2) and scale factor k = 2.

**A4.** A dilation with center C(1, 0) and scale factor k = 3 sends
P to P′(10, 6). Find P.

**A5.** A segment of length 6 cm is dilated to a segment of length
15 cm. What is the scale factor? Is it an enlargement or a
reduction?

**A6.** A dilation has center O(0, 0) and k = 3. The line y = 2x
passes through O. What is its image? What if the line were
y = 2x + 1 (which does not pass through O)?

**A7.** Triangle ABC has A(0, 0), B(3, 0), C(0, 4). Dilate it with
center the origin and k = 1/2. Find the image vertices, the
image perimeter, and the image area, and compare each with the
original's (perimeter 12, area 6).

**A8 (error analysis).** A learner dilates P(4, 3) with center
(2, 1) and k = 2 by doubling the coordinates: P′(8, 6). Explain
the error and give the correct image.

## Practice Set B — Similarity defined (Lesson 02)

**B1.** Triangles have sides 8, 15, 17 and 24, 45, 51. Both are
right triangles (check: 8² + 15² = 17² and 24² + 45² = 51²).
Are they similar? With what scale factor?

**B2.** Rectangles measure 3 by 5 and 6 by 11. Are they similar?
Show the ratio table.

**B3.** ΔABC ~ ΔXYZ with AB = 5, BC = 7, CA = 9 and XY = 15. Find
YZ, ZX, and the perimeter of ΔXYZ.

**B4.** Explain in two or three sentences why any two squares are
similar, and why any square and any non-square rectangle are not
(except in the trivial case where the rectangle is itself a
square).

**B5.** A figure has perimeter 18. A similar figure with scale
factor 4 from the first has what perimeter? A similar figure with
scale factor 1/4 has what perimeter?

**B6.** A photograph 5 in. by 7 in. is enlarged so the 5 in. side
becomes 10 in. What does the 7 in. side become under a true
similarity? A print shop instead produces a 10 in. by 12 in.
print. Is it similar to the original? Why not?

**B7.** Triangles have angles: first triangle 40°, 80°, 60°;
second triangle 60°, 40°, 80° (named D, E, F in that order).
Write the correct similarity statement with correspondence in
order.

**B8.** A rhombus has side 4 and angles 50° and 130°. A square has
side 6. The side ratios are all 6/4 = 1.5. Are the figures
similar? Why does the side check alone fail here?

## Practice Set C — AA, side-splitter, Pythagoras by similarity (Lesson 03)

**C1.** Two triangles each have angles 30° and 90°. The first has
hypotenuse 20 (so it is a 30-60-90 triangle with legs 10 and
10√3). The second has hypotenuse 30. Prove the triangles similar
and find the second triangle's legs.

**C2.** In ΔABC, AB = 15, AC = 18. Point D is on AB with AD = 5,
and DE ∥ BC meets AC at E. Find AE and EC.

**C3.** In ΔABC, D is on AB with AD = 3, DB = 6, and E is on AC
with AE = 4, EC = 8. Is DE ∥ BC? Justify with the converse.

**C4.** In ΔABC, D is on AB with AD = 2, DB = 3, and E is on AC
with AE = 3, EC = 5. Is DE ∥ BC? Justify.

**C5.** A right triangle has legs 9 and 12 and hypotenuse 15. Use
the altitude-to-hypotenuse argument to find the two segments of
the hypotenuse and the altitude's length, and verify the segments
add to 15.

**C6.** In ΔABC, D is the midpoint of AB and E is the midpoint
of AC. Use the side-splitter converse to prove DE ∥ BC. (This
is the midsegment theorem's converse direction, proved again in
Lesson 05 by dilation.)

**C7.** In ΔABC, DE ∥ BC with AD = 4, DB = 6, AE = x, EC = 9.
Find x.

**C8 (proof).** Two parallel lines ℓ and m are cut by two
transversals that intersect at a point P between the lines,
forming two triangles with vertex P. Prove the triangles are
similar. (Hint: vertical angles, then alternate interior angles
from ℓ ∥ m, then AA.)

## Practice Set D — Applications (Lesson 04)

**D1 (shadow).** A 1.5 m stick casts a 2 m shadow. At the same
time, a building casts a 24 m shadow. How tall is the building?
State the method's conditions.

**D2 (mirror).** Your eye height is 1.5 m. You stand 1.2 m from a
mirror on flat ground; the mirror is 18 m from the base of a
tree. How tall is the tree?

**D3 (map).** A map has scale 1:100,000. Two landmarks are 6.5 cm
apart on the map. How far apart are they in kilometres?

**D4 (scale drawing).** A room measures 5 m by 4 m. Draw it at
scale 1:50. What are the drawing's dimensions in centimetres?

**D5 (area).** A rectangle 3 by 5 (area 15) is scaled by k = 4.
Find the new dimensions and area, and verify the area factor.

**D6 (volume).** A cube has edge 2 (volume 8). It is scaled by
k = 3. Find the new edge and volume, and verify the volume
factor.

**D7 (accuracy).** A stick 1.2 m tall casts a 1.6 m shadow; a
building's shadow measures 16.6 m (measured to the nearest
10 cm). Compute the building's height and report it to a
sensible precision, explaining your rounding.

**D8 (error analysis).** A learner measures a tree's shadow at
noon and a stick's shadow at 3 p.m., then computes the tree's
height from the proportion. A second learner measures both
shadows at the same time but on a hillside. Explain why each
result is unreliable.

## Practice Set E — Slope and midsegment (Lesson 05)

**E1.** A line passes through (0, 0), (6, 3), and (10, 5). Show
the slope is the same for both pairs of consecutive points, and
name the similar triangles that guarantee it.

**E2.** Line m has slope −2/3. What is the slope of a line
parallel to m? Of a line perpendicular to m?

**E3.** Prove the quadrilateral with vertices A(0, 0), B(5, 0),
C(6, 2), D(1, 2) is a parallelogram, using slopes.

**E4.** Triangle ABC has A(0, 0), B(10, 0), C(4, 6). Find the
midpoints of AB and AC, the midsegment's length and slope, and
the third side's length and slope. Verify the midsegment
theorem numerically.

**E5 (proof in words).** Explain, without computing a single
slope, why the slope between any two points of a nonvertical
line is the same. Your explanation must name the triangles, the
angle equalities, and the criterion (AA).

**E6.** A horizontal line has slope 0. A vertical line has
undefined slope. Explain why the negative-reciprocal rule does
not apply to this pair, and why the pair is still
perpendicular.

**E7.** A line of slope 2 passes through (1, 3). Find y when
x = 4.

**E8 (error analysis).** A learner says lines of slopes 2 and
1/2 are perpendicular "because they are reciprocals." What is
missing, and what is the correct perpendicular slope to a line
of slope 2?
