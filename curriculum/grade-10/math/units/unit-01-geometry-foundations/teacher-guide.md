# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Geometry Foundations

For the guiding adult. Student materials never contain answer keys; all
solutions are in `answer-key.md`. This guide covers pacing, per-lesson
teaching moves, the misconception table, project and assessment
administration, and reteach triggers.

## Unit at a glance

- **Time:** 20 sessions × ~50 minutes (course Weeks 3–6). Do not compress
  the two construction studios (S9, S13) or the proof workshop (S16) —
  they are where the skills become fluent.
- **Non-negotiables:** (1) the notation-order habit (ray *TS* vs. *ST*,
  vertex as middle letter); (2) "classify before you compute" on every
  angle problem; (3) a stated reason for every proof statement;
  (4) honest precision language (N-Q.3) on every measurement.
- **What counts as done:** the project rubric (≥18/24, no criterion
  below 2) *and* the unit assessment (≥38/52) — see the readiness bands
  in the assessment file.

## Per-lesson teaching notes

**Lesson 01 (S1).** Spend the full 10 minutes on undefined notions —
learners who rush this treat every later definition as "obvious" and
write circular proofs. The exit check's three items are the gate for S2:
re-teach any failed item same-day. If the learner struggles with
notation order, make a wall card: "RAY: endpoint FIRST. ANGLE: vertex
MIDDLE." Keep it up all year.

**Lesson 02 (S3).** Demo the segment copy *once* at full speed, then
once narrated step-by-step, then have the learner narrate while *you*
draw — role reversal exposes fuzzy steps before tools are touched.
Sharpen pencils before the lesson; a dull point is a 1 mm systematic
error. Announce "compass locked" before every transfer step until the
habit is automatic.

**Lesson 03 (S6).** The heaviest tool session. Teach constructions A–C
before the image activity, then D–E after — the image gives the hands a
rest and the brain a reasoning task mid-lesson. For the equidistance
theorem, draw the "engine" diagram once: segment *AB*, points *P* and
*Q* each marked equidistant, line *PQ* — and keep it visible for the
rest of the unit. Flag SSS justifications as "on loan from Unit 02"
explicitly; learners respect the honesty and remember the loan.

**Lesson 04 (S11).** Before the proof, do the readiness check *orally*
and let the learner's guesses sit uncorrected — then the proof *settles*
them, which is the emotional point of proof. When teaching the skeleton
("same quantity two ways, then cancel"), name it and promise it returns
in Lesson 05 — then point it out when it does. Accept any correct
reason wording ("subtraction property" vs. "subtract m∠CEB from both
sides") as long as the *logical* reason is right.

**Lesson 05 (S14).** The four-pair vocabulary is the lesson's load-bearing
wall — do not start the proofs until the learner can point to each pair
type on a blank diagram without hesitation. The color-coding support is
not optional for most learners; it is the cheapest memory aid in the
unit. End the lesson by having the learner *state the honest boundary*
aloud: "no parallel marking, no transversal theorem."

## Misconception table

| Misconception | Where it appears | Correction move |
|---|---|---|
| Ray *TS* = ray *ST* | L01, practice A | "Cover the first letter — where does the ray *start*?" Drill 5 rapid-fire namings per session until automatic. |
| Diagram appearance = proof fact | L01–L05 | "Marked or given?" — the two-word challenge, asked aloud every time a learner cites the picture. |
| Re-setting the compass mid-construction | L02–L03 | "Compass locked" call-and-response before transfer steps; check transfers first when debugging. |
| Vertical ⇒ sum to 180° / linear pair ⇒ set equal | L04–L05 | Mandatory written classification ("vertical" / "linear pair" / pair type) *before* any equation. No classification, no credit. |
| The arcs are the answer | L03 | "Arcs are scaffolding; the line/ray is the answer." Require the final object drawn darker. |
| Citing the Corresponding Angles Postulate without parallel given | L05 | "The parallel marking is a hypothesis, not decoration." Cover the markings and ask what remains provable. |
| Extra digits = extra accuracy | L02–L03, N-Q.3 | "What does your tool actually read to?" — make the learner state the tool's precision before reporting. |
| Paragraph proofs are "less rigorous" | L04 | Rigor lives in the *reasons*, not the layout. Have the learner highlight every reason in their paragraph in color. |

## Project administration (S9, S13, S17)

- **S9 studio:** the adult provides the originals (segment ~6 cm, angle
  ~70°, angle 100°, segment 9 cm, line + exterior point, circle r=4 cm).
  Circulate while building; correct grip and compass handling early.
  Collect nothing — sheets stay with the learner.
- **S13 studio:** proofs are written closed-lesson first (memory
  retrieval), then checked against the lessons. The paragraph rewrite
  must be compared *sentence by sentence* against the two-column version
  — "rearranged, not reduced" is the grading standard.
- **S17 gallery:** the adult scores the rubric *with* the learner
  present, discussing every deduction. This is a teaching session, not a
  sentencing. Record the score and the one weakest criterion; that
  criterion drives S18's reteach.

## Assessment administration

- **Week-04 quiz (S18):** 25 minutes, closed-lesson. Score same-day;
  fill the reteach guide at the quiz file's end and execute the flagged
  reteach *before* S19. A learner below 18/26 should not sit the unit
  assessment yet — use S20's flex time for the reteach instead.
- **Unit assessment (S19):** 50 minutes. Parts I, III, IV closed-lesson;
  Part II allows compass/straightedge (no protractor except item 5's
  verification step, which explicitly asks for it). Apply the scoring
  bands at the file's end.
- **Reteach triggers:** any proof item scored below 70% → re-teach that
  theorem's proof from the lesson (not from the learner's notes) with
  the learner narrating reasons aloud. Any construction item below 70%
  → one supervised rebuild with the "set — carry — mark — check"
  narration. N-Q.3 item weak → the two-sentence tool-precision drill:
  "My tool reads to ___. So I report ___."

## Pacing adaptations

- **Ahead of pace:** add the Lesson 03 and Lesson 04 extensions
  (hexagon-from-a-side; linear-pair bisectors proof; exterior-angle
  preview) and the Lesson 05 enrichment paragraph on the Parallel
  Postulate. Do not start Unit 02 early — depth here pays off there.
- **Behind pace:** protect S1, S3, S6, S11, S14 (the lessons), S16 (the
  workshop), and S19 (the assessment). Compress S8 (Euclid reading) to a
  20-minute excerpt session and fold S10/S15 reviews into practice
  sessions. Never compress by dropping justifications — drop *items*,
  not *reasons*.
- **One-learner setting:** the "gallery" is a walkthrough with the
  adult; the "critique" is the adult asking the learner to defend one
  construction's justification and one proof's reasons aloud.

## Answer-key conventions

`answer-key.md` solves every practice item, quiz question, and
assessment task independently of the lesson prose, then reconciles any
difference (none remain — see the validation record in the delivery
comment). For open responses it gives sample reasoning plus acceptable
variation. Misconception notes are embedded per item where a known
trap exists.
