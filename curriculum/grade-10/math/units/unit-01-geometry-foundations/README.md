# Unit 01 — Geometry Foundations: Definitions and Proof

**Grade 10 · Math · Geometry pathway · Weeks 3–6 of the course year**
(draft, delivered 2026-10-13 for issue #46; not educator-reviewed)

## Overview

Every proof in this year's geometry course rests on two things: saying
exactly what we mean, and building figures whose properties we can trust.
This unit establishes both. First, the learner builds precise definitions
of the basic objects — segment, ray, angle, parallel and perpendicular
lines, circle — from a small set of undefined notions (point, line, plane,
distance), learning the notation and diagram conventions the whole course
uses. Second, the learner masters the classical compass-and-straightedge
constructions (copying segments and angles; bisectors; perpendiculars;
parallels; inscribed equilateral triangles, squares, and regular hexagons),
each with an honest account of *why* the steps work. Third, the learner
writes real proofs: the vertical-angles theorem, linear-pair relationships,
and the angle theorems for transversals across parallel lines, in both
two-column and paragraph formats, with a stated reason for every step.

The essential question running through all four weeks is: **how do precise
definitions turn pictures of shapes into statements we can prove?**

## Prerequisites

From the grade-9 track (Algebra I pathway): solving linear equations in one
variable (needed for angle-chasing with algebra), reading coordinate graphs,
and the Pythagorean Theorem in coordinate contexts. No prior formal geometry
is assumed — definitions, constructions, and proof formats are all taught
here from the ground up. Weeks 1–2 diagnostics should confirm equation
solving; the unit re-teaches it in context where needed.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. State precise definitions of angle, circle, perpendicular line, parallel
   line, and line segment from the undefined notions of point, line, and
   distance, and distinguish defined terms from undefined notions. (G-CO.1)
2. Perform formal constructions with compass and straightedge — copy a
   segment, copy an angle, bisect a segment and an angle, construct a
   perpendicular from a point to a line and the perpendicular bisector of a
   segment, construct a parallel through an exterior point, and inscribe an
   equilateral triangle, a square, and a regular hexagon in a circle — and
   explain why each construction's steps guarantee the result.
   (G-CO.12, G-CO.13)
3. Prove theorems about lines and angles — vertical angles are congruent;
   corresponding and alternate interior angles formed by a transversal
   across parallel lines are congruent; same-side interior angles are
   supplementary — in two-column and paragraph formats with a stated reason
   for every step, and use these theorems to determine unknown angle
   measures. (G-CO.9)
4. Choose measurement units and precision appropriate to the task (e.g.,
   nearest millimeter or sixteenth of an inch for lengths, nearest degree
   for angles), and explain how measurement limits bound what a physical
   construction can verify. (N-Q.3)

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, High
School Geometry (verified against the published domain pages at
thecorestandards.org during the track audit, 2026-10-07; descriptions here
are paraphrases). This unit addresses:

- **G-CO.1** — precise definitions of angle, circle, perpendicular line,
  parallel line, and line segment from undefined notions (Lesson 01).
- **G-CO.12** — formal constructions: copying segments and angles,
  bisecting segments and angles, perpendiculars, and parallels
  (Lessons 02–03).
- **G-CO.13** — constructing an equilateral triangle, a square, and a
  regular hexagon inscribed in a circle (Lesson 03).
- **G-CO.9** — proving theorems about lines and angles: vertical angles
  and angles formed by a transversal (Lessons 04–05). The
  perpendicular-bisector equidistance property is stated and used to
  justify constructions; its full proof (which needs triangle congruence)
  is enrichment here and returns in Unit 02.
- **N-Q.3** — choosing an appropriate level of accuracy when reporting
  measurements (Lessons 02–03, practice sets, project).

No state adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification is claimed.

## Materials

Compass, straightedge (an unmarked ruler), protractor, ruler with
millimeter markings, plain and grid paper, pencil, geometry notebook with
*definitions / theorems / proofs* sections, and access to GeoGebra Geometry
(free, no account) for checking constructions. The adult previews any
website before the learner opens it.
