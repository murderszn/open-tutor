# Unit Assessment — Unit 01: Geometry Foundations

**Time:** ~50 minutes · **Total:** 52 points · Covers all four unit
objectives (Lessons 01–05). Closed-lesson for Parts I, III, and IV;
compass and straightedge permitted for Part II. Solutions and scoring
notes are in `answer-key.md` — adults only.

**Instructions to the learner:** Write the angle-pair classification
before every equation. Every proof statement needs a reason from the
allowed list (given, definition, postulate, algebra, previously proved
theorem).

## Part I — Definitions and notation (10 pts)

1. (2 pts) Define *angle* using only the undefined notions and
   previously defined terms.
2. (3 pts) Points *A*, *B*, *C* are collinear with *B* between *A* and
   *C*. (a) Name all three segments determined by these points.
   (b) Name two *different* rays with endpoint *B*.
3. (3 pts) A diagram marks lines *l* ∥ *m* (arrowheads) cut by
   transversal *t*. Angle 2 (upper-right, top intersection) and angle 8
   (lower-right, bottom intersection) each carry one arc mark.
   (a) State the one congruence you are directly entitled to, with its
   reason. (b) Name the pair type of angles 2 and 8. (c) State one
   conclusion about the diagram you may *not* draw, though it looks
   true.
4. (2 pts) True or false, each with a one-sentence reason:
   (a) "A ray has two endpoints." (b) "Perpendicular lines form four
   right angles."

## Part II — Constructions (13 pts)

*Label every arc-crossing point. Keep construction arcs visible.*

5. (5 pts) Construct the perpendicular bisector of the 6 cm segment the
   adult provides. Verify by measuring both halves (nearest millimeter)
   and the crossing angle (nearest degree); report the three numbers.
6. (5 pts) Inscribe an equilateral triangle in the circle the adult
   provides (radius 3.5 cm). In two sentences, justify the construction
   with the 60° central-angle reasoning.
7. (3 pts) Explain why the angle-copy construction requires the compass
   width to stay unchanged between its arcs, citing the definition of a
   circle.

## Part III — Proofs (14 pts)

8. (8 pts) **Two-column proof.** Lines *AB* and *CD* intersect at *E*.
   Given that ∠*AEC* and ∠*BED* are vertical angles, prove ∠*AEC* ≅
   ∠*BED*. Full table required: numbered statements, one reason per
   statement.
9. (6 pts) **Paragraph proof.** Lines *l* ∥ *m* are cut by transversal
   *t*; ∠3 and ∠6 are alternate interior angles. Prove ∠3 ≅ ∠6 in
   paragraph form, citing the Corresponding Angles Postulate and the
   Vertical Angles Theorem by name. Every sentence must carry its
   reason.

## Part IV — Angle chasing with algebra (11 pts)

10. (3 pts) m∠*A* = (4x − 7)° is vertical to m∠*B* = (2x + 13)°.
    Find x and both measures. Show the check.
11. (4 pts) ∠*P* and ∠*Q* form a linear pair. m∠*P* = (6x + 8)°,
    m∠*Q* = (4x + 12)°. A ray bisects ∠*P*. Find the measure of each
    half. Show the check.
12. (4 pts) Lines *a* ∥ *b* are cut by transversal *c*. A pair of
    alternate interior angles measures (7y + 4)° and (3y + 52)°.
    (a) Find y and both angle measures. (b) Find the same-side interior
    angle adjacent to the second angle. Show both checks.

## Part V — Precision and application (4 pts)

13. (4 pts) A learner bisects a right angle with compass and
    straightedge, then reports the halves as "45.00° and 45.00°,"
    measured with a protractor that reads to the nearest degree.
    (a) What is wrong with the report? (b) What should the honest
    report be? (c) In one sentence, state what the protractor *can*
    verify about this construction and what it *cannot*.

---

**Scoring bands (adult):** 45–52 = secure; 38–44 = proficient with minor
gaps (reteach flagged items, then proceed); 30–37 = developing (reteach
per `teacher-guide.md` before Unit 02); below 30 = rebuild the unit's
proof and construction routines before advancing. The project rubric
(24 pts) is scored separately; both feed the readiness decision.
