# Lesson 05 — Angles with a Transversal: Proving with Parallel Lines

**Unit 01 · Week 5, Session 14 · ~50 minutes**

**Goal (learner-facing):** I can name every angle pair a transversal
makes, prove the alternate-interior-angles theorem from the Corresponding
Angles Postulate, and determine all eight angle measures from a single
given one.

**Materials:** this lesson, notebook (*theorems* section), pencil,
protractor (for checking only).

**Readiness check (5 min):** Draw two horizontal lines and one slanted
line crossing both. Label the 8 angles 1–8 (1–4 top intersection, 5–8
bottom). Without any theorems: which pairs *look* equal? Write them down
— today we find out which look-alikes we are *entitled* to.

---

## Part 1 — The vocabulary of a transversal (explicit teaching, 10 min)

A **transversal** is a line that crosses two (or more) other lines. With
two lines cut by a transversal, eight angles form, in four named pairs:

- **Corresponding angles:** same relative position at each intersection
  (e.g., upper-right at the top and upper-right at the bottom).
- **Alternate interior angles:** between the two lines, on opposite sides
  of the transversal.
- **Alternate exterior angles:** outside the two lines, on opposite sides
  of the transversal.
- **Same-side interior angles:** between the two lines, on the same side
  of the transversal.

Memory aid: *interior* = between the lines; *alternate* = opposite sides
of the transversal; *corresponding* = matching corners. Draw one reference
diagram in your notebook and label all four pair types — you will reuse
it all year.

**Corresponding Angles Postulate** (accepted without proof): *If two
parallel lines are cut by a transversal, then corresponding angles are
congruent.* This is a postulate — a starting point, not a conclusion.
Everything else in this lesson is *proved from it*.

## Part 2 — Proving the other theorems (explicit teaching, 15 min)

**Theorem (Alternate Interior Angles).** *If two parallel lines are cut
by a transversal, alternate interior angles are congruent.*

*Proof (two-column).* Given: lines *l* ∥ *m* cut by transversal *t*;
angles 3 and 6 are alternate interior. Prove: ∠3 ≅ ∠6.

| # | Statement | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lines *l* ∥ *m* cut by transversal *t* | Given |
| 2 | ∠6 ≅ ∠2 (∠2 is the angle corresponding to ∠6) | Corresponding Angles Postulate |
| 3 | ∠2 ≅ ∠3 (∠2 and ∠3 are vertical) | Vertical Angles Theorem (Lesson 04) |
| 4 | ∠3 ≅ ∠6 | Transitive Property of Congruence (∠3 ≅ ∠2 and ∠2 ≅ ∠6) |

See the architecture: the new theorem is a *two-step combination* of a
postulate and an already-proved theorem. This is how the course grows —
each proof stands on the last.

**Theorem (Same-Side Interior Angles).** *If two parallel lines are cut
by a transversal, same-side interior angles are supplementary.*

*Proof sketch (paragraph).* Let ∠4 and ∠5 be same-side interior. ∠4's
corresponding angle is ∠8, so ∠4 ≅ ∠8 by the Corresponding Angles
Postulate. But ∠8 and ∠5 form a linear pair, so m∠8 + m∠5 = 180° by the
Linear Pair Postulate. Substituting gives m∠4 + m∠5 = 180°, i.e., the
angles are supplementary by definition. (Write the full two-column
version in your notebook — it is Practice item 1.)

**Honest boundary.** None of this says anything when the two lines are
*not* parallel: corresponding angles of non-parallel lines are simply not
congruent, and no theorem applies. Always check the parallel marking
first. The converses (proving lines parallel *from* angle relationships)
are proved in Unit 02; until then, treat "equal corresponding angles ⇒
parallel" as a stated-but-unproved tool, as in Lesson 03's construction D.

## Worked example 1 — The full eight from one

*Problem.* Lines *l* ∥ *m* are cut by transversal *t*. The upper-right
angle at the top intersection measures 72°. Find the other seven
measures, naming the theorem or postulate for each.

*Solution.* Label the top intersection 1 (upper-left), 2 (upper-right =
72°), 3 (lower-left), 4 (lower-right); bottom 5, 6, 7, 8 likewise.
- m∠6 = 72°: ∠2 and ∠6 are corresponding ⇒ Corresponding Angles
  Postulate.
- m∠4 = 72°: ∠2 and ∠4 are vertical ⇒ Vertical Angles Theorem.
- m∠8 = 72°: ∠4 and ∠8 are corresponding (or ∠6 and ∠8 vertical) ⇒
  Corresponding Angles Postulate.
- m∠1 = 108°: ∠1 and ∠2 form a linear pair ⇒ Linear Pair Postulate
  (180 − 72).
- m∠3 = 108°: ∠1 and ∠3 vertical ⇒ Vertical Angles Theorem.
- m∠5 = 108°: ∠1 and ∠5 corresponding ⇒ Corresponding Angles Postulate.
- m∠7 = 108°: ∠3 and ∠7 corresponding (or ∠5 and ∠7 vertical).
Check: every same-side interior pair sums to 180 (72 + 108 ✓). The
pattern — four angles of one measure, four of its supplement — is the
signature of parallel lines cut by a transversal. Learn to see it.

## Worked example 2 — Algebra meets the transversal

*Problem.* Lines *a* ∥ *b* are cut by transversal *c*. One pair of
alternate interior angles measures (3x + 20)° and (5x − 40)°. Find x and
the same-side interior angle adjacent to the first one.

*Solution.* Alternate interior angles are congruent (theorem, Part 2):
3x + 20 = 5x − 40 → 60 = 2x → x = 30. Each alternate interior angle
measures 3(30) + 20 = 110°. The same-side interior angle adjacent to it
is supplementary (theorem, Part 2): 180 − 110 = 70°. Checks: 5(30) − 40
= 110 ✓; 110 + 70 = 180 ✓. **Common error:** writing the alternate
interior pair as supplementary — classify first (*alternate interior ⇒
equal*; *same-side interior ⇒ supplementary*).

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. Expand the paragraph proof of the Same-Side Interior Angles Theorem
   into a full two-column proof. (Five rows: given; corresponding angles
   postulate; linear pair postulate; substitution; definition of
   supplementary.)
2. Lines *l* ∥ *m*, transversal *t*; one angle is 55°. Find all eight
   measures with a reason per angle. (Four 55° angles, four 125° angles —
   map each to corresponding, vertical, or linear-pair reasoning.)
3. Prove: *alternate exterior angles formed by parallel lines cut by a
   transversal are congruent.* (Hint: chain corresponding angles with
   vertical angles, exactly as in Part 2's proof.)
4. **Explain your reasoning:** Why can't we *prove* the Corresponding
   Angles Postulate the way we proved the Alternate Interior Angles
   Theorem? (A proof must start from accepted statements; the postulate
   is one of the starting points. Proving it would require something
   more basic to prove it *from* — that is what "postulate" means.)
5. In a diagram *without* parallel markings, two "corresponding-looking"
   angles both measure 90°. May you conclude the lines are parallel?
   Explain. (No — the postulate needs the parallel *given*. The converse
   is true but unproved in this unit; and measured 90° angles carry
   instrument error anyway.)

## Independent practice

1. Write the Alternate Interior Angles Theorem proof from memory in
   two-column form. Check against Part 2; any missing reason is your
   study target.
2. Prove: *If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, then any two
   angles that are neither congruent nor supplementary cannot occur — in
   fact every angle is congruent or supplementary to every other.*
   (This is the "four-and-four" pattern from Worked example 1, stated as
   a theorem. Argue by cases using the pair-type definitions.)
3. Lines *p* ∥ *q* cut by transversal *r*. Corresponding angles measure
   (4x − 12)° and (2x + 44)°. Find x, then find the vertical angle to the
   first angle and the same-side interior angle adjacent to it.
   (x = 28; corresponding angles 100°; vertical 100°; same-side interior
   80°. Checks: 4(28) − 12 = 100 ✓; 100 + 80 = 180 ✓.)
4. A roof truss has a horizontal beam crossed by a diagonal strut making
   a 38° angle with the beam. A second beam, parallel to the first, is
   crossed by the same strut. Find the corresponding angle on the second
   beam and the alternate interior angle, with reasons. (38° each —
   Corresponding Angles Postulate; Alternate Interior Angles Theorem.)
5. **Error analysis:** A learner writes "∠1 ≅ ∠2 because they are
   alternate interior angles" in a diagram where the two lines are not
   marked parallel. Identify the two distinct mistakes. (The theorem
   requires the parallel *given* — the reason is incomplete without it;
   and even the converse direction is unproved in this unit.)
6. Draw two *non-parallel* lines cut by a transversal. Measure one pair
   of "corresponding" angles with a protractor. What do you observe, and
   what does it confirm about the postulate's hypothesis? (They differ —
   confirming that "parallel" is load-bearing in the postulate, not
   decoration.)
7. **Extension:** The *exterior angle* idea previews Unit 02: in any
   triangle, extend one side and consider the angle outside. Using only
   this unit's theorems plus the fact that a triangle's interior angles
   sum to 180° (stated, proved in Unit 02), prove the exterior angle
   equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles. (Let interior
   angles be a, b, c with a + b + c = 180°; the exterior angle e forms a
   linear pair with c, so e = 180° − c = a + b.)

## Application

Staircase railings: the balusters (vertical posts) are parallel, and the
handrail is a transversal. A building code requires the handrail to meet
each baluster at the same angle for a uniform look. (a) Which theorem
guarantees that *if* the balusters are parallel, the corresponding angles
are automatically equal? (b) A carpenter instead measures each angle
separately and adjusts. In two sentences, contrast the mathematical
guarantee with the carpenter's method using the N-Q.3 precision idea.
((a) Corresponding Angles Postulate. (b) The postulate is exact but
assumes perfect parallelism; the carpenter's measurements absorb real
imperfections but each carries instrument error — mathematics certifies
the design, measurement certifies the execution.)

## Exit check

(1) Define the four transversal pair types from memory, with a labeled
diagram. (2) Given parallel lines and one 63° angle, state the other
seven measures and one reason each — timed, 3 minutes. (3) One sentence:
what is the *only* new unproved assumption this lesson added?
(The Corresponding Angles Postulate; everything else was proved from it.)

## Supports

- **Reading/language access:** Color-code the reference diagram: one
  color per pair type, with the names written beside each pair. Say the
  pair names aloud while pointing — "alternate *interior*" while
  pointing *between* the lines on *opposite* sides.
- **Alternate response mode:** Angle-chasing tasks may be done on a
  GeoGebra diagram with the angle tool *verifying* each computed measure
  after the reason is written. Proofs may be dictated for scribing.
- **Extension:** Investigate: the Corresponding Angles Postulate is
  equivalent to Euclid's Fifth (Parallel) Postulate. Write a paragraph on
  what "equivalent" means here (each can be proved from the other) and
  why mathematicians care which statement is taken as basic.

## Teacher note

The classification-before-equation habit from Lesson 04 now pays off at
scale: with eight angles, learners who do not classify first drown.
Require the pair-type word ("corresponding," "alternate interior,"
"same-side interior," "vertical," "linear pair") written next to *every*
equation in practice — it is the cheapest error filter available. Also
watch for "postulate shopping": learners citing the Corresponding Angles
Postulate when the lines are not parallel. The parallel marking is a
hypothesis, not a decoration. See `teacher-guide.md` (Lesson 05) and
`answer-key.md` (Practice Set D).
