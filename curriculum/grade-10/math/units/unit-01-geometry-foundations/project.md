# Project — Construction Gallery & Proof Portfolio

**Unit 01 · studios in Week 4 (S9) and Week 5 (S13); gallery in Week 6 (S17)**

## Purpose

Two products that together show what the unit taught: hands that can
build exactly what the definitions promise, and writing that can prove
what the theorems claim. The gallery is public within the learning
setting (shown to the guiding adult); the portfolio stays in the
learner's notebook. No learner work, names, or photos go into any public
repository — see the privacy boundaries in the track README.

## Materials

Compass, straightedge, protractor, millimeter ruler, 3–4 sheets of plain
unlined paper, pencil, notebook. Optional: GeoGebra Geometry to *check*
constructions after they are built by hand.

## Part 1 — Construction gallery (studio: Session 9)

Build **six** constructions on clean paper, one per half-sheet or
quarter-sheet, each labeled with its name and numbered steps:

1. Copy of a segment (adult provides the original, ~6 cm).
2. Copy of an angle (adult provides an angle of ~70°).
3. Angle bisector of a 100° angle (adult provides the angle).
4. Perpendicular bisector of a 9 cm segment.
5. Perpendicular from an exterior point to a line.
6. **Choice:** inscribed regular hexagon *or* inscribed square in a
   4 cm-radius circle (adult provides the circle).

Requirements for each sheet: every arc-crossing point labeled with a
letter; the construction lines kept light and the final result drawn
darker; a one-line "why it works" citing the definition, postulate, or
theorem that guarantees it (SSS-based reasons may be cited as "on loan
from Unit 02" — honesty counts).

Then choose **one** of constructions 3, 4, or 5 and write a half-page
"why it works" explanation: walk through the steps and show how the
equidistance theorem (for 4 or 5) or the SSS reasoning (for 3) turns the
arcs into a guarantee. This is the gallery's centerpiece — mount it
beside its drawing.

## Part 2 — Proof portfolio (studio: Session 13)

In the notebook's *proofs* section, write **three** theorems in full
two-column form, each with every reason stated:

1. The Vertical Angles Theorem (from memory, then checked).
2. The Alternate Interior Angles Theorem (given parallel lines).
3. **Choice:** the Same-Side Interior Angles Theorem *or* "the bisectors
   of a linear pair are perpendicular" (Lesson 04, extension).

Then rewrite **one** of the three as a paragraph proof on a clean page.
The paragraph must contain every reason the two-column version had —
rearranged, not reduced.

Finish with a **reflection paragraph** (5–8 sentences): which proof was
hardest and what made it hard; one specific error you caught and fixed;
and one sentence on how precise definitions changed the way you read a
diagram.

## Deliverables

- Six labeled construction sheets + one half-page "why it works"
  explanation.
- Three two-column proofs + one paragraph proof in the notebook.
- Reflection paragraph.
- (Studio process, not graded: recipe cards from Lesson 03, draft
  attempts — keep them; they show revision.)

## Rubric

Score each criterion 0–4. Total 24 points. The adult scores with the
learner present and discusses every deduction.

| Criterion | 4 — Exemplary | 3 — Proficient | 2 — Developing | 1–0 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Precision of constructions** (N-Q.3) | All copies within 1 mm / 1° of targets; arcs clean, points labeled | Most within 1 mm / 1°; minor labeling gaps | Several within 2 mm / 2°; missing labels or heavy stray marks | Constructions incomplete or far off; unlabeled |
| **Completeness** | All 6 constructions + explanation + 3 proofs + paragraph + reflection | One minor piece missing or thin | Two pieces missing; or proofs lack full reasons | Half or more missing |
| **Justification correctness** | Every "why it works" cites the right definition/postulate/theorem; SSS loans flagged honestly | One mis-citation; loans flagged | Reasons vague ("because geometry") or mismatched | No reasons, or reasons contradict the steps |
| **Proof format and rigor** | Two-column tables complete; paragraph proof keeps every reason; no gaps | One dropped reason across all proofs | Several gaps; reasons like "looks true" appear | Proofs are calculations without reasons |
| **Clarity of communication** | A stranger could rebuild each construction and follow each proof from the writing alone | Minor ambiguities; steps still followable | Key steps ambiguous; reader must guess | Writing does not match the drawings |
| **Reflection** | Names the hardest proof, a caught error, and a definitions insight — all specific | Two of three specific | Generic ("proofs are hard") | Missing or one vague sentence |

**Readiness signal:** 18+/24 with no criterion below 2 → ready for the
unit assessment. A score below 18, or any 0–1, triggers the reteach plan
in `teacher-guide.md` (targeted re-proof of the weak theorem, one
rebuilt construction) before Session 19.

## Safety and access notes

Compass points are sharp — cap or close the compass when not drawing, and
keep fingers clear of the needle path. All construction work may be done
in GeoGebra Geometry instead of on paper (the written steps and
justifications are still required). The reflection may be dictated for
adult scribing. No learner names, photos, or identifying information
appear on any sheet that leaves the private learning setting.
