# Unit 05 — Quadrilaterals, Polygons, and Coordinate Proofs

**Grade 10 · Math · Geometry pathway · Weeks 19–22 of the course year**
(draft, delivered 2026-11-12 for issue #46; not educator-reviewed)

## Overview

Units 01–04 built the tools: precise definitions, proof, rigid motions,
similarity, and trigonometry. This unit turns those tools on the
four-sided figures — the polygons a learner meets everywhere, from a
book cover to a city block. The unit proceeds in three movements.
First, the learner *measures* polygons: the interior-angle sum
(n − 2)·180° and the exterior-angle sum 360°, proved by triangulation
(Lesson 01). Second, the learner *proves* the quadrilateral theorems:
parallelogram properties from congruent triangles, the special
parallelograms, trapezoids, kites, and the whole hierarchy with its
symmetries (Lessons 02–03). Third, the learner *proves with
coordinates*: slope, distance, and midpoint tests classify any
quadrilateral from its vertices, a section formula locates points that
divide a segment in a given ratio, and the shoelace formula finds
perimeters and areas on the grid — all applied to designing a real
courtyard (Lessons 04–05, project).

The essential question running through all four weeks is: **when a
figure is described two ways — by its shape and by its coordinates —
how do you prove the two descriptions say the same thing, and which
test settles the question fastest?**

## Prerequisites

- Unit 01: precise definitions, two-column and paragraph proof with a
  stated reason for every step, parallel-lines/transversal theorems
  (alternate interior angles), and the triangle angle sum (180°).
- Unit 02: triangle-congruence criteria (SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS) with
  rigid-motion reasoning; the midsegment theorem; slope criteria for
  parallel and perpendicular lines were first met here and in Unit 03.
- Unit 03: similarity, the Pythagorean Theorem, and the slope
  criteria proved from similar right triangles (G-GPE.5); distance
  and midpoint formulas from grade 9 coordinate work.
- Solving linear equations; simplifying radicals; the distance
  formula as the Pythagorean Theorem in coordinate form.
- No new trigonometry is assumed beyond Unit 04's right-triangle
  ratios, used only in one extension.

The Weeks 1–2 diagnostic already verified equation solving, radical
simplification, and coordinate graphing; this unit re-teaches them in
context where needed.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Derive and apply the polygon interior-angle sum (n − 2)·180° and
   the exterior-angle sum 360°; compute interior and exterior angles
   of regular polygons and the number of sides from a given angle.
   (G-CO.3 supports the regular-polygon symmetry work)
2. Prove parallelogram theorems — opposite sides and angles
   congruent, diagonals bisecting each other — and apply the
   rectangle, rhombus, and square properties (congruent diagonals,
   perpendicular-bisector diagonals, angle bisection) to find
   unknown sides, angles, and diagonals. (G-CO.11)
3. Classify trapezoids (including isosceles trapezoids), kites, and
   the full quadrilateral hierarchy; state each figure's symmetries
   (lines of reflection and rotational symmetry); and use the
   trapezoid midsegment and kite diagonal properties in problems.
   (G-CO.3, G-CO.11)
4. Prove geometric facts with coordinates: use slope criteria to
   test parallel and perpendicular sides, the distance formula to
   test congruent sides and diagonals, and the midpoint formula to
   test whether diagonals bisect each other; classify any
   quadrilateral from its vertices and find a missing vertex.
   (G-GPE.4, G-GPE.5)
5. Find the point that divides a segment in a given ratio (including
   the midpoint as the 1:1 case); compute perimeters and areas of
   polygons from coordinates (including the shoelace method); and
   model a physical site with a quadrilateral, reporting lengths,
   areas, and costs with units and a reasonableness check.
   (G-GPE.6, G-GPE.7; G-MG.1, N-Q.1, N-Q.3)

Objectives 5 and 10 of the track objectives (see the track README)
are the ones in play in this unit; objectives 1–4 and 6 are revisited
in Week 22.

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, High
School Geometry — Congruence and Expressing Geometric Properties with
Equations domains (descriptions verified against the published
standards text; wording here is paraphrase; the scope-and-sequence
crosswalk was verified against the official framework on 2026-10-07).
This unit addresses:

- **G-CO.3** — for rectangles, parallelograms, trapezoids, and
  regular polygons, describe the rotations and reflections that
  carry the figure onto itself (Lesson 03; regular polygons in
  Lesson 01).
- **G-CO.11** — prove theorems about parallelograms: opposite sides
  congruent, opposite angles congruent, diagonals bisecting each
  other, and rectangles having congruent diagonals (Lessons 02–03).
- **G-GPE.4** — use coordinates to prove simple geometric theorems
  algebraically, e.g., classifying quadrilaterals and testing
  whether diagonals bisect each other (Lesson 04; project).
- **G-GPE.5** — prove the slope criteria for parallel and
  perpendicular lines and use them to solve geometric problems
  (Lesson 04; used in Lesson 05 and the project).
- **G-GPE.6** — find the point on a directed line segment between
  two given points that partitions the segment in a given ratio
  (Lesson 05; project).
- **G-GPE.7** — use coordinates to compute perimeters of polygons
  and areas of triangles and rectangles, e.g., using the distance
  formula (Lesson 05; project).
- **G-MG.1** — use geometric shapes, measures, and properties to
  describe objects, e.g., modeling a courtyard plot as a
  quadrilateral (Lesson 05; project).
- **N-Q.1, N-Q.3** — units guide multi-step solutions; accuracy is
  chosen appropriate to measurement limits (Lesson 05; project).

**Definition conventions used in this unit (stated here so the adult
and learner never have to guess).** A **trapezoid** is a quadrilateral
with *exactly one* pair of parallel sides (the exclusive definition
used by Khan Academy's high-school geometry course and by the
K–12 LibreTexts geometry text, verified 2026-11-12). Some sources
use the inclusive definition (*at least* one pair), under which a
parallelogram is also a trapezoid; Lesson 03 names that difference
explicitly and the teacher guide records it. A **kite** has two
*distinct* pairs of adjacent congruent sides (again per LibreTexts);
a rhombus is therefore not a kite in this unit. Definitions in this
unit are inclusive elsewhere: a square is a rectangle and a rhombus,
and both are parallelograms. No state adoption, accreditation, or
alignment certification is claimed.

## Accessibility and supports

- Every coordinate task lists vertices in order and in text, so the
  work does not depend on reading a picture alone; the generated
  courtyard illustration carries a full text-only alternative, and
  the hierarchy diagram is also given as a nested text list in
  Lesson 03.
- Proof skeletons (statement column pre-filled) for the Lesson 02
  parallelogram proofs; paragraph-proof option always allowed.
- A "test chain" template in Lesson 04 (slopes → distances →
  midpoints) so a learner who cannot hold all three tests in mind
  still classifies correctly, one test at a time.
- Oral explanation accepted for exit checks; the adult scribes if
  needed.
- Extension: the trapezoid midsegment proof, the kite-area
  derivation, and the shoelace proof by decomposition are marked in
  the teacher guide.
