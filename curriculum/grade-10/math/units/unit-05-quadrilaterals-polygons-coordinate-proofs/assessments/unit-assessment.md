# Unit Assessment — Unit 05: Quadrilaterals, Polygons, and Coordinate Proofs

**Grade 10 · Math · Unit 05 · Session 19 · 50 points · ~50 minutes**

Show your work for every question. Exact values unless a decimal is
asked for. A proof needs a reason for every statement. All solutions
are in `answer-key.md` (adults only).

## Part A — Short answers (5 questions × 4 points = 20 points)

1. A regular dodecagon has 12 sides. Find its interior-angle sum
   and each interior angle.
2. In parallelogram ABCD, ∠A = 4x − 10 and ∠B = 2x + 40. Find x and
   the measure of each angle of the parallelogram. What special
   parallelogram is ABCD?
3. A rhombus has diagonals 24 and 10. Find its side length.
4. Classify the quadrilateral (0, 0), (6, 0), (7, 4), (1, 4). Give
   the slope evidence and say whether it is a rectangle or a
   rhombus, with a reason.
5. Find the point dividing the segment from (1, 5) to (9, 1) in the
   ratio 3:1.

## Part B — Problems (3 questions × 6 points = 18 points)

6. An isosceles trapezoid has bases 12 and 20 and legs of length 5.
   Find its height, its midsegment, and its area.
7. Consider the quadrilateral W(2, 1), X(6, 3), Y(5, 7), Z(1, 5).
   (a) Classify it, with slope and distance evidence (4 points).
   (b) Find its area by the shoelace formula (2 points).
8. A kite has symmetry diagonal 30 and the other diagonal 16. One
   of the shorter sides (joining the symmetry diagonal's end to a
   vertex of the other diagonal) is 10. (a) Find the length of the
   longer sides, exactly and to two decimal places (3 points).
   (b) Find the kite's area (3 points).

## Part C — Proof (12 points)

9. Prove that the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.
   Write a two-column proof (statement + reason for each step).
   Given: ABCD is a parallelogram; its diagonals AC and BD meet
   at E. Prove: AE = CE and BE = DE.

   You may use: the definition of a parallelogram, alternate
   interior angles, the opposite-sides theorem of Lesson 02, ASA
   congruence, and CPCTC.
