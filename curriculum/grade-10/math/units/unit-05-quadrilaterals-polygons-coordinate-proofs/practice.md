# Practice — Unit 05: Quadrilaterals, Polygons, and Coordinate Proofs

Work each set after its lesson. Show every step: the formula used, the
substitution, and a check (a second method, a 360° sum, or a distance
ratio). All solutions are in `answer-key.md` (adults only). Use exact
values (radicals, fractions) unless a decimal is asked for; round
decimals to two places unless the question says otherwise.

## Set A — Polygon angle sums (after Lesson 01)

1. Find the interior-angle sum of a nonagon (9 sides).
2. Find each interior angle of a regular hexagon.
3. Each exterior angle of a regular 15-gon is 24°. What is each
   interior angle? (First find the exterior angle from the number
   of sides, or use the interior sum — either way, show the step.)
4. A regular polygon has each exterior angle 45°. How many sides
   does it have, and what is each interior angle?
5. A pentagon has interior angles 90°, 105°, 115°, 120°, and x.
   Find x.
6. A heptagon has six interior angles of 130° each. Find the seventh
   angle.
7. True or false, with a one-sentence reason: "The exterior angles
   of a 20-sided convex polygon add to 360°."
8. A regular polygon has each interior angle 144°. How many sides
   does it have? Show both the exterior method and the algebra
   method.

## Set B — Parallelogram theorems (after Lesson 02)

1. Parallelogram ABCD has AB = 11, BC = 6, and ∠B = 73°. Find CD,
   AD, ∠A, ∠C, and ∠D.
2. The diagonals of parallelogram ABCD meet at E. DE = 9 and
   CE = 7. Find BD and AC.
3. In parallelogram ABCD, ∠A = 2x + 15 and ∠C = 5x − 45. Find x
   and the measure of each angle.
4. A rectangle has diagonal 25 and one side 7. Find the other side.
5. A rhombus has side length 13 and one diagonal 10. Find the other
   diagonal.
6. Prove: a parallelogram with one right angle is a rectangle.
   (Write a two-column or paragraph proof.)
7. Parallelogram EFGH has EF = 3x + 2 and GH = 5x − 8. Find x and
   the length EF.
8. Error analysis: a learner writes, "The diagonals of parallelogram
   ABCD are 10 and 14, and they bisect each other, so AC ⊥ BD."
   Explain the error in two or three sentences.

## Set C — Trapezoids, kites, hierarchy (after Lesson 03)

1. An isosceles trapezoid has bases 6 and 16 and legs 13. Find its
   height, its midsegment, and its area.
2. A kite has diagonals 18 and 24. Find its area.
3. A kite has AB = AD = 10, CB = CD = 17, and the symmetry diagonal
   AC = 21. Find the other diagonal BD and the kite's area.
4. A trapezoid's midsegment is 15 and one base is 12. Find the other
   base.
5. A quadrilateral has both pairs of opposite angles congruent. What
   can you conclude, and why? (One sentence for the conclusion, one
   for the reason.)
6. How many lines of symmetry does a regular pentagon have, and what
   is its smallest angle of rotational symmetry? Then find each
   interior angle.
7. True or false, with a one-sentence reason each: (a) "Every rhombus
   is a parallelogram." (b) "Every parallelogram is a rhombus."
8. A kite has one diagonal 16 and area 96. Find the other diagonal.

## Set D — Coordinate proofs (after Lesson 04)

1. Classify A(0, 0), B(2, 0), C(3, 2), D(1, 2) using the test chain.
   Give the slope evidence.
2. Prove that P(1, 2), Q(4, 6), R(7, 2), S(4, −2) is a rhombus.
   Then decide whether it is a square, with evidence.
3. A(2, 1), B(6, 2), C(5, 6) are three vertices of parallelogram
   ABCD in order. Find D, and check your answer with slopes.
4. Classify the quadrilateral (0, 0), (5, 1), (8, 5), (3, 4). Give
   slope, distance, and diagonal-midpoint evidence.
5. Is the quadrilateral (0, 0), (4, 0), (4, 3), (0, 3) a square?
   Justify with computations.
6. True or false, with a one-sentence reason: "If a quadrilateral's
   diagonals have the same midpoint, it must be a rectangle."
7. A(0, 0), B(2, 0), C(2, 2) are three vertices of square ABCD in
   order. Find D.
8. Error analysis: a learner lists the side slopes of a quadrilateral
   in order as 1, 2, 1, 3 and concludes, "Two slopes match, so it is
   a parallelogram." Explain the error.

## Set E — Section formula, area, modeling (after Lesson 05)

1. Find the point dividing the segment from (2, 1) to (8, 9) in the
   ratio 1:1.
2. Find the point dividing the segment from (0, 0) to (12, 4) in the
   ratio 2:1, and check your answer with distances.
3. Find the area of the triangle (2, 3), (7, 3), (2, 8) by shoelace,
   and verify it with (1/2)·base·height.
4. Find the perimeter of the quadrilateral (0, 0), (3, 0), (4, 2),
   (1, 2). Give an exact answer and a decimal to two places.
5. The point P(10, 8) divides AB in the ratio 3:2, where A(4, 2).
   Find B.
6. Find the area of the rectangle (1, 2), (5, 2), (5, 6), (1, 6) by
   shoelace, and verify it by length × width.
7. A triangular garden has vertices (0, 0), (10, 0), (0, 6), in
   metres. Turf costs $12 per square metre (fictional practice
   price). Find the area and the turf cost.
8. Error analysis: a learner lists the vertices (0, 0), (4, 3),
   (4, 0), (0, 3) in that order for the shoelace and gets area 0.
   Explain the error, give the correct vertex order, and find the
   correct area.
