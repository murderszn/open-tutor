# Project — Design the Courtyard

**Unit 05 · Sessions 14–15 (studios) and 17 (gallery) · ~50 minutes each**

## The brief

A community group is turning an empty plot into a small courtyard
garden. The plot's corners, in metres on a survey grid, are

A(0, 0), B(12, 0), C(10, 8), D(0, 8).

The illustration `assets/quadrilateral-courtyard-illustration.png`
shows the site from above: a four-sided lawn with a diagonal stone
walkway, a rectangular patio in one corner, round planters, benches,
and a tree. The illustration shows the layout only — every number in
this project comes from the coordinates above, given in the text
(the text-only alternative is in Lesson 05).

Your job is the geometry: classify the plot, measure it, design the
walkway and patio, place a planter, price the work, and write a short
design report the group can actually use. All prices below are
**fictional practice prices**, labeled as such; the adult checks any
real price before a real purchase.

## Deliverables

1. **Classification sheet** — the plot's type, proved with slope,
   distance, and angle evidence (not a picture guess).
2. **Measurement sheet** — perimeter (fencing), area (turf), and the
   diagonal walkway length, each with the computation shown and a
   reasonableness check.
3. **Design sheet** — the rectangular patio (corner coordinates and
   area) and the planter point on the walkway (coordinates, ratio
   shown).
4. **Cost table** — fencing, turf, walkway, and patio paving, with a
   total, all from the measurements in deliverables 2–3.
5. **Design report** (one page) — a labeled sketch of the plot with
   the walkway, patio, and planter marked; a short paragraph
   explaining the classification and the design choices; and a note
   on accuracy (what the measurements support, and what would need
   a re-survey before building).

## Steps

1. **Classify (studio 1, first half).** Compute the four side slopes
   and the four side lengths. Name the quadrilateral and justify it
   with the test chain from Lesson 04. State the angles at A and D
   (use slopes) and say what kind of trapezoid the plot is.
2. **Measure (studio 1, second half).** Compute the perimeter and
   the area two ways (trapezoid formula and shoelace — they must
   agree). Compute the diagonal walkway length AC exactly and to two
   decimal places. Run the bracket check from Lesson 05: the area
   must sit between the 10-by-8 and 12-by-8 rectangles.
3. **Design the patio (studio 2, first half).** A rectangular patio
   4 m by 3 m is planned with one corner at D(0, 8) and sides
   parallel to the axes, inside the plot. Give the coordinates of
   its other three corners and its area, and verify it fits inside
   the plot (check the right-hand boundary at the patio's height).
4. **Place the planter (studio 2, first half).** A planter is to sit
   on the walkway AC at the point dividing AC in the ratio 2:1 from
   A. Find its coordinates with the section formula and check them
   with distances.
5. **Price it (studio 2, second half).** Fencing $18 per metre,
   turf $9 per square metre, walkway stone $24 per metre, patio
   paving $30 per square metre (all fictional practice prices).
   Build the cost table and total. Round money to cents and the
   total to the nearest dollar, and say which measurement the total
   is most sensitive to.
6. **Report (studio 2 end + gallery).** Draw the labeled sketch
   (graph paper or GeoGebra), write the one-page report, and present
   it in the gallery session. The adult critiques one number and one
   design choice; address the critique or log it as an open question.

## Materials

Grid paper, ruler, protractor, calculator, the courtyard illustration
and the hierarchy diagram; optional device with a browser for
GeoGebra (free, no account) to model the plot and check the
classification visually. Adult supervision for any measuring done on
a real site; the whole project can be completed from the coordinates
alone, with GeoGebra as the simulation alternative — no site visit
is required.

## Rubric (24 points)

| Criterion | 4 — complete | 3 — good | 2 — partial | 1 — attempted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Classification (4) | Type named with full slope + distance evidence; angles at A and D stated; trapezoid kind named | Type named with slope evidence; one angle or the kind missing | Type named from the picture with partial computation | Type guessed without computation |
| Perimeter and area (4) | Both correct (38.25 m, 88 m²) with work shown, two methods for area, bracket check done | Both correct with work shown, one method | One correct, or both with arithmetic slips | Measurements attempted without method |
| Walkway and planter (4) | Walkway exact (2√41 m) and decimal (12.81 m); planter (20/3, 16/3) with distance check | Both correct, check missing | One correct | Attempted without the formulas |
| Patio (4) | Corners (4, 8), (4, 5), (0, 5) correct, area 12 m², fit verified against the right boundary | Corners and area correct, fit check missing | Corners or area wrong | Patio placed outside the plot or unmeasured |
| Cost table (4) | All four lines correct with units, total ≈ $2,148, sensitivity named | All four lines correct, total or sensitivity missing | Two or three lines correct | Costs attempted without measurements |
| Report and sketch (4) | Labeled sketch accurate; paragraph explains classification and choices; accuracy note honest and specific | Sketch labeled; paragraph present; accuracy note vague | Sketch or paragraph incomplete | Report missing or unreadable |

**What counts as done:** 16/24 or better, with the classification and
measurement criteria each at 3 or better. Below that, reteach with
the Lesson 04 test chain and the Lesson 05 worked examples, then
revise the two weakest deliverables.
