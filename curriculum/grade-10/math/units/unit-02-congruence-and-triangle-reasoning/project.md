# Project — Truss Rigidity Studio

**Unit 02 · Sessions 14–15 (studios), Session 17 (gallery) · Weeks 9–10**

## Driving question

*Why do engineers build bridges out of triangles — and what does that
have to do with SSS?*

## Materials

Drinking straws or craft sticks (at least 30); paper fasteners, pipe
cleaners, or brass brads for joints; scissors (adult use or adult
supervision); ruler; protractor; notebook; the unit's bridge-truss
illustration (`assets/bridge-truss-illustration.webp`); optional device
with GeoGebra for the simulation alternative.

**Safety.** An adult handles or directly supervises all cutting. Joints
should move freely at first — do not glue anything until Part 1's
observations are recorded.

---

## Part 1 — What holds its shape? (Session 14, ~50 min)

1. Build a **triangle** from three straws, joined loosely at the
   corners (fasteners, not glue). Try to deform it — push a corner
   sideways. Record what happens.
2. Build a **quadrilateral** from four straws, same loose joints. Try
   to deform it. Record what happens (it shears into different
   parallelogram shapes).
3. Build a second quadrilateral with the *same four side lengths* but
   a different shape (e.g., a square and a skinny rhombus). Confirm
   both use identical straws.
4. Now brace your quadrilateral with a diagonal straw (making two
   triangles). Try to deform it again. Record the change.
5. **Write-up (notebook, ~15 min):** Explain observations 1–4 using
   SSS. Your explanation must use these facts: three side lengths
   determine a triangle completely (Lesson 03's rigid-motion
   justification); four side lengths do *not* determine a
   quadrilateral; the diagonal created two triangles, each fixed by
   SSS. Conclude: *why is the triangle the engineer's rigid unit?*

**Simulation alternative.** In GeoGebra, construct a triangle and a
quadrilateral with the polygon tool. Drag vertices. Record which
figure's shape is forced by its side lengths and which is not. (The
adult previews GeoGebra for advertising; no account is needed.)

## Part 2 — Truss design + congruence-mapping report (Session 15, ~50 min)

1. **Observe** the AI-generated bridge-truss illustration
   (`assets/bridge-truss-illustration.webp`; AI illustration, not a
   photograph — proportions approximate). Count the repeating
   triangular units. In your notebook, answer: (a) how many triangles;
   (b) what "these triangles are congruent" means in rigid-motion
   language; (c) your SSS-based prediction from Part 1 about why the
   designer repeated triangles.
2. **Design** your own truss on paper: a bridge span made of **at
   least 8 triangles**, all mutually congruent, sharing sides the way
   the illustration's units do. Mark every triangle's vertices
   (*T*₁, *T*₂, …). Label side lengths once (they are all the same —
   say so explicitly rather than re-marking).
3. **Congruence-mapping report** (one page, the graded deliverable):
   - Pick any two of your truss triangles. Name a rigid motion —
     translation by a stated vector, reflection across a stated line,
     rotation about a stated center through a stated angle — that
     carries the first exactly onto the second. (More than one answer
     is possible; yours must be *specific*.)
   - State which triangle-congruence criterion your mapping
     illustrates, and why SSA-style ambiguity cannot arise here.
   - Explain, in 3–5 sentences, how SSS explains your Part 1
     observation that braced structures hold their shape.
4. **Gallery prep:** mount or photograph the straw builds and the
   truss design for Session 17.

---

## Deliverables

1. Part 1 observation log + SSS explanation (notebook).
2. Truss design drawing (≥ 8 congruent triangles, labeled).
3. One-page congruence-mapping report.
4. (Session 17) Gallery presentation: 2-minute walkthrough of the
   builds and the report's rigid motion.

## Rubric (24 points)

| Criterion | 6 — Strong | 4 — Adequate | 2 — Developing | 0–1 — Missing/unclear |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Observation accuracy** | Triangle holds shape / quadrilateral shears / braced quad. holds — all recorded with specifics | Two of three observations recorded specifically | Vague ("it moved") or one observation only | No usable log |
| **SSS reasoning** | Correctly links "3 sides fix a triangle; 4 don't fix a quadrilateral" to the observations, in own words | Links SSS to observations with one gap | Mentions SSS without connecting it to the builds | No SSS reasoning |
| **Congruence mapping** | A *specific* rigid motion (vector/line/center+angle stated) mapping one truss triangle onto another; criterion named correctly | Motion named but underspecified (e.g., "a translation" with no vector) | Motion incorrect or correspondence order wrong | No mapping attempted |
| **Communication** | Report organized; terms (rigid motion, congruent, SSS, correspondence) used correctly; gallery walkthrough clear | Mostly correct terminology; minor disorganization | Frequent term misuse; hard to follow | Incoherent or missing |

**Scoring:** 21–24 = exceeds; 16–20 = meets (project counts as done);
11–15 = revise one criterion with the adult and resubmit; ≤ 10 =
reteach SSS and rigid motions, then rebuild.

## Reteach and extension

- **Reteach trigger:** if the SSS explanation is missing or the mapping
  motion is wrong, revisit Lesson 03's worked examples 1–2 with
  tracing paper before the gallery.
- **Extension:** prove that a *regular* truss triangle (equilateral)
  has 3 lines of symmetry and 120° rotational symmetry, and show how
  those symmetries give *three different* rigid motions mapping the
  triangle onto itself. (Connects Lessons 01–02 symmetry work to the
  congruence definition.)
