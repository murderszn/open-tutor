## Set A — Translations, reflections, rigid motions (Week 7, Session 2; supports Lesson 01)

1. Translate △*ABC* — *A*(2, 3), *B*(6, 3), *C*(6, 7) — by the vector
   "3 left, 1 down": (*x*, *y*) → (*x* − 3, *y* − 1). Write the image
   coordinates and verify that *AC* = *A′C′* with the distance formula.
2. Reflect point *P*(5, 2) across the line *y* = *x*. Write *P′* and
   verify that the midpoint of *PP′* lies on the mirror line.
3. On grid paper, draw any triangle and a mirror line that does *not*
   touch it. Reflect the triangle. Measure one side and one angle in
   the preimage and the image; record both pairs.
4. Which of these have at least one line of symmetry? For each "yes,"
   state how many: (a) isosceles triangle, (b) non-rectangle
   parallelogram, (c) regular pentagon, (d) circle.
5. **Error analysis.** "A translation moves each point a different
   distance — points farther from the origin move more." Explain the
   error and state the correct rule.
6. A dilation with scale factor 2 maps segment *AB* (length 5 cm) to
   *A′B′*. Is it a rigid motion? Find *A′B′* and explain.
7. **Pool-table aim.** The rail is the *x*-axis. The cue ball is at
   (1, 2); the target ball is at (5, 3). (a) Reflect the target across
   the rail. (b) Write the equation of the aiming line through the cue
   ball and the reflected point. (c) Find the bounce point (where the
   line meets the rail).

## Set B — Rotations, composition, orientation (Week 7, Session 4; supports Lesson 02)

1. Rotate point *P*(3, 1) 90° counterclockwise about the origin. Write
   *P′* and verify *OP* = *OP′*.
2. Rotate △*ABC* (*A*(1,1), *B*(4,1), *C*(4,3)) 180° about the origin.
   List the image coordinates. What is the coordinate rule for a 180°
   rotation about the origin?
3. Reflect point *Q*(2, 0) across the line *y* = 1, then across
   *y* = 4. (a) Predict the single motion *before* computing.
   (b) Compute and confirm. (c) How far apart are the mirror lines,
   and how does that relate to your answer?
4. Two mirrors meet at 30°. A figure is reflected across the first,
   then the second. Name the resulting single motion (type, center,
   angle).
5. △*PQR* is labeled clockwise. It undergoes a reflection, then a
   rotation (in that order). Is △*P″Q″R″* labeled clockwise or
   counterclockwise? Explain by counting direct/opposite motions.
6. A regular pentagon is centered at the origin. List all its
   rotational symmetries (angles strictly between 0° and 360°).
7. **Error analysis.** "A 90° rotation about (1, 1) sends (1, 1) to
   (2, 2)." What is wrong? Where does (1, 1) actually go?

## Set C — Congruence, SSS, SAS (Week 8, Session 7; supports Lesson 03)

1. △*ABC* has *AB* = 6, *BC* = 8, *CA* = 10. △*DEF* has *DE* = 10,
   *EF* = 6, *FD* = 8. (a) Are the triangles congruent? By which
   criterion? (b) Write the congruence statement with correct
   correspondence order. *(Hint: match longest side to longest side.)*
2. Write a two-column proof: Given *AB* = *DE*, *AC* = *DF*,
   ∠*A* ≅ ∠*D*. Prove △*ABC* ≅ △*DEF*. Then add one sentence naming
   the rigid-motion sequence the proof relies on.
3. Surveying data: ∠*P* = 40°, *PQ* = 9, *QR* = 6 (∠*P* is not between
   the known sides). Does this determine △*PQR*? Use the perpendicular
   distance 9·sin 40° (≈ 5.79) to decide whether the arc cuts the ray
   0, 1, or 2 times.
4. Explain why SSS needs the triangle inequality: what goes wrong, in
   the rigid-motion justification, with side lengths 2, 3, 8?
5. A bridge truss repeats triangles with sides 5 m, 5 m, 6 m.
   (a) Why must all the truss triangles be congruent? (b) What does
   that let the engineer assume about every joint angle?
6. △*ABC* ≅ △*DEF* with correspondence *A*↔*D*, *B*↔*E*, *C*↔*F*.
   If ∠*A* = 70° and *AB* = 4 cm, find m∠*D* and *DE*. Name the
   principle.
7. Draw the SSA counterexample from Lesson 03 (∠*A* = 30°, *AC* = 7,
   *BC* = 4). Label both possible positions *B*₁ and *B*₂ and the two
   triangles. Mark the three given parts identically on both.
8. **Error analysis.** A learner writes: "*AB* = *DE*, *BC* = *FD*,
   *CA* = *EF*, so △*ABC* ≅ △*DEF* by SSS." The side equalities are
   all true. What is wrong with the conclusion as written? Write a
   correct congruence statement or explain why none follows from the
   given labeling.

## Set D — ASA, AAS, perpendicular bisector (Week 8, Session 9; supports Lesson 04)

1. ∠*G* = ∠*T*, *GH* = *TU*, ∠*H* = ∠*U*. (a) Which criterion?
   (b) Write the full congruence statement. (c) Which side is the
   *included* side?
2. ∠*A* = ∠*D* = 35°, ∠*B* = ∠*E* = 80°, *BC* = *EF* = 7 cm.
   (a) Find m∠*C* (show the borrowed lemma you use).
   (b) Write the congruence statement and name the criterion.
3. Prove: *if P lies on the perpendicular bisector of AB, then
   △APM ≅ △BPM where M is the midpoint of AB.* (This is the engine of
   the forward direction — two-column, every reason stated.)
4. *A*(−3, 1), *B*(5, 1). (a) Write the equation of the perpendicular
   bisector of *AB*. (b) Show *P*(1, 7) is equidistant from *A* and
   *B*. (c) Is *P* on the bisector? What did the theorem predict?
5. **Cell tower.** Towns at *A*(0, 0) and *B*(8, 0); the highway is
   the line *y* = 3. (a) Where on the highway can an equidistant
   tower stand? (b) Verify equidistance numerically. (c) If the
   highway were *y* = 0 instead, how many solutions? Explain.
6. Write the *converse* of the perpendicular-bisector theorem as a
   paragraph proof (assume *PA* = *PB*; prove *P* is on the bisector).
   You may compress the algebra, but every geometric reason must be
   stated.
7. **Error analysis.** In proving the converse, a learner writes
   "△*PAM* ≅ △*PBM* by SAS." Identify the circular reasoning: which
   angle would SAS require, and why is it unavailable at that step?

## Set E — Triangle reasoning (Week 9, Session 12; supports Lesson 05)

1. A triangle's angles measure (*x* + 20)°, 2*x*°, (3*x* − 20)°.
   Find *x* and each angle. Check the sum.
2. △*PQR* is isosceles with *PQ* = *PR* and vertex angle ∠*P* = 36°.
   Find the base angles. Identify the base.
3. Two remote interior angles measure 45° and 78°. Find the exterior
   angle. Then find the adjacent interior angle.
4. Prove: *the three exterior angles of any triangle (one per vertex,
   taken in the same rotational direction) sum to 360°.*
5. △*ABC* ≅ △*XYZ*. The perimeter of △*ABC* is 24 cm; *XY* = 9 cm,
   *YZ* = 7 cm. (a) Find *XZ*. (b) A learner says "so *AB* = 9 cm."
   Is that forced? Explain with correspondence order.
6. **Surveying.** Baseline *AB* = 100 m; from *A* the sight line to
   *C* makes 60° with the baseline; from *B*, 70°. (a) Find the third
   angle. (b) Which criterion guarantees the triangle — and hence the
   distance *AC* — is determined? (c) Why can't this unit compute
   *AC* numerically yet?
7. **Error analysis.** "∠*A* = ∠*D* and ∠*B* = ∠*E*, so △*ABC* ≅
   △*DEF* by AA." (a) What is the error? (b) What *is* true about the
   two triangles? (c) Which unit studies that relationship?
8. **Challenge.** △*ABC* has *AB* = *AC* = 5 cm and vertex angle
   ∠*A* = 50°. (a) Find the base angles. (b) Describe the reflection
   that proves your answer (mirror line? what swaps with what?).
   (c) Explain where the hypothesis *AB* = *AC* is used — what breaks
   without it?
