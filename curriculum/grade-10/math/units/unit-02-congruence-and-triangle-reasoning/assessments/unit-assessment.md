# Unit Assessment — Unit 02: Congruence, Rigid Motions, and Triangle Reasoning

**Unit 02 · Week 10, Session 19 · ~50 minutes · 50 points**

Full worked solutions are in `../answer-key.md` (adults only). This is
the culminating assessment for the unit's five objectives. Show all
work; a correct answer without reasoning earns half credit. Proofs need
a stated reason for every step (two-column or paragraph).

**Readiness bands:** 42–50 = exceeds (ready for Unit 03); 35–41 =
meets (unit counts as done); 28–34 = revise: redo the missed
objectives' practice sets and retake a parallel form; below 28 =

## Part A — Rigid motions (12 points)

1. (4 pts) Translate △*ABC* — *A*(0, 1), *B*(3, 1), *C*(3, 4) — by the
   vector "2 right, 3 down". (a) Write the image coordinates.
   (b) Verify with the distance formula that *AB* = *A′B′*.
   (c) In one sentence, say what (b) confirms about translations.

2. (4 pts) A square is centered at the origin. (a) List all its
   rotational symmetries (angles strictly between 0° and 360°).
   (b) How many lines of symmetry does it have? (c) A learner says
   "a square has 8 symmetries." Explain what the 8 are.

3. (4 pts) Point *P*(1, 1) is reflected across the line *y* = 2, then
   across the line *y* = 7. (a) Predict the single equivalent motion
   *before* computing. (b) Compute the final image of *P* and confirm
   your prediction.

## Part B — Congruence criteria (18 points)

4. (5 pts) △*DEF* has *DE* = 9, *EF* = 12, *FD* = 15. △*PQR* has
   *PQ* = 15, *QR* = 9, *RP* = 12. (a) Are they congruent? By which
   criterion? (b) Write the congruence statement with correct
   correspondence order. (c) Find m∠*D* if m∠*Q* = 90° — and name the
   principle that lets you.

5. (5 pts) Two-column proof. Given: *AB* = *AC* in △*ABC*; *AD* is
   the bisector of ∠*A* (with *D* on *BC*). Prove: △*ABD* ≅ △*ACD*,
   and then prove ∠*B* ≅ ∠*C*. (Name the criterion and the final
   principle.)

6. (4 pts) ∠*J* = ∠*X* = 40°, ∠*K* = ∠*Y* = 65°, *KL* = *YZ* = 10 cm.
   (a) Find m∠*L*, showing the lemma you borrow. (b) Write the
   congruence statement and name the criterion.

7. (4 pts) A truss designer marks two triangles with: *AB* = *DE*,
   *BC* = *EF*, ∠*B* = ∠*E*. (a) Which criterion proves them
   congruent? (b) The designer then claims "three matching angles
   would prove it just as well." Is that right? Explain what three
   matching angles *do* guarantee, and name the unit that studies it.

## Part C — Theorems and reasoning (20 points)

8. (5 pts) Prove **one** direction of the perpendicular-bisector
   theorem (your choice): either "point on the bisector ⇒ equidistant"
   or "equidistant ⇒ point on the bisector." Two-column or paragraph;
   every step needs a reason.

9. (4 pts) The interior angle-sum proof uses an auxiliary line through
   *C* parallel to *AB*. The skeleton below is missing its reasons —
   fill them:
   - (i) Through *C*, draw ℓ ∥ *AB*. Reason: ______
   - (ii) ∠*A* ≅ ∠*ACE* (alternate interior). Reason: ______
   - (iii) ∠*B* ≅ ∠*BCD* (alternate interior). Reason: ______
   - (iv) m∠*ACE* + m∠*ACB* + m∠*BCD* = 180°. Reason: ______
   - (v) Therefore m∠*A* + m∠*B* + m∠*C* = 180°. Reason: ______

10. (4 pts) An exterior angle of a triangle measures (4*x* + 10)°;
    the two remote interior angles measure (*x* + 10)° and
    (2*x* + 30)°. Find *x*, the exterior measure, and the adjacent
    interior measure. Check your work.

11. (4 pts) △*ABC* is isosceles with legs *AB* = *BC* and vertex angle
    ∠*B* = 50°. (a) Identify the base and the base angles.
    (b) Find each base angle. (c) Find the exterior angle at *B*.

12. (3 pts) A surveyor measures a 100 m baseline and two angles to a
    point across a river, then announces the distance is *determined*
    — but cannot say the number of meters yet. (a) Which criterion
    makes the distance determined? (b) Explain the difference between
    "determined" and "computed," and name the unit that will compute
    it.
