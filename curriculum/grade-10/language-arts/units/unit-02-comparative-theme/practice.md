# Unit 02 Practice Sessions (A–F)

Six named practice sessions woven into the 20-session pacing (see
[README](README.md)). Each names its activity and success criterion; the
[answer key](answer-key.md) solves every item. Sessions are done with the
adult unless marked independent.

## Practice A — Comparison outline drill (Session 4, week 7)

**Activity:** Build *two* outlines for the same shared question —
*"What does each story claim we owe a stranger?"* — one point-by-point,
one in blocks. Use "The Salt Road" and "The Harbor Light."

**Do it:**
1. Point-by-point outline: three points. Under each point, one quoted or
   cited detail from each story, then a one-line "so what" for the point.
2. Block outline: one block per story (each block: the story's claim in
   one sentence + two details), then a comparison block with the named
   difference and its "so what."
3. Error analysis: swap outlines with the adult. The adult deliberately
   introduces *one* fairness violation into your point-by-point outline
   (an unshared question, incomparable evidence, or a hidden difference).
   Find it, name the broken rule, and repair it.

**Success criterion:** Both outlines answer the same shared question with
comparable evidence; the planted violation is found and named.

## Practice B — Thesis revision clinic (Session 7, week 8)

**Activity:** Revise four weak theses into arguable three-part claims.

**Do it:** For each draft below: (1) name the missing part(s) —
shared question, named difference/likeness, "so what"; (2) rewrite;
(3) underline the "so what" clause.

1. "I will compare 'The Salt Road' and 'The Harbor Light' in this essay."
2. "'The Salt Road' has a flashback but 'The Harbor Light' does not."
3. "Both stories are about being kind, which is a good theme."
4. "While 'The Salt Road' is exciting, 'The Harbor Light' is slow, so the first is better for action fans."

Then: take your strongest revision and subject it to the Lesson 02
skeptic test — can the adult agree with it without reading any evidence?
If yes, revise again.

**Success criterion:** Four revised theses, each with all three parts; the
skeptic test fails (in the good way) on at least three.

## Practice C — Dual theme-tracker workshop (Session 10, week 8)

**Activity:** Build full dual trackers for a *new* shared theme across
both stories: **memory** — what each story claims memory is *for*.

**Do it:**
1. "Salt Road" tracker: three rows minimum (use the road-law line, the
   Tam flashback, and the final "the debt is paid"). End at a v3.
2. "Harbor Light" tracker: three rows minimum (the opening frame, the
   cedar chest / father's coat, the closing lamp). End at a v3.
3. Write the comparative claim the two v3s support, in two sentences.
   Then write one sentence on what this comparison reveals that the
   hospitality comparison (Lesson 03) did not.

**Success criterion:** Two trackers with quoted details and at least one
revision each; the comparative claim names a difference the evidence
earns.

## Practice D — Structural contrast chart + effect paragraphs (Session 12, week 9)

**Activity:** Complete the six-cell chart from Lesson 04 guided practice,
then convert two cells into effect paragraphs.

**Do it:**
1. Chart: rows = event order, pacing, point of view; columns = the two
   stories. One cited detail per cell.
2. Choose the two cells where the *difference* is sharpest. Write one
   paragraph per cell-pair in the form: *choice in A → effect; choice in
   B → different effect; what the contrast reveals.* Quote once per story
   per paragraph.
3. Error analysis: the adult offers this claim — "The Harbor Light would
   be more exciting with a flashback." Diagnose it using Lesson 04's
   fitness rule (which effects does *this* story need?) in three
   sentences.

**Success criterion:** Chart complete with citations; two paragraphs each
attach a choice to an effect and compare; the error analysis names the
fitness rule.

## Practice E — Source-transformation lab (Session 14, week 9)

**Activity:** Apply the three-layer inventory to a *new* transformation
you invent.

**Do it:**
1. Choose a story, myth, or fairy tale you know well (adult-approved).
   Write a 150-word transformed opening: change the teller or the form
   (letter, diary, news report, monologue) but keep the core facts.
2. Build the three-layer inventory (kept / changed / transformed) for
   your opening, with specifics.
3. Write one paragraph answering: what does your version claim that the
   source did not? (If the answer is "nothing yet" — that is useful data.
   Revise the opening until something moves.)
4. Exchange with the adult: the adult names your transformation's layer-3
   shift in their own words. If they can't find one, revise.

**Success criterion:** A 150-word transformed opening plus a complete
three-layer inventory; the adult can name the theme shift.

## Practice F — Essay revision studio + quiz-miss review (Session 17, week 10)

**Activity:** Revise the comparative essay draft against the rubric, then
close the week's quiz gaps.

**Do it:**
1. Read your draft aloud (or have the adult read it). Mark every
   quotation: is it *handled* (introduced, cited, explained) or just
   dropped in? Repair all dropped quotations.
2. Find your "so what." Highlight it. Ask: does the *conclusion* answer
   it, or just repeat it? Rewrite the conclusion so it answers.
3. Counter-thesis test: write the strongest one-sentence case *against*
   your thesis, then add one sentence to the essay acknowledging and
   answering it.
4. Quiz-miss review: revisit every missed item on the week-10 quiz with
   the adult. For each miss, write the rule it violated (fairness,
   thesis parts, tracking, structure→effect, layers, medium trade) in
   your own words.

**Success criterion:** Draft with handled quotations, an answered
"so what," and a counter-thesis sentence; every quiz miss re-solved with
its rule named.
