# Unit 02 — Comparative Theme Structure and Perspective

**Grade:** 10 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #48](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/48), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

U01 taught reading *within* a text. This unit teaches reading *across*
texts — the signature comparative move of the track. Across four weeks
the learner builds six moves: judging a comparison fair (shared
question, comparable evidence, named differences); writing an arguable
comparative thesis; tracking a theme's development inside each text
*before* comparing (dual theme tracking); analyzing how event order,
pacing, and point of view create effects in two stories; analyzing how a
retelling draws on and transforms its source; and comparing one scene
across two artistic mediums. It ends in a 900–1,200-word comparative
essay and a Two-Tradition Theme Casebook project.

## Essential question

When two texts take up the same human concern, what does their
*difference* reveal that neither text shows alone?

## Prerequisites

From U01 and the diagnostic weeks:

- Cite strong and thorough textual evidence; sort evidence from
  assumption (RL.9-10.1)
- Determine a theme and analyze its development; write an objective
  summary (RL.9-10.2)
- Analyze point of view and cultural experience (RL.9-10.6); analyze
  word choice and tone (RL.9-10.4)

What is *new* in this unit: **comparison as an argument** (not a
ranking), the **three-part comparative thesis**, **dual theme tracking**
(v1→v3 per text), **structure→effect analysis across two texts**
(RL.9-10.5), **source transformation** (RL.9-10.9), and **cross-medium
comparison** (RL.9-10.7).

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Judge a comparison fair using the three rules — shared question,
   comparable evidence, named differences — and organize one
   point-by-point and in blocks.
2. Write an arguable three-part comparative thesis (texts + shared
   question, named difference/likeness, "so what") that a skeptic cannot
   agree with for free (W.9-10.2, W.9-10.9).
3. Track a theme's development in detail *within* each of two texts
   (v1→v3 with quoted details) before making any comparative claim, and
   compare what the two developed themes reveal (RL.9-10.2).
4. Analyze how each text's choices of event order, pacing, and point of
   view create specific effects, and compare the effects (RL.9-10.5).
5. Analyze how a later work draws on source material and transforms it —
   distinguishing kept, changed, and transformed, including theme-level
   transformation (RL.9-10.9).
6. Analyze the representation of one scene in two artistic mediums,
   naming what each emphasizes and leaves out and what the trade reveals
   (RL.9-10.7).

## Materials

Notebook or paper, pen/pencil, index cards for exit checks, a folder for
the Casebook project, the six lesson pages. No purchases; no hazardous
materials. All reading is on the page — no outside book required.

## 20-session pacing (4 weeks × 5 sessions, ~50 minutes each)

The six lessons are the direct-instruction core (each spans a teach
session plus follow-up work). The remaining sessions are practice,
independent reading, project work, and assessment. Each session names
its activity and success criterion — see the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md)
for the full map.

| Session | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (Wk 7) | Lesson 01 teach: fair comparison; point-by-point vs. block | Learner states the three fairness rules in own words |
| 2 | Lesson 01 independent practice + read "The Salt Road" | Shared question + comparable evidence listed |
| 3 | Lesson 02 teach: comparative thesis, three parts | Weak thesis diagnosed by missing part |
| 4 | **Practice A** — comparison outline drill (both shapes) | Two outlines, same question; planted violation found |
| 5 | Read "The Harbor Light"; discussion: first v1 themes | v1 theme per story recorded |
| 6 (Wk 8) | Lesson 03 teach: dual theme tracking, v1→v3 | Tracker rows quote details; one revision shown |
| 7 | **Practice B** — thesis revision clinic | Four theses revised; skeptic test applied |
| 8 | Lesson 02/03 catch-up; independent reading | Thesis draft with underlined "so what" |
| 9 | Casebook project: Step 1 (concern + second text, with adult) | Text chosen; edition/source recorded |
| 10 | **Practice C** — dual tracker workshop (theme: memory) | Two v3 trackers; comparative claim written |
| 11 (Wk 9) | Lesson 04 teach: structure → effects; contrast chart | Chart cells cited; one effect paragraph drafted |
| 12 | **Practice D** — chart completion + effect paragraphs | Two paragraphs attach choice to effect |
| 13 | Casebook: Steps 2–3 (trackers + chart/medium page) | Drafts checked for quotation accuracy |
| 14 | **Practice E** — transformation lab (invented retelling) | 150-word opening + three-layer inventory |
| 15 | Lesson 05 teach: source transformation (fable → "The Rematch") | Layer-3 shift named in own words |
| 16 (Wk 10) | **Week-10 formative quiz** | Adult scores; misconceptions logged |
| 17 | **Practice F** — essay revision studio + quiz-miss review | Quotations handled; "so what" answered |
| 18 | Casebook: Steps 4–5 (brief + talk); submit | Casebook complete per deliverables list |
| 19 | **Unit assessment, Part A** (short items) | All six items attempted with evidence |
| 20 | **Unit assessment, Part B** (comparative essay) + unit reflection | Essay meets rubric; learner names one growth |

Independent reading log runs all four weeks (daily 25–35 min,
adult-checked weekly): the unit texts plus the Casebook's second text.
