# The Keeper at the Crossroads

*An original passage written for this unit — the text half of the
Lesson 06 two-medium comparison. Read it first, then study the
illustration in `../assets/crossroads-keeper-illustration.png`.*

Every night at dusk, the keeper climbs to the stone shrine where the two
roads cross, and lights the lanterns.

There are four lanterns on the shrine's ledge. She lights three. The
fourth she leaves dark — for the traveler who is not coming, she says,
though no one has asked her in years.

The taper hisses when it catches. The oil smells of sesame and smoke.
The first lantern takes the flame the way a sleeper takes waking —
slowly, then all at once — and the amber light finds the keeper's hands,
knotted as roots, and the worn places on the stone where a thousand
hands have steadied themselves. The second lantern. The third. Each one
a small sun, each one burning for no one.

She remembers when the roads were busy. Mule trains at noon, pilgrims at
dusk, the shrine's ledge crowded with offerings — ribbons, coins, a
child's wooden horse. Now the new highway runs ten miles east, and the
crossroads belongs to the mist and the bare branches and the keeper, and
the mist does not need light, and the branches do not thank her.

Still she comes. The taper. The hiss. The smell of oil. Three small
suns in the blue dark.

A traveler who passed at dusk last spring — the first in three years —
asked her why she lights lanterns no one sees. The keeper looked at the
empty roads, one running north into the mist, one running south, and
said: "The light is not for them."

She did not finish the sentence. But the taper hissed, and the third
lantern caught, and the empty roads lay amber and blue in the dusk, and
the traveler understood, or thought he did, and walked on:

*The light is for the road itself — that it remembers it was once
chosen.*

The fourth lantern stays dark. For the traveler who is not coming.

She will light it anyway, she thinks, on the night he finally comes.
