# Week-10 Formative Quiz

**Unit 02 — Comparative theme structure and perspective** · 10 items ·
Adult scores; misconceptions logged for the review session.
**Answer key:** [answer-key § Q](../answer-key.md#week-10-quiz)

Read the two passages first. Both are original to this unit.

## Passage 1 — "The Gate"

The law was older than the gatekeeper, and she had never once broken it:
the city gate stays shut from dusk until dawn, for everyone, without
exception.

So when the wounded soldier came at midnight — an enemy soldier, by the
colors on his ruined coat — Marta did not open the gate. She stood on the
wall and watched him sink down against the wood, and she did what the law
did not forbid: she lowered bread and bandages in a basket on a rope,
and a skin of water, and then she kept watch until dawn so no one would
accuse her of opening for him in the dark.

At sunrise the soldier was gone. The bread was gone. Leaning against the
gate, unbroken, stood a single enemy arrow — laid down, not fired.

Marta picked it up, carried it inside, and hung it above her door. She
never told anyone what it meant. She was not sure herself.

## Passage 2 — "The Door"

*From a letter, written by a lighthouse keeper's daughter to her brother:*

You will scold me when you read this, so let me say first that Mother
already has, thoroughly, and I survived it.

The storm took the *Grey Gull* on the rocks Tuesday night — a raider
sloop, Tomas, one of *theirs*. He came up the path half-drowned, and I
did what you would have called stupid: I left the cottage door unlatched
and went back to bed, so that if he tried it, he would find it open, and
if he didn't, no one would ever know I had offered.

He tried it. I heard the latch in the night and pretended to sleep. In
the morning he was gone — and the broken shutter, the one Father meant
to mend for two years, was mended. Properly. With wedges.

Mother says I risked all our lives for a raider. Perhaps. But the door
was mine to leave open, and the shutter is mended, and I find I do not
regret it, which troubles me more than the scolding did.

— L.

## Items

1. A learner writes: *"The Gate is better than The Door because Marta's
   choice is harder."* Name the fairness rule this breaks (Lesson 01),
   and rewrite the comparison's *question* so it would be fair. (2 pts)
2. Write a shared question for comparing the two passages on the topic
   of *what is owed to an enemy*. (1 pt)
3. Complete the thesis — fill the missing "so what" clause: *"While
   'The Gate' shows mercy given at a distance — bread lowered on a rope,
   the gate never opened — 'The Door' shows mercy given up close, the
   latch lifted in the night; the difference reveals __________."* (2 pts)
4. A learner's v1 theme for "The Gate": *"The story claims we should help
   everyone."* Using the arrow detail (laid down, unbroken, hung above
   her door), write the v2 revision the detail forces. (2 pts)
5. "The Gate" is told chronologically; "The Door" is a retrospective
   letter. Name one *effect* of each choice, and what is compared by
   holding them side by side. (2 pts)
6. What does the letter form let "The Door" do that "The Gate"'s
   third-person narration cannot? Name one specific thing, with a detail.
   (1 pt)
7. Suppose a later author retells "The Gate" from the *soldier's* point
   of view, years later, as a confession. Classify each as kept, changed,
   or transformed: (a) the bread lowered on a rope; (b) the letter/form
   of the telling; (c) the theme — from "mercy at a distance" to "what a
   spared life owes." (3 pts)
8. In Lesson 06 terms: the crossroads *passage* can state why the fourth
   lantern stays dark; the *illustration* cannot. Name one thing the
   illustration emphasizes that the passage never mentions. (1 pt)
9. Write one *named difference* between "The Gate" and "The Door" that a
   lazy comparison ("both are about helping enemies") would hide. One
   precise sentence. (2 pts)
10. Error analysis: *"Both passages show that kindness to enemies is
    good, which is an important theme."* Explain in two sentences why this
    is a topic, not a thesis — and what single addition would make it
    arguable. (2 pts)

**Total: 20 points.** 18–20 = exceeding · 15–17 = meeting · 12–14 =
approaching (review misses with Practice F) · below 12 = re-teach with
the adult before the unit assessment.
