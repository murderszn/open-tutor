# Unit 02 Assessment

**Comparative theme structure and perspective** · Two parts ·
**Answer key:** [answer-key § UA](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment)

## Part A — Short items (18 points)

Read the two passages first. Both are original to this unit.

### Passage 1 — "The Oath"

The captain was already dying when Joss reached him — that was the first
thing, and everything else followed from it.

"Swear it," the captain said, blood dark on his teeth. "The ridge
message. It goes to command tonight, whatever happens to me."

Joss swore. He had never sworn an oath before; the words came out of him
in the wrong order, and the captain smiled at that, which was worse than
the blood.

Joss ran. He ran through the burning treeline with the message case
hammering his ribs, and he did not stop when the arrows started, and he
did not stop when they stopped — because a dead man's voice was running
with him, saying the words back in the right order.

He delivered the message at midnight. Command read it, nodded, and filed
it with a hundred others. No one asked what it had cost. Joss walked out
past the torches, sat down with his back against the wall, and said the
oath once more — in the right order this time — to no one.

### Passage 2 — "The Promise"

*From a nurse's diary:*

March 14. — Mrs. Alder asked me today to promise her something, and I
said yes before she had finished asking, the way you do. She wants the
garden planted — the one behind the east ward that went to weeds when
her husband died. "Promise me it won't be weeds when the roses come,"
she said. I promised.

April 2. — I planted the first row today on my day off. My back aches.
Mrs. Alder watched from her window and clapped when I finished, which
made the other patients laugh. It is a ridiculous garden. I am planting
it anyway.

May 30. — Mrs. Alder died Tuesday. The roses are not up yet. I went
today anyway and weeded the rows and watered them, and I will keep going
until they bloom, and then I will sit there exactly once, and think of
her, and be done. A promise is a promise, even when the person you made
it to is gone. Especially then.

### Items (3 points each)

1. Write a **shared question** for comparing the two passages on the
   topic of *promises to the dying*. Then name the **comparable
   evidence** (same kind, both sides) you would use to answer it.
2. A learner's v1 theme for "The Oath": *"The story claims promises
   matter."* Use the detail of command filing the message "with a
   hundred others" to force a **v2 revision**. Write v2.
3. "The Oath" opens *in medias res* ("already dying"); "The Promise" is
   told in dated diary entries. Name the **effect** of each structural
   choice, and write the comparative claim the two effects support.
4. What does the diary form let "The Promise" show that "The Oath"'s
   third-person narration cannot? Answer with one specific detail and
   what it reveals.
5. Complete the thesis with an arguable "so what" clause: *"Although
   'The Oath' treats a promise as a burden carried at speed — sworn in
   the wrong order, kept at a run — 'The Promise' treats a promise as
   labor returned to slowly, in dated entries across three months; the
   contrast reveals __________."*
6. Error analysis: a learner writes, *"The diary is more emotional than
   the oath story, so it is the better passage."* Name the broken
   fairness rule and explain in two sentences why "more emotional" is
   not a comparable measure here.

## Part B — Comparative essay (12 points)

**Length:** 900–1,200 words · **Draft due:** Session 17 · **Final due:**
Session 19

Choose **one** pair and answer its question:

- **A. The paired stories:** "The Salt Road" and "The Harbor Light" —
  *What does each story claim we owe a stranger, and how do the
  authors' structural choices build those claims?*
- **B. Source and transformation:** Aesop's "The Tortoise and the Hare"
  and "The Rematch" — *What does the retelling keep, change, and
  transform — and what does the transformation reveal about who owns a
  story?*
- **C. Two mediums:** "The Keeper at the Crossroads" (passage) and its
  illustration — *What does each medium emphasize and leave out, and
  what does the trade reveal about the scene's meaning?*

**Requirements:** a three-part arguable thesis (shared question, named
difference/likeness, "so what"); at least four handled quotations (two
per text/medium, introduced and explained — see Practice F); one
paragraph of structural or medium analysis tied to an effect; a
counter-thesis sentence acknowledged and answered; a conclusion that
*answers* the "so what" rather than repeating it.

### Essay rubric (12 points)

| Points | Thesis & argument | Evidence | Analysis | Conventions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Arguable three-part thesis; "so what" answered in conclusion; counter-thesis addressed | 4+ quotations, all handled (introduced, cited, explained); evidence comparable across texts | Structural/medium choices tied to effects; transformation reaches layer 3 where relevant | Clear organization (point-by-point or block, chosen deliberately); varied sentences; few errors |
| 2 | Thesis arguable but "so what" thin; counter-thesis missing | 3–4 quotations, mostly handled | Choices named; effects vague or uncompared | Organization present; some errors |
| 1 | Thesis = topic or announcement | Fewer than 3 quotations or dropped quotations | Description without effects | Disorganized or error-dense |
| 0 | No thesis | No textual evidence | No analysis | — |

**Total: 30 points.** 27–30 = exceeding · 23–26 = meeting · 18–22 =
approaching (revise one rubric row with the adult) · below 18 = re-teach
and rewrite.
