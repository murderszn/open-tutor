# Formative Quiz — Week 06: Cultural Context and Interpretation

*Covers RL.9-10.1 (strong textual evidence), RL.9-10.2 (theme development), RL.9-10.6 (point of view / cultural experience). 8–10 items mixing evidence selection, theme judgment, and POV analysis. The adult scores with the draft answer key; items 9–10 are short written responses.*

**1. (RL.9-10.1)** Which quotation is the STRONGEST textual evidence that Hal's world treats the ferry fare as fixed and public?
- a. "The river was low that season, and the current ran thin and gray over the stones."
- b. "the toll schedule written in the ledger at the ferry house said *two coppers*"
- c. "He handed her his own oilcloth cloak without a word, and she took it, surprised."
- d. "the boat was gone into the mist like it had never been there."

**2. (RL.9-10.1)** A learner claims: "The traveler comes from a wealthier background than Hal." Which best evaluates this claim?
- a. Strong — her city boots and journal prove she is wealthy.
- b. Strong — Hal's worn rope and firewood show he is poor.
- c. Weak — the text gives clues (city boots, a journal) but never states wealth; this is the reader's assumption, not textual evidence.
- d. Weak — the story is fictional, so no claims about background are allowed.

**3. (RL.9-10.6)** The traveler writes that people in Hal's world "pay in stories instead of coin." What does this line mainly reveal?
- a. That Hal's economy literally runs on storytelling.
- b. That the traveler is interpreting Hal's professional courtesy through her own cultural frame, mistaking routine for personal generosity.
- c. That the traveler has learned Hal's world accurately.
- d. That the narrator is lying to the reader.

**4. (RL.9-10.6)** Which detail best shows the LIMIT of Hal's point of view — something his frame keeps him from seeing?
- a. "He had never once taken three for a crossing, rain or shine."
- b. "She was the first who seemed to think it was a gift from him personally."
- c. "He entered instead, in the ledger's margin... *one passage, no fare.*"
- d. "He blew the ink dry and went inside to his supper."

**5. (RL.9-10.2)** Which is a THEME statement (not a topic) about "The Night Market"?
- a. Honesty and prices.
- b. The story suggests that a public price means nothing unless the private record behind it tells the same truth — and that closing the gap costs something.
- c. Milo is the apprentice and Ren is the spice seller.
- d. The market is described as honey-colored and busy at dusk.

**6. (RL.9-10.2)** In "The Night Market," which detail most COMPLICATES the simple theme "honesty is always rewarded"?
- a. The chalkboard prices written in neat handwriting.
- b. Ren's line "Damp pepper is still pepper, and the board price is the board price."
- c. Milo running through the market with the sound pepper.
- d. The child laughing by the well.

**7. (RL.9-10.4/RL.9-10.1)** Compare: Townsend's ants speak "in derision"; Jones's ants "chuckled and went on with their work." What is the best analysis of this difference?
- a. Townsend's ants are colder and more condemnatory; "derision" is contempt, while "chuckled" is amusement — the translator's word choice changes the fable's moral temperature.
- b. The two translators disagree about the plot.
- c. Jones's version is more accurate because it is newer.
- d. The difference doesn't matter since the moral is the same.

**8. (RL.9-10.1)** Which statement about the two Aesop translations is TEXTUAL EVIDENCE (not reader assumption)?
- a. Townsend's translation is more accurate because it is older.
- b. Jones's grasshopper is female ("her," "she said") and Townsend's is male ("him," "He replied").
- c. Victorian readers preferred formal language.
- d. Aesop intended the ants to represent the middle class.

**9. (RL.9-10.6, written)** In two to three sentences, explain how the traveler's and Hal's *cultural experiences* produce different meanings from the same coin-refusal. Quote one phrase from each half of the story.

**10. (RL.9-10.2, written)** State a theme of "The Night Market" as a full sentence with a verb. Then name the one detail that most *refined* (changed) your theme as you tracked it, and explain the refinement in two sentences.
