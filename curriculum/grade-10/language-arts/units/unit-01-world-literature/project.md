# Project — Cultural Context Case File

*Adult-supervised. The learner selects one adult-approved public-domain world text, investigates its context, and builds a case file. Suggested sources (all public domain, lawful): a Grimm tale (e.g., Hunt 1884 translation), an Aesop fable (Townsend 1867 or Jones 1912), a tale from Andrew Lang's fairy books (1889+), or a fable from the *Panchatantra* in a pre-1929 English translation. The adult confirms the text is public domain before work begins. Timeline: begin in Week 4, submit end of Week 6.*

## Task

You are building a **case file** on one short public-domain world text — the way a researcher builds a file on a case: context first, evidence organized, conclusions argued.

**Step 1 — Choose and verify (Week 4).** With the adult, select one short text (a fable, tale, or episode — under ~1,500 words) from an adult-approved public-domain source. Record: title, author/tradition, translator (if any), edition, and publication date. *Why this step matters:* Lesson 03 taught that *whose English* you're reading changes the reading. Your file starts by naming it.

**Step 2 — Context map (Week 4–5).** Build the four-layer map from Lesson 01 (historical moment / social norms / belief systems / genre traditions) for your text's world. Each layer needs at least two details with quoted words — and one labeled *reader's assumption* you caught yourself making and corrected. Add a one-paragraph "context caution": what background *informs* your reading, what it must not *decide*, and one stereotype a careless reader might project that you are refusing.

**Step 3 — Edition/translator note (Week 5).** Write a 150–200 word note in the style of the Aesop file's source notes: who translated it, when, for whom — and *two* word-choice observations showing the translator's hand (compare with a second translation if you can find one; if not, compare two renderings of one key sentence — one formal, one plain — that *you* write, labeled as your own). End with one sentence: how does knowing the translator change your reading?

**Step 4 — Theme track (Week 5–6).** Track one theme through the text with the Lesson 04 tracker (beginning / middle / end, quotations, version history showing at least one revision). Final theme stated as a full sentence with a verb.

**Step 5 — Interpretive paragraph (Week 6).** One paragraph (8–12 sentences): an arguable claim about a cultural point of view or experience in the text + how your tracked theme develops + two introduced, cited, explained quotations + one sentence acknowledging a complicating detail.

## Deliverables

1. Case file cover page: text title, author/tradition, translator, edition/date, word count.
2. Four-layer context map with quotations + one corrected assumption + context-caution paragraph.
3. Edition/translator note (150–200 words) with two word-choice observations.
4. Completed theme tracker with version history.
5. Interpretive paragraph (8–12 sentences) meeting the evidence standard.
6. Sources list: every edition consulted, with publication dates.

## Rubric — observable criteria

| Criterion | 4 — Exemplary | 3 — Proficient | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Context map | All 4 layers, 2+ quoted details each; corrected assumption labeled with the words that exposed it | All 4 layers with quotations; assumption labeled | 2–3 layers, or details without quotations | 1 layer or no textual grounding |
| Context caution | Names what informs vs. decides; names and refuses a specific stereotype | Distinguishes inform vs. decide | Generic caution, no specific stereotype addressed | Missing |
| Translator note | Translator/date/audience named; 2 word-choice observations tied to quoted words; reading-change sentence specific | All parts present; observations thinner | Translator unnamed or no word-choice analysis | Missing |
| Theme tracker | 3+ sections, quotations, visible revision (v1→v2+), final theme is a sentence with a verb | Tracker complete; revision present but shallow | No revision shown or theme is a topic word | Tracker missing |
| Interpretive paragraph | Arguable claim; 2 quotations introduced/cited/explained/connected; complication acknowledged | Claim + 2 quotations; one handling step weak | Claim vague or 1 quotation; no complication | Summary, not interpretation |
| Sources & presentation | Every edition dated; file organized, readable | Editions listed, one date missing | Sources vague | No sources list |

**Scoring:** 24–21 = strong case file; 20–16 = proficient with revision notes; 15–11 = revise and resubmit one weakest criterion; below 11 = rebuild with adult support, starting from Step 2.
