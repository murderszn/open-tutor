# Unit 01 — World Literature: Cultural Context and Interpretation

**Grade:** 10 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #48](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/48), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A reader meeting a story from another world faces one core question: *what
does this text say, and what am I adding from my own world?* This unit
builds four moves across four weeks — mapping a story's cultural context
from its own words, analyzing how point of view and cultural experience
shape meaning, reading translation as interpretation, and tracking a theme
as it develops — all on original short stories and verified public-domain
translations, ending in an interpretive essay and a Cultural Context Case
File project.

## Essential question

How do readers separate what a story from another world *says* from what
their own world *adds* — and use both honestly?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the grade-9 track:

- Cite strong and thorough textual evidence for explicit and inferential
  claims (RL.9-10.1, RI.9-10.1); determine a theme and write an objective
  summary (RL.9-10.2); analyze word choice for tone (RL.9-10.4)
- Write complete, varied sentences; quote and paraphrase with attribution

The unit re-teaches the evidence/assumption distinction from the ground
up — no prior mastery is assumed. What is *new* in grade 10: **cultural
context as an analytical tool** (not background trivia), **point of view
read through cultural experience** (RL.9-10.6), and **translation
comparison** as evidence work.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Cite **strong and thorough** textual evidence for analysis of literary
   and informational texts, and sort textual evidence from the reader's
   own cultural assumptions (RL.9-10.1, RI.9-10.1).
2. Determine a theme, analyze **in detail** how it emerges and is shaped
   and refined by specific details across a full selection, and write an
   objective summary (RL.9-10.2, RI.9-10.2).
3. Analyze how a narrator's or character's **point of view and cultural
   experience** shape what a story notices, misunderstands, and leaves out
   (RL.9-10.6).
4. Analyze **word choice** — comparing two translations of one text — for
   cumulative impact on tone and meaning (RL.9-10.4).
5. Draw evidence from literary and informational texts to support an
   interpretive essay with an arguable claim, handled quotations, and an
   answered complication (W.9-10.9).

## Materials

Notebook or paper, pen/pencil, index cards for exit checks, a folder for
the Case File project, the six lesson pages. No purchases; no hazardous
materials. All reading is on the page — no outside book required.

## 20-session pacing (4 weeks × 5 sessions, ~50 minutes each)

The six lessons are the direct-instruction core (each spans two
sessions: teach + guided work, then independent work). The remaining
sessions are practice, independent reading, discussion, project work, and
assessment. Each session names its activity and success criterion — see
the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) for the full map.

| Session | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (Wk 3) | Lesson 01 teach: cultural context, context map, evidence/assumption sort | Learner defines the four context layers in own words |
| 2 | Lesson 01 independent practice + illustration observation sort | Context map has quoted details; sort is labeled |
| 3 | Lesson 02 teach: POV and cultural experience (ferryman vs. traveler) | Two in-character retellings distinguish frames |
| 4 | **Practice A** — context-map drill on "The Night Market" | Four layers mapped with quotations; tricky case explained |
| 5 | Independent reading: "How Translators Work" + edition notes; discussion | Learner explains why an edition note matters |
| 6 (Wk 4) | Lesson 03 teach: translation comparison (Aesop × 2) | Word-choice differences tied to tone effects |
| 7 | **Practice B** — POV rewrite (Ren / merchant) + error analysis | Retellings in character; "biased/unbiased" error diagnosed |
| 8 | Lesson 04 teach: theme tracking on "The Night Market" | Tracker started with v1 and one revision |
| 9 | Case File project: Step 1 (choose + verify text) with the adult | Text chosen; edition data recorded |
| 10 | **Practice C** — translation comparison lab | Both final lines quoted/labeled; one new difference found |
| 11 (Wk 5) | Lesson 05 teach: objective summary vs. retelling | Summary draft covers arc with zero judgment words |
| 12 | **Practice D** — theme-tracker workshop ("The Ferryman's Toll") | Tracker with 4+ rows, quotations, visible revision |
| 13 | Case File project: Steps 2–3 (context map + translator note) | Map drafted; translator note drafted |
| 14 | **Practice E** — summary clinic | Summary passes all four checklist items after revision |
| 15 | Lesson 06 teach: interpretive essay frame + planning | Claim sentence arguable; 3 quotations collected |
| 16 (Wk 6) | **Week-06 formative quiz** | Adult scores; misconceptions logged |
| 17 | **Practice F** — essay revision studio + review of quiz misses | Quotations handled; complication answered |
| 18 | Case File project: Steps 4–5 (theme track + interpretive paragraph); submit | Case file complete per deliverables list |
| 19 | **Unit assessment, Part A** (short items on "The Well at the Crossroads") | All six items attempted with quotations |
| 20 | **Unit assessment, Part B** (interpretive essay) + unit reflection | Essay meets rubric; learner names one growth |

Independent reading log runs all four weeks (daily 25–35 min, adult-checked
weekly): the learner reads the unit texts plus one adult-approved
public-domain world text for the Case File.
