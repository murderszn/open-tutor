# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: World Literature: Cultural Context and Interpretation

*Adult-side guidance for Grade 10 Language Arts, Unit 01 (track weeks
3–6). Learner-facing lessons never contain answers; keep this guide and
the [answer key](answer-key.md) out of the learner's hands.*

## Unit at a glance

Twenty 50-minute sessions. Six lessons (each taught across two
sessions: direct instruction + guided work, then independent work), six
practice sessions, a formative quiz, an essay revision studio, a project,
and a two-part culminating assessment. The adult reviews written
practice the same day and scores the quiz and assessment with the answer
key.

**Core moves the unit builds:** (1) map a story's cultural context from
its own words; (2) sort textual evidence from the reader's cultural
assumption; (3) analyze point of view through cultural experience
(RL.9-10.6); (4) compare translations as interpretation (RL.9-10.4);
(5) track a theme through revision (RL.9-10.2); (6) summarize
objectively; (7) argue an interpretation with thorough evidence
(W.9-10.9).

## Differentiation

- **Reading access:** every lesson allows the adult to read texts aloud;
  the analytical work (sorting, mapping, comparing) is the assessed
  skill, not decoding.
- **Alternate response modes:** sorts as card piles, maps as labeled
  diagrams, comparisons as two-column charts, essays dictated and
  scribed — all named in each lesson's Supports section.
- **Extensions:** each lesson has one; the strongest are the Lesson 01
  traveler-context map, the Lesson 03 era-research piece, and the
  Lesson 04 cross-story theme test.
- **Pacing:** if sessions run short, move independent practice to the
  reading-log slot the same week; never skip the exit check.

## Misconception watch (unit-wide)

1. **Evidence vs. assumption:** learners sort by confidence ("I'm sure
   it's true") instead of by source ("the text states it"). Keep the
   physical test: *point at the words.*
2. **One right view:** learners crown Hal's view "correct." The story
   says the traveler's *feeling* was real; only her *explanation* was
   wrong.
3. **Older = truer translation:** Townsend (1867) is not closer to
   Aesop than Jones (1912); he is closer to 1867.
4. **Topic as theme:** "honesty" is not a theme. Every theme must be a
   sentence with a verb that a detail could contradict.
5. **Summary as review:** judgment words ("brave," "shady," "greedy")
   smuggled into summaries. Score objectivity separately from
   completeness.
6. **Moral judgment instead of interpretation:** essays about whether
   Ren was wrong, instead of about what the story develops. Redirect:
   judge the text's meaning, not the characters' morals.

## Answer key use

The [answer key](answer-key.md) solves every practice item, quiz
question, and assessment task with reasoning, acceptable alternatives,
and misconception notes. Lessons link to it with `answer-key § Ln`
anchors. Solve items yourself first when the learner's answer is
unusual — the key lists common alternatives but not every defensible
one.

## Readiness and reteaching signals

- **Ready for U02** (comparative theme across cultures): the learner's
  assessment essay has an arguable claim, handled quotations, and a
  tracked theme.
- **Reteach in use:** if the quiz shows evidence/assumption confusion,
  open U02's first comparative discussion with a fresh sort on the new
  texts.
- **All year:** the four-layer context map and the evidence/assumption
  sort are reusable tools — bring them back in U02, U07, and U08.
