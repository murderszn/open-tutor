# Practice Sessions — Unit 04: Syntax, Tone, Vocabulary, Conventions

Each practice set is one session (or part of one). Work in order;
the adult scores from the [answer key](answer-key.md). Keep the
word bank (Practice D) running through Session 20.

## Practice A — Clause labels and repairs (Sessions 2–3)

1. Label every clause in: *When the meeting ended, the chair
   announced what the committee had decided.*
2. Label every clause in: *The book that won the prize was written
   by an author who had never left her village.*
3. Name the sentence type: (a) *The rain stopped, and the road
   dried quickly.* (b) *Because the bridge was closed, the buses
   waited.* (c) *The buses waited, and the drivers talked while the
   rain fell.*
4. Repair each non-sentence; write OK for each correct sentence:
   (a) *Before the library opened.* (b) *The dog barked all night
   the neighbors complained.* (c) *She finished the report and
   sent it.* (d) *The travelers, tired and hungry, reached the inn.*
5. Combine these sentences into one compound-complex sentence and
   label its clauses: *The tide turned. The keeper lit the lamps.
   The boats came home.*
6. Error analysis: a learner labeled *carrying a lantern* a clause
   in the anchor sentence. Explain the error in two sentences.

## Practice B — Phrase expansion and combining (Session 7)

1. Expand *The train arrived* with one participial phrase, one
   prepositional phrase, and one absolute phrase. Label all three.
2. Combine into one sentence using at least two phrase types:
   *The musician practiced daily. Her fingers ached. The recital
   was near.*
3. Name the phrase type of each named group: (a) *in the quiet
   archive* in "She worked in the quiet archive." (b) *its pages
   yellowed* in "The book, its pages yellowed, lay on the desk."
   (c) *having finished the letter* in "Having finished the letter,
   she sealed it."
4. Fix the dangling modifier: *Crossing the bridge, the river
   sparkled below.*
5. Rewrite this paragraph using at least three phrase types (label
   each): *The festival began. Musicians played. Children danced.
   The square was crowded.*
6. Error analysis: a learner wrote *Running down the street, the
   bus left without him* and called it correct. Explain the error
   and fix the sentence.

## Practice C — Semicolons and colons (Session 9)

1. Punctuate with a semicolon (add a conjunctive adverb where it
   fits): (a) *The first draft was messy the idea was strong.*
   (b) *The map was old the route was still clear.* (c) *The
   committee disagreed the report was published anyway.*
2. Punctuate with a colon or write NO COLON and explain why:
   (a) *The toolbox held a hammer a saw and a level.* (b) *The
   toolbox held the tools were old.* (c) *Her advice was simple
   "Begin."*
3. Correct or write OK: (a) *The storm passed; the harbor lay
   still.* (b) *The storm passed, the harbor lay still.* (c) *The
   storm passed; and the harbor lay still.* (d) *The cities were
   Harrow, in the north; Selby, upriver; and Dunmere, by the
   marsh.*
4. Rewrite using one semicolon and one colon, each justified in a
   margin note: *The class read poems essays and plays. The poems
   were hardest. The teacher gave extra time.*
5. Error analysis: a learner wrote *The keeper lit the lamps;
   because the tide had turned.* Explain the error and fix it two
   ways.

## Practice D — Word bank (Sessions 12, 15)

Complete bank entries for all 12 words from Lesson 04 (*candid,
diligent, ambiguous, coherent, reluctant, substantiate, meticulous,
sustain, precise, interpret, evident, compose*). Each entry needs:
the sentence where you met the word, your context guess and clue
type, a word-change pattern or reference note, the verified meaning,
and a new sentence of your own. Then:

1. Give the word-change family for *interpret* (verb): noun and
   adjective forms, each in a sentence.
2. Give the word-change family for *precise* (adjective): noun and
   adverb forms, each in a sentence.
3. A learner guessed *diligent* means "slow" because the keeper
   worked "through the whole winter." Correct the guess using the
   Lesson 04 method (clue + reference + substitution).
4. Write three sentences, each using a different bank word, so that
   a reader could guess each word's meaning from context alone.

## Practice E — Tone pairs (Session 14)

1. For each pair, name the tone of each version with two quoted
   words as evidence: (a) *The room was quiet.* / *The room held
   its breath.* (b) *The manager said jobs would be cut.* / *The
   manager said the company would "streamline its workforce."*
2. Write a euphemism sentence and its plain-fact sentence for a
   school closing early. Name what the euphemism gains.
3. Write an oxymoron sentence about a farewell that expresses a
   real tension. Explain the tension.
4. Describe the harbor illustration in two sentences with a
   *lonely* tone. Use at least one relative clause and one word
   with a negative connotation.
5. A learner claims Version A of Lesson 05 (*The boat came in. The
   water was calm. The lights were on.*) has "no tone." Explain in
   two sentences why that claim is wrong.

## Practice F — Editing studio (Session 17)

1. Edit this paragraph with the three passes. List each change with
   its pass and reason: *The meeting started late, everyone was
   already tired. The chair, she opened the notes. It was a good
   discussion though. The group agreed on three things, they wrote
   them down, the notes they were clear.*
2. Rewrite this message for a formal letter and for a note to a
   friend, without changing the facts: *We have to cancel Friday's
   practice because the hall is closed.*
3. A draft uses semicolons before every *however*, colons after
   fragments, and *very nice* five times. Name one fix from each
   pass (structure, language, conventions) for this draft.
