# Unit Assessment — Unit 04: Syntax, Tone, Vocabulary, Conventions

**Sessions 19–20 · Answer-key reference:**
[answer-key § Assessment](../answer-key.md#assessment)
**Scoring:** Part A, 10 items × 2 points = 20. Part B, rubric below
(20 points). Readiness for U05: Part A ≥ 16 and Part B ≥ 15, with
no remaining comma splices in the learner's own writing.

## Part A — Short items (Session 19)

1. Label every clause in: *What the travelers found surprised them,
   because the inn they trusted had closed for the winter.* Name
   the sentence type.
2. Write one compound-complex sentence about a library at night.
   Label its clauses.
3. Expand *The letter arrived* with one participial phrase and one
   absolute phrase. Label both.
4. Fix this dangling modifier two ways: *Reading the map, the road
   seemed longer than expected.*
5. Punctuate: *The first essay was short the second was thorough
   however the class preferred the first.* (Use a semicolon at
   least once; justify each mark in a margin note.)
6. Punctuate with a colon or write NO COLON and explain why:
   *The exhibit showed three things maps letters and photographs.*
7. Correct or write OK, with the rule for each: (a) *The team
   practiced daily; the recital was near.* (b) *The team practiced
   daily, the recital was near.* (c) *The team practiced daily;
   because the recital was near.*
8. Give the verified meaning of *ambiguous* in: *The treaty was
   ambiguous: both sides claimed it favored them.* Name the clue
   type and the verification step you used.
9. A thesaurus lists *tired, weary, exhausted* for *diligent*.
   Explain in two sentences why none of these is a safe substitute
   for *diligent*, using the Lesson 04 method.
10. Name the figure and its role in: *After the defeat, the general
    spoke of a "strategic withdrawal."* Then name the tone of this
    sentence with one quoted word as evidence: *The general,
    flanked by aides, announced the withdrawal in a steady voice.*

## Part B — Editing and tone-pair task (Session 20)

Edit the flawed passage below with the three passes (mark each
change S, L, or C), then write a clean revised version. After it,
write the same scene in the opposite tone (if your revision reads
calm and certain, write an uneasy version; if it reads uneasy,
write a calm one) in 80–120 words, changing only word choice and
syntax — not the facts. Finish with three sentences explaining
which choices created the tone difference, quoting your own words.

Flawed passage: *The harbor woke early, fishermen loaded crates
all morning. The captain, he checked the ropes twice. It was a
fine day though. The boats left one by one, the quay they grew
quiet.*

**Part B rubric (20 points):**

- Clause/phrase correctness and variety (5): no fragments,
  run-ons, or dangling modifiers remain; at least two phrase types
  used deliberately.
- Punctuation by rule (5): every semicolon/colon correct; no
  comma splices remain.
- Vocabulary precision (4): vague words (*fine* and any others
  introduced) replaced with precise, evidence-fitting words.
- Tone pair (4): the two versions' tones are distinguishable from
  word choice/syntax alone; facts unchanged.
- Tone explanation (2): three sentences quoting the learner's own
  choices that created the difference.
