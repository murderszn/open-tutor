# Project — The Editor's Portfolio

**Due:** Session 19 · **Working time:** Sessions 4, 14, 17, 19
(plus drafting between sessions) · **Answer-key reference:**
[answer-key § Project](answer-key.md#project)

## The task

You are your own editor. Assemble a portfolio that proves you can
use this unit's tools on real writing — your own.

## Deliverables

1. **A revised piece (Step 1, Session 4; Step 3, Session 19).**
   Choose one piece of your own U01–U03 writing (at least 300 words).
   Submit the original with your three-pass edits marked in the
   margin (S = structure, L = language, C = conventions) plus a
   clean revised version. Every mark must name the rule it applies
   (for example: "C — semicolon needs two independent clauses" or
   "L — *nice* replaced with *precise* because the evidence is a
   measurement").
2. **A two-tone passage (Step 2, Session 14).** Write one short
   passage (150–250 words) describing the same scene or event twice:
   once in a calm, certain tone and once in an uneasy tone. Change
   only word choice and syntax — not the facts. Label at least one
   phrase type, one clause type, and one figure of speech or
   connotative choice in each version.
3. **An editor's rationale (Step 3, Session 19).** A half-page note
   (about 200–300 words) naming the three most important changes in
   Deliverable 1 and, for each, the audience/purpose reason it serves.
   Close by naming one habit from this unit you will carry into U05
   and one weakness you still need to practice.

## Steps

- **Step 1 (Session 4):** Choose the piece. Write baseline edit
  notes: three issues you already notice, each labeled S, L, or C.
- **Step 2 (Session 14):** Draft the two-tone passage. Trade
  versions with the adult: can the adult name each tone from the
  words alone, without being told?
- **Step 3 (Session 19):** Finish the marked revision and clean
  copy, write the rationale, and assemble the portfolio in order:
  original (marked), clean revision, two-tone passage, rationale.

## Rubric

Each criterion is scored 4 (Exemplary), 3 (Proficient), 2
(Developing), or 1 (Beginning). Proficient (3) in every criterion
meets the unit standard; the adult records the score per criterion,
not a single total, so the learner sees what to practice next.

| Criterion | 4 — Exemplary | 3 — Proficient | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clause and phrase control (Objectives 1–2) | Every sentence is correct; varied clause and phrase types are used deliberately and labeled accurately | Sentences are correct with varied clauses/phrases; at most one labeling slip | Several sentences correct but variety is thin, or 2–3 labeling errors | Frequent fragments, run-ons, or dangling modifiers; labels mostly wrong |
| Punctuation by rule (Objective 3) | Every semicolon and colon is correct and justified; no comma splices remain | Semicolons/colons correct with at most one slip; no comma splices | Some correct uses but 2–3 rule errors or a remaining splice | Semicolons/colons used as decoration; splices remain |
| Vocabulary precision (Objective 4) | Word choices are precise and verified; bank words used correctly in new contexts | Word choices precise; bank words used correctly | Some vague words remain; a bank word misused | Vague or incorrect words throughout; no evidence of verification |
| Tone and style (Objective 5) | Two tones are unmistakable from word choice/syntax alone; rationale ties each major change to audience/purpose | Tones distinguishable; rationale explains major changes | Tones blur, or rationale lists changes without reasons | Tones indistinguishable, or rationale missing |
| Revision completeness | All three passes visible in marks; clean copy matches marks | Three passes visible; clean copy mostly matches | One pass missing, or clean copy diverges from marks | Marks absent or decorative; no clean copy |

## Accessibility and alternatives

- The revised piece may be dictated and scribed; marks may be spoken
  and recorded by the adult.
- The two-tone passage may be presented orally (read aloud in each
  tone) with the labels spoken.
- If no U01–U03 piece survives, the learner may revise a new
  300-word passage written in Session 4 instead; note the
  substitution in the rationale.
