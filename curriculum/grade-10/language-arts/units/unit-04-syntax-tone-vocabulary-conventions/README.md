# Unit 04 — Language, Syntax, Tone, Vocabulary, and Conventions

**Grade:** 10 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #48](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/48), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

U01 taught reading *within* a text, U02 taught reading *across* texts,
and U03 taught judging nonfiction arguments. This unit turns to the
writer's own tools: the sentence. Across four weeks the learner builds
five moves: analyzing and building clauses; varying phrases for meaning
and interest; punctuating with semicolons and colons; running a
vocabulary system (context, word-change patterns, reference materials,
verification); and choosing words and syntax for tone and audience —
then applying all five in a full editing pass over an earlier piece of
the learner's own writing. The unit's tools are then enforced in every
later unit's editing passes. It ends in an Editor's Portfolio project.

## Essential question

How do the smallest choices a writer makes — a clause, a phrase, a
semicolon, a single word — change what a reader understands and feels?

## Prerequisites

From U01–U03 and the diagnostic weeks:

- Cite strong and thorough textual evidence; write an objective
  summary (RL.9-10.1, RL.9-10.2, RI.9-10.1, RI.9-10.2)
- Analyze word choice for cumulative impact on meaning and tone
  (RL.9-10.4) and analyze point of view and cultural experience
  (RL.9-10.6)
- Delineate and evaluate an argument's claims (RI.9-10.8)
- Grade-9 conventions: varied phrase/clause types at an introductory
  level, semicolons at an introductory level, vocabulary strategies
  (L.9-10.1.b, L.9-10.2.a, L.9-10.4, L.9-10.6)

What is *new* in this unit: **clause-level sentence analysis**
(independent, dependent, noun, relative, adverbial clauses),
**phrase variety as a meaning choice** (participial, prepositional,
absolute phrases), **semicolon and colon rules with reasons**,
**a repeatable vocabulary system**, **figures of speech in context**
(euphemism, oxymoron), and **style as an editing decision**
(language in different contexts; conforming work to a style manual).

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Analyze a sentence into independent and dependent clauses —
   including noun, relative, and adverbial clauses — name its sentence
   type, and repair fragments and run-ons (L.9-10.1.b).
2. Use varied phrase types (noun, verb, adjectival, adverbial,
   participial, prepositional, absolute) to convey specific meanings
   and add variety and interest to writing (L.9-10.1.b).
3. Use a semicolon (and, where fitting, a conjunctive adverb) to link
   closely related independent clauses, and use a colon to introduce
   a list or quotation (L.9-10.2.a; colon use per Purdue OWL).
4. Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown and multiple-meaning
   words by context, word-change patterns, reference materials, and
   verification, and use general academic and domain-specific
   vocabulary accurately (L.9-10.4.a–d, L.9-10.6).
5. Interpret figures of speech (euphemism, oxymoron) in context,
   analyze the cumulative impact of word choices on meaning and tone,
   apply knowledge of language to different contexts, and edit work
   toward a style manual's guidelines (L.9-10.5.a, RL.9-10.4,
   L.9-10.3, L.9-10.3.a, W.9-10.5).

## Materials

Notebook or paper, pen/pencil, index cards for exit checks, a folder
for the Editor's Portfolio project, the six lesson pages, one earlier
piece of the learner's own writing (from U01–U03), and dictionary
access (print or free digital). No purchases; no hazardous materials.
All reading is on the page — no outside book required.

## 20-session pacing (4 weeks × 5 sessions, ~50 minutes each)

The six lessons are the direct-instruction core (each spans a teach
session plus follow-up work). The remaining sessions are practice,
project work, and assessment. Each session names its activity and
success criterion — see the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) for the
full map.

| Session | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (Wk 15) | Lesson 01 teach: clause architecture | Learner labels the anchor sentence's clauses correctly |
| 2 | Lesson 01 independent practice + Practice A | Clause labels and two repairs correct |
| 3 | Clause-combining studio (Practice A, continued) | Four combined sentences, each labeled |
| 4 | Project Step 1: choose a U01–U03 piece; baseline edit notes | Piece chosen; three baseline issues named |
| 5 | Lesson 01/02 bridge: clause analysis of a new passage | Passage clauses labeled without help |
| 6 (Wk 16) | Lesson 02 teach: phrase variety | Phrase types named in two rewritten sentences |
| 7 | Lesson 02 independent practice + Practice B | Two expansions + two combinations correct |
| 8 | Lesson 03 teach: semicolons and colons | Semicolon/colon choices justified by rule |
| 9 | Lesson 03 independent practice + Practice C | Punctuation drill ≥ 8/10 |
| 10 | Phrase-and-punctuation studio: flat-paragraph rewrite | Rewrite uses 3+ phrase types and 1+ semicolon correctly |
| 11 (Wk 17) | Lesson 04 teach: vocabulary systems; word bank opens | Word-bank entries show clue + pattern + verification |
| 12 | Lesson 04 independent practice + Practice D | Word-bank draft of 12 words complete |
| 13 | Lesson 05 teach: figures of speech and tone (image activity) | Tone claims cite word choice + syntax |
| 14 | Lesson 05 independent practice + Practice E; project Step 2 | Two-tone passage draft complete |
| 15 | Vocabulary-and-tone studio: word-bank application | 6 bank words used correctly in new sentences |
| 16 (Wk 18) | Lesson 06 teach: style and editing | Flawed paragraph edited with reasons |
| 17 | Lesson 06 independent practice + Practice F editing studio | Editing pass covers structure, language, conventions |
| 18 | **Week-18 formative quiz** + miss review | Adult scores; misconceptions logged |
| 19 | Project Step 3: portfolio assembly + rationale; assessment Part A | Portfolio complete per deliverables; Part A attempted |
| 20 | **Unit assessment, Part B** (editing + tone-pair task) + reflection | Task meets rubric; learner names one growth |

A word bank runs from Session 11 to Session 20: 12 words, each with a
context clue, a word-change pattern or reference note, and a verified
meaning the learner can use in a new sentence.
