# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Nonfiction Rhetoric, Reasoning, and Source Evaluation

**Grade 10 Language Arts · Track weeks 11–14 · Issue #48, section U03**
Companion to the [unit README](README.md). Answer key: [answer-key.md](answer-key.md)
(adult only — never hand it to the learner).

## What this unit is for

U01 taught reading *within* a text; U02 taught reading *across* texts.
U03 teaches reading *against* a text — not hostile reading, but
accountable reading: trace the idea (L01–L02), name the persuasion
(L03), test the reasoning (L04), compare seminal purposes (L05), and
integrate accounts across mediums while evaluating a speaker (L06).
The throughline: **a claim is only as good as its reasoning, its
evidence, and its source — and each must be checked separately.**

## How to use the lessons

Each lesson is built for a teach session followed by a practice session.
The worked examples are models, not scripts — read them with the
learner, cover the reasoning, and ask the learner to reproduce it on the
guided items before independent work. The exit check is a daily pulse:
if it fails two days running, re-teach before advancing.

## Per-lesson notes

- **L01:** The most common failure is topic-as-idea ("libraries").
  Require a sentence with a verb and a disagreeable claim before moving
  on. Accept any accurate one-sentence claim, not one wording.
- **L02:** Run the smuggled-verdict check out loud on the learner's
  first summary. Words to hunt: "clearly," "obviously," "brilliantly,"
  "only," "just," and any praise or blame the author did not state.
- **L03:** Do not let "pathos" become a synonym for "emotional, therefore
  bad." The speech's pathos is attached to real reasons (the logbook,
  the sign-up sheet); the evaluation question is always "attached to
  what?"
- **L04:** Insist on the two-step fallacy form (quote the move, explain
  the failed test). If a learner cannot name the failed test, the
  fallacy name alone earns no credit. Also: the counter-editorial's
  fair point (sign-ups overstate attendance) must be credited — a
  learner who finds nothing fair has not read carefully.
- **L05:** Sensitive content, handled directly: the Declaration's
  "all men" did not include enslaved people or women in 1776 practice.
  Name that gap in the lesson, do not smooth it over, and do not let
  the discussion become a verdict on the learner's own views. The
  analytical task is: what does the document claim, what did it do, and
  how did later documents widen the claim? Keep the UDHR discussion
  focused on the excerpts; the adult previews the full text before
  assigning more.
- **L06:** The image is an AI-generated fictional scene. State that
  before the activity, and correct any learner who cites the image as
  evidence about a real meeting. The text-only alternative carries the
  same information; a learner who uses it has completed the activity
  fully.

## Reteaching triggers

- Summary contains verdicts after two attempts → re-teach L02 with the
  learner's own summary as the worked example (circle every verdict
  word together).
- Fallacy names without failed tests → return to the three-test card
  (does it follow? does it bear on the claim? is there enough?) and
  re-do Practice Set D items 1–3 orally.
- Essay thesis is a summary, not a claim → re-teach with the
  assessment rubric: a thesis must be something a reasonable reader
  could dispute.
- Quiz score below 12/20 → re-teach the missed standards in small
  groups (L01/L02 for items 1–2, L04 for 3–5, L03 for 6, L05 for 8)
  before the unit assessment; do not simply re-administer the quiz.

## Scoring notes

- Practice sets: 12 points each (2 per item). Accept paraphrase
  variants that preserve the reasoning; penalize invented quotations.
- Quiz: 20 points; see the answer key for acceptable variants.
- Assessment: Part A 20 points, Part B 30 points per the rubric in the
  assessment file. An essay that invents quotations loses all quotation
  points for the affected section.
- Project: 40 points per the project rubric. The revision note is
  scored on specificity, not on whether the revision was large.

## What this unit does not do

It does not teach citation format (U07), sentence-level editing (U04),
or research across perspectives (U07) — those arrive in later units.
Do not hold this unit's essays to U06/U07 standards; hold them to this
unit's rubric: claim, delineation, three-test evaluation, named
analysis, fair treatment, conclusion.
