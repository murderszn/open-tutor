# Project — The Alder Creek Library Question: Source Evaluation Dossier

**Grade 10 Language Arts · Unit 03 project (Sessions 14, 17, 19).** You
are an independent evaluator reporting to the Alder Creek library board.
Your job is not to pick a side first and then find reasons — it is to
evaluate the accounts, say what each one can and cannot show, and make a
recommendation the evidence actually supports.

## The question

Should the Alder Creek library's six-month evening-hours trial continue,
continue with changes, or end?

## Deliverables (submit all four)

1. **Account inventory (1–2 pages).** One row per account — the pro-trial
   editorial, the counter-editorial, the Delgado speech, and the practice
   data table. Each row: the account's central claim, its two strongest
   pieces of evidence or reasoning, its weakest inference (with the
   fallacy name if one applies), and what question it cannot answer.
2. **Credibility ratings (1 page).** Rate each account on credibility
   (who made it, and what would let you check it) and accuracy (whether
   its checkable claims hold up). Use a 3-point scale — strong, mixed,
   weak — with one sentence of justification per rating. The data table
   is fictional practice data: rate it as a *practice* source and say
   what a real table would need (source, collection method, cost and
   incident data) before the board could rely on it.
3. **Recommendation memo (300–400 words).** Addressed to the library
   board. State your recommendation (continue / continue with changes /
   end), give the three strongest reasons with account citations, name
   the one gap that most weakens your recommendation, and say what
   information would close that gap.
4. **Three-minute talk (Session 19).** Present your recommendation to
   the class (or to the guiding adult) as a short speech. You may use
   notes, not a read-aloud script. After the talk, answer two questions
   from the audience (or the adult) that challenge your recommendation,
   and respond with evidence rather than repetition.

## Steps

1. **Session 14 — Inventory.** Build the account inventory using your
   Lesson 01–04 work. The adult checks it for invented quotations before
   you proceed: every quotation must match the text file exactly.
2. **Session 17 — Draft.** Write the credibility ratings and the memo
   draft. Peer-review (or adult review) checks: does each memo reason
   cite an account? Is the gap real, not decorative?
3. **Session 19 — Present and revise.** Give the talk, answer the two
   challenges, then revise the memo's weakest paragraph in light of the
   questions asked. Submit the revised memo with the inventory, ratings,
   and a one-paragraph revision note saying what changed and why.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | Full marks | Partial marks | Missing/weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Account inventory (10) | All four accounts; claims accurate; weakest inference named with evidence; unanswerable question per account | Three accounts complete, or one claim misquoted | Fewer than three accounts, or invented quotations |
| Credibility ratings (8) | Every rating justified in one sentence; practice-data limitation named; real-data needs listed | Ratings given but justifications vague, or practice-data caveat missing | Ratings without justification |
| Recommendation memo (12) | Recommendation follows from the reasons; three cited reasons; a real gap named with a closing plan | Recommendation asserted more than argued, or gap is decorative | No recommendation, or reasons without citations |
| Talk and responses (6) | Clear 3-minute talk from notes; two challenges answered with evidence | Talk read aloud, or one challenge dodged | No talk, or challenges unanswered |
| Revision note (4) | Names the revised paragraph and why the questions forced the change | Vague note ("I improved it") | No revision note |

## Accessibility and honesty notes

- If speaking aloud is difficult, the talk may be delivered as a
  recorded audio note or a written talk text with the same structure —
  the adult scores content, not delivery mode.
- Every quotation must come from the unit's text files. Do not invent
  statistics, incidents, or quotations, and do not present the fictional
  practice data as real library figures.
- The illustration in Lesson 06 is an AI-generated fictional scene, not
  evidence about any real meeting; do not cite it in the dossier.
