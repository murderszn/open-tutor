# Unit Assessment — Unit 03

**Grade 10 Language Arts · Unit 03 · 50 points.** Part A: short items
(20 points). Part B: evaluation essay (30 points). The adult scores with
`answer-key.md` and the rubric below. Do not use the answer key.

## Part A — Short items (20 points)

Read the passage, then answer items 1–6.

### Passage — Mini-editorial: "The Festival Belongs to Everyone" (original, written for this assessment)

> The Alder Creek River Festival has charged admission for three years.
> Before that, it was free, and the whole town came: the families from the
> north side, the farm workers from outside town, the teenagers with no
> money at all. Now the gate fee is twelve dollars, and the crowds look
> different — smaller, quieter, and, if we are honest, richer. The
> organizers say the fee pays for safety and cleanup. But the festival
> ran safely for twenty free years, and volunteers cleaned up then, as
> they do now. If safety and cleanup were handled before without a fee,
> the fee cannot be necessary for safety and cleanup. The organizers
> also say attendance is "up" — but they count tickets sold, not people
> through the gate, and a family that used to come free now buys one
> ticket and sends one person instead of four. A festival that charges
> the poor to watch the river they live beside is not a festival. It is a
> fence with music.

1. (3 points) State the passage's central idea in one sentence, and
   name the paragraph-level move (problem, proposal, objection,
   principle, or test) that the final sentence performs.
2. (3 points) Write an objective summary of the passage in 60–90 words.
   No verdict of your own.
3. (4 points) Delineate the passage's argument about the fee and safety
   into one claim and the evidence offered for it. Then judge it: is
   the reasoning valid? Explain in two sentences.
4. (3 points) The organizers' attendance claim counts tickets, not
   people. Is that evidence relevant to the claim "attendance is up"?
   Explain in two sentences, distinguishing tickets from people.
5. (3 points) Name one rhetorical strategy in the final sentence ("It
   is a fence with music."), and explain what it does for the reader in
   two sentences.
6. (4 points) The passage and the UDHR both discuss equal access to a
   shared good. Name one UDHR article from Lesson 05 that supports the
   passage's theme, quote it briefly, and explain the connection in two
   sentences.

## Part B — Evaluation essay (30 points)

Choose **one** of the following. Write 700–900 words.

- **Option 1:** Evaluate the counter-editorial "The Library Can Wait"
  as an argument: delineate its claims, judge validity, relevance, and
  sufficiency, name at least two fallacies with quotations, credit its
  fairest point, and end with a verdict (accept, reject, or
  revise-and-retest) supported by your analysis.
- **Option 2:** Evaluate Delgado's speech "A Speech at the Town Hall"
  as rhetoric and argument: analyze its purpose and audience, its use
  of ethos, pathos, and logos (one example each with quotations), one
  place where its reasoning overreaches, and end with a verdict on
  whether the speech earns the vote it asks for.

### Essay rubric (30 points)

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Precise, arguable thesis that answers the prompt | 4 |
| Accurate delineation of claims/reasons with quotations | 6 |
| Evaluation: validity, relevance, sufficiency judged separately | 8 |
| Fallacy/rhetoric analysis named and evidenced (Option 1: two fallacies; Option 2: three appeals + one overreach) | 6 |
| Fair treatment: credit where due, counter-consideration addressed | 3 |
| Conclusion follows from the analysis; formal tone; quotations accurate | 3 |

A thesis that merely summarizes ("This essay will discuss the editorial")
earns no thesis points. An essay that invents quotations or statistics
loses all quotation points for the affected section.
