# Formative Quiz — Week 13

**Grade 10 Language Arts · Unit 03 · 20 points (10 items × 2 points).**
Read the two short passages, then answer. Write in your own words; quote
briefly where an item asks for evidence. Solutions are in `answer-key.md`
(adult only).

## Passage 1 — Mini-editorial: "Keep the Pool Open" (original, written for this quiz)

> For ten years, the Alder Creek pool has closed every August for
> "repairs" that nobody ever sees. The town pays a maintenance company
> every spring, the pool closes every August, and the repairs are never
> listed anywhere. Either the company is doing work it never reports, or
> it is being paid for work it never does. There is no third possibility.
> Meanwhile, the children who swim there in July — the same children whose
> parents pay the taxes that fund the pool — are told to wait until
> September. A pool is not a decoration. It is the one place in this town
> where a child who cannot afford a vacation can spend a whole summer
> afternoon for free. Keep it open, publish the repair list, and let the
> town judge for itself.

## Passage 2 — Mini-speech excerpt (original, written for this quiz)

> "I coached the swim team for twelve years. I know these children by
> name. When I tell you the pool matters, I am not reading a report — I
> am remembering the girl who learned to swim here the summer her family
> lost their house, and who told me, years later, that the pool was the
> only place that summer where she felt normal."

## Questions

1. State Passage 1's central idea in one sentence of your own words.
2. Write one objective summary sentence for Passage 1 (no verdict of
   your own).
3. Passage 1 says: "Either the company is doing work it never reports,
   or it is being paid for work it never does. There is no third
   possibility." Name the fallacy, and explain in one sentence why the
   reasoning fails.
4. Passage 1's claim "the repairs are never listed anywhere" is offered
   as evidence. Is that evidence relevant to the claim that the company
   may be paid for work it never does? Answer yes or no with one reason.
5. Is Passage 1's evidence sufficient to conclude the company is
   dishonest? Answer yes or no with one reason.
6. In Passage 2, name the appeal (ethos, pathos, or logos) doing the
   most work, quote the move, and say what it does for this audience in
   one sentence.
7. Give one fair point in Passage 1 — something the author gets right
   even if the argument as a whole is weak — and explain why it is fair.
8. A classmate says Passage 1 and the Declaration of Independence both
   argue "from a principle to a list of charges." Is that comparison
   accurate? Answer in two sentences, naming the principle and one
   charge in each text.
9. Suppose a data table showed pool attendance by month for three
   summers. What question could the table answer that Passage 1 cannot,
   and what question could Passage 1 answer that the table cannot?
   (One sentence each.)
10. Evaluate the speaker in Passage 2 on one dimension only — evidence:
    is her evidence checkable, relevant, and sufficient for the claim
    "the pool matters"? Answer in three sentences, one per test
    (checkable / relevant / sufficient).
