# Library Evening Use: Practice Data

*Fictional practice data written for this unit. These numbers were invented
to give learners something to analyze. They are not observations of any
real library, and they must not be cited as facts about any real place.*

## What the table shows

A six-month evening-hours trial at the fictional Alder Creek Public
Library, three evenings a week (Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, 6–9 p.m.).
"Evening visitors" counts each person who entered after 6 p.m.; one person
visiting three evenings counts three times. "Study-room bookings" counts
reserved seats in the library's two evening study rooms.

| Month | Evening visitors | Study-room bookings | Evening programs held | Program attendance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Month 1 | 412 | 58 | 2 | 41 |
| Month 2 | 538 | 71 | 3 | 63 |
| Month 3 | 491 | 66 | 2 | 38 |
| Month 4 | 612 | 84 | 4 | 97 |
| Month 5 | 587 | 79 | 3 | 71 |
| Month 6 | 655 | 92 | 4 | 108 |
| **Six-month total** | **3,295** | **450** | **18** | **418** |

## What the table does not show

- Who the visitors were (age, work hours, reason for visiting) — the
  trial counted entries, not identities.
- Whether evening visitors were new users or daytime users visiting
  later — the count cannot answer that question.
- Evening staffing cost, energy cost, or incident reports — those were
  recorded separately and are not in this table.
- Any comparison town or control group — there is no second library here
  to compare against.

## How to use this table honestly

A careful claim from this table sounds like: "Evening visits rose from 412
in Month 1 to 655 in Month 6, and study-room bookings rose from 58 to 92,
so use grew during the trial." A careless claim sounds like: "Thousands of
new readers now depend on evening hours" — the table cannot show that
visitors were new, or that they were readers rather than, say, students
using the computers. Lesson 06 practices telling those two claims apart.
