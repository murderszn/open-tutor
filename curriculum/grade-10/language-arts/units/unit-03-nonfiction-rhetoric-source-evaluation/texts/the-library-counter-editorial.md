# The Library Can Wait

*An original editorial written for this unit. The town of Alder Creek, the
library, and every person and number named here are fictional. This editorial
was written to be evaluated, not agreed with: it contains weak reasoning on
purpose, and Lesson 04 names the problems. Any resemblance to a real place
is coincidental.*

Everyone loves libraries. Nobody is against reading. But the proposal to
keep the Alder Creek Public Library open until nine o'clock is a solution
looking for a problem, and the town should say no.

First, consider what libraries are for. Libraries are quiet places for
study during the day. Everyone knows that a library at night is a
different creature entirely — noisier, harder to supervise, and open to
exactly the kind of loitering that daytime hours avoid. If we open at
night, we will either have to hire security or accept the consequences.
There is no middle ground.

Second, the so-called demand is invented. The board points to a sign-up
sheet that "filled in four days." A sign-up sheet is not a promise. People
sign things all the time and never show up — my own neighbor signed up for
a marathon once and never ran a step. Signing a sheet costs nothing, so it
proves nothing.

Third, we already know evening programs fail in this town. The community
center tried a Thursday night lecture series last year and only twelve
people came to the first talk. Twelve people! A library is not a nightclub,
and taxpayers should not fund an empty room so that a handful of night
owls can feel catered to.

Finally, think about who is really pushing this. The loudest voices for
evening hours are people who do not pay property taxes in this town —
students passing through, renters, newcomers. The people who built this
library with their taxes deserve a say, and their say is: keep things as
they are.

The library works. It has worked for twenty years. Changing it now would
be reckless, wasteful, and unfair to the residents who made it possible.
The library can wait. So can the people asking for more hours — they have
waited this long already.
