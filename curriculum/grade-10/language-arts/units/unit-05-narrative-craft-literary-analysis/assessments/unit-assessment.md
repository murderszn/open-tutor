# Unit Assessment — Unit 05: Narrative Craft and Literary Analysis Essays

*Adult-scored with the [answer key](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment).
Two parts, about 90 minutes total (Part A: ~30 minutes; Part B: ~60
minutes). Part A uses the passage below; Part B is an essay. The adult
scores Part A with the key and Part B with the essay rubric in the key.*

## Part A — Reading a new story with the unit's tools (30 minutes)

Read the original micro-story below, then answer items 1–6. Quote the
story in every answer that asks for evidence.

### The Borrowed Coat

*An original micro-story written for this assessment. Fictional.*

The foreman looked Tomas up and down, then looked at the coat, and
Tomas knew the interview was over before it had begun.

"Ever worked a boat?" the foreman asked.

"Not yet," Tomas said. "But I can learn. I'm a fast learner."

The foreman nodded toward the sleeves, which hung past Tomas's
fingertips. "That coat's too big for you."

"It was my brother's," Tomas said. "He worked the trawlers for three
years."

"And where's your brother now?"

"Gone north. To the oil rigs." Tomas tugged the left sleeve up, and it
fell straight back down. "I thought — if I wore his coat, I'd look like
I knew what I was doing."

The foreman was quiet for a moment. Behind him, the boatyard clanged
and hissed, and the smell of paint and diesel drifted across the dock.
Then he said, "A coat doesn't know anything. Take it off."

Tomas took it off. His own shirt was thin and too short at the wrists,
and the wind off the water went straight through it. He stood there
waiting to be sent home.

"Good," the foreman said. "I'd rather have a boy who tells the truth
than a coat that lies. Trainee spot. Six in the morning. Don't be late."

That morning, Tomas had taken the coat from the hook by the door while
his brother's room stood empty behind him. He had rolled the sleeves
three times and told himself it fit. On the long walk to the boatyard
he had practiced what to say — *three years on the trawlers, ask me
anything* — and by the time he reached the gate, he almost believed it.

The coat was his brother's. The hands were his.

*Word count: about 290 words.*

### Items

1. **Want and need.** Name Tomas's want in one sentence and his need in
   one sentence, each with a quoted line. Then write one sentence: how
   do the want and the need point in different directions?
2. **Point of view.** Name the story's point of view (first person,
   third-person limited, or omniscient) and the character it stays close
   to. Then name one thing that point of view hides from the reader, with
   a quoted line.
3. **Scene vs. summary.** The interview is written as scene; the walk to
   the boatyard is written as summary ("On the long walk to the
   boatyard he had practiced what to say…"). Explain in two sentences
   why each speed fits its stretch.
4. **Flashback.** The paragraph beginning "That morning, Tomas had taken
   the coat…" is a flashback. Explain in two sentences what it changes
   about the interview scene — what does the interview mean *before*
   the flashback, and what does it mean *after*?
5. **Dialogue.** Quote one line of dialogue that reveals character, and
   explain in two sentences what it reveals and how you can tell from
   the words alone.
6. **Telling detail.** Quote one telling or sensory detail from the story
   and explain in two sentences what work it does (what it shows, sets
   up, or prepares).

**Part A scoring:** 6 items × 3 points (correct identification, quotation,
explanation) = 18 points. 18–15 = secure; 14–11 = review the missed
lesson; below 11 = re-teach before Part B.

## Part B — Literary analysis essay (60 minutes)

Write a literary analysis essay of **800–1,000 words** (state the word
count at the top) on **one** of these texts:

- (a) "The Kite Maker's Last Flight" (this unit's anchor story), or
- (b) "The Ferryman's Toll" (Unit 01,
  [`../../unit-01-world-literature/texts/the-ferrymans-toll.md`](../../unit-01-world-literature/texts/the-ferrymans-toll.md)).

Your essay must argue — with a debatable thesis and handled textual
evidence — **how the author's choices create specific effects**. Address
at least two of the following: complex character development (RL.9-10.3),
structure and event order (RL.9-10.5), time manipulation (RL.9-10.5).
Your essay must contain:

- A one-sentence arguable thesis naming a choice and an effect.
- At least three body paragraphs, each making one claim about a choice,
  supporting it with at least one introduced, accurately quoted, and
  explained quotation, and naming the effect on the reader.
- A conclusion that follows from the analysis (no new evidence, no moral).
- Accurate quotations with the story's wording; no invented quotations.

The adult scores Part B with the essay rubric in the
[answer key](../answer-key.md#part-b-essay-rubric)
(20 points). **Unit total:** 38 points; 38–32 = strong; 31–25 =
proficient; 24–18 = revise one weakest area; below 18 = rebuild with
adult support.
