# Project — The Story Workshop Portfolio

*Adult-supervised. The learner writes a complete original short story,
documents the craft decisions behind it, and reads an excerpt aloud.
Timeline: plan in Week 21 (Session 10), draft in Week 22 (Sessions
12–13), revise in Week 22–23 (Sessions 14–15), submit end of Week 23
(Session 18).*

## Task

You are the author *and* the analyst of your own story. First you build
it, using every tool from Lessons 01–05; then you write about how you
built it, using the analytical tools from Lesson 06. The portfolio
proves two things at once: that you can make a story move, and that you
can explain *how* you made it move.

**Step 1 — Story plan (Week 21, Session 10).** With the adult, approve
your plan: the problem/situation in one sentence; the character's want
and need (pointing in different directions); a five-event causal chain;
the point of view and what it hides; two plot threads and two cut
points. *Why this step matters:* Lesson 01 taught that architecture
comes before sentences. A plan the adult approves is a story that can
be built.

**Step 2 — First draft (Week 22, Sessions 12–13).** Write the complete
first draft: **1,500–2,500 words**. Draft without stopping to polish;
get the events down in causal order with the POV consistent. Mark any
place you are unsure with [?] in the margin — those are revision
targets, not failures.

**Step 3 — Three revision passes (Week 22–23, Sessions 14–15).**
Run the passes in order and log each one (see Deliverable 3):
structure pass (chain check, earned ending, POV consistency, braid
cuts), language pass (dialogue test, sensory ladder, sentence variety,
one justified semicolon), conventions pass (spelling, dialogue
punctuation, paragraph breaks at speaker changes, consistent tense).
Do not start the conventions pass until the structure pass is signed
off by the adult.

**Step 4 — Craft memo (Week 23, Session 15).** Write a 500–700-word
memo explaining your craft decisions: your architecture (want/need and
one causal link), your POV choice and what it hides, one dialogue or
sensory decision, one pacing decision (scene vs. summary or flashback
placement), and one revision decision from each pass — each with a
quoted example from your own story. End with one sentence: what would
you change if you had one more week?

**Step 5 — Author reading (Week 23, Session 18).** Choose a 150–200-word
excerpt and mark it for delivery (pauses, stressed words, pace changes).
Read it aloud in formal English. The delivery choices must match the
passage's meaning: slow where the story slows, pause where a decision
lands, stress the word that carries the turn.

## Deliverables

1. Short story, 1,500–2,500 words (word count stated on the cover page).
2. Craft memo, 500–700 words, covering all five decision areas with
   quoted examples.
3. Revision log: one entry per pass (structure, language, conventions),
   each naming the change made and why.
4. Author-reading excerpt (150–200 words) with delivery marks, plus a
   one-sentence note on the delivery choice.
5. Story plan (approved in Session 10) attached as the portfolio's first
   page.

## Rubric — observable criteria

| Criterion | 4 — Exemplary | 3 — Proficient | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Architecture | Want and need point in different directions; every event causes the next; ending earned by an earlier event | Want/need distinct; chain mostly causal; ending follows | Want and need blur together, or 1–2 events are decorations | No distinguishable want/need; events merely follow each other |
| POV and plot lines | POV consistent; what it hides is named in the memo; two threads braided with cuts at pauses | POV consistent; braid present; one cut lands mid-scene | POV slips once, or threads never cross | POV changes without reason; single thread only |
| Dialogue and sensory language | Dialogue reveals character (passes cover-the-names test); 1+ precise sensory detail per scene | Dialogue mostly revealing; sensory detail present in most scenes | Dialogue states information only; description generic | Dialogue is filler; no sensory detail |
| Pacing | Scenes for change-moments, summary for passing time; flashback (if used) changes what the present means | Speeds mostly well chosen; flashback present but thin | Everything scenified, or everything summarized | No discernible pacing choice |
| Revision | Three passes logged in order; structure pass signed off before conventions; log names change + reason per pass | Three passes logged; order mostly kept | Passes merged or out of order; log lists fixes without reasons | No log; draft submitted unrevised |
| Reflective conclusion | Follows from events and reflects on what was experienced; could only belong to this story | Reflects on the experience; slightly generic | Moral or summary rather than reflection | Missing or unrelated |
| Craft memo | All five decision areas covered with quoted examples; trade-offs named honestly | All five areas covered; one example thin | 3–4 areas covered, or examples missing | Memo missing or purely descriptive |
| Author reading | Delivery marks match meaning (pace, pause, stress); formal English; excerpt well chosen | Delivery mostly matched; one mark unexplained | Read flat; marks decorative | No reading submitted |

**Scoring:** 32–27 = strong portfolio; 26–21 = proficient with revision
notes; 20–15 = revise and resubmit one weakest criterion; below 15 =
rebuild with adult support, starting from Step 1.
