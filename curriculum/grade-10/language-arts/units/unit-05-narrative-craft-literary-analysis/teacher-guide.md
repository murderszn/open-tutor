# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Narrative Craft and Literary Analysis Essays

*Adult-side guidance for Grade 10 Language Arts, Unit 05 (track weeks
20–23). Learner-facing lessons never contain answers; keep this guide and
the [answer key](answer-key.md) out of the learner's hands.*

## Unit at a glance

Twenty 50-minute sessions. Six lessons (each taught across two sessions:
direct instruction + guided work, then independent work), six practice
sets, a short-story portfolio project, a formative quiz, and a two-part
culminating assessment. The adult reviews written practice the same day
and scores the quiz and assessment with the answer key.

**Core moves the unit builds:** (1) design story architecture — want vs.
need, causal event chains; (2) choose point of view as a knowledge
decision and braid two plot lines; (3) write dialogue that reveals
character and description built from telling, sensory details; (4)
control pacing through scene, summary, and flashback placement; (5)
revise in three ordered passes and write a reflective conclusion; (6)
turn craft knowledge into a literary analysis essay — arguable thesis,
handled evidence, named effects — and present an excerpt aloud.

## Differentiation

- **Reading access:** every lesson allows the adult to read texts aloud;
  the analytical and craft work (mapping, chaining, rewriting, arguing)
  is the assessed skill, not the decoding.
- **Writing access:** the story draft may be handwritten, typed, or
  dictated to the adult; the craft memo and essay may be dictated and
  then edited by the learner. Handwriting is never the barrier to the
  thinking.
- **Pacing:** the drafting studios (Sessions 12–13) may stretch across
  extra days; the unit's 20 sessions are a plan, not a deadline. If the
  draft is not ready at Session 14, delay the language pass rather than
  skipping the structure pass.
- **Discussion:** the author reading (Session 18) may be recorded rather
  than performed live; the delivery marks must still be submitted in
  writing. A quiet learner's reading can be for the adult alone.
- **Extension:** strong learners may analyze the *same* choice in two
  paragraphs (Lesson 06 extension) or revise the anchor story's opening
  paragraph and defend the change (Lesson 05 extension).

## Misconception watch (across the unit)

- **Want = need.** Learners collapse the two ("she wants the prize and
  she wants approval"). Press the difference until the learner can state
  it: a want is a goal the character can name; a need is a change shown
  in choices. Sef handing over the string is the model.
- **POV as preference.** "I like first person" is not a reason. Every
  POV hides something; the learner must name what theirs hides (Lesson
  02 memo, Practice D3).
- **Dialogue as transcription.** Real talk is not story dialogue. The
  cover-the-names test and the adverb rule (revise the words, not the
  adverb) are the correctives.
- **Flashback = backstory.** Any past information feels like a flashback.
  The test: does the inserted past *change what the present scene
  means*? If not, it is backstory and belongs in summary or on the
  cutting-room floor.
- **Revision = proofreading.** Learners proofread first and then refuse
  to cut. Enforce the pass order physically (one color per pass) and do
  not accept a conventions pass until the structure pass is signed off.
- **Analysis = device list.** "Imagery, symbolism, foreshadowing" is not
  an argument. The thesis test (could a skeptic disagree?) and the
  handling drill (introduce, quote, explain) are the correctives.
- **Moral = reflection.** "The lesson is never give up" is a platitude.
  A reflective conclusion could only be thought by *this* character
  after *these* events.

## Scoring guidance

- **Practice sets:** scored formatively; the adult reviews the same day
  and logs the one skill to re-teach. The success criterion at the end
  of each set is the scoring checklist.
- **Quiz (Session 16):** 18 points; see the quiz file. Use the misses to
  target Session 17's review — do not re-teach the whole unit.
- **Project (Session 18):** 8 criteria × 4 points = 32; bands in
  `project.md`. The craft memo is the adult's best evidence that the
  learner understands the tools, not merely used them.
- **Unit assessment:** Part A 18 points + Part B essay 20 points = 38;
  bands in the assessment file. The essay rubric is in the answer key.
  Score the essay on argument (thesis, claims, evidence, effects), not
  on whether the adult agrees with the reading — a well-argued minority
  reading earns full marks.

## Author-reading coaching (Session 18)

The reading is assessed on *delivery matching meaning*, not on
performance flair. Before the reading, check the learner's marks:
pauses where a decision lands (Sef's "Yours now"), stress on the word
that carries the turn ("*same* kite"), slower pace for the reflective
close. During the reading, listen for formal English (SL.9-10.6) and
for the line of reasoning staying clear (SL.9-10.4) — the listener must
be able to follow the passage's sense from the delivery alone. After the
reading, ask one question: "Which delivery choice changed the meaning
most, and why?" The answer is the assessment, as much as the reading
itself.

## Reteaching triggers

- If the quiz shows the want/need distinction is insecure, re-teach
  Lesson 01 with the Mara scenario (Practice A) before the drafting
  studios — a story built on a collapsed want/need cannot be fixed by
  revision passes.
- If Practice D shows the chain is decorative, return to Lesson 01's
  chain test with the learner's own draft before Session 14.
- If the thesis clinic (Practice F) shows summary-theses, re-teach
  Lesson 06's Worked example 1 with the learner's own thesis candidates
  before the assessment.

## Materials and safety

Notebook or paper, pen/pencil, index cards, a folder for the portfolio.
No lab materials, no hazards, no purchases, no paid accounts. The adult
previews every external link in the Resource Pack before the learner
opens it; mature literary themes (grief, loss) are handled in the anchor
story with the adult setting expectations first.
