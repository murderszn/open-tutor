# Unit 05 — Narrative Craft and Literary Analysis Essays

**Grade:** 10 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 20–23
**Backlog:** [issue #48](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/48), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

U01 taught reading *within* a text, U02 taught reading *across* texts,
U03 taught judging nonfiction arguments, and U04 taught the writer's
sentence-level tools. This unit turns those tools on *making* stories:
across four weeks the learner builds five moves — designing a story's
architecture (want vs. need, events that build); choosing point of view
and braiding plot lines; writing dialogue, description, and sensory
language; controlling pacing through scene, summary, and time
manipulation; and revising in three passes — then turns the same craft
knowledge into a literary analysis essay that argues how a studied
author's choices create effects. It ends in a short-story portfolio and
an author reading.

## Essential question

How does a writer turn a good idea into a story that *moves* — and how
does knowing the maker's tools change the way you read someone else's
story?

## Prerequisites

From U01–U04 and the diagnostic weeks:

- Cite strong and thorough textual evidence; determine a theme and write
  an objective summary (RL.9-10.1, RL.9-10.2)
- Analyze point of view and cultural experience (RL.9-10.6); analyze
  word choice for cumulative impact on meaning and tone (RL.9-10.4)
- Analyze how an author's structural choices and time manipulation
  create effects (RL.9-10.5); analyze complex characters (RL.9-10.3)
- Delineate and evaluate arguments (RI.9-10.8); write arguments with
  claims and evidence (W.9-10.1)
- Use varied phrase/clause types, semicolons, and a style manual's
  guidelines in editing (L.9-10.1.b, L.9-10.2.a, L.9-10.3.a)

What is *new* in this unit: **narrative architecture as a designed
object** (want vs. need, causal event chains); **point of view as a
knowledge decision** (what the reader is allowed to know); **dialogue
as character evidence**; **pacing as a meaning choice** (scene vs.
summary, flashback placement); **revision as three ordered passes**;
and **literary analysis as argument about craft** — thesis, handled
evidence, named effects.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Design a story's architecture: state a problem/situation, distinguish
   a character's want from their need, and build an event sequence in
   which each event causes the next (W.9-10.3.a, W.9-10.3.c).
2. Establish and control point of view and narrator — including multiple
   plot lines braided at chosen cut points — and explain what each
   choice lets the reader know and hides (W.9-10.3.a, W.9-10.3.b,
   RL.9-10.6).
3. Use narrative techniques (dialogue, pacing, description, reflection)
   and precise sensory language to develop experiences, events, and
   characters (W.9-10.3.b, W.9-10.3.d, RL.9-10.4).
4. Revise a narrative draft in separate structure, language, and
   conventions passes, and write a conclusion that follows from and
   reflects on what was experienced, observed, or resolved (W.9-10.5,
   W.9-10.3.e).
5. Write a literary analysis essay (800–1,000 words) that argues how a
   studied author's choices — complex characters, structure, time
   manipulation — create specific effects, with strong textual evidence;
   and present an excerpt aloud with deliberate delivery choices
   (RL.9-10.3, RL.9-10.5, RL.9-10.1, W.9-10.9, SL.9-10.4, SL.9-10.6).

## Materials

Notebook or paper, pen/pencil, index cards for exit checks, a folder for
the Story Workshop Portfolio project, the six lesson pages. No purchases;
no hazardous materials. All reading is on the page — no outside book
required.

## 20-session pacing (4 weeks × 5 sessions, ~50 minutes each)

The six lessons are the direct-instruction core (each spans two
sessions: teach + guided work, then independent work). The remaining
sessions are practice, drafting studios, project work, and assessment.
Each session names its activity and success criterion — see the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) for the full map.

| Session | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (Wk 20) | Lesson 01 teach: story architecture, want vs. need, illustration observation | Learner maps Lian's and Sef's want/need with quotations |
| 2 | Lesson 01 independent practice + story-seed sketch 1 | Seed has want, need, and a 5-event causal chain |
| 3 | Lesson 02 teach: POV, narrator, multiple plot lines | Learner names what each POV hides and shows |
| 4 | **Practice A** — story seeds + want/need maps + chain test | Two seeds pass the "because" test; one decoration cut |
| 5 | Independent reading: anchor story re-read with architecture/POV annotations; discussion | Annotations mark want/need, POV moments, and braid cuts |
| 6 (Wk 21) | Lesson 03 teach: dialogue, description, sensory language | Learner passes the cover-the-names test on one exchange |
| 7 | Lesson 03 independent practice + **Practice B** (dialogue drills) | Dialogue reveals character with no narration; ladder reaches rung 3 |
| 8 | Lesson 04 teach: pacing — scene vs. summary, flashback | Learner labels each story section scene/summary with reasons |
| 9 | **Practice C** — sensory ladder + pacing conversions | One scene and one summary of the same event, gains/losses named |
| 10 | Project Step 1: story plan (architecture + POV memo + braid plan) with the adult | Plan approved: want/need, POV, two threads, cut points |
| 11 (Wk 22) | Lesson 05 teach: drafting + three revision passes | Learner can state the pass order and why it matters |
| 12 | Drafting studio 1: first draft of the short story (1,500–2,500 words) | Draft's events in causal order; POV consistent |
| 13 | Drafting studio 2 + **Practice D** (structure pass on own draft) | Three structure revisions written with reasons |
| 14 | **Practice E** — language + conventions pass (U04 tools) | "Very" count at zero; one semicolon used with reason |
| 15 | Project Step 2: craft memo draft + revision log | Memo explains 4+ craft decisions with examples |
| 16 (Wk 23) | **Week-23 formative quiz** | Adult scores; misconceptions logged |
| 17 | Lesson 06 teach: literary analysis essay + quiz review | Thesis passes the skeptic test; evidence handled |
| 18 | **Practice F** — thesis clinic + model paragraph; project submit (story + craft memo); author reading | Portfolio complete per deliverables list; excerpt read aloud with marked delivery |
| 19 | **Unit assessment, Part A** (short items on "The Borrowed Coat") | All items attempted with quotations |
| 20 | **Unit assessment, Part B** (analysis essay 800–1,000 words) + unit reflection | Essay meets rubric; learner names one growth |

Independent writing runs all four weeks: the learner drafts in Sessions
12–13, revises in Sessions 14–15, and plans the analysis essay in
Session 17. The adult reviews written practice the same day.
