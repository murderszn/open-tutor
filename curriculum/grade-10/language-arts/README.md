# Grade 10 Language Arts

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are
delivered; the eight units are planned, not yet written. See
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained
for one grade-10 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 10 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-10 language arts course following the
"world literature and composition" pathway: reading literature from outside
the United States for cultural point of view, with explicit attention to
translation as interpretation; comparative theme analysis across cultures;
narrative structure, characterization, and author craft; nonfiction rhetoric,
argument evaluation, and source judgment; grammar precision, syntax variety,
tone, and vocabulary systems; narrative craft and literary analysis essays;
argument synthesis with fair counterargument; research across perspectives
with multimodal composition; and poetry, drama, discussion, and a revised
portfolio. The bar for the year is the grade-10 tier of the text-complexity
standards: by year's end the learner reads independently and proficiently at
the high end of the grades 9-10 complexity band (RL.9-10.10, RI.9-10.10).
Sessions are about 50 minutes, five per week, blending reading, discussion,
modeled writing, drafting and revision, and independent written practice that
the adult reviews the same day.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Cite strong and thorough textual evidence to support analysis of what a
   literary or informational text says explicitly and of inferences drawn
   from it (RL.9-10.1, RI.9-10.1).
2. Determine a theme or central idea and analyze in detail its development
   over the course of a text, including how it emerges and is shaped and
   refined by specific details; write an objective summary; compare how
   related themes develop in works from different cultures (RL.9-10.2,
   RI.9-10.2).
3. Analyze how complex characters develop, interact with other characters,
   and advance the plot or develop the theme; analyze how an author unfolds
   an analysis or series of ideas or events (RL.9-10.3, RI.9-10.3).
4. Determine the meaning of words and phrases as used in a text, including
   figurative, connotative, and technical meanings, and analyze the
   cumulative impact of specific word choices on meaning and tone; interpret
   figures of speech (e.g., euphemism, oxymoron) in context and analyze
   their role in the text (RL.9-10.4, RI.9-10.4, L.9-10.5.a).
5. Analyze how an author's choices concerning text structure, event order
   (e.g., parallel plots), and time manipulation (e.g., pacing, flashbacks)
   create effects such as mystery, tension, or surprise; analyze in detail
   how claims are developed and refined by particular sentences, paragraphs,
   or larger portions of a text (RL.9-10.5, RI.9-10.5).
6. Analyze a particular point of view or cultural experience reflected in a
   work of literature from outside the United States, drawing on a wide
   reading of world literature (RL.9-10.6); analyze the representation of a
   subject or key scene in two different artistic mediums, including what is
   emphasized or absent in each (RL.9-10.7); analyze how an author draws on
   and transforms source material in a specific work (RL.9-10.9).
7. Delineate and evaluate the argument and specific claims in a text,
   assessing whether the reasoning is valid and the evidence is relevant and
   sufficient, and identifying false statements and fallacious reasoning
   (RI.9-10.8); analyze seminal U.S. documents of historical and literary
   significance, including how they address related themes and concepts
   (RI.9-10.9).
8. Write arguments to support claims in an analysis of substantive topics or
   texts, using valid reasoning and relevant and sufficient evidence:
   precise claims distinguished from alternate or opposing claims, fair
   development of both, cohesive links, formal style and objective tone, and
   a concluding statement that follows from and supports the argument;
   draw evidence from literary or informational texts to support analysis,
   reflection, and research (W.9-10.1, W.9-10.9).
9. Write narratives to develop real or imagined experiences or events using
   effective technique, well-chosen details, and well-structured event
   sequences, with precise sensory language and a conclusion that reflects
   on what was experienced, observed, or resolved (W.9-10.3).
10. Write informative/explanatory texts that examine and convey complex ideas
    clearly through effective selection, organization, and analysis of
    content, with formatting, graphics, or multimedia when useful
    (W.9-10.2); conduct short and sustained research projects to answer a
    question: gather relevant information from multiple authoritative print
    and digital sources using advanced searches, assess each source's
    usefulness, synthesize multiple sources, integrate information
    selectively without plagiarism, and follow a standard format for citation
    (W.9-10.7, W.9-10.8).
11. Demonstrate command of grade 9-10 conventions and knowledge of language:
    varied phrases (noun, verb, adjectival, adverbial, participial,
    prepositional, absolute) and clauses (independent, dependent; noun,
    relative, adverbial) to convey specific meanings and add variety
    (L.9-10.1.b); semicolons to link closely related independent clauses
    (L.9-10.2.a); work conformed to a style manual (MLA) (L.9-10.3.a); word
    meaning clarified through context, word-change patterns, reference
    materials, and verification (L.9-10.4); and accurate general academic
    and domain-specific vocabulary (L.9-10.6).
12. Initiate and participate effectively in collaborative discussions with
    diverse partners: come prepared, draw explicitly on textual evidence,
    propel the conversation with questions, respond thoughtfully to diverse
    perspectives, and summarize agreement and disagreement (SL.9-10.1);
    integrate information from diverse media or formats, evaluating the
    credibility and accuracy of each source (SL.9-10.2); evaluate a speaker's
    point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric, identifying
    fallacious reasoning or distorted evidence (SL.9-10.3); present
    information clearly, concisely, and logically, making strategic use of
    digital media to enhance understanding, and adapting speech to formal
    English when appropriate (SL.9-10.4, SL.9-10.5, SL.9-10.6).

## Verified standards summary

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for English Language Arts,
grades 9-10 band** (Reading: Literature, Reading: Informational Text, Writing,
Speaking & Listening, Language). Each strand's code and description was checked
against [thecorestandards.org](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/)
on 2026-10-07; the per-unit crosswalk is in the scope-and-sequence. Notable
band facts used by this track: RL.9-10.8 does not exist at this band (the
strand goes 7 to 9); RL.9-10.10 and RI.9-10.10 carry a two-tier expectation —
end of grade 9, proficiently with scaffolding as needed at the high end of
the 9-10 band; end of grade 10, independently and proficiently at the high
end — which sets this track's text-selection ceiling and its move from
scaffolded to independent reading. RL.9-10.6 (cultural experience in world
literature from outside the United States) is this track's signature reading
standard. No claim of state adoption, accreditation, or certification is made;
Common Core is used here as a national reference framework, not a graduation
requirement.

## Planned units (in prerequisite order)

- U01 — World literature cultural context and interpretation
- U02 — Comparative theme structure and perspective
- U03 — Nonfiction rhetoric reasoning and source evaluation
- U04 — Language syntax tone vocabulary and conventions
- U05 — Narrative craft and literary analysis essays
- U06 — Argument synthesis and counterargument workshop
- U07 — Research across perspectives and multimodal composition
- U08 — Poetry drama discussion and revised portfolio

Plus four flexible weeks across the year: diagnostic placement (weeks 1-2),
midyear review (week 19), and final review/portfolio (week 36). The full
week-by-week plan is in the [scope-and-sequence](scope-and-sequence.md).

## Guidance for the adult

- Preview every text and video before assigning; this course discusses mature
  literary themes (conflict, power, injustice, mortality) across cultures.
  Choose editions and excerpts appropriate to the learner, and set expectations
  before difficult passages.
- World literature means the adult, not the learner, vets cultural context:
  confirm background notes for accuracy, avoid treating one work as standing
  for an entire culture, and prefer scholarly or community-vetted
  introductions for traditions outside the adult's own background.
- Translation is interpretation: when a work is read in translation, the
  adult notes the translator and edition, and comparison across two
  translations of one passage is a lesson tool, never a puzzle the learner
  must solve alone.
- Keep discussion evidence-based and inquiry-focused. The track studies how
  arguments work; it does not require the learner to adopt any position.
- Spelling and keyboarding fluency are supported, not assumed; conventions
  are taught in U04 but reinforced in every unit's editing passes.
- Use original or public-domain texts for readings; never reproduce
  copyrighted books, poems, or worksheets without permission. Link to lawful
  sources (Project Gutenberg, LibriVox, CommonLit, Poetry Foundation, Folger
  Shakespeare Library, Library of Congress) rather than copying.
- All units carry accessibility supports (text alternatives for images,
  audiobook options, adjustable pacing, caption checks) and safe, low-cost
  materials. Nothing in this track requires a paid account.
