# Unit Assessment — Reactions, Balancing, and Stoichiometry

Learner-facing. Solutions are in `../answer-key.md` (adult only). Use the unit's rounded molar
masses (g/mol): H 1.0, C 12.0, N 14.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Mg 24.3, Al 27.0, S 32.1, Cl 35.5,
K 39.1, Ca 40.1, Fe 55.8, Cu 63.5, Zn 65.4. Avogadro's number: 6.02 × 10²³ particles/mol.
Show working and units for every numerical item. This assessment is not standardised or
validated; it checks this unit's objectives only.

## Part A — Reaction types and balancing (Lessons 01–02)

1. A zinc strip in blue copper sulfate solution fades the blue colour and gains a reddish-brown
   coating. Classify the reaction, write its balanced equation, and state the evidence that
   this is a chemical change. (4 marks)
2. Balance: C₂H₄ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O. Show the inventory. (3 marks)
3. Balance: Fe + HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂. Show the inventory. (3 marks)
4. Balance: Na₂CO₃ + HCl → NaCl + H₂O + CO₂. Show the inventory. (3 marks)

## Part B — The mole (Lesson 03)

5. A sample contains 22.0 g of carbon dioxide. How many moles is this, and how many molecules
   does it contain? (3 marks)
6. What is the mass of 3.00 mol of ammonia, NH₃? (2 marks)
7. Which contains more atoms: 24.3 g of magnesium or 12.0 g of carbon? Show the mole
   calculation for each. (3 marks)

## Part C — Stoichiometry (Lesson 04)

8. 2Al + 3H₂SO₄ → Al₂(SO₄)₃ + 3H₂. What mass of hydrogen gas forms from 5.4 g of aluminium?
   (Al 27.0, H₂ 2.0.) Use the 4-step setup. (4 marks)
9. CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O. What mass of water forms from 16.0 g of methane? (CH₄ 16.0,
   H₂O 18.0.) Use the 4-step setup and check by conservation of mass. (5 marks)

## Part D — Limiting reactants and percent yield (Lesson 05)

10. 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. You have 6.0 g of H₂ and 32.0 g of O₂. Identify the limiting reactant
    with the divide-by-coefficient step, compute the mass of water formed, and state the mass
    of excess reactant remaining. (6 marks)
11. A reaction's theoretical yield is 44.0 g and the actual yield is 38.5 g. Calculate the
    percent yield and give one plausible reason it is below 100%. (3 marks)

## Part E — Explanation and error analysis (Lessons 01, 06; HS-PS1-2, HS-PS1-7)

12. Sodium (a soft metal) and chlorine (a toxic gas) react to form sodium chloride (a stable
    white solid). Use outermost-electron reasoning to explain why the product's properties
    differ from the reactants', and state the mole ratio in 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl. (4 marks)
13. A student balances H₂ + O₂ → H₂O as H₂ + O₂ → H₂O₂ and says "the counts match, so it is
    balanced." Explain why the counts matching is not enough. (3 marks)
14. 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO. A student starts with 12.2 g of Mg and excess O₂ and reports 20.2 g of
    MgO. Show, with a mass check that includes the oxygen consumed, whether the report is
    consistent with conservation of mass. (4 marks)
15. The project reacts baking soda with vinegar in an open beaker and measures a mass loss.
    If 8.4 g of baking soda reacts with excess vinegar, what mass of CO₂ should escape?
    (NaHCO₃ 84.0, CO₂ 44.0.) (3 marks)
16. Cumulative: Unit 02 taught that different elements have different atomic masses. Explain
    how that fact, combined with the mole, lets a chemist weigh out equal *numbers* of atoms
    of two different elements. (3 marks)

Total: 56 marks. Scoring guide: 48–56 secure; 40–47 mostly secure; 32–39 partial — reteach the
weakest part (A: Lessons 01–02; B: Lesson 03; C: Lesson 04; D: Lesson 05; E: Lessons 01/06)
before Unit 05; below 32: full reteach of the weakest two parts plus a second attempt at the
project prediction.
