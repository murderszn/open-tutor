# Formative Quiz — Week 16 (Lessons 01–03)

Learner-facing. Solutions are in `../answer-key.md` (adult only). Use the unit's rounded molar
masses (g/mol): H 1.0, C 12.0, N 14.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Mg 24.3, Cl 35.5, Ca 40.1.
Avogadro's number: 6.02 × 10²³ particles/mol. Show working for numerical items.

1. Classify the reaction 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O and state the pattern you matched. (2 marks)
2. A student heats a colourless liquid, sees bubbles, and claims a chemical change. Give one
   reason the claim is weak and one extra observation that would strengthen it. (2 marks)
3. Balance: Mg + O₂ → MgO. Show the atom inventory. (3 marks)
4. Balance: Al + HCl → AlCl₃ + H₂. Show the atom inventory. (3 marks)
5. How many moles are in 18.0 g of water? (2 marks)
6. How many molecules are in 0.500 mol of carbon dioxide? (2 marks)
7. What is the mass of 2.00 mol of sodium chloride? (NaCl 58.5 g/mol.) (2 marks)
8. True or false, with a reason: "If an equation will not balance, you may change a subscript
   to make the counts match." (2 marks)
9. Compute the molar mass of calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂. Show the sum. (2 marks)
10. In one or two sentences, explain why coefficients in a balanced equation are mole ratios
    and not mass ratios. (2 marks)

Total: 22 marks. Scoring guide: 19–22 secure; 15–18 mostly secure with one reteach topic;
below 15: reteach the weakest lesson (by item group: 1–2 Lesson 01, 3–4 Lesson 02, 5–10
Lesson 03) before Session 11.
