# Practice Sets — Unit 04

Learner-facing. Solutions are in `answer-key.md` (adult only). Use the unit's rounded molar
masses (g/mol): H 1.0, C 12.0, N 14.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Mg 24.3, Al 27.0, S 32.1, Cl 35.5,
K 39.1, Ca 40.1, Fe 55.8, Cu 63.5, Zn 65.4. Avogadro's number: 6.02 × 10²³ particles/mol.
Show working and units for every numerical item.

## Set A — Evidence and reaction types (Lesson 01)

For each item, name the type (synthesis, decomposition, single replacement, double replacement,
combustion) and state the evidence or pattern you matched.

1. 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
2. 2KClO₃ → 2KCl + 3O₂
3. Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂
4. AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃ (a white solid appears)
5. C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O
6. CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂
7. A colourless liquid is heated and bubbles form, but the bubbles stop when heating stops and
   the liquid is unchanged. Chemical or physical change? Why?
8. Iron nails left outside become coated in a flaky orange-brown solid. Chemical or physical?
   Name two lines of evidence.

## Set B — Balancing with inventories (Lesson 02)

Write the skeleton, the before/after inventory, and the balanced equation.

1. H₂ + N₂ → NH₃
2. Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃
3. C₂H₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O
4. Na + H₂O → NaOH + H₂
5. Fe₂O₃ + CO → Fe + CO₂
6. BaCl₂ + Na₂SO₄ → BaSO₄ + NaCl
7. C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O
8. NH₃ + O₂ → NO + H₂O
9. Ca(OH)₂ + H₃PO₄ → Ca₃(PO₄)₂ + H₂O
10. KClO₃ → KCl + O₂

## Set C — The mole (Lesson 03)

1. How many moles are in 40.0 g of NaOH? (Na 23.0, O 16.0, H 1.0.)
2. What is the mass of 0.750 mol of CaCO₃? (Ca 40.1, C 12.0, O 16.0.)
3. How many molecules are in 2.00 mol of CO₂?
4. How many moles are in 1.20 × 10²⁴ atoms of iron?
5. What is the mass of 3.01 × 10²³ formula units of NaCl? (NaCl 58.5.)
6. Which weighs more: 1.00 mol of CO₂ or 1.00 mol of C₃H₈? By how many grams?
7. A sample contains 0.250 mol of water. How many hydrogen atoms does it contain?
8. How many moles of oxygen atoms are in 2.00 mol of Al₂O₃?

## Set D — Stoichiometry (Lesson 04)

Use the 4-step setup for every problem: (1) balance, (2) convert to moles, (3) mole ratio,
(4) convert to the wanted unit. Show all four steps.

1. 2Al + 3Cl₂ → 2AlCl₃. What mass of AlCl₃ forms from 5.4 g of Al? (Al 27.0, AlCl₃ 133.5.)
2. 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl. What mass of NaCl forms from 11.5 g of Na? (Na 23.0, NaCl 58.5.)
3. CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂. What mass of CO₂ is released from 20.0 g of CaCO₃? (CaCO₃ 100.1, CO₂ 44.0.)
4. 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. What mass of O₂ is needed to react completely with 6.0 g of H₂?
   (H₂ 2.0, O₂ 32.0.) Check your answer by conservation of mass.
5. Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. What mass of iron forms from 32.0 g of Fe₂O₃?
   (Fe₂O₃ 159.6, Fe 55.8.)

## Set E — Limiting reactants and percent yield (Lesson 05)

Show the divide-by-coefficient step for every limiting-reactant item.

1. 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. You have 8.0 g H₂ and 32.0 g O₂. Which limits? What mass of H₂O forms?
   What mass of excess reactant remains?
2. N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃. You have 28.0 g N₂ and 12.0 g H₂. Which limits? What mass of NH₃ forms?
3. Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂. You have 13.0 g Zn and 14.6 g HCl. Which limits? What mass of H₂
   forms? (Zn 65.4, HCl 36.5, H₂ 2.0.)
4. Theoretical yield 25.0 g, actual yield 21.5 g. Percent yield?
5. A student reports a percent yield of 104%. Give two possible causes, and state whether the
   reaction was "better than perfect."

## Set F — Cumulative mixed problems (Lesson 06)

1. Balance: C₄H₁₀ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O. Then: what mass of CO₂ forms from 11.6 g of butane, C₄H₁₀?
   (C₄H₁₀ 58.0, CO₂ 44.0.)
2. Balance: Mg + HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂. Then: you have 4.8 g Mg and 7.3 g HCl. Which limits, and what
   mass of H₂ forms? (Mg 24.3, HCl 36.5, H₂ 2.0.)
3. A reaction's theoretical yield is 40.3 g and the actual yield is 36.0 g. Percent yield?
   Name one loss-type reason and one measurement-type reason the yield could be below 100%.
4. Explain, with a balanced equation and a mass check, why 12.0 g of carbon burning in excess
   oxygen makes 44.0 g of carbon dioxide — more mass than the fuel alone. (C 12.0, O₂ 32.0,
   CO₂ 44.0.)
5. A student writes: "2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O proves mass is conserved because both sides have 3
   molecules." Is the reasoning correct? Explain what is conserved and what is not.
