## Teaching

### Conservation as a check, not a slogan

"Mass is conserved" is only useful if you use it. Every stoichiometry answer in this unit can be
checked: total mass of reactants consumed equals total mass of products formed, and leftover
excess plus products equals total starting mass. If the check fails, the answer is wrong even if
the arithmetic looks tidy — because the arithmetic was done on the wrong setup.

Worked example — a full check. *2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO. A student starts with 24.3 g Mg and excess O₂
and reports 40.3 g MgO. Is the answer right?*

Moles Mg = 24.3 ÷ 24.3 = 1.00 mol. Ratio MgO : Mg = 2 : 2 = 1 : 1, so 1.00 mol MgO = 40.3 g.
Check: oxygen consumed = 0.500 mol × 32.0 g/mol = 16.0 g. Mass in = 24.3 + 16.0 = 40.3 g. Mass
out = 40.3 g. The check passes. Note what the check needed: the *consumed* oxygen, not "excess
oxygen" as an unmeasured amount. A check that ignores a reactant is not a check.

### Error analysis: three flawed solutions

Diagnose each. For each one, name the faulty step (balance / convert / ratio / convert-back /
limiting logic) and give the corrected value. Full solutions are in the answer key; try before
looking.

**Flaw A.** *Problem: what mass of CO₂ forms from 8.0 g of CH₄ (CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O)?
Student's working: 8.0 g CH₄ ÷ 44.0 g/mol = 0.182 mol; × (1 ÷ 1) = 0.182 mol CO₂; × 44.0 = 8.0 g.*

**Flaw B.** *Problem: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O; 10.0 g H₂ and 64.0 g O₂. Student's working: "Hydrogen is
10.0 g and oxygen is 64.0 g, so hydrogen limits. Water formed = 10.0 g + 64.0 g = 74.0 g."*

**Flaw C.** *Problem: percent yield where theoretical = 20.2 g and actual = 17.4 g. Student's
working: (20.2 ÷ 17.4) × 100% = 116% — "a great yield, better than perfect!"*

### Cumulative review: Units 01–04

Map each unit to the skill it gave this unit:

- Unit 01 (measurement): significant figures in every reported mass (project masses to ±0.1 g,
  the balance's precision); data tables with units.
- Unit 02 (atoms): why molar masses differ — heavier atoms mean fewer atoms per gram (Lesson
  03's exit check).
- Unit 03 (bonding): why formulas have fixed subscripts — the identity Lesson 02's balancing
  rules protect.
- Unit 04 (this unit): equations, moles, ratios, yields — the toolkit Unit 05 (gases) and Unit
  06 (solutions) will assume.

## Exit check

1. A student reports 50.0 g of product from 20.0 g of reactants with nothing left over. Without
   seeing the working, what do you know, and what is the most likely error? (Answer: mass
   cannot be created; the most likely error is using a wrong molar mass or ratio that inflated
   the product, or forgetting that a second reactant's mass must be counted.)
2. State your Session 19 reteach list: the two problem types you most need to practise.

## Supports and extension

- **Support:** if error analysis feels hard, cover the student's answer and solve the problem
  yourself first. Comparing two solutions is easier than hunting for a flaw in a vacuum.
- **Extension:** design a problem that *looks* balanced but is not (for example, one with a
  subscript changed to force a match) and explain why the counts matching is not enough — the
  formulas must also be the right substances. Swap with the adult and diagnose each other's.
- **Alternate response mode:** the cumulative-review paragraph may be dictated.
