  gas — particles clustered low with short motion trails. Right box: hot gas — the same
  particles spread through the whole box with long motion trails."

- **Text-only alternative:** in Lesson 01, directly under the figure — both boxes hold the
  same number of identical blue particles; the left box shows slow motion (short trails), the

- **Factual review (inspected 2026-11-12):** the image is clean — two identical boxes, the
  same particle count in each, no text, no labels, no mangled details. One deliberate
  limitation, recorded here and taught in Lesson 01: the cool gas is drawn clustered at the
  bottom of its box, as if cold gas settled like a liquid. That is an exaggeration, not a
  fact — a real gas fills its container at any temperature; cool particles are spread through
  the box and simply move more slowly and hit the walls less often. The lesson makes the
  critique explicit ("what does the picture get wrong?") so the exaggeration becomes a
  teaching point rather than a smuggled misconception. No lab observation is fabricated: the
  picture is a model of particle motion, not a record of an experiment.
