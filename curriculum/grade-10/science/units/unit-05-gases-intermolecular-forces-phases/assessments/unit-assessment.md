# Unit Assessment — Unit 05: Gases, Intermolecular Forces, and Phases

**50 points · ~50 minutes · adult-supervised.** Show the set-up for every calculation. Use the
data table in the unit README (vapor pressures at 25 °C; boiling points) where a question asks
for it. Solutions are in `answer-key.md` (adult only).

## Part A — Concepts (12 pts, 2 pts each)

1. A gas is heated from 200 K to 400 K at fixed volume. What happens to the average kinetic
   energy of its particles, and what happens to its pressure? Give the factor for each.
2. Explain why gas-law calculations must use kelvin, in one or two sentences.
3. What is the difference between an intramolecular bond and an intermolecular force? Give one
   example of each.
4. Define vapor pressure in your own words, and state how it relates to boiling point.
5. A heating curve is flat at 100 °C while water boils. Where does the added heat energy go
   during the flat segment?
6. What is the triple point of a substance?

## Part B — Gas-law calculations (18 pts)

7. (3 pts) A gas at 0.500 atm occupies 8.00 L. At constant temperature it is compressed until
   the pressure is 2.00 atm. Find the new volume.
8. (3 pts) A gas occupies 1.20 L at 250 K and constant pressure. It is heated to 400 K. Find
   the new volume.
9. (4 pts) A gas occupies 2.50 L at 1.50 atm and 300 K. It is compressed to 1.25 L and the
   pressure rises to 3.00 atm. Find the new temperature.
10. (4 pts) What pressure does 0.750 mol of gas exert in a 4.00 L container at 350 K? Use
    PV = nRT with R = 0.08206 L·atm/(mol·K).
11. (4 pts) What mass of nitrogen gas, N₂ (28.02 g/mol), occupies 8.96 L at STP?

## Part C — Intermolecular forces and data (10 pts)

12. (3 pts) Rank CH₄, NH₃, and H₂O by increasing boiling point, and justify the order with
    the intermolecular forces in each.
13. (3 pts) At 25 °C, acetone has a vapor pressure of 229.5 mmHg and water has a vapor
    pressure of 23.8 mmHg. Which liquid evaporates faster, and what does the difference tell
    you about their relative intermolecular-force strengths? Cite the numbers.
14. (2 pts) Predict whether hexane or water evaporates faster, and explain why hexane is
    never used at the bench in this unit even though its data appears in the table.
15. (2 pts) Hydrogen bonding occurs only when hydrogen is bonded directly to certain elements.
    Name those three elements, and explain why hydrogen bonded to carbon does not count.

## Part D — Phases (6 pts)

16. (2 pts) How much energy is needed to boil 25.0 g of water at 100 °C? (Heat of
    vaporization 2,260 J/g.)
17. (2 pts) Dry ice (solid CO₂) sublimes at −78.5 °C at 1 atm and never melts at normal
    pressure. Explain why, using the triple point of CO₂ (−56.6 °C at 5.11 atm).
18. (2 pts) Water boils at 100 °C at sea level but at about 90 °C on a high mountain. Explain
    why, using vapor pressure and outside pressure.

## Part E — Investigation analysis (4 pts)

19. (4 pts) **Data task (fictional practice data).** Three liquids were tested for drying time
    (mean of three trials each): acetone 1.6 min, ethanol 6.0 min, water 14.8 min.
    (a) Infer the relative intermolecular-force strength order, with reasoning (2 pts).
    (b) Name one limit of what this investigation can prove (2 pts).
