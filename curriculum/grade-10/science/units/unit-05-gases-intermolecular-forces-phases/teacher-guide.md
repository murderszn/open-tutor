# Teacher Guide — Unit 05

Adult-only. This guide covers per-lesson misconceptions, reteaching moves, safety checks, and
scoring guidance for the quiz, assessment, practice sets, and project. Student-facing questions
are never answered here in the learner's presence — work from `answer-key.md` when marking.

## Pacing and placement

- Weeks 19–22, 20 sessions. Sessions 8 (syringe/simulation investigation) and 15–17 (project)
  are the load-bearing sessions: protect them from being squeezed out by the written lessons.
- If the learner has not done Unit 03, do not start Lesson 04 until polarity is secure. A
  20-minute polarity re-teach (polar vs. nonpolar from electronegativity difference and shape)
  is cheaper than a confused Lesson 04.
- If the learner has not done Unit 04, Lesson 03's mole work will stall: pre-teach molar mass
  and the 22.4 L/mol conversion in a single warm-up session before Lesson 03.

## Lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — Kinetic molecular theory

- **Core move:** the image critique. The learner must say, in their own words, that a gas
  fills its container at any temperature and that the left panel's clustering is an exaggeration.
  If they only repeat "hot is faster," the critique has not landed — ask "where would the cold
  particles actually be?"
- **Misconception:** "pressure comes from particles pushing each other." Correct to
  "particles hitting the walls" every time it appears.
- **Reteach:** if kelvin conversion is shaky, spend 10 minutes converting a dozen Celsius
  values before Lesson 02. Every later calculation depends on it.

### Lesson 02 — Pressure, temperature, diffusion

- **Core move:** connect each pressure change to the collision picture before any formula is
  used. A learner who can say "fewer particles, fewer hits" will not mix up the gas laws later.
- **Misconception:** Graham's law inverted (heavier = faster). Drill "lighter is faster, and
  the ratio puts the *other* gas's mass on top."
- **Reteach:** pressure-unit conversions — require the conversion factor written as a fraction
  that cancels the old unit, every time, until it is automatic.

### Lesson 03 — Gas laws

- **Core move:** the before/after table (P₁, V₁, T₁ / P₂, V₂, T₂) before choosing a law.
  Learners who pick laws by matching letters fail; learners who fill the table succeed.
- **Misconception:** Celsius in proportions. Require the kelvin conversion line in every
  solution; mark any solution that skips it as incomplete even if the number is right.
- **Reteach:** if combined-law problems fail, drop back to one-variable-at-a-time reasoning
  (Worked example 3's factor-by-factor check) before reintroducing the full formula.
- **Session 8:** the syringe investigation (or PhET "Gas Properties") is the concrete anchor
  for Boyle's law. If the syringe is unavailable, the simulation's "hold temperature constant,
  change volume" task is the substitute — do not skip the session.

### Lesson 04 — Intermolecular forces

- **Core move:** the two-column sort — inside the molecule (bonds) vs. between molecules
  (forces). Every new term goes on the card before anything is written about it.
- **Misconception:** hydrogen bonding treated as intramolecular, or as "any H near O/N/F."
  Stress both halves: H must be *bonded to* N, O, or F, and the attraction is *between*
  molecules.
- **Reteach:** the size trap (Worked example 2) — rank by polarity *and* size. If a learner
  ranks CCl₄ below CH₃OH, walk through molar masses (154 vs. 32 g/mol) explicitly.

### Lesson 05 — Phases, heating curves, phase diagrams

- **Core move:** the energy-accounting calculation. Learners who compute the 309 kJ total
  themselves stop thinking a plateau means "nothing happening."
- **Misconception:** evaporation = boiling. Contrast surface-only/any-temperature vs.
  throughout/one-temperature in every lesson until it sticks.
- **Reteach:** if phase diagrams confuse, start with the water diagram only, trace one path
  (constant temperature, falling pressure) with a finger, and narrate the phase at each step
  before introducing CO₂.

### Lesson 06 — Bulk-properties investigation (HS-PS1-3)

- **Safety is non-negotiable.** Goggles on, adult dispenses ethanol and acetone, small drops,
  ventilated room, no flames/heat/sparks, bottles capped immediately. If any condition cannot
  be met, use the simulation version — same objectives, no penalty.
- **Core move:** three trials and a mean before any inference. A single trial is not evidence.
- **Misconception:** overclaiming ("this proves hydrogen bonding"). The rubric grades the
  inference-vs-proof distinction explicitly — insist the learner writes the limit in the same
  paragraph as the inference.

## Scoring guidance

- **Week-20 quiz (25 pts):** a calculation earns full marks only with the law named, the
  substitution with units, and a check line. A correct number with no set-up earns at most
  half. Q2 (open response) earns full marks for any one postulate stated correctly plus a
  matching observation.
- **Unit assessment (50 pts):** Part A open responses — accept any correct paraphrase; the
  answer key gives the required content points. Part E (investigation analysis) — the inference
  (2 pts) requires both the order *and* the reasoning; the limit (2 pts) requires naming
  something the investigation cannot establish (a measured force, a proof of the force type,
  or a causal mechanism beyond ranking).
- **Practice sets:** use them formatively. A set below 70% means reteach that lesson before
  moving on — do not push forward on a shaky gas-law foundation, because Lessons 04–06 reuse
  the same proportional reasoning.
- **Project (40 pts):** see the rubric in `project.md`. The safety row is a gate: a report
  that skipped adult approval or safety steps cannot pass. "Strong" reports cite all three
  bulk properties (evaporation, boiling point, vapor pressure) in the cross-check.

## Common reteaching sequences

- **Gas-law confusion:** back to Lesson 02's collision picture → one law at a time with the
  before/after table → combined law as "do the factors one at a time."
- **IMF ranking errors:** rebuild from structure — draw the molecule, mark polarity, check for
  O–H/N–H/F–H, then check size — in that order, every time.
- **Heating-curve confusion:** rebuild as a table (segment, temperature range, energy) first,
  then sketch the curve from the table.
- **Investigation write-ups:** model one inference paragraph aloud (claim → evidence → limit)
  before the learner writes theirs.

## Standards and sources (adult reference)

  against the California Department of Education's NGSS pages (see resource pack). The unit
  teaches IMF names; the standard's clarification governs what the investigation must infer
  (relative strength from bulk evidence), not the vocabulary.
- Vapor-pressure and boiling-point values come from NIST Chemistry WebBook Antoine-equation
  fits (water 23.8 mmHg/100 °C; ethanol 59.0/78.3 °C; acetone 229.5/56.3 °C; n-hexane
  151.3/68.7 °C; diethyl ether about 520/34.6 °C, all at 25 °C and 1 atm as applicable).
- Gas laws and the ideal gas law are foundational chemistry content, not a separate NGSS
  performance expectation at this band; the unit states this openly in the README.
