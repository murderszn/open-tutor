# Practice — Unit 05

Work each set after its lesson. Show the set-up for every calculation: the law or formula, the
substitution with units, and a one-line reasonableness check. Solutions are in `answer-key.md`
(adult only). Point values are for the adult's tracking, not a grade.

## Set A — Kinetic molecular theory (Lesson 01)

1. Convert to kelvin: (a) 0 °C, (b) 25 °C, (c) −40 °C, (d) 100 °C. (4 pts)
2. A gas in a rigid box is heated from 300 K to 600 K. What happens to (a) the average kinetic
   energy of its particles and (b) its pressure? Give the factor for each. (2 pts)
3. **Error analysis.** A student writes: "When a gas is cooled to 0 °C, its particles stop
   moving." Identify the mistake and write the correct statement. (2 pts)
4. Two identical boxes hold the same gas. Box X is at 200 K, Box Y at 400 K. Which box's
   particles have the greater average kinetic energy, and by what factor? (2 pts)
5. Which KMT postulate explains why a gas can be compressed into a much smaller volume, and
   why can a liquid not be compressed nearly as much? (2 pts)
6. **Application.** An aerosol can warns "Do not heat above 50 °C." Explain the warning with
   kinetic molecular theory in two or three sentences. (2 pts)

## Set B — Pressure, diffusion, and the simple gas laws (Lessons 02–03)

1. Convert 1.50 atm to (a) kPa and (b) mmHg. (2 pts)
2. A rigid tank holds gas at 2.00 atm and 300 K. It is heated to 450 K. What is the new
   pressure? (2 pts)
3. At the same temperature, how much faster does hydrogen gas (H₂, 2.02 g/mol) diffuse than
   oxygen gas (O₂, 32.00 g/mol)? Show the Graham's-law set-up. (2 pts)
4. How much faster does helium (4.00 g/mol) effuse than nitrogen (N₂, 28.02 g/mol) at the
   same temperature? (2 pts)
5. A gas at 2.00 atm occupies 4.00 L. At constant temperature it expands to 8.00 L. Find the
   new pressure. (2 pts)
6. A gas occupies 500 mL at 300 K and constant pressure. It is heated to 600 K. Find the new
   volume. (2 pts)
7. A rigid tank at 250 K holds gas at 100 kPa. It is heated to 500 K. Find the new pressure
   in kPa. (2 pts)
8. A gas occupies 2.00 L at 1.00 atm and 300 K. It expands to 4.00 L and the pressure falls
   to 0.50 atm. What is the new temperature? (2 pts)

## Set C — Ideal gas law and STP (Lesson 03)

1. How many moles of gas occupy 44.8 L at STP? (1 pt)
2. What mass of carbon dioxide, CO₂ (44.01 g/mol), occupies 3.36 L at STP? (2 pts)
3. What pressure does 2.00 mol of gas exert in a 10.0 L tank at 300 K? Use PV = nRT with
   R = 0.08206 L·atm/(mol·K). (2 pts)
4. What volume does 0.500 mol of gas occupy at 2.00 atm and 400 K? (2 pts)
5. Calculate the density of oxygen gas at STP in g/L (molar mass 32.00 g/mol; molar volume
   22.4 L/mol). (2 pts)
6. **Error analysis.** A student uses Charles's law to claim that heating a gas from 20 °C
   to 40 °C doubles its volume. Show the correct calculation and explain the mistake. (3 pts)

## Set D — Intermolecular forces (Lesson 04)

1. For each substance, name all intermolecular forces present: (a) N₂, (b) HBr, (c) H₂O,
   (d) CH₄. (4 pts)
2. Rank these by increasing boiling point and justify the order in one sentence each:
   Ne, HF, H₂O. (Reference boiling points, rounded: Ne about −246 °C; HF about 20 °C;
   H₂O 100 °C.) (3 pts)
3. Explain why iodine (I₂) is a solid at room temperature while fluorine (F₂) is a gas, even
   though both are nonpolar diatomic molecules. (2 pts)
4. 1-Propanol (CH₃CH₂CH₂OH) and diethyl ether (CH₃CH₂OCH₂CH₃) are isomers, both C₃H₈O.
   1-Propanol boils at 97 °C; diethyl ether boils at 35 °C. Explain the difference. (2 pts)
5. At 25 °C, which has the higher vapor pressure — water (23.8 mmHg) or acetone
   (229.5 mmHg)? Explain what that tells you about their relative IMF strengths. (2 pts)
6. **Explain your reasoning.** A drop of water forms a tall dome on waxed paper, while a drop
   of hexane spreads flat. What does each observation imply about the forces at work? (3 pts)

## Set E — Phases, heating curves, phase diagrams (Lesson 05)

1. Using Figure 2 in Lesson 05 (or `assets/heating-curve-water.svg`), name the temperature of
   each plateau and state what is happening at each one. (2 pts)
2. How much energy warms 50.0 g of liquid water from 20 °C to 80 °C? (Specific heat of water
   4.18 J/g·°C.) (2 pts)
3. How much energy melts 25.0 g of ice at 0 °C? (Heat of fusion 334 J/g.) (1 pt)
4. Reproduce the Lesson 05 total: how much energy takes 100 g of ice at −20 °C to steam at
   120 °C? Show each of the five steps. (5 pts)
5. Carbon dioxide at 1 atm: dry ice sublimes at −78.5 °C. A sample of CO₂ is held at 1 atm
   and warmed from −90 °C to −70 °C. Describe its phase at each end of the warming, and say
   whether it melts at any point. (2 pts)
6. Explain why water boils at a lower temperature on a mountaintop than at sea level, using
   vapor pressure and outside pressure. (2 pts)

## Set F — Investigation and mixed review (Lesson 06 + whole unit)

1. In the Lesson 06 evaporation investigation, name three controlled variables and, for each,
   say what would go wrong if it were not controlled. (3 pts)
2. **Data task (fictional practice data).** Three trials give these mean drying times: acetone
   1.6 min, ethanol 6.0 min, water 14.8 min. (a) Rank the liquids by evaporation rate.
   (b) Infer the relative IMF strength order. (c) Check the inference against the boiling
   points in the unit README (acetone 56.3 °C, ethanol 78.3 °C, water 100 °C) — does the
   reference data agree? (3 pts)
3. **Error analysis.** A group runs one trial with unequal drop sizes and reports that ethanol
   dried faster than acetone. Give two reasons the adult should not accept their conclusion
   that ethanol has weaker IMFs. (2 pts)
4. A weather balloon holds 3.00 L of helium at 1.00 atm and 300 K. At altitude the pressure
   is 0.80 atm and the temperature is 280 K. Find the new volume with the combined gas law.
   (2 pts)
5. Rank these by increasing boiling point and justify in one sentence each: chloromethane
   CH₃Cl, methanol CH₃OH, carbon tetrachloride CCl₄. (Reference boiling points, rounded:
   CH₃Cl about −24 °C; CH₃OH about 65 °C; CCl₄ about 77 °C.) (3 pts)
6. **Explain your reasoning.** A heating curve is flat at 100 °C while water boils, even
   though heat is still being added. Where does the added energy go, and why does the
   temperature not rise? (2 pts)
