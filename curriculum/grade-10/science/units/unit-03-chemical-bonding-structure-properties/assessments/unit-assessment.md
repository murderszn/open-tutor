# Unit Assessment — Unit 03: Chemical Bonding, Structure, and Properties (50 points)

Adult-supervised. The Lesson 01 electronegativity table is provided. Solutions and scoring
guidance are in `answer-key.md` (adult only). Show all calculations; a bare label without a
ΔEN calculation earns at most half marks in Part A.

## Part A — Bond classification (10 points, 2 points each)

For each pair, show the ΔEN calculation, name the bond type, and give the electron story in
one sentence.

1. Na–Br (Na 0.93, Br 2.96)
2. C–O (C 2.55, O 3.44)
3. H–H (H 2.20)
4. Ca–O (Ca 1.00, O 3.44)
5. S–H (S 2.58, H 2.20)

## Part B — Lewis structures, shape, and polarity (16 points, 4 points each)

1. Draw the Lewis structure for nitrogen gas, N₂, showing all lone pairs and the total
   valence-electron count.
2. Draw the Lewis structure for the hydroxide ion, OH⁻, with brackets and charge, and state
   its total valence-electron count.
3. For water, H₂O: count the electron domains on oxygen, name the shape, give the
   approximate bond angle, and state polar or nonpolar with the symmetry reason.
4. For carbon dioxide, CO₂: count the electron domains on carbon, name the shape, and explain
   why the molecule is nonpolar even though its bonds are polar.

## Part C — Bulk properties and investigation (14 points)

1. **(4 pts)** Rank these from lowest to highest melting point, with a reason for each step:
   CO₂, H₂O, NaCl, diamond. Use Lesson 04's reference data.
2. **(4 pts)** Explain why graphite conducts electricity but diamond does not, even though
   both are pure carbon. Your answer must use their structures.
3. **(6 pts)** Unknown V is a white solid. It does not conduct as a solid. It dissolves
   readily in water, and its solution conducts electricity. It melts at a high temperature.
   (a) What bond type is most likely? (2 pts) (b) Give the two strongest pieces of evidence
   for it. (2 pts) (c) Name one alternative type and rule it out with a reason, and name one
   further test that would strengthen your identification. (2 pts)

## Part D — Designed materials (10 points)

Choose ONE of the two provided structures below and write a paragraph (6–10 sentences)
explaining why it works for its job, using the Lesson 05 three-step pattern (structure →
forces → function). Your paragraph must name the forces involved and must not claim any
property the structure does not explain.

- **Option 1 — Copper wire (provided structure):** positive copper ions in a regular
  arrangement, valence electrons delocalised through the whole piece. Job: carrying electric
  current in a cable that must bend around corners.
- **Option 2 — Polyethylene bag (provided structure):** very long carbon chains with hydrogen
  atoms attached, tangled together; strong covalent bonds along each chain, much weaker
  forces between chains. Job: a bag that folds small, stretches a little without tearing, and
  keeps water out.

Scoring for Part D: structure correctly described (3 pts); forces correctly named (3 pts);
function connected to forces, not just asserted (3 pts); no unsupported property claimed
(1 pt).
