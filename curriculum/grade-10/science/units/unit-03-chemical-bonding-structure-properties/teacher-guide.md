# Teacher Guide — Unit 03 (Adult Only)

## How to use this unit

Teach the six lessons in order; Lessons 01–03 build the tools (classification, Lewis
structures, shape/polarity) and Lessons 04–06 apply them (bulk properties, designed
materials, exceptions). Do not skip Lesson 06: the unit assessment tests unknown
identification and the HF exception directly, and learners who only memorised the ΔEN
cut-offs fail those items. Give feedback on every written product before the next session
begins, as the pacing plan assumes.

## Per-lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — Why atoms bond

- Watch for: "molecules of salt" (correct every time, kindly, until it disappears);
  metallic bonding described as "no bonding" (correct: the sea *is* the bond);
  ΔEN cut-offs treated as exact laws (ask: "what happens at 1.69 versus 1.71?").
- If the image activity stalls, ask: "Are the particles in separate groups, or one big
  pattern?" before naming any bond type. Colour must never be the cue — the activity is
  designed so that structure alone decides.
- Reteach: if ΔEN subtraction is shaky, drill five subtractions to two decimal places
  before re-attempting classification; the chemistry is not the problem in that case.

### Lesson 02 — Lewis structures

- Watch for: double-counting (a line drawn plus its two electrons as dots); hydrogen given
  an octet; brackets/charges forgotten on ions; a short central atom "fixed" by adding
  electrons instead of making multiple bonds.
- Reteach move: cover the learner's structure and ask for the valence total first. If the
  total is wrong, the structure cannot be right — fix the count, then redraw.
- The sulfate extension is enrichment only. Do not assess expanded octets in this unit.

### Lesson 03 — Shape and polarity

- Watch for: double bonds counted as two domains; NH₃ called tetrahedral; bond polarity
  equated with molecular polarity; angles quoted as exact.
- Reteach move: have the learner say the domain sentence aloud ("two bonds, two lone pairs,
  four domains, bent") before naming any shape. The sentence prevents most errors.
- PhET "Molecule Shapes": preview it yourself first, and set the task (build H₂O, CO₂,
  NH₃; toggle lone pairs) rather than leaving free exploration, which drifts off-task.

### Lesson 04 — Bulk properties

- Watch for: "melting breaks bonds" without saying which bonds; all ionic solids assumed
  soluble (MgO and CaF₂ are the counterexamples used in this unit — note CaF₂ is only
  slightly soluble, and accept that answer); conductivity attributed only to electrons
  (ions carry charge in solution too); graphite treated as a rule-breaker rather than a
  layer-structure consequence.
- The quiz follows this lesson (Session 10). If quiz question 4 or 5 is weak, reteach with
  the Lesson 04 worked examples before Session 11 — Lesson 05 assumes the bulk-properties
  table is secure.

### Lesson 05 — Designed materials

- Watch for: "strength" used without saying which strength (hardness, durability, tensile
  strength, flexibility); HS-PS2-6 answers that name a material without explaining it from
  a provided structure; semiconductor explanations that drift into quantitative band theory
  (stop at the qualitative doping model).
- Scoring Part D of the unit assessment: a paragraph with no forces earns at most 3/10,
  however fluent it reads. Insist on the three steps.

### Lesson 06 — Unknowns and exceptions

- Watch for: bare classifications with no uncertainty where evidence conflicts; vocabulary
  corrections without reasoning corrections ("I meant formula unit" with no lattice reason
  is not a correction).
- Reteach move: for any unknown, require the sentence "Ruled out ___ because ___" before
  accepting an identification. That sentence is the assessed skill in Part C of the unit
  assessment.

## Project supervision checklist

- [ ] Conductivity tester assembled and checked by the adult; no warm wires; 9 V battery
  only, never mains.
- [ ] Goggles worn for crushing and vigorous stirring.
- [ ] Samples limited to the listed solids; no unknown household substance tested.
- [ ] "Off" results recorded as data, not as failures.
- [ ] Report distinguishes the learner's observations from Lesson 04 reference data.
- [ ] Peer review completed and one section revised before submission.

## Reteaching and readiness

- **Ready to move on from Lessons 01–03:** the learner classifies 8/8 Set A items with
  calculations shown, draws H₂O, CO₂, NH₃, CH₄ correctly, and states one shape/polarity
  pair with symmetry reasoning.
- **Ready for the project:** the learner predicts conductivity and solubility for NaCl,
  sugar, and copper correctly from bond type alone.
- **Ready for the unit assessment:** the learner corrects all five Lesson 06 error
  statements with reasons and identifies a fictional unknown with a ruled-out alternative.
- **If the quiz (Session 10) is below 18/25:** reteach Lessons 01–04 with the worked
  examples, then re-attempt the quiz items that were missed (not a new quiz) before
  Session 11.

## Scoring notes

- Quiz and assessment rubrics are in `answer-key.md`. Partial credit: a correct ΔEN
  calculation with a wrong label earns half marks in Part A; a correct shape with no
  polarity reasoning earns half marks in Part B items 3–4.
- The project rubric (20 points) rewards honest disagreement with the expected pattern
  over silent "correction" of data. Invented data scores zero on the data-table criterion.
- Do not claim this unit's assessments are standardised or validated. They are
  teacher-made checks against the unit's stated objectives.
