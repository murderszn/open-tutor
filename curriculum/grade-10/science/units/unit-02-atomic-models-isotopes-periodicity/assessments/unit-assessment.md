# Unit Assessment — Unit 02: Atomic Models, Isotopes, and Periodicity

**50 points · ~50 minutes · calculator allowed · periodic table allowed · adult-supervised**

Answer in full sentences where the question asks for a reason or explanation. A prediction
without a justification earns at most half marks for that item. Show all working for
calculations, including the fraction conversion and sanity checks for averages.

## Part A — Particles and notation (10 pts)

- **A1. (3 pts)** An atom has 19 protons, 20 neutrons, and 18 electrons. Give its element,
  mass number, charge, and full isotope notation.
- **A2. (3 pts)** How many protons, neutrons, and electrons are in ³¹₁₅P³⁻? Show how you got
  each number.
- **A3. (2 pts)** Which of these pairs are isotopes of each other: (a) ¹²₆C and ¹³₆C;
  (b) ¹⁴₆C and ¹⁴₇N? Give a reason for each answer.
- **A4. (2 pts)** Explain why changing the number of neutrons in an atom does not change
  which element it is, but changing the number of protons does.

## Part B — Models and evidence (10 pts)

- **B1. (4 pts)** Name the five atomic models in order, and for the Thomson → Rutherford
  step, state the experiment, the observation, and the conclusion.
- **B2. (3 pts)** Use the gold-foil illustration in Lesson 02 (or its text-only alternative):
  describe the pattern and explain what it implies about the structure of the atom.
- **B3. (3 pts)** Bohr's model explained hydrogen's spectrum but was replaced by the
  quantum-mechanical model. State one thing Bohr's model could not explain, and state what
  replaced fixed orbits in the modern model.

## Part C — Configurations and valence (10 pts)

- **C1. (4 pts)** Write the electron configuration of silicon (Z = 14), state its valence
  electrons, and give its group.
- **C2. (4 pts)** A neutral atom has configuration 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶4s¹. Identify the element,
  give its group, and predict the ion it most likely forms, with a reason.
- **C3. (2 pts)** Predict the ion formed by sulfur, with a one-sentence valence reason.

## Part D — Isotopes and average atomic mass (10 pts)

- **D1. (4 pts)** Chlorine (exact masses): chlorine-35, 34.96885 u, 75.76%; chlorine-37,
  36.96590 u, 24.24%. Calculate the average atomic mass, showing all steps and checks.
- **D2. (4 pts)** Bromine (exact masses): bromine-79, 78.9183 u, 50.69%; bromine-81,
  80.9163 u, 49.31%. Calculate the average atomic mass, showing all steps and checks.
- **D3. (2 pts)** Explain why the periodic table's atomic mass for an element is usually not
  a whole number, and why it is still the right value to use for a large sample of that
  element.

## Part E — Trends, predictions, and nuclear change (10 pts)

- **E1. (3 pts)** Rank F, Cl, Br by atomic radius (smallest to largest) and give the reason
  for the ranking.
- **E2. (4 pts)** Selenium is in group 16, period 4. Predict: (a) its valence-electron count,
  (b) its likely ion, (c) whether it is a metal, nonmetal, or metalloid, and (d) one bond
  type it forms, with a named partner element. Justify each part from electron patterns.
- **E3. (3 pts)** Complete and check these two nuclear equations (state the missing product
  and show both the mass-number and atomic-number checks): (a) ²³⁸₉₂U → ²³⁴₉₀Th + ___ ;
  (b) ¹⁴₆C → ¹⁴₇N + ___ . Name the decay type in each case.
