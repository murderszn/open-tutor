# Answer Key — Unit 02 (Adult Only)

Solutions were solved independently from the questions and reconciled with the lessons. Every
calculation shows its steps; where a question accepts more than one phrasing, the key gives the
required content, not the only acceptable wording. Do not hand this file to the learner.

## Lesson items

### Lesson 01

- **Guided 1:** 17 protons → chlorine; A = 17 + 18 = 35; charge = 17 − 18 = −1; **³⁵₁₇Cl⁻**.
- **Guided 2:** ⁴⁰₂₀Ca²⁺ has 20 protons, 20 neutrons (40 − 20), 18 electrons (20 − 2).
- **Guided 3:** Yes, isotopes (same element, different neutron count); yes, same element
  (both have 8 protons — oxygen).
- **Application (iodine tracer):** 53 protons, 78 neutrons → A = 131; neutral atom has 53
  electrons; **¹³¹₅₃I**. It is still iodine because 53 protons define iodine, whatever the
  neutron count.
- **Exit check:** neutrons = A − Z. 9 protons, 10 electrons → charge = 9 − 10 = −1, an anion.

### Lesson 02

- **Guided 1:** Order: Thomson, Rutherford, Bohr, quantum-mechanical. (Dalton precedes all.)
- **Guided 2:** Error: passing through proves mostly *empty* space, not solidity. Correction:
  "Most particles pass through because atoms are mostly empty space; the few that bounce back
  reveal a small, dense, positively charged nucleus."
- **Guided 3:** Cathode-ray particles smaller than atoms, from every material tested,
  contradict "indivisible" — a model must explain all reliable evidence, not just combining
  ratios.
- **Application paragraph (acceptable content):** the surprise is the back-scattered particles;
  Thomson's model predicted only slight deflections; the observation was rare large-angle
  deflection and bounce-back; the fitting model is Rutherford's nuclear atom, because a
  concentrated positive nucleus explains both the mostly-empty passage and the rare violent
  repulsion.

### Lesson 03

- **Guided 1:** Fluorine: **1s²2s²2p⁵**; valence = 2 + 5 = **7** (whole outer level, not just 2p⁵).
- **Guided 2:** Potassium: **1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶4s¹**; valence **1**.
- **Guided 3:** Group 16, period 3 → **sulfur**, 6 valence electrons.
- **Independent (argon):** 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶ gives a full outer level (8 valence electrons), so
  argon has no tendency to gain, lose, or share electrons — hence unreactive.
- **Exit check:** Nitrogen: **1s²2s²2p³**, valence **5**; it gains 3 electrons → **N³⁻**.

### Lesson 04

- **Guided 1 (carbon):** fractions 0.9893, 0.0107 (sum 1.0000 ✓); (12 × 0.9893) + (13 × 0.0107)
  = 11.8716 + 0.1391 = 12.0107 u → **12.01 u**; between 12 and 13, near 12 ✓.
- **Guided 2 (copper):** fractions 0.6915, 0.3085 (sum 1.0000 ✓); (62.9296 × 0.6915) +
  (64.9278 × 0.3085) = 43.5158 + 20.0302 = 63.5460 u → **63.55 u**; between 63 and 65 ✓.
- **Guided 3:** 38.2 u cannot be right because (a) it lies outside the 35–37 u range of
  chlorine's isotopes, and (b) any weighted average must lie between the smallest and largest
  isotope masses.
- **Independent (neon):** neon-20 dominates, so the average sits just above 20; the heavier
  isotopes (neon-21, neon-22) are present only in small proportions, pulling the average up
  slightly but nowhere near 21.
- **Extension (backwards chlorine):** 34.96885x + 36.96590(1 − x) = 35.453 → x = (36.96590 −
  35.453) / (36.96590 − 34.96885) = 1.51290 / 1.99705 = 0.7576 → **75.8%** chlorine-35.

### Lesson 05

- **Guided 1:** Calcium: group 2, period 4, s-block, alkaline earth metal.
- **Guided 2:** Silicon is the metalloid (on the staircase); sulfur is a nonmetal, sodium a metal.
- **Guided 3:** Group 17, period 4 → **bromine**; 7 valence electrons; likely ion **Br⁻**.
- **Independent:** the table groups by valence count because valence electrons determine
  bonding behavior; color or density groupings would not predict chemistry.
- **Exit check:** 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p¹ → aluminum; group 13, period 3, p-block, metal
  (the dataset's category is "post-transition metal").

### Lesson 06

- **Guided 1:** Radius: F < Cl < Br (each step down adds an energy level, outer electrons
  farther out). Electronegativity: Br < Cl < F (pull on shared electrons weakens with distance
  and shielding down the group).
- **Guided 2:** Aluminum: group 13, 3 valence electrons, loses all three to reach the full
  level below → **Al³⁺**.
- **Guided 3:** 238 = 234 + 4 ✓; 92 = 90 + 2 ✓.
- **Independent paragraph (acceptable content):** burning carbon rearranges atoms (C + O₂ →
  CO₂) with nuclei unchanged, so carbon stays carbon; carbon-14 decay changes a neutron to a
  proton, so carbon becomes nitrogen — a new element — with far more energy per atom. A
  process that changes the element cannot be a chemical reaction.
- **Exit check:** (a) radius decreases across a period, increases down a group; (b) sulfur:
  6 valence electrons, gains 2 → **S²⁻**; (c) nuclear change can change the element and
  releases far more energy per atom than chemical change.

## Practice Sets

### Set A

- **A1:** Sodium; A = 23; charge +1; **²³₁₁Na⁺**.
- **A2:** Chlorine; A = 37; charge 0; **³⁷₁₇Cl**.
- **A3:** Oxygen; A = 18; charge −2; **¹⁸₈O²⁻**.
- **A4:** Iron; A = 56; charge 0; **⁵⁶₂₆Fe**.
- **A5:** Bromine; A = 81; charge −1; **⁸¹₃₅Br⁻**.
- **A6:** Sam's error: isotopes must share the *same* element (same proton count). Carbon-14
  (6 protons) and nitrogen-14 (7 protons) are different elements with the same mass number —
  that makes them isobars, not isotopes.

### Set B

- **B1:** Cathode-ray particles were smaller than atoms and identical whatever material was
  tested, contradicting Dalton's indivisible spheres — atoms must have smaller parts.
- **B2:** (1) Most particles pass through → atoms are mostly empty space. (2) A few bounce
  back → the atom's positive charge and most of its mass sit in a tiny, dense nucleus.
- **B3:** (a) Most particles continue straight to the screen behind the foil. (b) A few
  deflect widely; a very few return toward the source. (c) Straight passage implies empty
  space; wide deflection and bounce-back imply a concentrated, positively charged nucleus.
- **B4:** Bohr's fixed circular orbits were replaced by **orbitals** — probability regions
  where an electron is likely to be found. "Where the electron is" becomes a likelihood, not
  a track.
- **B5:** Dalton → Thomson (cathode rays: subatomic electrons) → Rutherford (gold foil:
  nuclear atom) → Bohr (hydrogen spectrum: quantized levels) → quantum-mechanical (spectra
  of larger atoms: orbitals).
- **B6:** Error: passing through shows emptiness, not solidity. Correction: "Most particles
  pass through because atoms are mostly empty space."

### Set C

- **C2:** Oxygen: **1s²2s²2p⁴**; valence = 2 + 4 = **6**.
- **C3:** Calcium: **1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶4s²**; valence **2**.
- **C4:** Chlorine: **1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁵**; valence **7**; likely ion **Cl⁻** (gains 1 to complete
  its outer level).
- **C5:** Electrons: 2 + 2 + 6 + 2 + 3 = 15 → **phosphorus**; group 15; valence **5**.
- **C6:** Potassium → **K⁺** (group 1, loses its 1 valence electron). Sulfur → **S²⁻**

  35.48 u — the 0.03 u difference is the rounding error of the integer approximation.

### Set E

- **E1:** (a) sodium: group 1, period 3, s-block, alkali metal. (b) selenium: group 16,
  period 4, p-block, nonmetal. (c) calcium: group 2, period 4, s-block, alkaline earth metal.
  (d) bromine: group 17, period 4, p-block, halogen (nonmetal). (e) neon: group 18, period 2,
  p-block, noble gas.
- **E2:** Radius: Li < Na < K (new energy level each step down group 1). Ionization energy:
  K < Na < Li (outer electron farther and better shielded down the group, so easier to
  remove).
- **E3:** Electronegativity: Br < Cl < F (pull on shared electrons weakens with distance and
  shielding down group 17).
- **E4:** Mg → **Mg²⁺** (loses 2 valence electrons); O → **O²⁻** (gains 2); charges balance
  1:1 → **MgO**; metal + nonmetal → ionic.
- **E5:** Al → **Al³⁺** (loses 3 valence electrons); O → **O²⁻** (gains 2). Balance: 2 × (+3)
  = +6 and 3 × (−2) = −6 → **Al₂O₃**.
- **E6:** Bromine; 7 valence electrons; likely ion **Br⁻** (gains 1 to complete its outer
  level); ionic bond with potassium — metal + nonmetal, electron transfer; nonmetal.

### Set F

- **F1:** ²⁴₁₂Mg²⁺: 12 protons, 12 neutrons (24 − 12), 10 electrons (12 − 2).
- **F2:** Sulfur: **1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁴**; valence **6**; likely ion **S²⁻** (gains 2 to complete
  its outer level).
- **F3 (lithium):** fractions 0.0759, 0.9241 (sum 1.0000 ✓); (6.015 × 0.0759) + (7.016 ×
  0.9241) = 0.4565 + 6.4835 = 6.9400 u → **6.94 u**; between 6 and 7 ✓.
- **F4:** Correction: atomic radius *decreases* across a period. More protons pull the
  electrons of the same level inward more strongly; adding electrons to the same level does
  not push the atom outward.
- **F5:** (a) Chemical change; element unchanged (carbon stays carbon). (b) Nuclear change;
  element changes (carbon-14 becomes nitrogen-14).
- **F6 (acceptable content):** potassium has the lower first ionization energy. Both are
  period 4, so their outer electrons sit in level 4, but potassium (Z = 19) has a smaller
  nuclear charge than calcium (Z = 20) pulling on that level, and potassium's single 4s
  electron is lost to reach the full level below, while calcium's two 4s electrons are held
  more tightly. Full marks require the comparison of nuclear charge and the valence
  difference, not just "potassium is group 1."

  dense, positively charged center.

  it kept electrons on fixed circular tracks). The modern model replaces fixed orbits with
  **orbitals** — probability regions for finding an electron.
