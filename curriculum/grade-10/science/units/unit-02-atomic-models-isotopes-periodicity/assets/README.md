## `gold-foil-experiment.png` (generated raster illustration — required generated asset)

- **Prompt (verbatim):** "A clear educational illustration of the Rutherford gold-foil experiment,
  stylized and diagram-like, with no text, no labels, no letters, and no numbers anywhere in the
  image: on the left a small source emits a horizontal beam of tiny glowing particles toward a
  very thin vertical gold foil sheet in the center; most particles pass straight through the foil
  to a curved detector screen on the right where they make small bright flashes in a straight
  line, while a few particles bounce off at wide angles, one of them nearly straight back toward
  the source. Clean flat colors, dark background, simple shapes, suitable for a high-school
  chemistry lesson."
- **Tool:** Muse built-in image generation (`media.generate_image`), 2026-10-21.
- **File:** PNG, 1920 × 1280.
- **Used in:** Lesson 02, Activity A (gold-foil image question). The lesson embeds the image with
  the caption and alt text below, and supplies a text-only alternative carrying the same
  information, so a learner who cannot see the image can still answer.
- **Caption:** "Stylized illustration of the Rutherford gold-foil experiment (not to scale, not a
  photograph). Most particles pass straight through the foil; a few deflect at wide angles, and
  a very few bounce back toward the source."
- **Alt text:** "Diagram of the gold-foil experiment: a particle source on the left fires a beam
  at a thin vertical gold foil in the center. Most particles continue straight to a curved
  detector screen on the right; a few deflect at wide angles, and one returns toward the source."
- **Text-only alternative (in Lesson 02):** "A beam of alpha particles is fired at a thin gold
  foil. Almost all particles pass straight through and strike the detector screen directly
  behind the foil. A small number deflect at large angles. A very small number — roughly one
  in thousands in the real experiment — bounce almost straight back toward the source."
- **Factual review (2026-10-21, inspected before linking):** The image contains no text, labels,
  or numbers, so it cannot carry a wrong label. Its pattern matches the historical result: most
  particles undeflected, a minority deflected, a tiny minority back-scattered. Limitations, stated
  in the lesson: the foil, particles, and detector are not to scale; in the real apparatus the
  detector screen surrounded the foil rather than sitting only behind it, and the deflected
  particles did not all travel to a single screen. This is a **stylized reconstruction, not a
  photograph and not experimental data**. It must not be presented as a record of an actual run.
- **Known limitations:** The two wide-angle paths in the image stop before reaching the detector
  screen; the lesson's text alternative corrects this by stating that deflected particles were
  detected at angles around the foil.

## `electron-shells-sodium-chlorine.svg` (deterministic diagram — supplement, not the generated asset)

- **How made:** Generated with a short Python script (circles placed by computed angles), not by
  image generation. Electron counts are exact: sodium 2, 8, 1; chlorine 2, 8, 7.
- **Used in:** Lesson 03, to read shell diagrams alongside written electron configurations.
- **Caption:** "Bohr-style shell diagrams for sodium (2, 8, 1) and chlorine (2, 8, 7). The shells
  are a model and are not to scale; electrons do not travel in fixed circular paths."
- **Alt text:** "Two shell diagrams. Sodium: a central nucleus with 2 electrons in the first
  shell, 8 in the second, and 1 in the third. Chlorine: 2 electrons in the first shell, 8 in the
  second, and 7 in the third."
- **Factual review:** Shell counts match the electron configurations 1s²2s²2p⁶3s¹ (sodium) and
  1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁵ (chlorine). The diagram is labeled as a model in both the caption and Lesson 03,
  because the quantum-mechanical model replaces fixed shells with probability regions
  (orbitals).
