## Lesson 01

**Guided 1.** (1) Stop and step back from the bench (stop rule, rule 7). (2) Tell the adult
immediately (rules 5, 7). (3) Let the adult handle the broken glass; check your hands for cuts
(rule 5). Order matters: protect yourself first, then report, then let the adult clean.

**Guided 2.** (a) Digital balance, grams — tare with the empty container first. (b) Graduated
cylinder, milliliters — water displacement. (c) Thermometer, °C — at start and after one hour.

**Guided 3.** Base: meter, second, mole. Liter, gram, milliliter are not base units: the liter is
a derived/accepted volume unit (1 L = 1 dm³); the gram is 1/1000 of the base kilogram; the
milliliter is 1/1000 of a liter.

**Independent 4.** Accept: the beaker's printed scale is approximate (often ±5% or worse) because
beakers are manufactured for holding and pouring, not measuring. A density calculation divides by
the volume, so a 5% volume error becomes a 5% density error — enough to misidentify a metal.
2–3 sentences stating this earn full credit.

**Independent 5.** Rules triggered: (1) eye protection — goggles on; (6)/(heating) — the adult
must preview and approve the heating procedure and supervise it. Accept any two correct rules
with the adult-approval requirement stated.

**Independent 6.** Base: kelvin, ampere, candela. Derived: g/mL, cm³. °C: a convenient scale, but
the SI base unit of temperature is the kelvin (0 °C = 273.15 K by definition).

**Exit 1.** Stop, step back, tell the adult — no penalty for stopping. **Exit 2.** The graduated
cylinder; never the beaker. **Exit 3.** Any three of: meter (m), kilogram (kg), second (s),
kelvin (K), mole (mol), ampere (A), candela (cd).

## Lesson 02

**Guided 1.** 1.8 kg × (1000 g / 1 kg) = 1800 g. Sanity: grams are smaller than kilograms, so the
number gets bigger — 1800 > 1.8 ✓.

**Guided 2.** 2.50 L × (1000 mL / 1 L) = 2500 mL.

**Guided 3.** −10.0 + 273.15 = 263.15 → **263.2 K** (tenths place). No conversion factor exists
because °C and K differ by a zero *shift*, not a scaling — the degrees are the same size, so you
add/subtract 273.15.

**Independent 4.** 0.045 kg × (1000 g / 1 kg) × (1000 mg / 1 g) = 45,000 mg = **4.5 × 10⁴ mg**.

**Independent 5.** 5.00 min/km × (60 s / 1 min) = 300 s/km; 300 s per 1000 m = **0.300 s/m**
(3 s.f.).

**Independent 6.** 0.125 L × (1000 mL / 1 L) = 125 mL = **125 cm³** (1 mL = 1 cm³).

**Independent 7.** Error: the factor is upside down — 300 cm × (100 cm / 1 m) gives cm²/m, which
does not cancel to meters, and 30,000 m is absurd (a football field is ~100 m). Correct:
300 cm × (1 m / 100 cm) = **3.00 m**. Rule: arrange each factor so the unwanted unit cancels,
leaving only the wanted unit.

**Application.** 0.250 L × (1000 mL / 1 L) = **250 mL**; 0.020 kg × (1000 g / 1 kg) = **20 g**.

**Exit 1.** 0.750 L. **Exit 2.** 15.5 + 273.15 = 288.65 → **288.7 K**. **Exit 3.** The setup is
correct when unwanted units cancel and only the wanted unit remains.

**Extension.** 1 km/h = 1000 m / 3600 s = 0.2777… m/s → **0.2778 m/s** (4 s.f.); 100 km/h × 0.2778
= **27.78 m/s**.

## Lesson 03

**Guided 1.** Exactly on the 24 mL mark → **24.0 mL**. The decimal digit is still required: the
marks are every 1 mL, so the estimated digit is the 0.1 mL place, and "24.0" claims tenths-place
precision honestly.

**Guided 2.** Not the same. 13.2 g = 3 s.f. (tenths); 13.20 g = 4 s.f. (hundredths) — the second
claims ten times finer precision.

**Guided 3.** Parallax error. Read at eye level with the bottom of the meniscus instead.

**Independent 4.** One quarter from 41 toward 42 = 41.25 → rounded to the estimated 0.1 mL place:
**41.3 mL**.

**Independent 5.** 132.3 mm − 50.0 mm = 82.3 mm = **8.23 cm**.

**Independent 6.** Two errors: (a) reading the *top* edge where water meets glass instead of the
*bottom* of the meniscus; (b) "closer to the top" is not a method — the correct reading estimates
one digit beyond the marks, so halfway between 24 and 25 is **24.5 mL**.

**Exit 1.** Record one estimated digit beyond the instrument's smallest marking. **Exit 2.** The
bottom of the meniscus; reading at an angle causes parallax error. **Exit 3.** Yes, legal: marks
every 0.5 mL → the estimated digit is the 0.05 mL (hundredths) place, so 17.25 mL is exactly one
estimated digit beyond the markings.

## Lesson 04

**Guided 1.** (a) 0.00450 m → **3** (leading zeros not significant; trailing zero after decimal
is). (b) 25.0 °C → **3**. (c) 1000 mL → ambiguous; write 1.000 × 10³ mL (4) or 1 × 10³ mL (1).
(d) 52.48 g → **4**.

**Guided 2.** 8.2 + 1.75 = 9.95 → fewest decimal places is tenths → **10.0 cm** (the trailing zero
holds the tenths place).

**Guided 3.** 13.20 ÷ 4.0 = 3.30 → fewest figures is 2 (from 4.0) → **3.3 g/mL**.

**Independent 4.** The 3 is a count — exact, infinite s.f. Answer keeps the measurement's 3
figures: **73.5 mL**.

**Independent 5.** 50.00 − 12.5 = 37.50 → tenths place → **37.5 mL**.

**Independent 6.** 27.35 ÷ 10.2 = 2.68137… → 3 s.f. → **2.68 g/mL**.

**Independent 7.** Error: "250" with no decimal is ambiguous — it does not show 3 s.f. Since
1000 mL/L is exact, the answer keeps the measurement's 3 figures and must be written
**2.50 × 10² mL**.

**Exit 1.** 2. **Exit 2.** Each rounding discards information; rounding mid-stream lets the error
propagate into later steps. **Exit 3.** 20.05 + 3.1 = 23.15 → tenths → **23.2 g**.

**Extension.** Rounding at each step of Worked example 5: 66.5 × 12.0 = 798.0 → 798 mm² (same
here); a longer chain (e.g., three multiplications) typically drifts in the last kept digit —
accept any correctly computed demonstration of drift ≥ 1 in the final digit, or a correct
argument that this particular chain is stable.

## Lesson 05

**Guided 1.** (a) Accurate and precise. (b) Precise, not accurate — systematic error. (c) Accurate
on average, not precise — random error. (d) Neither accurate nor precise.

**Guided 2.** Sum = 107.7; mean = 107.7 ÷ 5 = 21.54 → **21.5 °C**; range = 21.7 − 21.4 =
**0.3 °C**; report **21.5 ± 0.3 °C**.

**Guided 3.** Systematic — every reading is shifted +5.00 g. Fix: zero/tare the balance with
nothing on the pan (and check it sits on a hard, level surface).

**Independent 4.** Accept: random error scatters both high and low, so averaging cancels the
fluctuations; systematic error pushes every reading the same way, so the average is shifted by the
same bias — no amount of repetition removes it. 3–4 sentences to this effect.

**Independent 5.** Two problems: (a) a range requires at least two readings — one reading gives no
spread, so ± 0.0 is fabricated; (b) "± 0.0" claims perfect precision no real instrument has.

**Independent 6.** |122 − 125| ÷ 125 × 100% = 3 ÷ 125 × 100% = **2.4%**. Systematic example: the
thermometer reads 3 °C low (miscalibrated). Random example: reading the melting column at
slightly different eye levels across trials. Accept any plausible pair.

**Independent 7.** The flaw: precision describes agreement among readings; accuracy describes
closeness to truth. The 62.3-kg scale was perfectly precise and 4.2 kg wrong — tight clustering
proves consistency, not correctness.

**Exit 1.** Accuracy: closeness to the true value. Precision: closeness of repeats to each other.
**Exit 2.** Sum = 51.6; mean = 10.32 → **10.3 mL**; range = 10.5 − 10.2 = **0.3 mL**; report
**10.3 ± 0.3 mL**. **Exit 3.** The ± states the spread of honest repetition — it tells the reader
exactly how much is *not* known, which is what makes the known part trustworthy.

## Lesson 06

**Guided 1.** Trial densities: 41.20 ÷ 22.4 = 1.83928… → **1.84**; 41.20 ÷ 22.5 = 1.83111… →
**1.83**; 41.20 ÷ 22.3 = 1.84753… → **1.85 g/mL** (each 3 s.f.). Mean = 5.52 ÷ 3 = **1.84**;
range = 1.85 − 1.83 = **0.02**; report **1.84 ± 0.02 g/mL**.

**Guided 2.** (a) Systematic — mass too low → density too low. (b) Systematic — volume too high →
density too low. (c) Random (single reading) — mass too high → that trial's density too high.

**Guided 3.** (a) Conclusion. (b) Procedure. (c) Calculation.

**Independent 4.** Accept: Question — "What is the density of cooking oil?" Procedure sketch —
tare balance with dry cylinder; pour oil to ~50 mL; read meniscus bottom at eye level; record
mass; density = mass ÷ volume; safety note — wipe spills (slip hazard), goggles on. 5–8 steps
with one safety note earns full credit.

**Independent 5.** Rewrite: "The density is consistent with aluminum." "Proved" is wrong because
proof belongs to mathematics/logic — a measurement supports a conclusion within its uncertainty
but never eliminates every alternative.

**Independent 6.** Percent error = |2.9 − 2.70| ÷ 2.70 × 100% = 0.20 ÷ 2.70 × 100% = 7.407…% →
**7.4%**. Likely cause pushing the result up: under-reading the volume (e.g., reading the top of
the meniscus instead of the bottom, or water splashed out during insertion lowering the final
level). Mechanism: density = mass ÷ volume — a smaller measured volume (denominator) gives a
larger quotient.

**Exit 1.** Question, Procedure, Data, Calculation, Error analysis, Conclusion. **Exit 2.** Data
holds the raw readings with units; Calculation shows the arithmetic with unrounded values, rounded
answers, and the rules used. **Exit 3.** The direction tells you whether the error can actually
explain the observed gap — an error pushing the result *down* cannot explain a result that came
out *high*.

## Practice Sets

**A1.** Stop, step back, tell the adult (rule 7); the adult handles the broken glass (rule 5);
check hands for cuts. **A2.** (a) Balance, grams. (b) Graduated cylinder, mL. (c) Thermometer, °C.
**A3.** The SI base unit of volume is the cubic meter (m³). The liter (1 dm³) and milliliter are
accepted non-SI units — neither is a base unit. **A4.** Base: second, mole, kelvin. Derived:
pascal, g/cm³, newton. Accept any one correct explanation (e.g., "the newton is kg·m/s², built
from base units, so it is derived").

**B1.** 450 mL × (1 L / 1000 mL) = **0.450 L**. Sanity: liters are bigger than milliliters, so the
number should shrink — 0.450 < 450 ✓. **B2.** **2200 g**. **B3.** 37.0 + 273.15 = 310.15 →
**310.2 K**. **B4.** 42.2 km × (1000 m / 1 km) × (1000 mm / 1 m) = 42,200,000 mm =
**4.22 × 10⁷ mm** (3 s.f.). **B5.** The factor is upside down: 5.00 m × (1 m / 100 cm) leaves
m²/cm. Correct: 5.00 m × (100 cm / 1 m) = **500 cm**. The units reveal it: only a setup that
cancels to the wanted unit can be right.

**C1.** **24.5 mL** — marks every 1 mL, so the estimated digit is the 0.1 mL place. **C2.** 97.5 −
10.0 = 87.5 mm = **8.75 cm**. **C3.** **36.8 °C**. 36.80 °C would claim 0.01 °C precision — one
digit beyond what 1 °C marks allow (0.1 °C). **C4.** Errors: (a) reading from standing height =
parallax — read at eye level; (b) reading the top edge where water touches glass instead of the
bottom of the meniscus.

**D1.** (a) 3; (b) 4; (c) 3 (6.02 × 10²³). **D2.** 15.5 + 2.25 = 17.75 → tenths → **17.8 cm**.
**D3.** 4.50 ÷ 1.5 = 3.0 → 2 s.f. → **3.0 g/mL**. **D4.** 8.25 × 4.0 = 33.0 → 2 s.f. → **33 cm²**;
33 cm² × (100 mm² / 1 cm²) = 3300 mm² → **3.3 × 10³ mm²** (2 s.f.). **D5.** The reasoning is
wrong: addition rounds by *decimal places*, not figure counts. Correct rule: fewest decimal
places (tenths) → 15.6 mL. (The digits coincide here by luck.)

**E1.** Mean = 50.0 ÷ 5 = **10.0 mL**; range = 10.2 − 9.8 = **0.4 mL**; report **10.0 ± 0.4 mL**.
**E2.** Precise, not accurate; systematic error. Lab analogue: an untared balance, or consistent
parallax — accept any real analogue. **E3.** |1.15 − 1.00| ÷ 1.00 × 100% = **15%**. Upward
systematic: untared balance (mass reads high → density high), or consistently reading the top of
the meniscus (volume reads low → density high). Accept either with the mechanism. **E4.**
Disagree: averaging cancels random scatter but a systematic bias shifts every reading the same
way, so the mean carries the full bias. Twenty biased trials give a precisely wrong answer.

**Retrieval spiral.** (1) One estimated digit beyond the smallest marking. (2) 750 mL.
(3) 4 (2, 0, 0, 5 — zeros between nonzero digits count). (4) An error that shifts every
measurement the same direction.
