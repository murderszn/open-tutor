# Project — The Density Investigation: What Is This Metal?

**Sessions 13–14 (bench work) + Session 17 (report writing) · Adult-supervised**

## Driving question

*What is the density of the unknown metal sample, and which known metal is it consistent with?*

You will determine the density of a metal sample by water displacement, report it with an honest
uncertainty, identify the metal by comparing with reference densities, and write a six-section lab
report. This project is the evidence for unit objectives 1, 4, 5, and 6.

## Materials

- Safety goggles (worn for the entire investigation)
- Digital balance (0.01 g readability) or triple-beam balance; weigh boat or small dry beaker
- 100 mL graduated cylinder (1 mL graduations)
- Metal sample: an aluminum object (e.g., a thick aluminum bolt or bar, ~25–30 g). The adult
  confirms the sample is aluminum from its source and keeps the identity private until reports are
  submitted — the reference table below is your identification tool, not a spoiler.
- Tap water, paper towels, notebook
- **Simulation alternative:** if bench materials or supervision are unavailable, run the PhET
  "Density" simulation (resource pack) for three trials on the aluminum block and complete the
  identical report from sim data. The adult previews the sim first.

Reference densities at room temperature (for identification): aluminum 2.70 g/mL · iron 7.87 g/mL
· copper 8.96 g/mL · lead 11.3 g/mL · zinc 7.14 g/mL.

## Hazard review (adult and learner, before Session 13)

Water and a metal sample: no heat, no chemicals, no fumes. Hazards are glassware breakage and
slips from spilled water — both controlled by the Lesson 01 contract (inspect glassware, report
breakage, adult handles cleanup, wipe spills at once). The adult approves the written procedure
before any water is poured. If any step cannot be supervised, substitute the simulation.

## Procedure

1. Write the question and a prediction: which metal do you *guess* the sample is, and why?
   (A guess is fine — the data will confirm or refute it.)
2. Inspect the graduated cylinder for chips or cracks. Put on goggles.
3. Tare the balance with the dry container; add the sample; record the mass to the balance's full
   precision. Repeat the mass reading twice more without moving the sample (three mass trials).
4. Pour about 50 mL of water into the cylinder. Read the initial level at the bottom of the
   meniscus, eye level, to 0.1 mL. Record.
5. Tilt the cylinder and slide the sample down the side — no splashing. Read the final level the
   same way. Record.
6. Compute the displaced volume (final − initial) for trial 1.
7. Repeat steps 4–6 twice more (three displacement trials total; re-tare and re-weigh if the
   sample was removed and dried between trials — decide with the adult and note the choice).
8. For each trial: density = mass ÷ volume, rounded to the correct significant figures. Then the
   mean, range, and ± report across trials.
9. Compare the mean density with the reference table; compute the percent error against the
   closest match.
10. Write the six-section lab report (Lesson 06): Question, Procedure, Data, Calculation, Error
    analysis, Conclusion.

## Deliverables

1. **Data table** with units: three trials of mass, initial/final volume, displaced volume, and
   trial density — every reading at instrument precision.
2. **Calculation sheet** showing each trial's unrounded and rounded density with the rounding rule
   named, the mean/range/± report, and the percent-error computation.
3. **Lab report** (six sections) with an error analysis naming at least two specific error
   sources, each classified systematic/random with its likely direction of effect.
4. **Conclusion statement** using honest phrasing ("consistent with," never "proved").

## Rubric (24 points)

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Proficient | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Measurement technique (4) | Every reading at instrument precision; eye-level meniscus reads; tare used; units on all data | One precision slip or one missing unit | Repeated precision errors or parallax evident | Readings lack precision or units throughout |
| Significant figures (4) | Every calculation rounded by the correct rule, named; unrounded values shown | One rounding error or one unnamed rule | Rule confusion (places vs. figures) in ≥2 items | No honest rounding; calculator dumps |
| Uncertainty reporting (4) | Mean, range, and ± report correct; ± interpreted as spread, not mistake | Minor arithmetic slip in mean/range | Range confused with percent error, or ± omitted | No uncertainty reported |
| Error analysis (4) | ≥2 specific sources, each classified with direction of effect; no "human error" filler | 2 sources but one lacks classification or direction | Vague sources ("human error," "mistakes") | Missing or purely generic |
| Conclusion with evidence (4) | Answers the question; cites the ± result and percent error; honest "consistent with" phrasing | Evidence cited but phrasing overclaims ("proved") | Conclusion stated without citing data | Missing or contradicts the data |
| Report structure & safety (4) | All six sections in order; procedure repeatable; safety steps documented | One section thin or safety implied not stated | ≥2 sections missing or procedure not repeatable | No recognizable report structure |

**Scoring bands:** 21–24 exemplary · 16–20 proficient · 11–15 developing (revise the two lowest
criteria and resubmit) · ≤10 beginning (re-do the investigation with the adult).
