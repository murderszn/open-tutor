# Unit Assessment — Chemical Measurement, Lab Methods, and Uncertainty

**50 points · ~60 minutes · Calculator allowed · Adult supervises**

Covers Lessons 01–06 and the density investigation. This is a curriculum-draft assessment, not a
standardized or validated instrument. Show all setups and rounding reasoning; a bare number earns
at most half credit.

## Part A — Concepts (18 points)

1. (3) A scale reads 62.3 kg five mornings in a row; the true mass is 58.1 kg. Classify the
   accuracy and the precision, name the error type, and state one fix.
2. (3) For each, name systematic or random and explain in one sentence: (a) a thermometer that
   reads 2 °C high in every measurement; (b) the last digit of a balance flickering between
   readings; (c) reading every meniscus from above eye level.
3. (3) A beaker's printed scale says 100 mL. Explain in 2–3 sentences why you must not use it to
   measure the water volume for a density calculation.
4. (3) List the SI prefixes from kilo to milli in order with their powers of ten. Then convert
   3.5 cm to millimeters.
5. (3) A learner says reporting "52.49 ± 0.05 g" makes the measurement look bad, so they write
   just "52.49 g." Explain why the ± report is scientifically stronger, in 2–3 sentences.
6. (3) Identify the lab-report section for each sentence: (a) "The balance was tared with the dry
   beaker before each weighing." (b) "27.35 g ÷ 10.2 mL = 2.68137… → 2.68 g/mL (3 s.f.)."
   (c) "The density is consistent with aluminum (2.70 g/mL reference; 0.74% error)."

## Part B — Calculations (22 points)

7. (4) Convert 1.55 kg to milligrams with a chained factor-label setup (two factors). Give the
   answer in scientific notation with the correct significant figures.
8. (4) Convert −15.5 °C to kelvin, rounded correctly. Show the unrounded value first.
9. (4) (a) Multiply 2.50 × 3.2 and round correctly. (b) Add 100.0 mL + 2.55 mL and round
   correctly. Name the rule used in each part.
10. (4) A ruler has 1 mm marks. A rod starts exactly at the 4.0 cm mark and ends 113.5 mm past the
    ruler's zero. What is the rod's length in cm? Show the subtraction.
11. (6) A learner's three density trials (fictional practice data): mass 41.20 g each trial;
    displaced volumes 22.4, 22.5, 22.3 mL. (a) Compute each trial's density with correct
    significant figures (show unrounded values). (b) Give the mean, range, and ± report.
    (c) In one sentence, judge whether the trials show good precision and why.

## Part C — Error analysis and investigation design (10 points)

12. (5) A learner determines an aluminum sample's density as 3.10 g/mL (reference 2.70 g/mL).
    (a) Calculate the percent error. (b) The result is too *high*. Propose the single most likely
    procedural cause and explain the mechanism by which it pushes the density upward.
13. (5) Design a check: *how would you test whether a graduated cylinder's printed scale is
    accurate?* Write (a) a testable question, (b) a 4–6 step procedure sketch using a balance and
    water (hint: 1 mL of water has a mass of ~1.00 g), (c) the independent, dependent, and two
    controlled variables.

## Scoring bands (adult)

- **43–50:** strong — ready for Unit 02.
- **35–42:** proficient — review the two lowest-scoring parts with the teacher guide, then proceed.
- **25–34:** developing — reteach the missed lessons (see teacher guide) and re-attempt the
  corresponding part before Unit 02.
- **Below 25:** beginning — rework the unit's practice sets with the adult before reassessing.
