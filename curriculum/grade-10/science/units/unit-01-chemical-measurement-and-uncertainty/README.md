# Unit 01 — Chemical Measurement, Lab Methods, and Uncertainty

**Grade 10 Science (Chemistry pathway) · Weeks 3–6 · 20 sessions of ~50 minutes**

Unit 01 of [issue #47](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/47). Status: **validated
draft** — delivered for educator review, not merged. This unit is self-contained for one grade-10
learner; a guiding adult supervises all bench work and approves every procedure before it begins.

## Essential question

How do chemists know their measurements can be trusted — and how do they say, honestly, what they
do *not* know?

## What the learner will be able to do

1. Follow the unit's lab-safety contract, select required protective equipment, and choose the
   correct instrument (balance, graduated cylinder, thermometer, ruler) for a stated measurement.
2. Convert fluently among SI units with the factor-label method — including multi-step conversions
   (mL↔L, g↔kg, cm↔m, °C↔K) — with units canceling correctly.
3. Read a graduated-cylinder meniscus, a digital balance, a thermometer, and a metric ruler to each
   instrument's precision, recording the value with correct units.
4. Apply significant-figure rules to measured versus exact numbers and round multi-step results to
   the correct number of significant figures.
5. Distinguish accuracy from precision, compute the range of repeated measurements as a simple
   uncertainty estimate, and report a measurement as *value ± range*.
6. Plan and carry out the unit's density investigation: write a testable question, identify
   variables, record data in tables with units, calculate density with correct significant figures,
   evaluate sources of error, and communicate conclusions with evidence in a structured lab report.

Objectives 1–6 above serve track objectives 1, 2, and 12 of the
[grade-10 science track](../../README.md) and the practices named in
[scope-and-sequence.md](../../scope-and-sequence.md) (planning investigations, analyzing data,
using mathematics).

## Prerequisites

- Grade-9 science: basic lab-safety habits; reading simple data tables; the idea that investigations
  test one variable at a time.
- Grade-9 math: proportional reasoning, scientific notation, rearranging formulas, multi-step
  arithmetic with units.
- The Weeks 1–2 diagnostic (see scope-and-sequence) confirms unit-conversion and graph-reading
  readiness; learners who need it get conversion warm-ups inside Lessons 1–2 rather than a
  separate remediation unit.

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS), high-school (9–12) band. No
HS-PS1 performance expectation names measurement directly; this unit builds the **science and
engineering practices** every HS-PS1 expectation assumes:

- **SEP-3** — plan and carry out investigations (Lessons 1, 5, 6; density investigation).
- **SEP-4** — analyze and interpret data (Lessons 3, 5; practice sets; assessments).
- **SEP-5** — use mathematics and computational thinking (Lessons 2, 4, 6).

Codes and the DCI wording were verified 2026-10-07 against nextgenscience.org during the track
audit. **This crosswalk is a planning reference, not a claim of state adoption, accreditation, or
certification of alignment.**

Unit conversion factors used in this unit (SI definitions; exact by definition): 1 L = 1000 mL,
1 kg = 1000 g, 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm, K = °C + 273.15, density of aluminum 2.70 g/mL (typical
room-temperature reference value; the unit names the value it compares against). The density-lab
datasets in lessons and practice are **fictional practice data**, labeled as such — they are not
the learner's own observations.

## 20-session pacing

Five sessions per week. Lessons are written for ~50 minutes; practice, lab, review, and
assessment sessions fill the unit as shown — six lessons alone do not fill twenty sessions.

| Session | Week | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | Lesson 01 — Lab safety, equipment, and the SI system | Learner can state the safety contract and name the SI base units |
| 2 | 3 | Guided practice: safety scenarios; notebook setup | All scenario judgments correct; notebook sections labeled |
| 3 | 3 | Lesson 02 — SI prefixes and unit conversion | Learner converts mL↔L, g↔kg with correct cancellation |
| 4 | 3 | Practice Set B + conversion error analysis | ≥ 80% on conversions; can explain one worked error |
| 5 | 3 | Review/extension: conversion challenge; reading (resource pack) | Stretch conversions solved; reading notes recorded |
| 6 | 4 | Lesson 03 — Reading instruments to their precision | Reads meniscus, balance, thermometer, ruler to precision |
| 7 | 4 | Bench/simulation practice: measure real objects | Data table with units; every reading at instrument precision |
| 8 | 4 | Lesson 04 — Significant figures | Applies sig-fig rules to +/− and ×/÷ correctly |
| 9 | 4 | Practice Set D + error-analysis task | Explains *why* a rounded answer is wrong, not just the digit |
| 10 | 4 | **Formative quiz** (`assessments/week-04_quiz.md`) + review | Gaps identified; reteach plan set with teacher guide |
| 11 | 5 | Lesson 05 — Accuracy, precision, and uncertainty | Distinguishes the two; computes range; reports ± range |
| 12 | 5 | Data-set practice: repeated-measurement analysis | Mean, range, and ± report correct with units |
| 13 | 5 | Density investigation, session A (project.md) | Question, variables, procedure approved; data table started |
| 14 | 5 | Density investigation, session B | Complete data; density calculated with correct sig figs |
| 15 | 5 | Error discussion: sources of error in the investigation | Two error sources named with their likely direction of effect |
| 16 | 6 | Lesson 06 — The density investigation and lab reports | Can name every lab-report section and its job |
| 17 | 6 | Lab-report writing (project deliverable) | Draft report with all six sections |
| 18 | 6 | Unit review: mixed practice + reading | Learner explains their two weakest objectives aloud |
| 19 | 6 | **Unit assessment** (`assessments/unit-assessment.md`) | Completed under adult supervision |
| 20 | 6 | Flex: catch-up, extension, or PhET Density sim exploration | Open items closed; extension task or sim reflection done |

Retrieval practice is built into Sessions 4, 9, 12, and 18 (each opens with 3 questions from an
earlier lesson). The adult gives feedback on every written product before the next session begins.

## Safety

All bench work is adult-supervised. The density investigation uses only water, a metal sample
(aluminum), and standard glassware — no heat, no chemicals beyond tap water. The adult previews
every procedure, provides safety goggles, and substitutes the PhET Density simulation (see
resource pack) whenever supervision or materials are inadequate. The full safety contract is in
Lesson 01; the investigation's hazard review is in `project.md`.
