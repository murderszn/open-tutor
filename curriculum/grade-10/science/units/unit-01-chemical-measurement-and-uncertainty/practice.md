# Practice Sets A–E

Work guided items with the adult or the lesson open; do independent items closed-book, then check
against `answer-key.md` (adult only — do not open it yourself). Each set mixes computation with at
least one explain-your-reasoning task.

## Set A — Safety, equipment, SI (Lesson 01)

**A1 (guided).** During the density lab you notice a small crack in the graduated cylinder *after*
filling it with water. List your first three actions in order, citing the contract rule number for
each.

**A2.** For each task name the instrument and the unit: (a) the mass of 100 mL of cooking oil;
(b) the volume of water a small rock displaces; (c) whether a metal rod expands when warmed.

**A3 (independent).** A learner says "milliliters and liters are both SI base units because they
both measure volume." Correct the statement: which is the SI base unit of volume, and what are mL
and L relative to it?

**A4 (independent).** Sort into base or derived and explain one of your choices: pascal (Pa),
second, mole, g/cm³, kelvin, newton (N).

## Set B — SI prefixes and unit conversion (Lesson 02)

**B1 (guided).** Convert 450 mL to liters. Write the factor, cancel the units on paper, and add a
one-sentence sanity check.

**B2.** Convert 2.2 kg to grams.

**B3.** Body temperature is 37.0 °C. Convert to kelvin, rounded correctly.

**B4 (independent).** A marathon is 42.2 km. Convert to millimeters in one chained setup (two
factors). Give the answer in scientific notation with correct significant figures.

**B5 (independent, error analysis).** A learner converts 5.00 m to cm and writes 0.05 cm, showing
5.00 m × (1 m / 100 cm). Identify the error, show the correct setup, and state in one sentence how
the units themselves reveal the mistake.

## Set C — Reading instruments (Lesson 03)

**C1 (guided).** Using `assets/meniscus-reading-scale.svg`: state the reading with units and
explain why it has one decimal digit.

**C2.** A ruler has 1 mm marks. A strip starts exactly at the 1.0 cm mark and ends 97.5 mm past
the ruler's zero. What is the strip's length in cm? Show the subtraction.

**C3 (independent).** A thermometer has 1 °C marks; the liquid column stops four-fifths of the way
from 36 °C toward 37 °C. Record the temperature. Then explain why 36.80 °C would be a dishonest
reading here.

**C4 (independent, error analysis).** "I read the cylinder from my normal standing height because
the meniscus looked bigger and easier to see, and I read the very top edge of the water where it
touches the glass." Name the two errors and state the correct technique for each.

## Set D — Significant figures (Lesson 04)

**D1 (guided).** Count the significant figures and say which rule applies: (a) 0.02030 m;
(b) 100.0 s; (c) 6.02 × 10²³.

**D2.** Add 15.5 cm + 2.25 cm. Show the calculator result and the correctly rounded answer.

**D3 (independent).** Divide 4.50 g by 1.5 mL. Give the unrounded value and the correctly rounded
answer with units.

**D4 (independent).** A rectangle measures 8.25 cm by 4.0 cm. Compute the area with the correct
number of significant figures. Then convert the answer to mm² (exact conversion) keeping the
correct figures.

**D5 (independent, error analysis).** A learner computes 12.0 mL + 3.55 mL = 15.55 mL and rounds to
15.6 mL "because 12.0 has 3 significant figures and 3.55 has 3, so the answer keeps 3." The final
digits happen to be right — explain why the *reasoning* is still wrong and state the correct rule.

## Set E — Accuracy, precision, uncertainty (Lesson 05)

**E1 (guided).** Five volume readings (fictional practice data): 9.8, 10.1, 9.9, 10.0, 10.2 mL.
Compute the mean, the range, and the ± report with units.

**E2.** A dartboard shows a tight cluster of darts in the triple-20, but the game was cricket and
the target was the bullseye. Classify the accuracy and precision, name the error type, and give
one real lab analogue of this situation.

**E3 (independent).** A learner's experimental density is 1.15 g/mL; the accepted value is
1.00 g/mL. Calculate the percent error. Then name one systematic error that could push a density
result *upward* and explain the mechanism.

**E4 (independent, explain your reasoning).** "If I repeat a measurement twenty times and average,
any error will average out." Agree or disagree, and justify with the systematic/random distinction
in 3–4 sentences.
