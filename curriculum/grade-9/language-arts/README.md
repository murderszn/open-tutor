# Grade 9 Language Arts

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are
delivered; the eight units are planned, not yet written. See
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained
for one grade-9 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 9 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-9 language arts course following the
"literary reading and composition" pathway: close reading of literature and
informational texts for theme and evidence; narrative structure,
characterization, and author choices; rhetoric, argument, and media literacy;
grammar precision, syntax, vocabulary, and style; narrative and creative
writing; argument essays with source integration and revision; research inquiry
with credibility judgment, citation, and synthesis; and poetry and drama with a
comparative final portfolio. Sessions are about 50 minutes, five per week,
blending reading, discussion, modeled writing, drafting and revision, and
independent written practice that the adult reviews the same day.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Cite strong and thorough textual evidence to support analysis of what a
   literary or informational text says explicitly and of inferences drawn
   from it (RL.9-10.1, RI.9-10.1).
2. Determine a theme or central idea and analyze in detail its development
   over the course of a text, including how it emerges and is shaped and
   refined by specific details; write an objective summary (RL.9-10.2,
   RI.9-10.2).
3. Analyze how complex characters develop, interact with other characters,
   and advance the plot or develop the theme; analyze how an author unfolds
   an analysis or series of ideas or events (RL.9-10.3, RI.9-10.3).
4. Determine the meaning of words and phrases as used in a text, including
   figurative, connotative, and technical meanings, and analyze the
   cumulative impact of specific word choices on meaning and tone
   (RL.9-10.4, RI.9-10.4); interpret figures of speech in context
   (L.9-10.5.a).
5. Analyze how an author's choices concerning text structure, event order,
   and time manipulation (pacing, flashbacks) create effects such as mystery,
   tension, or surprise; analyze how claims are developed and refined by
   particular sentences, paragraphs, or larger portions of a text
   (RL.9-10.5, RI.9-10.5).
6. Analyze the representation of a subject or key scene in two different
   artistic mediums; analyze how an author draws on and transforms source
   material; analyze seminal U.S. documents of historical and literary
   significance (RL.9-10.7, RL.9-10.9, RI.9-10.7, RI.9-10.9).
7. Delineate and evaluate the argument and specific claims in a text,
   assessing whether reasoning is valid and evidence is relevant and
   sufficient, and identifying false statements and fallacious reasoning
   (RI.9-10.8).
8. Write arguments to support claims in an analysis of substantive topics or
   texts, using valid reasoning and relevant and sufficient evidence:
   precise claims distinguished from counterclaims, fair development of both,
   cohesive links, formal style and objective tone, and a supported conclusion
   (W.9-10.1).
9. Write narratives to develop real or imagined experiences or events using
   effective technique, well-chosen details, and well-structured event
   sequences, with precise sensory language and a reflective conclusion
   (W.9-10.3).
10. Conduct short and sustained research projects: gather relevant information
    from multiple authoritative print and digital sources using advanced
    searches, assess each source's usefulness, synthesize multiple sources,
    integrate information selectively without plagiarism, and follow a
    standard citation format (W.9-10.7, W.9-10.8, W.9-10.9).
11. Demonstrate command of grade 9-10 conventions and knowledge of language:
    varied phrases and clauses (participial, prepositional, absolute;
    noun, relative, adverbial) for specific meaning and variety; semicolons
    with conjunctive adverbs; work conformed to a style manual (MLA); and
    accurate general academic and domain-specific vocabulary (L.9-10.1.b,
    L.9-10.2.a, L.9-10.3.a, L.9-10.4, L.9-10.6).
12. Initiate and participate effectively in collaborative discussions using
    textual evidence, propel and challenge ideas, respond thoughtfully to
    diverse perspectives, and present findings clearly and logically with
    strategic digital media in formal English (SL.9-10.1, SL.9-10.4,
    SL.9-10.5, SL.9-10.6); evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and
    use of evidence (SL.9-10.3).

## Verified standards summary

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for English Language Arts,
grades 9-10 band** (Reading: Literature, Reading: Informational Text, Writing,
Speaking & Listening, Language). Each strand's code and description was checked
against [thecorestandards.org](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/)
on 2026-10-06; the per-unit crosswalk is in the scope-and-sequence. The standard
omits RL.9-10.8 at this band, and RL.9-10.10/RI.9-10.10 distinguish end-of-grade-9
from end-of-grade-10 text-complexity expectations. No claim of state adoption,
accreditation, or certification is made; Common Core is used here as a
national reference framework, not a graduation requirement.

## Planned units (in prerequisite order)

- U01 — Literary close reading: theme and evidence
- U02 — Narrative structure, characterization, and author choices
- U03 — Informational rhetoric, argument, and media literacy
- U04 — Grammar precision, syntax, vocabulary, and style
- U05 — Narrative and creative writing workshop
- U06 — Argument essays: source integration and revision
- U07 — Research inquiry: credibility, citation, and synthesis
- U08 — Poetry, drama seminar, and comparative portfolio

Plus four flexible weeks across the year: diagnostic placement (weeks 1-2),
midyear review (week 19), and final review/portfolio (week 36). The full
week-by-week plan is in the [scope-and-sequence](scope-and-sequence.md).

## Guidance for the adult

- Preview every text and video before assigning; this course discusses mature
  literary themes (conflict, power, injustice, mortality). Choose editions and
  excerpts appropriate to the learner, and set expectations before difficult
  passages.
- Keep discussion evidence-based and inquiry-focused. The track studies how
  arguments work; it does not require the learner to adopt any position.
- Spelling and handwriting/keyboarding fluency are supported, not assumed;
  conventions are taught in U04 but reinforced in every unit's editing passes.
- Use original or public-domain texts for readings; never reproduce
  copyrighted books, poems, or worksheets without permission. Link to lawful
  sources (Project Gutenberg, LibriVox, CommonLit, Poetry Foundation, Folger
  Shakespeare Library) rather than copying.
- All units carry accessibility supports (text alternatives for images,
  audiobook options, adjustable pacing, caption checks) and safe, low-cost
  materials. Nothing in this track requires a paid account.
