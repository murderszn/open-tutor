# Unit Assessment — Unit 04 (Grade 9 Language Arts)

**Time:** ~50 minutes, timed · **Total:** 30 points ·
**New material:** every sentence and passage below is new to
this assessment; none is copied from a lesson, practice, or
quiz. Answers are in the separate
[`answer-key.md`](../answer-key.md).

## Part A — Analysis (6 points, 1 each)

For each sentence, name its type and label each clause and each
bracketed phrase.

1. The apprentice, [hoping for a clear night], polished the lens.
2. The lens, [which had cracked in the winter], was replaced at dawn.
3. [After the storm passed], the crew repaired the gallery, and the keeper wrote the report.
4. The report stated [that the bell had rung twice].
5. [The tide having turned], the boats left the harbor.
6. Mara checked the oil, trimmed the wick, and logged the hour.

## Part B — Revision (8 points, 2 each)

Rewrite each item as directed, without changing any fact.

1. Combine into one complex sentence: "The fog lifted. The
   boats returned."
2. Combine into one compound-complex sentence: "The keeper
   lit the lamp. The fog thickened. The boats turned toward
   the light." (Keep the fog as the timing of the lighting.)
3. Repair the dangling modifier: "Repairing the lamp, the
   tools were spread across the table."
4. Repair the comma splice with a semicolon and a conjunctive
   adverb of your choice: "The chart was old, Mara trusted it."

## Part C — Vocabulary paragraph (8 points)

Write a paragraph of five to six sentences about a writer
revising a draft. Use **three** of these words accurately:
*revise, precise, transform, advocate, clarify*. Underline the
three words. Then, for each word, name in one sentence the
strategy (context, word-change pattern, reference, or
verification) a reader could use to confirm its meaning in your
paragraph, and what that strategy would show.

## Part D — Style edit (8 points)

Edit the paragraph below. Keep every fact. Fix: one fragment,
one comma splice, one misplaced modifier, and one vague word.
Then list your four fixes in the form *original → revised →
why* (2 points per fix: 1 for the correct repair, 1 for the
meaning-based reason).

> The editor opened the manuscript. Because she wanted to
> check the citations. She found an error, she corrected it
> with a red pen. She praised the draft with enthusiasm on the
> telephone. The draft was good.
