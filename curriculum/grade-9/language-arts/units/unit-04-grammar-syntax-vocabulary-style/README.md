# Unit 04 — Grammar Precision, Syntax, Vocabulary, and Style

**Grade:** 9 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #44](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/44), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

U01 taught learners to read for theme and evidence, U02 taught them
to name author choices, and U03 taught them to evaluate arguments
and media. U04 turns to the sentence itself. A writer who cannot
control clauses, phrases, punctuation, and word choice cannot fully
control meaning — and a reader who cannot parse a complex sentence
cannot fully evaluate the argument inside it. This unit teaches the
grade 9–10 Language standards explicitly: varied phrases and clauses
(L.9-10.1.b), semicolons with conjunctive adverbs (L.9-10.2.a),
conforming edited work to a style manual (L.9-10.3.a), vocabulary
strategies and figures of speech (L.9-10.4, L.9-10.5), and accurate
academic vocabulary (L.9-10.6). Every skill is then enforced in the
editing passes of U05, U06, and U07 — this unit is the toolkit those
units assume.

The throughline question: **"Does this sentence say exactly what I
mean, in the shape that makes the meaning clearest?"** The unit ends
in a full editing pass over the learner's own earlier writing and a
Style Portfolio that proves, sentence by sentence, that revision is
thinking, not tidying.

## Essential question

How do sentence structure, punctuation, and word choice control
meaning, emphasis, and tone?

## Prerequisites

From U01–U03 and the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2):

- Cite textual evidence and write objective summaries (RL.9-10.1–2)
- Analyze author choices and word-choice impact on tone (RL.9-10.4–5)
- Evaluate an argument's claims and evidence (RI.9-10.8)
- Write a structured paragraph and a short essay with revision
  (W.9-10.1, W.9-10.5)
- Grade-8 conventions: verbals, active/passive voice, semicolons
  and colons at an introductory level, conjunctive adverbs (L.8.1–3)

The unit re-teaches clauses, phrases, and semicolons from the ground
up — no prior mastery is assumed. What is *new* in U04:
**noun, relative, and adverbial clauses as meaning tools** (not just
labels); **participial, prepositional, and absolute phrases** used
for variety and emphasis; **semicolons as a meaning choice** between
closely related independent clauses; **vocabulary as a system**
(context, word-change patterns, reference, verification); **figures
of speech in context** (euphemism, oxymoron); and **syntax as style**
— choosing sentence shapes deliberately for an audience and purpose.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Identify and use varied phrases (noun, verb, adjectival,
   adverbial, participial, prepositional, absolute) and clauses
   (independent, dependent; noun, relative, adverbial) to convey
   specific meanings and add variety and interest to writing
   (L.9-10.1.b).
2. Distinguish simple, compound, complex, and compound-complex
   sentences; diagnose and repair fragments, comma splices, and
   run-ons in their own and others' writing.
3. Use a semicolon (and, where appropriate, a conjunctive adverb)
   to link two or more closely related independent clauses, and
   use semicolons correctly in a complex list (L.9-10.2.a).
4. Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown and multiple-meaning
   words by choosing flexibly among context clues, word-change
   patterns, reference materials, and verification (L.9-10.4.a–d);
   interpret figures of speech in context and analyze their role
   (L.9-10.5.a); acquire and use academic vocabulary accurately
   (L.9-10.6).
5. Apply knowledge of language to make effective choices for
   meaning and style across contexts, and edit work so that it
   conforms to a style manual's guidelines (MLA, as summarized in
   this unit and in Purdue OWL) (L.9-10.3, L.9-10.3.a).
6. Complete a full editing pass over a piece of their own earlier
   writing, documenting each change as a meaning decision, and
   explain why at least three revised sentences are stronger than
   their originals (W.9-10.5).

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for English
Language Arts, grades 9–10 band** (thecorestandards.org
ELA-Literacy, Language strand). Codes and descriptions were
re-verified on 2026-11-02 for this unit (see below); the track
audit's 2026-10-06 check is the earlier verification. No state
adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification is claimed.

| Unit objective | Standard | Official description (abridged paraphrase) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | L.9-10.1.b | Use various types of phrases (noun, verb, adjectival, adverbial, participial, prepositional, absolute) and clauses (independent, dependent; noun, relative, adverbial) to convey specific meanings and add variety and interest to writing or presentations. |
| 2 | L.9-10.1 (in part) | Demonstrate command of the conventions of standard English grammar and usage when writing or speaking. |
| 3 | L.9-10.2.a | Use a semicolon (and perhaps a conjunctive adverb) to link two or more closely related independent clauses. |
| 4 | L.9-10.4.a–d | Determine or clarify meaning of unknown and multiple-meaning words, choosing flexibly from context clues, word-change patterns, reference materials, and verification. |
| 4 | L.9-10.5.a | Interpret figures of speech (e.g., euphemism, oxymoron) in context and analyze their role in the text. |
| 4 | L.9-10.6 | Acquire and use accurately general academic and domain-specific words and phrases sufficient for reading, writing, speaking, and listening at the college and career readiness level. |
| 5 | L.9-10.3.a | Write and edit work so that it conforms to the guidelines in a style manual (e.g., MLA Handbook, Turabian's Manual for Writers) appropriate for the discipline and writing type. |
| 6 | W.9-10.5 | Develop and strengthen writing as needed by planning, revising, editing, rewriting, or trying a new approach. |

## Materials

## 20-session pacing (4 weeks × 5 sessions, ~50 minutes each)

The six lessons are the direct-instruction core; the remaining
sessions are practice, reading, discussion, project work, and
assessment. Each session names its activity and success criterion —
see the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) for the full map.

| Session | Week | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 | Lesson | [L1 — Sentence architecture: clauses and sentence types](lessons/lesson-01-sentence-architecture-clauses.md) (+ generated illustration) |
| 2 | 15 | Practice | [Practice A](practice.md#practice-a--session-2-clause-identification) — clause identification (Set 1) |
| 3 | 15 | Practice | [Practice B](practice.md#practice-b--session-3-sentence-combining) — sentence combining (Passage A) |
| 4 | 15 | Reading | Sentence hunt in the learner's current reading: find one example of each sentence type, copy it, label it |
| 5 | 15 | Practice | [Practice C](practice.md#practice-c--session-5-fragment-and-run-on-repair) — fragment and run-on repair |
| 6 | 16 | Lesson | [L2 — Phrase variety: participial, prepositional, absolute](lessons/lesson-02-phrase-variety.md) |
| 7 | 16 | Practice | [Practice D](practice.md#practice-d--session-7-phrase-identification) — phrase identification (Set 2) |
| 8 | 16 | Practice | [Practice E](practice.md#practice-e--session-8-phrase-expansion-and-modifier-repair) — phrase expansion + dangling/misplaced repair |
| 9 | 16 | Discussion | Syntax seminar: three versions of one paragraph — which is strongest, and what proves it? |
| 10 | 16 | Practice | [Practice F](practice.md#practice-f--session-10-phrase-and-clause-sorting) — sorting + project launched |
| 11 | 17 | Lesson | [L3 — Semicolons and conjunctive adverbs](lessons/lesson-03-semicolons-and-conjunctive-adverbs.md) |
| 12 | 17 | Lesson | [L4 — Vocabulary systems and figures of speech](lessons/lesson-04-vocabulary-systems-and-figures-of-speech.md) |

| 14 | 17 | Practice | [Practice H](practice.md#practice-h--session-14-vocabulary-in-context) — vocabulary in context + project Part 1 checkpoint |
| 15 | 17 | Quiz | [Formative quiz](assessments/week-17_quiz.md) (20 pts; reteach if < 16) |
| 16 | 18 | Lesson | [L5 — Style: syntax choices, register, and MLA basics](lessons/lesson-05-style-syntax-register-mla.md) |
| 17 | 18 | Practice | [Practice I](practice.md#practice-i--session-17-style-revision) — style revision (two registers, one paragraph) |
| 18 | 18 | Lesson + studio | [L6 — The full editing pass](lessons/lesson-06-the-full-editing-pass.md) + [Practice J](practice.md#practice-j--session-18-editing-studio) editing studio |

Retrieval and feedback are built in: every practice session is
adult-reviewed the same day; the quiz (session 15) gates week 18
with reteaching; the review session revisits misconceptions before
U05 (narrative writing), where these tools become drafting habits.
