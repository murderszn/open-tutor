# Teacher Guide — Unit 04 (Grade 9 Language Arts)

**For the adult.** This guide maps sessions, flags likely
misconceptions, and states reteaching moves. Student questions
are in the lessons, practice, and assessments; their solutions
are only in [`answer-key.md`](answer-key.md).

## Session map (20 sessions, ~50 minutes each)

| Session | Focus | Success criterion | If it goes wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | L1 clauses + sentence types + illustration activity | Learner labels clauses in the four illustration sentences correctly | Re-teach subject–verb finding: cover everything but one verb at a time |
| 2 | Practice A | 6/8 identifications correct | Return to Lesson 1's worked example 1; sort clauses physically on cards |
| 3 | Practice B | Four-sentence Passage A rewrite keeps all eight facts | Count facts aloud against the original before accepting the rewrite |
| 4 | Reading: sentence hunt | One labeled example of each sentence type from real reading | Allow the learner's own writing as the hunting ground if reading is too hard that day |
| 5 | Practice C | Each repair named correctly (fragment / splice / run-on) | Distinguish splice from run-on by the comma: splice has one, run-on has none |
| 6 | L2 phrases | Learner distinguishes phrase from clause by the subject–verb test | Re-run Lesson 2's readiness check with new examples |
| 7 | Practice D | 6/8 phrase kinds correct, with modified word named | For each miss, ask "what word does this phrase describe?" before re-labeling |
| 8 | Practice E | Dangling/misplaced repairs give the phrase its true subject | Ask "who is doing the -ing action?" — if the answer is not the subject, it dangles |
| 9 | Syntax seminar | Learner defends a "strongest version" with structure, not taste | Require the defense to name a clause, phrase, or punctuation choice |

## Misconception watchlist

- **"That" = relative clause.** Test by job: after "knew,"
  a that-clause is usually a noun clause; after a noun it
  identifies, it is relative.
- **Compound verb = compound sentence.** "Lit the lamp and
  logged the hour" is one independent clause, hence simple.
- **Long = run-on; short = fragment.** Length is never the
  test; clause count and joining are.
- **Semicolon = fancy comma.** It only joins independent
  clauses and claims closeness; it never joins a dependent
  clause or a phrase.
- **"However" joins clauses with a comma.** It does not; it
  needs the semicolon–comma pair when it stands between two
  independent clauses.
- **Absolute phrase = comma splice.** "The wind gone" has no
  tensed verb, so it is not an independent clause.
- **First dictionary sense = the meaning.** Part of speech
  first, then fit to context, then substitution.
- **Formal = long words + passive voice.** Formal is precise
  and audience-aware; "The lamp went out at 02:14" is formal
  and short.
- **Editing = changing everything.** A reasonless change is
  returned; editing that invents facts is refused (Lesson 6,
  Worked example 2).

## Reteaching

- **Quiz below 16:** re-teach Lessons 1–3 over two sessions
  using the quiz's missed items as the new worked examples;
  re-administer Part B of the quiz with new pairs before
  Lesson 5.
- **Assessment below 21:** identify the weakest part
  (A analysis, B revision, C vocabulary, D style edit) and
  re-teach that lesson only, with the assessment's items as
  worked examples; then re-administer that part with new
  items. Do not re-administer the whole assessment unchanged.
- **Project below 28/40:** return the weakest criterion with
  the rubric descriptor for the next level up and one worked
  example at that level (see answer-key project notes); allow
  one revision.

## Readiness and next steps

- **Ready for U05** if the learner can: name sentence types
  and clause kinds without prompting; repair fragments,
  splices, and run-ons; punctuate a conjunctive adverb
  correctly; and defend at least three editing changes as
  meaning decisions.
- **Not yet ready** if semicolon decisions are still guesses
  or if the revision log lists changes without reasons — in
  that case, repeat Practice G and Practice J with new
  passages before U05's drafting begins, since U05 assumes
  these tools as drafting habits.
- **Extension for a fast learner:** the Lesson 6 extension
  (editing a public-domain paragraph and judging whether the
  passes improved or intruded) and a second, longer Style
  Portfolio piece in the opposite register.

## Accessibility and adult notes

- Text alternatives are provided for the generated image and
  the clause diagram; the image activity can be completed
  from the text-only alternative alone.
- All practice may be dictated, drawn as diagrams, or done
  with phrase/clause cards instead of written paragraphs; the
  assessed work is the grammatical decision, not handwriting.
- Preview the dictionary tool before Lesson 4: confirm it
  gives part of speech and numbered senses, and that the
  learner can navigate them.
- No paid account is required for any core objective; videos
  in the Resource Pack are enrichment only.
