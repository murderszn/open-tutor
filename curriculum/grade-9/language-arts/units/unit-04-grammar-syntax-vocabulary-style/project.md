# Project — The Style Portfolio

**Launched:** session 10 · **Presented:** session 20 ·
**Weight:** major project, scored with the rubric below (40 pts)

## The task

You are building a **Style Portfolio**: a small dossier that
proves, sentence by sentence, that editing is thinking. It has
three parts.

**Part 1 — Two sentence analyses (analytical, ~200 words each).**
Pick **two** sentences — one from a unit text (Passage A, Passage
B, or the sentence bank) and one from your own U01–U03 writing.
For each sentence:

- Quote the sentence exactly as it first appeared.
- Name its architecture precisely: every clause (independent or
  dependent, and the dependent kind), every phrase kind that
  matters, and its sentence type.
- Explain the **mechanism**: step by step, how does this
  sentence's structure produce its meaning or emphasis? (What
  is foregrounded, what is backgrounded, what does the
  punctuation claim?)
- Answer one skeptic's objection with evidence. Example
  skeptic: "This sentence would mean the same thing as two
  short sentences." Show why it would not — or concede, with
  evidence, that it would, and say what the longer version
  gains anyway.

**Part 2 — The revision experiment (creative).**
Take **one paragraph** from Passage A or from your own writing
and rewrite it twice: once in a formal register (logbook/report
style) and once in an informal register (letter-to-a-friend
style), keeping every fact in both versions. Then write a
100–150-word lab note: what did each register gain, what did
each lose, and what does that prove about register as a choice
rather than a rule?

**Part 3 — The editing pass (applied).**
Complete a full six-pass editing pass (Lesson 6) over a piece
of your own U01–U03 writing (at least 200 words; a different
piece from Part 1's sentence, or the same piece extended).
Submit: the original, the edited version, and the revision log
grouped by pass, in the form *original → revised → why*.
Highlight the three changes you are proudest of, with a
two-sentence defense of each.

## Materials

Notebook or paper, the unit texts, a dictionary, the rubric
below. No purchases.

## Presentation (session 20)

A 3-minute gallery walkthrough of **one** sentence analysis:
read the sentence aloud, name its architecture, explain its
mechanism, take one skeptic question from the adult. You are
scored on the explanation, not on performance.

## Rubric (40 pts)

| Criterion | Excellent (9–10) | Proficient (7–8) | Developing (5–6) | Beginning (0–4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Architecture identification** (10) | Both sentences analyzed with technical precision: every clause and key phrase named correctly, sentence types correct | Both sentences analyzed; minor mislabeling (one phrase or clause kind) | Analysis vague or one sentence missing; several mislabels | Sentences misidentified or missing |
| **Mechanism explanation** (10) | Step-by-step chain from structure to meaning/emphasis in both analyses; skeptic answered with evidence | Chain present in both; one skeptic answer thin | Effect asserted more than explained; mechanism missing in one analysis | No mechanism; summary instead of analysis |
| **Revision experiment** (10) | Both registers keep every fact; lab note names a specific gain and loss per register and draws a conclusion about register as choice | Both versions complete; gain/loss general; conclusion present | A fact dropped or a register not actually distinct; lab note missing or generic | Experiment missing or one version missing |
| **Editing pass + log** (10) | Six passes evident; every change defended as a meaning decision; three highlighted changes defended in two sentences each | Passes evident; most changes defended; highlighted changes present | Log lists changes without reasons, or passes missing; changes unexplained | Pass or log missing; edits invent facts or are unexplained |

**Scoring notes for the adult:** a portfolio can score
Excellent with shorter-than-maximum word counts if every
criterion is met — precision beats length. The skeptic's
objection in Part 1 must be a *real* objection (one a smart
reader could hold), not a straw man; if it is a straw man, cap
Mechanism at Proficient. An editing pass that invents facts
(Lesson 6, Worked example 2) cannot score above Proficient in
that criterion, however polished it looks. See
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md#project-notes) for annotated
sample responses at each level.
