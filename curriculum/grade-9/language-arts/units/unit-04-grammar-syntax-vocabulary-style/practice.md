# Practice — Unit 04 (Grade 9 Language Arts)

Work in order where a task builds on a lesson; the adult reviews
each practice the same day. Answers are in
[`answer-key.md`](answer-key.md) — do not work from the key.

## Practice A — Session 2: clause identification

Use Set 1 in [`texts/sentence-bank.md`](texts/sentence-bank.md).
For each sentence, (a) name its type (simple, compound, complex,
compound-complex), and (b) label each clause as independent (IC)
or dependent, naming the dependent kind (noun, relative,
adverbial) where there is one.

1. The keeper lit the lamp.
2. The keeper knew that the storm was coming.
3. The lamp, which had burned all night, guided the boat home.
4. Because the tide turned, the crew secured the boat.
5. The gulls circled the rocks, and the keeper watched from the tower.
6. When the fog thickened, the keeper lit the lamp, and the boats turned toward the light.
7. The boat that carried no lantern passed unseen.
8. Mara wrote while the tide rose.

## Practice B — Session 3: sentence combining

Rewrite Passage A ("The Lighthouse Log") as exactly four
sentences, without dropping any of its eight facts. Then, in
two sentences, explain which facts you put in dependent clauses
and why those facts belong in the background. Label the type of
each of your four sentences.

## Practice C — Session 5: fragment and run-on repair

Repair each item. Name the error (fragment, comma splice, or
run-on) and give one repaired version; where two repairs are
honest, give both and say which you prefer and why.

1. Because the tide turned.
2. The lamp burned all night, the boats found the harbor.
3. The keeper rang the bell the boats turned toward the sound.
4. Mara climbed the tower. Because she wanted to see the harbor.
5. The fog was thick, she could see nothing.
6. Running to the tower.
7. The log was complete, the inspector signed it, the crew celebrated.
8. The boats turned toward the sound, they had heard the bell.

## Practice D — Session 7: phrase identification

Use Set 2 in the sentence bank. For each sentence, name the
underlined phrase's kind — in the printed version below, the
phrase is given in brackets — and state what it modifies.

1. [Tired and salt-stained], the crew secured the boat.
2. [In the early morning], the keeper climbed the tower.
3. [The wind gone], the sea lay flat.
4. The old brass lamp [on the top shelf] had not been lit for years.
5. Mara, [hoping for clear weather], checked the sky at dawn.
6. The keeper worked [with steady hands].
7. [To see the harbor], Mara climbed to the gallery.
8. The logbook, [covered in salt stains], recorded thirty years of storms.

## Practice E — Session 8: phrase expansion and modifier repair

1. Expand "The keeper wrote in the logbook" with a participial
   phrase, then again with a prepositional phrase, then again
   with an absolute phrase. Label each version.
2. Repair and name the error: "Climbing the tower, the lamp
   came into view."
3. Repair and name the error: "Mara found the logbook on the
   shelf with the torn cover." (Assume the cover is torn, not
   the shelf.)
4. Repair and name the error: "Walking home, the rain started."
5. Rewrite Passage A as four to six sentences using at least
   one participial, one prepositional, and one absolute phrase.
   List each phrase, its kind, and what it modifies.
6. Explain in a paragraph why "Tired and salt-stained, the crew
   secured the boat" is stronger than "The crew was tired and
   salt-stained, and the crew secured the boat."

## Practice F — Session 10: phrase and clause sorting

Sort each item into exactly one box — clause or phrase — and,
if a clause, name it independent or dependent; if a phrase,
name its kind. Items:

1. when the fog thickened
2. in the early morning
3. the keeper lit the lamp
4. tired and salt-stained
5. that the storm was coming
6. with steady hands
7. the wind gone
8. which had burned all night

Then launch the project (see [`project.md`](project.md)):
choose your portfolio's three passages and write the
one-sentence claim for Part 1.

## Practice G — Session 13: semicolon repair

Correct Passage B ("A Letter Home") in full. List each error
you fixed, in order, naming it (comma splice, fragment,
semicolon–comma pair, etc.) and giving the corrected sentence.
Do not change any fact in the letter.

## Practice H — Session 14: vocabulary in context

1. For vocabulary sentences 2, 3, 7, and 9 in the sentence
   bank, name the context-clue kind and write the guessed
   meaning before checking a dictionary.
2. Give the noun form of "precise," the verb form of
   "analysis," and the noun form of "advocate" — and state,
   for each family, whether the meaning stays the same across
   forms.
3. Identify the figure and its role in each sentence:
   (a) "The committee said the program would be 'sunsetted'
   rather than closed." (b) "It was an open secret that the
   tower would close." (c) "After the storm, a deafening
   silence filled the tower."
4. Project checkpoint: draft Part 1 of the Style Portfolio
   (two sentence analyses, ~200 words each) and bring it for
   adult review.

## Practice I — Session 17: style revision

1. Rewrite Passage A twice: once as a logbook entry (formal
   register) and once as a letter to a friend (informal
   register). Each version must keep all eight facts.
2. Repair the parallelism: "The keeper's duties were checking
   the wick, to trim the flame, and log the hour."
3. Decide active or passive for each, with a one-sentence
   justification: (a) "The tower was built in 1890."
   (b) "Mistakes were made in the log." (c) "Mara signed the
   log at midnight."
4. Write a six-sentence paragraph about a routine task using:
   one long cumulative sentence, one short sentence for
   emphasis, one parallel series, and one deliberate passive
   sentence. Label each device in the margin.

## Practice J — Session 18: editing studio

1. Run Passes 1–3 over one paragraph of your own U01–U03
   writing. List every fragment, splice, run-on, phrase
   repair, and semicolon decision, with a one-sentence defense
   for each semicolon.
2. Run Passes 4–5 over the same paragraph. List every word
   replaced or cut, with the reason, and confirm the MLA
   formatting (or note honestly that the piece has no sources
   and needs no Works Cited page).
3. Write the full revision log (Pass 6) and choose the three
   changes you are proudest of, explaining in two sentences
   each why the revised sentence is stronger.
4. Assemble the Style Portfolio draft (all three parts) for
   the session-20 gallery.
