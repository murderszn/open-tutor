# Texts — Unit 04 (Grade 9 Language Arts)

All passages below are **original to this unit** and written for
grammar, syntax, and vocabulary practice. No copyrighted text is
reproduced. The lighthouse keeper, Mara, is a fictional character;
the log entries are practice texts, not real records.

## Passage A — "The Lighthouse Log" (flat paragraph for revision)

Read this paragraph first exactly as written. Lessons 1, 2, 3, and 5
return to it, and Practice B, Practice E, and Practice I revise it.

> The keeper climbed the tower. The lamp was cold. She trimmed the
> wick. She lit the lamp. The fog was thick. The boats could not see
> the light. She rang the bell. The boats turned toward the sound.

Eight short sentences, all in the same shape: subject — verb —
object or complement. Nothing is wrong with a short sentence. A whole
paragraph of identical short sentences, however, hides which ideas
matter most, which ideas cause which, and where one thought ends and
the next begins. Revision is not decoration: it is deciding those
relationships and showing them in the sentence structure.

## Passage B — "A Letter Home" (editing passage)

This letter contains exactly the errors named in its margin notes.
It is used in Lesson 3 (semicolons), Lesson 6 (editing pass), and
Practice G.

> Dear Ana,
>
> I arrived at the station on Monday, the road was washed out so I
> walked the last two miles. The keeper met me at the gate, she
> showed me the logbook and the lamp room. Because the fog was
> thick. I stayed on the tower until midnight, I logged every
> passing light, however, I missed the small fishing boat that
> passed without a lantern.
>
> The work is steady, the pay is fair, the food is plain. I will
> write again soon, I promise to send the photograph of the tower
> at dusk.
>
> Yours, Mara

Margin notes (for the adult; do not hand these to the learner before
the editing task): sentence 1 of paragraph 1 is a comma splice;
sentence 2 of paragraph 1 is a comma splice; "Because the fog was
thick." is a fragment; the "however" sentence needs a semicolon
before "however" and a comma after it; the final sentence is a comma
splice. The corrected letter is in the answer key.

## Passage C — Vocabulary sentences (Lesson 4)

Each sentence below is used in Lesson 4 or Practice H. The target
word is in bold; the surrounding sentence is the first clue, not the
whole definition.

1. The keeper's log was **meticulous**: every lamp check, tide
   time, and visitor was recorded to the minute.
2. Mara tried to **anticipate** the storm, so she checked the
   lamp, the oil, and the bell before the clouds arrived.
3. One loose bolt had a serious **consequence**: the lamp
   shutter jammed at midnight.
4. Before she trusted the new chart, Mara **evaluated** it,
   checking its date, its source, and its measurements against
   the old chart.
5. The **precise** time in the log was 6:42, not "about
   seven."
6. A new lens would **transform** the light, changing a weak
   glow into a beam visible for miles.
7. The keeper **advocated** for a second bell, arguing
   patiently, in every report, that fog alone was not enough
   warning.
8. Mara **revised** the log entry, replacing a vague sentence
   with the exact times and names.
9. The crew had to **sustain** the light through the longest
   night, keeping it burning without a break.
10. A short note at the top of the page **clarified** the
    entry below it, explaining that "north light" meant the
    light on the north headland, not a direction of travel.
11. The word "lighthouse" **derives** from "light" plus
    "house": a house built to hold a light.
12. In **context**, "the light failed" meant the lamp went
    out — not that the keeper had failed at her job.

## Word bank (12 words, with working definitions in the unit's own words)

| Word | Working definition | Word-family note |
|---|---|---|
| meticulous | very careful about small details | — |
| anticipate | expect something and prepare for it | anticipation (noun) |
| consequence | a result, especially of an action | consequently (adverb: "as a result") |
| evaluate | judge how good, true, or useful something is, with reasons | evaluation (noun) |
| precise | exact; stated with no vagueness | precision (noun) |
| transform | change something's form or character completely | transformation (noun) |
| advocate (verb) | argue in favor of something | advocacy (noun) |
| revise | change a text to improve it | revision (noun) |
| sustain | keep something going over time | sustainable (adjective, different sense: able to continue) |
| clarify | make something clear or easier to understand | clarification (noun) |
| derive | get something from a source; trace a word to its origin | derivation (noun) |
| context | the words and situation around a word that fix its meaning | contextual (adjective) |

## Sentence sets for identification work

These numbered sentences are used in Practice A, Practice D, and
the formative quiz. Their analyses are in the answer key; do not
print the key beside them.

**Set 1 (clauses and sentence types):**

1. The keeper lit the lamp.
2. The keeper knew that the storm was coming.
3. The lamp, which had burned all night, guided the boat home.
4. Because the tide turned, the crew secured the boat.
5. The gulls circled the rocks, and the keeper watched from the tower.
6. When the fog thickened, the keeper lit the lamp, and the boats turned toward the light.
7. The boat that carried no lantern passed unseen.
8. Mara wrote while the tide rose.

**Set 2 (phrases):**

1. Tired and salt-stained, the crew secured the boat.
2. In the early morning, the keeper climbed the tower.
3. The wind gone, the sea lay flat.
4. The old brass lamp on the top shelf had not been lit for years.
5. Mara, hoping for clear weather, checked the sky at dawn.
6. The keeper worked with steady hands.
7. To see the harbor, Mara climbed to the gallery.
8. The logbook, covered in salt stains, recorded thirty years of storms.
