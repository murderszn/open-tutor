**50 points · ~45 minutes · timed · open-text** (you may use "Night

the new passage first, then complete both tasks. Solutions and scoring

## The passage (new — written for this assessment, 2026)

### "The Blue Envelope"

**1.** The envelope was already open in June's hands before she
remembered deciding to open it.

**2.** Blue paper, her mother's handwriting, the library's date stamp
bleeding through from the back: *DUE OCT 14*. The book — *A Field
Guide to Backyard Birds*, of all the unromantic things — had sat on
the returns cart all morning, and June had shelved it without looking
at it twice, the way you shelve anything on a Tuesday.

**3.** *"For the girl who feeds the sparrows when she thinks no one is
watching —"* June stopped reading. Her throat did something
complicated. Nobody watched her feed the sparrows. That was the entire
point of feeding them behind the garage.

**4.** She should put it back. That was the rule about lost things:
you turned them in, you didn't read them. The envelope had clearly
been meant for someone — a bookmark, a note passed in a book and
forgotten, none of her business in any direction. June slid the letter
back into the envelope with the care of someone defusing something,
and set the envelope on the returns cart like it was hot.

**5.** It sat there for the rest of her shift, blue as a held breath,
accusing nothing, asking nothing. Twice she carried books past it.
Twice she did not pick it up.

**6.** At closing, Mrs. Abernathy counted the register and said,
"You're quiet tonight, June." June said, "Tired," which was true and
was also the word she used when something else was true. She took the
envelope off the cart and put it in her apron pocket. *Lost and found,*
she told herself. *I'll turn it in tomorrow.* The lie sat in her
pocket all the way home, light as a sparrow and twice as alive.

## Task 1 — Reading the new passage (20 pts)

Answer **all four** in complete sentences. Quote by paragraph number.

a. (5 pts) The passage opens *in medias res*. Identify the
   **inciting incident** of this passage's plot and the plot stage of
   paragraph 1. Then explain in 2–3 sentences what the *in medias
   res* opening gains versus starting chronologically with paragraph 2.

b. (5 pts) Name the **point of view** of the passage. Identify one
   thing this POV lets the reader know and one thing it withholds
   (something a different POV could have shown).

c. (5 pts) In paragraph 5, the envelope is "blue as a held breath."
   Is "held breath" being used for its **denotation** or its
   **connotation** here — and what tone does the comparison create?
   Then name **one more** connotative word choice from paragraphs 4–6
   and explain what it adds.

d. (5 pts) June's motivations conflict. Name **two** of them with
   one quoted phrase of evidence each, and state in one sentence
   which motivation wins by the end of the passage — and what it
   costs her.

## Task 2 — Comparative analysis (30 pts)

Choose **one** author choice in "The Blue Envelope" (its *in medias
res* opening, its point of view, its pacing, or its word choice) and
compare it with the **corresponding choice** in **one** unit text
("Night Train," a "Monkey's Paw" excerpt, or a craft vignette).

Write 2–3 paragraphs (~250–350 words) that:

1. State an **arguable thesis**: which author's choice serves its
   story better, and why — or, if neither is "better," what different
   effect each choice is built to create.
2. Support the claim with **at least two quotes from each text**
   (four total), each with its location.
3. Explain the **mechanism** — *how* each choice produces its effect
   (not just *that* it does).
4. Answer one **skeptic's objection** ("that's just your opinion")
   with evidence from the words on the page.

You are scored with the project rubric's criteria (claim, evidence,
mechanism, organization, conventions) at the "comparing across texts"
level — see [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment) for the
scoring descriptors. A paragraph that summarizes either story instead
of analyzing choices cannot score above Developing.
