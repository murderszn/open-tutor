# Project — The Author's Choices Casebook

**Launched:** session 14 · **Presented:** session 20 ·
**Weight:** major project, scored with the rubric below (40 pts)

## The task

You are building a **casebook**: a small dossier that proves, with
evidence, that the choices authors make *cause* the effects readers
feel. Your casebook has three parts.

**Part 1 — Two case studies (analytical, ~250–350 words each).**
Pick **two** author choices from *two different* unit texts ("Night
Train," the "Monkey's Paw" excerpts, or the craft vignettes). At least
one case study must concern **structure or time** (event order,
flashback, pacing, offstage vs. onstage) and at least one must concern
**character or language** (motivation, foil, POV/distance, word choice
and tone). For each case study:
- Name the choice precisely (not "good description" — e.g.,
  "Jacobs stages the climax offstage, through sound alone").
- Quote **two** passages where the choice is visible.
- Explain the **mechanism**: step by step, how does this choice
  produce its effect on the reader? (Withholding → imagination fills
  the gap → the gap is scarier than any description… show the chain.)
- Answer one skeptic's objection with evidence.

**Part 2 — The rewrite experiment (creative).**
Take **one scene** from either "Night Train" or the "Monkey's Paw"
excerpts and rewrite it (150–250 words) with **one author choice
changed**: different point of view, chronological instead of
nonlinear order, onstage instead of offstage, or opposite tone
through word choice only. Then write a 100–150 word lab note:
what did the change gain, what did it lose, and what does that prove
about the original choice?

**Part 3 — The verdict (synthesis, ~150 words).**
Which of the two choices you studied is doing *more* work in its
story — and what is your evidence that the story would break, or
merely wobble, without it? End with the single sentence you would
tell a writer who asked, "Do these choices really matter?"

## Materials

Notebook or paper, the unit texts, index cards (optional, for the
gallery walk), the rubric below. No purchases.

## Presentation (session 20)

A 3-minute gallery walkthrough of **one** case study: read your two
quotes aloud, explain the mechanism, take one skeptic question from
the adult. You are scored on the explanation, not on performance.

## Rubric (40 pts)

| Criterion | Excellent (9–10) | Proficient (7–8) | Developing (5–6) | Beginning (0–4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Choice identification** (10) | Both choices named with technical precision; categories (structure/time vs. character/language) both covered | Both choices named; categories covered; minor imprecision | Choices vague ("good writing") or one category missing | Choices misidentified or missing |
| **Evidence** (10) | 4+ well-chosen quotes, precisely located; each quote clearly shows the choice | 4 quotes located; most clearly show the choice | Fewer than 4 quotes, or quotes loosely connected to the choice | Quotes missing, misquoted, or irrelevant |
| **Mechanism explanation** (10) | Step-by-step causal chain from choice to reader effect, in both case studies; skeptic answered with evidence | Causal chain present in both; one skeptic answer thin | Effect asserted more than explained; mechanism missing in one study | No mechanism; summary instead of analysis |
| **Rewrite experiment + verdict** (10) | Rewrite changes exactly one choice; lab note names a specific gain and loss; verdict argues break-vs-wobble with evidence | Rewrite and lab note complete; gain/loss general; verdict present | Rewrite changes multiple things or lab note missing; verdict asserted | Experiment or verdict missing |

**Scoring notes for the adult:** a casebook can score Excellent with
shorter-than-maximum word counts if every criterion is met — precision
beats length. The skeptic's objection in Part 1 must be a *real*
objection (one a smart reader could hold), not a straw man; if it is a
straw man, cap Mechanism at Proficient. See
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md#project-notes) for annotated sample
responses at each level.
