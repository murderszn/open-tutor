# Teacher Guide — Unit 02 (Grade 9 Language Arts)

**Unit:** Narrative structure, characterization, and author choices ·
**Track weeks:** 7–10 · **Sessions:** 20 × ~50 minutes ·
**Issue:** [#44](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/44), section U02 ·
**Status:** validated draft; not educator-reviewed, not merged

## How this unit works

U01 taught learners to read with a pen and build evidence-based cases
about theme. U02 turns the lens from *what the text means* to *how the
author made it mean that* — structure, character, point of view,
pacing, and diction as deliberate choices with observable effects.
Every lesson names the choice, shows the mechanism, and asks the
learner to manipulate the choice themselves (rewrites, reorderings,
tone revisions). The unit's throughline question: **"What did the
author decide, and what does the decision do to you?"**

The anchor texts are one original short story ("Night Train," told in
medias res with a flashback), three verified excerpts from W. W.
Jacobs's public-domain "The Monkey's Paw" (1902), and six short
original craft vignettes that isolate one choice at a time. Nothing
here reproduces copyrighted material.

## Unit misconceptions to watch

1. **"Structure" = "the plot diagram I memorized."** Learners can
   label the six stages without understanding that *order is a
   choice*. Keep asking: "What changes if this comes first instead?"
   The reorder tasks (B3, C, H2) exist to break the labeling habit.
2. **Dynamic = active/loud.** Learners will call Herbert "dynamic"
   because dramatic things happen *to* him. Redirect: dynamic means
   the character's understanding or nature changes. Mrs. White is the
   unit's best test case.
3. **Unreliable narrator = liar.** Version 3's narrator is not exactly
   lying — they believe the plan is excellent. Unreliability includes
   self-deception, limited understanding, and motivated emphasis.
   Ask: "What does the narrator *need* to be true?"
4. **One strong word = tone.** Learners will point at "reeked" and
   stop. Insist on the pile-up: cover all but one changed word in
   Vignette C and ask whether the tone survives. It doesn't — that is
   the lesson.
5. **"The author wanted to…" as mind reading.** Redirect to mechanism:
   not what Jacobs *intended* but what the choice *does*, step by
   step, to a reader. Intentions are unknowable; effects are
   observable.

## Discussion prompts (session 9 seminar and beyond)

- "Maya gets off at the wrong stop. Is that the climax or the
  resolution — and does your answer change what the story is *about*?"
- "Morris throws the paw on the fire and then tells White exactly how
  to use it. Which action shows what he really wants?"
- "Whose story is 'The Monkey's Paw' — the Whites', Morris's, or the
  fakir's? Who gets to speak, and who only gets spoken about?"
- "The knocking scene never shows the thing at the door. Is Jacobs
  being kind to the reader or cruel?"

## Differentiation

- **Reading access:** the adult may read "Night Train" and the
  excerpts aloud while the learner follows; the analysis is the work,
  not the decoding. The "Monkey's Paw" excerpts may be replaced by the
  full Gutenberg text for strong readers (link in
  [texts/monkeys-paw-excerpts.md](texts/monkeys-paw-excerpts.md)).
- **Alternate response modes:** plot maps, charts, and timelines may be
  drawn, dictated, or built with index cards; the rewrite tasks may be
  recorded as audio. The exit checks may be answered orally.
- **Extensions:** rewrite the "Night Train" ending from the old man's
  first-person POV (what did *he* see?); research the 1902 publication
  context of "The Monkey's Paw" (Harper's Monthly, British Empire)
  and write a one-page cultural-POV analysis; revise Vignette C into
  a *third* kitchen with a nostalgic tone.
- **Support:** for learners new to timed writing, the unit assessment
  may be split across two sittings without penalty; score Task 1 and
  Task 2 separately and conference before recording.

## Reteaching guide

- **Quiz < 24:** use session 16's first 15 minutes for targeted
  reteach of the missed skill (structure → redo Practice A task 1
  orally; character → redo one D chart; POV → Vignette A v1/v2
  sort; pacing → read Vignette B aloud with tapping). Do not reteach
  the whole unit.
- **Assessment Task 1 weak, Task 2 strong:** the learner can compare
  but not yet read closely — assign two more Practice B-style
  foreshadowing hunts on the independent reading book.
- **Assessment Task 2 weak:** the mechanism chain is missing — return
  to L6's worked examples and require the skeleton (claim → 2 quotes
  → mechanism → skeptic) before any drafting, in U03's writing work.

## Answer-key use

[answer-key.md](answer-key.md) solves every lesson item, practice task,
quiz question, assessment task, and project criterion independently.
Keep it adult-only. When a learner's answer differs from the key,
check first whether it is *defensibly different* (the key notes
acceptable variation) — in literary analysis, a well-evidenced
minority reading is a success, not an error.
