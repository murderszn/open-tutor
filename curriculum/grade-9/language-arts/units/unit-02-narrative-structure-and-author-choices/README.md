# Unit 02 — Narrative Structure, Characterization, and Author Choices

**Grade:** 9 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #44](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/44), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

U01 taught learners to read with a pen and build evidence-based cases
about theme. U02 turns the lens from *what the text means* to *how the
author made it mean that*: plot structure and event order, complex
characters, point of view and narrative distance, pacing and time
manipulation, and the cumulative impact of word choice. Every lesson
names a choice, shows its mechanism, and asks the learner to manipulate
the choice themselves — reorderings, rewrites, tone revisions. The
throughline question: **"What did the author decide, and what does the
decision do to you?"** The unit ends in a comparative analysis essay
and an Author's Choices Casebook.

## Essential question

How do authors' choices — about structure, character, point of view,
pacing, and language — create the effects readers feel?

## Prerequisites

From U01 and the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2):

- Annotate with a purpose; distinguish observation from inference
- Cite strong and thorough textual evidence (RL.9-10.1); determine
  theme and write objective summaries (RL.9-10.2)
- Analyze complex characters' motivations at a basic level (RL.9-10.3,
  introduced in U01 Lesson 5)
- Build a close-reading essay with the skeptic move (U01 Lesson 6)

The unit re-teaches plot vocabulary and POV types from the ground up —
no prior mastery is assumed. What is *new* in U02: **event order as a
choice** (story order vs. chronological order), **narrative distance**,
**unreliable narration**, **pacing as time control**, **cumulative**
word-choice impact on tone, and **comparative** analysis across texts.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Analyze how an author's choices concerning text structure, event
   order, and time manipulation (flashbacks, in-medias-res openings,
   offstage staging, pacing) create effects such as mystery, tension,
   or surprise (RL.9-10.5).
2. Analyze how complex characters develop over a text, interact with
   other characters, and advance the plot or develop the theme —
   including conflicting motivations, arcs, and foils (RL.9-10.3).
3. Analyze a particular point of view or cultural experience reflected
   in a work of literature from outside the United States (RL.9-10.6).
4. Determine figurative, connotative, and technical meanings of words
   and phrases, and analyze the *cumulative* impact of specific word
   choices on meaning and tone (RL.9-10.4).
5. Compare author choices across two texts in a structured analysis,
   supporting claims with quoted evidence and answering a skeptic's
   objection (W.9-10.9).

## 20-session pacing (4 weeks × 5 sessions, ~50 minutes each)

The six lessons are the direct-instruction core; the remaining sessions
are practice, reading, discussion, project work, and assessment. Each
session names its activity and success criterion — see the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) for the full map.

| Session | Week | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7 | Lesson | [L1 — Plot structure and event order](lessons/lesson-01-plot-structure-and-event-order.md) (+ generated illustration) |
| 2 | 7 | Practice | [Practice A](practice.md#practice-a--session-2-plot-mapping) — plot mapping (story vs. chronological order) |
| 3 | 7 | Practice | [Practice B](practice.md#practice-b--session-3-foreshadowing-hunt--reorder-rewrite) — foreshadowing hunt + reorder rewrite |
| 4 | 7 | Reading | "Night Train" reread with plot-map check; reading log |
| 5 | 7 | Practice | [Practice C](practice.md#practice-c--session-5-two-openings-one-story) — two openings compared |
| 6 | 8 | Lesson | [L2 — Complex characters](lessons/lesson-02-complex-characters.md) (motivations, arcs, foils) |
| 7 | 8 | Practice | [Practice D](practice.md#practice-d--session-7-character-motivation-charts) — motivation charts (Maya, Morris) |
| 8 | 8 | Practice | [Practice E](practice.md#practice-e--session-8-two-conflict-analyses) — conflict analyses + foil paragraph |
| 9 | 8 | Discussion | Character seminar: "Which character changes most?" (Socratic-style, quote-backed) |
| 10 | 8 | Practice | [Practice F](practice.md#practice-f--session-10-character-arc-timelines) — arc timelines (static vs. dynamic) |
| 11 | 9 | Lesson | [L3 — Point of view and narrative distance](lessons/lesson-03-point-of-view-and-narrative-distance.md) (incl. unreliable narrator, cultural POV) |

| 15 | 9 | Quiz | [Formative quiz](assessments/week-09_quiz.md) (30 pts; reteach if < 24) |
| 16 | 10 | Lesson | [L5 — Word choice and cumulative tone](lessons/lesson-05-word-choice-and-cumulative-tone.md) |

four weeks (now with a weekly "author choice in the wild" entry);
every practice session is adult-reviewed the same day; the quiz
(session 15) gates week 10 with reteaching; the review session
revisits misconceptions before U03 (informational rhetoric).
