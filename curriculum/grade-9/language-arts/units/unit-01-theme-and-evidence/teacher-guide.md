# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Theme and Evidence

*For the guiding adult. Student materials never link here. Scoring guidance
for every item lives in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md); this guide covers
how to teach the unit, not what the answers are.*

## Unit at a glance

Four weeks (track weeks 3–6), 20 sessions × ~50 minutes. The unit moves
from **noticing** (annotation, observation vs. inference) to **proving**
(strong/thorough evidence, embedded citation) to **claiming** (theme as an
arguable sentence, tracked across texts) to **arguing** (the close-reading
essay). The project (Theme Case File) runs alongside weeks 3–4 and presents
in the final review session.

## Teaching notes by lesson

- **L1:** The illustration activity works best *before* the story — it
  teaches inference as hypothesis, and the story then confirms/revises.
  Insist on the physical point-at-words test all unit.
- **L2:** The single most reteachable lesson. If the learner quote-dumps
  (three quotes, no commentary), stop and rebuild one paragraph together
  before continuing. Paragraph-number accuracy deserves one explicit
  counting session — errors here cascade into every later citation.
- **L3:** Watch for morals ("you should...") vs. themes. Also watch
  summary bloat: cap summaries at the word count and make cuts together.
- **L4:** The Frankenstein excerpts are the unit's hardest reading
  (19th-century syntax). Read them aloud first, paraphrase hard sentences
  together, *then* chart. The chart is the thinking; don't let decoding
  swallow the session.
- **L5:** Learners default to adjectives; force the "wants X, shown when he
  does Y" form. The Darnell point-of-view writing task is the deepest check
  of characterization — read it carefully.
- **L6:** The skeptic's paragraph is the unit's signature move. Model it
  once on your own theme before asking the learner to write theirs.

## Mature content and adult preview

"The Salt Box" deals with an aging grandmother's move to assisted living
and family grief — gentle, but preview it. The *Frankenstein* excerpts
include body horror (Ch. 5) and murder (referenced, not depicted). "The
Platform" deals with widowhood. Nothing graphic; the adult should still
read everything first and set expectations, per the track README.

## Reteaching triggers

- Quiz < 24/30: reteach the failed part's lesson (not the whole unit)
  using the key's "reteach" notes, then re-administer 3–4 fresh questions
  you write in the same style.
- Essay missing commentary (quotes without explanation): one joint
  rebuild of a single paragraph, then the learner revises the rest alone.
- Theme never revised across drafts: return to Practice E with a new text
  (the learner's independent book) before the assessment.

## What "ready for U02" looks like

The learner can: annotate with a purpose; select and rank evidence and
explain the ranking; cite precisely; state an arguable theme and revise it
against details; write an objective summary; map a character's conflicting
motivations; and assemble a short evidenced argument that answers an
objection. U02 (narrative structure, characterization, author choices)
assumes all of this and adds structural analysis.

## Accessibility

All lessons include reading-access (read-aloud), alternate response mode
(dictation, card sorting, oral), and extension paths. The project and both
assessments have dictated/recorded alternatives. The generated illustration
carries alt text, a caption, and a text-only alternative in Lesson 1.
Adjust session length (not content) for attention needs; split 50-minute
sessions into two 25-minute halves rather than cutting practice.
