# The Audition

*An original story written for Unit 01 (Grade 9 Language Arts), Lesson 5. About 900 words.*

---

Marcus had the monologue memorized down to the breaths. He had been saying it in the shower for three weeks — Mercutio's Queen Mab speech, which Ms. Okafor had called "ambitious for a sophomore" in a tone that meant *try it anyway*. The auditions were Thursday after school. The hardware store schedule, taped to the fridge, said Marcus worked Thursday after school.

He had not told his mother about the schedule conflict because his mother had enough to not-tell. His father was on the road crew until November. His brother Darnell had quit the store in August to "find himself," which so far meant sleeping until noon in the room they shared. The Thursday shift existed because somebody had to cover the register from four to eight, and Marcus was the somebody.

On Tuesday he asked Mr. Abernathy, who owned the store and called everyone "chief," if he could swap Thursday for Saturday.

"Saturday's Darnell's," Mr. Abernathy said, not looking up from the invoices.

"Darnell hasn't shown up on a Saturday since August."

"That's between Darnell and Darnell's mother, chief. The schedule's the schedule."

Marcus said it to Darnell that night while Darnell was eating cereal standing up, which was how Darnell ate everything, as if sitting down were a commitment.

"Cover Thursday and I'll cover your Saturday."

"I got plans Thursday."

"You got plans every day. You've had plans since August."

Darnell poured more cereal. "You think you're better than me now? Theater kid?"

"I think the schedule's on the fridge and your name's on Saturday."

Darnell left the kitchen. Marcus heard the bedroom door close — not slam, which was worse. A slam would have meant a fight he could win or lose. The quiet close meant Darnell had decided something.

On Wednesday, Marcus told Ms. Okafor he might have a conflict.

"How firm is *might*?" she said.

"My brother might cover."

"Might is not a plan, Marcus."

"I know."

She looked at him the way teachers look at you when they're deciding whether to be disappointed now or later. "The callback list goes up Friday. If you're not in the room Thursday, I can't put you on it."

That night he ran the monologue in the shower until the water went cold. *O, then, I see Queen Mab hath been with you.* He did the part where Mercutio laughs at himself for believing his own dream, and he thought about how the speech was funny and sad at the same time, which was the whole point, which was why he wanted it.

Thursday at 3:40 he was behind the register, and the store smelled like cut lumber and fertilizer, and the clock above the key-cutting station ticked loud enough to hear over the radio. At 4:15 Darnell walked in.

Marcus stared. "What are you doing here?"

"Covering your shift, genius. Go." Darnell went behind the counter and picked up the pricing gun like it was a foreign object. "Abernathy said if I'm touching the register I have to wear the apron. Where's the apron?"

"You hate the apron."

"I hate a lot of things. Go, before I change my mind."

Marcus went. He ran four blocks in his work shoes, and he made the audition with eleven minutes to spare, and he did the monologue with his heart hammering so loud he was sure Ms. Okafor could hear it through his ribs. When he finished, she was quiet for a moment, and then she said, "The laugh," and he said, "What?" and she said, "You laughed at the sad part. On purpose. Good."

He got the callback. He found out Friday, and he told Darnell first, before his mother, before anyone.

Darnell was on the couch. He said, "Cool," without looking up from his phone. Then, after a minute, he said, "Abernathy says I can have Saturdays back. Regular. If I want."

"Do you want?"

Darnell shrugged. "It's money."

Marcus sat on the arm of the couch. He wanted to say thank you in a way that didn't sound small, and he couldn't find it, so he said, "You'd be good at the register. You're good at math."

"I'm good at a lot of things," Darnell said, and for the first time in months he almost smiled. "Somebody had to tell you."
