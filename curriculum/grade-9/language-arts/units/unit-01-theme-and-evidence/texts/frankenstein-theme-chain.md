# Frankenstein — Theme Chain: Ambition and Responsibility

*Five short excerpts from Mary Shelley's* Frankenstein; or, The Modern
Prometheus, *selected for Lesson 4's theme-tracking work (RL.9-10.2). Each
excerpt is quoted exactly from the public-domain text (Project Gutenberg
ebook #84, the 1831 revised edition; checked 2026-10-13). Ellipses [...] mark
omissions made for this exercise. The full text is free at
<https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/84>.*

*Text-rights note: Mary Shelley died in 1851; this work is in the U.S. public
domain. Quoted with author, work, and location for every excerpt.*

---

## Excerpt 1 — Letter 1: ambition announced

*Context: Robert Walton writes to his sister Margaret from St. Petersburg
before his Arctic voyage. He has not yet met Victor Frankenstein.*

> I shall satiate my ardent curiosity with the sight of a part of the world
> never before visited, and may tread a land never before imprinted by the
> foot of man. [...] My life might have been passed in ease and luxury, but I
> preferred glory to every enticement that wealth placed in my path.

## Excerpt 2 — Chapter 5: ambition's price

*Context: Victor Frankenstein has just brought the creature to life after
nearly two years of secret work.*

> How can I describe my emotions at this catastrophe, or how delineate the
> wretch whom with such infinite pains and care I had endeavoured to form?
> [...] I had worked hard for nearly two years, for the sole purpose of
> infusing life into an inanimate body. [...] I had desired it with an ardour
> that far exceeded moderation; but now that I had finished, the beauty of the
> dream vanished, and breathless horror and disgust filled my heart.

## Excerpt 3 — Chapter 10: the creature answers

*Context: On the glaciers above Chamounix, the creature confronts his
creator and demands to be heard.*

> I was benevolent and good; misery made me a fiend. Make me happy, and I
> shall again be virtuous.

## Excerpt 4 — Chapter 16: the creature names the cause

*Context: The creature, telling Victor his history, explains how rejection
turned him toward revenge.*

> [...] I am malicious because I am miserable. [...]

## Excerpt 5 — Chapter 24: Victor's final warning

*Context: Dying aboard Walton's ship, Victor gives Walton — who is chasing
his own glorious ambition — his last advice.*

> Farewell, Walton! Seek happiness in tranquillity and avoid ambition, even if
> it be only the apparently innocent one of distinguishing yourself in
> science and discoveries. Yet why do I say this? I have myself been blasted
> in these hopes, yet another may succeed.

---

*Use: Lesson 4 asks learners to state a candidate theme from Excerpt 1, then
revise it after each subsequent excerpt, recording which specific words or
details forced the revision. The chain shows a theme emerging (ambition as
glory), being complicated (ambition's horror; the creature's counter-claim
about responsibility), and being restated with a crack in it (Victor warns
against ambition yet cannot quite believe his own warning).*
