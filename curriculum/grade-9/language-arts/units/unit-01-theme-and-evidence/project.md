# Project — The Theme Case File

*Unit 01 investigation (RL.9-10.1, RL.9-10.2, RL.9-10.3). Work sessions:
14 (launch), 17–18 (build alongside the essay). Presented in session 20's
review.*

## The task

You are building a **dossier** — the kind of file a lawyer, a scholar, or a
detective builds — that proves a theme is really in a text. Choose one text
from this unit: "The Salt Box," the *Frankenstein* theme chain, or "The
Audition." Your case file argues for one theme and proves it the way this
unit taught you: claim, development across the text, thorough evidence,
named objections answered.

## Deliverables

1. **Theme claim** (one arguable sentence) on the cover page, with the text
   title and author.
2. **Evidence portfolio:** 8–10 pieces of evidence, each on its own card or
   page, with: the quotation (brief — a line or two, never a whole page),
   its exact location (paragraph number, or Letter/Chapter for
   Frankenstein), what it proves, and which part of the theme's development
   it belongs to (emergence / complication / refinement / confirmation).
3. **Theme-development narrative** (1–2 pages): how the theme emerges and is
   shaped and refined by specific details across the text — your Lesson 4
   chart written as prose, quoting at least four times.
4. **Objective summary** of the text (60–90 words for the stories; 80–110
   for the Frankenstein chain), opinion-free.
5. **Skeptic's page:** the strongest objection to your theme, stated
   fairly in 4–6 sentences with at least one quotation — then your answer
   in 3–5 sentences.
6. **Three-minute presentation:** walk the adult through the file as if
   they are the jury. No reading aloud from the pages — talk *from* the
   evidence.

## Materials

Notebook or paper, index cards (or cut paper slips) for the evidence
portfolio, the unit texts, a folder or binder for the dossier. Nothing to
buy; nothing hazardous.

## Steps

1. **Session 14:** choose the text; draft the theme claim; collect six
   candidate evidence pieces with locations (Practice I).
2. **Session 17:** grow the portfolio to 8–10; sort each card into
   emergence / complication / refinement / confirmation; draft the
   development narrative.
3. **Session 18:** write the objective summary; write the skeptic's page
   and your answer; assemble the dossier in order.
4. **Session 20:** present (3 minutes); the adult scores with the rubric
   below and returns one revision target.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | Excellent (9–10) | Proficient (7–8) | Developing (5–6) | Points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Theme claim | Arguable, precise, full sentence; clearly about *this* text | Arguable but broad or slightly off-text | Topic, moral, or plot summary instead of a claim | /10 |
| Evidence portfolio (8–10) | All located precisely; spread across the text; each labeled by development stage | 8+ located; mostly spread; stages mostly right | Fewer than 8, vaguely located, or clustered in one section | /10 |
| Development narrative | Shows the theme *changing* across the text with ≥4 well-chosen quotations | Shows development with ≥3 quotations | Retells plot or asserts the theme without showing change | /10 |
| Summary + skeptic's page | Summary opinion-free and complete; objection stated fairly and answered with evidence | Minor opinion leakage or thin answer | Opinions throughout, or objection straw-manned | /10 |

**Total: /40.** 32+ = ready for U02's heavier lifting; 24–31 = revise one
criterion with the adult; below 24 = rebuild the portfolio with adult
guidance before the unit assessment.

## Accessibility and alternatives

- The dossier may be built digitally (slides or a document) with the same
  six deliverables in order.
- The presentation may be recorded as audio/video instead of live.
- Evidence cards may be dictated and scribed; locations must still be
  exact.
