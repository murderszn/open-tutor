## Practice B — Session 3: inference sort + reading log launch

1. **Inference sort.** The adult reads these six statements aloud; sort
   each into OBSERVATION or INFERENCE and defend the sort:
   - a. Maya is sixteen years old.
   - b. Maya's grandmother has died.
   - c. The salt box sat unused for years.
   - d. Maya's mother feels guilty about the rolls too.
   - e. The family will keep cooking together from now on.
   - f. The recipe cards are in two different handwritings.
2. **Reading log launch.** Choose an independent book (any lawful source).
   Log format (daily, 20–30 min): date, pages, one-sentence objective
   summary of what you read, one inference with its evidence. The adult
   checks the log weekly.

**Success criterion:** all six sorted correctly with defenses; log format
set up and first entry complete. (Key: a = observation; b = inference —
the text says she moved to assisted living, never that she died; c =
inference — "never once seen salt in it" supports but doesn't state
duration; d = inference; e = inference and a weak one; f = observation.)

## Practice C — Session 5: evidence ranking

**Claim:** The kitchen's emptiness at the start of "The Salt Box" reflects
emotional loss, not just a move.

1. Find five candidate quotations; rank them 1–5 (strongest first). For
   each, write one sentence: what makes it strong or what disqualifies it.
2. Write one paragraph using your top two — one quoted with a lead-in, one
   paraphrased — with ("The Salt Box," par. N) citations.

**Success criterion:** ranked list with reasons; paragraph with two
correctly cited, correctly introduced pieces of evidence.

## Practice D — Session 6: embedded-citation workshop

Write three separate evidence paragraphs (5–7 sentences each), each proving
a different claim about "The Salt Box":

1. Maya changes over the course of the story.
2. The mother and Maya grieve differently.
3. The recipe cards function as communication between generations.

Each paragraph: claim sentence → quotation with lead-in and citation →
commentary → second piece of evidence (quote or paraphrase) → commentary →
closing sentence.

**Success criterion:** all three paragraphs follow the shape; no dropped
quotations; all citations in ("The Salt Box," par. N) form with correct
numbers.

## Practice F — Session 9: summary workshop + vocabulary

1. Write two objective summaries of "The Salt Box" (60–90 words each):
   one emphasizing *plot*, one emphasizing *theme development*. Compare:
   what did each version have to leave out?
2. **Vocabulary in context.** Use each word correctly in a sentence about
   this unit's work: *annotation, inference, explicit, evidence, citation,
   theme, topic, objective, summary, thorough, commentary, motivation,
   arc, skeptic, paraphrase.* The adult checks that each sentence shows the
   meaning — a definition copied from a dictionary does not count.

**Success criterion:** two summaries, each opinion-free and complete; 15
vocabulary sentences demonstrating meaning in context.

## Practice H — Session 12: Frankenstein chart + development paragraph

1. Finish the Lesson 4 chart for excerpts 4 and 5 (if not completed in the
   lesson).
2. Write the theme-development paragraph (8–12 sentences): final theme,
   then the chain — how each excerpt shaped or refined it — quoting briefly
   at least three times with locations (Letter 1 / Ch. 5 / Ch. 10 /
   Ch. 16 / Ch. 24).

**Success criterion:** completed chart; paragraph with ≥3 brief, correctly
located quotations and visible revision logic.

## Practice I — Session 14: character analysis + project launch

1. **Character paragraph:** choose Darnell or Ms. Okafor from "The
   Audition." Write 6–8 sentences: the character's conflicting motivations,
   the action/word gap, and how the character advances the plot or develops
   the theme. Two quotations with paragraph numbers.
2. **Project launch:** choose your Theme Case File text ("The Salt Box,"
   the Frankenstein chain, or "The Audition") and draft the theme claim.
   List six candidate evidence pieces with locations — you will need 8–10
   in the final dossier.

**Success criterion:** character paragraph with motivations, gap, and
theme/plot link; project text chosen with claim and six located candidates.

## Practice J — Sessions 16–18: essay drafting studio

Session-by-session use of Lesson 6:

- **Session 16:** write the skeleton (claim / development / evidence
  blocks); adult plays skeptic for five minutes; record the questions.
- **Session 17:** draft the essay (2–3 paragraphs, ~300–450 words)
  answering the skeptic's questions; run the revision checklist.
- **Session 18:** adult marks claim / lead-ins / citations / commentary /
  objection-answer; revise exactly the gaps. Write the skeptic's paragraph
  (strongest case against your own theme, 4–6 sentences with a quotation).

**Success criterion:** finished essay passing the revision checklist; a
separate skeptic's paragraph on file.
