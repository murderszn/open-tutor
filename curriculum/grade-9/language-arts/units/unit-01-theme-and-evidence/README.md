# Unit 01 — Literary Close Reading: Theme and Evidence

**Grade:** 9 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #44](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/44), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A close reader at grade 9 does four things: reads with a pen (annotation
with a purpose), selects evidence that is *strong and thorough*, states a
theme as an arguable claim and revises it as details accumulate, and builds
the case into an essay that answers its skeptic. This unit develops those
moves across four weeks — first on two original short stories, then on an
original informational passage, then on a verified five-excerpt chain from
Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein* (public domain), ending in a close-reading
essay and a Theme Case File dossier.

## Essential question

How do readers build a case — selecting and presenting evidence — that a
text's deeper meaning is really there?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the grade-8 track:

- Cite the strongest textual evidence for explicit and inferential claims
  (RL.8.1, RI.8.1); determine a theme and write an objective summary
  (RL.8.2); analyze word choice for tone (RL.8.4)
- Write complete, varied sentences; quote and paraphrase with attribution

The unit re-teaches annotation, quotation/paraphrase mechanics, and summary
from the ground up — no prior mastery is assumed. What is *new* in grade 9:
**thoroughness** (plural, distributed evidence), **theme revision** across
a text, and **complex characters** with conflicting motivations.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Cite **strong and thorough** textual evidence to support analysis of
   what literary and informational texts say explicitly and what can be
   inferred from them (RL.9-10.1, RI.9-10.1).
2. Determine a theme or central idea, analyze **in detail** its development
   over a text — how it emerges and is shaped and refined by specific
   details — and write an objective summary (RL.9-10.2, RI.9-10.2).
3. Analyze how a **complex character's** conflicting motivations,
   development, and interactions advance the plot or develop the theme
   (RL.9-10.3).
4. Integrate quotations and paraphrases with precise location citations,
   introduce and comment on evidence, and explain why chosen evidence is
   stronger than rejected alternatives (W.9-10.9).

## Materials

Notebook or paper, pen/pencil, two highlighter colors (or underline/circle
marks), index cards for the evidence portfolio and project, a folder or
binder for the Theme Case File dossier, the five lesson pages. No
purchases; no hazardous materials.

## 20-session pacing (4 weeks × 5 sessions, ~50 minutes each)

The six lessons are the direct-instruction core; the remaining sessions
are practice, reading, discussion, project work, and assessment. Each
session names its activity and success criterion — see the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) for the full map.

| Session | Week | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | Lesson | [L1 — Reading with a pen: annotation, observation, inference](lessons/lesson-01-annotation-reading-with-a-pen.md) (+ generated illustration) |
| 2 | 3 | Practice | [Practice A](practice.md#practice-a--session-2-annotation-drill) — annotation drill + evidence chart ("The Salt Box") |
| 3 | 3 | Practice | [Practice B](practice.md#practice-b--session-3-inference-sort--reading-log-launch) — inference sort; reading log launch |
| 4 | 3 | Lesson | [L2 — "Strong and thorough" evidence](lessons/lesson-02-strong-and-thorough-evidence.md) |
| 5 | 3 | Practice | [Practice C](practice.md#practice-c--session-5-evidence-ranking) — evidence ranking + embedded citations |
| 6 | 4 | Practice | [Practice D](practice.md#practice-d--session-6-embedded-citation-workshop) — embedded-citation workshop (3 paragraphs) |
| 7 | 4 | Lesson | [L3 — Theme is a claim: development + objective summary](lessons/lesson-03-theme-development-objective-summary.md) |
| 8 | 4 | Practice | [Practice E](practice.md#practice-e--session-8-theme-tracker) — theme tracker worksheet |
| 9 | 4 | Practice | [Practice F](practice.md#practice-f--session-9-summary-workshop--vocabulary) — summary workshop + vocabulary |
| 10 | 4 | Reading | [Practice G](practice.md#practice-g--session-10-informational-central-idea) — informational passage: central idea + evidence |
| 11 | 5 | Lesson | [L4 — Tracking theme across a full-length text](lessons/lesson-04-tracking-theme-across-a-full-text.md) (Frankenstein chain) |
| 12 | 5 | Practice | [Practice H](practice.md#practice-h--session-12-frankenstein-chart--development-paragraph) — chart + theme-development paragraph |
| 13 | 5 | Lesson | [L5 — Complex characters](lessons/lesson-05-complex-characters-theme-carriers.md) ("The Audition") |
| 14 | 5 | Practice | [Practice I](practice.md#practice-i--session-14-character-analysis--project-launch) — character analysis + Theme Case File launch |
| 15 | 5 | Quiz | [Formative quiz](assessments/week-05_quiz.md) (30 pts; reteach if < 24) |
| 16 | 6 | Lesson | [L6 — The close-reading essay](lessons/lesson-06-close-reading-essay.md) — architecture + skeptic |
| 17 | 6 | Writing | [Practice J](practice.md#practice-j--sessions-1618-essay-drafting-studio) — essay draft |
| 18 | 6 | Revision | Practice J — gap revision + skeptic's paragraph |
| 19 | 6 | Assessment | [Unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md) (50 pts, timed; new passage) |
| 20 | 6 | Review | Project presentations; misconception revisit; reading-log close |

Retrieval and feedback are built in: the reading log runs daily all four
weeks; every practice session is adult-reviewed the same day; the quiz
(session 15) gates the essay sessions with reteaching; the review session
revisits the week's misconceptions before U02.
