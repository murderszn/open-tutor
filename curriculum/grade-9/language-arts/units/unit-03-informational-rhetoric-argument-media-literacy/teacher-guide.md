# Teacher Guide — Unit 03 (Grade 9 Language Arts)

**Track weeks:** 11–14 · **Prerequisites:** U01 (close
reading, evidence, objective summary, skeptic move) and U02
(author choices, comparative analysis). U03 re-teaches
central-idea and summary skills from the ground up; what is
*new* here: argument delineation, fallacy naming, source
tracing across media, seminal-document theme tracing, and the
evaluation essay (which U06 will extend into full argument
essays).

## Readiness signals and reteach paths

- **Ready for week 12** if the learner's Practice A map names
  a real job for every paragraph and the Practice B summary
  contains no opinion words. If not, re-teach L1 with ¶5 as
  the worked example and re-do Practice A.
- **Ready for week 13** if Practice D's inventory quotes the
  text for every appeal and Practice F judges both editorials
  by the same criterion. If not, re-teach L2's worked example
  2 and re-do Practice D.
- **Ready for week 14** if the quiz is ≥ 24 and Practice G
  names a fallacy (not just "bad reasoning") for at least four
  of five snippets. If not, reteach L3 with S5 as the modeled
  example and re-quiz with new snippets before L5.
- **Ready for U04** if the unit assessment is ≥ 38 and the
  project dossier scores ≥ 14. Otherwise reteach L3 + L6 and
  reassess with a new passage.

## Misconception index

1. Topic ≠ central idea (L1). 2. Rhetoric ≠ dishonesty (L2).
3. A fallacious argument can have a true conclusion (L3).
4. Repeats ≠ confirmations; dependence ≠ falsehood (L5).
5. An independent observation proves only what was observed
   (L5). 6. Fair ≠ neutral; a fair essay can reach a strong
   verdict (L6). 7. Old-vs-new is not analysis; mechanism is
   (L4).

## Materials, safety, and content notes

- Notebook or paper, pen/pencil, the unit texts, index cards
  for source maps. No purchases; no hazardous materials.
- All unit texts are original except the Gettysburg Address
  (public domain, Bliss copy via the National Park Service).
  The modern response is labeled fictional wherever it appears.
- The Gettysburg Address concerns the Civil War and its dead;
  the unit contains no graphic content. The adult frames it as
  a cemetery dedication speech.
- Media-literacy work uses fictional outlets and events only;
  real-news application (Lesson 5's applied task, project
  Option 2) is adult-selected and adult-previewed.
- Accessibility: every lesson offers an alternate response
  mode (dictation, drawing, spoken recording); the Lesson 5
  image has a text-only alternative in the lesson itself.
