# Keep the Library Open Late

**An original editorial written for this unit. Any resemblance to a real
town or person is coincidental. All places, people, and figures below
are fictional practice material.**

---

Our town library closes at six o'clock every evening, and that single
fact decides who gets to use it. Students who finish sports practice at
five-thirty arrive to locked doors. Parents who work until six never get
in at all. The library is a public building, paid for by everyone, yet
its hours serve only the people whose schedules already fit.

The solution is simple: keep the library open until nine on weeknights.
Three extra hours would cost less than most residents think. The
library already pays for heat, light, and security through the evening
because the building hosts adult classes in two of its rooms. Extending
public hours would mainly mean staffing the front desk, not heating a
dark building.

Opponents say longer hours would invite trouble. They point to two
incidents last year when teenagers were loud in the parking lot after
closing. But those incidents happened *because* the building was
closed: with nowhere to go inside, young people gathered outside. An
open, staffed library is a calmer place than a locked one with a dark
parking lot.

Longer hours would also pay for themselves in a way that is easy to
miss. When the library ran a four-week trial of late hours last spring,
visits rose by a third, and the number of students finishing homework
in the study rooms doubled. A building that is used more is a building
the town is more likely to keep funding. Use creates support, and
support keeps the doors open.

Some will argue that books matter less now that everyone has a phone.
That misses what the library actually is. On any evening, the study
rooms hold students working in groups, job seekers using the computers,
and new residents practicing English with a tutor. The library is not
a warehouse for books. It is the one room in town where anyone can sit,
learn, and ask for help without paying.

We should stop treating six o'clock as a natural law. It is a choice,
made years ago for a town that no longer exists. Our town works later,
studies later, and needs its public rooms later. Keep the library open
until nine — and let everyone in.
