# The Fallacy Set — Eight Short Arguments

**Eight original argument snippets written for this unit. All people,
places, and figures are fictional practice material. Some snippets
argue soundly; others contain a named flaw. The flaws are identified
in the teacher guide and answer key — not here.**

All eight snippets concern the same fictional question: should
Willow Creek spend town money on a new bus route to the county
college?

---

**S1.** "The bus route is a good idea because every smart town has
one. Towns without college bus routes are simply behind the times."

**S2.** "Last year, 214 Willow Creek residents took classes at the
county college, and 68 percent of them told the town survey they
would ride a direct bus at least twice a week. A bus that runs
twice each morning and twice each evening would serve those riders
and cost less per trip than the town's current dial-a-ride van."

**S3.** "Council member Alvarez supports the bus route, but she
failed her driving test twice, so her judgment about transport
cannot be trusted."

**S4.** "Either we fund the bus route or we admit we do not care
about young people's futures. There is no middle position."

**S5.** "If we fund one bus route, next year every neighborhood will
demand its own route, the town budget will collapse, and taxes will
double within five years."

**S6.** "My neighbor's son rode a bus to college in another town and
dropped out after one semester, which proves bus routes do not help
students succeed."

**S7.** "The bus route would help working students, but it would
also add a late-evening bus on a residential street. That noise is a
real cost, and the town should weigh it against the benefit to
riders before deciding. On balance, the ridership numbers justify
the route, provided the last bus runs by 9:30 p.m."

**S8.** "No one at the town meeting spoke against the bus route, so
everyone in Willow Creek must support it."

---

**How to use this set:** Lesson 3 models S2 (sound) and S4
(false dilemma) in full. Practice G asks learners to classify S1,
S3, S5, S6, and S8, defend S7's reasoning, and rewrite one flawed
snippet so its claim matches its evidence.
