# Formative Quiz — Unit 03 (Session 15)

**30 points · ~30 minutes · closed notes except the unit
texts.** Covers Lessons 1–4 and the fallacy set. Reteach if
the total is below 24: revisit the lowest-scoring objective
with the lesson named in the key, then re-quiz with new
snippets. Answers and reteach notes are in
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#formative-quiz).

1. **(2 pts)** In one sentence, state the difference between a
   topic and a central idea, using the library editorial as
   your example.
2. **(3 pts)** The library editorial's ¶3 answers the
   "trouble" objection by reversing it. In two sentences,
   explain what job ¶3 does in the editorial's unfolding plan
   and what it connects to.
3. **(3 pts)** Name one appeal (logos, ethos, or pathos) in
   Editorial A ("Build the Skate Park"), ¶1. Quote the words
   that carry it and explain the appeal in one sentence.
4. **(3 pts)** Classify S1 from the Fallacy Set: name the
   flaw and the test it fails (validity, relevance, or
   sufficiency).
5. **(3 pts)** Classify S3 from the Fallacy Set: name the
   flaw and the test it fails.
6. **(3 pts)** Classify S8 from the Fallacy Set: name the
   flaw and the test it fails.
7. **(4 pts)** Quote the Gettysburg Address sentence in which
   Lincoln transfers dedication from the ground to the
   living, and explain the transfer in two sentences.
8. **(4 pts)** In the modern response ("At the Memorial"),
   what changes and what stays the same compared with
   Lincoln's speech? Answer in three sentences, quoting at
   least one phrase from the response.
9. **(3 pts)** In "Three Reports, One Announcement," how many
   independent roots support the inspection claim, and which
   accounts share the root? Answer in two sentences.
10. **(2 pts)** A classmate says, "Three outlets reported the
    pavilion is ready, so it must be ready." In one sentence,
    correct the reasoning without claiming the pavilion is
    unsafe.
