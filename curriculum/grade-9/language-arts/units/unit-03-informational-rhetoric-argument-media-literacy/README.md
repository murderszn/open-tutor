# Unit 03 — Informational Rhetoric, Argument, and Media Literacy

**Grade:** 9 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #44](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/44), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

U01 taught learners to read with a pen and build evidence-based
cases about theme; U02 turned the lens to how authors make
meaning. U03 turns it outward: how informational authors argue,
how to test their arguments, and how to tell one source from
many. Learners trace a central idea through an editorial,
inventory rhetoric across paired editorials, name fallacies and
fix them, read Lincoln's Gettysburg Address against a modern
response, trace a news claim to its root, and end by judging an
argument fairly in an evaluation essay. The throughline
question: **"Who says so, how do you know, and does the
reasoning hold?"** The unit ends in a Source Audit Dossier and
a timed unit assessment on a new passage.

## Essential question

How do you judge whether an argument — and the sources behind
it — deserve your belief?

## Prerequisites

From U01, U02, and the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2):

- Annotate with a purpose; distinguish observation from inference
- Cite strong and thorough textual evidence (RL.9-10.1, now
  applied to informational text as RI.9-10.1); write objective
  summaries (RL.9-10.2, now RI.9-10.2)
- Analyze complex characters and author choices (U02); compare
  two texts in a structured analysis (W.9-10.9)
- Build a close-reading essay with the skeptic move (U01
  Lesson 6) and a comparative analysis essay (U02 Lesson 6)

The unit re-teaches central-idea tracing and objective summary
from the ground up in informational text — no prior mastery is
assumed. What is *new* in U03: **claim-tracing maps**,
**rhetoric inventories** (appeals, repetition, framing),
**argument delineation and fallacy naming**, **seminal-document
theme tracing**, **source tracing across media** (one root vs.
independent corroboration, lateral reading), **speaker
evaluation**, and the **evaluation essay** (which U06 extends
into full argument essays).

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Determine a central idea of an informational text, analyze
   its development over the text (including how it emerges and
   is shaped by specific details), and provide an objective
   summary (RI.9-10.2, RI.9-10.3, RI.9-10.5).
2. Determine an author's point of view or purpose and analyze
   how the author uses rhetoric — appeals, repetition,
   framing, word choice — to advance that point of view or
   purpose (RI.9-10.4, RI.9-10.6).
3. Delineate and evaluate an argument and its specific claims,
   assessing whether the reasoning is valid and the evidence
   is relevant and sufficient, and identifying false
   statements and fallacious reasoning (RI.9-10.8).
4. Analyze a seminal U.S. document (the Gettysburg Address)
   and trace how it addresses themes a modern text takes up
   again (RI.9-10.9).
5. Analyze accounts of one subject told in different media,
   determining which details each emphasizes; integrate
   multiple sources while evaluating each source's credibility
   and accuracy; and evaluate a speaker's point of view,
   reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric (RI.9-10.7,
   SL.9-10.2, SL.9-10.3).
6. Write an evaluation essay that introduces a precise claim,
   develops claims and counterclaims fairly with relevant
   evidence, uses formal style and objective tone, and
   concludes with the change that would most strengthen the
   argument (W.9-10.1, W.9-10.9).

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for English
Language Arts, grades 9–10 band** (thecorestandards.org
ELA-Literacy). Codes and descriptions were verified against the
official standard wording on 2026-10-27 (RI.9-10.1–9; SL.9-10.2–3);
the unit paraphrases them below. No state adoption,
accreditation, or alignment certification is claimed.

| Unit objective | Standard | Official description (abridged paraphrase) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | RI.9-10.1, RI.9-10.2, RI.9-10.3, RI.9-10.5 | Cite strong and thorough evidence; determine a central idea and analyze its development, including how it emerges and is shaped by details; provide an objective summary; analyze how an author unfolds an analysis or series of ideas; analyze how ideas or claims are developed and refined by particular sentences, paragraphs, or larger portions. |
| 2 | RI.9-10.4, RI.9-10.6 | Determine the meaning of words and phrases, including figurative, connotative, and technical meanings; analyze the cumulative impact of word choices on meaning and tone; determine an author's point of view or purpose and analyze how the author uses rhetoric to advance it. |
| 3 | RI.9-10.8 | Delineate and evaluate the argument and specific claims in a text, assessing whether the reasoning is valid and the evidence is relevant and sufficient; identify false statements and fallacious reasoning. |
| 4 | RI.9-10.9 | Analyze seminal U.S. documents of historical and literary significance (including the Gettysburg Address), including how they address related themes and concepts. |
| 5 | RI.9-10.7, SL.9-10.2, SL.9-10.3 | Analyze various accounts of a subject told in different mediums, determining which details are emphasized in each account; integrate multiple sources of information presented in diverse media or formats, evaluating the credibility and accuracy of each source; evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric, identifying fallacious reasoning or exaggerated or distorted evidence. |
| 6 | W.9-10.1, W.9-10.9 | Write arguments to support claims with clear reasons and relevant evidence, including fair treatment of counterclaims, formal style, and a concluding statement; draw evidence from literary or informational texts to support analysis, reflection, and research. |

## 20-session pacing (4 weeks × 5 sessions, ~50 minutes each)

The six lessons are the direct-instruction core; the remaining
sessions are practice, reading, discussion, project work, and
assessment. Each session names its activity and success
criterion — see the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) for the
full map.

| Session | Week | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 11 | Lesson | [L1 — Central idea and development](lessons/lesson-01-central-idea-and-development.md) |
| 2 | 11 | Practice | [Practice A](practice.md#practice-a--session-2-claim-tracing-maps) — claim-tracing maps (¶3, ¶5, ¶6) |
| 3 | 11 | Practice | [Practice B](practice.md#practice-b--session-3-objective-summaries) — objective summary + persuasive-language audit |
| 4 | 11 | Reading | Library editorial reread; reading log ("claim I would test") |
| 5 | 11 | Practice | [Practice C](practice.md#practice-c--session-5-the-hardest-working-paragraph) — hardest-working-paragraph argument + skeptic paragraph |
| 6 | 12 | Lesson | [L2 — Rhetoric and purpose](lessons/lesson-02-rhetoric-and-purpose.md) (appeals, repetition, framing) |
| 7 | 12 | Practice | [Practice D](practice.md#practice-d--session-7-rhetoric-inventory) — rhetoric inventory for Editorial B |
| 8 | 12 | Practice | [Practice E](practice.md#practice-e--session-8-genre-contrast-paragraph) — neutral-report rewrite |
| 9 | 12 | Discussion | Rhetoric seminar: "Which editorial argues more honestly?" (quote-backed, no vote) |
| 10 | 12 | Practice | [Practice F](practice.md#practice-f--session-10-paired-editorial-comparison) — comparison on evidence quality |
| 11 | 13 | Lesson | [L3 — Evaluating arguments](lessons/lesson-03-evaluating-arguments.md) (validity, relevance, sufficiency, fallacies) |

| 15 | 13 | Quiz | [Formative quiz](assessments/week-13_quiz.md) (30 pts; reteach if < 24) |
| 16 | 14 | Lesson | [L5 — Media literacy](lessons/lesson-05-media-literacy.md) (+ generated illustration; source tracing; speaker evaluation) |

Retrieval and feedback are built in: the reading log runs daily
all four weeks (now with a weekly "claim I would test" entry);
every practice session is adult-reviewed the same day; the quiz
(session 15) gates week 14 with reteaching; the review session
revisits misconceptions before U04 (grammar precision, which
will edit U01–U03 pieces).
