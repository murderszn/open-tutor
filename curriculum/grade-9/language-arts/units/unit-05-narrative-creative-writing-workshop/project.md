# Project — The Story Workshop Portfolio

**Launched:** session 8 · **Presented:** session 20 (author readings) ·
**Weight:** major project, scored with the rubric below (40 pts)

## The task

You are building a **portfolio** with three parts: a finished short
story, a craft log that proves you made deliberate choices, and an
author reading. The portfolio is the unit's culminating evidence that
you can write a narrative — not just analyze one.

**Part 1 — The final story (1,200–2,000 words).**
A complete short story, revised through three passes (structure,
language, conventions). It must contain: a specific problem; a
character whose want and need pull in opposite directions; an event
sequence in which each event causes the next; a consistent point of
view; at least two scenes of dialogue that reveal character; at least
one deliberate scene/summary choice; precise sensory language; and a
reflective conclusion. The story may grow from one of the unit's story
seeds or from your own idea; if you use a seed, say so in the craft
log and note what you changed.

**Part 2 — The craft log (500–800 words).**
A working record of the choices you made, kept across the unit and
finished in session 18. It has four sections:

1. **Plan** (from Lessons 1–2 and Practices A–C): the problem, the
   want and need, the six-event chain, the POV decision memo.
2. **Drafting notes** (from Lessons 3–5 and Practices D–F): the
   dialogue, pacing, and reflection choices you made while drafting —
   and the part of the draft that surprised you.
3. **Revision record** (from Lesson 6 and Practices G–H): the
   cut/keep/move list and the language change log (at least 8 changes,
   in before → after pairs).
4. **Reflection** (150–200 words): what did you learn about your own
   writing in this unit, and what is the one craft move you will carry
   into U06's argument essays? (A craft move that only works in
   stories does not count — name one that crosses over, such as
   sentence-length control or the removal test.)

**Part 3 — The author reading (2–3 minutes).**
Read a 300–400 word excerpt of your final story aloud — usually the
climax or the reflective conclusion — with deliberate delivery choices
(pauses, speed changes, eye contact at paragraph ends). The reading is
scored on the *delivery choices*, not on performance polish. A
recorded reading is an accepted alternative to a live one.

## Materials

Notebook or paper, the unit texts, the illustration, the rubric below.
No purchases. Optional digital publication (W.9-10.6) — the paper
manuscript is the no-technology alternative.

## Presentation (session 20)

A 2–3 minute author reading of one excerpt, followed by one question
from the adult: "What is the one choice in this excerpt you most want
the listener to notice — and what does it do?" You answer in 2–3
sentences. You are scored on the explanation and the delivery choices,
not on performance.

## Rubric (40 pts)

| Criterion | Excellent (9–10) | Proficient (7–8) | Developing (5–6) | Beginning (0–4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Story architecture** (10) | Specific problem; want and need pull in opposite directions; every event causes the next; climax is the want/need collision | Problem and want/need present; chain mostly causal; climax identifiable | Problem vague or want/need point the same way; chain has gaps | No clear problem or chain; events are a list |
| **Craft techniques** (10) | Dialogue reveals character (beats + subtext); scene/summary chosen for purpose; sensory language precise (one detail per sense); multiple plot lines meet | Techniques present and mostly purposeful; one technique decorative | Techniques attempted but often telling instead of showing; pacing uncontrolled | Dialogue announces; description is judgment adjectives; no scene/summary control |
| **Revision** (10) | Three passes visible in the craft log: real cuts, a move, 8+ language changes with reasons; conventions clean (MLA format, dialogue punctuation) | Passes visible; cuts and changes present; minor convention errors | One pass missing or superficial (proofreading only); change log thin | No revision evidence; draft is the first draft |
| **Conclusion + reading** (10) | Reflective conclusion passes the four-part audit; reading excerpt well chosen with marked, audible delivery choices; the one-choice answer is precise | Conclusion passes 3 of 4 audit items; reading rehearsed with some delivery choices | Conclusion announces a lesson or only ends the story; reading unrehearsed | No reflective conclusion; no reading |

**Scoring notes for the adult:** a story can score Excellent below the
1,200-word minimum only if the adult approved a shorter length in
advance for a documented support reason — otherwise the word count is
part of the task, and a story under 1,200 words caps Architecture at
Proficient. Precision beats length above the minimum: a 1,300-word
story that does everything well outscores a 2,000-word story that
wanders. The craft log is scored on the *specificity* of its choices,
not its length — a log that names exact lines and exact changes beats a
long log of generalities. See
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md#project-notes) for annotated sample
responses at each level.
