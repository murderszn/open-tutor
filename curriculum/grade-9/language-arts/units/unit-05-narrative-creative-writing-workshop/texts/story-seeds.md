# Story Seeds — Unit 05 (Grade 9 Language Arts)

Three original story seeds for Practice A and for any learner who wants
a starting point instead of inventing one from nothing. A **seed** is
not a story: it is a character, a want, a complication, and one opening
line. The learner grows the seed into a full story in Lessons 1–6 and
the project. Each seed is written so that the want and the need pull in
opposite directions — that tension is what makes a story worth writing.

---

## Seed 1 — "The Unsent Letter"

**Character:** Marcus, 15, who works Saturday mornings shelving books at
the public library.

**Situation:** Inside a returned copy of *Treasure Island*, Marcus finds
an envelope addressed in careful handwriting to someone named "E." The
letter inside was never sent. It is dated forty years ago, and it
apologizes for something.

**Want:** Marcus wants to find E. and deliver the letter — it feels like
the kind of thing a good person does, and it would make a great story to
tell.

**Need:** Marcus needs to learn that some apologies belong to the people
who wrote them, not to whoever finds them — and that curiosity is not
the same as kindness.

**Complication:** The library's records show the book was checked out
only twice in forty years, both times by the same family name. The second
borrower still lives three streets away.

**Opening line (use it, change it, or write your own):**
> The envelope had been used as a bookmark for so long that the book had
> grown around it, the way a tree grows around a fence.

## Seed 2 — "The Last Game"

**Character:** Priya, 16, goalkeeper for a school team that has lost
every game this season. Tonight is the last game, against the
undefeated team from across town.

**Situation:** Ten minutes before kickoff, Priya learns that the
opposing striker is her former best friend, Lena — the two have not
spoken since Lena changed schools last year.

**Want:** Priya wants to win — one win, just one, to end the season
with something other than a zero.

**Need:** Priya needs to stop keeping score against Lena and start
playing the game in front of her; the friendship will not be fixed by
a scoreline either way.

**Complication:** In the second half, Lena goes down injured in front
of Priya's goal — and only Priya sees that the referee missed the foul
that caused it.

**Opening line (use it, change it, or write your own):**
> Priya had always liked the ten minutes before a game, when the field
> was still empty and the grass still believed whatever you told it.

## Seed 3 — "The Recipe Box"

**Character:** Ana, 14, who has just moved with her family into her
late grandmother's house.

**Situation:** In the kitchen, Ana finds her grandmother's recipe box.
Every card is written in the same neat hand — except one card, the one
for the birthday cake her grandmother made every year, which is missing.
In its place is a note: "Ask Rosa."

**Want:** Ana wants the recipe back exactly as it was, so this year's
birthday can feel normal again.

**Need:** Ana needs to accept that the cake was never really about the
recipe — it was about the person who made it, and the person who will
have to make it now.

**Complication:** Rosa is her grandmother's oldest friend, and Rosa has
not spoken to Ana's family since an argument nobody will explain.

**Opening line (use it, change it, or write your own):**
> The recipe box smelled like cinnamon and pencil shavings, and it was
> the first thing in the new house that felt like it belonged to her.

---

## How to use a seed

1. **Choose** one seed, or combine two (a seed's character with another
   seed's complication is allowed — note the change in your craft log).
2. **Name the want and the need** in your own words before you draft
   (Practice B). If they point the same way, find the tension — a story
   with no pull between want and need is a list of events, not a plot.
3. **Keep the opening line only if it earns its place.** A good first
   line makes a promise the story must keep. If your story changes
   direction, change the line.
