# Unit 05 — Narrative and Creative Writing Workshop

**Grade:** 9 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 20–23
**Backlog:** [issue #44](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/44), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

U01 taught learners to read with a pen and build evidence-based cases
about theme. U02 turned the lens to how authors *make* meaning — plot
order, character, point of view, pacing, word choice. U03 taught
learners to evaluate arguments, and U04 gave them the grammar, syntax,
and vocabulary to control their sentences. U05 is where all of that
becomes **making**: the learner writes a complete short story
(1,200–2,000 words), from seed to first draft to three-pass revision,
and then reads an excerpt aloud to an audience. The throughline
question: **"What does your story want to be about — and what craft
choices will make a reader feel it?"**

The mentor text is the original story ["The Long Way
Home"](texts/the-long-way-home.md), written for this unit, with a
generated illustration of its decision moment. The learner studies how
that story is built, then builds their own — using a provided story seed
or one of their own — with the same tools: want vs. need, event
sequences that cause one another, point of view, dialogue, pacing,
sensory language, reflection, and a reflective conclusion.

## Essential question

How do writers turn a situation into a story — and a story into an
experience a reader feels?

## Prerequisites

From U01–U04, the diagnostic weeks, and the week-19 midyear review:

- Cite strong and thorough textual evidence; determine theme and write
  objective summaries (U01; RL.9-10.1, RL.9-10.2)
- Name the six parts of a plot arc; separate story order from
  chronological order; analyze complex characters, point of view,
  narrative distance, pacing, and cumulative word choice (U02;
  RL.9-10.3–6)
- Delineate and evaluate arguments; recognize how claims develop
  (U03; RI.9-10.5, RI.9-10.8)
- Control sentence architecture: clauses, phrase variety, semicolons,
  vocabulary systems (U04; L.9-10.1.b, L.9-10.2.a, L.9-10.4)
- Write a close-reading essay and a comparative analysis essay (U01
  Lesson 6, U02 Lesson 6) — the learner has drafted and revised
  sustained prose before

What is *new* in U05: **writing a narrative from nothing** (not
analyzing someone else's); **want vs. need** as the engine of plot;
**dialogue as characterization** rather than information delivery;
**pacing as a deliberate choice** between scene and summary; **multiple
plot lines**; **reflection** inside a narrative; and **reading one's own
work aloud** as a performance with delivery choices.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Design a story's architecture — a problem or situation, a character
   whose **want** and **need** pull in opposite directions, and an event
   sequence in which each event causes the next (W.9-10.3.a).
2. Establish a point of view and narrator, keep them consistent, and
   explain in a decision memo what the choice gains and what it loses
   (W.9-10.3.a).
3. Use narrative techniques — dialogue, pacing (scene vs. summary),
   description, reflection, and multiple plot lines — to develop
   experiences, events, and characters (W.9-10.3.b).
4. Use precise words and phrases, telling details, and sensory language
   to convey a vivid picture of experiences, events, setting, and
   characters (W.9-10.3.d); revise a draft in three passes — structure,
   language, conventions (W.9-10.5).
5. Provide a conclusion that follows from and reflects on what is
   experienced, observed, or resolved (W.9-10.3.e), and present an
   excerpt orally with strategic delivery choices in formal English
   (SL.9-10.4, SL.9-10.6).

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for English Language
Arts, grades 9–10 band** (thecorestandards.org ELA-Literacy). Codes and
descriptions were verified in the track audit on 2026-10-06; the audit
makes no claim of state adoption, accreditation, or certification.

- **W.9-10.3** — Write narratives to develop real or imagined
  experiences or events using effective technique, well-chosen details,
  and well-structured event sequences. (a) Engage and orient the reader
  by setting out a problem, situation, or observation and its
  significance, establishing one or multiple point(s) of view, and
  introducing a narrator and/or characters; create a smooth progression
  of experiences or events. (b) Use narrative techniques, such as
  dialogue, pacing, description, reflection, and multiple plot lines,
  to develop experiences, events, and/or characters. (c) Use a variety
  of techniques to sequence events so that they build on one another
  to create a coherent whole. (d) Use precise words and phrases,
  telling details, and sensory language to convey a vivid picture of
  the experiences, events, setting, and/or characters. (e) Provide a
  conclusion that follows from and reflects on what is experienced,
  observed, or resolved over the course of the narrative.
- **W.9-10.4** — Produce clear and coherent writing in which the
  development, organization, and style are appropriate to task,
  purpose, and audience (the story, the craft log, and the author
  reading are three different tasks for three different audiences).
- **W.9-10.5** — Develop and strengthen writing as needed by planning,
  revising, editing, rewriting, or trying a new approach (the
  three-pass revision cycle, Lessons 5–6 and Practices G–H).
- **W.9-10.6** — Use technology, including the Internet, to produce,
  publish, and update individual or shared writing products (optional
  digital publication of the final story; the paper manuscript is the
  no-technology alternative).
- **SL.9-10.4** — Present information, findings, and supporting
  evidence clearly, concisely, and logically (the author reading,
  session 20).
- **SL.9-10.6** — Adapt speech to a variety of contexts and tasks,
  demonstrating command of formal English (rehearsed delivery choices
  in Lesson 6).
- **L.9-10.1.b, L.9-10.2, L.9-10.3.a** — Reinforced in the revision
  passes (U04 tools applied to the learner's own story): varied phrases
  and clauses, semicolons, and conformity to a style manual (MLA for
  the manuscript) in the conventions pass.
- **RL.9-10.3, RL.9-10.5** — Reinforced as reading skills: the mentor
  story is analyzed for complex character development and for the
  author's structural choices before the learner writes (Lessons 1–2).

All unit texts are original to this unit. The one public-domain model
story recommended in the Resource Pack — O. Henry's "The Gift of the
Magi" (1905) — is linked, not reproduced.

## Differentiation

- **Reading access:** the adult may read the mentor story aloud while
  the learner follows; the analysis is the work, not the decoding.
  "The Gift of the Magi" (Resource Pack) is an optional strong-reader
  extension, not required reading.
- **Alternate response modes:** story maps, charts, and timelines may be
  drawn, dictated, or built with index cards; drafts may be dictated or
  recorded as audio first, then transcribed. Exit checks may be answered
  orally. The author reading may be recorded rather than performed live.
- **Extensions:** write a second story from a different seed in a
  different point of view; revise the mentor story's ending from Mrs.
  Okafor's point of view; study "The Gift of the Magi" for its twist
  structure and write a 300-word analysis of how O. Henry withholds
  information.
- **Support:** for learners new to long-form drafting, the 1,200-word
  minimum may be reached across two sittings with the adult scribing
  the plan; the unit assessment may be split across two sittings
  without penalty; score its two tasks separately and conference
  before recording.

## How the unit is assessed

- **Formative quiz** (session 15, 30 pts): want/need identification,
  event-sequence analysis, POV choice, dialogue revision, pacing
  judgment, sensory revision.
- **Unit assessment** (session 19, 50 pts, timed, new original
  passage): craft analysis (identify and explain three techniques in a
  passage the learner has not seen) + revision task (rewrite a flat
  passage using two techniques, with a change log).
- **Project — The Story Workshop Portfolio** (40 pts): final story
  (1,200–2,000 words) + craft log + author reading. See
  [project.md](project.md) and the rubric there.
- Full solutions and scoring guidance are in
  [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) (adult-only) and the
  [teacher-guide.md](teacher-guide.md).
