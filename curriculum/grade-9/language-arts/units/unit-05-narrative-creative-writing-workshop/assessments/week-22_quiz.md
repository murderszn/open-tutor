# Formative Quiz — Unit 05, Week 22 (Session 15)

**Grade 9 Language Arts · 30 points · ~25 minutes · Closed book
(the mentor story and vignettes are not in front of you; all questions
give you the text you need)**
**Answer-key reference:** [answer-key.md §
Week-22 quiz](../answer-key.md#week-22-quiz)

**Instructions:** answer every question. For short answers, one or two
sentences are enough — but name the *mechanism*, not just the label.
Score: 30 points. Reteach if below 24 (see the teacher guide).

---

## A. Want vs. need (6 pts)

Read this story opening (original, written for this quiz):

> Lena had exactly one hour to get across town before the library
> closed, and the only bus that went her way had just broken down two
> stops from her. In her backpack was a library book, three weeks
> overdue, and a letter she had written to her grandmother and never
> sent. She could run the whole way and make it before closing — or she
> could stop at the post office on the corner and mail the letter, and
> miss the library entirely.

1. (2 pts) In one sentence each, name Lena's **want** and her **need**.
   (Hint: the want is the fast fix; the need is the deeper one.)
2. (2 pts) What is the **problem**, stated as specifically as you can
   in one sentence?
3. (2 pts) Name **one event** that could come next and would make the
   want/need pull *stronger* — and say in one sentence why it tightens
   the tension rather than resolving it.

## B. Event sequences (6 pts)

4. (3 pts) These four events are from a story, out of order. Number
   them 1–4 in the order that makes each event *cause* the next, and
   write the cause on the arrow between each pair:
   - (a) Maya tells the teacher what happened.
   - (b) Maya finds a wallet on the bus.
   - (c) The teacher asks Maya to stay after class.
   - (d) Maya's classmate accuses her of stealing the wallet.
5. (3 pts) Apply the **removal test** to event (b): if Maya never finds
   the wallet, what breaks in the chain? Write two sentences — one
   naming what disappears, one naming what the story would have to
   invent to replace it.

## C. Point of view (6 pts)

6. (3 pts) A story is told in third-person limited, staying close to a
   boy named Theo. In the climax, the narration reports: "His mother,
   watching from the doorway, thought he had finally learned his
   lesson." What is wrong with this sentence? Name the error and the
   fix (two sentences).
7. (3 pts) The same story, told in first person by Theo, cannot show
   the reader what his mother is thinking. Name **one** thing the
   writer can do instead to let the reader know what the mother thinks
   — and give a one-sentence example of it in action. (Hint: what
   people do with their hands, and what they avoid saying.)

## D. Dialogue and pacing (6 pts)

8. (3 pts) Rewrite this exchange so that neither character announces a
   feeling. Keep the same information (he is angry; she is sorry).
   4–6 lines, at least one beat:
   > "I am very angry with you," Tom said. "I am sorry," Sara said.
   > "I know you are sorry, but I am still angry."
9. (3 pts) A story spends a full page describing, moment by moment, a
   character riding an elevator to the third floor — and then summarizes
   the argument that ends her friendship in two sentences. What is
   wrong with this pacing? Name the error (one sentence) and say which
   gear each moment should use (one sentence).

## E. Sensory language (6 pts)

10. (3 pts) Rewrite this flat sentence with **one telling detail** and
    **no judgment adjectives**:
    > The old house was scary and creepy.
    (One or two sentences. A telling detail is one small, specific
    thing that implies a large truth.)
11. (3 pts) **Error analysis:** a classmate writes, "The very dark,
    super creepy, extremely old house was terrifying." Explain in 2–3
    sentences why adding more adjectives did not fix the flat sentence
    — and what the writer should do instead.
