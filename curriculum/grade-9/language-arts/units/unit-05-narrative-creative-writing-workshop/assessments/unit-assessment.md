# Unit Assessment — Unit 05 (Grade 9 Language Arts)

**Grade 9 Language Arts · 50 points · ~50 minutes, timed · New passage
(the learner has not seen it before)**
**Answer-key reference:** [answer-key.md § Unit
assessment](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment)

**Instructions:** read the passage below (original, written for this
assessment), then complete both tasks. Work alone and in silence. You
may use your notebook for planning; the passage stays in front of you.
Score: 50 points. Tasks are scored separately (Task 1: 30 pts; Task 2:
20 pts); conference before recording if one task is much weaker than
the other.

---

## The passage — "The Last Rehearsal" (original)

Read the passage carefully. It is a complete short scene, not an
excerpt from a longer story.

¶1. The auditorium smelled like dust and old paint, and the stage
lights buzzed faintly, the way old lights do when they are deciding
whether to stay on.

¶2. Nadia stood in the wings with her script in both hands, the pages
soft at the corners from a month of folding and unfolding. Her part was
four lines. She had rehearsed them two hundred times, in the shower, on
the bus, to her little brother, who had finally said, "I know, Nadia. I
know all four lines now too."

¶3. "Places," the director called. Nadia stepped into the light. The
audience was mostly empty chairs — a rehearsal, not a performance — but
her throat closed anyway, the way it always did.

¶4. She said her first line to the back wall, because the back wall
could not judge her. It came out flat. The second line came out worse.
On the third line, she forgot the words entirely and stood there, the
silence stretching, while the director watched her from the third row
with his pen still.

¶5. Then, from the back of the auditorium, somebody clapped — once,
slowly. Nadia looked up. It was her little brother, sitting alone in
the last row, holding up her script like a flag. He mouthed the third
line at her. She said it. She said the fourth line after it, louder
than she had said anything all month.

¶6. The director stood up. "Again," he said. "From the top. And this
time, Nadia — talk to *him*." He pointed at the last row. "The back
wall doesn't need you. He does."

¶7. She did it again. This time she did not look at the wall at all.

## Task 1 — Craft analysis (30 pts)

Answer **all three** parts. Write in sentences, not labels — name the
mechanism each time (how the choice creates its effect).

1. **(10 pts) Want vs. need.** Name Nadia's want and her need, each in
   one sentence, and explain in 2–3 sentences how the passage shows
   the two pulling in opposite directions. Quote **one** line that
   proves the want and **one** line that proves the need.
2. **(10 pts) Two techniques.** Identify **two** narrative techniques
   from this unit that the passage uses (choose from: dialogue,
   pacing/scene vs. summary, description/sensory language, reflection,
   multiple plot lines, foreshadowing, point of view). For each
   technique: name it, quote the exact line or lines where it appears,
   and explain in 2–3 sentences *how* it creates its effect on the
   reader — step by step, not just "it makes it vivid."
3. **(10 pts) The turning point.** Which paragraph is the passage's
   turning point — the moment where the story changes direction — and
   why? Write a paragraph (4–6 sentences) that names the paragraph,
   explains what changes there, and explains what the passage would
   lose if that paragraph were cut. (Hint: the turning point is not
   always the loudest moment.)

## Task 2 — Revision (20 pts)

Read the flat passage below (original, written for this assessment).
It tells the same kind of moment as "The Last Rehearsal" — a performer
freezing — but it tells it flatly.

> Elena was very nervous before her violin recital. She was scared she
> would make a mistake. Her teacher was nice and told her to relax. She
> played the first notes and they sounded bad. Then she remembered to
> breathe and she played better. Everyone was happy at the end.

1. **(12 pts) Rewrite** the passage (8–12 sentences) using **at least
   two** of these techniques: dialogue with a beat, sensory language
   (one detail per sense), scene instead of summary for the turning
   moment, sentence-length control for pacing. Keep the same events
   (nervous → bad start → breath → better → end). Do not add new
   events.
2. **(8 pts) Change log.** List **at least four** changes you made, in
   before → after pairs, and for each one write one sentence saying
   which technique it uses and what it gains. (Example format: Before:
   "She was scared she would make a mistake." → After: [your sentence].
   Technique: sensory language. Gain: the reader feels the fear instead
   of being told about it.)
