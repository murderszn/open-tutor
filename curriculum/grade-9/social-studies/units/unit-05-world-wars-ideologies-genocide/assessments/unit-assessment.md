# Unit 05 Assessment — World Wars, Ideologies, Genocide, and Historical Evidence

**Unit 05 · Week 23, Session 5 · ~50 minutes · Culminating**

Answer in your notebook. Score with the [answer key](../answer-key.md)
(Assessment section). Parts A–C are short answers; Part D is the
extended argument. Total 30 points.

## Part A — Causes and course (9 points)

**A1.** (3 points) Explain how militarism, alliances, and nationalism
interacted to turn the assassination of Franz Ferdinand into a
European war. One fact per cause.

**A2.** (3 points) Name three terms of the Treaty of Versailles and
explain, for each, why it was contested after 1919.

**A3.** (3 points) Compare the First and Second World Wars in one
paragraph (4–5 sentences): one similarity in how they started, and
two differences — one about who died, one about how the war was
fought.

## Part B — Ideologies and evidence (11 points)

**B1.** (3 points) Define liberalism, fascism, and communism as the
people of the 1930s understood them, using the same comparison frame
for all three (who holds power; how dissent is treated).

**B2.** (4 points) A classmate says, "The Great Depression caused WWII."
Explain why that claim is incomplete: name one way the Depression
mattered and one way leaders' decisions mattered, with a fact for
each.

**B3.** (4 points) Using the Holocaust as your example, name two
different kinds of historical evidence, give one specific example of
each, and state one limit of each kind. Then state the death-toll
figure and its source.

## Part C — Institutions and memory (4 points)

**C1.** (2 points) Name two postwar institutions, state each one's
purpose, and name one limit of each.

**C2.** (2 points) Two textbooks describe the end of WWII
differently: one emphasizes liberation, the other emphasizes its own
country's 26.6 million dead. What does each emphasize, what does each
omit, and what does D2.His.6.9-12 tell a historian to do with that
difference?

## Part D — Extended argument (6 points)

**D1.** "Did WWII resolve or relocate the crises of the interwar
years?" Write 8–10 sentences with: a clear claim; three or more
pieces of evidence from at least two lessons; a counterclaim and your
reply; and one sentence on what your evidence cannot show. (Scored
with the project rubric's Argument row: claim 1, evidence 2,
counterclaim 1, limits 1, accuracy 1.)

**Scoring:** 26–30 = exemplary; 21–25 = proficient; 15–20 =
developing (re-teach flagged lessons, then resubmit Part D); below 15
= re-teach the unit's core lessons before U06. Record standing in the
[teacher guide](../teacher-guide.md) §6.
