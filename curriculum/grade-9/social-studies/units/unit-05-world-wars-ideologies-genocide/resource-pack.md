# Unit 05 Resource Pack

**Goal (one sentence):** Give a grade-9 learner (and guiding adult)
reputable, free, no-account sources for the First World War's causes
and settlement, the interwar crisis and ideologies, the Second World
War's scope and home fronts, the evidence of genocide, and the postwar
institutions — checked 2026-11-12.

**Age fit:** Grade 9 (ages 14–15). Videos supplement the lessons; they
never replace close reading. War and genocide are handled with adult
preview (see teacher guide §8); learner-facing excerpts in the lessons
are original prose or short public-domain passages, and no graphic
imagery is used anywhere in the unit.

## Web references (checked 2026-11-12)

1. **United States Holocaust Memorial Museum — "Introduction to the
   Holocaust"** (Holocaust Encyclopedia) — the six-million figure, the
   1933–1945 frame, the "Final Solution," and why the Nazis targeted
   Jews; the unit's core Holocaust reference. Free, no account.
   Relevance: Lesson 04; Practice D1–D2; Project Part 3.
   https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/introduction-to-the-holocaust
2. **National WWII Museum — "Research Starters: Worldwide Deaths in
   World War II"** — ~15 million battle deaths, ~45 million civilian
   deaths, with the note that Chinese civilian deaths alone may be far
   higher; the unit's WWII casualty reference. Free, no account.
   Relevance: Lesson 03; Practice C2; Project Part 1.
   https://www.nationalww2museum.org/students-teachers/student-resources/research-starters/research-starters-worldwide-deaths-world-war
3. **Encyclopaedia Britannica — "World War I: Killed, wounded, and
   missing"** — ~8.5 million soldiers dead of wounds or disease;
   civilian deaths perhaps as many as 13 million; total deaths
   commonly 15–22 million. Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 01;
   Practice A3, C2.
   https://www.britannica.com/event/World-War-I/Killed-wounded-and-missing
4. **Encyclopaedia Britannica — "How many people died during World
   War I?"** — the same figures in short form, with the warning that
   exact casualty lists were never compiled. Free, no account.
   Relevance: Lesson 01; teacher background.
   https://www.britannica.com/question/How-many-people-died-during-World-War-I
5. **Wikipedia — "Armenian genocide"** — dates (1915–1917), the 24
   April 1915 arrests, the 600,000–1.5 million death range, the
   scholarly consensus, and the Turkish government's dispute of the
   term; use as an index and confirm figures against the sources it
   cites. Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 04; Practice D3; Project
   Part 3. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide
6. **United Nations Independent Inquiry (1999) — "Report of the
   Independent Inquiry into the actions of the United Nations during
   the 1994 genocide in Rwanda"** (ReliefWeb mirror of the UN report)
   — ~800,000 killed in about 100 days; the finding that the UN system
   as a whole failed to prevent or stop the genocide. Free, no account.
   Relevance: Lesson 04; Practice D3; teacher background.
   https://reliefweb.int/report/rwanda/report-independent-inquiry-actions-united-nations-during-1994-genocide-rwanda
7. **University of Minnesota, Center for Holocaust and Genocide
   Studies — "Rwanda" resource guide** — the 100-day frame, the
   ~800,000 figure, the April 6, 1994 trigger, UNAMIR and General
   Dallaire's warning, and further reading. Free, no account.
   Relevance: Lesson 04; Practice D3; extension.
   https://cla.umn.edu/chgs/holocaust-genocide-education/resource-guides/rwanda
