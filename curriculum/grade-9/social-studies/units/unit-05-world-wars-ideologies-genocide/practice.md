# Unit 05 Practice Sets and Writing Workshops

Practice Sets A–E support the S2/S4 sessions of Weeks 20–23; the four
writing workshops are the S4 sessions. Work with the adult or the
[answer key](answer-key.md) nearby for guided items; independent items
are checked after completion. All sources are the unit's lessons and
the [resource pack](resource-pack.md).

## Practice Set A — WWI causes and settlement (Week 20, S2)

**A1. Cause map.** Draw a map of causes: put "War in Europe, August
1914" in the center. Add four branches — Militarism, Alliances,
Imperialism, Nationalism — and under each, one fact from Lesson 01
and one arrow showing how it connects to another branch. Write one
sentence under the map: which connection made the war hardest to stop?

**A2. Article 231 close read.** Re-read the Article 231 quotation in
Lesson 01, Part 3. Answer: (a) who wrote it, (b) who had to accept it,
(c) what exactly Germany "accepts," and (d) why a German in 1919
would call it unfair and why a French person in 1919 would call it
just. Two sentences for (d).

**A3. Timeline check.** Place these eight events in order on a line
and write the year under each: assassination in Sarajevo; Germany
invades Belgium; Battle of the Somme; United States enters the war;
Russia leaves the war; armistice; Treaty of Versailles signed;
League of Nations founded. Then circle the two events that changed
the war's *course* and underline the two that changed its *settlement*.

**A4. Error analysis.** A classmate writes: "The Treaty of Versailles
was fair because Germany started the war and should pay." Find two
errors in that reasoning (use Lesson 01's worked examples) and rewrite
the sentence as a fairer claim.

**B2. Depression data.** The lesson gives two figures: U.S.
unemployment about 25% in 1933, and German unemployment over six
million. Write two sentences: (a) what each figure shows about the

**B4. Explain your reasoning.** "Appeasement failed because Britain
and France were cowards." Is that a good historical explanation? Give
two reasons why not, using Lesson 02, Part 3.

## Practice Set D — Evidence of genocide (Week 22, S3)

**D1. Evidence sorting.** For each item, name the evidence kind and
one thing it can and cannot show: (a) the Wannsee Conference minutes;
(b) a survivor's account of a ghetto; (c) a photograph of a camp gate;
(d) a pre-war and post-war census of a Jewish community; (e) a railway
timetable showing trains to Treblinka.

**D2. The denial test.** A website claims: "No single document orders

## Practice Set E — Institutions and memory (Week 23, S2)

**E1. Institution cards.** Make three cards — UN, IMF/World Bank,
Nuremberg trials. Each card: purpose (one sentence), one limit (one
sentence), and one fact that shows it mattered after 1945.

**E2. UDHR close read.** Re-read the UDHR Article 1 quotation in
Lesson 05, Part 3: "All human beings are born free and equal in
dignity and rights." Answer: (a) when was it adopted, (b) what event
had just ended, (c) what does "dignity" add that "free" alone does
not, and (d) name one group from this unit whose rights were denied
before 1948.

**E3. Textbook comparison.** Two textbooks describe the end of WWII
(see Lesson 05, Part 2, worked example 3). For each textbook, write:
what it emphasizes, what it omits, and one question you would ask its
author. Then write one sentence on what D2.His.6.9-12 means in plain
words.

**E4. Explain your reasoning.** "The UN failed, so international law
is pointless." Give two reasons why that reasoning is weak, using
Lesson 05, Part 1.

## Writing Workshop 1 — Causes vs. triggers (Week 20, S4)

**W1.** Write a 6–8 sentence argument answering: "Was the
assassination of Franz Ferdinand the cause of WWI?" Include: a clear
claim; at least three pieces of evidence (one from each of militarism,
alliances, and nationalism or imperialism); a counterclaim and your
reply to it; and a final sentence on the difference between a trigger
and a cause. Use the graphic organizer in the teacher guide (§5).

## Writing Workshop 2 — Deliberation (Week 21, S4)

**W2.** Deliberation: "Why did different societies choose different
answers in the 1930s?" Prepare both sides: for "economic crisis
decided it," give two facts; for "leaders' choices decided it," give
two facts. Then write a 5-sentence judgment: which side is stronger,
and what evidence would change your mind?

## Writing Workshop 3 — Responsibility (Week 22, S4)

**W3.** Structured writing: "Who bears responsibility for the
Holocaust?" Write a claim supported by three cited pieces of evidence
(one document, one testimony or statistic, one other kind), plus a
paragraph on the limits of the evidence and one sentence on the
difference between the responsibility of leaders, participants, and
bystanders. Adult previews the draft; no graphic detail is used.

## Writing Workshop 4 — Unit argument (Week 23, S4)

**W4.** Unit argument: "Did WWII resolve or relocate the crises of
the interwar years?" Write 8–10 sentences with: a claim; three or
more pieces of evidence from at least two lessons; a counterclaim and
your reply; and a source-limit note (one thing your evidence cannot
show). End with one sentence on what the answer means for U06's
Cold War.
