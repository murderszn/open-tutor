# Project — The Evidence Dossier: Two Wars, One Inquiry

**Unit 05 · Weeks 20–23 · Sessions S3 each week + assembly in Week 23, S3**

## Overview

Over four weeks you will build an **Evidence Dossier** — a historian's
case file that answers the unit's inquiry question: *how did two world
wars and the ideologies between them change what people believed a just
society could be — and how do we know what really happened?* Each part
is completed in the week's S3 session and revised in Week 23. The final
dossier is presented to the adult (or a small audience) in Week 23, S3,
with a defense of its evidence and its limits.

**Sensitive content:** Parts 3 uses documentary and textual sources
only — no graphic imagery, no photographs of victims. The adult
previews every source and offers the reflection alternative for any
part the learner finds overwhelming (see teacher guide §8).

## Part 1 — War-causes timeline and map (Week 20, S3)

Build a timeline of 1914–1919 with at least **8 events** (include the
assassination, the July Crisis, Germany's invasion of Belgium, the
Somme, U.S. entry, Russia's exit, the armistice, and Versailles).
For each event write one sentence: what happened, and which MAIN cause
it shows. On a blank world map, mark the Western Front, the Eastern
Front, and one non-European theater, and write one sentence on what
each front's location shows about the war's reach.

**Deliverable:** one timeline page + one annotated map page.

## Part 2 — Ideology case files (Week 21, S3)

Make three case files — liberalism, fascism, communism — one page each.
Each file states: (a) the ideology's answer to the Depression in its
supporters' own terms (two sentences); (b) one country and year where
it held power; (c) one fact that shows its appeal and one that shows
its cost; (d) one limit of your evidence (what you could not check).
Use the comparison frame from Lesson 02; do not use present-day
political labels.

**Deliverable:** three one-page case files.

## Part 3 — Evidence case study (Week 22, S3)

Choose the Holocaust and one other genocide from Lesson 04 (Armenian
or Rwandan). For each, write a one-page case study: (a) what happened,
in three sentences; (b) two kinds of evidence with one specific
example each; (c) the death-toll range and its source; (d) one limit
of the evidence; (e) one question you would still ask. Then write a
half-page comparison: one similarity in how the killing was organized,
one difference in how it was stopped or answered, and one sentence on
why the evidence kinds differ between the two cases.

**Deliverable:** two one-page case studies + one half-page comparison.

## Part 4 — Institutions and memory argument (Week 23, S3)

Assemble the dossier and add a final two-page argument: "Did the
postwar world learn the right lessons?" Use one fact about the UN, one
about the Bretton Woods institutions or Nuremberg, and one about
historical memory. Include a counterclaim and your reply, and a
paragraph on what your evidence cannot show. Present the dossier to
the adult: summarize each part in two sentences, then answer two
questions about your evidence and one about its limits.

**Deliverable:** final argument (two pages) + 10-minute presentation
and defense.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Causation (Parts 1–2)** | Explains interacting causes with specific dated evidence; trigger vs. cause is explicit | Explains causes with evidence; mostly distinguishes trigger from cause | Lists causes with little evidence; confuses trigger and cause | Names causes without evidence or order |
| **Evidence use (Part 3)** | Two+ evidence kinds per case, each with a specific example, a stated limit, and a sourced range | Two evidence kinds per case with examples; ranges sourced; limits noted | One evidence kind per case; ranges given without sources; limits vague | Claims without cited evidence or ranges |
| **Comparison (Parts 2–3)** | Similarities and differences are precise, on a consistent frame, with evidence | Clear similarities and differences with some evidence | Comparison is general or one-sided | No real comparison |
| **Argument (Part 4)** | Claim, 3+ evidence pieces from 2+ lessons, counterclaim with reply, limits paragraph | Claim, 3 evidence pieces, counterclaim, limits noted | Claim with 1–2 evidence pieces; counterclaim or limits missing | Opinion without evidence |
| **Accuracy and sourcing** | All dates, figures, and quotations are correct and sourced; ranges used where scholars disagree | Mostly correct and sourced; minor errors do not change the argument | Several errors or missing sources | Frequent errors; no sources |
| **Presentation** | Clear summary of all four parts; answers evidence and limits questions with specifics | Clear summary; answers questions with some specifics | Summary incomplete; answers are vague | Cannot summarize or defend the dossier |

**Scoring:** 20–24 = exemplary; 15–19 = proficient; 10–14 = developing;
below 10 = revise with the teacher guide's reteach steps (§6) and
resubmit one part. The dossier is a draft for learning, not a
published claim — record its limits honestly.
