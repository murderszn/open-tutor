# Unit 05 Teacher Guide

**Grade 9 · Social Studies · Weeks 20–23 · For the guiding adult only**

This guide supports the learner-facing lessons. Keep it separate from
student materials; the [answer key](answer-key.md) is a separate file.

## 1. How to use this unit

Each week has five ~50-minute sessions: S1 (and S2 in Week 22) is the
taught lesson; S2/S4 are practice and writing; S3 is the project
investigation; S5 is review and check. Lessons carry their own
explicit teaching — your job is to read the lesson first, run the
readiness check, watch for the misconceptions listed in each lesson's
teacher note, and use the review prompts in §5. The unit's inquiry
question — *how did two world wars and the ideologies between them
change what people believed a just society could be, and how do we
know what really happened?* — should be visible (on the wall or the
journal's first page) all four weeks.

## 2. Session guidance

- **Week 20, S1 (Lesson 01):** Introduce MAIN with the acronym; have
  the learner say it back before Part 2. Stress trigger vs. cause
  (worked example 1) — it returns in every later unit. The Article 231
  quotation is public domain; read it aloud together.
- **Week 20, S2–S4:** Practice A2's close read needs the Versailles
  terms list in front of the learner. Workshop W1's graphic organizer
  is in §5.
- **Week 21, S1 (Lesson 02):** The ideology comparison table (Practice
  B1) is the week's anchor — complete it together in S1 if needed.
  Keep present-day political labels out of the discussion; the unit
  compares historical ideologies as their supporters described them.
- **Week 21, S4 (Workshop W2):** run as a real deliberation: the
  learner argues one side, you argue the other, then swap. The
  judgment paragraph is the deliverable.
- **Week 22, S1 (Lesson 03):** use the timeline SVG and a blank map;
  the learner should trace the war, not just hear about it. The
  internment discussion (Part 2) needs a calm, factual tone — see §8.
- **Week 22, S2 (Lesson 04):** read the sensitive-content note aloud
  first. Run the evidence-table observation with the image or its text
  alternative. Do not show photographs of victims; the unit uses none.
- **Week 22, S3–S4:** Project Part 3 and Workshop W3 are the week's
  core. Preview W3's draft before the learner finalizes it.
- **Week 22, S5:** the formative quiz (14 points). Score it the same
  day; reteach before Week 23 (see §6).
- **Week 23, S1 (Lesson 05):** the UDHR Article 1 quotation is public
  domain — have the learner memorize it; it returns in U08.
- **Week 23, S3:** the dossier presentation. Ask two evidence
  questions and one limits question; score with the project rubric.
- **Week 23, S5:** the unit assessment (30 points). Score with the
  answer key; record standing in §6.

## 3. Misconceptions to watch

- **"The assassination caused WWI."** Trigger vs. cause (Lessons 01,
  02). Ask: what made the trigger explosive?
- **"Article 231 proves Germany caused the war."** A treaty clause is
  a political claim by winners, not a historian's verdict.
- **"The Depression caused WWII."** Condition vs. cause. The
  Depression created openings; leaders' decisions started the war.
- **"Fascism and communism were the same."** Shared one-party rule and
  repression; different goals (nation vs. class), economics, enemies.
- **"D-Day won WWII."** Direction changed at Stalingrad/Midway
  (1942–43); D-Day opened a second front in a war already turning.
- **"Ranges mean we know nothing."** Ranges are honest where counting
  is hard; the fact of each genocide rests on converging evidence
  kinds, not on one agreed number.
- **"The UN failed, so international law is pointless."** Judge an
  institution by purpose, limits, and what changed because it existed.
- **"Memory is just what happened."** Memory is a choice about what a
  society emphasizes and omits; compare accounts with D2.His.6.9-12.

## 4. Differentiation

- **Reading/language access:** excerpt public-domain sources to the
  essential passage; read aloud or use text-to-speech; vocabulary is
  defined in context in every lesson and listed in the unit README.
- **Alternate response modes:** oral or scribed responses accepted for
  any written task with adult documentation; maps may be drawn on
  printed outlines; the evidence-table activity has a full text
  alternative.
- **Processing and output:** graphic organizers for every argument
  task (§5); sentence starters in each lesson; extended time in S5
  review sessions.
- **Extensions:** each lesson ends with one; the resource pack's
  queries support deeper dives; strong learners can take the
  Lesson 04 extension (Genocide Convention's adoption) as a short
  research task.
- **Sensitive content:** see §8 — advance notice, adult preview,
  reflection alternative, pacing control with the adult.

## 5. Review prompts and graphic organizers

**S5 review prompts (each week):** (1) Say the week's inquiry
question in your own words. (2) Name one thing you can now explain
that you could not on Monday. (3) Show one piece of evidence you used
this week and one thing it cannot show. (4) What is still unclear?
Record gaps and re-teach them in the next S5.

**Argument organizer (Workshops W1, W4; Assessment D1):**

| Claim | Evidence 1 | Evidence 2 | Evidence 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Counterclaim | Reply to counterclaim | What my evidence cannot show | |

**Cause map organizer (Practice A1):** center "War in Europe, August
1914"; four branches (MAIN); one fact and one cross-arrow per branch.

**Evidence-kinds organizer (Lesson 04; Practice D1):** five rows
(documents / testimony / photographs / physical evidence / statistics);
columns: example, what it shows, what it cannot show.

## 6. Readiness, reteaching, and scoring

**Readiness signals:** the learner can place 1914–1948 events on a
timeline, classify a source by kind, state a claim with two evidence
pieces, and name one limit of a piece of evidence. If any signal is
missing after Week 20, re-teach the U01–U04 source routines before
Week 22.

**Quiz (Week 22, 14 points):** 12–14 = ready; 9–11 = review the
flagged lessons (reteach the specific misconception from §3, then
re-run the missed quiz items orally); below 9 = re-teach Lessons
01–04 before Week 23.

**Unit assessment (Week 23, 30 points):** 26–30 = exemplary; 21–25 =
proficient; 15–20 = developing — re-teach flagged lessons, then
resubmit Part D; below 15 = re-teach the unit's core lessons before
U06. Record the score and the reteach plan here:

- Quiz score: ___ / 14. Reteach plan: ____________________________
- Assessment score: ___ / 30. Reteach plan: ______________________
- Project rubric total: ___ / 24. Parts to revise: ______________

**Next teaching steps:** U06 (decolonization, Cold War, new nations)
builds on Lesson 05's institutions and Cold War origins, and on
Lesson 04's evidence habits. If the learner struggled with evidence
kinds, repeat the Lesson 04 table with a new example before U06.

## 7. Answer-key use

The [answer key](answer-key.md) solves every lesson question, practice
item, workshop, quiz question, and assessment task independently. Check
the learner's work against it after completion, not during. For open
responses, the key gives sample reasoning and acceptable variation —
defensible alternatives are accepted when they cite evidence. Never
place the key beside a student question.

## 8. Sensitive-content handling

- **Advance notice:** tell the learner before Week 22 that the unit
  turns to genocide; describe in general terms what will be studied
  (documents, testimony, numbers — no graphic imagery) and that they
  may pause at any time.
- **Adult preview:** read every Lesson 04 source yourself first; the
  lessons contain no graphic descriptions or photographs of victims,
  and the generated image is a desk of documents, not a depiction of
  violence.
- **Framing:** keep discussion on evidence, responsibility, and
  remembrance — not on shock. Name victims with dignity; do not ask
  the learner to imagine themselves as a victim or a perpetrator.
- **Reflection alternative:** any learner who finds the material
  overwhelming may substitute a reflection: "Why do societies build
  memorials to victims of genocide, and what should a memorial include
  or leave out?" (3–5 sentences) for any Practice D item or Workshop
  W3. The alternative is assessed on reasoning, not on content about
  the killings.
- **Pacing:** the adult controls pacing; split Lesson 04 across two
  sessions if needed. The unit's S5 sessions include a debrief — ask
  how the learner is finding the material, not only what they learned.
