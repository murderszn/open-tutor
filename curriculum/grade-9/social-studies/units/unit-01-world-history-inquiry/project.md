# Unit 01 Project — The circa-1450 Connections Map and Argument

**Weeks 3–6 · S3 sessions + independent work · Adult-supervised**

You will produce two deliverables: (1) a hand-built, fully labeled
**connections map** of the world circa 1450, and (2) a short **evidence-based
argument** answering *which connection mattered most circa 1450*.

## Materials

- Large paper or a digital drawing surface; 5+ colors; ruler
- A printed blank world map for reference (any modern outline map — you
  will mark it "modern coastlines for reference only")
- Lessons 01–04, practice sets, the port illustration
  (`assets/indian-ocean-port-1450-illustration.png`)
- Notebook/inquiry journal

## Part 1 — The connections map (S3 of weeks 3, 4, 5)

### Steps

1. **Week 3 — skeleton.** Draw the three Afro-Eurasian systems in three
   colors with a key: Silk Roads (overland), Indian Ocean maritime network,
   trans-Saharan routes. Place and label **at least 10 nodes** (cities,
   ports, or regions) from Lessons 01–02.
2. **Week 4 — cargo and travelers.** At each node, annotate **one good,
   faith, idea, or technology** that passed through it (10+ annotations
   total, each labeled). Add Ibn Battuta's route and Zheng He's fleet
   routes in two new colors; label which was merchant-network and which
   was state-expedition.
3. **Week 5 — the other half of the world.** Shade Mexica, Inca, and
   Mississippian areas plus one Pacific voyaging zone. Add the one-sentence
   map note explaining why these areas carry no Afro-Eurasian routes
   (see practice task M3).
4. **Accuracy pass.** Check every label against your lesson notes. Fix any
   node placed in the wrong region. Add a "limits of this map" box naming
   two things the map simplifies or omits (for example: modern coastlines;
   routes drawn straighter than reality; no population data).

### Deliverable 1 requirements

- Key with 5 colors/symbols explained; title and "circa 1450" date label
- ≥10 labeled Afro-Eurasian nodes; 3 route systems drawn and named
- ≥10 cargo/idea annotations tied to nodes
- Both traveler routes drawn and correctly labeled by model
- Americas/Oceania shaded with the explanatory note
- "Limits of this map" box with 2 honest simplifications
- Neat enough for another learner to read without your help

## Part 2 — The argument (S3–S4 of week 6)

**Compelling question:** *Which connection mattered most to the world
circa 1450?*

### Steps

1. **Claim + yardstick (S2, week 6).** Write one arguable sentence, then
   complete: "mattered most for ___."
2. **Evidence hunt (S3).** Find at least **three** evidence pieces from at
   least **two different source kinds** (lesson text, modeled traveler
   passage, dataset, illustration, map you built). Record each with its
   source.
3. **Counterclaim (S3).** Write the strongest case *against* your claim in
   3–4 sentences, with one evidence piece.
4. **Draft and revise (S4).** Write 400–600 words: claim, evidence,
   counterclaim answered, and a limits paragraph (what your argument does
   *not* prove). Revise after adult/peer critique using the rubric below.

### Deliverable 2 requirements

- 400–600 words, learner's own prose
- Arguable claim + explicit yardstick
- ≥3 evidence pieces from ≥2 source kinds, each cited to its source
- Counterclaim stated fairly and answered
- Limits paragraph
- No invented facts: every factual claim traceable to a lesson, the
  dataset, or a Resource Pack source

## Rubric

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Map accuracy** | All nodes/routes correctly placed and labeled; annotations accurate; limits box insightful | Minor placement slips; annotations mostly accurate | Several mislabeled nodes or missing system; annotations thin | Map unreadable or largely inaccurate |
| **Evidence use** | ≥3 pieces, ≥2 source kinds, each cited; evidence genuinely supports claim | 3 pieces cited; connection to claim clear | <3 pieces or single source kind; citations vague | Claims without evidence |
| **Argument structure** | Arguable claim + yardstick; counterclaim answered; limits stated | Claim + counterclaim present; limits thin | Claim is factual not arguable, or counterclaim missing | No recognizable argument |
| **Historical thinking** | Change/continuity or perspective awareness shown; anachronisms avoided | Some awareness of perspective/limits | Present-day assumptions unexamined | Anachronistic throughout |
| **Communication** | Clear, organized, correctly captioned map; polished prose | Clear with minor issues | Hard to follow in places | Incoherent or incomplete |

**Scoring:** 17–20 = exemplary · 13–16 = proficient · 9–12 = developing ·
5–8 = beginning. Below proficient on *Evidence use* requires a reteach of
source citation before U02 (see teacher guide).
