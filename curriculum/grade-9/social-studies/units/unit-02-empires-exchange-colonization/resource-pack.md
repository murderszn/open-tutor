# Unit 02 Resource Pack

**Goal (one sentence):** Give a grade-9 learner (and guiding adult)
reputable, free, no-account sources for the maritime turn, the Columbian
exchange, conquest and colonial labor systems, the transatlantic slave trade
as a business system, resistance, and early-modern empire comparison —
checked 2026-10-20.

**Age fit:** Grade 9 (ages 14–15). Videos supplement the lessons; they never
replace close reading. Enslavement, conquest, and colonial violence are
handled with adult preview (see teacher guide §8). Learner-facing excerpts
in the lessons are original prose or short public-domain passages.

## Web references (checked 2026-10-20)

1. **Slave Voyages — Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database** (SlaveVoyages.org,
   hosted by Rice University; consortium: Emory, Harvard, Omohundro
   Institute, UC campuses, National Museum of African American History and
   Culture) — the ~36,000-voyage database behind this unit's scale figures
   (12.5M embarked / 10.7M disembarked, 1501–1866). Free, no account.
   Relevance: Lesson 03; Practice C4; Project Part 3. (Existence and figures
   verified via Rice/Oracle coverage and the database's encyclopedia
   summary, 2026-10-20.)
2. **Smithsonian Magazine — "A digital archive of slave voyages details the
   largest forced migration in history"** (smithsonianmag.com, May 2017, by
   the database team) — readable overview of the database: 12.5M figure,
   "before 1820, four enslaved Africans crossed for every European," voyage
   animations. Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 03 launch reading;
   extension task. https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/digital-archive-slave-voyages-details-largest-forced-migration-history-180963093/
3. **Wikipedia — "Voyages: The Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database"**
   (en.wikipedia.org) — neutral summary of the project's methods and the
   country-participation table (Portugal ~5.8M, Britain ~3.3M embarked).
   Free, no account; use as an index, confirm figures against Slave Voyages
   itself. Relevance: Lesson 03 figures; teacher background.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyages:_The_Trans-Atlantic_Slave_Trade_Database
4. **Gilder Lehrman Institute — "The Interesting Narrative of the Life of
   Olaudah Equiano, 1789"** (hamilton.gilderlehrman.org) — excerpted
   transcript (source: Project Gutenberg) with background noting the
   birthplace debate — ideal for the D2.His.8 sourcing lesson. Free, no
   account. Relevance: Practice Set C; Lesson 03. https://hamilton.gilderlehrman.org/key-document/interesting-narrative-life-olaudah-equiano-1789
5. **Project Gutenberg — Equiano, *The Interesting Narrative* (eBook
   #15399)** — full public-domain text for adult selection of excerpts.
   Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 03; Practice Set C.
   https://www.gutenberg.org/files/15399/15399-h/15399-h.htm
6. **Dickinson College, House Divided — "Olaudah Equiano, Interesting
   Narrative (1789)"** (housedivided.dickinson.edu) — the excerpt with
   discussion questions and further reading (Ira Berlin, Brycchan Carey on
   the birthplace question). Free, no account. Relevance: teacher
   background; extension.
   https://housedivided.dickinson.edu/sites/teagle/texts/olaudah-equiano-interesting-narrative-1789/
