# Practice Sets and Writing Workshops — Unit 02

These sets cover the S2 close-reading/practice sessions and the S4
writing/deliberation sessions. Work them in the order of the
[unit README](README.md) pacing table. The [answer key](answer-key.md)
solves every item independently — check your work after a genuine attempt.

## Practice Set A — Week 7, S2: tracing one exchange item (with the port image)

*Goal: follow a single item both directions across the Columbian exchange,
and read the port reconstruction for what it shows and hides.*

Use the text alternative in Lesson 01 if the image is unavailable.

**A1. Image observation.** List five visible clues that this port moved
goods between continents (not just along one coast). For each clue, write
one sentence about what it suggests.

**A2. Two-direction tracing.** Choose one item — **sugar**, **silver**,
**maize**, or **smallpox** — and complete this chain:

- Where did it originate?
- Which direction did it travel after 1492, and by what route?
- Name one effect on the *receiving* society and one on the *sending*
  society.
- Was the effect mainly biological, economic, or both? Explain in one
  sentence.

**A3. Error analysis.** A classmate writes: "The Columbian exchange was good
for Europe and bad for the Americas." Identify two things this sentence gets
right and two things it oversimplifies. Rewrite it as a more accurate
two-sentence claim.

**A4. The "triangle" debate.** The classic diagram shows Europe → Africa →
Americas → Europe in a neat triangle. Using Lesson 01's wind knowledge
(*volta do mar*) and the fact that most ships sailed bilateral (two-way)
routes, write three sentences: one defending the triangle as a teaching
model, one stating its biggest limit, and one concluding whether your Week
7 S3 map should use it, modify it, or replace it.

## Practice Set B — Week 8, S2: contrasting accounts of conquest

*Goal: compare two accounts of the same events by maker, purpose, and
evidence (D2.His.4, D3.1–D3.4).*

Read these two short passages — both original summaries of public-domain
material, with one brief quoted line each.

**Account 1 — Bernal Díaz del Castillo**, a Spanish foot soldier who fought
at Tenochtitlan, writing decades later in his *True History of the Conquest
of New Spain* (1632): Díaz presents the conquest as the achievement of a
small band of brave Christians, aided by God. He writes of the Mexica with a
soldier's respect and a conqueror's contempt, and minimizes the role of the
tens of thousands of Indigenous allies.

**Account 2 — Nahuatl accounts** recorded in Book 12 of the *Florentine
Codex*: Mexica survivors' recollections of the siege — the hunger, the
disease, the causeways cut, the dead filling the streets — gathered by
Sahagún's Indigenous collaborators decades after 1521, inside a Franciscan
missionary project.

**B1.** For each account, state: (a) maker and date, (b) intended audience,
(c) one purpose the maker likely had.

**B2.** Both accounts describe the siege of Tenochtitlan. Name one fact they
would likely *agree* on and one thing each would *emphasize* that the other
minimizes.

**B3.** Which account is more useful for answering "what did the siege feel
like for the city's people?" Which is more useful for "how did the Spanish
justify the conquest to readers back home?" Justify each in one sentence.

**B4. Explain your reasoning.** A student says Account 2 is automatically
more truthful because it is the Indigenous perspective. Write a paragraph
agreeing or disagreeing: consider mediation (who recorded it, when, in what
project) alongside perspective. (There is no single correct verdict —
there is a well-reasoned one.)

## Practice Set C — Week 9, S2: close read of Equiano (public domain)

*Goal: tie claims about the Middle Passage to exact lines of a firsthand
account.*

Read this excerpt from Olaudah Equiano's *The Interesting Narrative of the
Life of Olaudah Equiano* (1789), Chapter 2 — public domain (Project
Gutenberg eBook #15399). The adult selects the passage length.

> "The first object which saluted my eyes when I arrived on the coast was
> the sea, and a slave ship, which was then riding at anchor, and waiting
> for its cargo... I was soon put down under the decks, and there I received
> such a salute in my nostrils as I had never experienced in my life: so
> that, with the loathsomeness of the stench, and crying together, I became
> so sick and low that I was not able to eat... The closeness of the place,
> and the heat of the climate, added to the number in the ship, which was so
> crowded that each had scarcely room to turn himself, almost suffocated us."

**C1.** List three concrete conditions Equiano describes below decks. Quote
the exact phrase for each.

**C2.** What does "waiting for its cargo" reveal about how the trade
treated human beings? (One sentence, using the word *commodity* or
*property* correctly.)

**C3.** Equiano writes as an eyewitness *and* as an abolitionist publishing
in London in 1789. Name one strength and one limit this gives his account
as evidence about the Middle Passage.

**C4.** Pair with Lesson 03's figures: Equiano describes one ship. How do
the Slave Voyages estimates (~12.5M embarked, ~1.8M Middle Passage deaths)
change what one ship's story means? (Two sentences: the single account
gives the human texture; the database gives the system scale. Neither
replaces the other.)

*Goal: practice D2.His.8 — detect underrepresentation in the record.*

| Safavid | | | | |

| Spanish/Portuguese | | | | |

**D1.** For each of these four sources, state one thing it shows, one thing
it hides, and one inquiry it is useful for: (a) a Potosí mine owner's
account book, 1600; (b) a Jesuit missionary's letter from Brazil, 1650;
(c) a Mughal court chronicle; (d) an English joint-stock company's

## Writing Workshop 1 — Week 7, S4: cause and effect on demographic change

*Write a cause/effect chain of at least three linked steps about population
change after 1492, with evidence for each link.*

**Model (worked):** *Link 1:* Europeans carried smallpox to the Americas,
where populations had no prior exposure (Lesson 02: the Mexica case, 1520).
*Link 2:* Because disease killed a large share of the population — including
leaders and food producers — societies could not maintain defenses or
harvests. *Link 3:* Because colonial powers then needed labor for mines and
plantations, they turned to enslaved African labor, expanding the
transatlantic trade (Lesson 03). *Check:* each link names a cause, an
effect, and evidence; hedged language ("a large share") where numbers are
debated.

**Your turn:** Choose a different starting point — **maize in Europe**,
**silver in China**, or **sugar in Brazil** — and build your own three-link
chain. End with one sentence on what your chain shows about
"exchange vs. empire."

## Writing Workshop 2 — Week 8, S4: the same events, different makers

*Two paragraphs, two perspectives, one event.*

Choose the fall of Tenochtitlan (1521) or Cajamarca (1532). Write paragraph
one from the perspective of a Spanish participant (using Account 1's frame:
bravery, God, order restored) and paragraph two from the perspective of an
Indigenous survivor (using Account 2's frame: siege, hunger, loss). Each
paragraph must cite at least one fact from Lesson 02. Then add a final
sentence: what does the *difference between the paragraphs* teach about
sources?

## Writing Workshop 3 — Week 9, S4: deliberation prep — why did the system persist?

*Prepare both sides; you will deliberate in S4.*

**Question:** Did the transatlantic slave trade persist for 365 years
primarily because of **economics** (profit, credit, insurance, state
revenue) or primarily because of **racial ideology** (theories of
inferiority that justified enslavement)?

Prepare: (a) your side's two strongest evidence points (use Lesson 03
figures and institutions); (b) the other side's strongest point, stated
fairly; (c) one sentence explaining how the two explanations might
*interact* rather than compete. (Deliberation rule: you may be assigned
either side.)

## Writing Workshop 4 — Week 10, S4: the unit argument

*The unit's culminating writing task. Use the argument frame from U01:
claim, evidence, counterclaim, source-limit note.*

**Question:** Between 1450 and 1750, did **exchange** or **empire** change
the world more?

Requirements: (a) a clear claim; (b) at least three pieces of evidence from
different weeks of the unit; (c) a counterclaim stated fairly and answered;
(d) one sentence naming a limit of the sources your argument relies on
(D2.His.8). 300–450 words. Graphic organizer and sentence starters are in
the teacher guide.
