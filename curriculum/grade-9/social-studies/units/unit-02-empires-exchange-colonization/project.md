# Project — The Exchange & Empire Evidence Dossier

**Unit 02 investigation · Weeks 7–10 · S3 sessions (4 × ~50 min) + assembly**

## What you are building

A four-part **evidence dossier** — part map, part source collection, part
comparison brief — that answers the unit's inquiry question with documents
instead of just opinions: *between 1450 and 1750, did exchange or empire
change the world more — and whose story does the surviving record tell?*

You will assemble it across the unit's four S3 investigation sessions, then
present it in Week 10 alongside your unit argument (Writing Workshop 4).

## Materials

- Large paper or a digital document with four sections; blank world map
  (physical or printed); colored pencils or digital layers
- Notebook/inquiry journal; the unit lessons; `assets/`
  (port reconstruction, exchange schematic)
- Adult partner for source preview (especially Parts 2–3)

---

## Part 1 — Week 7, S3: Routes, winds, and the "triangle" debate

**Task:** Draw the Atlantic system as it worked, not as the textbook
triangle imagines it.

**Steps:**

1. On your map, draw the *volta do mar*: the wind circuit Portuguese
   sailors used — down the African coast on the northeast trades, then far
   out into the Atlantic to catch the westerlies home. Label the winds.
2. Add three real routes with dates: (a) Lisbon → Elmina/Calicut (post-1498),
   (b) Seville → Caribbean → back via the Gulf Stream (post-1492),
   (c) Acapulco → Manila (post-1565).
3. Mark what moved on each route in each direction (2+ items per arrow —
   use the exchange schematic in `assets/`).
4. Write a 4-sentence "triangle verdict" (from Practice Set A, A4): what
   the classic triangle diagram gets right, its biggest limit, and whether
   you kept, modified, or replaced it on your map — and why.

**Deliverable:** annotated routes map + triangle verdict paragraph.

## Part 2 — Week 8, S3: Labor-systems evidence cards

**Task:** Turn Lesson 02's three systems into evidence cards a historian
could actually use.

**Steps:**

1. Make three cards: **Encomienda**, **Mita (Potosí)**, **Plantation
   slavery (Brazil/Caribbean)**.
2. On each card record: (a) who worked and how they were recruited;
   (b) what was produced and who profited; (c) one primary source that
   could document the workers' experience (e.g., Las Casas's *Short
   Account* for the encomienda; mine-owner accounts vs. workers' absence
   for the mita) **and one limit of that source**.
3. On the back of each card, write one sentence connecting the system to
   the unit question: does this card show *exchange* changing the world, or
   *empire*?
4. Adult check: confirm each card's source is real (named work, author,
   date) and each limit is specific, not generic ("biased" is not a limit —
   *how* it is limited is).

**Deliverable:** three evidence cards, front and back.

## Part 3 — Week 9, S3: Resistance across three forms

**Task:** Document that resistance was constant, not exceptional.

**Steps:**

1. Choose one case for each form: **day-to-day** (e.g., work slowdowns,
   cultural preservation), **escape/marronage** (e.g., Palmares;
   Jamaican Maroons 1739–40), **revolt** (e.g., Pueblo Revolt 1680; Stono
   1739).
2. For each case record: what happened (3–4 sentences), what form of
   resistance it shows, what the outcome was — and **what kind of source**
   preserves it (colonial court record? oral tradition? missionary
   letter?) with one limit of that source type.
3. Write a 5-sentence synthesis: what do the three cases together prove
   about enslaved and Indigenous people's view of the colonial system?

**Deliverable:** three resistance case records + synthesis paragraph.

## Part 4 — Week 10, S3: Three sources, one question

**Task:** Compare what three different sources can and cannot tell you.

**Sources (adult-selected; originals or public-domain excerpts):**

- **A:** a merchant's ledger-style record (reconstructed from the lesson's
  description of port paperwork: goods, quantities, prices — no people).
- **B:** a missionary's letter describing an Indigenous community
  (summarized from the lesson's account of missionary aims).
- **C:** a transcription-style oral history (reconstructed from the
  lesson's description of Florentine Codex-type material: a survivor's
  account of conquest-era hunger and loss).

**Steps:**

1. For each source, answer the unit's three questions: *what does it show?
   what does it hide? what is it useful for?* (2–3 sentences each).
2. Then answer: which source is most useful for the question "how did the
   colonial economy work?" Which is most useful for "what did conquest feel
   like?" — and which question can *none* of the three answer well?
3. Write the comparison brief (200–300 words) with a final sentence naming
   one kind of source you would still need (D2.His.12: using source
   questions to plan further inquiry).

**Deliverable:** three-source comparison brief.

## Final assembly and presentation (Week 10, with S4)

Assemble the four parts into one dossier with a cover page: title, your
inquiry question, and a 5-sentence introduction stating your answer and
your strongest piece of evidence. Present the dossier to your adult
partner (10 minutes): walk through one part per minute, then take
questions — especially "what's the weakest part of your evidence?"

## Rubric

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Historical accuracy** | All dates, figures, and claims match lesson sources; estimates hedged where scholars debate | Minor imprecision; no invented facts | One significant factual error | Multiple errors or invented facts |
| **Evidence use** | Every part cites specific sources; ledger/letter/oral-history limits named precisely | Sources cited; limits stated generally | Some claims unsourced | Claims without sources |
| **Source-limit awareness (D2.His.8)** | Three-question routine applied to every source; missing voices named with reasons | Routine applied to most sources | Limits mentioned vaguely | No limit analysis |
| **Exchange-vs.-empire reasoning** | Each part connects to the inquiry question with a clear, defended judgment | Connections stated, lightly defended | Connection asserted without defense | No connection to the question |
| **Communication** | Dossier organized, labeled, legible; presentation answers questions with evidence | Clear with minor gaps | Disorganized in places | Incomplete or incoherent |

**Scoring:** 17–20 = exemplary dossier; 13–16 = proficient; 9–12 =
developing (revise weakest part with the adult); below 9 = rebuild one part
before the unit assessment. The adult records the score and one
next-teaching step (see [teacher-guide](teacher-guide.md) §7).
