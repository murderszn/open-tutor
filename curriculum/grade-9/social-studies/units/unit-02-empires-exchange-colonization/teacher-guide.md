# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Empires, Exchange, Colonization, and Resistance

**Adult-facing.** Keep this file separate from the learner: it contains
answers-by-reference, misconception notes, review prompts, reteach
criteria, and sensitive-content handling. Full worked solutions live in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## 1. How this unit runs

Twenty sessions across course weeks 7–10, ~50 minutes each, following the
track's weekly rhythm: **S1** lesson (explicit teaching + modeled
reasoning), **S2** close reading/practice, **S3** investigation (the
dossier), **S4** writing or deliberation, **S5** review + check. The S1
lessons do the heavy teaching; resist the urge to re-lecture in S2–S4 —
those sessions are for the learner's hands on sources.

**Pacing control:** if a week runs long, compress S5 review (never the S3
investigation or S4 writing). If the learner finishes early, use the lesson
extensions, not the next week's S1.

## 2. Session-by-session notes

### Week 7 — The maritime turn and the Columbian exchange

- **S1 (Lesson 01):** Stress that *motives* answer "who paid and why" and
  *means* answer "how it was possible." Learners often merge them ("they
  sailed because of caravels" — no: caravels explain *how*, crowns' desire
  for profit explains *why*). The port image is a reconstruction — say so
  out loud before the observation task.
- **S2 (Practice A):** For A3 (error analysis), accept any two valid
  oversimplifications: it ignores African/Asian agency, it treats "Europe"
  and "the Americas" as uniform, it misses that exchange also devastated
  Europe (disease went both ways; e.g., syphilis debates) — the point is
  the *habit* of qualifying, not a canonical list.
- **S3 (Project Part 1):** Check the wind arrows before the verdict
  paragraph — a wrong *volta do mar* undermines the triangle debate. The
  exchange schematic in `assets/` is the item bank for route labels.
- **S4 (Writing Workshop 1):** Model one link aloud if the learner stalls;
  then require the learner to build the other two. Insist on hedged
  language where numbers are debated ("large share," "scholars estimate").
- **S5:** Review prompts: *Which motive would a king mention in public?
  Which in private? What is one thing the triangle diagram hides?*

### Week 8 — Conquest and colonial systems

- **S1 (Lesson 02):** The central misconception is single-cause conquest
  ("guns won"). Use the causal-web sketch; make the learner draw arrows
  between causes. Name the colonial myth explicitly: Spanish accounts
  minimized Indigenous allies because a story of "a few brave Christians"
  justified the conquest.
- **S2 (Practice B):** For B4 there is no single correct verdict — score
  the *reasoning* (does it weigh both mediation and perspective?). If the
  learner declares one account "true," push: "What would change your mind?"
- **S3 (Project Part 2):** Vet each card's named source (real work,
  author, date). Reject generic limits ("it's biased") — require the
  mechanism ("the ledger records the owner's costs, so workers' voices
  never enter it").
- **S4 (Writing Workshop 2):** The final sentence (what the *difference*
  teaches about sources) is the actual learning goal; don't let it be an
  afterthought.
- **S5 + quiz:** Administer the [week-08 quiz](assessments/week-08_quiz.md).
  Scoring bands are on the quiz; record the reteach plan (see §7).

### Week 9 — The slave trade as a system; resistance (sensitive week)

- **Before S1:** give the advance notice promised in the track
  scope-and-sequence §7: what the week covers, why it matters, and the
  reflection alternative. Preview the Equiano excerpt yourself first.
- **S1 (Lesson 03):** Teach the figures as *estimates with stated limits*
  — learners should be able to say what each number counts and excludes.
  The "capitalized business" framing (companies, forts, insurance) is the
  week's hardest conceptual move; anchor it in the paperwork point: the
  trade's own records are what let us measure it.
- **S2 (Practice C):** Keep the close read textual — no dramatization of
  the Middle Passage. C4's pairing (one ship's story + the database scale)
  is the week's key synthesis; don't skip it.
- **S3 (Project Part 3):** For each resistance case, require the *source
  type and its limit* — this is where D2.His.8 gets practiced on the
  resistance side of the story.
- **S4 (deliberation):** Assign sides; the learner must argue the assigned
  side fairly before stating their own view. The interaction sentence
  (economics × racial ideology) is the mark of the strongest responses.
- **S5:** Debrief the week with the learner: what was hardest to read, and
  what helped? Note any pacing adjustments for U05 (which revisits
  genocide evidence).

### Week 10 — Synthesis

- **S1 (Lesson 04):** The comparison table is the week's engine — check one
  completed row together before the learner does all five. For the "which
  empire was strongest" worked example, enforce the rule: *state your
  measure before your answer.*
- **S2 (Practice D):** D3 (oral histories) often triggers strong opinions;
  score the structure (concede → argue → safeguard), not the conclusion.
- **S3 (Project Part 4):** The final question — "which question can *none*
  of the three sources answer well?" — is the dossier's capstone; it
  practices D2.His.12 (planning further inquiry).
- **S4 (Writing Workshop 4):** Use the U01 argument organizer. Require the
  source-limit sentence; an argument without it is incomplete.
- **S5:** Dossier presentations (10 min) + the
  [unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md). Score the dossier with
  the project rubric the same week so feedback is fresh.

## 3. Likely misconceptions (all weeks)

| Misconception | Correction move |
|---|---|
| "Europeans explored out of curiosity" | Ask who paid for ships and what they wanted back (L01 motives) |
| "A few Spaniards with guns beat empires" | Causal web: disease, allies, civil war, capture — guns were the smallest factor |
| "The triangle diagram is how trade worked" | Most ships sailed bilateral routes; the triangle is a simplified model with known limits |
| "12.5 million died in the slave trade" | 12.5M embarked; ~10.7M disembarked; ~1.8M Middle Passage deaths — and the figure excludes pre-/post-voyage deaths |
| "Resistance meant only revolts" | Day-to-day resistance and marronage were more common and equally historical |
| "An AI/historical illustration proves what happened" | Reconstructions illustrate; only period sources support claims |
| "One account is automatically true because of who wrote it" | Perspective ≠ reliability; weigh mediation, purpose, and corroboration |

## 4. Differentiation

- **Reading load:** excerpts may be shortened further or read aloud /
  text-to-speech; full documents are extensions.
- **Output:** oral or scribed responses accepted for any written task with
  adult documentation; graphic organizers for every argument task; sentence
  starters for claims and counterclaims (in practice.md workshops).
- **Processing:** extended time is built into S5 sessions; the dossier may
  be assembled digitally with templates.
- **Extension:** lesson extensions + the Slave Voyages database exploration
  (adult-supervised) + the "archive's missing shelf" task (L04).

## 5. Answer-key reference

Every learner-facing question is solved in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md),
reconciled against the lessons. If a learner's defensible answer differs
from the key (especially B4, D3, the deliberation, and the unit argument),
score the reasoning against the rubric criteria, not the key's sample
wording — and note the alternative in the margin for the next run's key
revision.

## 6. Review prompts (S5 bank)

- Week 7: *What is the difference between a motive and a means? Name one
  of each. What does the triangle diagram hide?*
- Week 8: *Give two causes of the Mexica fall besides weapons. What did
  the encomienda promise vs. deliver?*
- Week 9: *State the three Slave Voyages figures and what each excludes.
  Name one resistance form for each: daily, escape, revolt.*
- Week 10: *Which two empires taxed trade routes vs. farmland? What is one
  thing a merchant ledger can never tell you?*

## 7. Readiness and reteach

- **Quiz bands (week 8):** 17–20 proceed; 13–16 reteach missed concepts in
  Week 9 S5 (use the misconception table); below 13 revisit Lessons 01–02
  (re-teach with the causal web and motives/means sort) before Week 9 S1.
- **Unit assessment bands:** on the assessment; below 20, revisit the
  weakest week before U03 — U03's revolution-causation work assumes the
  multi-cause habit from this unit.
- **Dossier:** below 9/20, rebuild the weakest part before the unit
  assessment; the rubric's source-limit row is the most common gap.
- **Signals to slow down:** the learner treats estimates as exact counts;
  confuses perspective with reliability; cannot name what a source hides.
  Each signals a return to the three-question routine (show / hide /
  useful for), not more content.

## 8. Sensitive-content handling

- Advance notice before Week 9 (and a reminder at the week's start).
- Documentary/textual sources only; no graphic imagery, no dramatized
  reenactment of the Middle Passage or conquest violence.
- Frame discussion around evidence, causation, and human dignity — never
  around shock. The resistance material (Part 3, Lesson 03 Part 2) is
  deliberately the week's second half: agency, not only victimhood.
- Offer the observation/reflection alternative for any activity: the
  learner writes or discusses what the sources show about the system's
  structure instead of completing the close read.
- The adult previews every Week 8–9 source and may substitute a shorter
  excerpt. Pacing control stays with the adult throughout.
