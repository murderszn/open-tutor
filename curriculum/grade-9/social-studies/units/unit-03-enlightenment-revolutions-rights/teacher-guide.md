# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Enlightenment, Revolutions, Rights, and Consequences

*For the guiding adult. Student pages stay separate; this guide holds
session guidance, misconception notes, review prompts, and reteach
criteria. All question solutions live in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).*

## 1. How to use this unit

- **Pacing:** 20 sessions of ~50 minutes across Weeks 11–14 (see the
  unit [README](README.md) pacing table). S1 = lesson; S2 = practice
  set; S3 = project part; S4 = writing workshop; S5 = review/check.
- **Adult role:** preview every source (especially Week 13), read
  aloud where needed, scribe or record answers on request, and hold
  the two discussion rules: *explain before judging* and *name the
  measure before declaring success or failure*.
- **What "done" means:** a session is done when its success criterion
  (README table) is met, not when the clock runs out. If a session
  runs long, move its review to the next S5 rather than cutting the
  applied task.

## 2. Week 11 — Enlightenment ideas (Lesson 01, Set A, Project Part 1)

- **Teach for:** the four ideas as *tools* (matching claims to ideas),
  not as a list to memorize. Drill the matching move: "Which idea is
  doing the work in this sentence?"
- **Misconceptions:** (a) "The Enlightenment caused the revolutions" —
  correct to "supplied language; grievances, debts, and triggering
  events supplied the fuel." (b) "Everyone read Locke" — most met his
  ideas secondhand, in slogans, sermons, and pamphlets. (c) Treating
  Wollstonecraft as an afterthought — teach her as a theorist.
- **Image session (S2):** run the print-shop observation with the text
  alternative open for every learner, not only as a fallback. Ask the
  "who is missing" question before any learner answers the "what do
  you see" questions — it sets the unit's lens.
- **Reteach if:** the learner cannot match at least 3 of 4 ideas to
  claims by S5 → re-run Lesson 01's worked examples with new sentences
  you invent together.

## 3. Week 12 — America and France (Lesson 02, Set B, Project Part 2, quiz)

- **Teach for:** long-term vs. triggering causes as a *sorting habit*,
  and declarations as *arguments* rather than descriptions.
- **Misconceptions:** (a) "The French Revolution = the Terror" — the
  Terror (1793–94) was one phase of 1789–99. (b) "Slavery was an
  oversight in America" — it was debated, defended, and protected in
  constitutional compromises; say so plainly. (c) Single-cause
  explanations (tea tax, bread prices) — always ask for the second
  column.
- **Quiz (S5):** administer the week-12 quiz closed-book, then correct
  with notes open. Record which objectives missed: Q1–Q2 → Objective 1;
  Q3–Q5 → Objective 2; Q6 → Objectives 2/5. Reteach before Week 13.

## 4. Week 13 — Haiti and Latin America (Lesson 03, Set C, Project Part 3)

- **Teach for:** Haiti's threefold significance (proof of the
  universal claim, terror to slaveholding powers, economic punishment)
  and the creole/Haiti contrast (who led, who benefited).
- **Misconceptions:** (a) "Haiti is poor because Haitians failed" —
  weigh indemnity, isolation, and invasion threats before judging
  leadership. (b) "Latin America copied the U.S." — multiple models,
  adapted to societies with slavery and large Indigenous populations.
  (c) "France forgave the debt in 1838" — reduction (150→90 million)
  is not forgiveness; payments ran to 1947.
- **Sensitive content:** preview Lesson 03 and Set C with the learner
  at the start of the week. Offer the observation/reflection
  alternative for any activity: the learner writes or dictates what
  the sources show and what questions they raise, without role-playing
  an enslaved or victimized person. Never assign the learner to
  role-play enslavement, torture, or execution.

## 5. Week 14 — Synthesis (Lesson 04, Set D, Project Part 4, assessment)

- **Teach for:** the inclusion table as a *test* applied uniformly,
  and the two discussion moves: explanation vs. judgment (kept
  separate), and sourcing (who wrote it, when, for whom).
- **Misconceptions:** (a) "Exclusion means the ideas were worthless"
  — excluded groups used the same ideas to demand inclusion. (b)
  "Later historians make things up" — recovery scholarship rests on
  archives earlier writers ignored. (c) Confusing explanation with
  excuse — label the moves explicitly in discussion.
- **Deliberation (S4):** score both sides' fairness, not the side you
  agree with. A deliberation won by caricature is marked down.
- **Assessment (S5):** closed-book except Part C (open dossier).
  Score with the answer key's rubric; file the score band, not a
  letter grade, as the unit record.

## 6. Review prompts (S5 sessions)

- Week 11: "Which idea is hardest to put in your own words? Teach it
  back to me in two sentences." "Name two ways a person who could not
  read could still meet these ideas."
- Week 12: "Give one long-term and one triggering cause for each
  revolution without notes." "Which declaration would you print for
  your town, and what does it leave out?"
- Week 13: "Tell Haiti's story in five dates." "What did the indemnity
  cost, and who received the money?" "How did Latin American
  independence differ from Haiti's?"
- Week 14: "Fill one row of the inclusion table from memory." "What
  can your sources not show?" "Are rights universal? Answer in one
  sentence, then give your best evidence."

## 7. Reteach criteria and follow-ups

- **Week-12 quiz below 13:** revisit Lessons 01–02 with reteach prompts
  before Week 13 S1. Focus: idea matching (Lesson 01 worked examples)
  and cause sorting (Lesson 02, Part 1).
- **Project below 24:** learner revises the weakest rubric row only,
  with one adult conference, before the unit assessment.
- **Unit assessment below 32:** revisit Lessons 02–04 and retake Part B
  or C (adult's choice by weakest part). Do not re-administer the
  whole assessment unchanged; vary the passages.
- **Ready for U04 if:** quiz ≥13 (after correction), project ≥24,
  assessment ≥32, and the learner can state the unit question and
  answer it in two sentences with evidence.

## 8. Sensitive-content handling (all weeks)

- Preview all Week 13 sources and all video choices with the learner.
- Documentary/text sources only; no graphic imagery in this unit.
- Frame discussion around evidence and human dignity; do not ask the
  learner to defend slavery, terror, or the indemnity as positions in
  a debate — the deliberation topic is the universality of rights,
  not the legitimacy of enslavement.
- Offer the observation/reflection alternative for any activity the
  learner finds overwhelming, without penalty: the learner describes
  what the sources show, what is missing, and what questions remain.
- If the learner becomes distressed, pause the session; resume with a
  review activity (timeline, vocabulary) rather than pushing through
  the sensitive passage.

## 9. Answer-key use

- The [answer key](answer-key.md) solves every lesson practice item,
  every Practice Set item, the week-12 quiz, and the unit assessment,
  and scores the project rubric and the assessment's Part C. Read it
  before each S2/S4 session; do not hand it to the learner.
- Where the key says "acceptable variation," accept any answer that
  meets the stated reasoning standard, even if its wording differs.
