# Week 12 Formative Quiz — Unit 03

**Weeks 11–12 coverage · ~25 minutes · closed book, then open-note correction**

*Administer at the Week 12 S5 review. Score with the
[answer key](../answer-key.md). After scoring, the learner corrects missed
items with notes open — the correction, not the first score, is the learning
record (see [teacher-guide](../teacher-guide.md) §7).*

**1. Vocabulary (4 pts).** Match each term to its definition:

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| a. natural rights | i. government rests on the people's consent |
| b. social contract | ii. rights that exist before any government |
| c. separation of powers | iii. splitting lawmaking, enforcement, and judging |
| d. public sphere | iv. the network of print, letters, salons, and coffeehouses where ideas circulate |

**2. Thinkers and works (4 pts).** Match each thinker to the work:
(a) Locke, (b) Montesquieu, (c) Rousseau, (d) Wollstonecraft — to:
(i) *The Social Contract* (1762), (ii) *A Vindication of the Rights of
Woman* (1792), (iii) *Second Treatise of Government* (1689),
(iv) *The Spirit of the Laws* (1748).

**3. Causes (4 pts).** Name one long-term cause and one triggering cause
for the American Revolution, and one long-term cause and one triggering
cause for the French Revolution.

**4. Declarations (4 pts).** The U.S. Declaration of Independence (1776)
says governments derive "their just powers from the consent of the
governed." (a) Which Enlightenment idea is that? (b) The French
Declaration of the Rights of Man (1789) says "men are born and remain
free and equal in rights." Name one group each declaration did *not*
free or enfranchise in practice.

**5. Sequencing (2 pts).** Order these events earliest → latest:
(a) French Declaration of the Rights of Man, (b) Bastille stormed,
(c) U.S. Bill of Rights ratified, (d) Declaration of Independence adopted.

**6. Short explanation (2 pts).** In two sentences, explain why the
French Revolution's outcome by 1799 (Napoleon's coup) does not make the
1789 Declaration meaningless. (Hint: what survived — abolition of
feudal privilege, rights language — even under Napoleon?)

**Total: 20 pts.** Scoring bands: 17–20 secure (proceed); 13–16 partial
(reteach missed concepts in Week 13 S5); below 13 (revisit Lessons 01–02
with the teacher guide's reteach prompts before the Week 13 sessions).
