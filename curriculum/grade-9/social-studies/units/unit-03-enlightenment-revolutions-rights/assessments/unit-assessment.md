# Unit Assessment — Unit 03: Enlightenment, Revolutions, Rights, and Consequences

**Week 14, S5 · ~50 minutes · notes closed except where stated**

*Score with the [answer key](../answer-key.md). Part C is open-dossier:
the learner may use the Project Dossier (project.md) for Part C only.
All other parts are closed-book. The assessment tests Objectives 1–5.*

## Part A — Knowledge and sequencing (10 pts)

**A1. (4 pts)** Match: (a) Locke, (b) Montesquieu, (c) Rousseau,
(d) Wollstonecraft — to: (i) separation of powers, (ii) women's rights
claim, (iii) natural rights / social contract, (iv) general will.

**A2. (3 pts)** Order earliest → latest: (a) Haitian independence,
(b) Jamaica Letter, (c) French indemnity ordinance, (d) U.S.
Declaration of Independence, (e) Wollstonecraft's *Vindication*,
(f) Napoleon's coup.

**A3. (3 pts)** State the two indemnity figures and the year each
applied (original demand; reduced figure), and the year payments ended.

## Part B — Source analysis (10 pts)

Read these two short original passages (written for this unit, labeled
as reconstructions, not quotations):

> *Passage 1 (a Philadelphia printer, 1776):* "We print the Declaration
> today. The words 'consent of the governed' are the whole argument: if
> the king rules without our consent, we owe him no obedience."
>
> *Passage 2 (a Parisian market woman, 1793):* "They promised bread and
> rights. Bread still costs more than I earn, and the men who promised
> now arrest anyone who complains. I ask what rights are worth when the
> belly is empty."

**B1. (3 pts)** For each passage, state the maker's position and one
claim it makes about the revolution it describes.

**B2. (3 pts)** Which passage better illustrates the gap between a
declaration's promise and daily life? Justify in two sentences.

**B3. (4 pts)** A historian argues, "Passage 2 proves the French
Revolution failed." Give one reason to agree and one reason to
disagree, each in a sentence. (Disagreement must use evidence: feudal
privilege abolished, rights language survived, or the Terror as one
phase rather than the whole.)

## Part C — Argument (open dossier, 20 pts)

**C1. (20 pts)** Answer the unit question in 12–16 sentences: *When a
revolution declares rights "universal," who actually gets them — and
what happens to the people left out?*

Requirements: a clear claim; evidence from at least three revolutions;
at least one exclusion and one later use of rights language by an
excluded group; a counterclaim with a reply; one sentence on what the
sources cannot show. Scored with the unit rubric in the answer key
(claim 4, evidence 8, counterclaim 4, source-limit note 2, clarity 2).

## Part D — Inclusion table (10 pts)

Complete the table from memory for the **French** and **Haitian**
revolutions (rows) and two groups of your choice (columns), with a
phrase of evidence per cell. Then write one sentence explaining which
of the two revolutions extended rights further, and by what measure.
(8 cells × 1 pt; measure sentence 2 pts.)

**Total: 50 pts.** Scoring bands: 42–50 secure (proceed to U04);
32–41 partial (revise the weakest part before U04); below 32 (revisit
Lessons 02–04 with the adult and retake Part B or C).
