# Resource Pack — Unit 03: Enlightenment, Revolutions, Rights, and Consequences

**Goal:** support a grade-9 learner (with a guiding adult) in explaining
Enlightenment ideas, comparing the American and French revolutions,
analyzing Haiti and Latin American independence, and judging whose
rights each revolution secured. **Age fit:** grade 9; adult previews
all Week 13 sources and all video choices before use.

**Checked 2026-10-27.** Every external item below states whether it is
a direct resource or a labeled search link. No runtime, caption, or age
claim is made that was not checked. Videos supplement teaching; they do
not replace it. At least one free, no-account route supports every core
objective: the National Archives transcription, the UNESCO Jamaica
Letter page, the internal repository guides, and this unit's lessons
themselves.

## Web references (direct, checked 2026-10-27)

1. **National Archives — Declaration of Independence: A Transcription.**
   <https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/declaration-transcript> —
   official transcription of the Stone Engraving; free, no account.
   Use for Lesson 02 close reading. Confirmed live in search results,
   2026-10-27.
2. **UNESCO Memory of the World — Reply of a South American to a
   Gentleman of this Island (The Jamaica Letter, Simón Bolívar,
   1815).**
   <https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/lac/reply-south-american-gentleman-island-jamaica-letter-simon-bolivar-1815> —
   register entry confirming the letter's date (September 6, 1815),
   Kingston origin, and addressee (Henry Cullen). Free, no account.
3. **Haiti Independence Debt (background on the indemnity).**
   <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti_Independence_Debt> — summary of
   the 150-million-to-90-million reduction, the bank-loan mechanism,
   and payments lasting to 1947. Free, no account. Treat modern-dollar
   equivalents there as estimates that vary by method; this unit does
   not teach a single equivalent.
4. **Jamaica Letter (background).**
   <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_Letter> — summary confirming
   the September 6, 1815 date and the letter's argument. Free, no
   account. Cross-check claims against the UNESCO entry above.
5. **Library of Congress / National Archives founding-documents
   guides** — search the query `site:archives.gov founding documents
   Declaration Constitution Bill of Rights` for the Archives' own
   guides to the Charters of Freedom. Free, no account. (Labeled
   search; choose only archives.gov results.)
