# Project — The Industrial Empire Dossier

**Unit 04 investigation · Weeks 15–18, Session 3 each week · built in
four parts, submitted as one dossier at the end of Week 18.**

## Question

Who paid for industrial progress, who profited from it, and who got to
decide? Answer with evidence from all four weeks, not with slogans.

## Materials

- This unit's lessons, practice sets, and resource pack
- Blank outline maps (world; Africa) — the adult prints them; no
  borders pre-drawn
- Notebook/inquiry journal; colored pencils for maps
- The unit's mill-town image and pig-iron chart (or their text/data
  alternatives)

## Part 1 — Industrial spread map and timeline (Week 15)

1. On a world outline map, mark and date the spread of
   industrialization: Britain (c. 1760–1840), Belgium and France
   (early 1800s), Germany and the United States (mid-to-late 1800s),
   Japan (after 1868). Use arrows to show the order of spread.
2. Build a timeline with at least six dated points from Lesson 01
   (inventions, railways, steel).
3. Write a cause note (3–5 sentences): why did Britain go first, and
   why did followers industrialize faster than Britain had?

**Deliverable:** annotated map + timeline + cause note.

## Part 2 — Factory-life evidence board (Week 16)

1. Assemble two columns: *What the system offered* (wages, jobs,
   cheaper cloth, later reforms) and *What it cost* (hours, child
   labor, crowding, discipline). Each column needs at least three
   entries, each tied to a lesson fact or a model passage.
2. Add a "whose voice?" strip: one entry each from a worker's, an
   owner's, and an inspector's perspective, labeled as modeled or
   summarized evidence — never presented as a real document.
3. Write a verdict paragraph: on balance, for a textile worker's
   family in 1840, was the factory an improvement on farm labor?
   Qualify your answer by time and place.

**Deliverable:** evidence board (poster or two pages) + verdict
paragraph.

## Part 3 — Cape Colony under two empires (Week 17)

The Cape Colony is the unit's "one colony, two empires" case:

1. **Dutch period (1652–1806):** the Dutch East India Company founds
   a refreshment station at the Cape in 1652; it grows into a settler
   colony with enslaved labor and expanding frontiers, displacing
   Khoekhoe herders and clashing with Xhosa kingdoms to the east.
2. **British period (1806–1910):** Britain seizes the Cape during the
   Napoleonic Wars (1806; confirmed 1814), abolishes slavery in the
   1830s, and faces Boer treks inland; diamonds (1867) and gold (1886)
   then transform the region into a mineral economy with migrant
   labor, compound housing, and new wars — including the South African
   War (1899–1902).
3. Compare the two periods on one frame: who governed, who labored,
   who owned land, and how each group resisted (Xhosa wars, slave
   resistance, Boer republics, African workers' desertion and
   organization).
4. Write a conclusion: what stayed the same across the change of
   rulers, and what changed? (Hint: the flag changed; the extraction
   economy deepened.)

**Deliverable:** comparison table + 2–3 paragraph conclusion.
**Safety note:** textual sources only; the adult previews all
materials on slavery, war, and compound labor before use.

## Part 4 — Verdict brief: economic or political? (Week 18)

Write a 2–3 page brief answering: *Was the new imperialism primarily
economic or primarily political?*

Requirements:

- A thesis stated in the first paragraph.
- At least three sources of different kinds (a data series, a treaty
  provision, a resistance case, or a thinker's argument).
- One counterclaim, answered with evidence.
- A source-limit note: what your sources cannot show, and what
  evidence would change your mind.

**Deliverable:** verdict brief, attached to Parts 1–3 as the dossier's
final section.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (3) | Adequate (2) | Needs work (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence | Every claim tied to a lesson fact, figure, or source; data read accurately | Most claims evidenced; minor data slips | Claims asserted without evidence or with misread data |
| Comparison | Cape comparison uses the same frame for both periods; differences and continuities both named | Frame mostly consistent; one side thinner | Periods described separately, not compared |
| Argument | Thesis clear; counterclaim answered; source limits stated | Thesis present; counterclaim weak or limits missing | No thesis, or counterclaim ignored |
| Use of sources | Model passages labeled as modeled; treaty taught in paraphrase; image labeled as reconstruction | Labels mostly present | A model passage or the image presented as a real document |
| Presentation | Map/timeline legible and dated; dossier organized; text alternative used where needed | Mostly legible; minor gaps | Illegible, undated, or missing a part |

**Scoring:** 13–15 = dossier complete at unit standard; 10–12 = complete
with one revision (teacher guide §5 names the revision); below 10 =
reteach the weakest criterion and revise that part.
