# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Industrialization, Capitalism, Imperialism, and Social Change

**Grade 9 · Social Studies · Weeks 15–18 · adult-facing; keep separate
from student pages.**

## §1 Session-by-session guidance

- **Week 15, S1 (Lesson 01):** Stress the combination, not the single
  cause. If the learner names only coal, return to worked example 1.
  The pig-iron chart (or data table) should be on the table during
  Part 2 — learners retain the scale better when they compute it
  themselves.
- **Week 15, S2–S4:** Practice Set A first, then Project Part 1. The
  writing workshop paragraph is the week's key evidence: file it for
  comparison with the Week 18 verdict brief.
- **Week 16, S1 (Lesson 02):** Show the mill-town image *after* the
  readiness check, and read the text alternative aloud once even for
  learners using the image — the alternative names features learners
  may miss. Emphasize: the image is a reconstruction, not evidence.
- **Week 16, S2–S4:** Practice Set B; Project Part 2; the argument
  workshop (did lives improve?) is the natural rehearsal for the
  verdict brief. Insist on a time limit in every claim ("by 1850" vs.
  "by 1870").
- **Week 16, S5:** Week-16 quiz (25 min), then reteach planning from
  §5. Do not start Lesson 03 until the quiz is scored.
- **Week 17, S1 (Lesson 03):** Preview the Congo material yourself
  before the session (§8). Teach the General Act in paraphrase; the
  full text is reference only. The map task needs a blank Africa map —
  print it before S1.
- **Week 17, S2–S4:** Practice Set C; Project Part 3 (Cape Colony);
  the demand-to-policy chain is the week's hardest skill — model one
  chain together before assigning C3.
- **Week 18, S1 (Lesson 04):** Passages C and D are the unit's most
  important close read. Teach the "what does each writer count as a
  cost?" question explicitly; it transfers to the verdict brief.
- **Week 18, S2–S4:** Practice Set D; Project Part 4; deliberation.
  Run the deliberation as two prepared positions with evidence, not
  as a free debate — the goal is reasoning, not winning.
- **Week 18, S5:** Unit assessment (50 min) + dossier submission.

## §2 Misconception notes (by lesson)

- **Lesson 01:** inventions alone; Watt "invented" steam; immediate
  prosperity. See lesson teacher notes.
- **Lesson 02:** owners simply cruel; 1833 ended child labor; cities
  only bad. The corrective frame: systems reward behaviors; laws and
  organization changed the rewards.
- **Lesson 03:** Berlin divided Africa in one meeting; colonies were
  profitable; Africans did not resist. Corrective: rules → race →
  resistance, over three decades.
- **Lesson 04:** resistance "failed" because empires lasted; socialism
  = nationalism; Marx invented exploitation complaints. Corrective:
  duration is not consent; visions differ in the problem they see.

## §3 Differentiation

- **Reading/language access:** pre-teach each lesson's vocabulary
  list the day before; allow the text alternative for the image and
  the data table for the chart in every task.
- **Alternate response modes:** applied tasks and the dossier may be
  dictated and scribed, drawn as annotated maps/posters, or recorded
  orally; score the thinking per the rubrics, not the medium.
- **Extensions:** Lesson 01 inventor biography; Lesson 02 Acts
  comparison; Lesson 03 Menelik/Adwa research; Lesson 04 Hobson test.
  Each extension is optional and never a prerequisite for the next
  lesson.

## §4 Answer-key use

`answer-key.md` solves every practice, quiz, assessment, and project
item independently. Check the learner's work against the key, not
against your memory of the lesson. For open responses, the key gives a
model plus acceptable variations — accept any answer that meets the
rubric's stated criteria, even if its wording differs.

## §5 Readiness, reteach, and follow-up

- **Week-16 quiz:** 18+/22 = proceed. 14–17 = reteach the missed
  concept (usually cause-combination or Act provisions) and retest
  those items only. Below 14 = reteach Week 15–16 with the data table
  and image work redone before Lesson 03.
- **Unit assessment:** 24+/30 = unit standard met; proceed to U05.
  19–23 = revise the weakest part (data, sources, map, or essay)
  within one week. Below 19 = reteach Weeks 17–18, then retest Parts
  B and D.
- **Dossier:** 13–15 = complete. 10–12 = revise the weakest rubric
  row. Below 10 = reteach that criterion and revise that part.
- **Targeted follow-up:** cause-combination errors → Lesson 01 worked
  example 1 + Practice A2. Data errors → Practice A1 with the table.
  Source-frame errors → Lesson 04 worked example 3 + Practice D1.
  Argument errors → the Week 16 argument workshop, then the verdict
  brief frame in project.md Part 4.

## §6 Project scoring notes

- **Part 1:** accept any six dated points from Lesson 01; the cause
  note must name at least two interacting causes.
- **Part 2:** each column needs three evidenced entries; the verdict
  paragraph must be time-qualified (1840 vs. later).
- **Part 3:** the comparison frame (governor, labor, land, resistance)
  must be applied to both periods; the conclusion must name one
  continuity and one change.
- **Part 4:** thesis + three sources of different kinds + answered
  counterclaim + source-limit note. Missing any element caps the
  argument row at 2.

## §7 Review prompts (S5 sessions)

- Week 15: "What three things had to be true at once for Britain to
  go first? What does the pig-iron chart prove, and what does it not?"
- Week 16: "Whose perspective changed your mind this week, and which
  detail did it?"
- Week 17: "What did Berlin decide, what did it fail to decide, and
  who was not in the room?"
- Week 18: "Which vision — liberal, socialist, nationalist — best
  explains the Cape Colony case, and where does it fall short?"

## §8 Sensitive-content handling

- Preview all Week 17 materials (Congo rubber regime, Casement
  Report findings, famine references) before the week begins; give the
  learner advance notice of the topics.
- Teach colonial violence through mechanisms (quotas, taxes, land
  seizure) and documented findings, never through graphic detail or
  imagery. The unit contains no graphic images.
- Frame discussion around evidence and human dignity: name the people
  affected, their actions, and their resistance — not only their
  suffering.
- Offer an observation/reflection alternative for any activity the
  learner finds overwhelming: the learner may write a reflection on
  the sources instead of completing the activity, with no penalty.
- If the learner asks about death tolls: explain that estimates vary
  widely by method and source, that historians debate them, and that
  this unit teaches the mechanisms and documented findings rather
  than a single disputed number.

## §9 What this unit does not claim

- No state adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification.
- Standards codes are paraphrased; check the authoritative C3
  Framework for exact wording.
- Model passages A–D are original writing for this unit, not real
  documents; the mill-town image is an AI reconstruction, not a
  primary source; production figures are rounded historical
  approximations.
- The unit is a validated draft, not educator-reviewed curriculum.
