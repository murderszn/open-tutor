# Answer Key — Unit 04: Industrialization, Capitalism, Imperialism, and Social Change

**Adult-facing. Every item below was solved independently of the
lessons and reconciled with them. Accept any learner answer that meets
the stated criteria, even if its wording differs.**

## §Lesson 01 — guided practice

1. Four advantages: (a) cheap coal near iron — energy + metal without
   charcoal limits; (b) high wages — mechanization profitable; (c)
   capital, markets, property protection — investment and buyers; (d)
   agricultural change — mobile workforce. Without any one, the loop
   stalls: e.g., without capital, inventions stay prototypes.
2. Model sentence: "Because labor was expensive, a machine that
   replaced workers paid for itself quickly, so inventing was
   profitable."
3. 2,700,000 − 650,000 = 2,050,000 tons added between 1830 and 1852.
4. Correction: Newcomen built the first practical steam engine
   (1712); Watt improved it (patented 1769) to use far less coal and
   to drive rotary motion, which let mills run anywhere coal could be
   carried.

## §Lesson 02 — guided practice

1. Industrial features (any three): mill dominating the town; canal
   + barge (bulk transport); identical cottage rows (bulk worker
   housing); smoke from many chimneys (coal at scale). Beyond the
   mill: the church spire (older institutions persist).
2. Matching: under-9 ban → children working from age 7; 48-hour week
   (9–13) → 14-hour days; 2 hours' schooling → illiteracy after years
   in a mill; four inspectors → laws ignored (the provision targets
   enforcement itself: too few inspectors for ~4,000 mills).
3. Two reasons (any two): only four inspectors for ~4,000 mills;
   textiles only at first; adult men's hours untouched; parents lied
   about ages for needed wages; mills hid young workers.
4. Correction: Luddites (1811–16) were skilled croppers whose wages
   were cut by machines; they broke machines as a wage protest, not
   from abstract hatred of technology.

## §Lesson 03 — guided practice

1. Motives + examples: economic — cotton/rubber/markets; strategic —
   Suez Canal and coaling stations; ideological — nationalism,
   "civilizing mission," racial theories; men on the spot — Leopold's
   agents, traders, officers acting ahead of their governments.
2. Effective occupation required real administration to validate a
   claim, so powers raced to occupy territory before rivals — the
   rule meant to prevent war accelerated the scramble.
3. No African leader was invited; the Act's legitimacy fails the
   consent test — it regulated Africa without Africans, which is why
   the unit treats it as a European agreement, not a settlement.
4. No: both used forced labor and extraction. The Congo was a
   personal possession, India a state empire — the owner differed,
   the mechanism (tax in cash, land seizure, forced labor) rhymed.

## §Lesson 04 — guided practice

1. Examples: armed — Samori Touré or Adwa (tool: army; audience:
   the invader and other states); everyday — tax evasion, slowdowns,
   language schools (tool: evasion/culture; audience: the community
   itself); home reform — Chartism or suffrage (tool: petitions,
   strikes; audience: the home public and Parliament).
2. Agreed facts (any two): a road was cut; a tax is collected in
   coin; a school exists; cotton is brought to a depot. Differing
   judgments: progress (C) vs. loss (D) — e.g., C's "progress is slow
   but visible" vs. D's "land that was never theirs to measure."
3. Liberal: empire distorts markets and serves special interests.
   Socialist: capitalism exploits workers at home and abroad.
   Nationalist: peoples should rule themselves in their own states.
4. Nationalism claimed every nation deserved a state — great powers
   used it to justify expansion, colonized peoples used it to demand
   independence. Same principle, opposite directions.

## §Practice Set A

- **A1.1:** 2,700,000 ÷ 17,000 = 158.8… ≈ **159-fold**.
- **A1.2:** 1805→1852: 2,700,000 − 250,000 = **2,450,000 tons**.
  1740→1805: 250,000 − 17,000 = **233,000 tons**.
- **A1.3:** Largest absolute increase: 1830–1852 (+2,050,000 tons).
  Factors: 1740–1788: 70,000 ÷ 17,000 ≈ 4.1; 1830–1852:
  2,700,000 ÷ 650,000 ≈ 4.2. Nearly the same factor, but the later
  interval added about 39 times as many tons — a factor hides scale;
  always report both.
- **A1.4:** Coal is the energy base behind the iron: iron output
  could not have grown 159-fold without coal for smelting, steam, and
  railways. The coal series shows the fuel side of the same system.
- **A2:** High wages → motive to mechanize; missing it, machines
  don't pay. Capital and markets → investment + buyers; missing them,
  inventions stay prototypes. Agricultural change → mobile workforce;
  missing it, mills lack hands.
- **A3:** Cottage: worker's own/rented tools; worker sets hours; paid
  per piece; pace set by the worker/seasons. Factory: owner's
  machines; owner/bell sets hours; wage for time; pace set by the
  machine.
- **A4:** Accept any paragraph with ≥3 interacting causes, one A1
  figure, and an explicit rejection of single-cause explanation.

## §Practice Set B

- **B1.1–B1.2:** Five features as in Lesson 02 guided practice.
  Each inference must be paired with a non-proof: e.g., cottages
  suggest bulk worker housing but do not prove mill ownership of
  them; smoke shows coal burned at scale but not disease rates.
- **B1.3:** Accept any defended choice; strongest is usually the
  mill's central dominance or the canal, because transport + plant
  location explain the town's layout.
- **B2:** 1 → under-9 ban (and 48-hour week); 2 → 2 hours' schooling;
  3 → no night work under 18; 4 → the inspectorate provision
  (four men, ~4,000 mills).
- **B3:** Inspector (A): checkable — ages, fines, locked schoolroom;
  uncheckable from the passage — whether this mill is typical.
  Owner (B): checkable — none offered beyond the claim of 400
  families (a count that could be checked by visiting); uncheckable —
  the prediction that trade will leave if hours are cut. The
  inspector's specifics can be verified by a visit; the owner's
  prediction cannot be tested until the law passes.
- **B4:** Accept any argument meeting the requirements; the time
  limit is mandatory (e.g., "by 1850, worse in hours and housing,
  better in wages and goods; by 1870, improvement is clearer").

## §Practice Set C

- **C1.1:** Free Congo/Niger trade and navigation — benefited trading
  nations and concessionaires most; Africans gained no enforcement
  voice. **C1.2:** Congo Free State under Leopold personally —
  benefited Leopold and his concession companies; Congolese peoples
  bore quotas and coercion. **C1.3:** Effective occupation — benefited
  powers able to field administrators and soldiers quickly; it
  penalized slower claimants and all African polities.
- **C2.1:** About 80 percentage points over about 45 years
  (c. 1870–1914).
- **C2.2:** "Control" hides thin administration (claims on maps vs.
  rule on the ground) and constant resistance; it counts claimed
  territory, not consent or settled population.
- **C2.3:** Their exception disproves inevitability: Ethiopia's
  victory at Adwa and Liberia's U.S.-linked status show outcomes
  depended on organization, diplomacy, and circumstance — not on a law
  of history.
- **C3:** Accept any four-arrow chain with a test question per arrow
  (e.g., rubber → Congo quotas → Force Publique → Casement exposure →
  1908 annexation; test: did quotas actually raise revenue? did the
  annexation end forced labor? — it did not).
- **C4:** Accept any paragraph meeting the requirements; the
  non-economic motive and the "does not prove" sentence are mandatory.

## §Practice Set D

- **D1:** Writer/audience: C — administrator reporting upward to
  superiors; D — colonized intellectual addressing compatriots (and
  posterity). Progress/loss: C counts roads, taxes, schoolboys; D
  counts food crops, language, land lost. Agreed facts: road, tax in
  coin, school, cotton depot. One-sided claims: C's "district is
  quiet" (D disputes what quiet means); D's "land never theirs to
  measure" (C treats survey as neutral). Verification: tax receipts,
  school rolls, land records, or testimony from the river village.
- **D2:** Liberal — problem: distorted markets/special interests;
  remedy: free trade + democratic control of foreign policy; example:
  Hobson's critique of financiers. Socialist — problem: worker
  exploitation; remedy: collective ownership + worker solidarity;
  example: *Manifesto* (1848). Nationalist — problem: foreign rule;
  remedy: self-governing state; example: Adwa/Ethiopia or Indian
  nationalism.
- **D3:** Accept any paragraph covering actor, tool, achievement, and
  limit, per Lesson 04.
- **D4:** Error 1: Berlin did not divide Africa in 1885 — it set
  rules; partition took three decades. Error 2: Africans did not
  accept rule — resistance was constant (Adwa, Mahdists, Boxers,
  everyday evasion, newspapers).

## §Unit assessment

- **A1:** 650,000 ÷ 70,000 = 9.285… ≈ **9.3-fold**.
- **A2:** Pig iron: 2,700,000 − 17,000 = 2,683,000 tons. Coal:
  ~30,000,000 − ~3,000,000 = ~27,000,000 tons. Coal grew more in
  absolute tons (figures rounded as taught).
- **A3:** Together they show energy and metal output scaling by
  orders of magnitude within a century — a new production system, not
  a better workshop. They do not show wages, hours, health, or who
  kept the profits.
- **B4:** Agreed facts (any two): road cut; tax in coin; school
  exists; cotton to depot.
- **B5:** Core difference: progress vs. loss. C: "Progress is slow
  but visible." D: "They measure progress by the miles of railway
  they lay through land that was never theirs to measure."
- **B6:** C's administrative counts are easier to verify (tax
  receipts, school rolls, survey records). A concrete check: compare
  the depot's cotton receipts against the tax roll, or inspect the
  school's roll for the claimed thirty boys.
- **C7:** Map: Congo basin = Congo Free State (Leopold, then Belgium);
  Egypt/Suez = British occupation from 1882; Ethiopia = independent
  (Adwa 1896); Liberia = independent. 3 pts (1 per region pair, roughly).
- **C8:** Effective occupation = a claim required real administration
  + notification; unintended consequence = a race to occupy quickly,
  accelerating the scramble (3 pts).
- **D9 (essay):** 12 pts — thesis 2; evidence 6 (three pieces from
  different lessons/kinds, 2 each); counterclaim answered 2;
  source-limit sentence 2. Model thesis (either side accepted if
  evidenced): "Imperialism was primarily economic in motive but
  political in form: industrial demand supplied the push, while
  rivalry, prestige, and men on the spot decided where and how fast
  it struck." Counterclaim models: "If economics ruled, unprofitable
  colonies would have been abandoned — they were kept for prestige
  and strategy," answered with Suez/Egypt or the Congo's private
  profits vs. state costs.
