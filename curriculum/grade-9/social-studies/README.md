# Grade 9 Social Studies

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are
delivered; the eight units are planned, not yet written. See
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained
for one grade-9 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 9 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-9 **modern world history** course, circa 1450
to the present, built on the C3 Framework's inquiry arc: world regions and
global connections circa 1450; empires, exchange, colonization, and resistance;
Enlightenment ideas and the age of revolutions; industrialization, capitalism,
and imperialism; the world wars, interwar ideologies, and genocide studied
through historical evidence; decolonization and the Cold War; globalization,
migration, development, and environmental pressures; and a contemporary-world
inquiry capstone using comparative sources and civic reasoning.

Each of the eight units runs four weeks at about five 50-minute sessions per
week: a concept launch, a close-reading and skills session, a source
investigation or application session, an evidence-based writing or deliberation
session, and a review-and-check session. Learners work with public-domain
primary sources (travelers' accounts, declarations, treaty excerpts, speeches,
testimony), maps and datasets, structured deliberations, and argument writing.
Two diagnostic weeks open the year, with midyear and final review weeks.

This is a proposed pathway, not a universal requirement: high-school
social-studies course order varies by system. A guiding adult checks placement
before starting and previews every primary source and topic for mature themes
— slavery, conquest, war, and genocide are central to this course and are
handled with age-appropriate, adult-supervised inquiry (see the audit's source
and safety notes).

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. **Analyze world regions circa 1450:** compare the political organization,
   economies, and cultural systems of at least four major world regions using
   maps and data. (C3 D2.His.1, D2.His.2.9-12)
2. **Explain exchange, empire, and colonization:** trace how maritime trade,
   the Columbian exchange, and European colonial empires reshaped societies;
   analyze resistance to conquest and enslavement. (D2.His.1, D2.His.4,
   D2.His.14.9-12)
3. **Explain Enlightenment ideas and revolutionary outcomes:** trace how rights
   ideas traveled and produced different outcomes across the American, French,
   Haitian, and Latin American revolutions. (D2.His.2, D2.His.4, D2.His.5.9-12;
   D2.Civ.14.9-12)
4. **Analyze industrialization and imperialism:** explain how industrial
   capitalism changed production and labor, and how it fueled the "new
   imperialism" and the partition of Africa. (D2.His.14.9-12)
5. **Evaluate the world wars and interwar ideologies:** analyze causes and
   effects of WWI and WWII; compare liberalism, fascism, and communism; assess
   evidence about the Holocaust and at least one other genocide. (D2.His.6,
   D2.His.8, D2.His.10, D2.His.12, D2.His.14, D2.His.16.9-12)
6. **Explain decolonization and the Cold War:** evaluate how new nations formed
   after 1945 and how U.S.–Soviet rivalry shaped the world. (D2.His.1,
   D2.His.3, D2.His.5.9-12; D2.Civ.2.9-12)
7. **Analyze globalization:** explain how migration, trade, development
   policies, and environmental pressures connect societies; evaluate intended
   and unintended policy outcomes. (D2.Civ.13.9-12; D4.6, D4.7.9-12)
8. **Work with sources:** classify sources; infer maker, date, place, and
   audience; detect limitations and gaps; judge relevance and utility.
   (D2.His.8, D2.His.10, D2.His.12.9-12; D3.1–D3.4.9-12)
9. **Argue from evidence:** integrate multiple sources into reasoned arguments;
   critique arguments for credibility and missing perspectives. (D2.His.16.9-12;
   D4.1–D4.4.9-12)
10. **Inquire and take informed action:** frame compelling and supporting
    questions, plan source gathering, present conclusions, and analyze a
    contemporary public issue with disciplinary lenses. (D1.1–D1.5.9-12;
    D4.6, D4.7.9-12)

Standards references are to the College, Career, and Civic Life (C3) Framework
for Social Studies State Standards (National Council for the Social Studies),
grades 9–12 band. Codes were verified during the audit (see §4 of the
scope-and-sequence); descriptions there are brief paraphrases — check the
authoritative framework for exact wording. **No state adoption, accreditation,
or alignment certification is claimed.**

## Units (planned, not yet written)

- U01 — World-history inquiry: geography and global connections circa 1450
- U02 — Empires, exchange, colonization, and resistance
- U03 — Enlightenment, revolutions, rights, and consequences
- U04 — Industrialization, capitalism, imperialism, and social change
- U05 — World wars, ideologies, genocide, and historical evidence
- U06 — Decolonization, Cold War, and new nations
- U07 — Globalization, migration, development, and environmental pressures
- U08 — Contemporary world inquiry: comparative sources and civic reasoning
- R00 — Diagnostic, midyear/final review, cumulative assessment and keys
