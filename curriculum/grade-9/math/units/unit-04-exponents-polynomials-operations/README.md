# Unit 04 — Exponents: Polynomials and Operations

**Grade 9 Mathematics · Weeks 15–18 · Track issue [#42](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/42)**
Status: **validated draft** — not educator-reviewed or merged.

## Essential question

How can one set of exponent rules handle whole numbers, fractions, and
negative numbers — and how does that same structure let us add,
multiply, and rewrite polynomials without guessing?

## What this unit does

Units 01–03 built linear equations, linear functions, and systems.
This unit steps back to the algebra underneath them. First it extends
the exponent rules the learner already knows for integer exponents to
rational exponents: a fractional exponent is a root, and the familiar
product, power, and quotient rules keep working. The learner then
learns to rewrite radical expressions in simplest exact form and to
keep exact answers separate from decimal approximations — a
distinction that matters every time a root is not a whole number.

The second half turns to polynomials. The learner adds, subtracts, and
multiplies polynomials, uses area models to see why the distributive
property gives every term, and learns the special-product patterns
(difference of squares, perfect-square trinomials) that let structure
do work that brute-force multiplication would repeat. Week 18 is the
midyear review: cumulative problems across Units 01–04, a reteach
session driven by the formative quiz, and a reminder that the exact
form is the answer until a context asks for a rounded one.

All data in this unit's contexts is fictional practice data, invented
for the exercises. No prices, areas, or garden figures here describe a
real business or a real plot of land.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner can:

1. Extend integer-exponent properties to rational exponents:
   interpret `a^(1/n)` as the principal nth root of `a`, evaluate
   `a^(m/n)` and `a^(-m/n)` for bases where the root is a real
   number, and state when an even root of a negative number has no
   real value. (N-RN.1)
2. Rewrite expressions involving radicals and rational exponents
   using exponent properties; simplify radicals to simplest form
   (no perfect-power factor left inside the radical, no radical in a
   denominator); and distinguish an exact form such as `5√2` from a
   decimal approximation such as `7.07`. (N-RN.2, N-RN.3)
3. Add and subtract polynomials, write the result in standard form,
   and name its degree, leading coefficient, and constant term.
   Explain why polynomials are closed under addition, subtraction,
   and multiplication, by analogy with the integers. (A-APR.1)
4. Multiply polynomials using the distributive property and area
   models, including a binomial times a binomial and a binomial
   times a trinomial; state the degree of a product from the degrees
   of its factors. (A-APR.1, A-SSE.2)
5. Recognize and use special products — `(a + b)(a − b) = a² − b²`,
   `(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²`, `(a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²` — to
   rewrite expressions and compute mentally, and choose a level of
   accuracy (exact, or rounded to a stated place) appropriate to the
   context. (A-SSE.2, N-Q.3)

## Prerequisites

- Unit 01 skills: integer-exponent properties (`a^m · a^n = a^(m+n)`,
  `(a^m)^n = a^(mn)`, `a^(-n) = 1/a^n`); combining like terms;
  multi-step equation solving with a written justification.
- Unit 02 skills: reading a graph and a table; function notation is
  **not** required here — exponents and polynomials are written in
  variable form, and Unit 05 will connect factored polynomials to
  function graphs.
- Unit 03 skills: checking a claimed answer by substitution; the
  midyear review in Week 18 revisits systems and linear models
  briefly, and no new systems content is taught here.
- Grade-8 fluency (diagnostic weeks): square and cube roots of
  perfect powers; prime factorization for finding perfect-square
  and perfect-cube factors; rational-number arithmetic.
- **New here:** rational exponents; simplest radical form;
  polynomial vocabulary (term, degree, leading coefficient,
  standard form); polynomial addition, subtraction, and
  multiplication; area models for products; special products;
  exact-vs.-approximate discipline.

## Standards and source notes

- **N-RN.1** — explain how the definition of the meaning of rational
  exponents follows from extending the properties of integer
  exponents to those values, allowing for a notation for radicals in
  terms of rational exponents.
- **N-RN.2** — rewrite expressions involving radicals and rational
  exponents using the properties of exponents.
- **N-RN.3** — explain why the sum or product of two rational
  numbers is rational; that the sum of a rational number and an
  irrational number is irrational; that the product of a nonzero
  rational number and an irrational number is irrational; and that
  the product of two irrational numbers can be rational.
- **A-SSE.2** — use the structure of an expression to identify ways
  to rewrite it (for example, recognizing a difference of squares
  or a perfect-square trinomial pattern).
- **A-APR.1** — understand that polynomials form a system analogous
  to the integers, namely, they are closed under the operations of
  addition, subtraction, and multiplication; add, subtract, and
  multiply polynomials.
- **N-Q.3** — choose a level of accuracy appropriate to limitations
  on measurement when reporting quantities.
- Codes and descriptions follow the Common Core State Standards for
  Mathematics, High School — Number and Quantity and Algebra
  (corestandards.org, as cross-walked in this track's
  [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md), verified 2026-10-06).
  No claim of state adoption or accreditation is made.

## Session pacing — 20 sessions (four weeks × five 50-minute sessions)

| Session | Work | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lesson 01 — rational exponents as roots | Learner evaluates `a^(1/n)`, `a^(m/n)`, `a^(-m/n)` and names the no-real-value case |
| 2 | Practice A — rational exponents | 8/10 practice items correct; root-first order shown |
| 3 | Lesson 02 — rewriting radicals, simplest form | Learner simplifies a radical by factoring out the largest perfect power |
| 4 | Practice B — radical rewriting, exact vs. approximate | Every item left exact or rounded only as asked; approximations labeled with ≈ |
| 5 | Lesson 03 — polynomials, addition and subtraction | Learner adds/subtracts in standard form and names degree and leading coefficient |
| 6 | Practice C — polynomial addition/subtraction, closure | Like terms combined only; subtraction distributes the minus to every term |
| 7 | Reading / reference — Resource Pack refs 1–2; retrieval: Unit 01 exponent rules, Unit 03 checking habit | Learner restates N-RN.1 in their own words and re-solves one Unit 01 exponent item |
| 8 | Lesson 04 — multiplying polynomials, area models (generated image + SVG) | Learner multiplies by distributing every term and reads an area model |
| 9 | Practice D — polynomial multiplication, degree of a product | Products in standard form; degree stated from the factors before multiplying |
| 10 | Project launch — The Polynomial Garden | Learner defines bed dimensions as binomials and writes the area product |
| 11 | Lesson 05 — special products and structure | Learner uses `(a+b)(a−b)` and binomial squares without expanding term by term |
| 12 | Practice E — special products, error analysis | The `(x+3)² ≠ x²+9` error found and corrected with the missing middle term |
| 13 | Lesson 06 — exact, approximate, and midyear connections | Learner classifies rational/irrational sums and products and picks a sensible rounding |
| 14 | Practice F — exact/approximate, cumulative Units 01–03 retrieval, project work | Approximations labeled; cumulative items checked by substitution |
| 15 | Project work session — garden plan, numeric check, cost estimate | Draft plan, area polynomials, numeric check at a chosen `x`, and cost table complete |
| 16 | Formative quiz (`assessments/week-04_quiz.md`) | Gaps named by objective; reteach list written before the assessment |
| 17 | Review and reteach — highest-need objective from the quiz | Learner re-solves two missed-type items correctly with reasoning shown |
| 18 | Culminating assessment (`assessments/unit-assessment.md`) | All five objectives sampled; extended tasks show reasoning, not just answers |
| 19 | Feedback and revision — assessment corrections | Every missed item corrected with a written reason for the original error |
| 20 | Flex / extension — write-your-own special-product problem; portfolio assembly | Learner writes a solvable problem with a key, using a pattern from Lesson 05 |
