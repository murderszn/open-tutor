# Practice — Unit 04: Exponents, Polynomials, and Operations

Work each set in the session named in the unit pacing. Show the
root-first step for rational exponents, the factorization for
simplified radicals, and the partial products for polynomial
multiplication. Do not round an exact answer unless the item asks
for an approximation, and use `≈` whenever you do round.

## Practice A — Rational exponents (Session 2, after Lesson 01)

1. Evaluate: `144^(1/2)`, `1000^(1/3)`, `32^(1/5)`.
2. Evaluate, root first: `27^(2/3)`, `25^(3/2)`, `81^(3/4)`.
3. Evaluate: `36^(-1/2)`, `8^(-2/3)`, `64^(-1/3)`.
4. Evaluate or state that there is no real value, with a reason:
   `(-125)^(1/3)`, `(-64)^(1/2)`, `(-32)^(1/5)`.
5. Error analysis: a learner claims `16^(3/2) = 24`, "because
   `16 · 3/2 = 24`." Find the error and give the correct value.
6. Which is larger: `64^(1/2)` or `64^(1/3)`? Which is larger:
   `9^(3/2)` or `27^(2/3)`? Show both values in each pair.
7. A cube has volume `216` cubic inches. Its edge length is
   `216^(1/3)` inches. Find the edge length and check it by cubing.
8. Evaluate `49^(3/2)` and `125^(-1/3)`.

## Practice B — Radical rewriting, exact vs. approximate (Session 4, after Lesson 02)

1. Simplify: `√48`, `√162`, `∛135`.
2. Simplify: `√300`, `∛54`, and `√(75x²)` for `x ≥ 0`.
3. Evaluate, root first: `16^(5/4)` and `27^(4/3)`.
4. Rewrite `√(x³)` with a rational exponent, and rewrite `a^(2/5)`
   with a radical sign.
5. Combine like radical terms: `√12 + √27` and `√98 − √50`.
6. Give each length exactly and then approximately to two decimal
   places: `√90` and `√20`.
7. Rationalize the denominator: `2/√3`.
8. Error analysis: a learner simplifies `√72` as `3√8` and stops.
   Explain why this is unfinished, and finish it.

## Practice C — Polynomial addition and subtraction (Session 6, after Lesson 03)

1. Add: `(5x² + 3x − 1) + (2x² − 6x + 4)`.
2. Subtract: `(7x³ − 2x + 5) − (3x³ + 4x − 2)`.
3. Subtract: `(2x² + 5) − (5x² − 3x + 1)`.
4. Write in standard form and name the degree, leading
   coefficient, and constant term: (a) `9 − 2x² + 5x³`;
   (b) `−x⁴ + 3x² − 7`.
5. A rectangle has sides `(3x + 2)` feet and `(x − 5)` feet.
   Write its perimeter as a polynomial in standard form.
6. Is each expression a polynomial? Explain in one sentence each:
   (a) `x² + √x`; (b) `4x³ − 2`.
7. Explain closure: why is the sum of two polynomials always a
   polynomial, while a quotient such as `(2x)/(x²)` need not be?
   Simplify the quotient to make your point.
8. Add `(x² + 3x) + (2x² − x)`, then evaluate the sum at `x = 2`.
   Check by evaluating the two original polynomials at `x = 2`
   and adding the values.

## Practice D — Polynomial multiplication (Session 9, after Lesson 04)

1. Multiply: `(x + 9)(x + 2)` and `(x − 6)(x + 3)`.
2. Multiply: `(4x − 3)(2x + 5)`.
3. Multiply: `(x + 3)(x² − 2x + 4)`.
4. Multiply: `(2x − 1)(x² + x − 2)`.
5. Predict the degree of each product before multiplying, and
   name the leading coefficient of the product:
   (a) `(x³ + 1)(x² − 2)`; (b) `(3x²)(2x³)`.
6. A rectangle has length `(3x + 2)` feet and width `(x + 4)` feet.
   Write its area as a polynomial, and find the area when `x = 3`.
   Check by substituting first, then multiplying.
7. Error analysis: a learner claims `(x + 4)(x + 1) = x² + 4`.
   Find the missing terms and give the correct product.
8. Multiply `(x − 2)³`, showing the two-step multiplication
   `(x − 2)(x − 2)²`.

## Practice E — Special products (Session 12, after Lesson 05)

1. Multiply: `(x + 10)(x − 10)` and `(6x + 2)(6x − 2)`.
2. Expand: `(x + 11)²` and `(4x − 3)²`.
3. Compute with a pattern, showing the pattern step:
   `104 · 96` and `63²`.
4. Error analysis: a learner claims `(5x + 2)² = 25x² + 4`. Find
   the missing term and give the correct expansion.
5. Decide whether each product fits a special-product pattern, and
   expand it: (a) `(x + 3)(x − 7)`; (b) `(2x − 5)(2x + 5)`;
   (c) `(x − 9)²`. For the one that does not fit, say why.
6. Multiply: `(x² + 4)(x² − 4)`.
7. A square has side `(x + 5)` feet; a rectangle has length
   `(x + 5)` feet and width `(x − 5)` feet. Write both areas as
   polynomials. By how much (as a polynomial) is the square's area
   larger?
8. Expand `(a + b)² − (a − b)²` and simplify. What does the result
   say about the difference between the square of a sum and the
   square of a difference?

## Practice F — Exact, approximate, and cumulative retrieval (Session 14, after Lesson 06)

1. Classify each as rational or irrational, with a rule or a
   computation: `7 + √11`; `√7 · √28`; `√7 · √11`; `0 · √3`;
   `√2 + √2`.
2. Give each length exactly and then approximately to two decimal
   places: `√150` and `√60`.
3. (Unit 01 retrieval) Solve `5x + 3 = 2x + 15`, justifying each
   step.
4. (Unit 03 retrieval) Solve the system `3x + y = 14` and
   `x − y = 2` by a method of your choice, checking in both
   equations.
5. (Unit 02 retrieval) A line has equation `y = −2x + 5`. Name
   its slope and its `y`-intercept, and find `y` when `x = 3`.
6. (Project work) For the Polynomial Garden project, choose your
   bed's two binomial side lengths and write the area product
   (unexpanded). Predict the degree of the area polynomial before
   expanding it in Session 15.
7. Explain in two sentences why `3 + √5` cannot be rational.
   (Hint: if it were rational, what would `√5` have to equal?)
8. A length is `√45` feet. Give it exactly and to the nearest
   tenth. Square the rounded value: how far is the result from
   the exact area `45` square feet? What does that gap teach about
   rounding early?
