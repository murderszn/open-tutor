# Project — The Plan Audit

**Unit 01 investigation · Sessions 10–19 (alongside instruction) · Draft due
Session 16 · Final due Session 19**

## The situation (fictional data — do not use any real account information)

The Rivera family is choosing a mobile data plan. Two carriers publish these
fictional rate cards:

- **Plan A (FlatStart):** $30 per month + $4 for each GB of data used.
- **Plan B (BasePlus):** $50 per month + $1.50 for each GB of data used.

The family uses about 12 GB per month, and their monthly budget cap is $75.
You are the analyst: audit both plans with algebra and write a recommendation
memo. All numbers below are invented for this project.

## Your deliverables (one report, 2–4 pages)

1. **Cost expressions.** Write `C_A(x)` and `C_B(x)` for the monthly cost in
   dollars when `x` GB are used. For each expression, name the terms, the
   coefficient of `x`, and the constant term, and explain what each means in
   the plan's pricing.
2. **Equivalent forms.** Rewrite one plan's expression in an equivalent form
   (distribute or factor — your choice) and prove equivalence by substituting
   `x = 10` into both forms.
3. **Break-even analysis.** Write and solve `C_A(x) = C_B(x)`, justifying
   every step. Classify the equation (one solution / infinitely many / none)
   and interpret the result: below, at, and above the break-even usage, which
   plan is cheaper? Verify with one test value on each side.
4. **Budget inequalities.** The cap is $75/month. Write and solve a budget
   inequality for each plan, graph both solution sets on number lines, and
   state the maximum whole GB each plan allows under the cap.
5. **Rearranged formula.** Solve Plan B's cost formula for `x` (GB in terms
   of budget `B`). Use your rearranged formula to find the GB allowance for
   `B = 75` and confirm it matches Part 4.
6. **Recommendation memo** (one page or less, addressed to the Rivera family):
   which plan for ~12 GB/month, and why — citing your break-even point, the
   budget analysis, and the accuracy of your figures. State explicitly what
   your model leaves out (taxes, fees, coverage, throttling — anything the
   rate card does not say).
7. **Extension (optional, replaces one practice set):** A third fictional
   plan charges `$m` base plus `$p` per GB. For what values of `m` and `p`
   would it be *identical* to Plan A for every `x`? For what values would it
   *never* match Plan A's cost? Justify with the identity/contradiction
   language of Lesson 4.

## Materials

Paper or a notebook, calculator, ruler for number lines. Optional: the free
Desmos graphing calculator (no account) to graph `y = C_A(x)` and
`y = C_B(x)` and read the intersection — a graph is welcome but the algebra
must stand on its own.

## Rules and safety

- All plan data is fictional. Do not look up or enter any real carrier plan,
  real prices, or any personal or family account information.
- Work may be individual or in pairs; each learner submits their own memo.
- Show every algebraic step with its justification — unjustified steps earn no
  credit in the rubric below.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Expressions & structure (Parts 1–2) | Parts named correctly in pricing context; equivalent form proven by substitution | Parts named correctly; equivalence shown algebraically | Parts named with errors, or equivalence asserted without proof | Expressions incorrect or missing |
| Solving & justification (Parts 3–5) | Every step justified; classification correct; test values verify conclusions | Steps shown with mostly correct justifications; classification correct | Steps shown but justifications missing or wrong | Solution missing or algebraically wrong |
| Modeling & interpretation (Parts 4, 6) | Budget answers as sensible whole GB with rounding explained; memo cites evidence and names model limits | Whole-number answers given; memo cites the analysis | Fractional GB reported without comment, or memo ignores the analysis | No interpretation in context |
| Communication | Report organized, notation consistent, graphs labeled, memo addressed and concise | Report readable; minor notation or labeling slips | Disorganized; graphs unlabeled | Incomplete or incoherent |

**Scoring:** 14–16 = exceeds; 11–13 = meets; 8–10 = approaching (revise Parts
flagged); below 8 = reteach and resubmit. The extension adds up to 2 bonus
points.

## What readiness looks like / reteach triggers

- **Ready to move on:** break-even solved and interpreted correctly, budget
  inequalities graphed with correct boundary circles, memo references actual
  numbers from the analysis.
- **Reteach:** if the break-even equation is misclassified, return to Lesson
  4's "three endings" with the learner's own numbers; if budget answers are
  fractional GB with no comment, revisit Lesson 6's accuracy discussion.
