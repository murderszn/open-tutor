# Lesson 01 — Reading Expressions: Terms, Factors, and Coefficients

**Goal:** I can name the parts of an algebraic expression — terms, factors,
coefficients — in a real context, evaluate the expression, and treat a chunk
of it as one thing.
**Time:** 50 minutes · **Materials:** notebook, calculator (check only)

## Readiness check (5 min)

Simplify: (a) 3 + 4(2) − 1, (b) 2(5) + 3(5). If you can do both, you are ready:
(a) 3 + 8 − 1 = 10; (b) 10 + 15 = 25.

## Teaching: expressions have parts with names (15 min)

An **expression** is a combination of numbers, variables, and operations. It
does not contain an equals sign. Its parts have names because algebra moves
faster when you can point at a part and say what it does.

- A **term** is a piece added or subtracted. In `12 + 2.5h`, the terms are
  `12` and `2.5h`.
- A **coefficient** is the number multiplying a variable inside a term. In
  `2.5h`, the coefficient of `h` is 2.5.
- A **constant term** is a term with no variable: `12`.
- A **factor** is a piece multiplied. In `4(2x + 5)`, the factors are `4` and
  the whole chunk `(2x + 5)`.

**Why context matters.** A tutoring center charges a $12 signup fee plus
$2.50 for every hour tutored. The cost for `h` hours is `C = 12 + 2.5h`.
The constant term 12 is the fee you pay even for zero hours; the coefficient
2.5 is the price of each hour. If the numbers change meaning, your reading of
the expression must change too.

**Viewing a chunk as one entity.** In `4(2x + 5)`, you do not have to open up
`(2x + 5)` right away. Treat `(2x + 5)` as a single package that gets
quadrupled. This habit — seeing structure before computing — is what lets you
rewrite complicated expressions later without drowning in symbols.

### Worked example 1 — parts in context

> A bike rental costs $8 plus $3 per hour: `C = 8 + 3h`.

1. Terms: `8` and `3h`. Coefficient of `h`: 3. Constant term: 8.
2. Meaning: you pay $8 even for zero hours (the constant term); each hour
   adds $3 (the coefficient).
3. Evaluate for `h = 5`: `C = 8 + 3(5) = 8 + 15 = 23`. Five hours cost $23.

### Worked example 2 — a chunk as one entity

> Perimeter of a rectangle: `P = 2(l + w)`.

1. The expression `2(l + w)` has one visible multiplication: factor `2` times
   the chunk `(l + w)`.
2. View `(l + w)` as a single entity: the semiperimeter — half the walk around
   the rectangle (one length plus one width). The `2` says you walk it twice.
3. Evaluate for `l = 9`, `w = 4`: `P = 2(9 + 4) = 2(13) = 26`.

## Guided practice (10 min, with hints)

1. In `T = 25 + 0.10m` (texting plan: $25 base + $0.10 per message), name the
   terms, the coefficient of `m`, and the constant term. *Hint: which pieces
   are added?*
2. Evaluate `T` for `m = 120`. *Hint: substitute first, then compute.*
3. In `A = 5(3x − 1)`, name the factors. Which chunk can you view as one
   entity?
4. **Error analysis:** A learner says the coefficient in `−7y + 2` is 7.
   Explain the mistake and give the correct coefficient.

## Independent practice (12 min)

5. A plumber charges $45 to show up plus $60 per hour: write the expression
   for `h` hours, then name its parts (terms, coefficient, constant term).
6. Evaluate your plumber expression for `h = 3`. What does each number in your
   arithmetic mean in dollars?
7. In `S = 6(x + 2) − x`, identify the terms. View `(x + 2)` as one entity and
   describe in words what the expression says.
8. For `P = 2(l + w)` with `l = 7.5` and `w = 3`, find `P`. Keep the unit
   (meters) attached to your answer.

## Application (5 min)

A food truck's daily profit is `P = 8s − 120`, where `s` is sandwiches sold.
Name the terms and coefficient, and explain what the constant term `−120`
means for the business. For how many sandwiches is the profit zero? (You may
solve informally today; Lesson 3 makes it systematic.)

## Exit check (3 min)

In `E = 4(2x + 5) − 3x`: (a) name the terms, (b) name the coefficient of `x`
in the second term, (c) identify the chunk you would view as one entity.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading access:** read each expression aloud before analyzing ("twelve
  plus two point five h").
- **Alternate response:** point to or circle parts on a printed expression
  instead of writing names.
- **Extension:** In `V = πr²h` (cylinder volume), which part is the "single
  entity" a physicist sees first? Defend your choice in two sentences.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: learners confuse *term* (added piece) with *factor*
(multiplied piece) — in `4(2x + 5)`, some will call `4` a term. Anchor with
the context reading: a term is a separate charge on the bill; a factor scales
a whole chunk. Answer-key reference: [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-01).
