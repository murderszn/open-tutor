# Lesson 03 — Solving Linear Equations: Justify Every Step

**Goal:** I can solve multi-step linear equations and write a reason for each
step, then check the solution by substitution.
**Time:** 50 minutes · **Materials:** notebook

## Readiness check (5 min)

Solve `x + 7 = 15` and `2x = 18`. State in one sentence why your move was
legal ("I subtracted 7 from both sides to keep the equality true").

## Teaching: equations are balanced claims (15 min)

An equation asserts that two numbers are equal. Every legal move preserves
that equality — that is why the solution at the end still satisfies the
original equation. The equality properties:

- **Addition/subtraction property:** add or subtract the same quantity from
  both sides.
- **Multiplication/division property:** multiply or divide both sides by the
  same nonzero quantity.
- **Distributive property** and **combining like terms** rewrite one side
  without touching the balance.

Your job in this unit: **never write a step without its reason.** A solution
without justifications is an answer without an argument.

### Worked example 1 — multi-step with distribution

> Solve `3(x − 2) + 5 = 2x + 11`, justifying each step.

| Step | Equation | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `3x − 6 + 5 = 2x + 11` | Distributive property: `3(x − 2) = 3x − 6` |
| 2 | `3x − 1 = 2x + 11` | Combine like terms: `−6 + 5 = −1` |
| 3 | `3x = 2x + 12` | Add 1 to both sides (addition property) |
| 4 | `x = 12` | Subtract `2x` from both sides (subtraction property) |

**Check:** Left side `3(12 − 2) + 5 = 3(10) + 5 = 35`. Right side
`2(12) + 11 = 35`. Both sides equal 35 — the solution is verified.

### Worked example 2 — rational coefficients, clear first

> Solve `(2/3)x + 4 = 10 − (1/3)x`.

1. The fractions share denominator 3, so **multiply both sides by 3**
   (multiplication property): `2x + 12 = 30 − x`.
2. **Add `x` to both sides:** `3x + 12 = 30`.
3. **Subtract 12 from both sides:** `3x = 18`.
4. **Divide both sides by 3:** `x = 6`.
5. **Check:** `(2/3)(6) + 4 = 4 + 4 = 8`; `10 − (1/3)(6) = 10 − 2 = 8`. ✓

Clearing denominators first is a strategy, not a rule — you could also work
with fractions throughout. Choose the path with the fewest arithmetic traps.

## Guided practice (10 min, with hints)

1. Solve `5x + 9 = 3x − 7`, justifying each step. *Hint: gather variable terms
   on one side, constants on the other.*
2. Check your solution by substitution.
3. Solve `2(x + 4) = x + 13` with justifications. *Hint: distribute before
   anything else.*

## Independent practice (12 min)

4. Solve with justifications: (a) `7n − 3 = 4n + 15`, (b) `−2(k − 5) = 3k + 1`,
   (c) `(1/2)y + 6 = 14 − (3/2)y`.
5. **Error analysis:** "Tomas solved `4x − 8 = 12` as follows: `4x = 12 − 8`,
   so `4x = 4`, so `x = 1`." Find the illegal step, name what he should have
   done, and solve correctly.
6. Write your own two-step equation whose solution is `x = −3`, then solve a
   partner's (or your own, reversed) with full justifications.

## Application (5 min)

A repair shop charges $40 plus $25 per half hour: `C = 40 + 25h`, where `h`
is half-hours. A bill comes to $165. Write and solve the equation for `h`,
justify each step, and state the answer in hours and minutes.

## Exit check (3 min)

Solve `6(x − 1) = 2x + 10` with one justification per step, and check the
answer.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading access:** keep a justification word bank posted (distributive,
  combine like terms, add/subtract/multiply/divide both sides).
- **Alternate response:** dictate steps while an adult scribes; the learner
  must supply each justification aloud.
- **Extension:** Solve `(ax) + b = c` for `x` in terms of `a, b, c` (with
  `a ≠ 0`), justifying each step — a preview of Lesson 4.

## Teacher note

The most common illegal move: adding to one side only, or "moving" a term
without an inverse operation. Insist the justification name the property —
"move the 3 over" is not a justification. Answer-key reference:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-03).
