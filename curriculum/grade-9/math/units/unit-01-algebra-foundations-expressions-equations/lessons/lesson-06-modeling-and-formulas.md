# Lesson 06 — Modeling with Equations and Rearranging Formulas

**Goal:** I can turn a real situation into a one-variable equation or
inequality, solve it, interpret the answer in context, and rearrange a formula
to highlight the quantity I need.
**Time:** 50 minutes · **Materials:** notebook, calculator (check only)

## Readiness check (5 min)

A taxi charges $3 plus $2 per mile: `C = 3 + 2m`. (a) Write the equation for
a $17 ride. (b) What question does solving it answer? (a) `3 + 2m = 17`;
(b) "How many miles was the ride?")

## Teaching: from words to algebra and back (15 min)

Modeling is a round trip: **situation → equation → solution → interpretation.**
Most errors happen on the first and last legs — choosing the wrong operation,
or reporting a number without saying what it means.

**Four-step modeling habit:**
1. **Name the variable and its unit.** (`h` = tutoring hours, in hours.)
2. **Write the equation/inequality** from the story's structure.
3. **Solve** with justifications (Lessons 3–5).
4. **Interpret** — answer the story's question in a sentence, with units and
   sensible accuracy (money to cents, people as whole numbers — N-Q.3).

**Rearranging formulas** uses the exact same moves as solving; the "answer"
is an expression instead of a number. To highlight a quantity, isolate it:
undo addition/subtraction first or last — order does not matter as long as
every step is legal and you can check it by substitution.

### Worked example 1 — equation from a story

> A tutoring center charges a $12 signup fee plus $2.50 per hour. Maya pays
> $87. How many hours did she book?

1. Variable: `h` = hours booked (hours).
2. Equation: `12 + 2.5h = 87`.
3. Solve: subtract 12 → `2.5h = 75`; divide by 2.5 → `h = 30`.
4. Interpret: Maya booked **30 hours**. Check: `12 + 2.5(30) = 12 + 75 = 87` ✓.

### Worked example 2 — inequality from a story

> A club has $500 for a trip. Buses cost $180 each and each student ticket is
> $12. At most how many students can go if they rent 2 buses?

1. Variable: `s` = number of students (whole people).
2. Inequality: `2(180) + 12s ≤ 500`, i.e. `360 + 12s ≤ 500`.
3. Solve: `12s ≤ 140` → `s ≤ 11.67`.
4. Interpret: **at most 11 students** — 12 would cost `360 + 144 = 504`,
   over budget. Accuracy note: the algebra gives 11.67, but fractional
   students are nonsense; the context forces the whole-number answer 11.

### Worked example 3 — rearrange a formula

> The area of a triangle is `A = (1/2)bh`. Solve for `b` (base), then find
> `b` when `A = 48` cm² and `h = 8` cm.

1. Multiply both sides by 2: `2A = bh` (multiplication property).
2. Divide both sides by `h` (`h ≠ 0` — a height of zero is not a triangle):
   `b = 2A/h`.
3. Substitute: `b = 2(48)/8 = 96/8 = 12` cm.
4. Check in the original: `(1/2)(12)(8) = 48` ✓. Units: cm²/cm = cm ✓
   (N-Q.1 — the units guide the algebra).

## Guided practice (10 min, with hints)

1. A plumber charges $45 plus $60/hour. The bill is $285. How many hours?
   Follow all four modeling steps. *Hint: start by naming the variable.*
2. Solve `C = 2πr` for `r`. *Hint: what is `r` multiplied by? Undo that.*

## Independent practice (12 min)

3. A phone plan costs $20/month plus $0.08 per MB over 5 GB. Last month's bill
   was $34.40. How many MB over the allowance were used? (Four steps; check
   by substitution.)
4. A school budget allows at most $1,200 for field-trip lunches at $9.50 per
   student. What is the greatest whole number of students that can be fed?
   Explain the rounding decision.
5. Rearrange to highlight the indicated quantity: (a) `V = lwh` for `h`;
   (b) `ax + by = c` for `y` (assume `b ≠ 0`); (c) `P = 2l + 2w` for `l`.
6. **Explain-your-reasoning:** A learner rearranged `d = rt` as `r = d − t`.
   Explain the error using the "undo" idea, and give the correct rearrangement.

## Application (5 min)

A runner's target heart-rate zone is "between 120 and 150 beats per minute,
inclusive." Write this as a compound inequality in `r`, then write one
sentence explaining what a reading of `r = 118` means for the runner.

## Exit check (3 min)

Write the equation for: "A $50 gift card pays for $4.50 notebooks; $5.00
remains." Solve it and interpret the answer in a sentence.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading access:** underline the numbers and the question in the story
  before choosing operations.
- **Alternate response:** fill a four-row modeling template (variable / unit /
  equation / interpretation) instead of free prose.
- **Extension:** The Break-Even setup — two plans `C₁ = 20 + 0.5x` and
  `C₂ = 35 + 0.2x`. For what `x` do they cost the same? Which is cheaper for
  large `x`? (Direct preview of the project.)

## Teacher note

Learners will write `12 + 2.5 = 87h`-style reversed equations under time
pressure. The four-step habit plus the substitution check catches it: if the
check fails, the model — not the arithmetic — is usually wrong. Answer-key
reference: [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-06).
