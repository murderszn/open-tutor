# Unit 01 — Algebra Foundations: Expressions and Equations

**Grade 9 Mathematics · Weeks 3–6 · Track issue [#42](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/42)**
Status: **validated draft** — not educator-reviewed or merged.

## Essential question

How can the structure hidden inside an algebraic expression tell you how to rewrite it, solve it, and use it to model something real?

## What this unit does

Grade 8 ended with linear equations in one variable and qualitative function
reading. This unit turns that fluency into algebra: reading expressions the way
a mechanic reads an engine — naming every part, seeing how parts group into
entities, rewriting without changing value, and then solving with a written
justification for every move. The unit closes by building equations and
inequalities from real situations and rearranging formulas, so algebra is a
modeling tool from the start, not a symbol game.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner can:

1. Identify terms, factors, coefficients, and constant terms in expressions in
   context, and interpret a complicated expression by viewing one or more of
   its parts as a single entity. (A-SSE.1.a, A-SSE.1.b)
2. Rewrite expressions into equivalent forms using the distributive property,
   combining like terms, and factoring out common structure, and verify
   equivalence by substitution. (A-SSE.2)
3. Solve multi-step linear equations and inequalities in one variable —
   including with rational-number and letter coefficients — writing a
   justification for each step, and classify each equation as one solution,
   infinitely many, or no solution. (A-REI.1, A-REI.3)
4. Create equations and inequalities in one variable from real situations, use
   them to answer the question, and interpret the solution (or solution set)
   back in context. (A-CED.1)
5. Rearrange formulas to highlight a quantity of interest, choose units that fit
   the quantities, and report answers with accuracy appropriate to the context.
   (A-CED.4, N-Q.1, N-Q.3)

## Prerequisites

- All four operations with integers, fractions, and decimals; order of
  operations; number-line reasoning.
- Multi-step linear-equation solving with the distributive property; the
  one / infinitely-many / no-solution classification (revisited in Lesson 4).
- Exponent rules and scientific notation (used lightly in equivalence checks).

## Standards and source notes

- **A-SSE.1.a** — interpret parts of an expression, such as terms, factors, and
  coefficients, in context. **A-SSE.1.b** — interpret complicated expressions
  by viewing one or more of their parts as a single entity.
- **A-SSE.2** — use the structure of an expression to identify ways to rewrite
  it (for example, recognizing factors or the distributive pattern).
- **A-CED.1** — create equations and inequalities in one variable and use them
  to solve problems. **A-CED.4** — rearrange formulas to highlight a quantity
  of interest, using the same reasoning as solving equations.
- **A-REI.1** — explain each step in solving a simple equation as following
  from the equality of numbers asserted at the previous step; construct a
  viable argument to justify a solution method. **A-REI.3** — solve linear
  equations and inequalities in one variable, including equations with
  coefficients represented by letters.
- **N-Q.1** — use units as a way to understand problems and to guide the
  solution of multi-step problems. **N-Q.3** — choose a level of accuracy
  appropriate to limitations on measurement when reporting quantities.
- Codes and descriptions follow the Common Core State Standards for
  Mathematics, High School — Algebra and Number & Quantity (corestandards.org,
  as cross-walked in this track's
  [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md), verified 2026-10-06).
  No claim of state adoption or accreditation is made.

## Accessibility and safety notes

- All activities are paper- or screen-based; no hazards. The project uses
  fictional pricing data — learners must not enter personal or family account
  information.
- The tile image and the SVG both ship with text-only alternatives so every
  question is answerable without seeing the image.
- Number-line work can be done with raised-line or tactile materials; equation
  solving accepts dictated or scribed steps with the learner supplying each
  justification aloud.
