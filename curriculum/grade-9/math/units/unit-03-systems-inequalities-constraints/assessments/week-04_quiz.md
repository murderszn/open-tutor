# Formative Quiz — Unit 03 (Session 16)

Answer each item. Show the check in both equations (or in every
inequality) wherever a pair is claimed as a solution. This quiz is a
practice check, not a standardized or validated assessment.

1. Is `(3, 4)` a solution of the system `x + 2y = 11` and `3x − y = 5`?
   Show both checks.
2. Solve the system `y = 2x + 1` and `3x + y = 11` by substitution.
3. Solve the system `2x + y = 9` and `x − y = 3` by elimination.
4. Classify the system `y = 3x − 1` and `3x − y = 4`: one solution,
   infinitely many, or no solution? Give a reason.
5. Classify the system `2x − 2y = 4` and `x − y = 2`: one solution,
   infinitely many, or no solution? Give a reason.
6. A theater sells adult tickets for $12 and child tickets for $8. It
   sells 150 tickets for $1,560. How many adult and child tickets?
   (Fictional practice data.) Show the system and both checks.
7. For the inequality `y ≤ −x + 3`: is the boundary solid or dashed?
   Give a test point, its result, and state which side is shaded.
8. Test `(3, 3)` and `(5, 6)` in the system `x ≥ 0`, `y ≥ 0`,
   `x + y ≤ 10`, `2x + y ≤ 14`. Which are feasible?
9. A pair `(4.5, 2)` satisfies every inequality of a system about
   numbers of tables and chairs. Is it viable? Explain in one
   sentence.
10. In one or two sentences, explain why replacing an equation by the
    sum of it and a multiple of the other equation keeps the same
    solutions.

**Scoring guide (for the adult):** 1 point per item (items 6 and 8
earn their point only with both checks shown). 9–10: ready for the
assessment; 7–8: review the missed objective in Session 17; below 7:
reteach with the teacher guide before the assessment.
Solutions are in `answer-key.md`.
