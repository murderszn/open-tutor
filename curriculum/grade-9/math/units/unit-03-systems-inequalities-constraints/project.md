# Project — The School Fair Stall

**Sessions:** launch (Session 10), work session (Session 14), work
session (Session 15). **Materials:** notebook, ruler, colored pencils
(optional), calculator (check only).

## The situation (fictional practice data)

Your class runs a stall at the school fair. You sell two products:

- **Snack bags** (`x`): each sells for $6, costs $2 to make, and takes
  5 minutes of prep time. Each snack bag uses 1 unit of cooler space.
- **Drink cups** (`y`): each sells for $4, costs $2 to make, and takes
  2 minutes of prep time. Each drink cup uses 2 units of cooler space.

Your limits:

- **Prep time:** at most 120 minutes in total.
- **Cooler space:** at most 70 units in total.
- You cannot sell a negative number of either product, and you can
  only sell whole snack bags and whole drink cups.

Profit per snack bag is `$6 − $2 = $3`; profit per drink cup is
`$4 − $2 = $2`. Total profit is `P = 3x + 2y` dollars.

## Deliverables

1. **The system.** Write the prep-time inequality, the cooler-space
   inequality, and the nonnegativity constraints, with `x` and `y`
   defined with units. Explain in one sentence what each inequality
   says in plain words.
2. **The graph.** On labeled, scaled axes, graph the feasible region.
   Mark and label every corner point, and show how you found the
   corner where the two slanted boundaries meet (solve the system of
   the two boundary equations).
3. **The combo table.** List at least 6 feasible whole-number
   combinations, at least 1 combination that is feasible but *not*
   the most profitable, and at least 1 combination that is *not*
   feasible, with the failed constraint named for the non-feasible one.
   Compute the profit of every combination you list.
4. **The recommendation.** State the most profitable whole-number
   combination, its profit, and why you are confident no other
   whole-number combination beats it. (Hint: compare the corner points
   and the whole-number combinations nearest the best corner.)
5. **The "what if" paragraph.** In three or four sentences: if prep
   time rose to 150 minutes, would your recommendation change? Show
   the new corner calculation or explain why the old recommendation
   still wins.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Full marks | Partial marks | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| System written correctly (Deliverable 1) | Both inequalities plus `x ≥ 0`, `y ≥ 0`, variables defined with units, plain-words meanings correct | One inequality or definition missing or wrong | System missing or mostly wrong |
| Graph and corners (Deliverable 2) | Axes labeled and scaled, region correct, all corners labeled, slanted-corner calculation shown | Region mostly correct but a corner unlabeled or miscalculated | Region wrong or not graphed |
| Combo table (Deliverable 3) | ≥6 feasible whole-number combos, ≥1 non-feasible combo with failed constraint named, profits correct | Table present but short, or a profit wrong, or non-feasible combo unexplained | No usable table |
| Recommendation with justification (Deliverable 4) | Best whole-number combo correct with profit, and a corner/neighbor comparison justifying it | Best combo correct but justification missing or hand-waved | Best combo wrong |
| "What if" reasoning (Deliverable 5) | New corner computed or a correct argument that the recommendation stands, in the situation's terms | A guess with no calculation or argument | Missing |

Scoring: each criterion is worth 4 points (Full = 4, Partial = 2,
Not yet = 0); 16–20 = thorough, 10–14 = solid with gaps, below 10 =
revise with the teacher guide's reteach steps before the assessment.
