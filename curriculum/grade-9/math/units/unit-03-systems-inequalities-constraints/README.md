# Unit 03 — Systems: Inequalities and Constraints

**Grade 9 Mathematics · Weeks 11–14 · Track issue [#42](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/42)**
Status: **validated draft** — not educator-reviewed or merged.

## Essential question

When two rules have to hold at the same time, what pairs satisfy both —
and which pairs are ruled out by the limits of the situation?

## What this unit does

Units 01–02 built one-variable equations and single linear functions.
This unit puts two of them together. The learner first learns what it
means for one ordered pair to satisfy two equations at once, then earns
three ways to find that pair — graphing, substitution, and elimination —
and learns why elimination is legal: replacing one equation by the sum of
it and a multiple of the other leaves the solution set unchanged. The
unit then turns to modeling: systems built from ticket sales, pricing
plans, and mixtures, where the intersection answers a real question
("when do the plans cost the same?"). Finally it moves from equations to
inequalities: one inequality shades a half-plane, a system of
inequalities carves out a feasible region, and the situation itself —
whole items only, no negative quantities, a hard budget — decides which
points inside that region are actually viable. The project is a school
fair stall: the learner designs two products under prep-time and storage
limits and picks the most profitable feasible combination.

All data in this unit's contexts is fictional practice data, invented for
the exercises. No prices, plans, or stall figures here describe a real
business.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner can:

1. Solve a system of two linear equations exactly by graphing,
   substitution, or elimination; classify the system as having one
   solution, infinitely many solutions, or no solution; and verify a
   claimed solution by substitution into **both** original equations.
   (A-REI.6, A-REI.10, A-REI.11)
2. Justify elimination: show that replacing one equation by the sum of
   it and a multiple of the other produces a system with the same
   solutions, and choose the most efficient solution method for a given
   system with a reason. (A-REI.5)
3. Create a system of two equations in two variables from a context,
   graph it on labeled, scaled axes, and interpret the intersection (or
   its absence) in the context's own terms. (A-CED.2, A-REI.10, A-REI.11)
4. Represent constraints with systems of equations or inequalities and
   interpret solutions as viable or nonviable in context — including
   whole-number, nonnegativity, and budget limits. (A-CED.3, N-Q.2)
5. Graph a linear inequality in two variables as a half-plane (solid or
   dashed boundary, shading chosen by a test point) and a system of
   linear inequalities as the intersection of half-planes; name the
   feasible region and test points inside and outside it. (A-REI.12)

## Prerequisites

- Unit 01 skills: multi-step linear-equation solving with a written
  justification for each step; the one / infinitely-many / no-solution
  classification for one-variable equations; rearranging formulas.
- Unit 02 skills: graphing `y = mx + b` by hand on labeled, scaled axes;
  reading intercepts; function notation is **not** required here —
  systems are written as equations, and intersections are found by
  solving, not by reading `f(x) = g(x)` off a graph alone.
- Grade-8 fluency (diagnostic weeks): slope from a graph or two points;
  rational-number arithmetic with negatives and fractions.
- **New here:** systems of two equations; elimination and its
  justification; inequalities in two variables; feasible regions;
  viable vs. nonviable solutions.

## Standards and source notes

- **A-REI.5** — proving that, given a system of two equations in two
  variables, replacing one equation by the sum of that equation and a
  multiple of the other produces a system with the same solutions.
- **A-REI.6** — solve systems of linear equations exactly and
  approximately (e.g., with graphs), focusing on pairs of linear
  equations in two variables.
- **A-REI.10** — understand that the graph of an equation in two
  variables is the set of all its solutions plotted in the coordinate
  plane, often forming a curve (which could be a line).
- **A-REI.11** — explain why the x-coordinates of the points where the
  graphs of the equations `y = f(x)` and `y = g(x)` intersect are the
  solutions of the equation `f(x) = g(x)`; find the solutions
  approximately (used here in equation form, as in Unit 02).
- **A-REI.12** — graph the solutions to a linear inequality in two
  variables as a half-plane (excluding the boundary in the case of a
  strict inequality), and graph the solution set to a system of linear
  inequalities in two variables as the intersection of the
  corresponding half-planes.
- **A-CED.2** — create equations in two or more variables to represent
  relationships between quantities; graph equations on coordinate axes
  with labels and scales.
- **A-CED.3** — represent constraints by equations or inequalities, and
  by systems of equations and/or inequalities, and interpret solutions
  as viable or nonviable options in a modeling context.
- **N-Q.2** — define appropriate quantities for the purpose of
  descriptive modeling.
- Codes and descriptions follow the Common Core State Standards for
  Mathematics, High School — Algebra (corestandards.org, as cross-walked
  in this track's
  [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md), verified 2026-10-06).
  No claim of state adoption or accreditation is made.

## Session pacing — 20 sessions (four weeks × five 50-minute sessions)

| Session | Work | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lesson 01 — two equations, one pair (crossing-paths image) | Learner tests pairs in both equations and finds an intersection by solving |
| 2 | Practice A — testing pairs, graphing systems | 8/10 practice items correct; every claimed solution checked in both equations |
| 3 | Lesson 02 — substitution | Learner solves by substitution and checks the pair in both original equations |
| 4 | Practice B — substitution, including a sign-error analysis | Substitution steps shown; the flawed solution found and corrected |
| 5 | Lesson 03 — elimination, equivalent systems, classification | Learner justifies one elimination step and classifies three systems correctly |
| 6 | Practice C — elimination and one / infinite / none | Method chosen with a reason; classification justified, not guessed |
| 7 | Reading / reference — Resource Pack refs 1–2; retrieval: Unit 01 justifications, Unit 02 graphs | Learner restates A-REI.5 in their own words and re-solves one Unit 02 graph problem |
| 8 | Lesson 04 — modeling systems, break-even | Learner builds a system from a context and interprets the intersection in words |
| 9 | Practice D — ticket, plan, and mixture models | Systems defined with units; answers interpreted back in context, not left as bare pairs |
| 10 | Project launch — The School Fair Stall | Learner defines the two products, quantities, units, and constraints |
| 11 | Lesson 05 — inequalities in two variables | Learner graphs a half-plane with correct boundary style and shading, justified by a test point |
| 12 | Practice E — half-planes and boundary decisions | Boundary solid/dashed correct in every item; shading justified by a test point |
| 13 | Lesson 06 — systems of inequalities, feasible regions (SVG) | Learner names the feasible region's corners and tests points inside and outside it |
| 14 | Practice F — feasible regions, profit at corners, project work | Region graphed with labeled corners; at least 6 feasible combos listed |
| 15 | Project work session — graph, combos, recommendation | Draft graph, combo table, and profit comparison complete |
| 16 | Formative quiz (`assessments/week-04_quiz.md`) | Gaps named by objective; reteach list written before the assessment |
| 17 | Review and reteach — highest-need objective from the quiz | Learner re-solves two missed-type items correctly with justifications |
| 18 | Culminating assessment (`assessments/unit-assessment.md`) | All five objectives sampled; extended tasks show reasoning, not just answers |
| 19 | Feedback and revision — assessment corrections | Every missed item corrected with a written reason for the original error |
| 20 | Flex / extension — write-your-own constraint problem; portfolio assembly | Learner writes a solvable system with a viable/nonviable twist and a key |
