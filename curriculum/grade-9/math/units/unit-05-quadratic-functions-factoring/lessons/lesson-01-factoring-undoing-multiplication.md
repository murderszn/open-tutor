# Lesson 01 — Factoring Is Undoing Multiplication

**Goal:** I can factor a greatest common factor out of an expression,
and reverse a multiplication to factor a quadratic into two binomials.
**Time:** 50 minutes · **Materials:** notebook, Unit 04 area-model notes.

## Readiness check (5 min)

Multiply out `(x + 2)(x + 3)` and `3x(2x + 5)`. If you got
`x² + 5x + 6` and `6x² + 15x`, you are ready: factoring is the same work
run backwards. Today you start with the answer and find the factors.

## Teaching: factoring undoes a product (15 min)

In Unit 04 you multiplied polynomials. **Factoring** is the reverse:
given a polynomial, write it as a product of simpler polynomials. The
first move is always the same — look for a **greatest common factor
(GCF)**, the largest expression that divides every term. Factoring out
the GCF is like dividing every term by the same thing and keeping the
leftover in parentheses.

A factorization is never finished until you check it by multiplying
back. If the product is not the original expression, the factorization
is wrong — no matter how tidy it looks.

There is a second idea in this lesson. A quadratic such as `x² + 5x + 6`
came from multiplying two binomials: `(x + 2)(x + 3)`. To factor it, you
undo that multiplication. The area model from Unit 04 helps: the
quadratic is the area of a rectangle, and the factors are its side
lengths. The constant term `6` is the product of the two numbers at the
ends of the binomials, and the middle coefficient `5` is their sum.
Two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to 5 are 2 and 3 — so the sides
are `x + 2` and `x + 3`.

**Factor completely** means: keep going until no factor can be factored
further. `12x² + 24x = 4x(3x + 6)` is *not* complete, because `3x + 6`
still has a factor of 3 hiding inside it. The complete factorization is
`12x(x + 2)`.

### Worked example 1 — factor out the GCF

> Factor `6x² + 9x` completely.

1. List what divides both terms: both have a factor of 3, and both have
   a factor of `x`. The GCF is `3x`.
2. Divide each term by `3x`: `6x² ÷ 3x = 2x` and `9x ÷ 3x = 3`.
3. Write the product: `6x² + 9x = 3x(2x + 3)`.
4. **Check by multiplying:** `3x · 2x = 6x²` and `3x · 3 = 9x`. ✓

### Worked example 2 — reverse a binomial product

> Factor `x² + 5x + 6`.

1. The product must be two binomials `(x + m)(x + n)`, because the
   `x²` term comes from `x · x`.
2. The numbers `m` and `n` must multiply to the constant term, 6, and
   add to the middle coefficient, 5.
3. Factor pairs of 6: 1 and 6 (sum 7), 2 and 3 (sum 5). The pair 2 and
   3 fits, so `x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)`.
4. **Check by multiplying:** `(x + 2)(x + 3) = x² + 3x + 2x + 6
   = x² + 5x + 6`. ✓

## Guided practice (10 min, with hints)

1. Factor `8x² − 12x`. *Hint: the GCF of 8 and 12 is 4; both terms have an `x`.*
2. Factor `x² + 8x + 15`. *Hint: two numbers that multiply to 15 and add to 8.*
3. Factor `5x² + 10x`. *Hint: factor the GCF first, then ask whether anything inside the parentheses still factors.*

## Independent practice (10 min)

1. Factor `4x² + 16x`.
2. Factor `x² + 9x + 18`.
3. Factor `2x³ + 6x²`.
4. Factor `x² + 6x + 8`.
5. `12x² + 24x` was factored as `4x(3x + 6)` by one learner. Explain in
   one or two sentences why that answer is incomplete, and give the
   complete factorization.

## Application (5 min)

A rectangle has area `x² + 7x + 10` square units. Its length is
`x + 5` units. What is its width? Explain how factoring answers a
geometry question.

## Exit check (5 min)

Factor `10x² − 15x` completely, and show the multiplication that checks
your answer.

## Supports, extension, teacher note

- **Reading/language access:** read "factor completely" aloud as
  "keep factoring until nothing inside can be factored any more";
  keep the area-model rectangle on the board all lesson.
- **Alternate response mode:** a learner may state the GCF aloud and
  point to each term it divides before writing anything.
- **Extension:** factor `3x³ − 12x² + 9x` completely (answer:
  `3x(x − 1)(x − 3)`), and explain why the GCF must come out before
  the trinomial is touched.
- **Teacher note — misconception:** learners often stop after one
  factoring step (see Independent practice 5). Insist on the
  multiply-back check and the question "can anything inside still be
  factored?" until both are habits. **Answer key:** `answer-key.md`,
  Lesson 01.
