# Project — The Greatest Garden

**Sessions 10, 14, 15 · Investigation with a written recommendation**

## The situation

A gardener has **48 feet of fencing** and one long existing wall. The
fence will form the other three sides of a rectangular garden bed: two
short sides running out from the wall, and one long side parallel to
the wall. The gardener wants the **greatest possible area**. All
numbers in this project are fictional practice data.

## Your job

Find the rectangle that gives the greatest area, prove it is the
greatest, and write a recommendation the gardener can follow. Work
with a guiding adult; use graph paper or Desmos (free, no account) to
check your graph.

## Steps

1. **Define the variables.** Let `x` be the length in feet of each
   short side (the sides perpendicular to the wall). Write the length
   of the long side in terms of `x`, and explain why the domain of
   your model is `0 < x < 24`.
2. **Build the area function.** Write `A(x)`, the area in square feet,
   as a quadratic in standard form. Show how it comes from
   length × width.
3. **Make a table.** Compute the area for widths `x = 4, 8, 12, 16,
   20` feet. Record each rectangle's dimensions and area. What pattern
   do you see in the areas?
4. **Graph it.** Plot area against width (width on the horizontal
   axis, area on the vertical axis) for `0 ≤ x ≤ 24`. Mark the zeros,
   the y-intercept if it is in range, and the vertex. Label both axes
   with quantities and units.
5. **Complete the square.** Write `A(x)` in vertex form. Read off the
   width that gives the greatest area, the garden's dimensions at that
   width, and the greatest area.
6. **Check the factored form.** Factor `A(x)` and read off its zeros.
   Explain in one or two sentences what each zero means for the
   garden.
7. **Write the recommendation.** One short paragraph: the dimensions
   to build, the area they give, and why no other rectangle with this
   fencing can beat it. Name the vertex as your evidence.
8. **Extension (optional).** Suppose the gardener buys 60 feet of
   fencing instead. Write the new area function, find the new greatest
   area and dimensions, and explain in one sentence how the answer
   changed and why.

## Deliverables

- A definitions-and-model page (Steps 1–2).
- The table and graph (Steps 3–4), with labeled axes and units.
- The vertex-form work and factored-form check (Steps 5–6).
- The written recommendation (Step 7) and, if attempted, the extension
  (Step 8).

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (3) | Developing (2) | Starting (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model | Variables and units defined; `A(x) = −2x² + 48x` derived correctly from length × width; domain `0 < x < 24` justified | Area function correct but units or domain missing or unexplained | Area function incorrect or missing |
| Table and graph | All five areas correct; graph plotted and labeled with quantities, units, zeros, and vertex | Table or graph mostly correct; a label, point, or the vertex missing | Table or graph largely incorrect or unlabeled |
| Vertex form and factoring | Vertex form correct; greatest area 288 sq ft at width 12 ft (12 ft × 24 ft) read off correctly; factored form and zeros explained | Vertex form or factored form attempted with an arithmetic slip; conclusion still recognizable | Vertex form or factored form missing or wrong |
| Recommendation | Dimensions, area, and the vertex-as-evidence argument all present in clear sentences | Recommendation present but missing the evidence or the dimensions | No recommendation, or a recommendation that contradicts the work |
| Communication | Each step justified; checks shown (expand-back or multiply-back); work is readable without the author present | Most steps justified; some checks missing | Answers only, no reasoning or checks |

**Scoring:** 12–15 points total. 13+ is ready to move on; 10–12 means
revisit the vertex-form step with the teacher guide; below 10 means
re-teach completing the square (Lesson 04) before Unit 06.

## Safety and notes

- This is a paper-and-graph investigation. If the learner builds a
  real garden bed afterwards, an adult supervises measuring, cutting,
  and any tools.
- All fencing and area figures are fictional practice data for the
  investigation; real fencing comes in fixed lengths and real gardens
  have soil, paths, and gates this model ignores.
