# Unit 05 — Quadratic Functions and Factoring

**Grade 9 Mathematics · Weeks 19–22 · Track issue [#42](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/42)**
Status: **validated draft** — not educator-reviewed or merged.

## Essential question

A ball flies in an arc and a garden's area depends on its shape — how
does one curved shape, the parabola, describe both, and how does
rewriting the same quadratic in a different form answer a different
question about it?

## What this unit does

Unit 04 taught the learner to multiply polynomials and to read exact
forms. This unit turns multiplication around: factoring is undoing a
product, and the factored form of a quadratic is the form that reveals
where its graph crosses the x-axis. The learner first factors with a
greatest common factor and an area model, then learns the two-number
trick for `x² + bx + c`, the ac method for `ax² + bx + c`, and the two
special patterns — difference of squares and perfect-square trinomials —
always checking by multiplying back. Factored form then becomes a
graphing tool: the zeros of a polynomial come straight from its factors,
and the learner sketches parabolas from their zeros, vertex, and
intercepts. Completing the square produces the third form, vertex form,
which names the highest or lowest point at a glance. The unit then
studies the parabola itself: how `f(x) + k`, `k·f(x)`, `f(kx)`, and
`f(x + k)` move, stretch, and flip it, and how to read a quadratic's key
features — intercepts, vertex, symmetry, increasing and decreasing
intervals, end behavior, domain — in context. The investigation is a
garden: with a fixed length of fencing, which rectangle gives the
greatest area, and why does the answer sit at the vertex? Solving
quadratic *equations* — by factoring, completing the square, and the
quadratic formula — is Unit 06; this unit stays with functions,
factoring, forms, and graphs.

All data in this unit's contexts is fictional practice data, invented
for the exercises. No prices, measurements, or garden figures here
describe a real business or property.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner can:

1. Factor quadratic expressions completely — greatest common factor,
   `x² + bx + c`, `ax² + bx + c` by the ac method, difference of
   squares, and perfect-square trinomials — identify prime quadratics
   over the integers, and verify every factorization by multiplying
   back. (A-SSE.2, A-SSE.3.a)
2. Use factored form to find the zeros of a quadratic function and
   sketch its graph from its zeros, y-intercept, vertex, and axis of
   symmetry. (A-APR.3, A-SSE.3.a)
3. Complete the square to write a quadratic in vertex form
   `f(x) = a(x − h)² + k`, and read off the vertex, the axis of
   symmetry, and the maximum or minimum value. (A-SSE.3.b, F-IF.8.a)
4. Graph quadratic functions and describe the effects of the
   transformations `f(x) + k`, `k·f(x)`, `f(kx)`, and `f(x + k)` on the
   graph of `f(x) = x²`; given a graph, find the value of `k` that
   produces it. (F-BF.3, F-IF.7)
5. Interpret key features of a quadratic graph or table in context —
   intercepts, vertex, increasing/decreasing intervals, end behavior,
   and domain — and compare two quadratic functions given in different
   representations, choosing the equivalent form that answers the
   question asked. (F-IF.4, F-IF.5, F-IF.8, F-IF.9, A-REI.11)

## Prerequisites

- Unit 01 skills: expression structure (terms, factors, coefficients);
  solving linear equations with a written justification for each step.
- Unit 02 skills: function notation `f(x)`; domain and range; reading
  intercepts and intervals from graphs and tables; average rate of
  change.
- Unit 04 skills: polynomial multiplication (including the area model
  and special products); rational exponents are **not** needed here.
- Grade-8 fluency (diagnostic weeks): integer and fraction arithmetic;
  square roots of perfect squares; `x² = p` has two solutions.
- **New here:** factoring quadratics; zeros from factored form;
  completing the square and vertex form; parabola transformations;
  key-feature analysis in context.

## Standards and source notes

- **A-SSE.1.b** — interpret complicated expressions by viewing one or
  more of their parts as a single entity (used when a factored piece
  such as `(x − 3)` is read as one factor).
- **A-SSE.2** — use the structure of an expression to identify ways to
  rewrite it (for example, recognizing a difference of squares in
  order to factor it).
- **A-SSE.3.a** — factor a quadratic expression to reveal the zeros of
  the function it defines.
- **A-SSE.3.b** — complete the square in a quadratic expression to
  reveal the maximum or minimum value of the function it defines.
- **A-APR.3** — identify zeros of polynomials when suitable
  factorizations are available, and use the zeros to construct a rough
  graph of the function defined by the polynomial.
- **F-IF.4** — for a function that models a relationship between two
  quantities, interpret key features of graphs and tables in terms of
  the quantities, and sketch graphs showing key features given a verbal
  description of the relationship.
- **F-IF.5** — relate the domain of a function to its graph and, where
  applicable, to the quantitative relationship it describes.
- **F-IF.7** — graph functions expressed symbolically and show key
  features of the graph, by hand in simple cases and using technology
  for more complicated cases.
- **F-IF.8** — write a function defined by an expression in different
  but equivalent forms to reveal and explain different properties of
  the function.
- **F-IF.8.a** — use the process of factoring and completing the square
  in a quadratic function to show zeros, extreme values, and symmetry
  of the graph, and interpret these in terms of a context.
- **F-IF.9** — compare properties of two functions each represented in
  a different way (algebraically, graphically, numerically in tables,
  or by verbal descriptions).
- **F-BF.3** — identify the effect on the graph of replacing `f(x)`
  by `f(x) + k`, `k·f(x)`, `f(kx)`, and `f(x + k)` for specific values
  of `k` (both positive and negative); find the value of `k` given the
  graphs.
- **A-REI.11** — explain why the x-coordinates of the points where the
  graphs of the equations `y = f(x)` and `y = g(x)` intersect are the
  solutions of the equation `f(x) = g(x)` (used in Lesson 06 for
  comparing two quadratics graphically).

## Session pacing — 20 sessions (four weeks × five 50-minute sessions)

| Session | Work | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lesson 01 — factoring as undoing multiplication (GCF, area model) | Learner factors out a GCF and reverses one area-model product, checking by multiplying |
| 2 | Practice A — GCF and reversing products | 8/10 practice items correct; every factorization checked by multiplying back |
| 3 | Lesson 02 — factoring trinomials (`x² + bx + c`, ac method) | Learner factors two trinomials of each type and names one prime trinomial with a reason |
| 4 | Practice B — trinomials, sign analysis, prime check | Ac-method steps shown; sign of each factor justified from the signs of `b` and `c` |
| 5 | Lesson 03 — special forms and zeros from factored form | Learner factors a difference of squares and a perfect-square trinomial, then reads zeros off a factored form and sketches the parabola's crossings |
| 6 | Practice C — special forms, factor completely, zeros | "Factor completely" items fully factored (GCF first, then pattern); zeros stated with the graph sketched |
| 7 | Reading / reference — Resource Pack refs 1–3; retrieval: Unit 04 area models and special products | Learner restates the two special patterns from memory and re-multiplies one Unit 04 product |
| 8 | Lesson 04 — completing the square, vertex form | Learner writes two quadratics in vertex form and names vertex, axis, and max/min for each |
| 9 | Practice D — completing the square with `a ≠ 1` | Vertex form verified by expanding back; max/min stated with the `x` where it occurs |
