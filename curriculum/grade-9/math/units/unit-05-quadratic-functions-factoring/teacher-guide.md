# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Quadratic Functions and Factoring

**For the guiding adult.** This unit is a validated draft, not
educator-reviewed. Adapt pacing to the learner; nothing here is timed.

## How the unit flows

Factoring (Lessons 01–03) is the engine: every later lesson cashes in
a factoring skill — zeros from factored form in Lesson 03, vertex form
by completing the square in Lesson 04, and feature analysis in
Lesson 06. If factoring is shaky after Lesson 03, slow down there;
Lessons 04–06 will not rescue it. The project (Sessions 10, 14, 15)
is the unit's second engine: the garden's area function is the same
quadratic in three forms, and the recommendation is the learner's
first argument from a vertex.

## Common misconceptions and responses

- **"Factoring is done after one step."** The learner stops at
  `4x(3x + 6)` or at the GCF without checking the pattern inside.
  Response: require the multiply-back check and the question "can
  anything inside still be factored?" on every item until it is
  automatic (Lessons 01, 03; Practice A6, C3, C5, C10).
- **"The pair that multiplies to `c` is enough."** The learner picks
  factors that multiply to `c` but forget the sum must be `b`.
  Response: make them write "multiplies to ___, adds to ___" before
  choosing (Lesson 02; Practice B9).
- **"Difference of squares and perfect-square trinomials are the
  same thing."** The learner writes `(x − 4)²` for `x² − 16`, or tries
  to factor `x² + 16`. Response: the multiply-back check exposes both
  immediately; keep the two pattern boxes side by side (Lesson 03).
- **"Completing the square just means adding a number."** The learner
  adds `(b/2)²` and forgets to subtract it, or forgets the factor `a`
  when `a ≠ 1`. Response: the expand-back check is the whole lesson
  (Lesson 04; Practice D6).
- **"`(x − 2)²` shifts left."** The most durable error in the unit.
  Response: anchor on the vertex — `(x − 2)` is zero at `x = 2`, so
  the vertex moved right (Lesson 05; Practice E10).
- **"The vertex's x-coordinate is the maximum."** The learner answers
  "0.75 feet" for a maximum height. Response: always ask for both
  coordinates and what each one means (Lesson 06; Practice F6).
- **"The formula's domain is the answer."** The learner says the
  ball's domain is all real numbers. Response: ask when the ball is
  actually in the air (Lesson 06; Practice F5).

## Reteach paths

- **Factoring (Objective 1) weak after Practice B:** return to
  Lesson 02 with a factor-pair table for 12, 18, 24, 30, 36 on the
  board; do five `x² + bx + c` items orally before writing any ac
  method. Use Resource Pack Ref 2 for a second explanation.
- **Vertex form (Objective 3) weak after Practice D:** use algebra
  tiles or a drawn square to show why `(b/2)²` completes the square;
  do three `a = 1` items before any `a ≠ 1` item. Use Ref 4.
- **Transformations (Objective 4) weak after Practice E:** sketch
  `f(x) = x²` and one transformation at a time on graph paper; point
  to the vertex's movement before writing symbols. Use Ref 5 and
  Desmos to check each sketch.
- **Feature analysis (Objective 5) weak after Practice F:** work the
  ball table row by row with the image from Lesson 05 open; ask for
  the meaning of each feature before its number.

## Scoring and readiness

- **Quiz (Session 16):** 18–20 ready for the assessment; 14–17
  reteach the lowest objective in Session 17; below 14 reteach
  Lessons 01–02 and 04 before the assessment.
- **Assessment (Session 18):** 25–28 ready for Unit 06; 20–24 close
  gaps in Session 19; below 20 reteach the weakest objective before
  Unit 06. Solving quadratic *equations* is Unit 06 — do not
  introduce the quadratic formula here.
- **Project:** 13+ of 15 ready to move on; 10–12 revisit the
  vertex-form step; below 10 reteach Lesson 04 before Unit 06.

## What "ready to move on" looks like

The learner factors a mixed quadratic completely without prompting,
checks by multiplying; writes any `a = 1` quadratic in vertex form and
names its vertex and max/min; describes a transformation in words and
symbols; and reads vertex, zeros, intercepts, and domain off a
quadratic graph or table in context, saying what each feature means.
Speed is not a criterion — no timed work anywhere in this unit.

## Accessibility and supports

- Every graph ships with a text-only alternative (Lesson 05 image and
  SVG; the ball table in Lesson 06).
- Multiple response modes: written, oral, typed, or drawn; the adult
  scribes when writing stamina lags.
- High-contrast, large-format coordinate grids; graph features
  described in words as well as drawn; color is never the only cue.
- Desmos and GeoGebra (free, no-account browser versions) offer
  keyboard navigation for checking graphs; the adult previews for
  advertising and age suitability.
- Sessions are about 50 minutes with movement breaks; the project has
  a seated-table and a standing/digital variant.
