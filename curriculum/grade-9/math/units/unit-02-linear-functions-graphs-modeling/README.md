# Unit 02 — Linear Functions: Graphs and Modeling

**Grade 9 Mathematics · Weeks 7–10 · Track issue [#42](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/42)**
Status: **validated draft** — not educator-reviewed or merged.

## Essential question

How can one rule — a constant rate of change — describe straight-line
relationships wherever they appear, from prices and plans to falling water
and road trips?

## What this unit does

Unit 01 turned expressions into algebra: parts with names, solving with
justification, equations built from real situations. This unit turns algebra
into functions. The learner meets the function concept — exactly one output
for every input — writes functions in f(x) notation, graphs linear functions
by hand on labeled, scaled axes, computes and interprets average rate of
change as slope with units, and then does the real work: building linear
models from descriptions, pairs of points, tables, and graphs, and
interpreting slope and intercept in context. The project is a ride-share fare
audit — two fictional pricing plans modeled, graphed, and compared. Unit 03
builds directly on this unit's graphing and intersection work to solve
systems.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner can:

1. Decide whether a relation is a function (exactly one output per input),
   use function notation `f(x)`, evaluate functions, and interpret
   notation-based statements in context. (F-IF.1, F-IF.2)
2. State a function's domain and range from its graph and from the situation
   it describes, constraining the domain to what the situation allows.
   (F-IF.5)
3. Represent linear functions as tables, graphs, equations, and verbal
   descriptions; translate between representations; graph `y = f(x)` by hand
   with labeled axes and sensible scales; read intercepts and intervals in
   context. (F-IF.4, F-IF.7; A-CED.2)
4. Calculate average rate of change over an interval from a table, a formula,
   or a graph; interpret slope as a rate of change with correct units; and
   recognize that a constant rate is what makes a function linear. (F-IF.6,
   N-Q.1)
5. Build linear functions that model relationships between quantities from a
   description, two input-output pairs, a table, or a graph — defining the
   quantities and their units first. (F-BF.1.a, F-LE.2, A-CED.2, N-Q.2)
6. Interpret the slope and intercept of a linear model in context, choose
   units appropriate to the audience, distinguish linear from nonlinear
   growth by checking equal differences over equal intervals, and name what
   a model leaves out. (F-LE.5, F-LE.1.a/b light, N-Q.2)

## Prerequisites

- Unit 01 skills: evaluating expressions; multi-step equation solving with
  justification; rearranging formulas (used to solve for parameters).
- Grade-8 slope and `y = mx + b` fluency: unit rate as slope; reading slope
  and intercept from graphs.
- Rational-number arithmetic with negatives and fractions (slope and rate
  calculations).
- **New here:** function notation `f(x)`; formal domain and range; average
  rate of change; building models from two pairs. Function intuition from
  grade 8 ("a rule assigning exactly one output per input") is formalized,
  not assumed.

## Standards and source notes

- **F-IF.1** — a function assigns exactly one output to each domain element;
  `f(x)` is the output for input `x`; the graph of `f` is the graph of
  `y = f(x)`. **F-IF.2** — use function notation, evaluate for domain
  inputs, interpret notation-based statements in context.
- **F-IF.4** — interpret key features of graphs and tables in context and
  sketch graphs from verbal descriptions. **F-IF.5** — relate a function's
  domain to its graph and to the described quantitative relationship.
- **F-IF.6** — calculate and interpret average rate of change over an
  interval (symbolic or tabular); estimate rate of change from a graph.
  **F-IF.7** — graph functions expressed symbolically and show key features,
  by hand in simple cases.
- **F-BF.1.a** — determine an explicit expression from a context.
- **F-LE.2** — construct linear functions from a graph, description, or two
  input-output pairs. **F-LE.5** — interpret the parameters in a linear
  function in terms of a context. F-LE.1.a/b is used lightly (equal
  differences over equal intervals mark linear growth; no exponentials yet).
- **A-CED.2** — create equations in two or more variables to represent
  relationships; graph them on labeled, scaled axes. **A-REI.10** — the
  graph of a two-variable equation is the set of all its solutions plotted
  in the plane. **A-REI.11** (light touch) — intersection x-coordinates of
  `y = f(x)` and `y = g(x)` solve `f(x) = g(x)`.
- **N-Q.1** — use units to guide multi-step problem solving; interpret
  units, scale, and origin in graphs. **N-Q.2** — define appropriate
  quantities for descriptive modeling.
- Codes and descriptions follow the Common Core State Standards for
  Mathematics, High School — Functions and Algebra (corestandards.org, as
  cross-walked in this track's
  [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md), verified 2026-10-06).
  No claim of state adoption or accreditation is made.

## Accessibility and safety notes

- All activities are paper- or screen-based; no hazards. The project uses
  fictional pricing data — learners must not enter personal or family account
  information.
- The vending-machine image and the SVG both ship with text-only alternatives
  so every question is answerable without seeing the image.
- Graph work can use large-format grids, raised-line or tactile materials,
  and dictated or scribed steps with the learner supplying the reasoning
  aloud. Desmos and GeoGebra offer keyboard navigation — noted in the unit
  resource pack.
