# Project — The Fare Audit

**Unit 02 investigation · Sessions 8–19 (alongside instruction) · Draft due
Session 16 · Final due Session 19**

## The situation (fictional data — do not use any real ride-share app, real prices, or personal trip data)

Two fictional ride-share companies publish these practice rate cards:

- **ZipGo:** $4.50 base fare + $1.80 per mile.
- **GlideRide:** $9.00 base fare + $1.10 per mile.

A commuter takes a ~15-mile ride to work each day and wants to know which
service to use. You are the analyst: build both pricing models, graph them,
find the break-even distance, and write a recommendation memo. All numbers
below are invented for this project.

## Your deliverables (one report, 2–4 pages)

1. **The models.** Write `Z(m)` and `G(m)` for the fare in dollars when `m`
   miles are driven. Before any algebra, define the quantities (`m` and the
   fare) with units, and state the domain with a reason (N-Q.2, F-IF.5).
2. **Interpret the parameters.** For each plan, write one sentence
   interpreting the slope and one interpreting the intercept, with units
   (F-LE.5). Which plan's fare grows faster per mile? Which charges more
   before any miles are driven?
3. **Tables and graphs.** Build a table of values for each plan at
   `m = 0, 5, 10, 15, 20` miles. Graph both functions by hand on one set of
   labeled, scaled axes ("miles" and "dollars"), marking both intercepts
   (A-CED.2). Your graph must be readable enough that a classmate could find
   the break-even point on it.
4. **Break-even analysis.** Write and solve `Z(m) = G(m)` with justification
   for each step (Unit-01 habit). Interpret the result: below, at, and above
   the break-even distance, which plan is cheaper? Verify with one test
   value on each side of the break-even point. Explain what the intersection
   of your two graphs means for this equation (A-REI.11).
5. **Rates of change.** Compute the average rate of change of each plan over
   `[0, 20]` and interpret each with units (F-IF.6). Why are the two rates
   different, and which plan would a 40-mile airport run favor?
6. **Recommendation memo** (one page or less, addressed to the commuter):
   which service for the daily ~15-mile ride, and why — citing your
   break-even distance, the 15-mile fares from your table, and the parameter
   interpretations. State explicitly what your model leaves out (wait times,
   tips, surge pricing, traffic, vehicle type — anything the rate card does
   not say) and why the answer could change for a much shorter or longer
   trip.
7. **Extension (optional, replaces one practice set):** Find the distance at
   which a ZipGo ride costs exactly $40. Solve `Z(m) = 40`, verify by
   substitution, and explain why the answer is only an estimate in the real
   world (sensible accuracy, N-Q.3).

## Materials

Paper or a notebook, graph paper, ruler, calculator. Optional: the free
Desmos graphing calculator (no account) to check your hand graphs — a graph
check is welcome, but the by-hand work must stand on its own.

## Rules and safety

- All plan data is fictional and labeled as such. Do not look up or enter
  any real ride-share prices, real trip records, or any personal or family
  account information.
- Work may be individual or in pairs; each learner submits their own memo.
- Every parameter interpretation must name both quantities and their units —
  interpretations without units earn no credit in the rubric below.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Models & quantities (Parts 1–2) | Quantities defined with units; domain justified; slope and intercept interpreted in pricing context with units | Quantities defined; interpretations correct but a unit missing | Quantities vague or domain unjustified; interpretations swapped or unitless | Models incorrect or missing |
| Graphs & representations (Part 3) | Both lines on labeled, scaled axes; intercepts marked; table values exact; break-even readable on the graph | Graphs labeled with minor scale or labeling slips; table correct | Axes unlabeled or scale unusable; table errors | Graphs missing or unreadable |
| Analysis & solving (Parts 4–5) | Every step justified; break-even interpreted below/at/above; test values verify; rates interpreted with units | Steps shown with mostly correct justifications; interpretations correct | Steps shown but justifications missing; break-even found but not interpreted | Analysis missing or algebraically wrong |
| Modeling & interpretation (Part 6) | 15-mile fares cited from the table; memo cites evidence; limitations named explicitly; short/long-trip caveat given | Fares cited; memo cites the analysis; one limitation named | Memo ignores the analysis or omits limitations | No interpretation in context |
| Communication | Report organized, notation consistent (`Z`, `G` used throughout), memo addressed and concise | Report readable; minor notation slips | Disorganized; notation inconsistent | Incomplete or incoherent |

**Scoring:** 17–20 = exceeds; 13–16 = meets; 9–12 = approaching (revise Parts
flagged); below 9 = reteach and resubmit. The extension adds up to 2 bonus
points.

## What readiness looks like / reteach triggers

- **Ready to move on:** both models built with quantities and domain stated,
  break-even solved and interpreted correctly, memo cites actual fares from
  the analysis.
- **Reteach:** if the break-even equation is set up as `1.8m + 4.5 = 1.1m +
  9` but solved with the rates subtracted in the wrong order, return to
  Lesson 04's later-minus-earlier habit; if parameter interpretations lack
  units, revisit Lesson 06's sentence frames.
