## Task 1 — Design a fair investigation (Objective 1, 8 pts)

A company claims its "growth water" makes bean plants grow taller than
plain water.

a. (2 pts) Write a testable question for this claim.
b. (3 pts) Name the independent variable, the dependent variable, and two
   controlled variables.
c. (2 pts) Describe your control group and explain what it rules out.
d. (1 pt) How many plants per group, and why that number rather than one?

## Task 2 — Evidence and reasoning (Objective 2, 8 pts)

You are given this lab record from another learner:

> "I tested 3 foods for starch with iodine. Cracker: blue-black in 20
> seconds, all 3 trials. Banana: blue-black in 45 seconds, 2 of 3 trials
> (trial 3 stayed brownish-orange). Cheese: no color change, all 3 trials.
> Iodine on water: stayed brownish-orange."

a. (2 pts) List two observations and one inference from this record.
b. (3 pts) Write a full CER for the claim "The banana contains starch,"
   reporting the inconsistent trial honestly.
c. (2 pts) The learner concludes "cheese has no biomolecules." Identify
   the error and state what the evidence actually shows.
d. (1 pt) What was the control in this test, and what alternative
   explanation does it rule out?

## Task 3 — Biomolecules in food (Objective 3, 8 pts)

A new snack label reads per serving: total carbohydrate 22g (sugars 14g),
total fat 3g, protein 5g.

a. (2 pts) Which biomolecule class dominates? Cite the exact evidence.
b. (2 pts) List the atoms in the dominant class. Then list the atoms in
   the protein the label reports.
c. (2 pts) The label lists no nucleic acids. A learner says "so this snack
   has no DNA." Evaluate: is that a valid inference? Explain.
d. (2 pts) The snack's sugars are C, H, O. Its protein contains N. In 3–4
   sentences, trace where the protein's nitrogen plausibly came from,
   starting from soil or food.

## Task 4 — Modeling molecular assembly (Objective 4, 8 pts)

a. (3 pts) Three glucose molecules (C₆H₁₂O₆ each) link into a short chain
   by dehydration synthesis. Show the full atom inventory: starting total,
   waters released, final C/H/O counts and total.
b. (3 pts) Using words or a labeled drawing, model how the atoms of a
   sugar could recombine with nitrogen to form part of an amino acid.
   Include: what you start with, what joins, the conservation check, and
   one honest sentence about what your model leaves out.
c. (2 pts) A classmate's model ends with more atoms than it started with.
   Explain the error and how the conservation check catches it.

**Reflection (ungraded, required):** Which changed more for you in this
unit — your ideas about food, or your ideas about how science decides what
is true? Give one specific example.
