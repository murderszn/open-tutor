# Unit 01 — Biological Investigation: Evidence and Biomolecules

**Grade:** 9 · **Subject:** Science (Biology pathway) · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #43](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/43), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A bag of chips says "zero fat" and "0g protein" on the label. Does the food
inside match the package? In this unit the learner plans and carries out a
fair food-testing investigation (variables, controls, repeated trials),
learns to separate what is *observed* from what is *inferred*, tests real
grocery foods for starch and lipids, reads nutrition labels as evidence, and
builds physical models showing how the carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen from
sugars recombine with other elements into amino acids and other large
carbon-based molecules.

## Essential question

How do we know what's really in our food — and how does life build giant
molecules out of tiny atoms?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the grade-8 science track:

- Metric measurement (mass in grams, volume in milliliters) and reading
  simple tables and bar graphs
- Proportional reasoning sufficient for percent change and simple ratios
- Basic cell vocabulary (cell as the unit of life) — re-taught in use, not
  assumed fluent
- That plants make food using sunlight, water, and air — balanced equations
  and energy bookkeeping are U03's work, not assumed here

The diagnostic verifies measurement and graphing fluency; this unit's
Sessions 2–4 re-teach insecure skills before assuming them.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Plan and carry out a fair investigation: state a testable question,
   identify independent, dependent, and controlled variables, include a
   control or comparison, run repeated trials, and record data precisely.
2. Distinguish observations from inferences, and write a
   claim–evidence–reasoning explanation that uses evidence to support,
   revise, or reject a claim.
3. Classify foods and nutrients into the four biomolecule classes
   (carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, nucleic acids) and name which of the
   atoms C, H, O, N, and P each class is built from.
4. Build a model showing how the carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen in sugars
   combine with other elements (especially nitrogen) to form amino acids
   and other large carbon-based molecules, accounting for every atom
   (conservation of matter).

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS),
high-school life-science performance expectations** (HS-LS1). Codes and
descriptions were verified 2026-10-06 against a full official-text
transcription during the track's A00 audit; paraphrases below, not
reproductions. No state adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification
claimed. Practices (SEPs): planning and carrying out investigations,
constructing explanations, developing and using models.

| Unit objective | Standard / practice | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | HS-LS1-6 | Construct and revise an explanation — based on evidence — of how carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen from sugar molecules combine with other elements to form amino acids and/or other large carbon-based molecules. |
| 1 | SEP: Planning and carrying out investigations | Plan and conduct an investigation: decide what data to collect, how to control variables, how many trials, and how to record precisely. |
| 2 | SEP: Constructing explanations | Construct an explanation that includes a claim, the evidence, and the reasoning that connects them; revise it when evidence requires. |
| 3, 4 | SEP: Developing and using models | Develop and use a model (physical or drawn) to show how simple molecules assemble into larger ones and where every atom goes. |

## Materials

Notebook, pencils, ruler, index cards for vocabulary, measuring cups and
spoons, small clear cups (at least 12), iodine solution **handled only by
the adult**, brown paper bags or plain brown paper, grocery foods for tests
(bread, cracker, cooked rice or pasta, potato, cooking oil, butter, apple,
egg — the adult checks for food allergies first), printed or written
nutrition labels from 3–4 packaged foods, craft modeling materials for
molecule models (colored clay, beads, or ball-and-stick kits; one color per
element, used consistently), a printed copy of
[`assets/biomolecule-food-sorting.png`](assets/biomolecule-food-sorting.png),
and the resource pack's verified references. Digital option: a spreadsheet
app for data tables. **Nothing is tasted during testing; the adult handles
iodine; hands are washed after testing.** Every investigation includes an
observation-only or label-only alternative when reagents are unavailable.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts written, oral, typed, drawn, or modeled responses; the
adult scribes when writing stamina lags. Models are distinguishable by
shape and labeling, not color alone (element key uses color **plus** a
letter stamp on each atom token). Sessions are about 50 minutes with a
movement break. Vocabulary is taught with models and objects first, word
second; home-language labels are welcomed alongside English terms. The
generated illustration ships with a text-only alternative carrying every
detail the activity needs. Simulations and spreadsheet work note keyboard
alternatives in the teacher guide.
