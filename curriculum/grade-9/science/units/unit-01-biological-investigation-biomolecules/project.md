# Project — The Biomolecule Evidence Investigation

**Sessions 7–9 (lab), 14–15 (model + report), 18 (presentations).**
Full scoring guidance is in the [answer key](answer-key.md#project-rubric--scoring-notes);
session facilitation notes are in the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md#project-facilitation).

## Driving question

*Which biomolecules are in the foods I eat — and how does life build those
giant molecules from tiny atoms?*

## Deliverables

1. **A fair lab plan** (approved in Session 7): question, variables,
   controls, trials, data table.
2. **A precise data record** (Sessions 8–9): three trials per food per
   test, with controls, in the learner's notebook or spreadsheet.
3. **An atom-accounting model** (Session 14): physical tokens (or a labeled
   drawing) showing a sugar's atoms recombining with nitrogen into a larger
   molecule, with a written conservation check.
4. **A written evidence report** (Sessions 14–15): claim–evidence–reasoning
   explanations for each food, the HS-LS1-6 explanation with the model, and
   at least one visible revision where evidence overturned a prediction.
5. **A capstone presentation** (Session 18): 5-minute defense of one claim
   from the report, with the model, taking one challenging question.

## Lab sessions 1 & 2 (Sessions 8–9)

**Safety:** the adult handles all iodine. Nothing is tasted. Hands washed
after. Allergy check before choosing foods.

**Procedure:**

1. Set out four chosen foods (from the Lesson 4 plan) plus control
   materials (water drop, known starch-free food for iodine; water square
   for brown paper).
2. **Iodine test:** the adult places one drop of iodine on a small sample
   of each food; the learner observes and records the *precise* color at
   30 seconds. Run the water control alongside.
3. **Brown-paper test:** the learner presses or rubs each food on its own
   labeled brown-paper square (or places a drop for liquids); sets a timer
   for 2 minutes; holds each square to the light and records exactly what
   is seen. Run the water control square alongside.
4. **Label evidence:** for each packaged food, copy the serving size,
   total carbohydrate, total fat, and protein grams into the table.
5. Repeat every food test **three times** with fresh samples. Inconsistent
   trials are recorded, not deleted.

**Data table columns:** *Food | Trial | Test | Control run? | Observation
(precise) | Inference*. The learner fills the observation column during the
lab; inferences are written after, in a different pen color or a separate
column pass — observations and inferences stay visually separate.

### Label-only alternative

If iodine or brown paper is unavailable, the investigation runs on
**nutrition labels + the illustration predictions**: the learner collects
labels from 6+ packaged foods, records carbs/fat/protein grams, classifies
each food's dominant biomolecule class, writes CERs with the label as
evidence, and explicitly discusses what labels *cannot* show (which
carbohydrate; nucleic acids). The model, report, and presentation are
unchanged. The unit's objectives are still fully met.

## Model build and report sessions (Sessions 14–15)

**Session 14 — build and draft.** Build the atom-accounting model from
[Lesson 6](lessons/lesson-06-sugars-to-amino-acids.md#worked-example-1--the-atom-accounting-model):
glucose tokens (C₆H₁₂O₆) + nitrogen-source tokens → rearranged into a
nitrogen-containing larger molecule + leftover group. Photograph or draw
each stage; write the before/after inventory and the conservation check.
Then draft the report: one CER per food + the HS-LS1-6 explanation with
the model attached.

**Session 15 — peer critique and revision.** The adult (or a partner) reads
the draft against the rubric's *evidence* and *revision* rows and marks:
claim stated? evidence precise and separated from inference? reasoning names
the scientific idea? at least one prediction overturned and revised? The
learner revises the report the same session. The revision itself is graded —
a report with no visible revision cannot score above "developing" on that
row.

## Report structure (required sections)

1. **Question and plan summary** (5–6 sentences): what you tested, your
   variables, controls, and trials.
2. **Data record** (the table, complete and precise).
3. **Findings** (one CER per food): claim, exact evidence, reasoning.
4. **The HS-LS1-6 explanation** (one paragraph + model): how the carbon,
   hydrogen, and oxygen in sugars combine with other elements to form
   amino acids and other large carbon-based molecules — with the model's
   conservation check and an honest statement of what the model leaves
   out.
5. **Revision log** (at least one entry): what you predicted, what the
   evidence showed, and what you changed.

## Capstone presentations (Session 18)

Five minutes per presentation: state your strongest claim, show the exact
evidence (photo of the test, the data table row, the label), walk through
the model, and take one challenging question from the adult ("What would
change your mind?" is always fair). The audience scores with the rubric's
*communication* row.

## Rubric — Biomolecule Evidence Investigation

| Row | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fair design** | Variables named correctly; controls run alongside each batch; 3 trials per food; precise table used throughout | Variables named; controls present; 3 trials; table mostly precise | A variable misnamed or a control missing; fewer than 3 trials on some foods | No identifiable control; single trials; no usable table |
| **Observation vs. inference** | Every record separates sensed language from conclusions; inferences labeled as such | Separation maintained with 1–2 slips | Frequent mixing of observation and inference language | Records are conclusions only ("has starch") with no sensed detail |
| **Evidence use (CER)** | Each claim cites exact evidence (color, time, grams); reasoning names the scientific idea; controls cited where relevant | CERs complete; reasoning present but generic in places | Claims with evidence but no reasoning, or reasoning that restates evidence | Claims without evidence |
| **Atom model** | Every atom accounted for before/after (conservation check shown); nitrogen included; honest "leaves out" statement | Counts correct; conservation check present; leaves-out statement thin | Atom counts off but the error is found and noted, or nitrogen missing | No conservation check; counts not attempted |
| **Revision** | At least one prediction overturned by evidence and the explanation visibly revised; inconsistent trials reported | A revision present but minor, or inconsistent trials noted without discussion | No revision despite disagreeing evidence, or disagreeing trials hidden | Report matches predictions exactly with no mention of surprises |
| **Communication** | Presentation defends one claim with exact evidence and the model; answers the challenging question with reasoning | Claim defended; question answered adequately | Reads the report aloud without defending choices | Cannot state the claim or the evidence |

**Scoring:** 20–24 = ready to move on; 14–19 = revise the two lowest rows
before Unit 02; below 14 = re-teach with the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md#reteaching-map) and use a flex week.
