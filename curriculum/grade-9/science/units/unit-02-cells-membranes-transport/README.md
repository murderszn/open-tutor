# Unit 02 — Cells: Membranes and Transport

**Grade:** 9 · **Subject:** Science (Biology pathway) · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #43](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/43), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A cell is a city with a border wall that thinks. In this unit the learner
models the cell's specialized structures as jobs that keep the whole cell
running, compares plant and animal cells and explains what each difference
makes possible, builds the fluid-mosaic picture of the cell membrane piece
by piece, and learns to predict which way substances move across it —
diffusion down a gradient, osmosis chasing water balance, and active
transport spending energy to push against the flow. The unit closes with a
fair osmosis investigation the learner designs, runs, and defends with
evidence.

## Essential question

How does a cell keep its inside different from the outside world — and what
happens when the gate fails?

## Prerequisites

From Unit 01 and the diagnostic weeks:

- Planning a fair investigation: testable question, independent/dependent/
  controlled variables, control or comparison, repeated trials, precise
  recording (U01, Lessons 1–2)
- Claim–evidence–reasoning explanations; separating observation from
  inference (U01, Lesson 2)
- Basic cell vocabulary: cell as the unit of life; nucleus, cell membrane,
  plant/animal distinction (diagnostic; re-taught in use, not assumed
  fluent)
- Percent change and simple ratios for the investigation's data analysis
- Reading tables and bar graphs

This unit does not assume organelle-level function, membrane-transport
mechanisms, or the concentration-gradient model — those are the unit's new
content.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Model the functions of major cell structures (nucleus, ribosomes,
   endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, mitochondria, chloroplasts, cell
   wall, vacuole, cell membrane) and explain how each structure's job serves
   the whole cell; compare plant and animal cells and explain what each
   difference makes possible.
2. Explain selective permeability using the fluid-mosaic model: name which
   kinds of substances cross the phospholipid bilayer freely and which need
   protein help, and why.
3. Predict the direction of diffusion and osmosis from concentration
   differences, and explain the result with the concentration-gradient
   model (including hypertonic, hypotonic, and isotonic).
4. Plan and carry out a fair osmosis investigation with defined variables,
   a control, and repeated trials; use the evidence to support, revise, or
   reject a claim about water movement.
5. Explain when cells use active transport — moving substances against
   their gradient at an energy (ATP) cost — and connect transport to
   keeping internal conditions stable.

## Materials

Notebook, pencils, ruler, index cards for vocabulary, printed copy of
[`assets/dye-diffusion-prediction.png`](assets/dye-diffusion-prediction.png)
(the generated prediction image), printed or on-screen copy of
[`assets/membrane-cross-section.svg`](assets/membrane-cross-section.svg)
(the deterministic membrane diagram), craft modeling materials (clay, beads,
pipe cleaners, or ball-and-stick kit; one consistent color/shape per
structure), two clear tall glasses, water, blue food coloring (adult
handles), a dropper or spoon. For the investigation (adult-supervised):
**egg version** — 2–3 raw eggs, white vinegar, three salt/sugar solutions of
known concentration, kitchen scale, ruler, cups; **or potato version** — one
large potato, knife (adult only), salt, sugar, water, cups, ruler, kitchen
scale. Vinegar and raw egg are handled by the adult; nothing is tasted;
hands are washed after handling. Every investigation step lists an
observation-only or simulation alternative (PhET Membrane Channels) when
materials, hazards, or access make the hands-on version unsuitable.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts written, oral, typed, drawn, or modeled responses; the
adult scribes when writing stamina lags. Diagrams are labeled with text and
arrows, never color alone (the membrane SVG uses text labels for every
part; the generated image ships with a full text alternative). Sessions are
about 50 minutes with a movement break. Vocabulary is taught with models
and objects first, word second; home-language labels are welcomed alongside
English terms. The PhET simulation alternative notes keyboard operation in
the teacher guide; every simulation task also has a text-and-table
equivalent. Fluency work is strategy-based, never timed.
