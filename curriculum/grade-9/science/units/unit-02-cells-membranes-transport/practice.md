# Practice Sessions — Unit 02: Cells, Membranes, and Transport

Four named sessions referenced in the
[20-session pacing](README.md#20-session-pacing).
Each session is one 50-minute period. The adult reviews written work the
same day. Worked solutions are in the [answer key](answer-key.md); do not
open it during practice.

---

## Session A: What breaks if this stops?

*Session 2 · Retrieves: Lesson 1 organelle jobs and dependencies.*

**Part 1 — Malfunction matching (20 min).** Match each failure to the
structure responsible, then name one downstream consequence:

1. Proteins are built but never packaged or addressed — they pile up
   unfinished.
2. The cell cannot release energy from food; every energy-costly job
   stalls.
3. Instructions for building proteins are lost; nothing new gets built.
4. A plant cell wilts even though water is available at its roots.
5. Amino acids are available but no proteins are assembled.
6. A plant cell cannot make its own food, though it still respires.

**Part 2 — Dependency web (20 min).** Lay out the nine index cards and
draw arrows showing "needs": an arrow from A to B means "B needs A to do
its job." Include at least eight arrows, each labeled with *why* in under
ten words. Then answer: which single structure's failure cascades the
farthest? Defend your pick in two sentences.

**Part 3 — Error analysis (10 min).** "The Golgi apparatus is the most
important organelle because it ships everything." Explain why "most
important" is the wrong question — and rephrase the claim as a true
statement about dependencies.

## Session B: Annotate and compare

*Session 4 · Retrieves: Lesson 2 plant/animal differences; Lesson 1 vocabulary.*

**Part 1 — Blind annotation (20 min).** The adult prints or sketches two
*unlabeled* cell outlines (one with wall/chloroplasts/giant vacuole, one
without). Without looking at your Lesson 2 notes: label every structure
you can, add one job-phrase per label, and write "PLANT" or "ANIMAL" under
each with your evidence (name the difference-makers you used).

**Part 2 — Comparison table from memory (15 min).** Rebuild the
three-row table (structure | animal | plant | what the difference makes
possible) from memory. Check against
[Lesson 2](lessons/lesson-02-plant-vs-animal-cells.md); circle every cell
you missed or softened — those are your re-study targets for Session 17.

**Part 3 — Trade-off writing (15 min).** "The cell wall is pure advantage
for plants." Write a 5-sentence rebuttal: grant the real advantages, then
name the trade-off (rigidity vs. flexibility — which animal abilities does
the wall rule out?). End with one sentence stating the general principle
about structure and lifestyle.

## Session C: Gradient predictions

*Session 11 · Retrieves: Lessons 4–5; the two-question card; Sessions 8–9 lab thinking.*

**Part 1 — Prediction drills (25 min).** For each scenario, run the
two-question card ("Can it cross? Which way does the gradient point?"),
predict the net movement, and justify in one sentence:

1. O₂: high outside a muscle cell, low inside. Membrane permeable to O₂.
2. CO₂: high inside a leaf cell (respiring at night), low outside.
3. Glucose: high outside, low inside; glucose channels present.
4. Glucose: high outside, low inside; glucose channels blocked.
5. K⁺: high inside, low outside; K⁺ channels open.
6. Water: potato slice in distilled water.
7. Water: egg in 10% salt solution.
8. Water: red blood cell in 0.9% saline.

**Part 2 — PhET exploration (15 min, adult-supervised).** Open the PhET
Membrane Channels simulation (see
[resource pack](resource-pack.md#web-references-4-verified-2026-10-20)).
Turn on "Show Concentrations." Add green particles to one side only, with
no channels: what happens? Add a green leakage channel: what changes? Write
two sentences connecting what you saw to the gradient model — and one
sentence on what the simulation *cannot* show (it is a model, not a cell).

**Part 3 — Error analysis (10 min).** "In scenario 5, K⁺ moves out, so the
cell is doing active transport." Explain the error: what distinguishes
this K⁺ movement from active transport, even though the cell 'uses' a
channel?

## Session D: Transport-type sorting

*Session 13 · Retrieves: Lessons 3–6; the three-question machine.*

**Part 1 — Sort and defend (25 min).** Sort each scenario into **simple
diffusion / facilitated diffusion / osmosis / active transport /
endo-exocytosis**, then run the three-question machine aloud for two of
them:

1. O₂ enters a working muscle cell through the bilayer.
2. A root cell pumps mineral ions inward against their gradient.
3. Water enters a potato slice in distilled water.
4. Glucose enters a cell through a channel protein, high → low.
5. A white blood cell engulfs a bacterium.
6. A gland cell releases hormones in vesicles.
7. Na⁺ is pumped out of a nerve cell.
8. CO₂ leaves a leaf cell through the bilayer at night.
9. A channel protein carries K⁺ out of a cell, high → low.
10. Iodine (small, uncharged) crosses a membrane into a cell.

**Part 2 — Build the decision tree (15 min).** Draw a flowchart that sorts
*any* transport scenario: start with "Can it cross the bilayer?" → "Is a
protein involved?" → "With or against the gradient?" → "Large cargo?"
Each endpoint names the transport type. Test your tree on scenarios 4 and
7 — if the tree gives the wrong answer, fix the tree, not the scenario.

**Part 3 — Error analysis (10 min).** "Osmosis is just diffusion, so it
never needs a protein and never costs energy." Identify the true part, the
sloppy part (water channels exist — does that change the energy story?),
and restate the claim precisely.
