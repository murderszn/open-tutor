# Unit Assessment — Unit 02: Cells, Membranes, and Transport

**Session 20 of 20** · About 45 minutes · Matched to the five unit
objectives · Any response mode is accepted (written, oral, typed, drawn,
modeled); the adult scribes when writing stamina lags. Worked solutions
and scoring guidance are in the [answer key](../answer-key.md) — kept
separate from this page. This is a draft formative instrument, not a
standardized or validated test.

## Part A — Structures and models (Objectives 1–2)

**A1.** Rebuild the membrane cross-section from memory on a blank page:
a phospholipid bilayer (heads out, tails in), one channel protein, one
pump, and one carbohydrate ID tag. Label: hydrophilic head, hydrophobic
tails, outside the cell, cytoplasm. Then add and label three arrows: (i)
a small uncharged molecule passing *between* phospholipids, (ii) a large
polar molecule passing *through* the channel protein, (iii) an ion being
pumped *against* its gradient. Under the diagram, write one sentence
explaining what "selectively permeable" means.

**A2.** A plant cell and an animal cell are shown unlabeled (the adult
provides two outlines, or the learner draws from memory). Label the
structures, identify each cell as plant or animal with evidence (name the
difference-makers), and explain in 2–3 sentences what one plant-only
structure makes possible.

**A3.** "What breaks if this stops?" — the smooth endoplasmic reticulum of
a liver cell shuts down. Name the ER's jobs, predict two consequences for
the cell, and explain each with a dependency.

## Part B — Transport predictions (Objectives 3, 5)

*Run the three-question machine on each: (1) Can it cross? (2) Which way
does the gradient point? (3) With or against?*

**B1.** Oxygen: high outside a muscle cell, low inside; membrane permeable
to O₂. Predict the net direction, name the transport type, and say whether
ATP is spent.

**B2.** Sodium: the sodium-potassium pump moves Na⁺ from inside (low) to
outside (high). Name the transport type, say whether ATP is spent, and
explain *why* the cell pays this cost (connect to homeostasis in one
sentence).

**B3.** A vesicle inside a gland cell fuses with the membrane and releases
hormones outside. Name the process, classify it as passive or active, and
justify with the three-question machine.

**B4.** Error analysis: "Water always moves into cells because cells need
water." Using hypertonic/hypotonic language, explain when this is true,
when it is false, and what actually decides the direction.

## Part C — Osmosis data (Objectives 3–4)

*Fictional practice dataset — invented for this assessment, not
measurements the learner took.*

Four identical potato slices sat 3 hours in four solutions. Masses:

| Slice | Solution | Start mass (g) | End mass (g) |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | distilled water | 12.0 | 13.4 |
| Q | 1% salt | 12.2 | 12.3 |
| R | 10% salt | 11.9 | 10.2 |
| S | 5% salt | 12.1 | 11.5 |

**C1.** Compute the percent change for each slice. Show your work.

**C2.** Classify each solution as hypertonic, hypotonic, or isotonic
*relative to the potato cells*, and justify each classification with the
mass evidence.

**C3.** Which solution was most hypertonic? Explain how the numbers show
it — and explain why the 1% salt slice is the best control of the four.

**C4.** CER paragraph (4–6 sentences): *Claim:* state which direction water
moves by osmosis in general. *Evidence:* cite two specific numbers from the
table. *Reasoning:* explain the evidence with the concentration-gradient
model ("water follows solute").

## Part D — Synthesis (Objectives 1–5)

**D1.** "Diffusion evens things out; life spends energy to stay uneven."
Defend this thesis in a short paragraph (5–8 sentences): use the
sodium-potassium pump, one osmosis example, and the word *homeostasis*.
Then name one dependency from Lesson 1 that the thesis relies on (which
organelle pays the energy bill, and what happens when it cannot).

## Scoring guidance (adult use)

- **A1:** 2 pts diagram completeness (bilayer orientation, three proteins/
  tag, labels), 2 pts arrows correct (path + labels), 1 pt sentence.
- **A2:** 2 pts identification with evidence, 2 pts explanation of the
  plant-only structure's function.
- **A3:** 1 pt ER jobs, 2 pts consequences with dependencies.
- **B1–B3:** 2 pts each (prediction/process + type + energy justification).
- **B4:** 2 pts (true/false cases + decider).
- **C1:** 2 pts (correct percent changes with work).
- **C2:** 2 pts (classifications + evidence linkage).
- **C3:** 2 pts (most hypertonic + control reasoning).
- **C4:** 3 pts (claim + two numbers + gradient reasoning).
- **D1:** 4 pts (thesis defended with pump + osmosis + homeostasis; energy
  dependency named).
- **Total: 33.** Ready for Unit 03: ≥26 with no objective scoring below
  half. 20–25: re-teach the lowest part in a flex session, then proceed.
  Below 20: revisit the flagged lessons before Unit 03.
