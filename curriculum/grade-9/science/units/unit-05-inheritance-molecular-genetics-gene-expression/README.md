# Unit 05 — Inheritance: Molecular Genetics and Gene Expression

**Grade:** 9 · **Subject:** Science (Biology pathway) · **Track weeks:** 19–22
**Backlog:** [issue #43](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/43), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Mendel counted peas and found a 3:1 pattern he could not explain — he never
knew about DNA. In this unit the learner supplies the missing mechanism:
the double helix and its base-pairing rules, the code that turns a DNA
sequence into a protein, the chromosome behavior from Unit 04 that puts one
allele in each gamete, and the probability that turns those gametes into a
predicted 3:1 ratio. Then the unit tests the prediction against real
(fictional, labeled) population counts, asks why the same genome can make a
skin cell and a muscle cell, and traces a fictional family's trait through a
pedigree. The unit closes with a family-trait investigation the learner
designs, models, and defends with evidence.

## Essential question

How does a molecule built from four letters carry the instructions for a
whole organism — and how far can counting and probability take us in
predicting what the next generation will look like?

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–04 and the diagnostic weeks:

- Planning a fair investigation: testable question, variables, control or
  comparison, repeated trials, precise recording (U01, Lessons 1–2)
- Claim–evidence–reasoning explanations; separating observation from
  inference (U01, Lesson 2)
- Biomolecule basics: DNA is a nucleic acid built from nucleotides; proteins
  are built from amino acids (U01, Lessons 3, 5–6)
- Cell structures: the nucleus holds the instructions; ribosomes build
  proteins; mitochondria and chloroplasts do energy work (U02, Lesson 1)
- Meiosis: one diploid cell produces four varied haploid gametes; homologous
  chromosomes separate in meiosis I; crossing over and independent
  assortment create new combinations; 2^n possible chromosome combinations
  for n pairs (U04, Lessons 3–5)
- Sources of heritable variation: new meiotic combinations, viable
  replication errors, environment-caused mutations (U04, Lesson 5)
- Differentiation: one genome, many cell types through different gene
  expression (U04, Lesson 6 — re-taught in use here, not assumed fluent)
- Powers of 2, simple ratios, percent, and reading tables and bar graphs;
  Mendel's 705:224 flower-color count is **not** assumed — Lesson 3 teaches
  it from the data.

This unit does not assume DNA structure, base pairing, transcription or
translation, the codon table, Punnett squares, probability rules, pedigrees,
or inheritance patterns beyond dominant/recessive — those are the unit's new
content. The full codon table is never memorized: the unit uses a simplified
chart of the codons its examples need.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Model DNA structure — double helix, antiparallel strands, A–T and G–C
   base pairing — and explain how DNA structure determines protein
   structure: genes are DNA segments that code for proteins.
2. Explain the central dogma (DNA → RNA → protein) with a simplified codon
   chart: read a short mRNA sequence and predict the amino-acid sequence;
   explain how a single base change can alter a protein, leave it unchanged,
   or stop it early.
3. Ask and refine questions about how DNA and chromosomes code the
   instructions for traits; use Punnett squares to predict offspring
   genotype and phenotype ratios for monohybrid crosses.
4. Apply statistics and probability to trait variation in a population:
   use the product and sum rules, compare expected counts with observed
   counts, and explain why small samples deviate more from a prediction
   than large ones.
5. Explain gene expression — the same genome, different genes active in
   different cells, influenced by environment — and describe inheritance
   patterns (dominant/recessive, incomplete dominance, codominance,
   sex-linked) and mutation as a source of new variation, using evidence.

## Core texts (this unit)

All learner-facing texts are original to this unit. No copyrighted books,
worksheets, or lab observations are reproduced. Every dataset used for a
claim task is labeled **fictional practice data**; the learner never
reports it as a real observation. Mendel's historical counts (705 violet :
224 white) are real published results, cited from OpenStax Biology 2e

## Materials

Notebook, pencils, ruler, index cards for vocabulary, printed copies of
the generated image and the Punnett-square SVG, craft modeling materials
for DNA models (two colors of beads or paper strips for the two strands,
four bead colors or letter stamps for A/T/C/G, pipe cleaners or string for
the backbone), coins or tokens for the probability game (Lesson 4), the
fictional pedigree handout (Lesson 6 / project), and the fictional F2
dataset handout (Lesson 4 / project). No purchases beyond ordinary craft
supplies are required; no living material, microscope, or hazardous
substance is used. The observation alternative for every modeling step is
the labeled SVG and the OpenStax reference reading — no learner is required
to handle any biological specimen.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts written, oral, typed, drawn, or modeled responses; the
adult scribes when writing stamina lags. Diagrams are labeled with text
and arrows, never color alone (the Punnett-square SVG uses text labels for
every genotype and phenotype; the generated image ships with a full text
alternative carrying the exact flower counts). Sessions are about 50
minutes with a movement break. Vocabulary is taught with models and
objects first, word second; home-language labels are welcomed alongside
English terms. Every modeling task also has a text-and-table equivalent.
Fluency work is strategy-based, never timed. Family-trait work uses only
the fictional Alvarez pedigree and the learner's own *optional*, private
observations — no real family medical history is asked for, recorded, or
shared, and the learner may complete every task from the fictional data
alone.
