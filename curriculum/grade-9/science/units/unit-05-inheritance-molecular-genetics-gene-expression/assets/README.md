- **File:** `pea-flower-counting.png` (1920 × 1280 PNG)
- **Prompt:** "A bright, clear educational illustration of a school garden
  bed with pea plants in bloom, viewed from above at a slight angle. Some
  plants have purple flowers and some have white flowers, arranged in neat
  rows so each flowering plant can be counted. Green leaves and tendrils,
  simple flat textbook illustration style, daylight, no text, no labels,
  no numbers, no people."
- **Generation tool:** Muse built-in image generation
  (`media.generate_image`), 2026-11-10.
- **Learning purpose:** Lesson 3's counting-and-critique activity. The
  learner counts purple vs. white flowers, computes the observed ratio,
  compares it with the 3:1 prediction for a Bb × Bb cross, and then
  critiques the test itself — the bed was planted in color blocks, so the
  count cannot test Mendel's prediction.
- **Factual review (2026-11-10, inspected before linking):** flower colors
  are correct for Mendel's trait (purple/violet vs. white); pea plants
  shown with leaves, tendrils, and two flowers per plant; no text, labels,
  numbers, or people in the image; no false measurements or invented
  borders. The arrangement is stylized (rows planted by color) — the
  lesson explicitly uses that stylization as the critique point, and the
  caption states the image is an illustration, not a photograph of a real
  cross. Count verified by inspection: 20 plants in four rows of five,
  two open flowers per plant — 20 purple, 20 white (1:1 ratio). A learner
  counting 19–21 of either color (a bloom partly hidden behind a leaf)
  reaches the same conclusion.
- **Caption:** "A raised bed of pea plants in bloom. The back and third
  rows have purple flowers; the second and front rows have white flowers.
  AI-generated illustration for counting practice — not a photograph of a
  real cross, and not evidence about real plants."
- **Alt text:** "A raised garden bed with pea plants in bloom. The back
  and third rows have purple flowers; the second and front rows have white
  flowers."
- **Text-only alternative:** in [Lesson
  3](../lessons/lesson-03-chromosomes-alleles-and-punnett-squares.md#the-garden-bed-activity-uses-the-generated-image):
  "The bed holds 20 pea plants in four rows of five. Each plant shows two
  open flowers. Counting every open flower: 20 purple and 20 white — a

- **Known limitations:** AI-generated illustration, not a photograph; the
  plants are drawn in neat rows, which a real F2 population would not be;
  individual blooms may be partly hidden behind leaves, so counts may vary

  that variation.

## `punnett-square-bb.svg` (deterministic diagram)

- **File:** `punnett-square-bb.svg` (560 × 460 SVG)
- **Purpose:** a reproducible Bb × Bb Punnett square for Lesson 3's worked
  example 1 and the project presentations. Every genotype (BB, Bb, Bb,
  bb) and phenotype (purple/purple/purple/white) is text-labeled; color is
  never the only cue (the white-flower cell is white with a border and a
  text label, not distinguished by color alone).
- **Method:** hand-written SVG in this repository; no generation involved.
  Genotype ratio 1 BB : 2 Bb : 1 bb; phenotype ratio 3 purple : 1 white.
- **Factual review (2026-11-10):** grid verified against the Lesson 3
  worked example — top labels B, b (Parent 1 gametes); side labels B, b
  (Parent 2 gametes); cells BB, Bb, Bb, bb in the correct positions.
