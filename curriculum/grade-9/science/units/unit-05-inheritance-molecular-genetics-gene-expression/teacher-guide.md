# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Inheritance, Molecular Genetics, and Gene Expression

Adult-facing. Student lessons, practice, and assessments link here; the
[answer key](answer-key.md) holds all solutions.

## Unit at a glance

Twenty 50-minute sessions across track weeks 19–22. Weeks 19–20 build the
DNA-to-protein mechanism and the Punnett-square prediction; week 21 turns
the prediction into population data, expression, and inheritance patterns;
week 22 is review, check, present, and assess. The unit serves track
objectives 1 and 7 and standards HS-LS1-1, HS-LS3-1, HS-LS3-2 (revisit),
and HS-LS3-3.

## Session-by-session facilitation notes

- **S1 (L1):** build the DNA model live with the learner — do not
  pre-build it. The readiness check is the whole lesson in miniature.
- **S2 (Practice A):** the 50-million-base-pair item is the session's real
  product; let the learner do the multiplication.
- **S3 (L2):** keep the codon chart visible for the rest of the unit. The
  chart is a tool, never a memorization list — if the learner starts
  reciting it, redirect to translating.
- **S4 (Practice B):** check the frameshift item (B6) yourself first —
  the two-base deletion shifts every codon after it.
- **S5 (L3):** show the generated illustration *after* teaching the
  Punnett square, so the count uses new knowledge. State the honest
  framing aloud: the picture is a counting prompt, not data from a real
  cross. The critique (planted in blocks) is the point, not a flaw.
- **S6 (Reference):** you read aloud; the learner tracks ideas. If the
  session stalls, use the accessibility fallback — the ideas are the
  requirement, not the source.
- **S7 (L4):** run the coin game live — 16 tosses minimum. Do not skip it;
  it is the antidote to "probability is a promise."
- **S8–S9 (Project):** approve the pedigree labels tonight. The
  population-data table from Part 2 is the actual table used in Sessions
  14–15. Review it tonight if needed.
- **S10 (L5):** the Siamese-cat example carries the lesson. If the learner
  has never seen a Siamese cat, describe it or show a photo from the
  resource pack's references.
- **S11 (Practice C):** the twin-seedling item is the session's real
  product — the distinction between inherited genes and acquired growth.
- **S12 (L6):** keep the pedigree visible while working; shade filled
  symbols and write genotypes beside each symbol as you go.
- **S13 (Practice D):** the blood-type deduction (item 2) is the hardest
  item in the unit; let the learner struggle before hinting.
- **S14–S15:** protect the critique time — Session 15's revision is
  graded. A learner who "has nothing to revise" has not looked hard
  enough; prompt with "which claim has the weakest evidence?"
- **S16 (Quiz):** score same-day; sort misses by objective for S17.
- **S17 (Review):** re-teach only the lowest objective(s); use the
  re-check bank below.
- **S18 (Presentations):** ask the challenge question last, after the
  learner has shown everything.
- **S19 (Flex):** catch-up, or the Lesson 2/3/5 extensions.
- **S20 (Assessment):** calm conditions; scribe if needed.

## Misconceptions to watch (all five objectives)

- **"Dominant means more common."** Dominance is about expression in a
  heterozygote, not frequency. White flowers are recessive and common.
- **"The Punnett square predicts the exact next four offspring."** It
  gives probabilities per offspring. The coin game (L4) is the antidote.
- **"Every mutation changes the protein / is harmful."** Silent changes
  exist (L2); most mutations are neutral; harm is not guaranteed.
- **"Different cell types have different DNA."** Same genome, different
  expression (L5). Re-anchor with the Siamese cat.
- **"A carrier is sick."** A carrier shows no trait; carrier status
  matters for offspring.
- **"Gene and chromosome are the same thing."** A chromosome is the whole
  packaged molecule; genes are segments along it.
- **"A tan is inherited."** Expression is not sequence change (L5 vs. L6).

## Reteaching

- **Objective 1 (DNA structure):** rebuild the Lesson 1 model with
  beads; re-do Practice A items 1–3. Re-check: write the complement of
  5'-G C T A-3' and count its hydrogen bonds.
- **Objective 2 (central dogma):** re-transcribe one template together,
  slowly, with the chart open; then translate. Re-check: transcribe and
  translate template 3'-TAC AAA-5' (expect Met–Phe).
- **Objective 3 (Punnett squares):** re-draw Bb × Bb with letter tiles;
  then a test cross. Re-check: cross Tt × tt and give both ratios.
- **Objective 4 (probability):** re-run the coin game with 32 tosses;
  recompute the F2 expected counts. Re-check: P(bb) for one offspring of
  Bb × Bb, and P(two white in a row).
- **Objective 5 (expression/patterns):** re-tell the Siamese cat story;
  re-deduce one Alvarez genotype aloud. Re-check: two free-earlobed
  parents have an attached child — dominant or recessive, and why?

## Re-check bank (Session 17)

1. Complement of 5'-G C T A-3' (with direction) and its hydrogen-bond
   count. *(Expect 3'-CGAT-5' / 5'-TAGC-3'; 2 G–C × 3 + 2 A–T × 2 = 10.)*
2. Transcribe and translate template 3'-TAC AAA-5'. *(Expect mRNA AUG
   UUU → Met–Phe.)*
3. Cross Tt × tt; give genotype and phenotype ratios. *(Expect 1 Tt : 1
   tt; 1 tall : 1 dwarf.)*
4. P(bb) for one offspring of Bb × Bb, and P(two white offspring in a
   row). *(Expect 1/4 and 1/16.)*
5. Two free-earlobed parents have an attached-earlobe child. Dominant or
   recessive? Why? *(Expect recessive — the trait reappears from two
   carriers; both parents must be Ee.)*

## Readiness criteria

Ready for Unit 06 when the learner can: write a complementary strand and
explain base pairing; transcribe and translate a short sequence with the
chart; draw a monohybrid Punnett square and both ratios; compute a
two-offspring probability with the product rule; and explain gene
expression in one sentence using the Siamese cat or an equivalent example.
If any of these is insecure after Session 20, use the track's flexible
weeks before starting Unit 06.

## Safety and privacy

No living material, no hazardous substance, and no personal or family
information is used in this unit. The pedigree and population dataset are
fictional and labeled as such. Mendel's counts are historical published
data, not the learner's observations. If the learner discusses their own
family's traits, the adult keeps that conversation private and optional —
the project is complete from the fictional data alone.
