# Unit Assessment — Unit 04: Cell Cycle, Mitosis, and Meiosis

**Session 20 of 20 · 30 points.** Answer in writing, orally, or by model.
Show counts at every step; a count without its reasoning earns half
marks. The project report (40 points) is scored separately in
[project.md](../project.md).

## Part A — Model and count (12 pts)

A lab cell has 2n = 4 (two homologous pairs: pair 1 dark, pair 2 light).

1. (3 pts) Draw or model the cell after S phase. Label chromosomes (4)
   and chromatids (8) and explain, in one sentence, why the chromosome
   count did not double.
2. (3 pts) Draw or model the cell at mitotic metaphase, then the two
   daughter cells. Label counts at both stops.
3. (3 pts) Draw or model the same parent cell through meiosis I and
   meiosis II. Label chromosomes per cell after each division.
4. (3 pts) A human cell has 2n = 46. State: chromosomes per daughter
   after mitosis; chromosomes per gamete after meiosis; combinations
   from independent assortment alone (2^23 = 8,388,608).

## Part B — Compare and explain (10 pts)

5. (4 pts) Complete the table for one row you choose *not* to copy from
   Lesson 4: purpose, divisions, daughter cells, chromosome number,
   genetic identity — for mitosis AND meiosis. Then write two sentences
   on why one division cannot do both jobs.
6. (3 pts) Explain nondisjunction with counts: a 2n = 4 parent, one pair
   fails to separate in meiosis I. Give the four gamete counts and the
   zygote count after fertilization by a normal gamete (n = 2).
   (Gametes: 3, 3, 1, 1; zygote from a 3-gamete: 5; from a 1-gamete: 3.
   Either fertilization case earns full marks if the arithmetic is
   shown.)
7. (3 pts) Explain differentiation in a paragraph: same genome, different
   cells. Your paragraph must use "gene expression" correctly and must
   not claim the DNA differs.

## Part C — Defend a claim with data (8 pts)

Use the fictional practice dataset from Lesson 5 (reprinted here in
brief; it is invented data, not real observations):

- A: 100 gametes from a 2n = 4 parent showed all 4 combinations:
  27, 24, 26, 23.
- B: mutant colonies per 10,000 — standard strain 2; weakened-repair
  strain 15 (no UV in either group).
- C: mutant colonies per 10,000 — standard strain without UV 2; with
  UV 47.

8. (8 pts) Write a claim–evidence–reasoning paragraph defending the
   claim: "Heritable variation has more than one source." Your paragraph
   must cite at least one number from each experiment, name all three
   sources, and state one limit of the evidence (for example: the data
   are fictional practice data; experiment C alone does not prove the
   mutations were in gamete-forming cells).

**Scoring:** 27–30 secure; 21–26 mostly secure (re-teach the weakest
objective before Unit 05); below 21, re-teach with the teacher guide's
targeted follow-ups and re-assess the missed objective orally or by
model before moving on.
