# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Cell Cycle, Mitosis, and Meiosis

**Audience:** the guiding adult. Keep this file separate from student
materials; it contains answers and teaching moves, not just summaries.

## Session guidance

- **Sessions 1–4 (cycle, mitosis):** Do not let the centromere rule go
  untaught. Every counting task in the unit depends on it: one
  centromere = one chromosome. Re-teach it the moment a learner counts
  8 "chromosomes" after S phase for a 2n = 4 cell.
- **Session 6 (reference reading):** Read OpenStax §11.1 *with* the
  learner; do not assign it cold. The guide in the resource pack names
  the four stops. If the page fails to load, use the lesson text and the
  stage SVG — the session is not blocked.
- **Sessions 8–9 (modeling):** Check the model before the report: a
  mitosis model whose daughter sets disagree with the count table must
  be rebuilt in Session 15, not explained away. Craft quality is never
  scored.
- **Session 17 (midyear review):** Retrieve U01 (fair investigation,
  CER), U02 (membrane transport — ask: "Why does a dividing cell still
  need its membrane to choose?"), and U03 (energy transformations —
  ask: "Where does a dividing cell's energy come from?"). Spend the
  session on the weakest objective from the quiz, not on re-covering
  everything equally.
- **Session 18 (flex):** Catch-up first. Extension only if the quiz was
  18+/20: the Lesson 2 powers-of-2 extension, the Lesson 3
  nondisjunction preview worked fully, or the Lesson 6 stem-cell
  paragraph.

## Misconceptions and responses

| Learner says | Response |
|---|---|
| "Chromosomes double in S phase." | Count centromeres: 4 chromosomes, 8 chromatids. The DNA amount doubles; the chromosome count does not. |
| "Meiosis is mitosis twice." | Mitosis twice would give 4 cells with 2n each. Meiosis gives 4 cells with n each, because there is one S phase and two divisions. |
| "Homologs and sisters are the same thing." | Homologs = the pair (one from each parent). Sisters = the copies made in S phase. Meiosis I separates the pair; meiosis II separates the copies. |
| "Mutations are always harmful." | Most are neutral; some harmful; a few useful in context. The unit's claim is about variation, not benefit. |
| "A skin-cell mutation is passed to children." | Heritable means present in a gamete or its cell line. Body-cell mutations are not inherited, however they arose. |
| "Cancer is one mutation." | Typically several control failures accumulated over time. Keep it conceptual; no personal health discussion. |
| "The fictional dataset proves the real claim." | It proves the learner can *defend* a claim from data. Real claims need real, replicated data — say so explicitly. |

## Readiness and reteaching

- **Ready for Unit 05** if the learner can: order the cycle; count
  2n = 4 through mitosis and meiosis without the SVG; compute 2^n for
  n = 2, 3, 4, 23; and defend a three-source variation claim with at
  least one number per source.
- **Reteach objective 1–2** with: the centromere rule + Figure 1
  counting + Practice B, then a fresh 2n = 6 count from scratch.
- **Reteach objective 3** with: the pipe-cleaner model run twice —
  once narrating "pair apart, then copies apart" — plus the coin
  lottery for 2n = 4.
- **Reteach objective 4** with: the CER frame ("numbers, not
  adjectives") and one experiment at a time; do not re-teach all three
  sources in one sitting.
- **Reteach objective 5** with: the library analogy, then a restatement
  without the analogy ("which genes are on").

## Safety

No living material, microscope, chemical, or sharp tool is required.
Pipe cleaners and beads are the only manipulatives; check for latex or
material sensitivities and substitute drawings if needed. The cancer
segment is conceptual only: do not ask for, record, or discuss any
personal or family health information. If a learner raises a personal
concern, listen briefly and refer to a trusted adult or clinician.

The [answer key](answer-key.md) solves every lesson item, practice
