# Unit 04 — Cell Cycle: Mitosis and Meiosis

**Grade:** 9 · **Subject:** Science (Biology pathway) · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #43](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/43), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

One cell becomes two identical cells, or four varied ones — and that
difference explains growth, healing, and why siblings are never genetic
copies. In this unit the learner models the cell cycle from growth through
DNA copying to division, acts out mitosis and meiosis with pipe-cleaner
chromosomes, counts chromosomes at every step, and defends a claim about
where heritable variation comes from using data. The unit closes with a
midyear review that retrieves the investigation, membrane, and energy
moves from Units 01–03.

## Essential question

How does one cell produce identical copies for growth and repair, and
varied cells for reproduction — without ever losing or doubling the
instructions?

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–03 and the diagnostic weeks:

- Planning a fair investigation: testable question, variables, control or
  comparison, repeated trials, precise recording (U01, Lessons 1–2)
- Claim–evidence–reasoning explanations; separating observation from
  inference (U01, Lesson 2)
- Cell structures and their jobs, especially the nucleus as the
  instruction vault and DNA as instructions (U02, Lesson 1)
- Chromosomes as packaged DNA; genes as instruction segments (diagnostic;
  re-taught in use, not assumed fluent)
- Powers of 2 and simple ratios for chromosome-combination counting
  (2^2 = 4; 2^23 = 8,388,608)
- Reading tables and bar graphs

This unit does not assume the stages of mitosis or meiosis, the
difference between homologous chromosomes and sister chromatids, or the
sources of heritable variation — those are the unit's new content. Stage
names are taught as a *model* for reasoning, not as a memorization list:
the NGSS assessment boundaries for this unit's standards exclude rote
memorization of mitosis steps and of meiosis phases.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Model the cell cycle — interphase (G1, S, G2), the mitotic phase, and
   checkpoints, including G0 — and explain why DNA must be copied in S
   phase before any division.
2. Model mitosis stage by stage and predict chromosome numbers: one
   diploid (2n) parent cell produces two genetically identical diploid
   daughter cells.
3. Model meiosis — one round of DNA replication followed by two
   divisions, with crossing over and independent assortment — and predict
   that one diploid parent cell produces four genetically varied haploid
   (n) cells; compute 2^n possible chromosome combinations for n pairs.
4. Make and defend an evidence-based claim that inheritable genetic
   variation may result from new meiotic combinations, viable replication
   errors, and/or environment-caused mutations, using data.
5. Explain differentiation — one genome, many cell types through
   different gene expression — and describe, at a conceptual level, what
   happens when cell-division control fails.

## Materials

Notebook, pencils, ruler, index cards for vocabulary, printed copies of
the generated image and the stage SVG, pipe cleaners in two colors (one
color per homologous pair; two lengths or a bead per chromatid pair are
optional), beads or small stickers for centromeres, coins or tokens for
the combination-counting game, and the fictional dataset handout
(Lesson 5). No purchases beyond ordinary craft supplies are required; no
living material, microscope, or hazardous substance is used. The
observation alternative for every modeling step is the labeled SVG and
the OpenStax reference reading — no learner is required to handle any
biological specimen.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts written, oral, typed, drawn, or modeled responses; the
adult scribes when writing stamina lags. Diagrams are labeled with text
and arrows, never color alone (the stage SVG uses text labels for every
part and every count; the generated image ships with a full text
alternative). Sessions are about 50 minutes with a movement break.
Vocabulary is taught with models and objects first, word second;
home-language labels are welcomed alongside English terms. Every modeling
task also has a text-and-table equivalent. Fluency work is
strategy-based, never timed. Cancer is discussed only as a conceptual
example of control failure; no personal or family medical content is
asked for or recorded.
