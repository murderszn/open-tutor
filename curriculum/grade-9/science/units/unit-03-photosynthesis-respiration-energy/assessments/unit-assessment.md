# Unit Assessment — Unit 03: Photosynthesis, Respiration, and Energy Transformations

**Time:** about 50 minutes · **Adult scores from the
[answer key](../answer-key.md).** Show your working for every
calculation; a bare number earns partial credit only. Total: 40 points
as marked.

## 1. Two equations (6 points)

(a) Balance the photosynthesis equation: CO₂ + H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂.
Show the C, H, and O counts on each side. (3 points)
(b) Balance the cellular respiration equation: C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O.
Show the C, H, and O counts on each side. (3 points)

## 2. Chloroplast structures (4 points)

Match each structure to its job (write the letter, then the job in your
own words for full marks): mesophyll, stomata, guard cell, chloroplast,
stroma, thylakoid, chlorophyll. (Four of the seven are scored — the
adult picks any four; each correct match with a correct job is 1 point.)

## 3. Stomata close (3 points)

On a hot, dry day a plant closes its stomata. Predict what happens to
photosynthesis and explain the chain of causes in at least three steps.

## 4. Respiration is not breathing (3 points)

A learner says plants do not respire because they do not breathe.
Correct the claim in three sentences: define breathing, define cellular
respiration, and state whether plants respire (with where it happens).

## 5. Bond bookkeeping (3 points)

A teaching model (fictional numbers) says breaking the bonds in one
glucose and its oxygen costs 200 energy units, while forming the bonds
in carbon dioxide and water releases 260 units. Compute the net transfer
and explain what its sign means for the cell.

## 6. Yeast data (5 points)

A class measures balloon circumference (fictional practice data):

| Setup | 0 min | 10 min | 20 min | 30 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: warm + sugar + yeast | 0 cm | 15 cm | 30 cm | 44 cm |
| B: warm + yeast, no sugar | 0 cm | 0 cm | 1 cm | 1 cm |
| C: cold + sugar + yeast | 0 cm | 4 cm | 8 cm | 11 cm |

(a) Compute the average rate for A and for C over 30 minutes, showing
each division. (2 points)
(b) How many times faster is A than C? Show the division. (1 point)
(c) Which comparison proves warmth matters, and what is held constant in
it? (2 points)

## 7. Leaf-disk analysis (5 points)

A learner's leaf-disk counts (disks floating of 10):

| Cup | 0 min | 5 min | 10 min | 15 min | 20 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light + baking-soda solution | 0 | 2 | 6 | 9 | 10 |
| Dark + baking-soda solution | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Light + plain water | 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 2 |

(a) Estimate the time for half the disks (5 of 10) to float in the test
cup, by interpolation. Show the calculation. (2 points)
(b) State what the dark cup proves and what the plain-water cup proves,
one sentence each. (2 points)
(c) Name one limitation of floating as a measure of photosynthesis.
(1 point)

## 8. Carbon trace (3 points)

Trace one carbon atom from atmospheric carbon dioxide into a tree, into
a deer that eats the tree's leaves, and back to the atmosphere. Name the
process at each transfer.

## 9. Energy budget (4 points)

A meadow study (fictional practice data): producers capture 6,000 energy
units; herbivores gain 540; carnivores gain 65; top predators gain 6.
(a) Compute the percentage reaching each level (each as a percentage of
the level below it). (3 points)
(b) Is the rough one-tenth rule supported or refuted? Defend your verdict
in one sentence. (1 point)

## 10. Claim evaluation (2 points)

Evaluate this claim: "Energy is recycled like matter, because animals
give off heat and plants absorb it." Say whether it is true or false and
give the decisive reason.

## 11. Model choice (2 points)

For each question, name the best model (food chain, food web, or cycle
diagram) and say one thing it shows: (a) "How much usable energy reaches
a wolf?" (b) "Where does the carbon in a fallen log go?"

**Scoring:** 34–40 secure; 26–33 developing (re-teach missed objectives
with the teacher guide before Unit 04); below 26 re-teach the unit's
weakest objective and re-assess with new numbers before moving on.
Scores measure this unit only; they are not a course grade.
