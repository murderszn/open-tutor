# Practice Sessions — Unit 03

Four named sessions, referenced in the [20-session pacing](README.md#20-session-pacing).
Each session is about 50 minutes. Solutions are in the
[answer key](answer-key.md) — kept separate from student materials.

## Session A — Equation workshop

**Goal:** Balance photosynthesis and respiration equations fluently and
use the repository equation builder as a check, not a crutch.

1. Balance each equation by counting atoms (C, H, O) in a table before
   writing the answer: (a) CO₂ + H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂;
   (b) C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O; (c) a plant builds 4 glucose molecules —
   write the full photosynthesis equation.
2. **Find the error.** A learner balances respiration as
   C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O and then "simplifies" it to
   C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O, saying the ratios are the same. Are they?
   Explain why dropping coefficients breaks the equation even though the
   kinds of molecules match.
3. **Interactive check.** Open the repository's photosynthesis equation
   builder — [`Photosynthesis Equation Builder`](../../../../../assignments/stem/biology/resources/photosynthesis/index.html)
   (previewed 2026-10-26: four coefficient inputs, a Check button that
   accepts only 6, 6, 1, 6, and a Reset button; note that the page shows
   the balanced answer in its "In words" card, so attempt the balance on
   paper first, then use the builder to verify). Try coefficients 6, 6,
   1, 5 and record what the builder says; then try 6, 6, 1, 6. Write one
   sentence explaining why the first attempt fails in atom counts.
4. **Scale it.** A greenhouse plant makes 10 glucose molecules in a day.
   How many CO₂ molecules did it take in, and how many O₂ molecules did
   it release? Show the multiplication.

## Session B — Leaf anatomy and stage sorting

**Goal:** Connect chloroplast structure to the two stages and predict
the effects of stomata and light changes.

1. Label a chloroplast diagram from memory (outer membrane, inner
   membrane, stroma, thylakoid, granum), then check it against Lesson 02
   and correct it in a different color. List every correction you made.
2. **Stage sort, round two.** Assign each statement to stage 1, stage 2,
   or both/neither: (a) "Splits water." (b) "Builds G3P from CO₂."
   (c) "Happens in the stroma." (d) "Makes ATP." (e) "Needs chlorophyll
   directly." (f) "Continues for a while after lights out, then stops."
   (g) "Releases oxygen."
3. **Two-plant prediction.** Plant A sits in bright white light with open
   stomata. Plant B sits in the same light but in air with half the
   usual carbon dioxide. Predict which plant makes more sugar in an
   hour, and name the limiting input for plant B.
4. **Explain your reasoning.** A learner says leaves are green because
   chlorophyll absorbs green light. Write the correction in two
   sentences, using "absorb" and "reflect" correctly.

## Session C — Data lab: yeast and leaf-disk tables

**Goal:** Analyze provided practice data the way the project analyzes
real data — rates, comparisons, and estimates with labeled data status.

Both tables below are **fictional practice data**, invented for this
session. They are not lab results. Your project report must label its own
data as measured, and must never present these tables as measurements.

### Yeast table (from Lesson 04)

| Setup | 0 min | 10 min | 20 min | 30 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: warm + sugar + yeast | 0 cm | 12 cm | 25 cm | 38 cm |
| B: warm + yeast, no sugar | 0 cm | 1 cm | 2 cm | 2 cm |
| C: cold + sugar + yeast | 0 cm | 3 cm | 6 cm | 9 cm |

1. Compute the average rate for each setup over 30 minutes, showing each
   division.
2. Which single comparison proves sugar is needed? Which proves warmth
   matters? Name the variable each comparison isolates.
3. Predict setup A's circumference at 40 minutes if its rate held, and
   explain two reasons a real balloon would fall short of that prediction.

### Leaf-disk table (preview of the project analysis)

Ten disks per cup; counts are disks floating at each time.

| Cup | 0 min | 5 min | 10 min | 15 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light + baking-soda solution | 0 | 3 | 7 | 10 |
| Dark + baking-soda solution | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Light + plain water (no baking soda) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 |

4. Estimate the time for half the disks (5 of 10) to float in the
   light + baking-soda cup, by interpolation between the 5-minute and
   10-minute counts. Show the calculation.
5. What do the two comparison cups prove, one sentence each? (Dark cup:
   light is needed. Plain-water cup: a carbon dioxide source matters.)
6. A learner concludes: "The dark cup proves plants cannot respire in
   the dark." Is that conclusion supported by this table? Explain why
   not (the table measures oxygen production from photosynthesis via
   floating, not respiration — floating disks track photosynthesis, and
   respiration is not measured here at all).

## Session D — Carbon-cycle model build and energy budget

**Goal:** Build a labeled carbon-cycle model and a simple energy budget,
and defend both against a skeptic.

1. Build a carbon-cycle model (drawing or labeled list) with the four
   reservoirs (atmosphere, biosphere, hydrosphere, geosphere) and at
   least six labeled arrows: photosynthesis, respiration, feeding,
   decomposition (aerobic), decomposition (anaerobic, with methane),
   combustion. Add one sentence per arrow naming what moves and why.
2. **Energy budget.** A lake food web (fictional practice data):
   producers capture 8,000 units; herbivores gain 880; small fish gain
   70; large fish gain 8. Compute the percentage reaching each level
   (each as a percentage of the level below it, and large fish as a
   percentage of producers). Is the one-tenth rule roughly supported?
   Defend your verdict with the numbers.
3. **Skeptic round.** Answer each skeptic claim in two sentences:
   (a) "Burning wood destroys its carbon." (b) "Heat from animals
   returns to plants and powers photosynthesis." (c) "If I plant one
   tree, my car's emissions are canceled."
4. **Revise.** Add the anaerobic branch from Lesson 06 to your model, or
   correct one arrow you now think is wrong, and write one sentence
   explaining the change.
