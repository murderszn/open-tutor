# Unit 03 — Photosynthesis: Respiration and Energy Transformations

**Grade:** 9 · **Subject:** Science (Biology pathway) · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #43](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/43), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A plant does not eat soil, and it does not create energy. In this unit the
learner builds the two great matter-and-energy transformations of biology —
photosynthesis, which stores light energy in sugar, and cellular
respiration, which releases that stored energy for work — balances both
equations atom by atom, tests light and carbon dioxide with floating leaf
disks, and then follows one carbon atom and one packet of energy through
food webs and the carbon cycle to see why matter cycles while energy flows
one way and leaves as heat. The unit closes with a fair leaf-disk
investigation the learner designs, runs, and defends with evidence.

## Essential question

Where does a plant's mass come from, where does its energy go, and why can
matter be reused forever while energy cannot?

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–02 and the diagnostic weeks:

- Planning a fair investigation: testable question, independent/dependent/
  controlled variables, control or comparison, repeated trials, precise
  recording (U01, Lessons 1–2; U02 investigation)
- Claim–evidence–reasoning explanations; separating observation from
  inference (U01, Lesson 2)
- Biomolecule basics: sugars are built from carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen
  and can combine with other elements into larger molecules (U01)
- Cell structures: chloroplasts (plants and algae) and mitochondria (all
  cells); plant vs. animal cell differences (U02, Lessons 1–2)
- Percent change, simple ratios, reading tables and bar graphs
- Balancing simple equations is **not** assumed — Lesson 1 teaches it from
  atom counting.

This unit does not assume the photosynthesis or respiration equations,
chloroplast structure, aerobic/anaerobic conditions, or the carbon cycle —
those are the unit's new content.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Model photosynthesis as the transformation of light energy into stored
   chemical energy: name its matter inputs (carbon dioxide, water) and
   outputs (sugar, oxygen) and write and balance its equation.
2. Explain how photosynthesis happens in two stages — light-dependent
   reactions in the thylakoid membrane and the Calvin cycle in the stroma —
   naming the roles of chlorophyll, stomata, and chloroplast structures,
   and predict how light or stomata changes affect the process.
3. Model cellular respiration as a chemical process in which bonds of food
   and oxygen molecules break and new compounds form with a net transfer
   of energy; write and balance its equation and contrast it with
   photosynthesis.
4. Compare aerobic and anaerobic conditions using evidence: name the
   products of aerobic respiration and of fermentation (yeast: carbon
   dioxide and ethanol; muscle: lactate), and explain why anaerobic
   breakdown releases less usable energy.
5. Trace matter (carbon) through photosynthesis, respiration, feeding,
   decomposition, and combustion across the biosphere, atmosphere,
   hydrosphere, and geosphere; distinguish matter cycling from one-way
   energy flow using a simple energy budget.
6. Plan and carry out a fair leaf-disk investigation with defined
   variables, a control and a comparison, and repeated trials; use the
   evidence to support, revise, or reject a claim about light and carbon
   dioxide supply.

## Materials

Notebook, pencils, ruler, index cards for vocabulary, printed copy of
[`assets/sunlit-plant-inputs.png`](assets/sunlit-plant-inputs.png)
(the generated observation image) and of
[`assets/energy-matter-cycle.svg`](assets/energy-matter-cycle.svg)
(the deterministic cycle diagram). For the investigation
(adult-supervised): fresh spinach or similar leaves, a hole puncher,
a small syringe without a needle (adult operates), dilute baking-soda
solution (adult measures), three clear cups, water, a bright lamp
(LED preferred so disks do not overheat), a timer, and a notebook data
table. The adult handles the syringe and checks lamp heat; nothing is
tasted; hands are washed after handling leaves and solutions. Every
investigation step lists an observation-only or provided-data alternative
when materials, hazards, or access make the hands-on version unsuitable.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts written, oral, typed, drawn, or modeled responses; the
adult scribes when writing stamina lags. Diagrams are labeled with text and
arrows, never color alone (the cycle SVG uses text labels for every part and
every arrow; the generated image ships with a full text alternative).
Sessions are about 50 minutes with a movement break. Vocabulary is taught
with models and objects first, word second; home-language labels are
welcomed alongside English terms. Every simulation or interactive task also
has a text-and-table equivalent. Fluency work is strategy-based, never
timed.
