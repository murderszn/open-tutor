# Teacher Guide — Unit 03

**Audience:** the guiding adult. Student materials never contain these
solutions; the [answer key](answer-key.md) holds them separately.

## Session-by-session guidance

- **Session 1 (L1):** Spend the time on atom counting, not on memorizing
  the equation. Learners who can build the table (C/H/O, left/right) can
  rebuild the equation any time. Do not let a learner "simplify" away
  coefficients (see Practice A, question 2).
- **Session 2 (Practice A):** Let the learner attempt the balance on
  paper before opening the equation builder — the builder's page shows
  the answer. If the learner is stuck, go back to the Lesson 1 table,
  not to the builder.
- **Session 3 (L2):** Drill "stoma" vs. "stroma" aloud. The green-light
  prediction (Lesson 2, guided question 3) is the best quick check of
  real understanding: learners who say "green light is absorbed most"
  have the pigment story backwards.
- **Session 4 (Practice B):** The two-plant prediction is a pre-test for
  the project: learners who cannot name the limiting input will
  struggle to justify the project's controls.
- **Session 5 (L3):** The night-shift question is the unit's hinge. If a
  learner insists plants do not respire, stop and re-teach Lesson 3
  before Lesson 4 — everything after it assumes both processes run.
- **Session 6 (Reference):** Use the resource pack's three questions.
  Accept spoken answers. If the page is unreachable, Lesson 02's teaching
  section is the fallback text.
- **Session 7 (L4):** All yeast numbers are labeled fictional practice
  data. Do not let the learner copy them into a lab report as
  measurements — the project report's honesty rule depends on this
  distinction.
- **Sessions 8–9 (Lab):** The adult operates the syringe and checks lamp
  heat. If cups warm noticeably, record it as a confound, move the lamp,
  and continue — do not hide it. A failed run (disks never float) is
  still reportable: the conclusion becomes "no difference detected under
  these conditions," with a named suspect (old leaves, weak lamp, poor
  infiltration).
- **Session 10 (L5):** Use a token to trace the carbon atom physically
  before asking for the written trace.
- **Session 11 (Practice C):** The interpolation method (Session C,
  question 4) is the project's analysis method — check it here, not in
  the report.
- **Session 12 (L6):** The model-choice habit is the transfer skill for
  Unit 07 (ecology). Insist on "shows/hides" for every model named.
- **Sessions 13–15 (Practice D, report, revision):** Critique against
  the project rubric, not against neatness. A slow result honestly
  reported outscores a fast result faked.
- **Sessions 16–20:** Quiz, re-teach, presentations, flex, assessment.
  Use flex time for the weakest objective, not for new content.

## Misconceptions and reteaching moves

| Misconception | Reteach move |
|---|---|
| Plants gain mass from soil | Van Helmont story (Lesson 5): tree gained mass, soil lost almost none; mass comes from air (CO₂) and water |
| Oxygen is the purpose of photosynthesis | Water-splitting story (Lesson 2): oxygen is released when water is split for stage 1; sugar is the stored product |
| Energy is created by plants | Transformation language (Lessons 1, 6): light → chemical; nothing created; heat is the one-way exit |
| Respiration = breathing; plants do not respire | Card sort (Lesson 3): breathing moves gases; respiration is chemistry in cells; plants respire day and night |
| Green light is absorbed most | Pigment logic (Lesson 2): leaves look green because green is reflected, not absorbed |
| The Calvin cycle needs no light at all | Dependency chain (Lesson 2): stage 2 spends stage 1's ATP/NADPH; it stops soon after lights out |
| Lactate causes next-day soreness | Clearance fact (Lesson 4): lactate clears within about an hour; soreness is microdamage and repair |
| Energy is recycled like matter | Heat test (Lessons 5–6): plants cannot run photosynthesis on heat; usable energy leaves as heat |
| The one-tenth rule is a law | Measured percentages (Lesson 6, Practice D): values bracket 10% but vary; it is a rough average |
| Floating disks measure sugar directly | Indirect-measure talk (project): floating tracks oxygen accumulation, a stand-in for the photosynthesis rate |

## Readiness criteria (what "secure" looks like per objective)

1. Balances the photosynthesis equation from atom counts without
   prompting, and states the energy transformation in one sentence.
2. Names both stages, their locations, and their products, and predicts
   a stomata or light change correctly with a cause chain.
3. Balances the respiration equation, explains net energy from bond
   breaking/forming, and contrasts it with photosynthesis on at least
   two points besides direction.
4. Names aerobic and fermentation products correctly and uses a data
   table to isolate a variable (sugar or temperature).
5. Traces a carbon atom through at least three transfers with correct
   processes, and explains matter cycling vs. energy flow without
   claiming energy is recycled.
6. Designs or reports a fair leaf-disk investigation with justified
   controls, a complete data table, an interpolated half-float estimate,
   and at least two honest limitations.

If any criterion is unmet after Session 20, re-teach that objective
with its lesson and practice session before Unit 04 — do not carry the
gap into mitosis/meiosis, which assumes energy and matter fluency.

## Safety (adult responsibilities)

- Operate the syringe; keep it away from learners when not in use.
- Check lamp heat before and during the lab; prefer LED; stop if cups
  warm noticeably.
- Nothing is tasted. Leaves, solutions, and cups are not food.
- Wash hands after handling leaves and solutions; wipe spills at once.
- Yeast work in this unit is data analysis of provided tables (and, if
  the adult runs a demonstration, adult-only handling). No learner
  cultures unknown microbes; no tasting of fermentation products.
- Seated-table and observation-only alternatives are in the project
  file; use them without penalty whenever materials, hazards, or access
  require it.

## Accessibility notes

- Every diagram ships with a text alternative; color is never the only
  cue (all SVG labels are text; the generated image's activity works
  from its text alternative alone).
- Responses may be written, oral, typed, drawn, or modeled; scribe when
  writing stamina lags.
- Calculations accept a calculator; show the setup (division or
  multiplication), not just the answer.
- Sessions are about 50 minutes with a movement break; the lab may be
  split across Sessions 8–9 without penalty.
- The equation builder and any video are adult-previewed; if a video's
  captions are missing, use the lesson text instead.
