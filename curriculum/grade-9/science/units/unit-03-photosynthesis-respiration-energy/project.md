# Project — The Leaf-Disk Investigation

**Question:** Do light and a carbon dioxide supply change how fast leaf
disks produce oxygen?

**Deliverable:** A written investigation report (claim, method, data
table, analysis, conclusion, limitations) plus a short presentation of
the carbon-cycle model revision from Lesson 06. **Adult-supervised
throughout.** Nothing is tasted; hands are washed after handling leaves
and solutions.

## Background

When leaf disks are infiltrated with a baking-soda solution, they sink
because the air spaces in the leaf fill with liquid. As photosynthesis
produces oxygen inside the leaf, the oxygen collects in those spaces and
the disks become buoyant and float. Faster floating means faster oxygen
production — a visible stand-in for the photosynthesis rate. This is an
indirect measure: floating tracks oxygen accumulation, not sugar
directly, and the report must say so.

## Materials (adult measures and supervises)

- Fresh spinach or similar leaves (dark green, healthy), a hole puncher
- A small syringe without a needle (adult operates only)
- Dilute baking-soda solution: about 1/8 teaspoon of baking soda in a
  cup of water (adult measures); plain water for the comparison cup
- Three clear cups, a bright lamp (LED preferred so disks do not
  overheat; the adult checks that the lamp does not warm the cups
  noticeably), a timer, a notebook data table
- **Alternative (no materials, hazard, or access):** analyze the
  provided practice table in [Session C](practice.md#session-c-data-lab-yeast-and-leaf-disk-tables)
  instead of running the lab, and write the same report — but label it
  clearly as "analysis of provided practice data," never as your own
  experiment. An observation-only alternative: watch a classroom video
  of leaf-disk flotation (adult picks one from the
  [resource pack](resource-pack.md)) and record the same counts from
  the video, labeled as observed, not measured.

## Method

1. **Question and prediction.** Write the question above in your own
   words. Predict: which cup's disks will float fastest, and why?
   Name the two variables being tested (light, carbon dioxide supply).
2. **Variables.** Independent variables: light (light vs. dark) and
   solution (baking-soda solution vs. plain water). Dependent variable:
   number of disks floating at each time, and time for half to float.
   Controlled variables: same leaf type, same disk size, same number of
   disks (10 per cup), same water volume, same distance from the lamp,
   same timing.
3. **Controls and comparisons.** Cup 1 (test): light + baking-soda
   solution. Cup 2 (dark control): dark + baking-soda solution —
   everything the same except light. Cup 3 (comparison): light + plain
   water — everything the same except the carbon dioxide supply.
   Without cups 2 and 3, a fast float in cup 1 would prove nothing.
4. **Procedure (adult-supervised).**
   - Punch 10 disks per cup from healthy leaves; avoid major veins.
   - The adult infiltrates the disks with solution using the syringe
     (draw the disks and solution in, cover the tip, pull gently to
     remove air, release — repeat until disks sink). The learner never
     operates the syringe.
   - Place cup 1 and cup 3 under the lamp at equal distances; place
     cup 2 in darkness (covered box) at the same temperature.
   - Start the timer. Every 5 minutes for 20 minutes, count floating
     disks in each cup and record them. Do not move the cups while
     counting.
   - Repeat the whole run once more if time allows (repeated trials),
     or pool counts with the adult's parallel run.
5. **Safety.** The adult operates the syringe and checks lamp heat;
   leaves and solutions are not tasted; spills are wiped at once; hands
   are washed at the end. If a cup warms noticeably, stop, move the
   lamp farther away, and note the change — heat is a confounding
   variable, not a detail to hide.

## Leaf-disk lab: setup and first counts

Sessions 8–9 run the procedure above. Session 8: prediction, variables,
setup, first two counts (0 and 5 minutes). Session 9: remaining counts

table. Record counts as numbers, not impressions ("7 floating" beats
"most of them").

## Analysis

1. For the test cup, estimate the time for half the disks (5 of 10) to
   float, by interpolation between the two counts that bracket 5 — the
   same method as Session C, question 4.
2. Compare the three cups at 20 minutes. State what each comparison
   proves and what it cannot prove.
3. Write a claim–evidence–reasoning conclusion: claim (do light and
   carbon dioxide supply change the rate?), evidence (the counts and
   the half-float time), reasoning (oxygen from split water makes disks
   buoyant; no light means no water-splitting; plain water supplies
   little carbon dioxide for the Calvin cycle).
4. **Limitations.** Name at least two: floating is indirect (it measures
   oxygen accumulation, not sugar); disk size, leaf age, and lamp heat
   vary; counts are whole disks, so small rate differences are invisible.
   State what you would change in a second run.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Full marks (3) | Partial (2) | Needs work (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question, variables, controls | Testable question; both variables, dependent variable, and controls named; dark and plain-water comparisons justified | Most variables named; one comparison unjustified | Question vague; variables or controls missing |
| Data precision | Complete table, counts at every time, repeated run or pooled counts, units/times labeled | Table mostly complete; one gap | Impressions instead of counts; missing times |
| Analysis | Half-float time estimated with shown interpolation; cup comparisons stated correctly | Estimate given without working, or one comparison wrong | No estimate; comparisons missing |
| Explanation (CER) | Claim matches evidence; reasoning uses water-splitting and the Calvin cycle correctly | Claim roughly matches; reasoning thin | Claim contradicts evidence; reasoning wrong |
| Limitations and safety | Two or more real limitations; safety steps followed and noted; alternative labeled if used | One limitation; safety noted briefly | No limitations; safety ignored or alternative mislabeled |
| Presentation and model | Clear presentation; revised model includes the anaerobic branch with correct labels | Presentation mostly clear; model branch incomplete | Unclear; model unrevised |

**Scoring:** 18 = exemplary; 14–17 = secure; 10–13 = developing (re-teach
the weakest criterion before Unit 04); below 10 = re-run with adult
support. The rubric measures the investigation, not the plant: a slow
result honestly reported scores higher than a fast result faked.
