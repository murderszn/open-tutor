# Grade 9 Science

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are
delivered; the eight units are planned, not yet written. See
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained
for one grade-9 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 9 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-9 biology course: scientific investigation
and evidence; biomolecules and how organisms build large carbon-based
molecules; cell structure, membranes, and transport; photosynthesis and
cellular respiration as matter-and-energy transformations; the cell cycle,
mitosis, and meiosis; DNA, proteins, inheritance, and gene expression;
evolution, population genetics, and the evidence for common ancestry; ecology —
populations, carrying capacity, matter cycling, energy flow, ecosystem
resilience, and human impacts; and human body systems with feedback mechanisms
that maintain homeostasis. Sessions are about 50 minutes, five per week,
blending modeling and explanation with hands-on investigation (safe,
adult-supervised, kitchen- and household-scale materials), data analysis with
spreadsheets or free browser tools, and independent written practice that the
adult reviews the same day.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Plan and carry out investigations with defined variables, controls, and
   repeated trials; record data precisely; and use evidence to support, revise,
   or reject a claim about a biological phenomenon.
2. Model how the four biomolecule classes (carbohydrates, lipids, proteins,
   nucleic acids) are assembled from and broken down into atoms (primarily C,
   H, O, N, P); explain with evidence how sugars combine with other elements
   to form amino acids and other large carbon-based molecules.
3. Model cell structures and explain how specialized parts (nucleus, ribosomes,
   endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, mitochondria, chloroplasts, cell
   wall, vacuole, cell membrane) carry out the cell's essential functions;
   compare plant and animal cells and explain what each difference makes
   possible.
4. Explain and model selective permeability of cell membranes: predict the
   direction of diffusion and osmosis from concentration differences and
   explain when cells use active transport.
5. Model photosynthesis as the transformation of light energy into stored
   chemical energy (inputs: carbon dioxide and water; outputs: sugars and
   oxygen) and cellular respiration as a chemical process that breaks bonds in
   food and oxygen molecules and forms new compounds with a net transfer of
   energy; trace matter and energy through both processes.
6. Model mitosis and meiosis; explain the role of mitotic division and
   differentiation in producing and maintaining a complex organism; and defend
   a claim, with evidence, that heritable variation comes from new genetic
   combinations through meiosis, viable replication errors, and
   environment-caused mutations.
7. Explain how DNA structure determines protein structure and function through
   systems of specialized cells; clarify, with models, how DNA and chromosomes
   code the instructions for inherited traits; and apply probability to
   explain the variation and distribution of traits in a population.
8. Communicate scientific information, in more than one format, showing that
   common ancestry and biological evolution are supported by multiple lines of
   empirical evidence (DNA sequences, anatomy, embryology, fossils).
9. Explain evolution as the interaction of four factors — potential for
   population increase, heritable variation from mutation and sexual
   reproduction, competition for limited resources, and differential survival
   and reproduction; and use data to show how natural selection shifts trait
   distributions and adapts populations.
10. Use mathematical and graphical representations to explain factors affecting
    carrying capacity, biodiversity, and populations; support claims about the
    cycling of matter and flow of energy among organisms; and model the role
    of photosynthesis and respiration in the carbon cycle across the
    biosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, and geosphere.
11. Evaluate claims, evidence, and reasoning about ecosystem stability and
    resilience; evaluate evidence for the role of group behavior in survival
    and reproduction; and design, evaluate, refine, and simulate solutions
    that reduce human impacts on biodiversity.
12. Plan and conduct an investigation that provides evidence that feedback
    mechanisms maintain homeostasis in a living system.
13. Develop and use models of the hierarchical organization of multicellular
    life — cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism — and explain how
    interacting systems perform the organism's essential functions.

## Standards reference

Objectives reference the Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS) high-school
life-science performance expectations, verified against official-text
transcriptions 2026-10-06 (not a claim of state adoption, accreditation, or
alignment certification). Full crosswalk in
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md#4-standards-crosswalk):

- From Molecules to Organisms: Structures and Processes — HS-LS1-1 through
  HS-LS1-7
- Ecosystems: Interactions, Energy, and Dynamics — HS-LS2-1 through HS-LS2-8
- Heredity: Inheritance and Variation of Traits — HS-LS3-1 through HS-LS3-3
- Biological Evolution: Unity and Diversity — HS-LS4-1 through HS-LS4-6
- Secondary engineering-design connections — HS-LS2-7, HS-LS4-6 (design and
  simulation of biodiversity-impact solutions)

## Structure

- [Scope and sequence](scope-and-sequence.md) — audit, standards crosswalk,
  36-week pacing with weekly goals, session model, materials, accessibility.
- `units/` — eight units, planned (not yet written):
  1. Biological investigation: evidence and biomolecules
  2. Cells: membranes and transport
  3. Photosynthesis: respiration and energy transformations
  4. Cell cycle: mitosis and meiosis
  5. Inheritance: molecular genetics and gene expression
  6. Evolution: population genetics and evidence
  7. Ecology: ecosystems and matter cycling
  8. Human systems: homeostasis and biology capstone

## For the guiding adult

Grade-9 biology turns middle-grade familiarity with cells, ecosystems, and
heredity into evidence-based explanation and modeling: learners read and build
models of molecules, cells, and ecosystems, run safe household-scale
investigations with controlled variables, and argue from data. Learners enter
expected to know basic cell parts, that plants make food, and that traits are
inherited; the Weeks 1–2 diagnostic verifies this, and the track re-teaches
insecure skills in use rather than assuming them. Read the scope-and-sequence
before starting; it lists the diagnostic weeks, the midyear and final review
weeks, safe materials (grocery-store and household items, prepared slides or a
low-cost microscope, free no-account browser simulations), and accessibility
supports.

Until units are written, there is nothing here to assign yet. Every unit will
add fully written lessons with worked examples, an investigation or project,
separate teacher guides and answer keys, a verified Resource Pack, and a
generated teaching image with alt text, caption, and text alternative.
Investigations are adult-supervised; simulation or observation alternatives are
provided wherever materials, hazards, or access make hands-on work unsuitable.
