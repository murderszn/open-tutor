# Project — The Medieval World in Maps

**Unit:** [U01 — Medieval Geography: Sources and Historical Inquiry](README.md)
**Sessions:** 12 (draft), 14 (finish), 16 (present)

## The task

Build an **annotated map of Afro-Eurasia (circa 1200 CE)** showing the
four trade corridors, plus a **source log** proving that every major
feature on your map comes from a real source kind — not from imagination.

This is what historians actually do: every line on a historical map should
be traceable to evidence. Your map will be judged on that traceability,
not on artistic skill.

## Materials

- Large paper or poster board (or a digital drawing tool the adult
  approves), pencils, ruler, colored pencils
- The corridor schematic ([assets/trade-corridors-schematic.svg](assets/trade-corridors-schematic.svg))
  as a *starting reference only* — your map must add what the schematic
  deliberately leaves out
- Inquiry notebook (for the source log draft)
- `resources/un_countries.csv` for the modern-country column (optional)

## Steps

1. **Frame (Session 12, 15 min).** Write your compelling question for the
   map at the top of the source log. Example: *"How were the four
   medieval trade corridors connected around 1200 CE?"* Your map must
   answer it; anything that doesn't serve the question stays off.
2. **Draw the base (Session 12, 30 min).** Sketch Afro-Eurasia in rough
   outline — accuracy of *connections* matters more than coastlines. Add:
   title, key/legend, compass rose, scale bar, and grid. Label all four
   corridors and at least **8 cities** (two per corridor minimum).
3. **Annotate (Session 12 homework or Session 14, 20 min).** Add at least
   **4 annotations** — short notes ON the map explaining *why* a feature
   matters. Example: next to Timbuktu: *"Salt arrived here by camel
   caravan; gold left the same way — the desert crossing made both
   precious."*
4. **Source log (Session 14, 25 min).** For **6 features** on your map
   (at least one per corridor, and at least 3 different source kinds
   total), complete one log row each:

   | Map feature | Source kind | What the source says (1 sentence) | Limitation (1 sentence) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | e.g., Samarkand on the Silk Road | Traveler's account (classroom model, L04) | The merchant's note lists paper bought in Dunhuang for Samarkand's market. | One viewpoint; prices unchecked. |

   Every row must name a **real source kind** (map, traveler's account,
   artifact, coin, chronicle, secondary interpretation) — "I imagined it"
   scores zero. Classroom model texts from Lessons 02–04 may be cited as
   *"classroom model (kind)"*; the generated market illustration may
   appear ONLY as a labeled *"modern reconstruction — not evidence."*
5. **Self-check (Session 14, 10 min).** Answer in the log: (a) one thing
   your map PROVES, (b) one thing it CANNOT prove, (c) one question your
   map raises that you can't answer yet.
6. **Present (Session 16, 5 min each).** Present the map and walk through
   two source-log rows. The adult asks one "prove it" question; answer by
   pointing to the log row.

## Deliverables

1. The annotated map (title, key, compass rose, scale bar, grid, 4
   corridors, 8+ cities, 4+ annotations).
2. The source log (6 rows; ≥1 per corridor; ≥3 source kinds).
3. The self-check (3 answers).

## Rubric (adult scores)

| Criterion | 3 — strong | 2 — developing | 1 — starting | 0 — missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Map tools | All six tools present and correct | Five tools; minor error in one | Two+ tools missing/wrong | Not a readable map |
| Corridor accuracy | 4 corridors correctly routed with 8+ cities placed on the right corridors | 4 corridors; 1–2 cities misplaced | A corridor missing or misrouted | Corridors absent |
| Annotations | 4+ annotations explaining *why* features matter (trade, geography, connection) | 4 annotations but descriptive only ("this is X") | Fewer than 4 | None |
| Source log | 6 rows; ≥3 kinds; each limitation honest and specific | 6 rows; 2 kinds or one vague limitation | Fewer rows/kinds; limitations missing | No log |
| Self-check & presentation | (a)(b)(c) answered precisely; "prove it" answered from the log | Two of three precise; proof attempted | Vague answers | Missing |

**Score guide:** 12–15 = portfolio-ready; 8–11 = revise the weakest row
with the adult; below 8 = rebuild the log with the adult modeling two
rows. The project is the unit's applied performance evidence for
Objectives 2, 3, and 4.
