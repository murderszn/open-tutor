# Unit 01 — Medieval Geography: Sources and Historical Inquiry

**Grade:** 7 · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track issue:** [#37](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/37)
**Status:** validated draft — delivered 2026-10-13; not yet educator-reviewed or merged.
**Weeks:** 3–6 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 45 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

Before studying any medieval society, a historian needs a toolkit: how to ask
questions that history can actually answer, how to tell a real historical
source from a modern reconstruction, how to read and build a **map** as
evidence, and how to judge whether a source is trustworthy. This unit builds
that toolkit on medieval material. Every later unit in the track reuses it:
framing questions about Byzantine, Islamic, African, Asian, European, and
American societies (U02–U08), reading maps of trade corridors and regions
(U02–U06), classifying and evaluating medieval sources (U02, U05), and
weighing change against continuity (U07–U08).

The unit rebuilds three pieces of the legacy track library, as the A00 audit
required: the city-planning design lab becomes the Session 6 map-construction
studio with a map-building frame and rubric (Practice Set C); the capitals,
oceans/seas, and chokepoints quizzes become the review-week formative check
with corrected guide-section references and answer keys
([assessments/week-04_quiz.md](assessments/week-04_quiz.md)); and
`resources/un_countries.csv` supplies the "where in the modern world?" mapping
task (Practice Set D). The two quizzes the audit flagged as unassignable
(`weight-distance-quiz.md`, `global-energy-precious-metals-quiz.md`) are not
used here; the volatile "fraction of the world's oil" chokepoint item is
replaced with a stable "why chokepoints matter" reasoning item, per the audit.

## Prerequisites (re-taught here as needed)

- Reading a simple map: title, key/legend, compass rose, cardinal directions
  (grade-6 entry skill; re-taught at Afro-Eurasian scale in Lesson 03).
- Telling an observation apart from an inference about what it means.
- Writing in complete sentences; the adult scribes when writing is not the
  assessed skill.
- Safety: the adult supervises every hands-on investigation and previews all
  external media; nothing in the unit requires travel, tools, or materials
  beyond paper, pencil, ruler, and a notebook.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (mapped to the track
objectives in parentheses):

1. Write a **compelling question** and the **supporting questions** that help
   answer it about the medieval world, and explain which **source kinds**
   could answer each supporting question. (Track objective 1; D1.1–1.5.6-8)
2. **Read and construct** a map with title, key/legend, compass rose, scale
   bar, grid, and symbols; locate the Mediterranean, Indian Ocean,
   trans-Saharan, and Silk Road corridors on a schematic; use a scale bar to
   measure distance; and explain what a given map **shows and cannot show**,
   including what every flat world map must distort. (Track objective 2;
   D2.Geo.1–3.6-8)
3. **Classify** medieval historical sources by kind — map, traveler's
   account, artifact, coin, chronicle, or secondary interpretation — including
   spotting a **modern reconstruction**; and **detect limits** by stating what
   each source kind cannot tell us. (Track objective 11; D2.His.9, 10.6-8)
4. **Evaluate** a source's credibility from its origin, authority, structure,
   context, and corroborative value; **infer** a plausible maker, date, place
   of origin, or intended audience from clues inside the source. (Track
   objective 11; D3.1, 3.2.6-8; D2.His.11, 12.6-8)
5. **Classify** a medieval development as **change**, **continuity**, or
   **both**, citing evidence from at least two source kinds. (Track
   objective 3; D2.His.1, 2.6-8)

## Essential question

*How do historians build trustworthy stories about a world they can never
visit?*

## Vocabulary

**Compelling question** — a big, open historical question with no single
answer; worth arguing about. · **Supporting question** — a smaller,
answerable question whose answer helps answer a compelling question. ·
**Primary source** — a source made at the time being studied (a coin, a
traveler's journal, a map drawn then). · **Secondary interpretation** — a
later account built from primary sources (a textbook chapter, a museum
essay). · **Artifact** — a made or used object from the past. ·
**Chronicle** — a written record of events in time order, often by one
writer with a viewpoint. · **Corroboration** — checking a claim against a
second, independent source. · **Origin** — who made a source, when, and
where. · **Authority** — why a source's maker is (or is not) worth trusting
on this question. · **Scale bar** — the marked line showing what a map
length equals in real distance. · **Grid** — lettered columns and numbered
rows for locating places exactly. · **Key (legend)** — the box explaining
each map symbol. · **Projection** — a way of drawing the round Earth flat;
every projection distorts something. · **Trade corridor** — a long-used
route along which goods, people, and ideas moved. · **Change** — how
something became different over time. · **Continuity** — what stayed the
same. · **Inference** — an idea about what evidence *means*; one step beyond
what it directly shows. · **Limitation** — what a source cannot tell us, no
matter how carefully we read it.

  Practice Set B, unit assessment.

Historical content notes: the corridor cities named in this unit (Chang'an,
Samarkand, Baghdad, Constantinople, Venice, Alexandria, Aden, Hormuz,
Calicut, Malacca, Sijilmasa, Taghaza, Timbuktu, Gao) are standard reference
points for the medieval trade networks; background checked against the
[UNESCO Silk Roads Programme](https://en.unesco.org/silkroad) and the
[Metropolitan Museum's Heilbrunn Timeline](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/)
2026-10-13. Medieval mapmakers named (al-Idrisi, 1154; the Catalan Atlas,
1375) are documented holdings of major libraries and museums; see the
[resource pack](resource-pack.md). All "source excerpts" in lessons are
**original classroom model texts written for this unit**, clearly labeled —
they are not quotations from real historical works. Real works they model
(Marco Polo's *Description of the World*, Ibn Battuta's *Rihla*) are named
only as kinds, with links for the adult in the resource pack.

## 16-session pacing (Weeks 3–6)

Sessions are ~45 minutes. Each names its activity and success criterion.

| # | Week | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | **Lesson 01** — Framing historical inquiry | Learner writes 1 compelling + 3 supporting questions and names a source kind for each |
| 2 | 3 | Practice Set A — question workshop | Learner sorts 8 questions (compelling / supporting / neither) with reasons; revises one weak question |
| 3 | 3 | **Lesson 02** — Source kinds of the medieval world | Learner classifies 5 sources by kind and names one limitation of each |
| 4 | 3 | Practice Set B — source-kind sorting + the AI illustration | Learner explains why the generated market image is a reconstruction, not evidence |
| 5 | 4 | **Lesson 03** — Reading and building maps | Learner locates 4 corridor cities on the schematic and measures 2 distances on the practice map |
| 6 | 4 | Practice Set C — map-construction studio (rebuilt design lab) | Learner builds a trade-town map with title, key, compass rose, scale bar, grid, and location reasoning |
| 7 | 4 | Practice Set D — corridor practice + "where in the modern world?" | Learner traces all 4 corridors and matches 6 medieval cities to modern countries |
| 8 | 4 | Discussion — what maps show and cannot show | Learner lists 3 things the schematic proves and 3 it cannot prove |
| 9 | 5 | **Lesson 04** — Evaluating sources | Learner scores 2 model texts on origin, authority, structure, context, corroboration |
| 10 | 5 | Practice Set B continued — inference drills | Learner infers maker/date/place/audience for 3 sources and states the evidence for each inference |
| 11 | 5 | **Lesson 05** — Change and continuity | Learner classifies 3 developments as change / continuity / both with cited evidence |
| 12 | 5 | Project work — annotated map + source log draft | Draft map has 4 corridors + 6 labeled cities; source log has 4 entries with kinds and limits |
| 13 | 6 | Review session — question/map/source self-check | Learner corrects every missed review-seed item and can explain the fix |
| 14 | 6 | **week-04 quiz** + portfolio annotated map | Quiz complete; portfolio map finished with key and scale bar |
| 15 | 6 | **Unit assessment** | Assessment complete under adult supervision |
| 16 | 6 | Project presentations + reflection | Learner presents map and source log; names one question the unit left unanswered |

Sessions 2, 4, 6, 7, 10, and 13 draw on [practice.md](practice.md); the
project spans Sessions 12, 14, and 16 ([project.md](project.md)); formal
checks are Sessions 14–15
([assessments/](assessments/)). Retrieval practice is built in: Session 8
revisits questioning (W3), Session 13 revisits all three strands, and the
project reuses map, source-kind, and evaluation skills together.
