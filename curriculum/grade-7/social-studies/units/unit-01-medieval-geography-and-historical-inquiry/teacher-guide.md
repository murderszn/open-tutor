# U01 Teacher Guide

**Unit:** [U01 — Medieval Geography: Sources and Historical Inquiry](README.md)
**For the guiding adult only.** Keys for every item are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## How to use this unit

- **Pacing:** 16 sessions, ~45 minutes, 4 per week (Weeks 3–6). Do not
  compress the review week (Week 6): the formative quiz (Session 14) must
  be scored and retaught *before* the unit assessment (Session 15).
- **Your role:** you are the historian-in-the-room. Read worked examples
  aloud, press the two checks ("Can it be answered? From what leftover?"),
  and demand cited clues for every inference. Scribe when writing is not
  the assessed skill.
- **Evidence of readiness:** the learner moves to U02 when the unit
  assessment scores 21+/25 and the project source log has 6 honest rows.
  "Honest" matters more than "correct" — a well-stated limitation beats a
  confident overclaim.

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — Framing historical inquiry (Session 1)

- **Watch for:** "compelling = any big question." Keep returning to the
  two checks. The repair task (Guided practice 3) is the hinge of the
  lesson — do not skip it.
- **Reteach trigger:** if the learner cannot name a source kind for a
  supporting question, preview Lesson 02's kind names early.
- **Misconception:** *"Was it fair?"* feels historical to learners.
  Validate the curiosity, then show that fairness is a judgment the
  sources cannot settle — the repaired question is where the history
  lives.

### Lesson 02 — Source kinds (Session 3)

- **The image moment is the lesson.** Let learners list observations
  first, then reveal the 2026 generation date. Expect discomfort or
  disbelief — sit with it. The takeaway sentence to land: *"Pretty is not
  the same as true."*
- **Do not soften** the verdict: the illustration proves nothing about
  the medieval world. Learners who argue "but it shows what markets were
  like" need the reconstruction test repeated, not a compromise.
- **Reteach trigger:** if classification is shaky, sort the six kind
  names on cards before attempting B1.

### Lesson 03 — Maps (Session 5)

- **Check the measurements yourself** against
  `assets/oasis-town-practice-map.svg` before the session: market A1 →
  well C1 = 2 km by road; well C1 → inn C3 = 2 km by road; market → inn =
  4 km by road vs ~2.8 km straight-line; east gate E1 → well C1 = 2 km
  straight (no road drawn — the honest answer).
- **Schematic discipline:** learners WILL try to measure the corridor
  schematic. The subtitle ("NOT to scale") is a test item (Guided
  practice 3, exit check) — enforce it.
- **Orange-peel demo:** adult handles the fruit/knife; the point is one
  sentence — *flattening a sphere always damages something.*

### Lesson 04 — Evaluating sources (Session 9)

- **The five tests measure fitness for a question, not agreeableness.**
  A flattering chronicle can still be excellent evidence for what its
  writer wanted remembered.
- **Inference discipline:** every inference needs its cited clue. "The
  writer was a merchant" is a guess until the clue ("twelve bundles,"
  "cheaper by a third") is named.
- **Both model texts are original classroom models** — say so out loud.
  They teach the *moves*; real medieval texts arrive in U02.

### Lesson 05 — Change and continuity (Session 11)

- **Insist on the three steps** (precise name → before/after → cited
  evidence → verdict). The verdict word alone scores nothing.
- **"Both" is not a dodge:** it requires stating what changed AND what
  continued, with evidence for each. The steelman task (Independent
  practice 5) is where this clicks.

## Practice sessions (2, 4, 6, 7, 10, 13)

- **Session 6 (map studio):** the rebuilt design lab. Model ONE placement
  decision aloud ("the inn goes by the gate because tired travelers and
  their animals need water and walls first") before learners draw. Score
  with the studio rubric in [practice.md](practice.md); 10–12 =
  portfolio-ready.
- **Session 7 (D2):** the medieval→modern matching. Confirm the six
  trickiest yourself: Chang'an→China, Samarkand→Uzbekistan,
  Constantinople→Türkiye, Calicut→India, Sijilmasa→Morocco,
  Hormuz→Iran. The CSV uses "Türkiye" (with diacritic).
- **Session 13 (review seeds):** score Practice Set E together; every
  missed item gets a spoken fix from the learner before Session 14.

## Project conferring (Sessions 12, 14, 16)

- **Session 12:** approve the compelling question before drawing starts.
  Veto maps that try to "show everything."
- **Session 14:** check the source log draft for the two classic
  failures: (1) citing the AI illustration as evidence (must be labeled
  "modern reconstruction — not evidence"), (2) limitations that say
  "none" — every source has one; send those rows back.
- **Session 16:** one "prove it" question per presentation, answered
  from the log. End by asking each learner for one question the unit
  left unanswered — record it; it becomes a U02 supporting question.

## Assessment days (Sessions 14–15)

- **Quiz (Session 14):** 15 points; score the same day; reteach missed
  strands immediately; the learner corrects every miss aloud.
- **Unit assessment (Session 15):** 25 points; learner may use the
  schematic, practice map, and vocabulary list. Score guide: 21–25 =
  secure → U02; 15–20 = targeted reteach; below 15 = reteach strands,
  re-attempt missed tasks only.

## Differentiation quick-reference

- **Struggling:** halve the writing (dictate; adult scribes), keep the
  thinking whole. Sort cards instead of writing lists. Do Sessions 2 and
  4 as pure discussion before any independent writing.
- **Advanced:** the lesson extensions (counter-questions, real museum
  objects, perspective arguments) are designed to run without extra
  prep — assign freely.
- **Language access:** word wall all unit (kind names, five tests,
  corridor names, change/continuity frame). Read every worked example
  aloud twice.
