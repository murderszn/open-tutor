# Teacher Guide — Unit 03

**Unit:** [U03 — African Kingdoms: Trade, Knowledge, and Cultural Exchange](README.md) · **Adult only in this file's guidance; student content is in the lessons.**

## Pacing guardrails

- Sessions are ~45 minutes. If a lesson runs long, cut the extension,
  never the exit check or the worked examples.
- Sessions 2, 4, 6, 8, and 12 are practice sessions: the learner works,
  the adult checks the same day (see [answer-key.md](answer-key.md)).
  Do not let practice debt roll into the next week.
- The project draft (Session 10) must be checked for arrow directions
  before Session 13: a reversed arrow is the most common project error
  and the hardest to fix after the map is inked.
- If the learner finishes the quiz (Session 13) below 70% on any
  objective, re-teach that lesson's worked examples before the unit
  assessment (Session 14). Do not rush to the assessment to "stay on
  schedule" — the schedule serves the objectives, not the reverse.

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — West African kingdoms and the gold–salt trade

- **Watch for:** "Ghana mined gold" (correct to trade taxation);
  "the Sahara was empty" (salt mines, oases, towns); caravan numbers
  quoted as exact counts (teach source disagreement on day one).
- **The image:** display the generated caravan image with its caption
  every time. Never let it be copied or captioned as a real place.
  Learners who cannot see the image use the text-only alternative in
  the lesson — it carries every fact the activity needs.
- **Reteach:** if the learner cannot trace salt south and gold north,
  redo Part A with three colored strips (desert/savanna/forest) and
  physical tokens before moving on.

### Lesson 02 — Timbuktu: learning and cultural exchange

- **Watch for:** calling Sankoré a modern university (correct to
  mosque-madrasa learning quarter); "famous = significant" (require two
  reasons plus source kinds); one-way exchange paragraphs (require
  movement in both directions).
- **Sensitivity:** present-day Mali faces real conflict; if the
  extension on manuscript preservation is used, vet sources first and
  keep the discussion factual and brief.
- **Reteach:** if the significance test fails, redo Worked example 2
  with a familiar case (the learner's own school: why would a
  historian 500 years from now call it significant?).

### Lesson 03 — Swahili coast and the monsoon sea

- **Watch for:** "Swahili = Arab" (correct to African coastal identity
  with Arab/Persian contact); monsoon as a storm (correct to seasonal
  wind system); "Kilwa mined gold" (correct to trade control).
- **Sensitivity:** enslavement is named factually in Part B. Do not
  assign reenactment, first-person enslavement narratives, or any
  activity that asks the learner to play an enslaver or an enslaved
  person.
- **Reteach:** if the round-trip logic fails, act it out with a token
  on the schematic: sail, wait, sail back — and ask at each step what
  the crew is doing while waiting.

### Lesson 04 — Great Zimbabwe: stone, cattle, and gold

- **Watch for:** the colonial outsider-built myth — correct it
  explicitly with the archaeological evidence in Part A, briefly and
  plainly; Conical Tower purpose stated as fact (keep it debated);
  "decline = overnight collapse" (the 1400s shift was gradual, with
  successor states).
- **Reteach:** if proves/suggests/cannot-prove sorting fails, start
  with a familiar object (a school building: what does it prove about
  its builders? what can it not prove?) before returning to the wall.

### Lesson 05 — Why kingdoms rise and fall

- **Watch for:** date = cause; the dramatic battle swallowing the
  explanation; counterclaim confused with "the opposite opinion."
- **Praise explicitly:** paragraphs that admit limits. That admission
  is the unit's highest-order skill and should be named as success,
  not treated as hedging.
- **Reteach:** if a paragraph has only one cause, ask "what else was
  true at the same time?" three times, once per missing cause family
  (internal politics, military/technology, trade/economy).

## Discussion sessions (11–12)

- Session 11 (change/continuity): insist on cited evidence for every
  classification; "both" is acceptable only with two reasons (one for
  change, one for continuity).
- Session 12 (review): the learner's self-check (Practice Set E4) is
  the agenda. Re-teach the shakiest item first; do not re-teach the
  whole unit.

## Project supervision (Sessions 10, 13, 15)

- Check arrow directions and place-name spellings at draft stage.
- Check that the significance paragraph's reasons each carry a source
  kind, and that no debated point is stated as fact.
- Challenge questions for Session 15 (pick one if the learner stalls):
  "What would change on your map if the monsoon failed for a year?"
  "Which place would you remove, and what would break?" "Which of your
  two significance reasons is stronger, and why?"

## Readiness and next steps

- **Ready for U04** (South, Southeast Asian, and Pacific societies)
  when the learner can: trace a trade route and explain what shaped it;
  classify a source by kind and name its limit; judge significance
  with reasons and source kinds; and build a multiple-cause paragraph
  with a limit sentence.
- **Not yet ready:** re-teach the weakest objective with its lesson's
  worked examples, then re-check with the corresponding practice set —
  not with a new worksheet.
- **After U03:** file the portfolio map and significance paragraph
  with the U01 annotated map; U04 will reuse the same map-reading,
  source-kind, and significance skills on new regions.
