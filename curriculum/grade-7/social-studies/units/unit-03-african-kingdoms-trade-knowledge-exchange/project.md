# Project — Two Seas of Trade

**Unit:** [U03 — African Kingdoms: Trade, Knowledge, and Cultural Exchange](README.md)
**Sessions:** 10 (draft), 13 (finish), 15 (present) · **Adult-supervised**

## The task

Build a **trade-network map** and write a **significance paragraph**,
then present both. Your map must cover **both** seas of trade in this
unit — the Sahara (camel routes) and the Indian Ocean (dhow routes) —
so that a reader can see Africa connected to the wider world in two
directions at once.

## Deliverables

1. **Trade-network map** (one page, hand-drawn or built from the unit
   schematic). It must show:
   - At least 8 labeled places: Taghaza, Timbuktu, Gao, Djenné, Kilwa,
     Sofala, Great Zimbabwe, and one more of your choice (Mogadishu,
     Mombasa, or Sijilmasa).
   - Both routes, in different colors or line styles, with arrows
     showing the direction goods traveled.
   - A key/legend, a compass rose, and a title.
   - A written note on the map (2–3 sentences) explaining what the map
     shows and what it cannot show (for example: it is schematic, not
     to scale; it cannot show quantities, prices, or dates).
   - An honest label if you reuse the unit schematic as your base:
     "Adapted from the unit's schematic; positions approximate."
2. **Significance paragraph** (8–12 sentences) answering: *Why does one
   place on your map matter in history?* It must:
   - Choose one place and give at least two reasons for its
     significance.
   - Name a source kind behind each reason (traveler's account,
     chronicle, artifact, building, coin, manuscript).
   - Include one multiple-cause sentence (for a rise or a fall) and one
     limit sentence (what the evidence cannot prove).
   - Mention cultural exchange moving in **both** directions at that
     place, or explain honestly why the evidence for one direction is
     thinner.
3. **Presentation** (3–5 minutes, Session 15): present the map, read
   the paragraph, and answer one challenge question from the adult or
   a classmate (for example: "What would change on your map if the
   monsoon failed for a year?" or "Which place on your map would you
   remove, and what would break?").

## Materials

Paper, pencil, ruler, colored pencils; the unit schematic; notebook
notes. Optional: salt dough or modeling clay for a raised-relief
border decoration — not required, and never a substitute for the map
itself. The adult supervises all materials.

## Steps

1. **Session 10 — draft.** Choose your 8+ places and your significance
   place. Draft the map with labels and both routes. Draft the
   paragraph's two reasons and one limit.
2. **Between sessions — revise.** Check every place name against the
   lessons (spelling counts: Timbuktu, Gao, Djenné, Kilwa, Sofala).
   Check that each arrow's direction matches a good that actually
   traveled that way.
3. **Session 13 — finish.** Complete the key, compass rose, title, and
   map note. Read the paragraph aloud to yourself and fix any sentence
   that states a debated point (Conical Tower purpose, caravan sizes,
   student counts) as settled fact.
4. **Session 15 — present.** Present, answer one challenge question,
   and note one thing you would improve if you had another session.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Full marks (3) | Partial (2) | Needs work (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Map coverage | 8+ places, both routes, arrows correct for goods named | 6–7 places, or one route weak, or 1–2 arrow errors | Fewer places, one route missing, or arrows reversed |
| Map craft | Key, compass rose, title, and a shows/cannot-show note, all present | One element missing | Two or more missing |
| Significance reasons | 2+ reasons, each with a source kind | 2 reasons, source kinds missing or vague | 1 reason, or reasons without evidence |
| Multiple causes + limit | A multiple-cause sentence and an honest limit sentence | One of the two present | Neither present |
| Exchange both ways | Movement named in both directions, or an honest gap explained | One direction only, gap unacknowledged | No exchange discussed |
| Presentation | Clear, answers the challenge question with evidence | Clear, challenge answer vague | Unclear, or challenge question unanswered |

**Scoring:** 15–18 = ready for portfolio; 11–14 = revise one criterion
with the adult and resubmit; below 11 = rework with the adult before
the portfolio is closed. The adult records the score privately; scores
are never published.

## Safety and boundaries

- No travel, no real money, no reenactment of enslavement or warfare.
- The generated caravan image may be displayed beside the map if it is
  labeled as an AI illustration of a generic town — never as a picture
  of a mapped place.
- If the map is photographed for a portfolio, it must contain no
  learner names, photos, or personal information.
