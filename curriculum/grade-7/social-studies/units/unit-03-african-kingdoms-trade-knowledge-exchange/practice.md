## Practice Set A — Trade-flow and tax reasoning (Session 2, after Lesson 01)

*All numbers in this set are labeled fictional practice data, invented
for arithmetic practice — not historical records.*

A1. A caravan of 25 camels leaves Taghaza. Each camel carries 4 salt
slabs. How many slabs does the caravan carry in total?
A2. At Timbuktu, each slab is traded for goods worth 3 measures of grain
(a practice exchange rate). How many measures of grain is the whole
caravan's salt worth?
A3. The king of Mali taxes trade at 1 measure of grain for every 10
measures traded (a practice rate). How much tax does the king collect
from this caravan's salt trade?
A4. Explain in two sentences what the king's tax bought, using Lesson
01: protection for routes, soldiers, markets, judges, scholars.
A5. A second caravan carries 10 camels, each with 6 slabs. Which
caravan pays more tax, and by how many grain measures? (Use the same
practice rates.)
A6. **Reasoning:** A trader complains the tax is unfair. Give one reply
a market inspector could make, and one fair criticism of the tax, each
in one sentence.

## Practice Set B — Source kinds and significance (Session 4, after Lesson 02)

B1. Classify each model source by kind, and name one limit of each:
(a) a traveler's journal describing Kilwa's market; (b) a chronicle
recording Askia Muhammad's appointments; (c) a gold coin minted at
Kilwa; (d) the Great Enclosure's stone wall.
B2. Rewrite this weak significance claim with two reasons and a source
kind for each: "Great Zimbabwe was important because it was big."
B3. A source says, "Timbuktu's scholars were the wisest in the world."
Is that a fact, an opinion, or an inference? Explain in one sentence,
and say what evidence could support a humbler version of the claim.
B4. Which is stronger evidence that Kilwa was wealthy: one traveler's
praise, or a coin + a mosque + a traveler's praise together? Why?
(Corroboration across independent kinds.)

## Practice Set C — Monsoon map and "where in the modern world?" (Session 6, after Lesson 03)

Use the unit schematic
([assets/african-trade-networks-schematic.svg](assets/african-trade-networks-schematic.svg)).

C1. Trace a dhow round trip: Arabia → Kilwa → Arabia. At which point
must the ship wait, and why?
C2. Match each unit city to its modern country (use the adult-approved
atlas or `resources/un_countries.csv` for spellings): Timbuktu, Gao,
Djenné, Kilwa, Sofala, Great Zimbabwe.
C3. Which two cities on the schematic are furthest apart by route, and
what did a traveler need to cross between them (desert caravan, ocean
voyage, or both)?
C4. **Reasoning:** Why would a Kilwa merchant learn some Arabic and a
visiting Arab merchant learn some Swahili, rather than one side doing
all the learning? (Trade needs two-way communication; waiting months
in port gives time to learn.)

## Practice Set E — Review seeds: staples, corridors, and review (Session 12, review)

*Re-anchored from the legacy library per the A00 audit. No volatile
statistics are used; any modern producer data carries a check date.*

E1. **Staples on the move (from the global-agriculture quiz seed).**
Grain, salt, and dried fish are staples: foods people need regularly.
Explain why staples travel along trade routes even when luxury goods
(gold, porcelain) earn more per load. (Everyone needs them, in large
quantities, all year — volume and necessity beat price per load.)
E2. **Corridors and chokepoints (from the chokepoints quiz seed).** A
chokepoint is a narrow passage whose control affects a whole route.
Name one Saharan crossing and one Indian Ocean passage from this unit
where control mattered, and say who benefited from controlling each.
(Do not use the legacy quiz's oil-fraction item: it cites a volatile,
undated statistic, and it is retired.)
E3. **Retrieval round.** Without notes: (a) salt's direction of travel;
(b) the monsoon ship's name; (c) Great Zimbabwe's building technique;
(d) two causes of Songhai's fall.
E4. **Self-check.** Mark every item you missed in E1–E3, write the
correction in your own words, and tell the adult one item you still
find shaky. The adult re-teaches that item before the quiz.
