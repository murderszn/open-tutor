# Unit Assessment — Unit 03 (culminating)

**Unit:** [U03 — African Kingdoms: Trade, Knowledge, and Cultural Exchange](../README.md) · **Session:** 14 of 16
**Adult-supervised.** Work independently unless a task says otherwise.
Scoring guidance is in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) only.

## Task 1 — Trade map (Objectives 1, 4)

Using the unit schematic or a blank outline, label these places and
draw both routes with arrows: Taghaza, Timbuktu, Gao, Djenné, Kilwa,
Sofala, Great Zimbabwe. For each route, write one sentence naming a
good that traveled on it and its direction.

## Task 2 — Source analysis (Objectives 3, 5)

Read this **classroom model text** (written for this unit, not a real
quotation):

> "A traveler wrote that in Kilwa the markets were full of cloth from
> across the sea and gold from the interior, and that the town's mosque
> was the finest he had seen on the coast."

(a) What kind of source is this? (b) Name one thing it proves, one
inference from it, and one thing it cannot prove. (c) Name a second,
independent source kind that could corroborate the claim that Kilwa
was wealthy, and say what it would show.

## Task 3 — Comparison (Objective 2)

Compare Ghana and Kilwa in a short paragraph (5–7 sentences): how did
each grow rich, what did each control, and what is one important
difference between them? (Both grew rich by controlling and taxing
trade rather than by mining; Ghana controlled Saharan caravan trade,
Kilwa controlled Indian Ocean coastal trade; one was a kingdom, the
other an independent city-state.)

## Task 4 — Multiple causes (Objective 5)

Write a paragraph (6–8 sentences) explaining Great Zimbabwe's decline
with at least two causes, a source kind for each, and one limit
sentence stating what the evidence cannot prove.

## Task 5 — Change and continuity (Objectives 4, 5)

Classify each development as change, continuity, or both, and give one
reason with evidence:

(a) Swahili remained the coastal trading language after Kilwa's decline.
(b) West African trade shifted from Saharan routes toward Atlantic
coastal ports after the 1500s.
(c) Timbuktu remained a place of learning after Songhai's fall in 1591,
though poorer than before.

## Task 6 — Significance (Objective 3)

Answer in 4–6 sentences: why do historians judge Timbuktu historically
significant? Give two reasons, each tied to a source kind, and do not
use the word "famous" as a reason.
