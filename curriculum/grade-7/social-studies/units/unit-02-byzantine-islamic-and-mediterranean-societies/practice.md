## Practice Set A — Byzantine institutions and the solidus (Session 2)

**A1. Sort by function.** Each item below did more than one job, but each
has a *main* job. Sort into **administration**, **law**, **faith**, or
**economy** — and for one item of your choice, argue a second job it also
did.

a. The theme system (7th century)
b. The Corpus Juris Civilis (529–534)
c. The patriarch of Constantinople
d. The solidus gold coin
e. The imperial tax bureaucracy
f. Icons in churches

**A2. What a coin proves — and cannot prove.** A merchant digging a
foundation in a 7th-century market town far from Constantinople finds a
gold solidus stamped with Justinian's name and portrait.

- List **three** things the coin proves (or strongly suggests).
- List **three** things it cannot prove, no matter how carefully you study
  it.
- One-sentence verdict: is the coin a primary source for Justinian's reign?
  Explain.

**A3. Perspective factors.** For each person, name **two** factors
(geography, role, faith, interests) that would shape how they describe the
Byzantine emperor's power — and write the one-sentence description each
would most likely give.

a. A Byzantine tax collector in Anatolia
b. A Slavic farmer whose village was just visited by missionaries
c. A Venetian merchant living in Constantinople

**A4. Repair the claim.** *"The Byzantine Empire was just the Roman Empire
with a new name, so nothing changed in 330."* In three sentences: what is
right about this claim, what is wrong with it, and what evidence would
decide the argument?

## Practice Set D — The perspective frame (Session 9)

Rebuilt from the legacy civil-rights source-analysis template's
*technique* (context → perspective/purpose/audience → effect → citation);
the civil-rights content itself is not used.

**D1. Frame both accounts.** For each of the two model accounts of the
1204 sack of Constantinople (Lesson 05), complete the frame:

- **Creator** (who, in one phrase) · **Date** (when, approximately) ·
  **Audience** (written for whom) · **Purpose** (what the maker wanted the
  audience to think or do) · **Emphasis** (what it stresses) ·
  **Omission** (what it leaves out)

**D2. Corroboration drill.** The two accounts disagree about whether the
crusaders planned the sack long in advance. Name **two other source
kinds** (from U01's six) that could help decide, and for each, state what
it could show.

**D3. Claim and counterclaim.** *"The Venetian merchant's account is more
useful than the Byzantine chronicler's for understanding* why *the sack
happened."* Write the claim with two supporting reasons, then write the
counterclaim with two supporting reasons. End with one sentence: which
account would you trust *less* on the question of who suffered most, and
why?

## Practice Set E — Review seeds: ports, seas, and straits (Session 11)

Re-anchored from the legacy capitals, oceans/seas, and chokepoints
quizzes (per the A00 audit): stable concepts only — no volatile
statistics, corrected guide references, answer keys in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

**E1. Port cities.** For each city, name the sea or strait it sits on and
write one sentence explaining *why ships stopped there*
(Constantinople, Alexandria, Venice, Genoa).

**E2. Match the waterway.** Match each description to **Mediterranean
Sea**, **Bosporus**, **Strait of Gibraltar**, or **Adriatic Sea**:

a. The narrow strait Constantinople guards, linking the Black Sea to the
   Mediterranean world
b. The sea Venice sailed to reach Constantinople
c. The strait at the western end of the Mediterranean, near Córdoba's
   world
d. The sea itself — the highway all the others connect to

**E3. Why straits matter.** In three sentences, explain why a strait is
worth more to a state than an equal length of open coastline. Use
Constantinople as your example. (No statistics needed — this is a
reasoning item, per the audit.)

**E4. Retrieval — U01 tools on U02 material.**

a. Is the AI harbor-market illustration a primary source, a secondary
   interpretation, or a modern reconstruction? What is the one question it
   can never answer?
b. Write one *supporting* question about the translation movement that
   points at a specific source kind.
c. A classmate says, "The schematic proves Venice was the richest city."
   Name two things the schematic *cannot* show that you would need before
   believing that.
