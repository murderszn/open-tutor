# U02 Teacher Guide

**Unit:** [U02 — Byzantine, Islamic, and Mediterranean societies](README.md)
**For the guiding adult only.** Keys for every item are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## How to use this unit

- **Pacing:** 16 sessions, ~45 minutes, 4 per week (Weeks 7–10). Do not
  compress the review week (Week 10): the formative quiz (Session 14) must
  be scored and retaught *before* the unit assessment (Session 15).
- **Your role:** you are the historian-in-the-room. Read worked examples
  aloud, press the perspective question ("who is speaking, and what does
  their position let them see?"), and demand cited clues for every
  inference. Scribe when writing is not the assessed skill.
- **Tone guardrail:** this unit teaches Islamic belief and Orthodox
  Christian belief descriptively, in their own contexts. Describe beliefs
  as beliefs ("Muslims hold…"); never ask the learner to affirm or rank
  them. If discussion drifts toward "which was better," redirect to the
  unit's question: *what did each society value, and why?*
- **Evidence of readiness:** the learner moves to U03 when the unit
  assessment scores 24+/30 and the project comparison has 4 honest
  evidence rows with 2+ source kinds. "Honest" matters more than
  "correct" — a well-stated limitation beats a confident overclaim.

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — The Byzantine state (Session 1)

- **Watch for:** "Byzantine = Roman with a new name." The lesson's
  multi-factor reasoning is the antidote — law, faith, language, and
  geography each changed what "Roman" meant. Do not let the repair task in
  Practice Set A4 slide.
- **Reteach trigger:** if the learner cannot name two factors shaping a
  perspective, replay the worked example with a familiar figure (their own
  view of a school rule vs. a teacher's).
- **Misconception:** the 1054 schism as a single-day breakup. Stress that
  the excommunications were one moment in a centuries-long drift — the
  lesson's "drift, then break" framing matters for U07's Reformation
  parallels.

### Lesson 02 — Islamic societies (Session 3)

- **The respect check is the lesson.** Before assigning, preview the
  worked examples yourself for tone. The Five Pillars are described as
  practices of Muslims; the learner is never asked to evaluate the truth
  of beliefs, only to understand them in context.
- **Watch for:** "Islam spread only by conquest." The lesson names trade,
  missionaries, and pilgrimage routes explicitly — the assessment (Q2)
  tests this correction directly.
- **Reteach trigger:** if the idea-tracing task confuses the learner,
  draw the three-stage chain on paper first (India → Baghdad → Latin
  West) and fill one stage together.

### Lesson 03 — Mediterranean exchange (Session 5)

- **The image moment.** Let learners list observations from the AI
  illustration first, then ask the "whose eyes?" question — then reveal
  the generation record. The takeaway: *a picture can show a world and
  still prove nothing about it.* Do not let the illustration appear in any
  evidence row later; the project rubric scores such rows zero.
- **Check the schematic yourself** before the session: the dashed blue
  lines are sea routes, brown are land routes, and the file labels itself
  not to scale. The pepper-tracing worked example is the hinge — walk it
  slowly.
- **Misconception:** "trade = everyone got rich." Practice Set C4 (who
  gained, who paid) exists to break this; insist on specific gains and
  costs, not "money."

### Lesson 04 — Change and continuity (Session 8)

- **This is the unit's thinking-methods lesson.** The routine must be
  explicit: verdict first, then evidence from two source kinds. Accept
  "both" only when the learner can say *in what respect* each side holds.
- **The error-analysis task is the hinge** — do not skip it. Learners who
  can repair a flawed classification understand the routine; learners who
  only classify clean examples often do not.
- **Reteach trigger:** if verdicts come without evidence, go back to
  U01 Lesson 05's two-source-kind rule before continuing.

### Lesson 05 — Comparing perspectives (Session 10)

- **The frame must be complete.** Every account gets all six cells
  (creator, date, audience, purpose, emphasis, omission). "Biased" is not
  an omission — insist on naming what is actually left out.
- **Watch for:** treating disagreement as proof one account is lying.
  The lesson's rule — disagreement calls for corroboration, not a verdict
  — is the single most important sentence in the unit. Repeat it.
- **Bridge to the project:** the claim/counterclaim task (D3) is a dry
  run of the project's counterclaim paragraph. Review it together before
  Session 12.

## Practice sessions (2, 4, 6, 9, 11, 13)

- **Session 2 (Set A):** the solidus task (A2) re-teaches U01's
  proves/cannot-prove move on new material. If the learner lists only
  proves, re-run U01 Lesson 04's limitation drill.
- **Session 4 (Set B):** city profiles may use the Oak National Academy
  lesson (see resource pack) — preview it first. The B3 "clearest vs.
  faintest traces" question is the session's best discussion starter.
- **Session 6 (Set C):** do C1 on the printed schematic with a finger
  before writing. C4's matrix needs the specificity rule enforced
  ("money" alone earns nothing).
- **Session 9 (Set D):** this set *is* the rebuilt civil-rights template
  technique. Keep the technique, not the old content — do not pull up the
  original template file with the learner.
- **Session 11 (Set E):** review seeds only — if the learner misses more
  than two, use Session 13 to re-teach rather than pushing into the quiz.
- **Session 13 (review):** the learner self-checks against the answer key
  *with you present*. Every corrected item needs a spoken explanation of
  the fix, not just a copied answer.

## Project conferring (Sessions 12, 14, 16)

- **Session 12 checkpoint:** thesis + 4 evidence rows with source kinds
  named. Do not let the learner leave without the kinds — unnamed
  evidence becomes decoration by Session 14.
- **The most common failure:** one society doing all the work while the
  other decorates. If three of four rows are about one society, send the
  learner back for balance before Session 14.
- **Session 16 presentations:** 3–4 minutes; the audience asks one
  question each. The learner's reflection ("one question the unit left
  unanswered") is the seed of U03's inquiry — write it down.

## Assessment days (Sessions 14–15)

- **Session 14:** quiz first (~25 min), then portfolio time. Score the
  quiz the same day; re-teach misses at the start of Session 15 before
  the assessment — never after.
- **Session 15:** the learner may use the schematic and vocabulary list.
  Part D's accounts are model texts; if the learner asks "is this real?,"
  answer honestly: written for this unit, in the voice of the kind named.
- **After scoring:** record only the readiness band. Keep the scored
  papers private and out of the repository.

## Differentiation quick-reference

- **Reading/language access:** read all worked examples and model texts
  aloud; keep the vocabulary list on a word wall all unit; pre-teach
  *basileus, sharia, ulama, lateen* before their lessons.
- **Alternate response modes:** the learner may dictate the essay and
  project paragraphs while the adult scribes; sorting tasks (A1, E2) may
  be done by pointing or card-sorting; the schematic tracing (C1) may be
  done with a finger and narrated.
- **Extensions:** (1) compare al-Idrisi's 1154 world map (resource pack
  query 6) with the unit schematic — what does each show that the other
  cannot? (2) Research one more translation-movement figure (Ibn Rushd /
  Averroes) and add an evidence row to the project. (3) Write the
  "counter-question" for the unit's essential question — a question whose
  answer would complicate it.
