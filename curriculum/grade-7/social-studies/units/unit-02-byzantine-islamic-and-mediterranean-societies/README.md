# Unit 02 — Byzantine, Islamic, and Mediterranean Societies

**Grade:** 7 · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track issue:** [#37](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/37)
**Status:** validated draft — delivered 2026-10-19; not yet educator-reviewed or merged.
**Weeks:** 7–10 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 45 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

Unit 01 built the historian's toolkit — questions, source kinds, maps,
evaluation, change and continuity. This unit uses that toolkit on three
connected worlds of the medieval Mediterranean: the **Byzantine state**
(administration, law, Orthodox Christianity), **Islamic societies** (belief,
law, scholarship, cities), and the **Mediterranean exchange** (trade,
translation, and the movement of ideas and technologies) that tied them —
and Latin Europe — together. Each society is studied in its own context
first; only then are they compared. The unit's spine is **perspective**:
how geography, law, faith, and position in a trade network shaped what
different people noticed, valued, and wrote down — and how a historian
weighs those perspectives against each other without declaring a winner.
The track stays nonpartisan: Byzantine, Islamic, and Latin Christian
perspectives are analyzed in their own contexts, never ranked into a single
narrative.

The unit rebuilds one piece of the legacy track library, as the A00 audit
required: the civil-rights source-analysis template's *technique*
(context, perspective/purpose/audience comparison, effect, citation) is
rebuilt for medieval primary sources in Lesson 05 and Practice Set D; the
civil-rights content itself stays optional enrichment. The capitals,
oceans/seas, and chokepoints quizzes return as Practice Set E review seeds,
re-anchored to the Mediterranean (why port cities sit where they do) with
answer keys. All "source excerpts" in lessons are **original classroom
model texts written for this unit**, clearly labeled — they are not
quotations from real historical works. Real works they model (Procopius's
*Wars*, Ibn Battuta's *Rihla*, the Cairo Geniza merchant letters) are named
only as kinds, with links for the adult in the resource pack.

## Prerequisites (re-taught here as needed)

- U01 skills, used from Session 1: framing compelling/supporting
  questions, classifying source kinds, reading schematic maps, evaluating
  sources (origin, authority, structure, context, corroboration), and
  classifying change vs. continuity. Lesson 04 re-teaches the
  change/continuity routine explicitly before the unit leans on it.
- Reading a simple map: title, key/legend, compass rose, cardinal
  directions (U01 Lesson 03; re-taught at Mediterranean scale in
  Lesson 03).
- Writing in complete sentences; the adult scribes when writing is not the
  assessed skill.
- Safety: the adult supervises every session and previews all external
  media; nothing in the unit requires travel, tools, or materials beyond
  paper, pencil, ruler, and a notebook.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (mapped to the track
objectives in parentheses):

1. Explain the **Byzantine state** — imperial administration, Justinian's
   law code, and Orthodox Christianity — and analyze how **multiple
   factors** (geography, law, faith) shaped people's perspectives.
   (Track objective 4; D2.His.4.6-8; D2.Civ.5, 6.6-8)
2. Explain the **rise and spread of Islamic societies** — belief, law,
   scholarship, and cities — **in their own context**, and evaluate a
   source for the perspective of its maker. (Track objective 4;
   D2.His.1, 4, 6.6-8)
3. Explain **Mediterranean exchange** — trade, translation, and the
   movement of ideas and technologies — and explain how **physical and
   human characteristics of places** shaped connections and identities.
   (Track objective 4; D2.Geo.5, 6, 7.6-8)
4. **Classify** a medieval development as **change**, **continuity**, or
   **both**, citing evidence from **at least two source kinds**.
   (Track objective 3; D2.His.2.6-8)
5. **Compare two accounts** of the same event for perspective, purpose,
   and audience; develop a **claim and a counterclaim** about which
   account is more useful for a stated question, noting strengths and
   limitations. (Track objectives 11, 12; D2.His.5.6-8; D3.3, 3.4.6-8)

## Essential question

*How did living on the same sea produce such different societies — and what
did they make of each other?*

## Vocabulary

**Basileus** — the Byzantine emperor's Greek title; the autocratic ruler
at the head of the state. · **Corpus Juris Civilis** — Justinian's "Body
of Civil Law" (529–534), the organized collection of Roman law. ·
**Theme system** — Byzantine provinces (7th century on) combining military
and civilian rule. · **Solidus** — the Byzantine gold coin, famously
stable for centuries. · **Icon** — a religious image used in Orthodox
devotion; disputes over icons (Iconoclasm, 726–843) divided the empire. ·
**Schism** — a formal split; the 1054 East-West Schism divided Eastern
Orthodox and Roman Catholic Christianity. · **Caliph** — a successor to
Muhammad as leader of the Muslim community. · **Hijra** — the 622
migration to Medina; Year 1 of the Islamic calendar. · **Qur'an** — the
central religious text of Islam. · **Five Pillars** — the five core
practices of Islam: profession of faith, prayer, almsgiving, fasting, and
pilgrimage. · **Sharia** — Islamic law derived from the Qur'an, the sunna,
scholarly consensus, and analogy. · **Ulama** — Islamic scholars of law
and religion. · **House of Wisdom** — the Bayt al-Hikma in Baghdad, a
center of the translation movement and scholarship. · **Translation
movement** — the rendering of Greek, Persian, and Indian works into Arabic
(and later Arabic works into Latin). · **Astrolabe** — an instrument for
measuring the positions of stars, used in astronomy and navigation. ·
**Maritime republic** — a self-governing Italian port city (Venice,
Genoa) grown rich on sea trade. · **Lateen sail** — the triangular sail
that let ships maneuver in Mediterranean winds. ·
**Caravanserai** — a roadside inn sheltering caravans on land routes. ·
**Perspective** — how a person's position (role, faith, city, interests)
shapes what they notice and how they describe it. · **Corroboration** —
checking a claim against a second, independent source (revisited from
U01). · **Change** — how something became different over time. ·
**Continuity** — what stayed the same (revisited from U01).

Historical content notes: dates and names used in this unit are standard
reference points — Constantinople as capital from 330; Justinian
(527–565), Corpus Juris Civilis (529–534), Hagia Sophia completed 537;
theme system (7th century); Iconoclasm (726–843); East-West Schism (1054);
Fourth Crusade sack of Constantinople (1204); Ottoman conquest (1453);
Muhammad (c. 570–632), Hijra (622); Rashidun (632–661), Umayyad
(661–750, Damascus), Abbasid (from 750, Baghdad founded 762); Cairo
founded 969; al-Khwarizmi's *Kitab al-Jabr* (c. 825); al-Idrisi's world
map (1154, for Roger II of Sicily); Toledo translation activity (12th
century); Fibonacci's *Liber Abaci* (1202); Crusades (1095–1291).
Background checked 2026-10-19 against the Met Heilbrunn Timeline, the
UNESCO Silk Roads Programme, Khan Academy's "The rise of Islamic empires
and states," and the Oak National Academy Baghdad/Córdoba lesson; see the
[resource pack](resource-pack.md). "Byzantine" is a later historians'
label (16th century); the empire's people called themselves Romans. All
"model texts" are original classroom texts, clearly labeled; they are not
quotations. Where chroniclers' figures are traditionally reported but
debated (Córdoba's library), the unit says "chroniclers claimed."

## 16-session pacing (Weeks 7–10)

Sessions are ~45 minutes. Each names its activity and success criterion.

| # | Week | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7 | **Lesson 01** — The Byzantine state: law, faith, administration | Learner explains 3 Byzantine institutions and analyzes one perspective with 2+ factors |
| 2 | 7 | Practice Set A — institutions & the solidus; perspective factors | Learner sorts 6 institutions by function and traces what a solidus proves/cannot prove |
| 3 | 7 | **Lesson 02** — Rise and spread of Islamic societies | Learner explains belief, law, scholarship, cities in their own context; evaluates one model source for perspective |
| 4 | 7 | Practice Set B — city profiles; idea-tracing (numerals/algebra) | Learner completes 2 city profiles and traces one idea across 3 cultures with evidence |
| 5 | 8 | **Lesson 03** — Mediterranean exchange + the AI illustration | Learner traces the pepper route on the schematic; writes the "whose eyes?" analysis of the illustration |
| 6 | 8 | Practice Set C — exchange mapping; change/continuity of paper | Learner maps 4 goods flows and classifies paper's arrival as change/continuity/both with evidence |
| 7 | 8 | Discussion — who gained from exchange? | Learner names 3 groups and explains one gain and one cost for each |
| 8 | 8 | **Lesson 04** — Change and continuity across the era | Learner classifies 3 developments with evidence from 2+ source kinds; repairs 1 flawed classification |
| 9 | 9 | Practice Set D — perspective/purpose/audience frame on model sources | Learner completes the frame for 2 model texts (creator, date, audience, purpose, emphasis, omission) |
| 10 | 9 | **Lesson 05** — Comparing perspectives: whose story? | Learner compares 2 accounts of 1204, states agreements/disagreements, and explains each by perspective |
| 11 | 9 | Practice Set E — review seeds (ports, seas, chokepoints) | Learner corrects every missed item and explains the fix |
| 12 | 9 | Project work — "Two Societies, One Sea" draft | Draft has comparison thesis, 4 evidence rows with source kinds, and a labeled sketch map |
| 13 | 10 | Review session — society/perspective/exchange self-check | Learner corrects every missed review item and can explain the fix |
| 14 | 10 | **week-10 quiz** + portfolio comparison draft | Quiz complete; portfolio draft finished with map and source log |
| 15 | 10 | **Unit assessment** | Assessment complete under adult supervision |
| 16 | 10 | Project presentations + reflection | Learner presents the comparison; names one question the unit left unanswered |

Sessions 2, 4, 6, 9, 11, and 13 draw on [practice.md](practice.md); the
project spans Sessions 12, 14, and 16 ([project.md](project.md)); formal
checks are Sessions 14–15 ([assessments/](assessments/)). Retrieval
practice is built in: Session 7 revisits U01 source evaluation on new
material, Session 11 re-anchors U01 review seeds to the Mediterranean, and
Session 13 revisits all three strands before the quiz.
