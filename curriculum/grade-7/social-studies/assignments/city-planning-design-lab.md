# Learner — Design Your Own City (Grade 7 Social Studies)

**Subject:** Social Studies  
**Type:** Creative Design Lab

---

## The Challenge

You are the **Chief City Planner** of a brand-new city. You get to decide everything — where people live, where they work, where they play, and how they get around. Your job is to design a city that is safe, fair, and a great place for everyone who lives there.

---

## Part 1: Name & Describe Your City

Answer these questions in 2–3 sentences each:

1. **What is your city called?** (Make up a name — be creative!)
2. **Where is it located?** (Mountains? Coast? Plains? River valley?)
3. **What is the population?** (Small town of 5,000? Big city of 500,000?)
4. **What is your city known for?** (Tourism? Manufacturing? Tech? Sports?)

---

## Part 2: Zone Your City

Cities are divided into **zones** — areas set aside for specific purposes. Describe what you will put in each zone and *why* you chose that location in your city.

| **Green Space** (parks & nature) | | |
| **Civic** (schools, hospitals, government) | | |

---

## Part 3: Transportation Plan

How do people get around your city? Choose **at least 3** options and explain your choices:

- [ ] Roads & highways
- [ ] Public buses
- [ ] Subway / light rail
- [ ] Bike lanes
- [ ] Sidewalks & pedestrian zones
- [ ] Other: _______________

**Explain your transportation choices in 3–5 sentences:** Why did you pick these options? How do they connect your different zones?

---

## Part 4: Solve a City Problem

Every city faces challenges. Pick **one problem** from the list below and describe how your city will handle it:

- **Traffic congestion** — too many cars clogging the roads
- **Affordable housing** — making sure everyone can afford a place to live
- **Pollution** — keeping the air and water clean
- **Safety** — reducing crime and keeping neighborhoods safe
- **Green space** — making sure people have access to parks and nature

**Write a short paragraph (4–6 sentences):** What is the problem, why does it matter, and what is your city's solution?

---

## Part 5: City Map (Optional but Encouraged!)

Draw a simple map of your city on paper or in your notebook. You don't need to be an artist — use squares, circles, and labels. Include:

- [ ] Your zones (label each one)
- [ ] At least one major road or transit line
- [ ] One park or green space
- [ ] One landmark you're proud of (stadium, museum, famous bridge — your choice!)

---

## Reflection

When you're done, answer these two questions in your own words:

1. What was the hardest decision you had to make as city planner? Why?
2. If you could actually live in your city, what would be your favorite thing about it?
