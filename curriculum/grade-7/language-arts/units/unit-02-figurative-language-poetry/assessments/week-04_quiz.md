# Formative Quiz — Unit 02 (Session 15 / track Week 10)

**Standards:** RL.7.4, RL.7.5, RL.7.6, L.7.5 · **Time:** 30–40 minutes
**Texts:** Sandburg's "Fog" ([Lesson 01](../lessons/lesson-01-figurative-meanings.md)),
Shakespeare's Sonnet 18 ([Lesson 03](../lessons/lesson-03-form-and-structure.md)),
"Two Voices, One Storm" ([Lesson 04](../lessons/lesson-04-point-of-view.md)) —
the learner may re-read the poems during the quiz.

*Revised from the legacy track quiz
`quizzes/literary-terms-figurative-language-quiz.md` (audit decision:
re-anchor every item to read passages instead of the guide reading, and add
an answer key). The answer key is in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) §
Quiz — never beside these questions.*

Answer in complete sentences. Cite line numbers for every answer that asks
"how do you know" or "support."

1. **Extended metaphor.** "Fog" compares the fog to one thing across the
   whole poem. Name the comparison, quote two lines that carry it, and
   explain what the comparison suggests about the fog (two sentences).
2. **Connotation.** Why does Sandburg write "little cat feet" instead of
   just "cat feet"? Explain what "little" connotes and how the poem would
   feel different without it.
3. **Figure in context.** Sonnet 18, line 4: "And summer's lease hath all
   too short a date." Name the figure of speech and interpret it in
   context: what is being compared, and what does the comparison add to
   the poem's case against summer?
4. **Sound impact.** In "The Swing," the phrase "Up in the air" repeats in
   lines 1, 2, 5, 11, and 12. Name the device and explain its impact on
   the stanza it appears in — what feeling does the repetition create, and
   how?
5. **Form and meaning.** Where is the turn in Sonnet 18? Name the line and
   the turn word, and explain in two sentences what changes in the poem's
   argument after the turn.
6. **Point of view.** In "Two Voices, One Storm," name each voice's point
   of view (first/third person) and quote one line from each voice that
   proves it.
7. **Contrasting viewpoints.** In two sentences, explain what the contrast
   between Voice A and Voice B reveals about the storm that neither voice
   reveals alone.
8. **Error analysis.** A classmate says, "Fog is literally about a cat that
   sits on a harbor." Explain in two to three sentences why that reading
   fails, citing one line.
