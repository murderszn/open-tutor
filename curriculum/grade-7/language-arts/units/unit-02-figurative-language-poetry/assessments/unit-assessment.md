## Poem A — "The Wind" by Robert Louis Stevenson (1885; public domain)

> I saw you toss the kites on high (1)
> And blow the birds about the sky; (2)
> And all around I heard you pass, (3)
> Like ladies' skirts across the grass — (4)
> O wind, a-blowing all day long, (5)
> O wind, that sings so loud a song! (6)
>
> I saw the different things you did, (7)
> But always you yourself you hid. (8)
> I felt you push, I heard you call, (9)
> I could not see yourself at all — (10)
> O wind, a-blowing all day long, (11)
> O wind, that sings so loud a song! (12)

Margin gloss: *a-blowing* — blowing (an old poetic form).

## Poem B — "The Last Train" (original, written for this assessment)

> Under the buzzing lights I stood, (1)
> The last train sighed and would not come. (2)
> My ticket softened in my fist, (3)
> A small white flag I'd not yet waved. (4)
>
> The station clock blinked twice, then stopped, (5)
> As if it too had somewhere to be. (6)
> I told my feet to carry me home — (7)
> They stayed, like roots, in the concrete. (8)

## Tasks

Answer in complete sentences and cite line numbers.

1. **Figures of speech (L.7.5).** In Poem A, identify **two different**
   figures of speech (simile, metaphor, or personification). For each:
   quote the line, name the figure, and explain its meaning in context
   (one sentence each).
2. **Sound impact (RL.7.4).** Choose **one** sound device in Poem A
   (rhyme, alliteration, or the repeated refrain in lines 5–6 / 11–12).
   Explain its impact on its stanza: what feeling does it create, and how
   does the sound make that feeling? (Two to three sentences.)
3. **Connotation (RL.7.4).** In Poem A, line 9, the speaker says "I felt
   you push." Why *push* and not *touch*? Explain what *push* connotes
   here and how the line would feel different with *touch*.
4. **Point of view (RL.7.6).** Name the speaker's point of view in Poem B
   (first/third person) with a quoted line as proof. Then name **one
   thing this point of view lets the reader feel or know**, and **one
   thing it hides** (two sentences).
5. **Form and meaning (RL.7.5).** Poem A is built as two 6-line stanzas.
   What does the stanza break between lines 6 and 7 *do*? Explain in two
   sentences how the break separates the poem's two ideas.
6. **Synthesis paragraph (RL.7.4, RL.7.6).** In one paragraph (5–7
   sentences), compare how the two poems' speakers *feel* about their
   subjects (the wind; the missed train). Cite **one craft choice from
   each poem** — a figure of speech, a sound, a connotation, or a POV
   detail — and explain how that choice builds the speaker's feeling.
