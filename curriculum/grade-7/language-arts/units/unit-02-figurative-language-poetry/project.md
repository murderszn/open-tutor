# Project — Poetry Portfolio: Craft a Poem, Analyze a Poem

**Sessions 11–14 of 18** (two workshops, one polish session, one peer
review). **Standards:** RL.7.4, RL.7.5, RL.7.6, L.7.5.

You are both poet and critic this week: you will **craft** an original poem
using the unit's tools, then **analyze** how form or point of view shapes
meaning in a published poem. Both pieces go into your portfolio — the
track's Unit 10 review will ask you to revisit this work, so make it work
you're proud to re-read.

## Part 1 — Original poem (craft)

Write an original poem of **12–20 lines** about a subject you choose
(weather, a place, a memory, an animal — anything you can observe closely).

Requirements:

1. At least **three different kinds** of figurative language from this
   menu: simile, metaphor, personification, hyperbole, idiom, allusion.
   Label each one in the margin (e.g., "[simile — line 3]").
2. At least **one sound device** used on purpose: end rhyme, internal
   rhyme, alliteration, or a repeated refrain line. Label it too.
3. A **deliberate form choice**: stanza breaks that separate ideas, a
   volta/turn, or a closing couplet that lands a final thought. In 2–3
   sentences at the bottom, explain what your form choice does for the
   poem's meaning.
4. A **chosen point of view**: first-person speaker or a distinct
   third-person voice. In one sentence, state whose eyes the reader sees
   through and one thing that POV lets you show (or hide).

## Part 2 — Poem analysis (criticism)

Choose one unit poem ("Fog," "The Swing," Sonnet 18, "Hope is the thing
with feathers," "Two Voices, One Storm," or "City Fog"). Write **two
paragraphs**:

- **Paragraph 1 — craft:** How does the poem's *form or structure* (stanza
  shape, sonnet architecture, line breaks, turn, couplet) contribute to its
  meaning? Cite at least two lines.
- **Paragraph 2 — perspective:** Whose point of view speaks, how does the
  author develop it, and — if the poem invites comparison (or using Lesson
  05's paired fog poems) — what does a contrasting perspective reveal? Cite
  at least two lines.

## Part 3 — Revision log

Keep a short log as you revise (Sessions 13–14): for each change, write
*what* you changed and *why* (one line each, e.g., "Cut 'very big' → 'vast':
'vast' connotes awe, 'very big' connotes nothing"). Minimum four entries.

## Materials

Notebook or word processor, unit lessons for reference, the rubric below.

## Deliverables

1. Original poem (12–20 lines) with margin labels for figures, sound
   device, form choice, and POV statement.
2. Two-paragraph analysis with 4+ line citations total.
3. Revision log (4+ entries).

## Rubric

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Figurative language (L.7.5)** | 3+ kinds, each labeled correctly; every figure's in-context meaning is clear and fresh | 3 kinds labeled; meanings clear | 2 kinds, or labels partly wrong | 1 kind or figures don't make sense in context |
| **Sound and form (RL.7.4/7.5)** | Sound device + form choice both serve the poem's meaning; the 2–3 sentence explanation names the effect | Both present and labeled; explanation names an effect | Present but decorative; explanation vague | Missing or mislabeled |
| **Point of view (RL.7.6)** | POV is distinct and consistent; the one-sentence statement names what it shows/hides | POV consistent; statement present | POV drifts; statement vague | No discernible POV |
| **Analysis paragraphs** | Both paragraphs cite 2+ lines each and connect a craft move to meaning; contrast insight where relevant | Citations present; craft–meaning link stated | Citations thin; mostly summary | Retells the poem without analysis |
| **Revision log** | 4+ entries; each explains *why* with unit vocabulary (connotation, sound, form) | 4 entries with reasons | Fewer than 4, or reasons missing | No log |

**Scoring:** 17–20 = portfolio-ready with pride; 13–16 = portfolio-ready
after one revision pass; below 13 = revise with the adult using the
teacher guide's reteaching moves, then resubmit.

## Timeline

- **Session 11:** Draft the poem (all 12–20 lines, rough is fine); choose
  the analysis poem and write its thesis sentence (Lesson 05, task 3).
- **Session 12:** Draft both analysis paragraphs; start margin labels.
- **Session 13:** Revision checklist (below) + read-aloud revision; log
  every change.
- **Session 14:** Peer/adult review — trade drafts, give two specific
  craft comments using unit vocabulary, then finalize.

**Revision checklist (Session 13):** ☐ every figure labeled and making
sense in context · ☐ one sound device audible when read aloud · ☐ form
choice explained in 2–3 sentences · ☐ POV statement written · ☐ analysis
has 4+ citations · ☐ no summary-without-analysis sentences · ☐ title
added.
