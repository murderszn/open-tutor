# Practice, Discussion, and Review Sessions — Unit 02

Named sessions referenced by the [18-session pacing](README.md#18-session-pacing-4-weeks--4-sessions-40-50-minutes-each).
Each takes 40–50 minutes. The adult observes, gives hints on practice days,
and records exit checks. Sample responses for every task live in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md) § Practice — student pages never carry keys.

## Session A — Figurative-meaning hunt

*Session 2 (after L1). Materials: Dickinson's "Hope is the thing with
feathers" (printed in Lesson 01).*

1. **Guided — figure sort (15 min):** The adult reads six lines from the
   poem (answer key lists them). The learner sorts each into
   simile / metaphor / personification / none, and states the in-context
   meaning of each figure in one sentence.
2. **Independent — connotation pairs (15 min):** For three word pairs
   (*sweetest/sore*, *little Bird/mighty storm* implied, *chillest
   land/strangest Sea*), the learner writes each word's connotation and
   one sentence on why Dickinson chose that word instead of a neutral
   synonym.
3. **Exit check:** "Is 'the thing with feathers' a simile or a metaphor?
   How do you know?" Secure = metaphor, no *like/as*; Developing = correct
   label, shaky reason; Not yet = wrong label.

## Session B — Sound laboratory

*Session 4 (after L2). Materials: "The Swing" (Lesson 02), notebook.*

1. **Guided — hear it first (15 min):** The adult reads stanza 1 of "The
   Swing" three ways: flat monotone, bouncy and fast, slow and heavy. The
   learner picks which reading fits and names the sound features that make
   it fit (short lines, repeated "Up in the air," end rhymes).
2. **Independent — sound rewrite (15 min):** Rewrite stanza 1's first two
   lines *without* rhyme or repetition ("How do you like to go up in a
   swing, / Up in the air so blue?" → a plain prose version). Then write
   two sentences on what the poem *lost*.
3. **Discussion — impact statements (10 min):** The learner finishes three
   sentence frames: "The repetition of ___ makes the stanza feel ___,
   because ___." The adult pushes past "it sounds good" to a feeling word
   and a mechanism.

## Session C — Form-analysis clinic

*Session 7 (after the sonnet investigation). Materials: Sonnet 18 (Lesson
03), notebook.*

1. **Guided — form → meaning paragraph (20 min):** Using a paragraph frame
   ("Shakespeare's ___ (form choice) makes the reader feel/think ___
   because ___. For example, ___ (line). Without this form choice, ___"),
   the learner writes one paragraph on the couplet. The adult models one
   sentence, then hands over the pen.
2. **Independent — line-break lab (15 min):** Take the couplet's two lines
   and re-break them into four short lines. Read both versions aloud. Write
   two sentences: which version lands the final punch harder, and why?
3. **Exit check:** "In one sentence, what does the turn at line 9 do for
   the poem's argument?" Secure = names the shift from problem to answer;
   Developing = names a change without the argument link; Not yet = no
   turn identified.

## Session D — POV sort and contrast

*Session 9 (after L4). Materials: "Two Voices, One Storm" (Lesson 04).*

1. **Guided — narrator cards (15 min):** The adult reads eight statements
   about the two voices (from [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) § Practice D
   — a mix of facts each narrator knows and inferences). The learner sorts
   them under Voice A / Voice B / Both / Neither, citing the line that
   decides each.
2. **Independent + discussion — the contrast paragraph (20 min):** The
   learner writes four sentences: Voice A's stance (with line), Voice B's
   stance (with line), one craft choice that differs between them, and what
   the contrast reveals that one voice alone couldn't.
3. **Exit check:** "Name one thing Voice A knows that Voice B doesn't, or
   vice versa." Secure = correct with line citation; Developing = correct
   without citation; Not yet = describes feelings instead of knowledge.
