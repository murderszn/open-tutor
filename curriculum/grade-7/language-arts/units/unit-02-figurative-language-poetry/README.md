# Unit 02 — Literary Perspective, Figurative Language, and Poetry

**Grade:** 7 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #36](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/36), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Poets say things sideways on purpose. A fog doesn't really have "little cat
feet," a hope isn't really "the thing with feathers," and a tree can't really
keep watch — but saying it that way makes you *feel* something plain words
can't carry. In this unit the learner practices the reader's craft moves for
poetry and literary prose: figuring out what figurative language means in
context, hearing how sound patterns shape a verse, seeing how a poem's form
does meaning-work, and tracking how different points of view change what a
text says. Anchor texts are a mix of public-domain poems (printed in full in
the lessons, so every exercise is passage-based and rights-clean) and short
original pieces written for this unit, plus one AI-generated illustration used
in a real activity.

## Essential question

How do poets use comparisons, sounds, form, and point of view to make words
mean more than they literally say?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2), the grade-6 track, and U01:

- Determining theme and writing an objective summary (U01; reused when the
  learner writes the portfolio poem analysis)
- Citing textual evidence for claims about a text (U01; now applied to craft
  claims — "this line shows personification because…")
- Basic poetry vocabulary from grade 6: rhyme, stanza, verse (deepened here
  with meter, scansion, sonnet form, and point of view in verse)
- Using context clues for unfamiliar words (reused when interpreting
  allusions and connotative meanings)

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Determine **figurative and connotative meanings** of words and phrases as
   used in a poem or passage, and explain the meaning in context (RL.7.4).
2. **Interpret figures of speech** — simile, metaphor (including extended
   metaphor), personification, idiom, hyperbole, and literary allusion — in
   context, naming the comparison and explaining what it suggests (L.7.5.a).
3. Analyze the **impact of rhymes and other sound repetitions**
   (alliteration, rhythm, repeated words) on a specific verse or stanza,
   connecting sound choices to feeling or meaning (RL.7.4).
4. Analyze how a poem's **form or structure** — stanza breaks, line breaks,
   sonnet architecture — contributes to its meaning (RL.7.5).
5. Analyze how an author **develops and contrasts the points of view** of
   different characters or narrators in a text (RL.7.6).

## Differentiation and supports

- **Reading access:** all poems are short and printed in full; the adult
  reads each poem aloud once before analysis (poems are meant to be heard).
  Unfamiliar words get a margin gloss in each lesson.
- **Alternate response modes:** learners may answer analysis questions
  orally, with labeled drawings (e.g., draw the literal scene vs. the
  figurative picture), or by pointing to evidence lines; the exit checks
  accept any mode that names the evidence.
- **Extensions:** scan a full sonnet independently; find an allusion in a
  novel or song and unpack it; write a "translation" of a Shakespeare
  quatrain into modern speech and defend what was kept or lost.
- **Language access:** key terms appear with a one-line plain-language
  gloss on first use in every lesson.
