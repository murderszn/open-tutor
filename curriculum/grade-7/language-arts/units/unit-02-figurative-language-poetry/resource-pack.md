# Resource Pack — Unit 02: Figurative Language, Form, and Point of View

**Goal:** Give a grade-7 learner (and the guiding adult) trustworthy,
free, no-account places to hear poems read aloud, check poetic terms, and
see figurative language, sonnet form, and point of view taught by
established educators — supporting RL.7.4, RL.7.5, RL.7.6, and L.7.5.
All links checked 2026-10-19. Videos supplement the lessons; they never
replace reading the poems.

## Focused queries

Use these when the learner wants more examples beyond the unit:

## Videos

1. **"35 Literary Terms Explained"** — YouTube,
   <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R7HU0dpVKaI>. Covers simile, metaphor,
   personification, hyperbole, allusion, alliteration, rhyme, and repetition
   with simple definitions and examples. *Checked 2026-10-19; unverified
   runtime/captions — preview before assigning; direct resource, free,
   no account.* Supports Lessons 01–02 and Practice A/B.
2. **Khan Academy — "Figurative language | Reading"** (YouTube search:
   `Khan Academy figurative language reading`). Khan Academy's ELA series
   teaches identifying figurative expressions and their impact on meaning
   and tone. *Unverified search link — the adult should locate the current
   video on Khan Academy's channel and confirm it matches grade-7 reading
   level; free, no account.* Supports Lessons 01–02.
3. **TED-Ed — "What makes a poem … a poem?" (Melissa Kovacs)** (search
   `ed.ted.com "what makes a poem a poem" Kovacs`). Explores what
   distinguishes poetry from prose — useful framing for the whole unit.
   *Unverified search link; preview for age fit; free, no account.*
   Supports the unit overview and Lesson 03.
4. **Sonnet scansion read-aloud** (YouTube search: `Sonnet 18 read aloud
   scansion`). Hearing the iambic beat makes Lesson 03's investigation
   concrete. *Unverified search link; choose a clear, slow reading; free,
   no account.* Supports Lesson 03 and Practice C.

## Web references

1. **Common Core ELA — Reading: Literature, Grade 7** —
   <https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RL/7/>. Official text of
   RL.7.4, RL.7.5, RL.7.6 (verified verbatim 2026-10-19). Adult reference
   for standards wording; no state adoption claimed.
2. **Common Core ELA — Language, Grade 7** —
   <https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/L/7/>. Official text of
   L.7.5 and L.7.5.a–c (verified verbatim 2026-10-19). Adult reference.
3. **NPS — Carl Sandburg Poetry Collection: Seasonal Poetry** —
   <https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/carl-sandburg-poetry-collection-seasonal-poetry.htm>.
   "Fog" in full from the National Park Service's Sandburg site, with
   context on the poet. *Checked 2026-10-19; reputable (.gov); free, no
   account.* Supports Lesson 01 and the Lesson 05 comparison.
4. **ReadWriteThink — "Dancing Minds and Shouting Smiles: Teaching
   Personification Through Poetry"** —
   <https://www.readwritethink.org/classroom-resources/lesson-plans/dancing-minds-shouting-smiles-860.html?tab=1>.
   NCTE lesson comparing personification across poems — a model for the
   Lesson 05 comparison task. *Checked 2026-10-19; established educator
   organization; free, no account.* Adult/teacher reference.
5. **Kenn Nesbitt's Poetry Dictionary for Kids** —
   <https://poetry4kids.com/tag/dictionary/>. Plain-language glossary of
   poetic terms (alliteration, stanza, sonnet, metaphor…). *Checked
   2026-10-19; age-appropriate; free, no account.* Supports all lessons
   as a quick term-check.
6. **Owlcation — Complete Glossary of Poetic Terms** —
   <http://owlcation.com/humanities/complete-glossary-of-poetic-terms>.
   Broader glossary (volta, enjambment, refrain…) for extension work.
   *Checked 2026-10-19; free, no account; adult should skim for age fit.*
   Supports Lesson 03 extension and the project.

## Accessibility and account notes

Every core objective has a free, no-account route: all poems are printed in
full in the lessons (no external text needed), term definitions live in
`resources/language_arts_literary_terms.md` and the Poetry Dictionary for
Kids, and the standards text is public. Videos are optional enrichment.
The generated illustration ships with a full text alternative in Lesson 01.
