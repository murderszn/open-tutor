# Teacher Guide — Unit 05

For the guiding adult. Student materials never contain answers; this
guide and [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) hold them.

## How to use this unit

- Each lesson is one ~45-minute session; the pacing table in the unit
  [README](README.md) names all 16 sessions. Do not skip the practice
  sessions — the lessons introduce craft moves, the practices make them
  stick, and the project is where they combine.
- Review every written answer the same day. The unit's skills build on
  each other: a shaky Lesson 01 orientation will weaken the project
  draft, because the whole story grows out of its opening; weak
  dialogue in Lesson 03 will weaken the core scene in Workshop 1.
- The anchor story "The Cake" and every practice and assessment passage
  are **original and fictional**. The illustration is an **AI
  illustration of a fictional scene**. If the learner ever treats the
  story's events as real (for example, retelling June as a real person),
  stop and correct it: the story is a mentor text for craft, not a
  report about a real girl.
- The learner's own stories may be inspired by real life, but they must
  not name real people or include real private details (addresses,
  phone numbers, schedules, grades). Check the project draft for this
  before it goes in the portfolio.

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — Orientation and sequence

- Watch for: openings that start with "One day" and no place or person;
  sequences that list events instead of showing what changes. Both are
  caught by the three-question test and the "what changes" sentence.
- If the learner's orientation is vague, ask: "Where would I stand to
  watch this happen?" That question almost always surfaces a place.
- Reteach: re-do the Lesson 01 guided practice with the first two
  paragraphs only, then re-attempt the independent practice 1
  orientation.

### Lesson 02 — Showing and precise words

- Watch for: "showing" that is only more adjectives, and sentences
  that still name the feeling. The cover test is the check: cover the
  feeling word; if nothing is left, the sentence is still telling.
- The illustration's text alternative is not a lesser version of the
  image — it is the same information in words. A learner who uses only
  the alternative must be able to do the same activity, and their
  sentences should be scored the same way.
- The key check is independent practice 1: if the rewrites still
  contain the feeling word (or a synonym of it), re-teach with worked
  example 1 before moving on.

### Lesson 03 — Dialogue

- Watch for: dialogue that reports the plot ("As you know, the bus is
  late…") and tags doing the feeling work ("she said angrily"). The
  three-jobs test catches the first; the beat rewrite catches the
  second.
- The key check is independent practice 1 (the six-line exchange): if
  it has no beat, or if every line only informs the reader, re-teach
  with worked example 1 before the project workshops.
- Do not turn this lesson into a grammar worksheet. The punctuation
  rules are checked against the lesson's own examples; the point is
  dialogue that does work, with the punctuation correct as a matter of
  course.

### Lesson 04 — Pacing and transitions

- Watch for: transitions that all say "then," and the belief that more
  detail is always better. Worked example 1 (the boring scene version
  of the saving) is the corrective — more detail in the wrong place
  makes a story worse.
- The key check is independent practice 2 (the same stretch twice):
  if the learner cannot tell which version is scene and which is
  summary, return to the counting-dialogue support before moving on.
- The Lesson 02 extension versions are reused in independent practice
  1. If the learner did not write them, use the story's saving sentence
  vs. the shelter scene as the pair instead.

### Lesson 05 — Endings

- Watch for: morals ("you should always…"). The reflection test is
  the corrective: could the narrator have said this on page one? A
  moral can be said on page one; a reflection cannot.
- The Dev example (three misses, one make) is reused in the quiz and
  the assessment — keep it straight: the reflection is about belonging
  and about the misses not being the whole story, not about dreams.
- The key check is independent practice 2: if the learner cannot tell
  the stopping ending from the reflecting one, re-teach with worked
  example 2 before the project workshops.

### Lesson 06 — Revision

- Watch for: "revision" that is only spelling fixes. The order rule is
  the corrective: conventions come last, and the adult should refuse
  to check spelling until the revision log has three moves with whys.
- Watch for: accepting every peer suggestion. The reader reports
  experience ("I got lost here"); the writer decides what to change.
- The key check is the applied task: the final draft must come with
  its revision log attached. A polished story with no log is not
  finished work in this unit.

## Scoring guidance

- **Quiz (10 items):** 9–10 = ready for the assessment; 7–8 = review
  the missed lessons' practice sessions the same week; below 7 =
  reteach the missed skills before the assessment session.
- **Unit assessment (12 questions):** score Part 1, Part 2, and Part 3
  separately. A learner may pass Part 1 and fail Part 2 — that means
  revision craft, not reading comprehension, needs reteaching. Record
  one strength and one next step; do not record a single percentage
  as the unit's outcome.
- **Project rubric:** see [project.md](project.md). Score each
  criterion separately; a narrative can be well oriented but weakly
  revised, or well revised but flatly paced. The revision checklist is
  the reteach tool: return the narrative with unchecked items, not
  with a grade.
- **Readiness signals:** the learner is ready for Unit 06 when they can
  (a) orient a reader in two paragraphs, (b) write dialogue that does
  at least one job per line, and (c) end a narrative with a reflection
  that follows from the events.

## What to do when a session runs short or long

- Short: cut the extension, never the exit check. The exit check is the
  only same-day evidence for that lesson's skill.
- Long: move the applied task to the next session's warm-up rather
  than rushing the independent practice — independent practice is where
  misconceptions surface.
