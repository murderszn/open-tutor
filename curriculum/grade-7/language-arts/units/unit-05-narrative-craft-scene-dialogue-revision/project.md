# Project — A Complete Original Narrative

**Driving question:** What happened to *your* character — and what did
it mean to them?
**Deliverable:** a complete original narrative of 600–900 words, with a
story plan, a first draft, a revision log, and a final draft.
**Time:** two workshop sessions (Week 22, Sessions 13–14), plus
finishing at home if needed.

## What the narrative must contain

1. **Orientation** — context (where and when), point of view, narrator
   and characters introduced, and what is at stake (Lesson 01). The
   reader must be able to answer the three orientation questions after
   the first two paragraphs.
2. **One core scene (150–250 words)** — a scene, not a summary, with
   sensory detail in at least three places and one short dialogue
   exchange. This scene is the rebuilt
   [narrative-scene-revision assignment](../../assignments/narrative-scene-revision.md):
   draft the scene, revise it by adding sensory details in at least
   three places, add one short dialogue exchange, and highlight one
   sentence you improved and explain why.
3. **Dialogue that does work** — at least two exchanges in the whole
   narrative. Every line must do at least one job: reveal character,
   reveal relationship, or move the story (Lesson 03). Dialogue
   punctuation follows the Lesson 03 rules.
4. **Pacing on purpose** — at least one stretch told as a scene and at
   least one stretch told as a summary, with transitions that signal
   the shifts in time or setting (Lesson 04).
5. **A reflective conclusion** — an ending that follows from the events
   and reflects on them (Lesson 05). No sudden stop, no tacked-on
   moral, no new event in the ending.
6. **A revision log** — at least three revision moves with a *why* for
   each (Lesson 06), plus the reader check (one partner or adult
   reading, answering "Where did you get lost?" and "What could you
   picture?").

## Rules

- The story, characters, and places are your own invention. Do not
  copy "The Cake" — June, Mrs. Alvarez, and the birthday cake belong
  to the mentor text. You may reuse its *moves* (an object carried
  with care, a stranger's kindness, a wait that ends), not its people
  or plot.
- Real people in your life may inspire a character, but do not write
  about a real person by name, and do not put real private details
  (addresses, phone numbers, schedules, grades) in the story.
- 600–900 words for the final draft. Shorter is unfinished; longer
  needs a reason (ask the adult).
- The illustration from Lesson 02 may be used as your story's prompt
  or setting; if you use it, add one sentence saying what the image
  cannot show about your story.

## Workshop plan

- **Workshop 1 (Session 13):** fill the story plan (below) and draft
  the core scene (150–250 words). The adult checks: does the plan
  answer the orientation questions? Does the scene show instead of
  telling in at least three places?
- **Workshop 2 (Session 14):** draft the full narrative, then revise
  with the checklist below. Read the whole story aloud once before
  calling it done — anything you stumble on, rewrite.

## Story plan (complete before drafting)

- **Character:** who is telling the story, and who else matters?
- **Context:** where and when? What is at stake?
- **Event sequence (4–5 beats):** what changes at each beat?
- **The core scene:** which beat becomes the 150–250-word scene, and
  why that beat?
- **The ending:** what will the narrator understand at the end that
  they do not understand at the start?

## Revision checklist

- [ ] The first two paragraphs answer: where, when, who tells it,
  what is at stake.
- [ ] The core scene has sensory detail in at least three places and
  one dialogue exchange.
- [ ] Every line of dialogue does at least one of the three jobs.
- [ ] At least one scene and at least one summary, with transitions
  between them.
- [ ] The ending reflects on the events (and passes the "could I have
  said this on page one?" test — the answer must be no).
- [ ] The revision log has three or more moves, each with a why.
- [ ] The reader check is recorded ("Where did you get lost?" / "What
  could you picture?").
- [ ] Dialogue punctuation, spelling, and sentence boundaries checked
  last.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Meeting (2) | Exceeding (3) | Developing (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Orientation and sequence** | Context, point of view, and stakes are clear in the opening; events unfold in a logical sequence | Orientation is vivid and economical; the sequence has a beat that changes the story's direction | Opening leaves the reader unsure where, when, or what is at stake; events are listed, not sequenced |
| **Scene, description, dialogue** | Core scene is 150–250 words with sensory detail in 3+ places and one dialogue exchange; dialogue does at least one job per line | Description and dialogue work together — a beat or a sensory detail reveals character without a tag | Core scene is missing, too short, or all summary; dialogue only reports information |
| **Pacing and transitions** | At least one scene and one summary, with transitions signaling the shifts | Pacing is clearly purposeful — the slowest moment is the one that matters most | One speed throughout, or time/setting jumps with no transitions |
| **Reflection and revision** | Ending reflects on the events and follows from them; revision log has 3+ moves with whys; reader check recorded | Ending echoes an earlier line or detail; the log shows a revision that changed the story's meaning | Ending stops, moralizes, or adds a new event; no revision log, or moves without whys |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = strong narrative, ready for the portfolio; 7–9 =
solid, revise one criterion; 4–6 = revise with the adult before
portfolio. The adult records the score and one next step, not a grade
in this repository.

## Extension

Rewrite one scene of your narrative from a different character's point
of view (first person as that character, or third person). Two to three
paragraphs. What does the new narrator notice that the first one
missed — and what can they *not* know? Attach it as an appendix, not
as part of the 600–900 words.
