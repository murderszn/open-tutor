# Unit 05 — Narrative Craft: Scene, Dialogue, and Revision

**Grade:** 7 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 19–22
**Backlog:** [issue #36](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/36), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A story is not a list of things that happened. It is a *scene* the reader
can stand inside, with people who speak in their own voices, time that
speeds up and slows down on purpose, and an ending that means something
to the person who lived it. In this unit the learner studies one original
story — "The Cake," about a thirteen-year-old carrying a birthday cake
home through the rain — and then writes a complete original narrative of
their own: planning it, drafting it, and revising it twice. Along the way
they learn the five moves of narrative craft: orienting the reader,
showing instead of telling, writing dialogue that does work, controlling
pacing with scene and summary, and ending with reflection instead of
just stopping.

## Essential question

What makes a reader feel they were *there* — and what makes the ending
feel earned?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks, the grade-6 track, and U01–U04:

- Determining a theme and analyzing how story elements interact — how
  setting shapes characters and plot (U01, RL.7.3; the anchor story's
  rain is not weather decoration, it is a force on the plot)
- Determining figurative and connotative meanings; analyzing how an
  author develops and contrasts points of view (U02, RL.7.4, RL.7.6;
  the anchor story is first-person, and its point of view is a craft
  choice the learner can name and change)
- Writing an objective summary distinct from opinion (U01; used here
  in reverse — the learner summarizes a scene *before* expanding it,
  so the expansion has something to grow from)
- Explaining the function of phrases and clauses; choosing among
  sentence types; placing modifiers; choosing precise, concise language
  (U04, L.7.1–L.7.3; every revision pass in this unit uses those tools)
- Basic discussion habits: prepared, evidence-based, collegial (all units)

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. **Engage and orient the reader** by establishing a context and point
   of view and introducing a narrator and/or characters, and organize an
   event sequence that unfolds naturally and logically (W.7.3.a).
2. Use **narrative techniques** — dialogue, pacing, and description —
   to develop experiences, events, and/or characters (W.7.3.b).
3. Use **precise words and phrases, relevant descriptive details, and
   sensory language** to capture the action and convey experiences and
   events (W.7.3.d; L.7.3).
4. Use a **variety of transition words, phrases, and clauses** to convey
   sequence and signal shifts from one time frame or setting to another,
   and provide a **conclusion that follows from and reflects on** the
   narrated experiences or events (W.7.3.c, W.7.3.e).
5. Produce **clear, coherent writing** appropriate to task, purpose, and
   audience, and **develop and strengthen writing** through planning,
   revising, editing, and rewriting, with guidance and support from
   peers and adults (W.7.4, W.7.5).

## 16-session pacing (4 weeks × 4 sessions, ~45 minutes each)

Sessions rotate: craft lesson → drafting practice → revision/editing
practice → share and review. **Lessons alone do not fill the unit** —
the named practice, project, quiz, and assessment sessions below are
the rest of the 16.

| Wk | Session | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 19 | 1 | [Lesson 01](lessons/lesson-01-orientation-context-point-of-view.md) — orientation, context, point of view, event sequence | Names context, narrator, and point of view; maps the story's event sequence in order |
| 19 | 2 | **Practice A** — orientation and sequence | Writes a 3-sentence orientation and orders 5 events |
| 19 | 3 | [Lesson 02](lessons/lesson-02-sensory-description-precise-words.md) — sensory description, precise words; illustration activity | Writes 2 showing sentences from the illustration, using the text alternative |
| 19 | 4 | **Practice B** — show, don't tell clinic | Rewrites 4 telling sentences as showing |
| 20 | 5 | [Lesson 03](lessons/lesson-03-dialogue-that-does-work.md) — dialogue that does work | Punctuates 3 exchanges correctly; explains what one line of dialogue reveals |
| 20 | 6 | **Practice C** — dialogue workshop | Writes a 6-line exchange with tags and beats |
| 20 | 7 | [Lesson 04](lessons/lesson-04-pacing-and-transitions.md) — pacing and transitions | Marks scene vs. summary; adds 2 time-shift transitions |
| 20 | 8 | **Practice D** — pacing and transitions | Compresses one stretch, expands one moment |
| 21 | 9 | [Lesson 05](lessons/lesson-05-endings-that-reflect.md) — endings that reflect | Writes a reflective ending for a given scene |

## Differentiation, supports, and extensions

- **Reading access:** the anchor story is printed in Lesson 01 so the
  learner never hunts for a text; the adult reads any section aloud on
  request, and the learner may answer orally, by dictation, or in
  writing.
- **Response modes:** story maps, charts, and tables count as full
  answers wherever a paragraph is not the skill being tested;
  speech-to-text is always allowed for longer writing.
- **Scaffolds:** sentence starters are given in every lesson ("The
  scene opens when…"); the project provides a story-plan frame and a
  partially filled revision log for learners who need it.
- **Extensions:** the project extension asks the learner to rewrite
  one scene of their narrative from a different character's point of
  view; Lesson 02's extension asks for a second illustration-based
  scene written in summary instead of scene, to feel the pacing
  difference.
- **Reteaching signals:** if orientations keep starting with "One day"
  and no place or person, return to Lesson 01's orientation test
  before Lesson 03; if dialogue is all tags and no beats, return to
  Lesson 03's beat routine before the project workshops; if endings
  just stop, return to Lesson 05's reflection test before the final
  draft.
