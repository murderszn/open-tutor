# Practice Sessions — Unit 05

Six practice sessions, one per skill cluster. Each session takes about
45 minutes. All items use the anchor story "The Cake" (Lesson 01), the
generated illustration (Lesson 02), or a new short passage printed
here. Answers are in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## Session A — Orientation and sequence (Week 19, Session 2)

Use "The Cake."

1. Write a three-sentence orientation for a story of your own about a
   boy who finds a lost dog at a train station. Sentence 1 names where
   and when, sentence 2 introduces the narrator or a character,
   sentence 3 names what is at stake. Do not use the words "one day."
2. Put these five events from "The Cake" in the order they happened (chronologically):
   - June rides the bus home and reflects.
   - June leaves the bakery in the rain.
   - Mrs. Alvarez tells her own cake story.
   - June saves for three weeks and misses the earlier bus.
   - June meets Mrs. Alvarez at the shelter.
3. Which two beats of "The Cake" are backstory rather than the main
   scene, and how does the writer signal each time shift?
4. Rewrite this orientation so it passes the three-question test
   (where? who tells it? what is at stake?): "There was a girl. She
   was somewhere. It was a day."
5. Name the story's point of view, and one thing the reader cannot
   know because of that choice.
6. Extension: write a two-sentence orientation for the same cake story
   in third person ("June left the bakery…"). What changes about the
   reader's closeness to June?

## Session B — Show, don't tell clinic (Week 19, Session 4)

1. Rewrite each telling sentence as showing, in one or two sentences
   each. Do not use the feeling word from the original:
   a. "The principal was angry."
   b. "The basement was spooky."
   c. "Dad was proud."
   d. "The hike was exhausting."
2. Cover test: which of these two sentences shows, and which tells?
   "Ana was exhausted." / "Ana set her bag down, missed the chair,
   and sat on the floor instead." Explain in one sentence.
3. Describe the *inside* of the bus in two showing sentences — one
   for touch and one for sound. (Use the bus-stop scene as your
   starting point; invent only details the story allows.)
4. Precise verbs: replace the vague verb "went" in each sentence with
   a precise one, and say what changes:
   a. "June went to the shelter."
   b. "The bus went down the street."
   c. "The rain went on all evening."
5. Error analysis: "The room was very, very, very scary and
   frightening and terrifying." Why doesn't repeating feeling words
   help? Fix it in one showing sentence.
6. Extension: write the cake-box moment as a scene (three sentences,
   showing only) and then as a summary (one sentence). Label each.

## Session C — Dialogue workshop (Week 20, Session 6)

1. Fix the punctuation and capitalization in this exchange:
   > "are you coming to the recital" mara asked. "i wouldn't miss it"
   > her mother said "i'll be in the front row."
2. Write a six-line dialogue between June and Theo the morning after
   the birthday — about the math quiz and the cake. Rules: new
   paragraph per speaker; at least one beat; at most one tag that is
   not "said" or "asked"; the exchange must move the story (we learn
   whether Theo passed, and whether the cake survived).
3. Which of these lines best reveals character, and what does it
   reveal? (a) "The bus is late again," June said. (b) "Then it will
   come in five," Mrs. Alvarez said, "because buses are like that."
   (c) "I know," June said.
4. Turn this tag into a beat: "I'm fine," Dev said sadly.
5. Add dialogue to this bare paragraph — two lines that reveal the
   relationship between Dev and his coach: "Dev walked into the gym.
   The coach looked at him. Dev felt nervous."
6. Extension: write the same two-line exchange twice — once between
   friends, once between strangers. What changes: the words, the
   beats, or both?

## Session D — Pacing and transitions (Week 20, Session 8)

1. Label each stretch **scene** or **summary**, and give one reason:
   a. "Mara practiced every morning for a month. On the day of the
      recital, her hands shook."
   b. "'You are on in five minutes,' the teacher said. Mara picked up
      her violin. Her hands were shaking so hard the bow rattled
      against the strings."
   c. "Three weeks later, the letter came."
   d. "Sam opened the letter slowly, one corner at a time, reading the
      first line twice before he believed it."
2. Compress this scene into a one-sentence summary: "Dev stood outside
   the gym, bouncing the ball. His name was called. He walked in,
   missed three shots, made the fourth, and walked out smiling."
3. Expand this summary into a three-sentence scene: "Mara waited a
   week for the letter, then opened it in the kitchen."
4. Fill each gap with a transition from a *different* family
   (sequence, time passing, setting shift, flashback, cause):
   "___ the rain started. ___ June had missed the earlier bus. ___
   the bus turned the corner. ___ June sat on the bus."
5. Error analysis: "June waited. The bus came. June got on. June went
   home." Name the two craft problems (speed and steering) and fix the
   stretch with two transitions.
6. Extension: take your Practice C dialogue (Session C, item 2) and add
   one summary sentence before it and one after it, so the scene sits
   inside a faster stretch. Underline the two summary sentences.

## Session E — Endings clinic (Week 21, Session 10)

Use the Dev tryout scene from Lesson 05: Dev waits outside the gym,
his name is called, he misses three shots, then makes the fourth.

1. Judge these three endings for the Dev scene — sudden stop, moral,
   or reflection? One reason each:
   a. "Then tryouts were over and Dev went home."
   b. "And that is why you should never give up on your dreams."
   c. "Dev walked home thinking about the fourth shot. It had not
      erased the first three — but it had proved that the first three
      were not the whole story."
2. Write a reflective ending (two sentences) for this scene: "Sam
   finally finished the model rocket he had built all spring. At the
   launch, it flew straight up — then came down in the neighbor's
   tree." Do not use the word "always" or "never."
3. Revise this moral ending into a reflection (two sentences, no new
   event): "And that is why you should always practice."
4. Which line of "The Cake" does the ending echo? Explain the echo in
   one sentence.
5. Two endings for a story about losing a race and learning the
   course. Which one follows from the events, and why?
   a. "Lena learned that winning is not everything."
   b. "Lena walked the course the next morning, slowly, memorizing
      the hill that had beaten her — she had lost the race, but she
      had finally learned the hill."
6. Extension: write two different reflective endings for your own
   project-story idea — one about the main character, one about
   another person in the story. Which is stronger, and why?

## Session F — Revision clinic (Week 21, Session 12)

1. Revise this paragraph. Make three revision moves and log each
   (Move | Why):
   > "Tom was at the park. It was hot and he was bored. He had a kite.
   > The wind was bad. He was sad. A girl was there too. She was nice."
2. Editing pass: fix the dialogue punctuation in this exchange (two
   errors):
   > "Did you bring the kite" Tom asked. "Yes" the girl said, "but the
   > wind is wrong for it."
3. A reader reports: "I got lost when the scene changed, and I could
   picture the dog." Name the revision question each half points to
   (purpose, audience, craft, conventions) and one move for each.
4. Put these four revision steps in the right order: fix the spelling;
   cut a scene that does not belong; check that the ending reflects;
   ask whether a new reader can follow the sequence.
5. Write a revision-log entry (Move | Why) for this change: replacing
   "He was very happy" with "He laughed and spun the chair around."
6. Extension: take your Practice B item 1(a) rewrite ("The principal
   was angry") and revise it again for pacing — make it slower (more
   scene) or faster (more summary). Say which you chose and why.
