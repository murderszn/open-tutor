# Unit 01 — Close Reading: Theme and Interacting Story Elements

**Grade:** 7 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #36](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/36), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Good readers don't just follow what happens — they notice *how* a story is
built and *what* it means. In this unit the learner practices the close
reader's core moves: citing several pieces of textual evidence, separating
inference from stated fact, determining a theme and tracing how it develops,
writing an objective summary, and analyzing how setting, characters, and plot
push on each other. The unit's anchor text is an original short story, "The
Lantern on Greyrock Point," written for this unit so every exercise is
passage-based and rights-clean.

## Essential question

How do a story's elements work together to build a theme the author never
states outright?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the grade-6 track:

- Citing textual evidence for explicit meaning and for inferences (re-taught
  and deepened here with multi-evidence citation; not assumed secure)
- Determining theme/central idea and summarizing distinct from opinion
  (re-taught here at grade-7 depth: theme *development* across a text)
- Describing plot episodes and how characters change
- Grade-6 story-element vocabulary: plot, setting, character, conflict

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Cite **several pieces** of textual evidence to support analysis of what a
   text says explicitly and of inferences drawn from it (RL.7.1).
2. Distinguish an **inference** from a **stated fact**, and defend a
   plausible inference with textual evidence while acknowledging defensible
   alternatives (RL.7.1).
3. Determine a **theme**, write it as a complete theme statement, and analyze
   how it develops over the course of a text (RL.7.2).
4. Write an **objective summary** of a story that is distinct from personal
   opinion or judgment (RL.7.2).
5. Analyze how particular **elements of a story interact** — e.g., how
   setting shapes a character's choices and therefore the plot — and use
   grade-appropriate academic analysis verbs accurately (RL.7.3; L.7.6).

## Vocabulary

explicit · implicit · inference · textual evidence · cite · theme · topic ·
objective summary · plot (exposition, rising action, climax, falling action,
resolution) · conflict (person vs. person, vs. self, vs. nature, vs. society)
· setting (place, time, weather, social conditions) · protagonist ·
antagonist · static / dynamic character · direct / indirect characterization
· analysis verbs: analyze, infer, suggest, reveal, develop, convey, interact,
contrast, trace

## 16-session pacing (4 weeks × 4 sessions, ~40–50 minutes each)

Grade 7 sessions run longer than the K–5 model; each session below names its
activity and success criterion. Lessons, practice, project, and assessments
together fill the unit — six lessons alone do not.

### Week 3 — Evidence and inference (RL.7.1)

- **Session 1:** [Lesson 01](lessons/lesson-01-citing-textual-evidence.md) —
  citing several pieces of evidence for explicit meaning. *Success: the
  learner points to 2+ text details for each answer.*
- **Session 2:** [Practice A](practice.md#session-a--evidence-hunt) — evidence
  hunt in the anchor story. *Success: every claim in the learner's answers is
  followed by a citation.*
- **Session 3:** [Lesson 02](lessons/lesson-02-inference-with-evidence.md) —
  inference vs. stated fact; defending plausible inferences. *Success: the
  learner labels each statement "stated" or "inferred" and cites evidence
  for inferences.*
- **Session 4:** [Practice B](practice.md#session-b--inference-duel) —
  inference duel + discussion. *Success: the learner defends an inference
  with 2+ details and names one defensible alternative.*

### Week 4 — Theme and summary (RL.7.2; L.7.6)

- **Session 5:** [Lesson 03](lessons/lesson-03-theme-and-development.md) —
  topic vs. theme; writing theme statements; tracing development. *Success:
  the learner writes a complete-sentence theme statement (no character
  names).*
- **Session 6:** [Practice C](practice.md#session-c--theme-statement-workshop) —
  theme-statement workshop. *Success: the learner revises a weak theme
  statement using beginning/middle/end evidence.*
- **Session 7:** [Lesson 04](lessons/lesson-04-objective-summary.md) —
  objective summary distinct from opinion. *Success: the learner's summary
  contains no opinion words.*
- **Session 8:** [Practice D](practice.md#session-d--summary-repair-shop) —
  summary repair + discussion. *Success: the learner repairs an
  opinion-contaminated summary.*

### Week 5 — Interacting elements (RL.7.3)

- **Session 9:** [Lesson 05](lessons/lesson-05-setting-shapes-story.md) —
  setting's four parts; setting → character → plot chains; generated
  illustration activity. *Success: the learner names a setting detail and
  the choice it forces.*
- **Session 10:** [Practice E](practice.md#session-e--setting--choice-chains) —
  setting→choice chains. *Success: the learner builds two complete
  setting→choice→plot chains with citations.*
- **Session 11:** [Lesson 06](lessons/lesson-06-elements-interact.md) —
  the element-interaction map; theme emerges from interactions. *Success:
  the learner draws a map with labeled two-way arrows.*
- **Session 12:** [Practice F](practice.md#session-f--interaction-map-studio) —
  interaction-map studio + project launch + discussion. *Success: the learner
  selects a project text and lists its elements.*

### Week 6 — Project, review, assessment

- **Session 13:** [Project](project.md) work session — dossier drafting
  (interaction map, theme statement + citations, summary, counterfactual).
  *Success: all four dossier parts drafted.*
- **Session 14:** [Formative quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md) (revised
  story-elements quiz, passage-anchored, with answer key in the teacher
  materials) + feedback and re-teaching. *Success: gaps named; re-teach plan
  set (see [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md)).*
- **Session 15:** [Unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md) — new
  passage, independent. *Success: completed without the key; scored with the
  [answer key](answer-key.md).*
- **Session 16:** Portfolio — final theme statements and reflection; unit
  close. *Success: the learner files a revised theme statement and names one
  close-reading move they will reuse.*

## Anchor text

"The Lantern on Greyrock Point" is an **original story written for this
unit** (full text in Lesson 01). Because it is original, every quotation,
quiz item, and assessment reference is rights-clean. For the project, the
learner may alternatively work with an Aesop fable (public domain; adult
selects and retells) or an adult-selected myth from
`resources/language_arts_great_books_and_stories.md` — the adult previews
any external text before assigning it.

## Differentiation and supports

- **Reading access:** the anchor story may be read aloud by the adult or
  with text-to-speech; chunk it into the numbered paragraphs (1–12) and stop
  to cite evidence after each chunk.
- **Response modes:** exit checks and practice may be answered orally or
  dictated; the interaction map may be drawn, built with cards, or dictated.
- **Language support:** the vocabulary list is pre-taught with the story;
  sentence frames are provided in every lesson ("The text says ___, which
  shows ___").
- **Extension:** after Session 12, strong readers apply the full close-reading
  routine to an adult-selected myth (Icarus, Prometheus, or King Midas via
  the great-books guide) and compare its theme statement with the anchor
  story's.
