# Lesson 04 — Objective Summary: The Facts Without the Feelings

**Student goal:** I can write a short summary of a story that reports what
happens without adding my opinions or judgments.
**Time:** 40–50 minutes · **Unit:** [U01](../README.md) · **Session 7 of 16**
**Standard:** RL.7.2

## Materials

- "The Lantern on Greyrock Point" (Lesson 01)
- Notebook

## Readiness check (5 minutes)

Read aloud: "Mara is the bravest girl ever, and her mean grandfather finally
admits she was right." Ask: which words are *facts from the story* and which
are *the speaker's opinions*? ("bravest girl ever" and "mean" are opinions;
"admits she was right" is close to ¶12 but "finally" editorializes.) An
**objective** summary keeps only the facts.

## Teaching

An **objective summary** retells a text's key events accurately and
neutrally — no praise, no blame, no "I think," no adjectives the author
didn't earn. It is distinct from personal opinion: the summary answers
*what happened*, never *was it good*.

Use the **Somebody–Wanted–But–So** frame to stay objective and complete:

- **Somebody** (who): thirteen-year-old Mara, who lives at the Greyrock
  Point lighthouse with her grandfather, Keeper Hale.
- **Wanted** (goal): to keep the lamp burning through a gale so the overdue
  fishing boat *Alder* can find the harbor.
- **But** (obstacle): the oil is leaking and will run out by 2 a.m.; Hale
  orders her to stay inside because the gallery ices over.
- **So** (outcome): Mara climbs to the lamp room against orders, keeps the
  flame alive, the *Alder* rounds the point safely, and Hale gives her his
  keeper's badge.

Four to six sentences built on that frame cover the whole story. Leave out
anything that doesn't change the outcome (the color of the clouds, the exact
time she laughs) — a summary is a *reduction*, not a retelling.

**Key vocabulary:** *objective* (based on facts, free of personal opinion),
*summary* (a brief, accurate retelling of key events).

## Worked example 1 — spotting opinion contamination

The adult reads a flawed summary and thinks aloud:

> "'Mara heroically defies her stubborn grandfather and beautifully saves
> the helpless crew.' Facts check: Mara does defy the order (¶7), and the
> *Alder* does reach harbor (¶9). But 'heroically,' 'stubborn,'
> 'beautifully,' and 'helpless' are *my* judgments — the text never uses
> them. 'Helpless crew' is even invented: we never meet the crew. Repair:
> 'Mara disobeys Hale's order, climbs to the lamp room, and keeps the lamp
> lit; the *Alder* rounds the point and reaches the harbor.' Same facts,
> zero adjectives of mine."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** every adjective in a summary must pass the
test *"does the text say this, or do I?"* If it's mine, it goes.

## Worked example 2 — building the summary from the frame

> "Draft from Somebody–Wanted–But–So: 'Mara, thirteen, lives with her
> grandfather Keeper Hale at the Greyrock Point lighthouse. During an
> October gale, the lamp's oil is leaking and the fishing boat *Alder* is
> three hours overdue. Hale orders Mara to stay inside, but she climbs to
> the lamp room and keeps the flame burning through the night. The *Alder*
> rounds the point safely, and Hale gives Mara his keeper's badge.' Five
> sentences. Check: any opinion words? 'Safely' — is it opinion? The text
> says the boat's lights 'reached the harbor mouth' (¶9) — 'safely' is a
> fair paraphrase of reaching harbor, not praise. Keep it. Nothing about
> brave, stubborn, or heroic. Objective."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** I checked each sentence against the frame
(who, goal, obstacle, outcome) and ran the adjective test on every
descriptive word.

## Guided practice

With the adult, repair this summary: "The stupid leak almost ruins
everything, but lucky Mara miraculously saves the day and her proud grandpa
rewards her." Name each opinion word, then rewrite the sentence objectively.

## Independent practice

1. Write a 4–6 sentence objective summary of "The Lantern on Greyrock
   Point" using the Somebody–Wanted–But–So frame.
2. Exchange with the adult: circle any word in your partner's summary that
   fails the "does the text say this, or do I?" test.
3. **Error analysis:** "In conclusion, Mara is a hero and Hale was wrong to
   doubt her." Explain in two sentences why this cannot appear in an
   objective summary, even though the story supports admiring Mara.

## Application

Shrink your summary to **exactly two sentences** without losing who,
goal, obstacle, or outcome. What did you cut, and why was it safe to cut?

## Exit check

"Write a three-sentence objective summary of paragraphs 6–8 only (Mara's
decision and climb)." Secure = three sentences, all facts from ¶¶6–8, no
opinion words; Developing = facts right with one opinion word; Not yet =
retells with judgments or misses the outcome.

## Supports and extension

- **Frame on paper:** copy the Somebody–Wanted–But–So prompts and answer
  each in one sentence before drafting.
- **Opinion-word hunt:** keep a running list of banned words (best, worst,
  stupid, amazing, heroically) on the notebook's inside cover.
- **Extension:** write a *deliberately biased* two-sentence summary from
  Hale's point of view ("my disobedient granddaughter risked her life…"),
  then label every word that makes it non-objective. What does the
  comparison teach you about point of view?

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

Learners confuse *summary* with *review* ("I liked it because…") — the
Somebody–Wanted–But–So frame blocks this structurally. They also smuggle
opinions inside verbs ("miraculously saves," "finally admits") — teach the
adjective/verb test, not just the adjective test. Finally, some learners
write six *beginning* sentences and skip the ending; require all four frame
slots before drafting. Answer-key reference:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) § Lesson 04.
