# Unit Assessment — Unit 01: Close Reading, Theme, and Interacting Story Elements

**Standards:** RL.7.1, RL.7.2, RL.7.3; L.7.6 · **Time:** 50–60 minutes,
independent
**Directions:** Read the new story below (original, written for this
assessment) twice. Then answer all questions in complete sentences, citing
paragraph numbers. The answer key and scoring guidance are in
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) § Assessment — this page stays
key-free.

## Reading passage — "The Winter Market" (original)

> **1.** Every December, the town of Calleva held its winter market in the
> square below the old clock tower, and every December, Tomas sold his
> grandfather's carved wooden birds from a borrowed table. This year was
> different: his grandfather's hands had stiffened with arthritis, and the
> box Tomas carried held only eleven birds — the last his grandfather would
> ever carve.
>
> **2.** By midday Tomas had sold nine. A collector in a fur-trimmed coat
> offered him forty crowns for the last two — more than the table had
> earned all morning. Tomas's fingers closed around the coins in his
> pocket: twelve crowns so far, enough for the medicine his mother had been
> unable to afford since autumn.
>
> **3.** Then small, fierce-looking Ana Beltran arrived, dragging her little
> brother Pipo, whose birthday was that very day. Pipo pressed his nose to
> the last two birds — a wren and a kingfisher — and whispered, "They look
> alive." Ana counted her coins aloud: six crowns. She looked at Tomas, then
> at the collector, then back at Tomas.
>
> **4.** Tomas looked at the collector's forty crowns. He looked at Pipo's
> face. He lifted the wren and the kingfisher from the table and held them
> out to Ana. "Six crowns," he said. "For the pair — because it's his
> birthday."
>
> **5.** The collector sniffed and walked away. Ana stared. "You could have
> had forty," she whispered.
>
> **6.** "My grandfather carved birds so people would have something alive
> in winter," Tomas said. "Forty crowns can't do that." He tucked the six
> coins into his pocket next to the twelve, and walked home through the
> snow thinking of his grandfather's hands — and of Pipo's face when the
> wren landed in his palm.

---

1. **Explicit evidence (RL.7.1).** Why are there only eleven birds to sell
   this year? Quote or paraphrase the text and cite the paragraph. (3 pts)
2. **Inference (RL.7.1).** What can you infer about Tomas's family's
   financial situation? Give two pieces of textual evidence and label your
   answer as an inference. (4 pts)
3. **Inference with alternative (RL.7.1).** Why does Tomas sell the last
   two birds to Ana for six crowns instead of to the collector for forty?
   Support your answer with two details, and name one alternative reading
   the evidence also allows. (5 pts)
4. **Theme statement (RL.7.2).** Write a complete-sentence theme statement
   for this story on the topic of *generosity*. Then verify it against the
   three tests in writing. (5 pts)
5. **Theme development (RL.7.2).** Trace your generosity theme across the
   beginning (¶¶1–2), middle (¶¶3–4), and end (¶¶5–6): one cited detail per
   stage, using at least one academic verb (*develops, reveals, conveys,
   suggests*). (6 pts)
6. **Objective summary (RL.7.2).** Write a 4–5 sentence objective summary
   of the story. (5 pts — deduct 1 per opinion word, to a floor of 0)
7. **Elements interact (RL.7.3).** Explain how the **setting** (winter
   market, December, snow, the last birds) shapes Tomas's **choice** and
   therefore the **plot**. Build the full chain with citations. (5 pts)
8. **Error analysis (RL.7.2).** A classmate writes: "The theme is that
   Tomas is generous." Identify exactly what is wrong with this as a theme
   statement (which test does it fail, and why?), then rewrite it as a
   proper theme statement. (4 pts)

**Total: 37 points.** 31–37 = secure; 24–30 = proficient, revise one
criterion; below 24 = re-teach per the [teacher
guide](../teacher-guide.md) and reassess with an alternate passage from the
adult.
