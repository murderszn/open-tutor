# Project — Theme & Elements Dossier

**Sessions 12–13 (launch in Practice F, drafted in Session 13, filed in
Session 16).** The learner builds a four-part dossier proving a theme for a
story of their choice — the unit's full close-reading routine applied
independently.

## Choose your text (adult and learner together)

- **Option A — "The Lantern on Greyrock Point"** (the anchor story): go
  deeper — find a *second* defensible theme and prove it.
- **Option B — an Aesop fable:** the adult selects one fable (e.g., "The
  Tortoise and the Hare," "The Boy Who Cried Wolf," "The Ant and the
  Grasshopper") and reads or retells it aloud. Public-domain English texts
  are available via the [resource pack](resource-pack.md); the adult
  previews before assigning.
- **Option C — an adult-selected myth:** Icarus, Prometheus, or King Midas,
  chosen via `resources/language_arts_great_books_and_stories.md`. The
  adult provides a summary or a previewed public-domain retelling — never
  assign a linked title unpreviewed.

Whichever text is chosen, the learner may ask the adult to read it aloud.

## Deliverables (four parts, one folder)

1. **Element interaction map** — five element boxes (character, setting,
   plot, conflict, theme) with at least six arrows, each labeled with its
   mechanism (*forces, tests, reveals, confirms, redefines…*) and a text
   citation. Theme is drawn as a banner (the system's output), not a sixth
   box.
2. **Theme statement + evidence** — one complete-sentence theme statement
   (three tests applied in writing) plus a beginning/middle/end development
   trace with one cited detail per stage.
3. **Objective summary** — 4–6 sentences, Somebody–Wanted–But–So, zero
   opinion words.
4. **Setting-change counterfactual** — one paragraph: move the story to a
   different setting and explain which plot events become impossible and
   what that proves about how setting interacts with plot.

## Rubric (16 points)

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Proficient | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Evidence use** (RL.7.1) | Every claim cites the text; several pieces per major claim; quotes/paraphrases accurate | Most claims cited; at least one citation per claim | Some claims cited; citations vague or miscited | Claims without textual support |
| **Theme statement & development** (RL.7.2) | Complete sentence, no names, story-provable; three-stage trace with distinct cited details | Statement passes 3 tests; trace covers 3 stages, one thin | Statement fails one test; trace covers 2 stages | Topic phrase or plot summary; no trace |
| **Element interaction** (RL.7.3) | ≥6 labeled arrows with mechanisms + citations; paragraph explains theme emerging from interactions | ≥6 arrows, most labeled; paragraph names interacting elements | Arrows present, few labeled; paragraph retells plot | Boxes without arrows, or unlabeled decoration |
| **Objective summary** (RL.7.2) | 4–6 sentences; all four frame slots; zero opinion words | Frame complete; one opinion word slipped | Frame incomplete or 2+ opinion words | Opinion piece, not a summary |

**Scoring:** 14–16 = secure, ready for U02; 10–13 = proficient with one
targeted revision (adult names the criterion); below 10 = re-teach per the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) re-teaching map, then revise one
criterion and rescore.

## Adult notes

- Option B and C texts must be previewed by the adult for length and
  reading level (grades 6–8 band). Fables are short; the learner should
  still find *three-stage* development — in a fable the stages compress to
  setup/choice/consequence.
- Accept any defensible theme statement that passes the three tests — the
  [answer key](answer-key.md) § Project gives one sample, not the only
  answer.
- The counterfactual is the RL.7.3 check: look for the removal-test logic,
  not for a "correct" new setting.
