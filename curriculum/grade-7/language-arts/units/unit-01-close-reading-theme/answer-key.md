# Answer Key — Unit 01: Close Reading, Theme, and Interacting Story Elements

**Adult only.** Every item below was solved independently from the student
questions; accept any defensible alternative that cites the text and meets
the stated criteria. "The Lantern on Greyrock Point" paragraphs are numbered
¶1–¶12 in [Lesson 01](lessons/lesson-01-citing-textual-evidence.md); "The
Winter Market" paragraphs are ¶1–¶6 in the
[assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md).

## Lesson 01 — Citing textual evidence

**Guided**
1. Weather: gale by midnight (¶2, radio crackle); wind rose before supper
   with slate-colored clouds stacking (¶2); at eleven the wind howls through
   the tower seams (¶6); ice needles Mara's cheeks on the gallery (¶7). Any
   two cited.
2. Hale's order: "You stay inside. That's an order, not a suggestion." (¶5)
3. Mara's father was lost to "these same rocks six years ago, when the old
   light had failed in a storm" (¶6).

**Independent**
4. Hale cannot fix it himself that night because (a) he needs sleep before
   the morning repair — "You need sleep before the morning repair" is
   Mara's suggestion (¶4), and the repair is scheduled for morning,
   implying parts/daylight are needed; (b) the immediate crisis is the
   overnight burn rate, which requires *tending*, not a repair he can do in
   the dark during a gale. Accept: the leak needs a morning repair (¶3–¶4);
   the lamp needs all-night trimming, which is why Mara volunteers the
   watch (¶4). Two details cited.
5. Action: he unpins the brass keeper's badge from his own coat and presses
   it into her palm (¶12). Words: "A keeper's first duty is to the light,
   Mara. You understood that before I could teach it. But next time — you
   tell me first." (¶12)
6. "The *Alder* sank" is **unsupported** — contradicted, in fact: ¶9 states
   the *Alder* "rounded the point, her running lights winking," and Mara
   stays until "the boat's lights reached the harbor mouth." Correction must
   cite ¶9.

## Lesson 02 — Inference with evidence

**Guided** (stated vs. inferred)
1. "The *Alder* is a fishing boat" — **stated** (¶1 "the fishing boats of
   Harrow Bay"; ¶2 "the fishing boat *Alder*").
2. "Mara is afraid on the gallery walk" — **inferred**. The text states ice
   needles her cheeks and she crawls on hands and knees (¶7); "afraid" is
   our reading of those clues. (Accept "stated" only if the learner argues
   crawling + ice *show* fear explicitly — discuss the boundary.)
3. "Hale has been keeper for at least eleven years" — **inferred** (¶1: the
   lamp "has burned every night" for eleven years and it is "Hale's pride"
   — strongly implies his tenure; not outright stated).
4. "The crew sees the lamp and steers for harbor" — **inferred**. Stated:
   the boat rounds the point and reaches the harbor mouth (¶9). That the
   crew *saw* the lamp is our inference — reasonable but unstated.

**Independent**
5. Sample: "The text states the wind 'struck her like a wall' and 'ice
   needled her cheeks' (¶7), and I know that standing upright in a gale on
   an icy narrow walk risks being blown off, so I infer she crawls to stay
   low and keep her balance." Accept any version with stated detail +
   ordinary knowledge + inference labeled.
6. Sample inference: Hale waits because he already knows what she did and
   chooses to hear her out rather than drag her back mid-crisis — evidence:
   he is "waiting at the stair foot" (¶9), and his first words address the
   disobedience calmly (¶10) rather than with alarm. Alternative: he was too
   late to stop her and waited because going up himself in the gale would
   risk two lives. Both defensible.
7. "Mara's father is alive and works on the *Alder*" fails: ¶6 states her
   father was "lost to these same rocks six years ago" — "lost" in a storm
   context means died; and the *Alder* is described only as overdue (¶2),
   never as carrying her father. Two cited contradictions required.

## Lesson 03 — Theme and development

**Guided**
1. Sample: "Fear is natural, but duty can require acting through it."
   Tests: complete sentence ✓; no names ✓; story-provable — Mara acts
   despite the iced gallery (¶7), and the badge confirms the act was right
   (¶12) ✓.
2. Middle detail for "responsibility outweighs obedience": Mara's thought
   "If the lamp dies at two, the Alder dies with it" (¶6) — the moment duty
   overrides the order in her mind; or her climb "two at a time" with the
   kitchen lantern (¶7).

**Independent**
3. Sample duty theme: "Duty to protect others outranks personal safety and
   obedience alike." Tests: sentence ✓, no names ✓, provable — Mara risks
   the iced gallery (¶7) against a direct order (¶5), and Hale's badge
   ratifies it (¶12) ✓.
4. Sample trace: Beginning — the story *develops* the theme by establishing
   Hale's rule that "a keeper's first duty is to the light" (¶12 stated at
   the end but the promise to the boats is planted in ¶1). Middle — Mara's
   decision *reveals* duty overriding obedience (¶¶6–7). End — the badge
   *conveys* that the community of keepers judges her choice correct (¶12).
   Require one cited detail per stage and two academic verbs.
5. "Mara learns that lighthouses are important" fails test 1 (not a claim
   about life — it's a plot observation) and test 2 (names the story's
   specific object; it doesn't travel). Rewrite: "People who accept
   responsibility for others' safety must sometimes act alone." (Passes all
   three.)

## Lesson 04 — Objective summary

**Guided repair.** Contaminants: "stupid" (adjective of judgment), "almost
ruins everything" (judgment + vagueness), "lucky" (judgment), "miraculously
saves the day" (verb/adverb of judgment), "proud grandpa" ("proud" is
arguably inferable from the badge, but as a summary word it's
editorializing — cut it). Rewrite: "The lamp's oil is leaking and will run
out by 2 a.m. during a gale, with the fishing boat *Alder* overdue. Hale
orders Mara to stay inside, but she climbs to the lamp room and keeps the
flame burning. The *Alder* reaches the harbor, and Hale gives Mara his
keeper's badge."

**Independent**
1. Sample 5-sentence summary: "Thirteen-year-old Mara lives with her
   grandfather, lighthouse keeper Hale, at Greyrock Point. On a gale night
   in October, the lamp's oil is leaking and the fishing boat *Alder* is
   three hours overdue. Hale orders Mara to stay inside because the gallery
   walk ices over. Mara disobeys, climbs to the lamp room, and tends the
   flame until the *Alder* rounds the point and reaches the harbor. Hale
   gives her his brass keeper's badge." Zero opinion words.
2. Accept any correctly identified opinion word with the "text-or-me" test
   applied.
3. "Mara is a hero and Hale was wrong" cannot appear because a summary
   reports *what happened*, not *what it means* — "hero" and "was wrong"
   are the writer's judgments, and summaries are distinct from opinion by
   definition (RL.7.2). Admiring Mara is fine in *analysis*; it is banned in
   *summary*.

## Lesson 05 — Setting shapes the story

**Guided chain.** Setting detail: "the *Alder* still out past the reef,
overdue by three hours" (¶2). Choice: Mara decides the lamp must burn at
full trim all night despite the leak (¶¶6–8). Plot event: the *Alder* sees
the light and rounds the point (¶9). Removal test: no overdue boat → no
urgency → Mara could obey the order and trim the lamp low — no dilemma.

**Independent**
1. Social-setting chain: Hale's authority as grandfather *and* keeper (¶5
   "That's an order"; ¶12 "A keeper's first duty") → Mara must choose
   between two duties (obey grandfather vs. serve the light) → the
   disobedience-and-badge plot. Removal: if Hale were only her grandfather
   (no keeper's code), the badge ending and the "duty" theme collapse.
2. Sample decorative: "clouds the color of wet slate" (¶2) — removal
   changes nothing causal. Sample structural: the 2 a.m. oil deadline (¶3)
   — removal removes the all-night watch and Mara's reason to act *now*.
3. Rebuttal must cite two setting details doing causal work, e.g.: the iced
   gallery *causes* the forbidding order (¶5), and the leak's 2 a.m.
   deadline *causes* the overnight watch (¶3) — bravery alone, on a calm
   night with a full reservoir, produces no story.

## Lesson 06 — Elements interact

**Guided arrows.** Character (Mara) → plot: "**decides**: Mara's decision to
disobey *is* the rising action — without it there is no climb (¶7)."
Plot → character: "**proves/changes**: surviving the night changes how Hale
sees her — granddaughter to keeper, marked by the badge (¶12)."

**Independent**
1. Accept any six arrows with distinct mechanisms + citations. Check: each
   arrow names a *mechanism* (forces, tests, reveals, confirms, redefines),
   not just an event.
2. Sample paragraph: "The theme 'responsibility outweighs obedience'
   *emerges* from the interaction of setting and conflict: the leaking oil
   and gale *create* a dilemma no order can resolve, Mara's choice *tests*
   both duties, and the *Alder's* safe return *confirms* which duty ranked
   higher. The badge scene then *reveals* the story's verdict."
3. Diagnosis: twelve unlabeled arrows show *that* elements connect but not
   *how* — analysis requires the mechanism. Sample repairs: "Setting
   (gale) → character (Hale): **forces** the stay-inside order (¶5)."
   "Character (Mara) → plot: **decides** the disobedience that drives the
   rising action (¶7)."

## Practice sessions

**A — Evidence hunt.** 1. "Hale has been keeper for eleven years":
*unsupported as stated* — ¶1 says the *lamp* has burned eleven years and it
is Hale's pride; his exact tenure is inferred, not stated (good trap —
discuss). "Mara has climbed before": unsupported — no text says so. "The
*Alder* is in danger": supported — overdue three hours past the reef with a
gale by midnight (¶2); Mara believes "the Alder dies with it" if the lamp
dies (¶6). 2. Three problems: the gale arriving by midnight (¶2); the oil
leak with the 2 a.m. deadline (¶3); the *Alder* overdue past the reef (¶2);
Hale's order confining her inside (¶5) — any three cited. 3. "You stay
inside. That's an order, not a suggestion." (¶5)

**B — Inference duel.** Ten sort statements (5 stated / 5 inferred):
*Stated:* the lamp has burned eleven years (¶1); the *Alder* is three hours
overdue (¶2); the reservoir leaks (¶3); Hale orders Mara inside (¶5); the
badge is brass (¶12). *Inferred:* Mara loves her grandfather; the *Alder's*
crew is grateful; Hale is proud; Mara will become keeper one day; the storm
is the worst in years. Duel positions: "father-memory" side cites ¶6's
placement of the memory at the decision point; "keeper's-duty" side cites
¶12's "first duty is to the light." Both defensible — the steel-man round
is the point.

**C — Theme workshop.** Four statements: (a) "Duty to others can require
disobeying orders." — passes all three. (b) "Mara is brave." — fails test
1 (fragment) and 2-adjacent (judgment of a character, not a claim about
life). (c) "The theme of the story is the lighthouse." — fails test 1
(topic phrase). (d) "When lives are at stake, responsibility outweighs
obedience." — passes. Repairs: (b) → "Courage means acting for others
despite fear." (c) → "Isolated duty forges self-reliance."

**D — Summary repair.** Seeded summary: "Mara **bravely** climbs the tower
in the **terrible** storm because her **foolish** grandfather won't do it
himself, **miraculously** keeping the lamp lit until the **lucky** *Alder*
**stumbles** into harbor, proving she was **obviously** right all along."
Contaminants: bravely, terrible, foolish, miraculously, lucky, stumbles,
obviously (7 — "five" in the student text is approximate; accept 5+
found). Rewrite per Lesson 04 key.

**E — Setting chains.** Time chain: night + 2 a.m. deadline (¶3) → Mara
must tend the lamp *through the night*, not once → the all-night watch and
the *Alder's* 1:30 rounding (¶9). Weather chain: gale icing the gallery
(¶5) → Hale's order → Mara's dilemma and crawl (¶7). Social chain: keeper's
duty code (¶¶1, 12) → Hale's pride in the unbroken light vs. Mara's
competing duty → the badge verdict. Exit: the **oil leak** (removal kills
the deadline, the watch, and the dilemma) or the **gale** (removes the
order and the danger) — either defended causally.

**F — Map critique.** Flawed map diagnosis: boxes with arrows but no
mechanism labels or citations = decoration (Lesson 06). Sample repairs:
"Conflict (obey vs. save) → theme: **produces** — the dilemma manufactures
the theme (¶¶5–7)." "Plot (*Alder* survives) → character (Hale): **changes**
— Hale reclassifies Mara from child to keeper (¶12)."

## Quiz (week-04) — 3 pts each, 30 total

1. Stages: exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, resolution.
   Climax: ¶8–¶9 (Mara holds the flame; the *Alder* rounds the point) — the
   turning point because before it the outcome is uncertain and after it
   the danger resolves. Accept ¶9 alone with the same reasoning.
2. Conflicts: person vs. nature — the gale/ice threaten Mara on the gallery
   (¶7); person vs. person (authority) — Hale's order vs. Mara's
   disobedience (¶¶5, 10); person vs. self — Mara's fear vs. her resolve
   (¶6–¶7). Any two cited.
3. Setting: place — Greyrock Point lighthouse on sea cliffs; time — last
   evening of October, night; weather — rising gale, ice, black water;
   social — keeper's duty to the boats; grandfather's authority. Setting →
   choice: the icing gallery (weather) forces Hale's stay-inside order
   (¶5). Any complete four-part + one chained choice.
4. **Dynamic**: beginning — obedient granddaughter who asks permission (¶4);
   end — badge-bearing keeper-in-fact who acted on her own judgment
   (¶12). Static would require no change — contradicted by the badge.
5. Direct: "thirteen-year-old Mara" (¶1) — stated outright. Indirect:
   Mara "took the stairs two at a time" / crawled "on her hands and knees"
   (¶7) — we infer determination/fear from action. Any correctly labeled
   pair.
6. Sample: "Courage means acting to protect others even when you are
   afraid." Tests: complete sentence ✓; no names ✓; provable — ¶7 (acts
   despite ice/fear), ¶12 (badge confirms) ✓.
7. Beginning: Hale's promise to the boats / the stay-inside order plants
   duty-vs.-obedience (¶¶1, 5). Middle: Mara's thought "If the lamp dies at
   two, the Alder dies with it" and her climb — duty overrides order
   (¶¶6–7). End: the badge ratifies her choice (¶12).
8. Inference: pride (and relief) — evidence: he gives his *own* brass
   badge (¶12), an honor, and says she "understood [duty] before I could
   teach it" (praise, ¶12). Alternative: conflicted pride — his face was
   "unreadable" (¶9) and he still names it disobedience (¶10).
9. Score against the Lesson 04 key: 4–5 sentences, all four frame slots,
   zero opinion words = 3; one opinion word = 2; missing a frame slot = 2;
   two+ opinion words or missing outcome = 1.
10. Sample chain: leaking oil + gale (setting, ¶¶2–3) → Mara disobeys to
    tend the lamp (choice, ¶7) → the *Alder* rounds the point (plot, ¶9) →
    produces the theme "responsibility outweighs obedience." Proves:
    theme is not declared but *manufactured* by the elements interacting —
    remove the setting pressure and the theme has no story.

## Assessment ("The Winter Market") — 37 pts

1. His grandfather's hands stiffened with arthritis; the box held "the last
   his grandfather would ever carve" (¶1). (3)
2. Inference: money is tight. Evidence: twelve crowns is "enough for the
   medicine his mother had been unable to afford since autumn" (¶2) —
   unaffordable medicine implies need; Ana's six coins are counted aloud as
   if precious (¶3). Label "inference" required. (4)
3. Tomas values Pipo's joy / his grandfather's purpose over maximum profit:
   "My grandfather carved birds so people would have something alive in
   winter. Forty crowns can't do that" (¶6); he accepts six crowns "because
   it's his birthday" (¶4). Alternative: he knows the collector would resell
   coldly while the birds "look alive" to Pipo (¶3) — i.e., motive is
   honoring the carvings' purpose, not just kindness. (5)
4. Sample: "Generosity means giving up what you want so someone else can
   have what they need." Tests: sentence ✓; no names ✓; provable — Tomas
   surrenders forty crowns for six (¶¶2–4) ✓. (5)
5. Beginning: the story *develops* generosity's cost — forty crowns vs.
   twelve, medicine unaffordable (¶2). Middle: Tomas's choice *reveals*
   generosity in action — "Six crowns… because it's his birthday" (¶4).
   End: his words *convey* the theme's meaning — "Forty crowns can't do
   that" (¶6). One cited detail per stage + academic verb. (6)
6. Sample: "Each December Tomas sells his grandfather's carved wooden
   birds at Calleva's winter market. This year only eleven remain, the last
   his arthritic grandfather will carve. A collector offers forty crowns
   for the final two, but Tomas sells them to Ana for six crowns so her
   brother Pipo can have them on his birthday. Tomas walks home thinking of
   his grandfather's hands and Pipo's delight." Deduct 1 per opinion word.
   (5)
7. Chain: December market + snow + the last-ever birds (setting, ¶¶1–2) →
   Tomas must choose between forty crowns (medicine money) and Pipo's
   birthday joy (choice, ¶¶3–4) → he sells for six, the collector leaves
   (plot, ¶¶4–5). Without the "last birds" scarcity and the medicine need,
   the choice costs nothing and the plot has no tension. (5)
8. "The theme is that Tomas is generous" fails test 1 (no claim about life
   — it's a character judgment) and test 2 (names Tomas; doesn't travel).
   Rewrite: "Generosity is measured by what you give up, not by what you
   keep." (4)

## Project — sample responses (one valid set; accept alternatives)

- **Theme statement:** "Responsibility to others can outweigh obedience to
  authority." Three tests pass; trace: beginning — Hale's order and the
  keeper's code collide (¶¶1, 5); middle — Mara chooses the lamp (¶¶6–7);
  end — the badge ratifies the choice (¶12).
- **Objective summary:** the Lesson 04 key's five-sentence sample.
- **Counterfactual:** "Set the story in a desert relay station at noon: no
  gale means no icing order, so Mara faces no disobedience dilemma; the
  leak's deadline could become a water-pump failure at sundown. The duty
  theme survives only if the new setting supplies equivalent pressure —
  proving the original setting did causal work, not decoration."
- **Rubric notes:** score Evidence on citation density, not length; a
  fable dossier's three stages are setup/choice/consequence — accept the
  compression explicitly.
