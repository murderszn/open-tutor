# Practice, Discussion, and Review Sessions — Unit 01

Named sessions referenced by the [16-session pacing](README.md#16-session-pacing-4-weeks--4-sessions-40-50-minutes-each).
Each takes 40–50 minutes. The adult observes, gives hints on practice days,
and records exit checks. Sample responses for every task live in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md) § Practice — student pages never carry keys.

## Session A — Evidence hunt

*Session 2 (after L1). Materials: anchor story, two colored pencils.*

1. **Guided — claim clinic (15 min):** The adult gives three claims; the
   learner finds 2+ cited details for each or rejects the claim as
   unsupported:
   - "Hale has been keeper for eleven years."
   - "Mara has climbed to the lamp room before."
   - "The *Alder* is in danger before Mara acts."
2. **Independent — citation sprint (15 min):** Answer: "What three problems
   stack up against Mara on this night?" Every problem needs its own
   paragraph citation, quoted or paraphrased.
3. **Exit check:** "Quote the exact sentence in which Hale gives his order."
   Secure = exact quote with ¶5; Developing = paraphrase with ¶5; Not yet =
   wrong paragraph or invented wording.

## Session B — Inference duel

*Session 4 (after L2). Discussion-heavy session.*

1. **Guided — stated vs. inferred sort (15 min):** The adult reads ten
   statements about the story (mix of stated facts and inferences from
   [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) § Practice B). The learner sorts them
   into two columns and cites evidence for every "inferred" card.
2. **Independent + discussion — the duel (20 min):** The learner argues for
   one inference ("Mara acts mostly from love of her father" vs. "Mara acts
   mostly from keeper's duty"), citing 2+ details. The adult argues the
   other side. Then switch: each side names the strongest point of the
   *opposing* case.
3. **Exit check:** "Write one inference about Hale and one alternative the
   evidence also allows." Secure = both stated with citations; Developing =
   inference without alternative; Not yet = inference presented as fact.

## Session C — Theme statement workshop

*Session 6 (after L3).*

1. **Guided — test the statements (15 min):** The adult offers four theme
   attempts (two strong, two failing one of the three tests — see
   [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) § Practice C). The learner runs the three
   tests (complete sentence / no names / story-provable) and repairs the
   failures.
2. **Independent — trace it (20 min):** Take the learner's own theme
   statement from L3. Write the beginning/middle/end development trace with
   one cited detail per stage, using two academic verbs (*develops*,
   *reveals*, *conveys*, *suggests*).
3. **Exit check:** "Is 'Never give up' a theme statement for this story?
   Defend with the three tests." Secure = fails test 1 (slogan, no claim
   about life) — correctly identified; Developing = identifies failure
   without naming the test; Not yet = accepts it.

## Session D — Summary repair shop

*Session 8 (after L4).*

1. **Guided — contaminant hunt (15 min):** The adult provides a
   6-sentence summary seeded with five opinion words (see
   [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) § Practice D). The learner circles each
   and classifies it: adjective of judgment / verb of judgment / invented
   detail.
2. **Independent — rewrite (20 min):** Rewrite the contaminated summary as
   a 4–6 sentence objective summary using Somebody–Wanted–But–So. Then
   self-check with "does the text say this, or do I?" on every descriptive
   word.
3. **Exit check:** "Your partner wrote: 'The amazing Mara outsmarts her
   clueless grandfather.' Name two things wrong for an objective summary."
   Secure = both named (opinion adjectives; "outsmarts/clueless" unsupported
   by text); Developing = one named; Not yet = none.

## Session E — Setting → choice chains

*Session 10 (after L5). Uses the generated illustration.*

1. **Guided — image chains (15 min):** Study
   [`assets/storm-lighthouse-setting.png`](assets/storm-lighthouse-setting.png)
   (or its text alternative in Lesson 05). The adult names a visible detail
   (the narrow cliff path, the storm clouds, the glowing lamp); the learner
   completes the chain: detail → forced choice → plot consequence.
2. **Independent — text chains (20 min):** Build three complete chains from
   the story, one per setting part: one for **time** (night / 2 a.m.
   deadline), one for **weather** (gale/ice), one for **social** (keeper's
   duty / grandfather's authority). Each chain needs citations and the
   removal test ("if this detail vanished, what breaks?").
3. **Exit check:** "Which single setting detail, removed, would most
   completely destroy the story's conflict? One sentence of justification."
   Secure = detail named (the leak or the gale/ice) with causal
   justification; Developing = detail named, justification vague; Not yet =
   decorative detail chosen.

## Session F — Interaction map studio + project launch

*Session 12 (after L6).*

1. **Guided — map critique (15 min):** The adult shows a flawed sample map
   (four boxes, arrows without labels — see [answer-key.md](answer-key.md)
   § Practice F). The learner diagnoses it and repairs two arrows with
   mechanism labels and citations.
2. **Independent — my map (15 min):** Finish the learner's own interaction
   map (≥6 labeled arrows). Star the single arrow the learner believes does
   the most work in producing the theme.
3. **Project launch (15 min):** Read [project.md](project.md) together.
   Choose the project text (anchor story, an Aesop fable, or an
   adult-selected myth) and list its five elements in the dossier. *Success:
   text chosen, elements listed, Session 13 plan set.*
4. **Exit check (discussion):** "In one minute, explain to the adult how the
   theme of your project text *could* emerge from element interactions —
   even before you've analyzed it." Secure = names at least two interacting
   elements plausibly; Developing = one; Not yet = retells plot instead.
