# Unit 04 — Grammar: Clauses, Sentence Structure, and Vocabulary

**Grade:** 7 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #36](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/36), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Units 01–03 taught the learner to read like a critic: find a theme,
trace an argument, compare two authors. This unit turns the lens around:
how do you *build* sentences that say exactly what you mean? Grammar here
is not a list of labels to memorize. It is a toolkit for control — a
phrase that points at the right noun, a sentence type that shows whether
two ideas are equal partners or cause and effect, a word chosen because
its feeling (not just its dictionary meaning) fits the sentence. By the
end of the unit the learner can take a tangled, wordy, slightly wrong
draft and operate on it: diagnose the fault, name it, fix it, and explain
why the fix works.

## Essential question

How does the *shape* of a sentence change what a reader understands —
and how do you choose the shape on purpose?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks, the grade-6 track, and U01–U03:

- Parts of speech (noun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition,
  conjunction) and subject/predicate/object identification — the legacy
  parts-of-speech and subject/predicate quizzes are the entry check
  (see Practice A)
- Citing textual evidence and writing objective summaries (U01) — now
  applied to *your own* sentences: every revision needs a reason
- Word choice and tone analysis (U02) — now applied to connotation
  and precise word choice in revision
- Argument evaluation (U03) — a sentence that misplaces its modifier
  makes a claim its writer never intended, and this unit teaches the
  learner to catch that before a reader does

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Explain the **function of phrases and clauses** in general and in a
   specific sentence, distinguishing a phrase (no subject + verb pair)
   from a clause (subject + verb pair), and an independent clause from
   a dependent clause (L.7.1a).
2. **Choose among simple, compound, complex, and compound-complex
   sentences** to signal differing relationships among ideas — equal
   ideas, cause and effect, contrast, condition — and revise a sentence
   from one type to another without changing its facts (L.7.1b).
3. **Recognize and correct misplaced and dangling modifiers** by
   placing phrases and clauses next to the words they modify
   (L.7.1c).
4. Use a **comma to separate coordinate adjectives** (passing both the
   *and*-test and the reversibility test) and **spell correctly** by
   proofreading for commonly confused words (L.7.2a–b).
5. Choose language that expresses ideas **precisely and concisely**,
   recognizing and eliminating **wordiness and redundancy**
   (L.7.3a); present a revised paragraph in **formal English**
   (SL.7.6).
6. Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown and multiple-meaning
   words using **context clues, Greek and Latin affixes and roots, and
   reference materials**, verifying the preliminary determination; use
   **synonym/antonym and analogy relationships** and distinguish
   **connotations** of words with similar denotations (L.7.4a–d;
   L.7.5b–c).

## 16-session pacing (4 weeks × 4 sessions, ~45 minutes each)

Sessions rotate: explicit grammar lesson → revision/editing practice →
vocabulary practice → review. **Lessons alone do not fill the unit** —
the named practice, project, quiz, and assessment sessions below are
the rest of the 16. Week 18 is the scope-and-sequence's midyear review
week: the formative quiz doubles as the cumulative U01–U04 check, and
the final session is targeted reteaching plus the unit assessment.

## Differentiation, supports, and extensions

- **Reading access:** every lesson prints its sentences in full; the
  adult reads any sentence aloud on request, and the learner may answer
  orally, by dictation, or in writing.
- **Response modes:** labeling, sorting, and charting count as full
  answers wherever a paragraph is not the skill being tested;
  speech-to-text is always allowed for longer writing.
- **Scaffolds:** every lesson gives sentence starters ("This group is
  a ___ because ___") and a worked repair before the learner repairs
  alone; Practice C gives a partially repaired sentence for learners
  who need it.
- **Extensions:** Lesson 02's extension rewrites one market-scene
  paragraph in three different sentence-type patterns and compares
  their effects; Lesson 06's extension builds an original analogy set
  and a connotation spectrum for one word family.
- **Reteaching signals:** if phrase/clause labels are still swapped
  after Lesson 01, return to its subject-and-verb test before Lesson 02
  (sentence types are built from clauses); if modifier repairs only
  move words at random, return to Lesson 03's "who or what does this
  describe?" question before Practice C.
