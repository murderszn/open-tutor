If subject/predicate identification is shaky, redo Lesson 01's
readiness check before continuing — sentence types are built from

**Sort:** label each group phrase (P) or clause (C); if a clause,
independent (I) or dependent (D); then name its job (noun, adjective,
or adverb) in the sentence given.

1. *before the bell rang* — in "Before the bell rang, Maya finished
   her paragraph."
2. *the bell rang* — in "The bell rang."
3. *a quiet corner of the library* — in "A quiet corner of the

4. *who reads every morning* — in "The student who reads every

5. *to finish the race* — in "She trained hard to finish the race."
6. *because the lights went out* — in "We stopped reading because
   the lights went out."

## Practice B — Sentence combining and type choice (after Lesson 02)

Combine each set into the requested type. Do not change any fact, and
do not add new facts.

1. (Complex, cause) "The gate was locked. We waited outside."
2. (Compound) "The rain stopped. The team returned to the field."
3. (Simple) "The old lighthouse. It stood on the cliff. It warned
   ships." (Combine into ONE independent clause — you may drop
   repeated words.)
4. (Complex, time) "The bell rang. Maya closed her notebook."
5. (Compound-complex) "The wind was cold. The crowd stayed. The band
   played one more song." (Make one idea dependent: the crowd stayed
   *although* the wind was cold.)
6. (Your choice) "Jamal missed the bus. He ran to school. He arrived
   before the bell." Choose the type that best shows the
   relationships, and explain your choice in one sentence.

## Practice C — Modifier repair clinic (after Lesson 03)

For each sentence: name the fault (misplaced or dangling), say what
the faulty version wrongly claims, and write the repair. The first
one is partially repaired to show the routine — finish it.

1. *While jogging in the park, my phone fell out of my pocket.*
   Partial repair: the jogger is missing; the sentence claims the
   phone jogged. Finish: "While I was jogging in the park, ___."
2. *I saw a man on a horse wearing a cowboy hat.*
3. *After finishing dinner, the dishes were washed by Leo.*
4. *The waiter served dinner to the guests on silver trays.*
5. *After reading the book, the movie seemed disappointing to Lena.*
6. *She found a dog in the alley with a broken leg.*
7. *Driving to work, the traffic jam delayed Priya.*
8. *He bought a phone from a store with a cracked screen.*

## Practice E — Concision revision (after Lesson 05)

Revise each sentence to be concise with no meaning lost. Aim for at
least a quarter fewer words in each.

1. *Due to the fact that the store was closed, we went home in order
   to eat dinner there.*
2. *At this point in time, a large number of people are in the
   process of signing up for the trip.*
3. *Please return back the book so that other students will be able
   to read it in the event that they need it.*
4. *The true fact is that the final outcome was an unexpected
   surprise to everyone on the team.*
5. *She made the decision to give a free gift to each student for the
   purpose of thanking them.*
6. *In order to improve, he practiced every day, and due to the fact
   that he practiced, his skills got better and better.*

## Practice F — Word-study lab (after Lesson 06)

Complete a study log for these six words. For each word give: (a) a
guess from context or affix, naming the strategy; (b) the verified
dictionary meaning and part of speech; (c) a synonym and an antonym
(or "none" with a reason); (d) one original sentence using the word
correctly. (Sample entries are in the answer key; your sentences
will differ, but meanings and relationships must be right.)

1. *transport* — "Trucks transport grain across the country."
2. *benevolent* — "The benevolent neighbor shared vegetables with
   everyone on the street."
3. *predict* — "Forecasters predict rain by reading pressure and
   wind."
4. *malfunction* — "The printer's malfunction delayed the whole
   class."
5. *photograph* — "She took a photograph of the sunrise."
6. *persistent* — "The persistent runner finished the race despite
   the heat." (Also note: does *persistent* praise or criticize?
   Name its connotation and one word from the same family with the
   opposite feeling.)
