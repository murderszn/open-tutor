# Teacher Guide — Unit 04

For the guiding adult. Student materials never contain answers; this
guide and [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) hold them.

## How to use this unit

- Each lesson is one ~45-minute session; the pacing table in the unit
  [README](README.md) names all 16 sessions. Do not skip the practice
  sessions — the lessons introduce skills, the practices make them
  stick, and the project requires all six lessons' skills at once.
- Review every written answer the same day. The unit's skills build on
  each other: a shaky Lesson 01 (phrase vs. clause) will weaken Lesson
  02 (sentence types are counted in clauses), and a shaky Lesson 03
  (modifiers) will weaken the project diagnosis.
- Week 18 is the scope-and-sequence's midyear review week. Use the
  formative quiz session to name U01–U04 gaps out loud (one strength,
  one next step per unit is enough) and the final session for
  targeted reteaching before the unit assessment — do not rush the
  assessment into a session that has no review in it.
- All sentences, paragraphs, and word lists in this unit are original
  and written for the unit. The market-scene illustration is
  AI-generated (see [assets/README.md](assets/README.md)); it is a
  picture to write about, never evidence about a real market, and
  learners must never cite it as a source of facts.

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — Phrases and clauses

- Watch for: labeling by verb-looking words instead of by the
  subject-and-verb pair (participles and infinitives are the trap).
  The fix is always the same question: *who or what is doing
  something, and what are they doing?*
- The key check is guided practice 4 (the counterexample). If the
  learner cannot produce one, re-teach with Worked example 1 before
  moving on — Lesson 02 depends on this test.
- Reteach: cross out prepositional phrases first, then find the
  remaining subject-verb pair.

### Lesson 02 — Sentence types

- Watch for: counting words instead of clauses (a long simple
  sentence is still simple), and joining facts with *and* while
  claiming a cause relationship. Insist on the relationship word:
  *equal partners*, *cause*, *condition*, *contrast*, or *time*.
- The key check is independent practice 5 (why complex, not
  compound). A type label without a relationship reason is decoration.
- The image activity works from the text-only alternative alone —
  never require the picture. If the learner cannot see the image, use
  the alternative paragraph and nothing is lost.
- Reteach: build sentences with clause cards before writing, and
  label each card independent or dependent first.

### Lesson 03 — Modifiers

- Watch for: repairs that only move words at random, and repairs that
  add decoration (*quickly*) instead of the missing doer. The repair
  question is always: *who or what does this describe?*
- Note the distinction the key enforces: a described word that is
  present but misplaced is a **misplaced** modifier; a described word
  that is absent is a **dangling** modifier. Lesson 03 guided
  practice 1 (painting) is misplaced, not dangling — if the learner
  calls it dangling, re-teach that distinction before Practice C.
- Reteach: draw an arrow from each opening phrase to the noun right
  after it; a wrong arrow (or no arrow) is the diagnosis.

### Lesson 04 — Commas and spelling

- Watch for: "two adjectives = comma" (false — run both tests), and
  trusting a spellchecker with confusions (*their/there/they're* are
  all correctly spelled words).
- The key check is guided practice 4 (*a red, brick house*): the
  *brick* material test. If the learner commas it, re-teach with the
  material-adjective examples (*leather bag*, *wooden box*).
- Spelling is graded in Practice D, the project, and the assessment's
  Part A only where the question says so; elsewhere, note spelling
  separately and do not let it mask a correct grammar idea.
- Reteach: keep the learner's personal confusion list visible during
  every writing task in U05–U08, not just in this unit.

### Lesson 05 — Precise, concise language

- Watch for: cuts that lose meaning (a dropped cause, condition, or
  quantity fails the test) and "concision" that only deletes
  adjectives while keeping the padding. The test is meaning
  preservation, not word count alone.
- The key check is guided practice 4 (the *Bridge closed* example):
  if the learner calls it a good cut, re-teach the test before
  Practice E.
- Reteach: revise in two passes — first say exactly what happened
  (precision), then cut every word that adds no meaning (concision).

### Lesson 06 — Vocabulary strategies

- Watch for: root-guessing without verification (*rebel* carries
  *bell-* but means more than "war"), and synonym-swapping without
  checking connotation (*thrifty* for *stingy* changes the judgment).
- The key check is independent practice 4 (the *persistent /
  stubborn / determined / obstinate* sort): accept either order
  within the praising pair with reasoning, but not a sort that mixes
  praise and criticism.
- Reteach: run the four strategies in order (context → affix →
  reference → verification) and never skip step 4.

## Project scoring notes

- Diagnosis: 10 faults (see the key). Award full credit for 10 found
  with correct codes and explanations. Do not penalize a learner who
  defends *very happy* as deliberate emphasis, if the reasoning is
  sound — the key counts it as vague (V), but the defense is a
  legitimate judgment call.
- Repair: accept any repair that fixes the fault and keeps the facts,
  even if the wording differs from the key's model paragraph.
- Sentence types: check the labels, not just the presence — a
  compound sentence mislabeled complex loses that point even if the
  sentence itself is good.
- Formal delivery (SL.7.6): listen for complete sentences, precise
  words, and no slang. Note one strength and one fix; do not grade
  accent, volume, or speed.

## Reteaching signals and next steps

- If phrase/clause labels are still swapped after Lesson 01, return
  to its subject-and-verb test before Lesson 02.
- If modifier repairs only move words at random, return to Lesson
  03's "who or what does this describe?" question before Practice C.
- If coordinate-adjective commas are still guessed, return to
  Lesson 04's two tests with material adjectives (*leather*,
  *wooden*, *brick*) as the contrast set.
- If concision cuts lose meaning, return to Lesson 05's test
  (shorter version must mean exactly the same) before the project.
- Readiness for U05 (narrative craft): the learner can build and
  vary sentence types on purpose and repair their own modifier and
  wordiness faults in a draft. If not, carry one revision pass from
  this unit into U05's first drafting session rather than re-teaching
  the whole unit.
- Next section: U05 — Narrative craft: scene, dialogue, and revision
  (track weeks 19–22).
