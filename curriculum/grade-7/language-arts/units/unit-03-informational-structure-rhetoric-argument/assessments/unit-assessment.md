# Green Roofs: Another Way to Cool a Building

## A Roof That Acts Like a Meadow

From the street, Lincoln School looks ordinary. From the roof, it looks
like a meadow: low green plants spread across shallow soil where black
tar used to bake in the sun. On hot afternoons, the classrooms below
that green roof stay noticeably cooler than the classrooms under the
school's older, bare roof.

## How a Green Roof Cools

A green roof cools in two ways that will sound familiar. First, its
plants shade the roof surface, just as a tree shades a sidewalk — the
sun hits leaves instead of tar. Second, the plants and soil release
water vapor as they dry, carrying heat away with it. Underneath, the
soil layer also insulates, slowing heat's trip into the building.

## What One School Measured

On one 92-degree afternoon, the school's caretaker measured both roofs.
The bare roof's surface reached 138 degrees. The green roof's surface
reached 94 degrees. Classrooms under the green roof measured 4 degrees
cooler than classrooms under the bare roof. One roof, one afternoon —
but the gap is hard to ignore.

## Limits

Green roofs are not for every building. A roof must be strong enough to
hold wet soil, which is heavy, and a structural check costs money
before planting begins. Poor installation can leak, and the plants need
occasional weeding and watering in dry spells. A green roof is a good
tool for the right building — not a magic blanket for all of them.
