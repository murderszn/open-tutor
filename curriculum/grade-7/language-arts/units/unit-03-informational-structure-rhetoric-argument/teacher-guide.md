# Teacher Guide — Unit 03

For the guiding adult. Student materials never contain answers; this
guide and [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) hold them.

## How to use this unit

- Each lesson is one ~45-minute session; the pacing table in the unit
  [README](README.md) names all 16 sessions. Do not skip the practice
  sessions — the lessons introduce skills, the practices make them stick.
- Review every written answer the same day. The unit's skills build on
  each other: a shaky Lesson 01 summary will weaken Lesson 06's
  comparison, because comparison paragraphs are built from summaries.
- All numbers in Articles A and B, the Lesson 05 argument, and the
  assessment passage are a **fictional practice dataset**. If the
  learner ever cites one as a real fact (in discussion or in the
  project), stop and correct it: practice data trains analysis, it does
  not describe the world. The Resource Pack's EPA and Forest Service
  pages are the real-data routes.

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — Central ideas and summary

- Watch for: topic written as a central idea ("street trees"); opinion
  written as a central idea ("trees are great"); summaries containing
  "should." All three are caught by the same test: *can you point to
  the text, in more than one section, for this exact sentence?*
- If the learner finds only one central idea, ask: "What does the
  article say about *people* that it never says about *temperatures*?"
  That question almost always surfaces central idea 2.
- Reteach: re-do the Lesson 01 guided practice with section 4 only,
  then re-attempt the summary.

### Lesson 02 — Interactions

- Insist on cause words (because, so, led to) and a quoted detail for
  every arrow. A chain without a cause word is a sequence, not an
  interaction.
- The key check is independent practice 4 ("trees cause friendliness"):
  if the learner cannot spot the skipped mechanism, re-teach with the
  worked example 1 chain before moving on.

### Lesson 03 — Structure and word choice

- Push for the job word (introduce / explain / prove / widen / decide)
  alongside every structure label. A label without a job is decoration.
- The applied task ("What Could Go Wrong" section) should be left
  open — do not resolve it. Lesson 06 answers it with a whole second
  article, and the payoff depends on the wait.

### Lesson 04 — Author's position

- The pronoun trap is the main hazard: "first person" is not a
  position. If it appears, re-teach with the error-analysis item and
  the purpose-vs-position test ("why did she write this?" vs. "what
  does she want to happen?").
- Note that Article A names no opponent. Learners who insist an author
  must quote an opponent to "distinguish" should reread the section 5
  numbers-vs-fuller-story contrast.

### Lesson 05 — Evaluating arguments

- Separate agreement from evaluation, out loud, every time: "You may
  agree with planting trees and still judge this argument weak. Say
  both sentences."
- The three verdicts must stay separate: relevant-but-insufficient is
  the most common correct judgment in this unit, and learners who
  collapse it into "bad" will misjudge Claim 1 in both the lesson and
  the assessment.

### Lesson 06 — Two authors and media

- The contest trap: "B debunks A" is wrong — both authors favor
  planting. If it appears, return to guided practice 3 (whether vs.
  how) before the project workshops.
- The illustration is not evidence. If the learner cites it as proof,
  reread the worked example 2 verdict together: the image shows a
  feeling, not data.

## Scoring guidance

- **Quiz (10 items):** 9–10 = ready for the assessment; 7–8 = review
  the missed lessons' practice sessions the same week; below 7 =
  reteach the missed skills before the assessment session.
- **Unit assessment (12 questions):** score Part 1, Part 2, and Part 3
  separately. A learner may pass Part 1 and fail Part 2 — that means
  argument evaluation, not reading comprehension, needs reteaching.
  Record one strength and one next step; do not record a single
  percentage as the unit's outcome.
- **Project rubric:** see [project.md](project.md). Score each
  criterion separately; a briefing can be fair but weakly written, or
  well written but unfair. The revision checklist is the reteach tool:
  return the briefing with unchecked items, not with a grade.
- **Readiness signals:** the learner is ready for Unit 04 when they can
  (a) write an objective summary without opinions, (b) judge a claim
  with three separate verdicts, and (c) compare two authors on emphasis
  and interpretation without declaring a winner.

## What to do when a session runs short or long

- Short: cut the extension, never the exit check. The exit check is the
  only same-day evidence for that lesson's skill.
- Long: move the applied task to the next session's warm-up rather
  than rushing the independent practice — independent practice is where
  misconceptions surface.
