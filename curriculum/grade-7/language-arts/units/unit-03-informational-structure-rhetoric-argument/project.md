# Project — A Fair Briefing for the Town Council

**Driving question:** Should our town plant 200 street trees — and if so,
how?
**Deliverable:** a one- to two-page fair briefing, written for a town
council member who has read neither article.
**Time:** two workshop sessions (Week 14, Sessions 13–14), plus finishing
at home if needed.

## What the briefing must contain

1. **Evidence table** — one row per topic (temperature numbers, street
   life, costs, pollen/storms, timing/plan). Columns: what Article A
   says, what Article B says, and where they agree or differ. Every cell
   cites a section (for example, "A §3").
2. **Claim-evaluation chart** — three claims from the two articles (at
   least one from each author), each judged with the three verdicts from
   Lesson 05: sound reasoning? relevant evidence? sufficient evidence?
   One reason per verdict.
3. **Briefing letter** — two to three paragraphs:
   - Paragraph 1: what both authors agree on, in neutral language.
   - Paragraph 2: where they differ, and why (emphasis and
     interpretation — use those words).
   - Paragraph 3: your recommendation (plant all 200 now, plant in
     phases with a budget, or do not plant) with at least two reasons
     drawn from the articles.
4. **Sources note** — one sentence stating that the articles' numbers
   are a fictional practice dataset, not real measurements, and one
   sentence naming one real source from the Resource Pack that a council
   member could check next (EPA or Forest Service).

## Rules

- Present both authors fairly: a reader who only read your briefing
  should be able to state each author's position correctly.
- Do not invent new facts. Every number in the briefing must come from
  Article A or B, and every number must be labeled as practice data in
  the sources note.
- Your recommendation may agree with either author, or with neither —
  but it must be supported by the evidence table, not by personal taste
  ("I like trees" is not a reason).
- The illustration from Lesson 06 may be included with its caption; if
  you include it, add one sentence saying what it cannot show.

## Workshop plan

- **Workshop 1 (Session 13):** fill the evidence table and the
  claim-evaluation chart. The adult checks: does every row cite a
  section? Does every claim have three verdicts?
- **Workshop 2 (Session 14):** draft the briefing letter, then revise
  with the checklist below. Read the letter aloud once before calling it
  done — anything you stumble on, rewrite.

## Revision checklist

- [ ] Both authors' positions are stated, and a reader could tell them
  apart.
- [ ] Every number traces to a section of Article A or B.
- [ ] The sources note labels the numbers as fictional practice data.
- [ ] The recommendation has at least two reasons from the articles.
- [ ] No sentence says "Article A is right" or "Article B is wrong"
  without a verdict reason.
- [ ] The letter is in the learner's own words (quotations are short and
  quoted).

## Rubric

| Criterion | Meeting (2) | Exceeding (3) | Developing (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fairness and accuracy** | Both positions stated correctly; evidence table covers 4+ topics with section citations | Both positions stated with nuance (including what they agree on); table covers all 5 topics with precise citations | One position missing or misstated; table covers fewer than 4 topics or lacks citations |
| **Claim evaluation** | 3 claims judged with three verdicts each and a reason per verdict | Verdicts distinguish relevance from sufficiency (not just "good/bad"); fixes suggested for weak claims | Claims listed without verdicts, or verdicts given without reasons |
| **Briefing writing** | Letter has agreement, difference, and recommendation paragraphs in the learner's own words | Letter uses "emphasis" and "interpretation" correctly and the recommendation follows from the table | Letter missing a paragraph, or recommendation has no article-based reasons |
| **Sources honesty** | Sources note labels practice data and names one real checkable source | Sources note also explains *why* the distinction matters for a council decision | No sources note, or practice data presented as real facts |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = strong briefing, ready for the portfolio; 7–9 =
solid, revise one criterion; 4–6 = revise with the adult before
portfolio. The adult records the score and one next step, not a grade
in this repository.

## Extension

Add a one-paragraph "Article C" — a shopkeeper's position on the same
street — to the briefing as an appendix, following the same fairness
rules. Or: check one real EPA or Forest Service page from the Resource
Pack and add a line to the sources note comparing one real fact to the
articles' fictional numbers.
