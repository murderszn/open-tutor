# Resource Pack — Unit 05: Natural Selection, Adaptation, and Evolutionary Evidence

**Summary (adult planning judgment):** Grade 7 learners meeting
fossils, natural selection, and common ancestry do best when short
videos *supplement* — never replace — the layer-ordering work, the
forelimb and embryo comparisons, the Kettlewell chart reading, the
finch case, and the paper-moth simulation. The resources below
support the unit's six objectives: fossil-record patterns; anatomical
evidence; embryological evidence; the natural-selection mechanism;
trait-frequency mathematics; and artificial selection. The AMNH
NGSS deck, the Berkeley Tiktaalik page, the Britannica natural
selection page, and the peppered-moth Wikipedia pages were verified
2026-11-07; the remaining web references are the standard
encyclopedia pages for the named topics, and the adult checks each
link before assigning it. Video entries are labeled search links.
At least one free, no-account route supports every core objective.

## Web references (reputable; adult checks before assigning)

1. American Museum of Natural History — NGSS MS-LS4 card deck
   (MS-LS4-1–MS-LS4-6 performance expectations and disciplinary
   core ideas) —
   https://amnh.org/content/download/132667/2211291/file/ngss-amnh-card-deck-ms-ls4.pdf
   — checked 2026-11-07 via search results; supports the
   standards table in the unit README. Free, no account.
2. UC Berkeley Understanding Evolution — "What has the head of a
   crocodile and the gills of a fish?" (Tiktaalik, 375 million
   years old, found 2004, announced 2006; fish features and
   tetrapod features; why "missing link" is misleading) —
   https://evolution.berkeley.edu/evolibrary/news/060501_tiktaalik
   — checked 2026-11-07 by opening the page; supports
   Objectives 1–2 (Lessons 01–02) and Session 7 reading. Free,
   no account.
3. Britannica: Natural selection —
   https://www.britannica.com/science/natural-selection —
   supports Objectives 4–5 (Lesson 03): the definition, variation
   and differential reproduction, gene frequencies. Checked
   2026-11-07 by opening the page. Free to read; some Britannica
   pages show subscription prompts, so the Wikipedia and internal
   references above are the no-account route.
4. Wikipedia: Peppered moth evolution —
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peppered_moth_evolution —
   supports Objective 4 (Lesson 03): industrial melanism,
   Kettlewell 1953–1956, Manchester 1895 (98% dark), Majerus
   2001–2007 follow-up. Checked 2026-11-07 via search results.
   Free, no account.
5. Wikipedia: Industrial melanism —
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_melanism — supports
   Objective 4 (Lesson 03): soot, lichen loss, and the decline of
   melanism after pollution fell. Checked 2026-11-07 via search
   results. Free, no account.
6. Wikipedia: Tiktaalik —
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiktaalik — supports Objectives
   1–2 (Lessons 01–02): Late Devonian, about 375 million years
   ago; Ellesmere Island, 2004; 1.25–2.75 m long. Checked
   2026-11-07 via search results. Free, no account.
