## A. Fossil record and deep time (Sessions 2, 12, 14)

**P1.** A cliff shows four layers. Order the fossils from oldest
to youngest, and name one group that disappears from the record.

| Layer | Fossils found |
|---|---|
| Top (youngest) | Rabbit bones |
| Second | Dinosaur tooth, fern fronds |
| Third | Fish fossil |
| Bottom (oldest) | Trilobite |

**P2.** A second cliff shows: top layer — horse teeth and grass
seeds; middle layer — dinosaur bone and ammonite shell; bottom
layer — trilobite and brachiopod shells. Name one organism that
appears only in the top layer and one that appears only in the
middle layer. What does the horse's position tell you about when
horses appear in this record?

**P3.** On the paper timeline (2 m = 4.6 billion years),
Archaeopteryx lived about 150 million years ago. How far from the
left end (Earth's formation) does it sit? Show the calculation.

**P4.** A learner says: "The top layer is the oldest because it
was put there first, on top of everything else." Correct the
error in two sentences, using the word *superposition*.

**P5.** Write a 3–4 sentence claim-evidence-reasoning paragraph:
claim — "The fossil record shows that life has changed over
time"; evidence — two patterns from the P1 table; reasoning —
why those patterns show change rather than one fixed set of
animals.

## B. Natural selection (Sessions 6, 12, 14)

**P6.** A population of 200 mice has 100 light mice and 100 dark
mice living on dark volcanic sand. Owls eat 40 light mice and 10
dark mice. How many of each survive, and what is the dark
percentage among the survivors? Compare it with the starting
dark percentage (50%).

**P7.** Use the Kettlewell chart in Lesson 03: in the polluted
woods, how many times more often were dark moths recaptured than
light moths (40 ÷ 19)? In the unpolluted woods, how many times
more often were light moths recaptured than dark moths
(12.5 ÷ 6)? What does the flip show?

**P8.** Around Manchester, dark moths went from about 1% of the
population before industrialization to about 98% by 1895. In a
population of 2,000 moths, how many dark moths is that at each
time? What is the change in the number of dark moths?

**P9.** During the 1977 drought on Daphne Major, only large hard
seeds were left for the medium ground finches. Which finches
survived better — those with deep, strong beaks or those with
small, weak beaks — and what happened to the next generation's
average beak depth? Explain in two sentences.

**P10.** A learner says: "The moths needed to hide from birds,
so they turned dark." Correct the error in two sentences.

**P11.** Two beetles live in the same field. Beetle A is strong
and fast but has no offspring. Beetle B is average in strength
but has 40 offspring. Which beetle has higher fitness in the
sense this unit uses the word? Explain in one sentence.

**P12.** After clean-air laws reduced soot in England, the light
moth form became common again. Explain in two sentences why the
dark form's advantage disappeared.

**P13.** A population of 400 snails is 25% banded and 75%
plain. After birds eat many plain snails on a banded background,
the next generation has 160 banded snails out of 400. What is the
banded percentage now, and how many percentage points did it
change? Explain in one sentence what the numbers show.

**P14.** Write a 4–5 sentence claim-evidence-reasoning paragraph:
claim — "Natural selection caused the peppered-moth population
to change"; evidence — the Kettlewell recapture percentages and
the 98% Manchester figure; reasoning — how differential survival
changes trait frequencies over generations.

## C. Embryos and ancestry (Sessions 9, 12, 14)

**P15.** Name the three features that fish, chicken, and human
embryos all share early in development (Lesson 04 table), and
name one way their later development differs.

**P16.** Why do scientists not use Haeckel's 19th-century embryo
drawings as evidence? What should be used instead?

**P17.** Humans and chimpanzees share about 99% of their DNA.
What does that suggest about their relationship? Name one other
line of evidence that would strengthen or weaken the conclusion.

**P18.** Sort each as an adaptation or an acquired trait, with one
reason: (a) a dark moth's camouflage on a sooty trunk; (b) a
weightlifter's large muscles; (c) a finch's deep beak in a
drought; (d) skin tanned by the sun.

## D. Mixed review: anatomy, artificial selection, misconceptions (Sessions 12, 14)

**P19.** Match each vegetable to the part of wild mustard that
farmers selected: cabbage, broccoli, kohlrabi, Brussels sprouts.
Parts: leaves, flower buds, stem, side buds. (Two vegetables are
not listed — name them and the part selected for each.)

**P20.** Complete the comparison table for the peppered moth
(natural selection) and the dog (artificial selection): who or
what selects, what is the goal, and what do both cases need to
work?

**P21.** A learner says: "Evolution is progress — species get
better and better until they are perfect." Correct the error in
two sentences, using the peppered moth as evidence.

**P22.** A learner says: "Humans came from chimpanzees." Correct
the error in two sentences.

**P23.** Sort each pair as homologous or analogous, with one
reason: (a) bat wing and human arm; (b) bird wing and insect
wing; (c) whale flipper and human arm; (d) shark fin and dolphin
flipper.

**P24.** Whales have small hip bones buried inside their bodies.
What kind of structure is this, what is it a leftover of, and
what does it show about whale ancestry? Answer in two sentences.
