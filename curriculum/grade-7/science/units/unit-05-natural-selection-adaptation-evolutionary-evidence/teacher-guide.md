# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Natural Selection, Adaptation, and Evolutionary Evidence

**Adult only.** This guide supports the lessons; worked solutions
are in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md). Pacing is Sessions 1–16 in
the unit [README](README.md).

## Lesson-by-lesson guidance

### Lesson 01 — Reading the fossil record

- **Watch for:** reading the layers upside down (top = oldest);
  "one fossil proves it" (the pattern across layers is the
  evidence); confusing relative age (order) with absolute age
  (years). Superposition gives order only — the timeline adds the
  years.
- **The illustration:** accept either panel order as long as the
  reasoning names the layer and the rule. The fossils are
  stylized, not to scale, and the shell is a generic coiled shell,
  not a labeled species — do not let learners read species names
  or exact ages off the image.
- **Timeline arithmetic that must be automatic by Session 4:**
  2 m = 4.6 billion years, so 1 cm = 23 million years; distance
  from the left end = (4,600 − age in millions) ÷ 4,600 × 200 cm.
  First life ≈ 35 cm; first fish ≈ 177 cm; Tiktaalik ≈ 184 cm;
  Archaeopteryx ≈ 193 cm; dinosaur extinction ≈ 197 cm; humans
  at the very end, within a hair's width.
- **Reteach prompt:** "Which layer was laid down first — the one
  on the bottom, or the one on top? The bottom one was there
  first, so it is the oldest."

### Lesson 02 — Same bones, different jobs

- **Watch for:** sorting by job alone ("both fly, so related");
  "vestigial = useless" (vestigial structures may keep small
  jobs — the point is the shrunken form, not zero function);
  "Tiktaalik is a failed animal" (it was a complete animal in its
  own time; "transitional" describes its mix of features, not a
  defect).
- **The diagram:** bone counts in the fingers are simplified and
  the limbs are schematic, not to scale — the lesson says so.
  The point is the shared plan (humerus, radius + ulna, wrist,
  finger bones), not the count.
- **Reteach prompt:** "Forget the job for a moment. Look at the
  bones: same plan in the same order, or a different build?
  Same plan means shared ancestry; same job with a different
  build means analogous."

### Lesson 03 — How natural selection works

- **Watch for:** "need causes change" (the dark form already
  existed before the soot); "individuals evolve" (populations
  change across generations); "the moths turned dark" (no
  individual changed color); "fitness = strength" (fitness here
  is survival *and* reproduction).
- **Numbers that must be automatic by Session 6:** Kettlewell
  polluted woods — dark 40%, light 19% (dark ≈ 2.1× the light
  rate); unpolluted woods — light 12.5%, dark 6% (light ≈ 2.1×
  the dark rate); Manchester 1895 — about 98% dark; before
  industrialization — dark form very rare, about 1%.
- **The finch case:** keep it qualitative — the Grants'
  measurements exist, but this unit does not use invented beak
  numbers. The claim is the direction (deeper beaks survived;
  the next generation's average was deeper), not a value.
- **Reteach prompt:** "What already existed before the
  environment changed? And what changed — the moth, or the
  percentage of moths?"

### Lesson 04 — Embryos and ancestry

- **Watch for:** "humans came from chimpanzees" (common ancestor,
  cousins, not parent and child); treating Haeckel's drawings as
  evidence (they exaggerated similarities — use modern
  photographs); "embryo similarity proves it alone" (it is one
  line of evidence, strongest with fossils, anatomy, and DNA).
- **Boundary:** do not teach developmental mechanisms or gene
  regulation. The pattern stays at the level of the table: shared
  early features, divergent later development.
- **Reteach prompt:** "What do the embryos share early, and when
  do they start to differ? The sharing is the ancestry; the
  differing is the divergence."

### Lesson 05 — Humans shape inheritance

- **Watch for:** "farmers invented new genes" (they selected
  existing variation); value judgments ("natural is good,
  artificial is bad") — keep the comparison on mechanism;
  "genetic engineering is the same as selective breeding"
  (different technology, same goal of influencing inheritance).
- **Boundary:** genetic engineering is named only, with no
  how-to detail. Dog domestication is "more than 15,000 years
  ago" (the exact date is still debated); corn from teosinte is
  about 9,000 years ago in Mexico.
- **Reteach prompt:** "Who chose — the weather, a predator, or a
  person? That answer alone separates natural from artificial."

## Session 14 CER revision checklist

Before the quiz, check the learner's closing CER paragraph (Lesson
03 applied task / project Part 1) against: claim states how the
environment changed the population; evidence cites at least two
percentages from the simulation or Kettlewell data with numbers;
reasoning explains differential survival and reproduction and that
populations change across generations, not individuals within a
lifetime. Any missing element is a same-day revision, not a
deduction carried into the assessment.

## Formative quiz (Session 15) — reteach map

- Q1–Q2 wrong → Lesson 01, rebuild the layer ordering and
  recompute one timeline position.
- Q3–Q4 wrong → Lesson 02, re-label the forelimb diagram and
  re-sort one homologous/analogous pair.
- Q5–Q7 wrong → Lesson 03, re-read the Kettlewell chart and
  restate the four steps with the moth case.
- Q8 wrong → Lesson 04, re-read the embryo table and restate the
  shared early pattern.
- Q9–Q10 wrong → Lesson 05, rebuild the wild-mustard example and
  correct one misconception from the clinic.

## Unit assessment (Session 16) — scoring notes

Part A is 2 points per item; give 1 point when the process is
right but a number or a reason is missing, 0 when the process is
wrong. Part B1 dark percentages are 60% (Generation 1: 36 ÷ 60)
and 70% (Generation 3: 126 ÷ 180); trend: the dark percentage
rises from 50% to 70% across the four generations; cleaning the
background should reverse the trend (light moths camouflaged
again, dark percentage falls). Accept any clearly stated
equivalent. Part B2 needs all four elements (claim, evidence,
reasoning, labels) for full points; a paragraph that uses only
one line of evidence cannot score above 3/6.

## Project scoring

Use the rubric in [project.md](project.md) (20 points). The
walkthrough is the most informative part: a learner who can
explain all three parts in their own words has understood the unit
even if one percentage is miscalculated. Record the portfolio
score separately from the assessment score; do not average them
into a single grade without telling the learner which is which.
Check the simulation table at Session 12, not only at Session 14
— a missing round cannot be reconstructed honestly. Learners who
missed a round use the labeled practice dataset and say so.

## Accessibility and safety notes

- All lessons offer an oral, pointing, drawing, or model-building
  response mode; the illustration and both diagrams carry text
  alternatives, so no objective depends on seeing an image, and
  no question depends on color alone (the moth simulation uses
  light/dark paper *and* labeled counts).
- Investigation safety: adult supervision throughout; paper moths
  only — no live animals; scissors used with the adult. The
  practice dataset is an equal alternative, never a penalty.
- Sensitivity: this unit teaches the scientific consensus on
  natural selection and common ancestry as described by the NGSS
  performance expectations. Learners are never asked about their
  own or their family's beliefs or traits, and human examples stay
  at the general level described in the unit README. When a learner
  raises a belief question, acknowledge it respectfully, keep the
  lesson on the evidence and the mechanism, and note that
  questions of belief belong in family conversation.
