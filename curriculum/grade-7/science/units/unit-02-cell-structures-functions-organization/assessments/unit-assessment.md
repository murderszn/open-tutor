# Unit Assessment — Unit 02: Cell Structures, Functions, and Organization

**Administer at Session 16.** ~45 minutes. Parts A–C closed notes;
Part D (model task) may use your Unit 01 diagrams. Score with the
[answer key](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment); sample proficient
responses are given there. This assessment is a draft instrument — it
is not standardized or validated.

**Total: 40 points.**

## Part A — Structures and whole-cell function (12 points)

**A1. (4 pts)** For each pair, name one structure both cell types
share and one structure that differs, and state the whole-cell job
each structure does: (a) a leaf cell and a root cell; (b) a leaf cell
and a muscle cell.

**A2. (4 pts)** A cell has a nucleus, ribosomes, and ER, but no Golgi
apparatus. Describe exactly what breaks in the cell's protein
pipeline, naming what each of the three working structures does and
what the missing one was supposed to do.

**A3. (4 pts)** A single-celled pond organism lives in fresh water,
which constantly leaks *into* it by osmosis. It survives by pumping
the extra water back out, spending energy to do so. Explain what this
shows about the relationship between the membrane and the whole cell,
using the word *system*.

## Part B — Membrane transport and evidence (10 points)

**B1. (5 pts)** Demonstration data (fictional practice dataset): an
adult places one drop of food coloring in a cup of hot water and one
drop in a cup of cold water, without stirring. The color spreads
through the hot water in about 40 seconds and through the cold water
in about 3 minutes. (a) What process spread the color? (b) Explain
the time difference using particle motion. (c) A classmate says
"the heat pulled the color." Explain why this is wrong.

**B2. (5 pts)** A wilted lettuce leaf firms up after 20 minutes in
cold plain water. Explain using *selectively permeable*, *osmosis*,
and *cell wall* — and state the direction water moved.

## Part C — Organization levels (8 points)

**C1. (4 pts)** Trace the human example end to end: muscle cell →
muscle tissue → heart → circulatory system → human. For *each* level,
write one sentence stating what that level adds that the level below
cannot do.

**C2. (4 pts)** A learner traces "skin cell → skin → human." Identify
the two missing levels, name the correct example at each missing
level, and explain in one sentence per level why skipping it leaves
the trace incomplete.

## Part D — The cell as a system: argument and model (10 points)

**D1. (6 pts)** Write a claim-evidence-reasoning paragraph defending
the claim: *"A cell is a system of interacting parts, not a
collection of independent structures."* Requirements: at least two
pieces of evidence from this unit's lessons or demonstrations; one
reasoning sentence per piece of evidence explaining *why it counts*;
one final sentence naming an honest limit of your evidence.

**D2. (4 pts)** Choose one model from this unit (your labeled cell
diagram, the organization-levels diagram, or the diffusion
demonstration). (a) Name one scientific question it answers well.
(b) Name one question it cannot answer. (c) Name a second model you
would pair it with to cover the gap.
