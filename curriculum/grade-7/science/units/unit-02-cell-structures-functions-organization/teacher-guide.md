# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Cell Structures, Functions, and Organization

Adult-facing. Student pages never link here except where a lesson
says "ask the adult." All answers live in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md) — keep this guide and the key out of
the learner's hands.

## Unit at a glance

- **Prerequisites:** Unit 01 (cell theory, labeled plant/animal cell
  diagrams, CER paragraphs, data tables, SI measurement). The project
  reuses the learner's own Unit 01 diagrams — have them on hand from
  Session 1.
- **Core thread:** MS-LS1-2 — develop and use a model to describe the
  function of a cell as a whole and how the parts of cells contribute
  to that function. Every lesson returns to "what does the *whole*
  fail to do without this part?"
- **New content vs. Unit 01:** Unit 01 built the labeled models and
  argued cells-are-made-of-cells. This unit does *not* repeat the
  diagram task — it adds organelle-function depth, membrane transport
  with evidence, specialization, and the five organization levels.
- **Safety:** Lesson 02's demonstrations use kitchen-safe materials
  only; the adult handles all cutting and hot water. Food coloring
  stains — cover the table, wear old clothes. No tasting during
  investigations.

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — Cell structures and whole-cell function
- **Watch for:** learners reciting nicknames ("powerhouse") without
  connecting to the cell's work. Keep asking "what would the whole
  cell fail to do?" until the answer names a specific failure.
- **Common wrong turn:** "plants don't need mitochondria." Use
  Worked Example 1 (dark closet) — it usually fixes this in one
  discussion.
- **Differentiation:** struggling learners may annotate only the five
  NGSS-emphasized structures first (nucleus, chloroplasts,
  mitochondria, membrane, wall) and add the rest in Session 2.

### Lesson 02 — The membrane and moving materials
- **Setup:** start Demo B (potato) first, teach while the 30 minutes
  pass, then measure. Demo A takes ~5 minutes of watching.
- **The key redirect:** learners track the *solute* ("salt goes in").
  Every time, ask: "Where is the *water* more concentrated?" until
  they self-correct.
- **No-microscope / no-materials alternative:** if potato or food
  coloring is unavailable, use the fictional datasets in P13 and B1
  as paper investigations — the reasoning goals are identical.
- **Assessment note (MS-LS1-2 boundary):** do not assess biochemical
  mechanisms (active transport proteins, ATP). Assess *that* the
  membrane is selective and *what* diffusion/osmosis predict.

### Lesson 03 — Specialized cells and tissues
- **The illustration** is deliberately unlabeled — resist naming the
  cells before the matching activity. The text alternative in the
  lesson carries the same activity for learners who cannot use the
  image.
- **Factual note:** mammalian red blood cells lose their nuclei as
  they mature; this is the lesson's best specialization trade-off
  example. Do not generalize to all vertebrates without the adult
  checking (birds, fish, and reptiles keep nucleated red blood
  cells) — the lesson says "in mammals" deliberately.
- **Watch for:** "specialized = better." Redirect to trade-offs
  (Example 3).

### Lesson 04 — Levels of organization
- **Drill the five levels** until ordering is automatic — every
  incomplete trace in the practice bank comes from skipped levels.
- **Plant systems:** learners often balk at "shoot system." Affirm
  that plants have two systems (root, shoot) — the *logic* of levels
  is what transfers, not the inventory.
- **Debatable cases** (blood, skin) are features, not bugs — use them
  to show that models have fuzzy edges.

### Lesson 05 — The cell as a system
- **CER quality control:** the most common failure is restating
  evidence as reasoning. Require the literal sentence "This supports
  my claim because ___" in drafts.
- **Model limits:** praise honest limits — "our evidence comes from
  models and kitchen demos" is a *stronger* paragraph, not a weaker
  one.

## Sessions 11–16

- **Session 11 (project launch):** approve the learner's track
  choice; check that the cell layer reuses (not redraws from
  scratch) the Unit 01 diagram.
- **Session 13 (workshop):** the whole-system paragraph is the
  hardest deliverable — do a 5-minute CER refresher first.
- **Session 14 (review):** use the formative-quiz results, not a
  generic re-lecture. Reteach only missed objectives; the practice
  bank's Section D is the reteach set.
- **Session 15 (quiz):** score immediately with the learner present;
  self-scoring is part of the learning.
- **Session 16 (assessment + submit):** collect the project; file the
  scored rubric in private storage.

## Readiness and reteach signals

- **Ready for Unit 03:** the learner can name each major organelle's
  whole-cell job, predict osmosis direction from relative
  concentrations, and trace both columns of the organization diagram
  without skipping levels.
- **Reteach triggers:** confusing diffusion with osmosis after
  Session 14 → repeat Demo B as a paper task with new numbers;
  parts-list CER paragraphs → do Lesson 05's guided practice 1–3
  orally before rewriting; level-skipping → card-ordering drill.
