# Unit 02 — Cell Structures, Functions, and Organism Organization

**Grade:** 7 · **Subject:** Science · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #35](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/35), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Unit 01 proved that living things are made of cells and built labeled
cell models. This unit asks the next question: *how do the parts of a
cell keep it alive, and how do cells team up to build a whole
organism?* The learner goes deeper into what each organelle does *for
the whole cell*, investigates how the cell membrane controls traffic
through diffusion and osmosis (with safe kitchen demonstrations),
meets specialized cells and tissues, traces the five levels of
organization from a single cell to a whole organism (one human and one
plant example, end to end), and closes by arguing — with evidence —
that a cell is a system of interacting parts.

## Essential question

How do the tiny parts of a cell keep it alive, and how do cells team
up to build a whole organism?

## Prerequisites

From Unit 01 and the diagnostic weeks:

- Labeled plant and animal cell diagrams (reused and extended in the
  project — have them on hand)
- Cell-theory tenets; observation vs. inference; claim-evidence-
  reasoning paragraphs
- SI measurement and labeled data tables
- Reading simple diagrams and following multi-step procedures

No prior knowledge of diffusion, osmosis, or organization levels is
assumed — all are taught here from zero.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Explain the function of major organelles (nucleus, cell membrane,
   cell wall, mitochondria, chloroplasts, cytoplasm, ribosomes,
   endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, vacuole, lysosomes) and
   describe how each contributes to the function of the cell as a
   whole; compare plant and animal cell structures by their jobs.
2. Describe how the selectively permeable cell membrane controls what
   enters and leaves the cell; explain diffusion and osmosis and use
   evidence from a safe investigation to predict the direction of
   water movement.
3. Explain why cells in a multicellular organism are specialized;
   match specialized cells to their jobs from structure; and describe
   how similar cells form tissues.
4. Order the levels of organization (cell → tissue → organ → organ
   system → organism) and trace one human and one plant example end
   to end, stating what each level adds.
5. Argue with evidence that a cell is a system of interacting parts;
   identify strengths and limits of the unit's models.
