# Answer Key — Unit 02: Cell Structures, Functions, and Organization

Adult only. Every practice item, quiz question, assessment task, and
project criterion is solved below; open responses give a sample
proficient response plus acceptable variation. Reconciled against the
lessons 2026-10-19: each key was solved independently, then checked
against the lesson's teaching.

## Lesson 01

**Guided practice**
1. (a) Shared: mitochondria — both cells must release energy from
   food to power their work (the leaf cell photosynthesizes *and*
   respires). Differ: chloroplasts — only the leaf cell makes its
   own food; the muscle cell must receive food from the organism.
   (Accept: nucleus/membrane as shared with correct jobs; cell wall
   as the plant-only difference.) (b) Shared: nucleus — holds DNA
   instructions directing each cell's work. Differ: cell wall — the
   root cell (plant) has one for shape and support; the skin cell
   (animal) does not.
2. It could not release energy from food efficiently, so it could not
   do energy-demanding work (movement, active transport, building
   molecules). Prediction: either the cell has very low energy needs
   or it would not survive long.
3. Boundary and support: cell wall, cell membrane. Energy:
   chloroplast, mitochondria. Building and shipping: ribosome,
   endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus. Storage (own group):
   vacuole. (Note: the membrane also anchors some building work —
   accept with explanation.)
4. The wall alone could not choose what enters or leaves (no
   selectivity); the membrane alone could not hold the cell rigid
   against water pressure (no stiffness). The wall gives shape and
   protection; the membrane controls traffic.

**Independent practice**
5. Sample: *Nucleus* — holds the DNA instructions. Without it, the
   cell could not direct which proteins to build, when to grow, or
   when to divide. *Mitochondria* — release energy from food. Without
   them, the cell could not power contraction, transport, or building
   work. *Cell membrane* — controls what enters and leaves. Without
   it, the cell's contents would spill out and wastes would
   accumulate. (Any three with a named job + a specific whole-cell
   failure earn full credit.)
6. More like a **plant** cell. Evidence: it has a cell wall (animal
   cells do not) and chloroplasts (it makes its own food — animals
   cannot). The flagella are unusual but do not outweigh the two
   plant-only structures. (Accept "plant-like protist/alga" as an
   excellent answer.)
7. Flawed reasoning: size does not determine importance. Corrected:
   "Ribosomes are essential because they build every protein the
   nucleus's DNA describes; without them the cell could not grow,
   repair itself, or carry out its work."

**Application:** full credit for ≥4 structures each with a specific
"the cell could not ___" consequence (not "would die" alone).
**Exit check:** sample — "The chloroplasts capture light to make
food, the mitochondria release energy from that food to power the
cell's work, and the membrane lets water and minerals in while
keeping wastes out. The nucleus directs all of it with DNA
instructions. Together the parts keep the cell alive the way a
workshop's departments keep the workshop running."

## Lesson 02

**Guided practice**
1. Oxygen diffuses from the lung air (higher concentration) into the
   blood (lower concentration).
2. Water moves by osmosis *into* the cell, because the water
   concentration is higher outside than inside. The cell swells and
   may burst (it has no wall to resist the pressure).
3. Correction: "Osmosis is not a different process — it is the
   special case of diffusion in which *water* moves across a
   selectively permeable membrane, from higher water concentration
   to lower water concentration."
4. Plain-water piece: 42 − 40 = **+2 mm**. Salt-water piece:
   36 − 40 = **−4 mm**. The salt-water piece lost water (negative
   change; also visibly limp). Accept "the numbers show shrinkage."

**Independent practice**
5. Sample: "The plain water has a higher water concentration than the
   lettuce cells' interiors, so water moves by osmosis *into* the
   cells across their selectively permeable membranes. The water
   fills the central vacuoles, which press outward against the cell
   walls — and that pressure makes the leaf firm again."
6. Sample: "The membrane could fold inward around the sugar particle
   and pinch it off inside the cell. This shows the membrane is part
   of a living system: getting food in is active work the whole cell
   does, not just passive leaking." (Any reasonable mechanism +
   the systems point earns credit.)
7. Two errors: (1) it is *water* that moves in osmosis, not salt;
   (2) water moves from *higher* water concentration to *lower*
   water concentration (the original reverses it). Corrected:
   "Osmosis is the diffusion of water across a selectively permeable
   membrane, from where water is more concentrated to where it is
   less concentrated." Learners err because they track the solute
   instead of the water.

**Application:** full credit for a design that holds temperature and
drop size constant, changes only stirring, and states that *faster
spreading with stirring* would show movement has a non-diffusion
cause (bulk flow), while *equal spreading* would be inconclusive
without repeats. **Exit check:** sample — "Water moves by osmosis
into the cell across the selectively permeable membrane, filling the
central vacuole. The full vacuole presses against the stiff cell
wall, and that pressure holds the cell — and the plant — firm. The
membrane controls the traffic, the vacuole stores the water, and the
wall resists the push."

## Lesson 03

**Guided practice**
1. Sample: "Its long axon shape helps it carry messages over long
   distances." / "Its disc shape helps it pick up and drop off
   oxygen across a large surface." / "Its long hair shape helps it
   absorb water from soil." / "Their curved-pair shape helps them
   open and close to control gas and water flow."
2. Neuron → nervous tissue; red blood cell → blood tissue; root hair
   cell → root absorptive (epidermal) tissue; guard cells → leaf
   surface (epidermal) tissue.
3. The waxy cuticle is secreted *by* the epidermal cells, not made of
   cells itself. The tissue is the layer of epidermal cells beneath;
   their secreted coating does not disqualify them — a tissue is
   defined by its cells working together.
4. Sample revision: "The same DNA can produce different cells
   because cells *specialize*: each cell follows different
   instructions, changing its shape to fit one job in the
   multicellular team."

**Independent practice**
5. Full credit for a paragraph citing ≥2 shape features as evidence
   for the job (e.g., neuron: branches collect signals + long axon
   carries them far).
6. Full credit for any real specialized cell with a correct
   shape→job argument (sperm: streamlined + tail for swimming;
   mesophyll: many chloroplasts + broad shape for light capture;
   bone cell: hard mineralized surroundings for support).
7. "Simple" is wrong: the red blood cell is *highly* specialized —
   its disc shape maximizes surface area for gas exchange and losing
   its nucleus makes room for more oxygen-carrying hemoglobin. It
   traded self-repair for carrying capacity; that is advanced
   specialization, not simplicity.

**Exit check:** any two cells with job + one shape-feature
explanation each.

## Lesson 04

**Guided practice**
1. Cell: muscle cell. Tissue: muscle tissue. Organ: heart. Organ
   system: circulatory system. Organism: human.
2. Cell: leaf mesophyll cell. Tissue: leaf tissue. Organ: leaf.
   Organ system: shoot system. Organism: plant.
3. Nervous tissue — coordinates when the heart beats; connective
   tissue — holds the heart's structure together and connects it to
   vessels. (Any two non-muscle tissues with correct contributions.)
4. Sample rebuttal: "If the heart's cells die, the heart stops —
   the organ depends entirely on its cells. Bigger does not mean
   more important; in a system every level depends on the one below
   it."

**Independent practice**
5. Full credit for five sentences: neuron carries messages; nervous
   tissue coordinates many neurons; the brain integrates signals;
   the nervous system coordinates the whole body; the human is the
   complete organism.
6. Full credit for five sentences: root hair cell absorbs water;
   absorptive tissue absorbs across a wide area; the root anchors
   and gathers; the root system serves the whole plant's water and
   support needs; the plant is the complete organism.
7. Level error: the stomach is an **organ**, not a tissue — it is
   made of several tissues (muscle, nervous, epithelial, connective)
   working together to digest food.

**Exit check:** five labeled boxes in order with the human example;
   the sentence must state that a system coordinates multiple organs
   (e.g., "the circulatory system moves materials between the heart,
   lungs, and body, which no single organ can do alone").

## Lesson 05

**Guided practice**
1. Sample: Strength — "It shows which structures exist and where
   they sit relative to each other." Limit — "It cannot show that a
   real cell is 3D, that materials constantly move, or the true tiny
   scale."
2. **(b)** is interaction evidence: it shows one part (mitochondria)
   compensating when another (chloroplasts) goes idle. (a) is a
   parts list — no interaction shown.
3. Sample reasoning: "This supports the claim because the guard
   cells' opening *changes what the mesophyll cells can do* — the
   parts affect each other's work, which is exactly what makes them
   a system rather than independent parts."
4. Sample: "A system is a set of *interacting* parts that work
   together as a whole, so that changing one part changes what the
   whole can do."

**Independent practice**
5. Full credit: clear claim, ≥2 unit-based evidence items showing
   interaction, one "why it counts" reasoning sentence per item, and
   one honest evidence limit (e.g., "our evidence comes from models
   and kitchen demonstrations, not from watching molecules cross a
   real membrane").
6. Full credit: one good question + one unanswerable question per
   chosen model + a sensible pairing (e.g., cell diagram + diffusion
   demo to cover movement).
7. Right: models are simplified. Wrong: "less scientific" —
   models are central to science *because* their limits are named
   and tested; a model that hid its simplifications would be the
   unscientific one.

**Exit check:** sample — "The membrane does not just wall the cell
in: in the osmosis demo, water moving across the membrane changed
the whole potato piece's firmness, with the vacuole and wall
responding. A mere wall would only block; the membrane *regulates*
traffic, and the cell's behavior changes with what crosses. That
regulation — interacting with the vacuole's storage and the wall's
support — makes it a working part of the cell system."

## Practice bank

**P1.** Sample: Nucleus — holds DNA instructions; without it the
cell could not direct protein building, growth, or division.
Mitochondria — release energy from food; without them the cell
could not power its work. Cell membrane — controls entry/exit;
without it contents spill and wastes build up.
**P2.** The animal cell's mitochondria work harder — with no food
coming in, the animal cell must live on stored reserves, and every
bit of usable energy must be released by mitochondria. The plant
cell likely survives longer: it can draw on stored starch (and in
light would make more food). Reasoning must cite mitochondria's
energy-release job.
**P3.** Both: nucleus, mitochondria, cell membrane, ribosomes.
Plant only: cell wall (shape/support without a skeleton),
chloroplast (makes its own food), large central vacuole (water
storage + firmness). Animal only: many lysosomes (breaking down
varied incoming materials). (Accept "small vacuoles" noted for
animal cells.)
**P4.** Ribosomes build proteins; rough ER moves them along; Golgi
finishes, packages, and ships them. With no Golgi, proteins would be
built and moved but never finished, addressed, or shipped — the
pipeline stalls at the packaging step.
**P5.** Accurate: it receives, finishes, and ships proteins like a
shipping department. Breaks down: it is made of membranes inside one
cell, not a separate building; and its "packages" are molecules, not
boxes. (Any one accurate + one breakdown.)
**P6.** The root cell gets no light, so chloroplasts would be
useless — but it still needs energy, which only mitochondria
release. The leaf cell needs chloroplasts to make food *and*
mitochondria to release energy from that food.
**P7.** No — the membrane's job is holding the cell together and
controlling traffic. With it damaged, contents leak out, the
internal environment collapses, and intact organelles cannot
function in the wreckage.
**P8.** Corrected: "The vacuole is essential storage: in plant
cells the large central vacuole holds water whose pressure against
the cell wall keeps the plant firm — without it the cell goes
limp." (Must cite the water-pressure evidence.)
**P9.** Diffusion: movement of particles from higher to lower
concentration until evenly spread. Osmosis: the diffusion of water
across a selectively permeable membrane. Relationship: "Osmosis is
diffusion of water across a membrane."
**P10.** Hot water's particles move faster, so collisions spread the
color faster. Wrong explanation: "heat pulls/attracts the color" —
heat does not pull; faster particle motion spreads it.
**P11.** It will shrivel: the salt water has lower water
concentration than the grape's cells, so water moves by osmosis out
of the grape.
**P12.** Sample: "The plain water has higher water concentration
than the celery cells, so water moves by osmosis into the cells
across their selectively permeable membranes, filling the vacuoles
and pressing against the cell walls — restoring firmness."
**P13.** (a) Plain water: +2 mm; salt water: −4 mm. (b) The
salt-water piece lost water (negative change). (c) Water moved by
osmosis *out of* the potato cells into the salt water.
**P14.** Error: reversed direction and ambiguous "concentration."
Corrected: "Osmosis is water moving from *higher* water
concentration to *lower* water concentration across a selectively
permeable membrane." Learners err by tracking the solute's
concentration instead of the water's.
**P15.** cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism. Plant
examples: mesophyll cell, leaf tissue, leaf, shoot system, plant.
**P16.** Root hair cell (long hair → soil absorption); neuron
(long axon + branches → message carrying); guard cells (curved pair
→ gas/water control); red blood cell (disc → oxygen transport).
Each needs one shape feature cited.
**P17.** Sample: "They carry the same DNA but *specialize* — each
cell follows different instructions, so its shape changes to fit one
job in the multicellular team."
**P18.** Defensible either way with reasoning: yes — red blood
cells working together to transport oxygen fit "similar cells, one
job"; debated — blood also contains white blood cells and platelets
doing different jobs, plus plasma that is not cells. Full credit
for a definition-based defense that notes the debate.
**P19.** Five sentences: mesophyll cell photosynthesizes; leaf
tissue captures light across a surface; the leaf combines tissues
into a food-making organ; the shoot system positions leaves and
moves materials; the plant is the whole organism.
**P20.** Full five-level heart trace: muscle cell → cardiac muscle
tissue (coordinates contraction) → heart (pumps; combines muscle,
nervous, connective tissues) → circulatory system (moves materials
body-wide) → human.
**P21.** Full credit for a three-sentence indispensability argument
for either choice + one honest concession sentence for the other.
**P22.** Sample: (1) Water leaves its cells by osmosis (salt water
has lower water concentration) — the organism shrivels; the
membrane cannot stop it because it is permeable to water. (2) Its
membrane's selective transport is overwhelmed — needed substances
cannot be taken in against the gradient, so normal cell work
stalls.
**P23.** (a) Cell diagram: strength — shows which structures exist
and where; limit — static, 2D, not to scale. (b) Levels diagram:
strength — building order visible; limit — hides interactions.
(c) Diffusion demo: strength — shows movement over time;
limit — does not show a membrane. Only (c) can answer "how fast
does spreading happen / what speeds it up."
**P24.** Sample: "Claim: a tissue is more than a pile of cells.
Evidence: muscle tissue's cells all contract together to move a
limb — a random pile of cells could not do that. This supports the
claim because the coordinated shared job is what makes it a tissue,
not just the number of cells."

## Unit assessment

**A1. (4 pts)** (a) Leaf vs. root (both plant): shared — e.g.,
cell wall, which gives both cells shape and protection; differ —
chloroplasts: the leaf cell makes food with light, while the root
cell, underground, cannot and does without.
(b) Leaf vs. muscle: shared — mitochondria (both release energy
from food); differ — chloroplast/cell wall (plant) vs. neither
(animal); the leaf makes its own food while the muscle must be
supplied by the organism. (1 pt per structure+job; 4 total.)
**A2. (4 pts)** Ribosomes build proteins from DNA instructions; the
ER carries the new proteins along; the Golgi was supposed to finish,
package, and ship them. Without Golgi, proteins pile up unfinished
and unaddressed — the cell cannot deliver proteins where needed.
**A3. (4 pts)** Sample: "The membrane alone cannot keep the water
out — it is permeable to water, so water keeps entering. The *whole
cell* solves the problem by spending energy to pump water back out.
That is a system: the membrane sets the traffic rules and the
cell's energy supply pays for the exceptions — the parts work
together, so the cell is more than its boundary."

**B1. (5 pts)** (a) Diffusion. (b) Hot water's particles move
faster, so collisions spread the color faster. (c) Heat does not
pull — faster-moving particles collide and spread more quickly;
"pull" implies a force that is not there. (2+2+1.)
**B2. (5 pts)** Sample: "The cold plain water has a higher water
concentration than the lettuce cells, so water moved by osmosis
*into* the cells across their selectively permeable membranes,
filling the vacuoles and pressing against the cell walls — the leaf
firmed up." Must name all three terms and the inward direction.

**C1. (4 pts)** Muscle cell — contracts. Muscle tissue —
coordinates many cells' contraction. Heart — combines muscle,
nervous, and connective tissues into a pump. Circulatory system —
moves oxygen, food, and wastes body-wide. Human — the whole
organism all systems sustain. (Accept equivalent "adds" sentences.)
**C2. (4 pts)** Missing: tissue (epithelial tissue — skin cells
working together; without it the trace cannot explain coordinated
protection) and organ system (integumentary system — skin plus
hair/nails; without it the trace cannot show how skin cooperates
with other organs). 1 pt per level named + 1 pt per explanation.

**D1. (6 pts)** Sample proficient response: "A cell is a system of
interacting parts, not a collection of independent structures. When
potato cells sat in salt water, water left by osmosis across their
membranes and the whole pieces went limp — the membrane, the
vacuole's water, and the wall acted together, and no single part
explains the limpness alone. This supports the claim because changing
what crosses one part changed what the whole could do — the
definition of interacting parts. Likewise, when a plant cell loses
light, its chloroplasts go idle but its mitochondria keep it alive
on stored food — parts compensating for each other, which only makes
sense in a system. This counts because compensation is an
interaction: one part's state changes another part's workload. One
honest limit: our evidence comes from models, diagrams, and kitchen
demonstrations, not from watching molecules cross a real membrane."
(2 pts claim+structure, 2 pts evidence, 1 pt reasoning quality, 1 pt
limit. Accept any two genuine unit interactions.)
**D2. (4 pts)** Sample: cell diagram — (a) answers "which structures
does a cell have and where"; (b) cannot answer "how do materials
move in real time"; (c) pair with the diffusion demo. (1 pt per
part; the pairing must genuinely cover the gap.)

## Project rubric — scoring guidance

- **Levels (30%):** 3 = five correct levels + examples; 2 = four
  levels or one mislabeled; 1 = missing/out of order.
- **Structure-function (30%):** 3 = ≥4 "if-missing" annotations each
  naming a *specific* whole-cell failure + captions naming each
  level's added capability; 2 = vague consequences ("would die");
  1 = incorrect or missing.
- **Systems argument (25%):** 3 = ≥2 genuine interactions + one
  honest model limit; 2 = one interaction or no limit; 1 = parts
  list restated.
- **Presentation (15%):** 3 = fluent bottom-to-top trace + answers a
  follow-up; 2 = reads captions; 1 = heavy prompting needed.
- Totals: 12 exemplary · 9–11 proficient · 6–8 revise flagged part ·
  <6 reteach Lessons 01–04 and rebuild.
