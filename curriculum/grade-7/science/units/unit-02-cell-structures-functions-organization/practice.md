# Practice Bank — Unit 02: Cell Structures, Functions, and Organization

24 items across the unit's practice sessions. Items marked ★ are
revised from the legacy `grade-7/stem/quizzes/week-07-quiz.md` bank
(Q1–Q2, the Unit-02-relevant items, rewritten with new contexts and
full keys); Q3–Q6 of that quiz belong to Units 04/07 and are banked
there. Keys in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md#practice-bank).

## A. Organelle functions and the whole cell (Sessions 2, 12, 14)

**P1. ★** Name three organelles and, for each, state its function and
explain what the *whole cell* could not do without it.

**P2.** A plant cell and an animal cell are each cut off from all
energy input for a day (no light for the plant, no food for the
animal). Which cell's mitochondria work harder during that day, and
why? Which cell survives longer? Defend with structure-function
reasoning.

**P3.** Sort into "both cell types," "plant only," "animal only":
nucleus, cell wall, chloroplast, mitochondria, large central vacuole,
cell membrane, lysosomes (many), ribosomes. For each "only," name the
whole-cell job that explains the difference.

**P4.** A learner draws a cell with a nucleus, ER, and Golgi but no
ribosomes. Explain precisely what breaks in the cell's protein
pipeline, naming which step each structure handles.

**P5.** The Golgi apparatus is called the cell's "shipping
department." Name one way this comparison is accurate and one way it
breaks down.

**P6.** Explain why a root cell (underground, no light) still needs
mitochondria but does not need chloroplasts — and why a leaf cell
needs *both*.

**P7.** ★ A cell's membrane is damaged but all its organelles are
intact. Can the cell survive? Explain using the membrane's
whole-cell job.

**P8.** Error analysis: "The vacuole is just a storage bubble, so it
is the least important organelle." Rewrite this as a correct
structure-function statement, using the plant cell's water pressure
as evidence.

## B. Membrane transport: diffusion and osmosis (Sessions 4, 12, 14)

**P9.** Define *diffusion* and *osmosis* in your own words. Then
state the relationship between them in one sentence.

**P10.** A drop of food coloring spreads through hot water in 40
seconds and through cold water in 3 minutes. Explain the difference
using particle motion. What is the *wrong* explanation to avoid?

**P11.** Predict and explain: a grape placed in very salty water
overnight — will it swell or shrivel? Track the *water* in your
explanation.

**P12.** A wilted celery stalk firms up in plain water. Write the
osmosis explanation using all three terms: *selectively permeable*,
*osmosis*, *cell wall*.

**P13.** Data task (fictional practice dataset, not a real
observation): two identical potato pieces, initial length 40 mm each.
After 30 minutes, the piece in plain water measures 42 mm and the
piece in salt water measures 36 mm. (a) Compute each change.
(b) Which piece lost water? Cite the numbers. (c) State the
osmosis direction for the salt-water piece.

**P14.** Error analysis: "Osmosis is water moving from low
concentration to high concentration." Identify the error and correct
it — and explain why learners make this mistake (hint: concentration
of *what*?).

## C. Specialization, tissues, and levels (Sessions 6, 9, 12, 14)

**P15. ★** Put in order from smallest to largest: organ system,
tissue, organism, cell, organ. Then give the human-column example
for each level from Lesson 04.

**P16.** Using the [specialized-cells
illustration](assets/specialized-cells-illustration.png) (or its
text alternative in Lesson 03), match each cell to its job and cite
one shape feature as evidence for each match.

**P17.** A nerve cell and a muscle cell carry the same DNA. Explain
in two sentences how they can look and act so differently.

**P18.** Is blood a tissue? Defend your answer with the definition of
*tissue* — and note where the definition gets debatable.

**P19.** Trace the plant column end to end (mesophyll cell → plant),
writing one sentence per level on what that level adds.

**P20.** A learner traces: cell → organ → organism, skipping tissue
and system. Rewrite the trace for the heart example with all five
levels, naming each level's contribution.

## D. Mixed review and unit synthesis (Sessions 12, 14)

**P21.** Choose: the membrane or the nucleus. Argue in three
sentences that your choice is *indispensable to the cell as a
system* — then write one sentence conceding what the *other*
structure does that yours cannot.

**P22.** A single-celled pond organism is placed in salt water. Using
everything from Lessons 01–02, predict two things that happen to it
and explain each with a structure or process from the unit.

**P23.** Name one strength and one limit of *each* of these models:
(a) your labeled cell diagram, (b) the organization-levels diagram,
(c) the food-coloring diffusion demo. Which question can only (c)
answer?

**P24.** Write a three-sentence CER mini-argument for the claim "A
tissue is more than a pile of cells." Use one piece of evidence from
Lesson 03.
