# Unit Assessment — Unit 01: Scientific Evidence, Microscopy, and Cell Models

**Instructions:** Answer in your notebook. Show units on every number
and show your work on calculations. Explanations: 2–4 sentences unless
noted. The project (Cell Model Gallery) is submitted with this
assessment. Solutions are in the [answer key](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment);
this page contains no answers.

**Objectives checked:** 1–5.

## Part A — Evidence and measurement (Objectives 1–2)

**A1.** (a) Write one observation and one inference about a burning
candle. Label each. (b) Explain which one could serve as scientific
evidence as written, and what would make the other one testable.

**A2.** A scientist claims a new fertilizer "obviously works great."
Name two of the four evidence tests this claim fails, and rewrite the
claim as a testable, measurable statement.

**A3.** Convert 1.5 hours to seconds. Then convert 800 cm to meters.
Show each multiplication or division.

**A4.** Four temperature readings of the same water bath (true
temperature 60°C): 60.2°C, 59.8°C, 60.1°C, 59.9°C. Evaluate accuracy
and precision, and explain what the tight clustering tells you.

## Part B — Microscopy and scale (Objective 3)

**B1.** A microscope has a 10× eyepiece. (a) Give the total
magnification with the 4× and 40× objectives. (b) Explain why you
begin focusing with the 4× objective, giving two reasons.

**B2.** A red blood cell is about 8 µm across. (a) How many red blood
cells laid side by side span 1 mm? (b) The naked eye resolves about
100 µm. Explain in two sentences why red blood cells were unknown
before the microscope.

**B3.** Name the three tenets of cell theory and, for each, the
historical step that established it (Hooke / Leeuwenhoek /
Schleiden & Schwann / Virchow).

## Part C — Cells and models (Objectives 4–5)

**C1.** A classmate examines pond water at 100×, sees nothing moving,
and concludes "this pond water has no living things in it." Identify
two flaws in this investigation and describe how to fix each one.

**C2.** Is a mushroom unicellular or multicellular? Is yeast
unicellular or multicellular? For each, give the evidence a
microscope would show.

**C3.** Write a claim-evidence-reasoning paragraph defending this
claim: "Living things are made of cells." Use at least two distinct
pieces of evidence from this unit and explain the reasoning that
connects each piece to the claim. (4–6 sentences.)

**C4.** (a) Name the plant-cell parts that an animal cell lacks, and
explain what each contributes to the plant cell as a whole.
(b) Correct this label: "Cell wall — controls what enters and leaves
the cell." Name the part that actually does this job and explain the
difference between the two parts' roles.

**Scoring:** A1–A4, B1–B3, C1–C2, C4 = 3 points each (33); C3 = 7
points (claim 1, evidence 2, reasoning 2, writing 2) = **40 points
total**. 32+ = meets; 24–31 = approaching (revise missed objectives
with the listed lesson); below 24 = reteach the unit's weakest
objective before Unit 02, using the teacher guide's reteach plan.
