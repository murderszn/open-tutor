# Project — Cell Model Gallery

**Sessions:** 11 (launch), 13 (workshop); submitted with the Session
16 assessment.
**Question it answers:** How do a plant cell's parts and an animal
cell's parts each contribute to the cell's function as a whole — and
how do the two cells differ in ways that make sense for how each
organism lives?

## What you will make

A two-page "gallery": one labeled **plant cell model** and one
labeled **animal cell model** (drawn, built from craft materials and
photographed, or made in a digital drawing tool), plus two short
pieces of writing. This revises and extends the track's legacy
diagram tasks —
[animal-cells](../../../../grade-7/stem/assignments/animal-cells.md)
and
[plant-cells](../../../../grade-7/stem/assignments/plant-cells.md)
— adding the whole-cell explanation, the model-limits note, and a
full rubric, which those tasks lacked.

## Deliverables

1. **Plant cell model** — labeled: cell wall, cell membrane, nucleus,
   cytoplasm, chloroplasts, vacuole, mitochondria. (The legacy
   plant-cells task also listed ribosomes, ER, Golgi — include any of
   these you research, with a one-sentence job each, for enrichment
   credit.)
2. **Animal cell model** — labeled: cell membrane, nucleus, cytoplasm,
   mitochondria. (Legacy list also included ribosomes, ER, Golgi
   apparatus, lysosomes — same enrichment option.)
3. **Whole-cell caption** (one paragraph, 4–6 sentences): explain how
   the parts work together to keep the cell alive — a system, not a
   parts list. Must reference at least three parts and what each
   contributes to the whole.
4. **Comparison paragraph** (5–7 sentences): how plant and animal
   cells differ in energy capture (chloroplasts/photosynthesis vs.
   mitochondria/respiration in both), support (wall vs. flexible
   membrane), and why each difference makes sense for how the organism
   lives. (Revised from the legacy comparison paragraphs, now with
   explicit energy-pathway content.)
5. **Model-limits note** (2–3 sentences): one thing your model cannot
   show about a real cell (movement, true 3-D shape, scale, chemistry)
   and why that limit matters for interpreting it.
6. **Lab-report header**: use the
   [lab-report template](../../../../../assignments/science/physical-science-roadmap/lab-report-template.md)
   sections — question, variables (not applicable here: write "model
   build — no variables manipulated"), procedure (steps you followed),
   conclusion — as the cover page of your gallery.

## Steps

1. **Session 11:** Choose plant or animal first. List every required
   part and draft its one-sentence job *for the whole cell*. Sketch a
   layout with parts in plausible positions (nucleus inside the
   membrane — never outside it).
2. **Session 11 (continued):** Draft the whole-cell caption. Test
   every label with: "does this say what the part does *for the
   cell*?" Fix any slogan labels ("powerhouse," "brain").
3. **Session 13:** Build both models. Label each part with name +
   one-sentence job. Circle the parts that differ between plant and
   animal.
4. **Session 13 (continued):** Write the comparison paragraph and the
   model-limits note. Assemble the gallery with the lab-report cover
   page.
5. **Session 14:** Peer-check with the adult using the rubric below;
   revise before submitting in Session 16.

## Rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Solid | 2 — Developing | 1 — Starting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Plant model: parts & labels** | All 7 required parts present, correctly placed and labeled with name + job-for-the-whole-cell | 6 of 7 present and correct | 4–5 present, or several labels are name-only | Fewer than 4, or major misplacements (e.g., nucleus outside membrane) |
| **Animal model: parts & labels** | All 4 required parts present, correctly placed and labeled with name + job-for-the-whole-cell | All 4 present; most labels complete | 3 present, or several labels are name-only | Fewer than 3, or major misplacements |
| **Whole-cell caption** | 4–6 sentences; ≥3 parts each tied to the cell's life as a whole; reads as a system explanation | 3 parts tied to the whole; minor gaps | Parts listed with jobs but not connected to the whole | Parts list only, or inaccurate function claims |
| **Comparison paragraph** | 5–7 sentences; energy pathways (chloroplasts vs. mitochondria), wall vs. membrane, and *why* each difference fits the organism's life | Covers energy + structure with a partial "why" | Covers one difference accurately | Inaccurate differences or no explanation |
| **Model-limits note + lab-report cover** | Names a real limit and why it matters; cover page complete (question, procedure, conclusion) | Limit named; cover page mostly complete | Limit vague; cover page incomplete | Missing |

**Scoring:** 18–20 = exceeds; 14–17 = meets; 10–13 = approaching
(revise the lowest row and resubmit); below 10 = rebuild with the
adult, focusing on the two model rows first.

## Adult notes

- Accept any medium: drawing, collage, clay, digital. Judge the
  *explanation*, not the artistry.
- The enrichment parts (ribosomes, ER, Golgi, lysosomes, vacuole
  detail) are optional; do not penalize their absence, and award no
  more than the rubric allows for their presence — accuracy of the
  core is what counts.
- If the learner's caption says "the nucleus controls the cell," push
  for mechanism: *what* does it contribute? (It holds the DNA
  instructions the cell uses.)
