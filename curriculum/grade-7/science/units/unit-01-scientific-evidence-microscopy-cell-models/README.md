# Unit 01 — Scientific Evidence, Microscopy, and Cell Models

**Grade:** 7 · **Subject:** Science · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #35](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/35), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

How do we know that living things are made of cells? Nobody can see a
cell with the naked eye, so the claim rests on evidence: careful
observations, measurements with units, microscopes that extend our
senses, and models that explain how a cell works as a whole. In this
unit the learner builds the evidence-handling habits of the whole
track — observation vs. inference, SI measurement, accuracy vs.
precision, data tables — learns to use a light microscope (or to read
verified micrographs when no microscope is available), and assembles
the case for cell theory: that all living things are made of cells,
either one cell or many different kinds of cells. The unit closes with
labeled plant and animal cell models that show how each part helps the
cell work as a system.

## Essential question

What counts as evidence that something is alive and made of cells, and
how do microscopes and models turn that evidence into understanding?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the grade 6 track:

- Distinguishing a careful observation from an inference (re-taught here,
  not assumed)
- Measuring length, mass, and temperature in metric units
- Reading simple bar graphs and data tables
- Writing a paragraph that states a claim with one or two reasons

No microscope experience is assumed — Lesson 03 teaches it from zero,
with verified-micrograph alternatives for learners without a microscope.
No prior knowledge of cell theory is assumed.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Distinguish observations from inferences; explain what makes evidence
   scientific (testable, measurable, repeatable, checked by others); and
   sort statements into observation vs. inference with a reason.
2. Measure with SI units and always record the unit; evaluate accuracy
   vs. precision using repeated measurements; build labeled data tables
   for a simple investigation.
3. Identify the main parts of a light microscope and describe how to
   bring a slide into focus safely; compute total magnification from
   eyepiece and objective powers; explain, using scale, why cells must
   be magnified to be seen.
4. Use cell-theory tenets and evidence (microscope/micrograph
   observations, "is it living?" sorts) to argue that living things are
   made of cells — either one cell or many different numbers and types
   of cells.
5. Build labeled plant and animal cell models and explain, in a
   claim-evidence-reasoning paragraph, how the cell's parts contribute
   to its function as a whole.
