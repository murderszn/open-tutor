# Practice Bank — Unit 01

Work these in the practice sessions (Sessions 2, 4, 6, 7, 9, 12) and
any spare minutes. Items P1–P8 cover evidence and observation (Lesson
01); P9–P16 measurement and data (Lesson 02, consolidating the legacy
week-01 measurement quizzes with new context variety); P17–P20 lab
skills and variables (consolidating the legacy week-08 lab-skills
items Q1–Q6; the week-08 physical-science review items are not used
here); P21–P24 microscopy and scale (Lesson 03). Full solutions are in
the [answer key](answer-key.md#practice-bank).

## A. Evidence and observation (P1–P8)

**P1.** Sort each into observation (O) or inference (I):
(a) "The sidewalk is wet." (b) "It rained last night."
(c) "The beaker holds 250 mL." (d) "The beaker is half full because
someone poured some out."

**P2.** A learner writes: "I observed that the dog was happy because
its tail was wagging." Separate the observation from the inference and
rewrite as two clean sentences.

**P3.** Which of the four evidence tests (testable, measurable,
repeatable, checked by others) does "This is the best pizza in town"
fail? Rewrite it as a testable claim.

**P4.** Turn into scientific evidence: "The plant grew a lot."
Include a number, a unit, and a time span.

**P5.** Label claim, evidence, reasoning: "The milk has spoiled. It
smells sour and has lumps, and the carton date was two weeks ago.
Sour smell and curdling are signs that bacteria have grown in milk."

**P6.** A scientist measures a metal rod's length once: 15.2 cm, then
publishes "the rod is exactly 15.2 cm long." Name two evidence
problems with this.

**P7.** Two friends disagree: "The water is cold" vs. "The water is
14°C." Whose statement is stronger evidence, and which two evidence
tests does it pass that the other fails?

**P8.** Error analysis: "Since the seeds didn't sprout in the dark
cupboard, darkness kills seeds." Identify the inference, name a
rival inference that fits the same observation, and describe one test
that would distinguish them.

## B. Measurement and data (P9–P16)

**P9.** Name the SI base unit for (a) length, (b) mass, (c) time,
(d) temperature.

**P10.** Convert: (a) 4.2 km to meters; (b) 350 cm to meters;
(c) 2.5 hours to seconds.

**P11.** A runner covers 200 m in 25 s. Compute the average speed in
m/s. Show the division.

**P12.** A recipe calls for 0.75 L of water. How many milliliters is
that? Explain the conversion in one sentence.

**P13.** Three measurements of the same table: 1.52 m, 1.51 m,
1.53 m. True length 1.52 m. Accurate? Precise? One sentence each.

**P14.** A scale reads 50 g, 55 g, 48 g for a 50 g weight. Accurate?
Precise? Explain, and say what you would do next.

**P15.** Build a titled data table for this investigation: "Does
warm water dissolve sugar faster than cold water?" Include columns for
trial, water temperature (°C), and time to dissolve (s), with three
trials per temperature.

**P16.** Error analysis: a learner reports a leaf's width as
"8.473 cm" using a ruler marked in centimeters. What is wrong, and
what should be reported?

## C. Lab skills and variables (P17–P20)

**P17.** In an experiment testing whether light color affects plant
growth: name the independent variable, the dependent variable, and
two variables that must be controlled.

**P18.** List two common sources of experimental error in a
pendulum-timing experiment and one way to reduce each.

**P19.** Sketch (describe in words or draw) a data table for
recording the temperature of cooling water every minute for 10
minutes. What must the column headers include?

**P20.** What belongs in the conclusion of a lab report? List the
three things it must do, in order.

## D. Microscopy and scale (P21–P24)

**P21.** A microscope has a 10× eyepiece and 4×, 10×, 40× objectives.
Give the three total magnifications.

**P22.** A human hair is about 75 µm wide. How many hairs, laid side
by side, span 3 mm? Show the work.

**P23.** Put in order from smallest to largest: E. coli bacterium
(2 µm), virus (100 nm), red blood cell (8 µm), amoeba (500 µm).
Convert all to micrometers first.

**P24.** Error analysis: "I looked at pond water at 40× total
magnification, saw nothing moving, and concluded the water contains
no cells." Give two flaws in this investigation and how to fix each.
