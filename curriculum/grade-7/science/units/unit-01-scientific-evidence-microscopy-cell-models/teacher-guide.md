# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Scientific Evidence, Microscopy, and Cell Models

Adult-facing. Student pages never link here except where a lesson
says "the adult checks." Keep this file separate from the learner.

## Unit at a glance

- **Sessions:** 16 × 45–50 min (track weeks 3–6).
- **Core lessons:** Sessions 1, 3, 5, 8, 10 (Lessons 01–05).
- **Objectives:** 1 evidence, 2 measurement, 3 microscopy/scale,
  4 cells-are-made-of-cells investigation (MS-LS1-1), 5 explanatory
  cell models (MS-LS1-2).
- **Project:** Cell Model Gallery (Sessions 11, 13; submitted
  Session 16) — 20-point rubric in [project.md](project.md).
- **Assessments:** formative quiz Session 15 (measurement + lab
  skills, 20 pts), unit assessment Session 16 (all objectives,
  40 pts). Full keys in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## Session-by-session guidance

**Session 1 (L1).** The observation/inference distinction is the
track's load-bearing habit — spend the full lesson on it. Use the
readiness objects; insist learners *label* every statement. Do not
let "inference" become a synonym for "wrong answer."

**Session 2.** P1–P8. Watch P6/P8 (repeatability, rival inferences)
— these are the hardest. Start the lab notebook: date, question,
procedure, table, conclusion.

**Session 3 (L2).** Bring real tools. Have the learner measure the
same object three times *before* you teach accuracy vs. precision —
the dartboard lands harder on their own data. Drill "no naked
numbers": every number gets a unit, every time.

**Session 4.** P9–P16 + the three-objects measurement task. Check
data tables for the three defects (title, unit in header, numbered
trials). The legacy week-01 items are consolidated here with new
contexts — the originals remain in `stem/` untouched.

**Session 5 (L3).** The generated microscope illustration is
*unlabeled on purpose* — it is the labeling activity. Walk the
five-step focusing sequence with the real instrument or the
illustration; quiz the order until it is automatic. The scale
diagram (`cell-size-scale.svg`) is deterministic — trust its
positions. If you have no microscope, use two printed micrographs
with visible scale bars (resource pack) and teach "read the
magnification and scale bar first" as the focusing equivalent.

**Session 6.** Labeling activity + P21–P24. The text alternative in
Lesson 03 covers learners who cannot use the image.

**Session 7.** Read the lab-report template together; run the
variables clinic (P17–P20). Legacy week-08 items Q1–Q6 only — Q7–Q10
are physical-science review and stay out of this unit.

**Session 8 (L4).** The "is it living?" sort is the MS-LS1-1
investigation core. Include one tricky item (dormant seed, dried
yeast) and one non-living energy user (a candle flame or a wind-up
toy) — the five-sign checklist must do real work. Press the "one
cell or many?" inference and make learners label it as inference
when no microscope evidence exists.

**Session 9.** Finish sorts; draft the first CER paragraph with the
sentence frames. Sketching session: onion/cork under the microscope
or from micrographs — grade the *observation-vs-inference
discipline* in the sketch labels, not the drawing skill.

**Session 10 (L5).** Teach "contribution to the whole" as the
grading lens for every label. Ban slogan labels ("powerhouse,"
"brain") on sight — require the mechanism sentence. Preview the
project rubric.

**Session 11.** Project launch. Approve each learner's part list
before building (catches the nucleus-outside-membrane error early).

**Session 12.** Mixed review P1–P24 + project work. Pull the items
the learner missed in Sessions 2/4/6/7/9.

**Session 13.** Project workshop. Comparison paragraph is usually
the weakest deliverable — require the *why* sentence ("this
difference makes sense because…").

**Session 14.** Error-analysis clinic from the week's practice;
CER revision against this checklist: claim stated? ≥2 evidence
pieces? reasoning explains the connection (not just restates
evidence)? Then the formative quiz prep.

**Session 15.** Formative quiz → self-score with the key →
reteach missed objectives on the spot (see reteach plan).

**Session 16.** Unit assessment (closed-notebook except the scale
diagram, which may stay visible) + project submission.

## Misconception watch

| Misconception | Where it surfaces | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Inference = wrong/guess | L1, L4 | Inferences are how science advances; they must be *labeled and tested* |
| "Bigger number = more magnification is always better" | L3 | Trade-off: field of view, depth, brightness |
| Adding lens powers (10+40=50×) | L3, P21 | Multiply: each lens magnifies the previous image |
| Movement = alive | L4 | Full five-sign checklist; flames and rivers move |
| Once-living = living | L4 | "What life processes does it show *right now*?" |
| Wall controls entry/exit | L5, C4 | Membrane = selective boundary; wall = support |
| Slogan labels explain | L5, project | Require "what it does *for the whole cell*" |

## Differentiation

- **Support:** sentence frames for CER; vocabulary cards for the ten
  microscope parts and six organelle jobs; pointing/oral response
  modes for sorts and labeling; adult-scribed measurements with the
  learner verifying units.
- **Extension:** electron-microscope research (L3); extremophile case
  (L4); enrichment organelles with cited sources (project); timing
  experiment with range analysis (L2).

## Scoring and readiness signals

- **Quiz (20 pts):** 16+ ready; 12–15 review-and-reattempt; <12
  reteach Lessons 01–02.
- **Assessment (40 pts):** 32+ meets; 24–31 approaching — revise the
  missed objective's lesson; <24 reteach weakest objective before
  Unit 02.
- **Project (20 pts):** see rubric; <10 rebuild with the adult.
- **Reteach plan:** Objective 1 → L1 + P1–P8 + new sorts. Objective 2
  → L2 + re-measure task. Objective 3 → L3 focusing walkthrough +
  magnification drills. Objective 4 → L4 sort with new items + CER
  frames. Objective 5 → L5 part-job cards + caption rewrite.
