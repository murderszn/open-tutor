# Unit 03 — Photosynthesis, Respiration, and Matter–Energy Flow

**Grade:** 7 · **Subject:** Science · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #35](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/35), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Unit 02 showed how a cell's parts work together and how cells team up
into tissues, organs, and whole organisms. This unit asks where a
living thing's matter and energy actually come from: *how does a plant
build itself out of air and water, how does every living cell get
usable energy out of food, and how do matter and energy move through a
whole ecosystem?* The learner writes and balances the photosynthesis
equation on paper (the legacy assignment's "educator-provided"
equation-builder interactive is replaced here by an explicit
paper-based modeling task), traces carbon atoms from air to leaf to
animal, compares photosynthesis with cellular respiration as a
two-way flow, and closes by building a matter–energy flow model of
one ecosystem.

## Essential question

How do living things build their bodies out of air, water, and food —
and where does the energy to do it come from, and where does it go?

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–02 and the diagnostic weeks:

- Labeled plant and animal cell diagrams; chloroplasts and
  mitochondria named as the organelles of photosynthesis and energy
  release (functions deepened here, not re-taught from zero)
- Claim-evidence-reasoning paragraphs; observation vs. inference;
  labeled data tables
- Atom/molecule vocabulary at an introductory level (a molecule is a
  group of atoms bonded together); counting atoms in a chemical
  formula with subscripts
- Food chains at an everyday level (grass → rabbit → fox); no prior
  knowledge of chemical equations, respiration, or energy transfer
  is assumed — all are taught here from zero

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Write the photosynthesis equation in words and in symbols
   (6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂), name the inputs,
   outputs, and the role of chlorophyll and chloroplasts, and
   explain that light energy is stored as chemical energy in sugar.
2. Trace matter through photosynthesis: explain, with evidence, that
   most of a plant's dry mass comes from carbon dioxide in the air,
   not from soil, and describe the three fates of the sugar a plant
   makes (used now, stored, built into new molecules for growth).
3. Describe cellular respiration in words and symbols (food + oxygen
   → carbon dioxide + water + released energy), explain that food
   molecules are broken apart and rearranged into new molecules, and
   explain why plants as well as animals respire, day and night.
4. Compare photosynthesis and cellular respiration as opposite,
   linked flows of matter and energy, and use a model to show how
   matter cycles while energy flows one way through an ecosystem.
5. Build and explain a food-web-based matter–energy flow model of one
   ecosystem (producers, consumers, decomposers; at least five
   organisms), predict what happens when one organism is removed,
   and name one human impact and one possible response.
6. Write a claim-evidence-reasoning explanation, based on evidence,
   for the role of photosynthesis in the cycling of matter and the
   flow of energy into and out of organisms.

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS)**,
middle-school life science (MS-LS1, "From Molecules to Organisms:
Structures and Processes"; MS-LS2, "Ecosystems: Interactions, Energy,
and Dynamics"), 6–8 grade band. Codes and descriptions were verified
2026-10-05 against the official NGSS listings (see the track
scope-and-sequence) and re-checked against the track crosswalk and
the NSTA topic listing on 2026-10-25. Descriptions are paraphrases,
not reproductions; no state adoption, accreditation, or complete
alignment is claimed. Assessment boundaries are respected: no
biochemical mechanisms of photosynthesis or respiration are
assessed, and chemical reactions are not used to describe ecosystem
processes.

| Unit objective | Standard | Official description (paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 6 | MS-LS1-6 | Construct a scientific explanation based on evidence for the role of photosynthesis in the cycling of matter and flow of energy into and out of organisms. (Clarification: emphasis on tracing movement of matter and flow of energy. Assessment boundary: no biochemical mechanisms of photosynthesis.) |
| 3, 4 | MS-LS1-7 | Develop a model to describe how food is rearranged through chemical reactions forming new molecules that support growth and/or release energy as this matter moves through an organism. (Clarification: emphasis on molecules broken apart and put back together, with energy released. Assessment boundary: no details of the chemical reactions for photosynthesis or respiration.) |
| 4, 5 | MS-LS2-3 | Develop a model to describe the cycling of matter and flow of energy among living and nonliving parts of an ecosystem. (Clarification: emphasis on conservation of matter, flow of energy into and out of ecosystems, and defining the system boundary. Assessment boundary: no chemical reactions used to describe the processes.) |
| 2, 4 | MS-LS1-6, MS-LS2-3 | Matter tracing across the organism/ecosystem boundary: the same carbon atoms move from air to plant to animal to air again. |
| 1–6 | SEP-2 | Developing and using models: the paper equation-builder, the matter–energy cycle diagram, and the food-web model, each with named strengths and limits. |
| 2, 6 | SEP-7 | Engaging in argument from evidence: the van Helmont reasoning task and the closing CER explanation. |
| 4, 5 | CCC-5 | Energy and matter: matter cycles, energy flows one way; tracking flows across a system boundary. |
| 1, 3 | CCC-4 | Systems and system models: the leaf, the cell, and the ecosystem as nested systems. |
