# Unit Assessment — Unit 03: Photosynthesis, Respiration, and Matter–Energy Flow

**Adult-scored; 12 tasks, 45–50 minutes, plus the submitted project
model.** Keys and scoring guidance in
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment). This assessment
is a practice check, not a standardized or validated assessment.

**A1.** Write both equations (photosynthesis and cellular
respiration) in symbols, balanced, with reactants and products
labeled. (Objectives 1, 3)

**A2.** Count and compare: how many oxygen atoms are on each side
of the photosynthesis equation? Show both counts. (Objective 1)

**A3.** Van Helmont's willow gained about 164 pounds in five years
while its soil lost about 2 ounces. A learner concludes, "So trees
are made of water." Evaluate this conclusion in three sentences:
what the numbers rule out, what they leave open, and what the
equation adds. (Objective 2)

**A4.** A potato plant stores starch in its tubers all summer.
Explain, using the three fates of sugar, what happens to that
starch in spring — and why the plant can grow new shoots before
its new leaves photosynthesize much. (Objective 2)

**A5.** A green plant is kept in a dark cupboard for a week,
watered normally. Predict two changes (one about its mass or
appearance, one about the air around it) and explain each with
one equation. (Objectives 2, 3)

**A6.** Explain the difference between breathing and cellular
respiration in two sentences, then explain why a resting person
still respires. (Objective 3)

**A7.** Draw a food web for a garden (lettuce, caterpillar, bird,
snail, thrush, fungi) with at least eight arrows pointing from
food toward eater. Label each organism producer, consumer, or
decomposer. (Objective 5)

**A8.** In your A7 web, trace one carbon atom from the air through
four stops and back to the air, naming the process at each step.
(Objectives 4, 5)

**A9.** Using the approximate 10% enrichment rule, a garden's
lettuce fixes 500 units of energy. Estimate the new-growth energy
available to caterpillars, and then to birds. Show the arithmetic
and state one reason the estimate is rough. (Objective 4)

**A10.** A town removes all thrushes from a garden to protect
fruit. Predict one immediate and one delayed consequence for the
garden food web, each justified by an arrow in your web.
(Objective 5)

**A11.** CER explanation (revised version of the Lesson 05 draft):
write a paragraph answering "What is the role of photosynthesis
in the cycling of matter and the flow of energy into and out of
organisms?" Use the claim-evidence-reasoning frame, at least two
pieces of evidence, and the honest hedge "almost every" (or an
equivalent) for ecosystems that do not run on light.
(Objective 6)

**A12.** Model card: choose one model from this unit (the
equation-builder cards, the cycle diagram, or the seedlings
illustration). State one strength, two limits, and one question
it cannot answer. (Objectives 4, 6)
