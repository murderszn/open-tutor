# Project — Matter–Energy Flow Model

**Deliverable (due Session 16, with the unit assessment):** a
labeled model of one ecosystem — a meadow, pond, garden, or forest
of the learner's choice — that shows photosynthesis, cellular
respiration, and a food web, plus a short written walkthrough.
This project is the food-web entry point for Unit 06: Unit 06 will
deepen it with keystone species and the full 10%-rule treatment,
but this model must already stand on its own.

## What to build

Choose one ecosystem and build the model as a poster, a set of
drawn panels, or a digital diagram. The model must contain:

1. **The two equations, built in.** Show the photosynthesis
   equation (words and symbols) at a producer, and the respiration
   equation (words and symbols) at a consumer — and at the producer
   too, labeled "at night / all the time." Use the paper
   equation-builder cards as a draft, then copy the final equations
   onto the model.
2. **A food web, not a single chain.** At least five organisms:
   at least one producer, at least two consumers, and at least one
   decomposer. At least eight arrows, each pointing from food
   toward eater. Label each organism producer, consumer, or
   decomposer.
3. **A carbon-atom journey.** Pick one carbon atom and trace it
   through at least four stops (air → producer → consumer →
   decomposer → air, or an equivalent route), naming the process at
   each step. Draw the journey as a highlighted path on the model.
4. **The system boundary.** Mark what crosses into the ecosystem
   (sunlight, and any other input you claim), what crosses out
   (heat, and any other output), and what cycles inside. Label the
   boundary explicitly — a drawn frame with "IN," "OUT," and
   "CYCLES INSIDE" lists is enough.
5. **A written walkthrough (one page or about 250 words):**
   - (a) How matter cycles through your ecosystem (the carbon-atom
     journey in sentences).
   - (b) How energy flows through it — and where it leaves.
   - (c) A removal prediction: choose one organism, remove it, and
     predict two consequences (one immediate, one delayed), each
     justified by an arrow in your web.
   - (d) One human impact on your ecosystem and one possible
     response, each stated in one or two sentences.
   - (e) A model card: one strength of your model, two limits, and
     one question it cannot answer.

## Materials

Poster paper or digital drawing tool, colored pencils, the paper
equation-builder cards, the unit's lessons and cycle diagram for
reference. No live animals, no collected specimens, no chemicals.
If the learner wants a real-world observation to enrich the model,
the Session 4 foil-leaf log may be cited as dated observations;
otherwise the model is a constructed explanation, not a lab report
— do not present invented measurements as observations.

## Rubric (adult-scored; 16 points)

| Criterion | 4 — Proficient | 3 — Developing | 2 — Beginning | 1 — Missing/unclear |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Equations (4) | Both equations correct in words and symbols, balanced, placed at the right organisms; night respiration shown | One small symbol/balance slip, or night respiration missing | One equation wrong or missing labels | Equations absent or both wrong |
| Food web (4) | ≥5 organisms, all three roles, ≥8 arrows, all arrows food→eater | One role missing or 1–2 arrows reversed | Single chain only, or most arrows reversed | No web |
| Carbon journey (4) | ≥4 stops, processes named, path highlighted, atom conserved in words | 3 stops or one process unnamed | Journey given but processes missing or atom "created" | No journey |
| Walkthrough (4) | (a)–(e) all present; removal prediction justified by arrows; model card honest | One part thin or unjustified | Two or more parts missing | Walkthrough absent |

**Scoring guidance:** 14–16 = ready to move on; 10–13 = revise one
criterion with the teacher-guide prompts and resubmit that part;
below 10 = reteach with Lessons 04–05, then rebuild one section
(usually the web or the journey) rather than the whole model.

## What "done" looks like

A meadow poster: grass and clover labeled producers with the
photosynthesis equation; rabbit, mouse, fox, hawk as consumers;
fungi and earthworms as decomposers; ten arrows; a highlighted
carbon path air → clover → rabbit → fox → fungi → air; a frame
labeled "IN: sunlight; OUT: heat; CYCLES INSIDE: carbon, water,
oxygen"; and a one-page walkthrough ending with a model card that
admits the poster cannot show how fast anything happens.
