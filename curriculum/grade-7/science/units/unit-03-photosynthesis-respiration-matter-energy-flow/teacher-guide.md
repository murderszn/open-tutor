# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Photosynthesis, Respiration, and Matter–Energy Flow

**Adult only.** This guide supports the lessons; worked solutions
are in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md). Pacing is Sessions 1–16 in
the unit [README](README.md).

## Lesson-by-lesson guidance

### Lesson 01 — The photosynthesis equation

- **Watch for:** big numbers vs. subscripts (Worked example 1
  re-teaches this); light written as a product; "soil" listed as a
  reactant. Correct by counting atoms aloud together, not by
  memorizing the equation.
- **If the equation-builder stalls:** start from the word cards
  only, then flip one card at a time to symbols. Do not let a
  learner copy the symbol equation without counting at least one
  element.
- **Reteach prompt:** "Show me where the 18 oxygen atoms are on
  each side. If you cannot find them, the equation is not finished."

### Lesson 02 — Where does a plant's mass come from?

- **Watch for:** replacing "soil" with "water" as the mass source
  (Worked example 1 blocks this); height confused with health in
  the seedlings activity; seeds treated as "growing from nothing."
- **The foil-leaf observation (Session 4):** cover part of one leaf
  with foil, leave the plant in light, and log dated observations
  for a week. Expect the covered patch to look paler/yellower than
  the uncovered part (less chlorophyll maintained without light).
  If no plant is available, use the Lesson 02 text alternative and
  practice P21 — same objectives, no penalty. Never present the
  illustration as experimental data.
- **Reteach prompt:** "If the mass came from the soil, what should
  the soil have lost? What did it actually lose?"

### Lesson 03 — Cellular respiration

- **Watch for:** respiration = breathing (G2); "plants don't
  respire" (G3, Worked example 2); energy "created" by respiration
  (re-teach conservation: energy is released from sugar; atoms
  are conserved).
- **Sealed-jar discussion (G3):** in sunlight photosynthesis
  usually outruns respiration, so oxygen rises — but respiration
  never stops. Accept "rises, because photosynthesis is faster in
  light" as proficient; "stays the same" is the common error.
- **Reteach prompt:** "Write both equations. Which one makes sugar?
  Which one spends it? Now tell me what a plant does at midnight."

### Lesson 04 — Matter cycles, energy flows

- **Watch for:** arrows pointing eater → food (re-teach "arrow
  follows the food"); energy drawn as a circle (G3); decomposers
  described as "destroying" matter (they return it, in new forms).
- **10% rule:** enrichment only. If a learner treats it as exact,
  ask: "Would a sick rabbit pass on the same fraction as a healthy
  one?" The rule explains *why* top levels are small, not exact
  numbers.
- **Reteach prompt:** "Trace one carbon atom. If your atom ever
  disappears, your model lost matter — find where it went."

### Lesson 05 — One system, two flows

- **Watch for:** CER paragraphs that list facts without reasoning
  (Worked example 1's annotation is the fix); two-headed energy
  arrows; "every ecosystem" without the hedge (I3).
### Session 14 CER revision checklist

Use this checklist for the Lesson 05 draft and again before
assessment A11:

  1. Claim in one sentence — does it answer the essential
     question?
  2. At least two evidences, each named (equation, van Helmont
     numbers, seedlings, food-web base) — not vague ("science
     says")?
  3. Reasoning sentences that *connect* evidence to claim — not a
     repeat of the evidence?
  4. Honest hedge ("almost every") or a named exception
     (chemosynthetic ecosystems)?
  5. Energy direction correct (in as light, out as heat) and
     matter direction correct (cycles, never created/destroyed)?

## Formative quiz (Session 15) — reteach map

- Q1–Q2 weak → re-do Lesson 01 card build + atom counts.
- Q3–Q4 weak → re-do Lesson 02 van Helmont numbers + three fates.
- Q5–Q6 weak → re-do Lesson 03 equations side by side.
- Q7–Q9 weak → re-do Lesson 04 web arrows + one-way energy.
- Q10 weak → use the Session 14 checklist on the draft before
  the assessment.

## Unit assessment (Session 16) — scoring notes

- A1–A2: equations must balance; accept light energy written above
  the arrow or as a reactant.
- A3–A5: reward reasoning that cites numbers or equations, not
  bare conclusions. A5's two changes: paler/weaker growth (no new
  sugar) and rising CO₂ (respiration continues).
- A7–A8: arrows must point food → eater; accept any ecologically
  sensible web with the required counts.
- A9: 500 → ~50 → ~5; accept "about 50, about 5" with the roughness
  reason (health, waste, heat losses vary).
- A11: score with the Session 14 checklist; a paragraph without
  reasoning scores at most half, even if its facts are right.
- A12: a model card that names a genuine limit (not "it is small")
  is proficient.

## Project scoring

Use the rubric in [project.md](project.md#rubric-adult-scored-16-points).
Common fixable gaps: night respiration missing from the producer
(ask "what does your plant do at midnight?"); decomposer omitted
(ask "where does the dead fox go?"); removal prediction without
an arrow justification (ask "show me the arrow"). Resubmit of one
criterion is enough for a 10–13 score; do not require a full
rebuild.

## Accessibility and safety notes

- All image activities have text alternatives in the lessons.
  Learners may answer orally or by dictation with adult scribing;
  equation work may be done with cards instead of writing.
- The foil-leaf observation uses no chemicals, flames, or sharp
  tools beyond adult-handled scissors. Learners with no plant
  access use the paper alternative with no penalty.
- No live animals, no collected specimens, no iodine or chemical
  tests are used in this unit.
