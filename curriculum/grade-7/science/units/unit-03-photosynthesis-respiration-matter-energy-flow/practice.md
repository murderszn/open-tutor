# Practice Bank — Unit 03: Photosynthesis, Respiration, and Matter–Energy Flow

24 items across the unit's practice sessions. Keys in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md#practice-bank). Items marked ★ are
revised from the legacy `grade-7/stem/assignments/photosynthesis.md`
equation/explanation tasks, rewritten with new contexts and full
keys; the legacy food-web tasks are revised into Lesson 04 and the
project (full keystone/10%-rule treatment banked for Unit 06).

## A. The photosynthesis equation (Sessions 2, 12, 14)

**P1. ★** Write the photosynthesis equation in words, then in
symbols. Label reactants and products, and state where the energy
in the sugar came from.

**P2.** Count atoms: show that 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ is
balanced for carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen. Show each count.

**P3.** A learner writes the symbol equation without any big
numbers: CO₂ + H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂. Explain, with counts, why
this version cannot be right — and give the corrected equation.

**P4.** Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light and reflects green
light. A plant is grown under green-only light. Predict what
happens to photosynthesis and explain why, in two sentences.

**P5.** A sealed jar contains a plant, soil, water, and air, and
sits in sunlight. After a week the plant has grown and the jar's
oxygen level has risen. Explain both observations with the
equation. Then predict what happens if the jar is moved to a dark
room for a week (use Lesson 03 as well).

**P6. ★** In 5–7 sentences, explain the role of chlorophyll, where
photosynthesis happens (chloroplasts), and how photosynthesis
links to cellular respiration. (This is the legacy assignment's
explanation task, keyed in the answer key.)

## B. Cellular respiration (Sessions 6, 12, 14)

**P7.** Write the cellular respiration equation in words and in
symbols. Label reactants and products, and state where the
released energy was stored before respiration.

**P8.** A student says, "Only animals respire, because only animals
move and breathe." Give two counterexamples from plants and explain
what each one proves.

**P9.** Compare the two equations in a two-column table: for each,
list inputs, outputs, energy direction (stored or released), and
the organelle most associated with it.

**P10.** A runner's muscles respire faster during a sprint.
Explain three observable consequences (breathing, warmth, and one
more of your choice) using the equation.

**P11.** A potted plant loses a small amount of dry mass overnight
in a dark room. A learner says, "Nothing left the plant, so this
is impossible." Explain what left, in what form, and by which
process.

**P12.** Bread dough rises because yeast cells release carbon
dioxide. A baker forgets the yeast. Predict the result and explain
it with the respiration equation. (What reactant is missing from
the dough's point of view? None — think about who the "who" is.)

## C. Tracing matter and energy (Sessions 9, 12, 14)

**P13.** Trace one carbon atom: air → grass → rabbit → fox →
decomposer → air. Name the process that moves it at each step.

**P14.** A meadow's grass fixes 1,000 units of energy from sunlight
in a year. Using the approximate 10% enrichment rule, estimate the
new growth energy available to rabbits, and then to foxes. Show
the arithmetic, and state why the rule is approximate, not exact.

**P15.** Draw a food web for a pond (algae, minnow, heron, turtle,
snail, decomposers) with at least eight arrows. Check: do all
arrows point from food toward eater? Does every organism have at
least one arrow in or out (except producers, which need only
arrows out)?

**P16.** In your pond web, remove the algae. Predict two
consequences (one immediate, one delayed) and justify each with
an arrow chain from the web.

**P17.** Define the system boundary for a garden ecosystem in three
lists: crosses in, crosses out, cycles inside. Then answer: is
rainfall "in" or "cycles inside"? Defend your choice.

**P18.** A learner draws a food web with energy arrows forming a
circle. Explain the error, and redraw the key idea in words: what
shape does energy's path really have, and what shape does matter's
path have?

## D. Mixed review and reasoning (Sessions 12, 14)

**P19.** Error analysis: a learner's paragraph says, "Plants make
food from soil, and animals get energy from the sun directly."
Identify the two errors and rewrite the paragraph correctly in
three sentences.

**P20.** Model judgment: a poster shows only the photosynthesis
equation and the caption "How nature works." Name two things this
model explains well and two things it cannot explain, and state
which unit question it leaves unanswered.

**P21.** Fictional practice dataset (not observations you made):
two bean seedlings grown for two weeks — Seedling A (sunny
windowsill): height 14 cm, 9 broad green leaves, stem sturdy and
upright. Seedling B (dark cupboard, watered equally): height 21 cm,
4 small pale-yellow leaves, stem thin and bending. (a) Which
seedling photosynthesized more? Cite two numbers. (b) Why is B
taller yet weaker? (c) What does this dataset *not* prove, and what
would you change to make it a real experiment?

**P22.** A sealed terrarium with plants and soil is kept in light
for a month and thrives. A second, identical terrarium is kept in
the dark and its plants yellow and die. Explain the difference
using both equations, and state which terrarium's air ends the
month with more carbon dioxide — and why.

**P23.** CER outline: write a claim-evidence-reasoning outline (not
the full paragraph) for the statement "Photosynthesis is the
entry point for matter and energy into a meadow ecosystem." Give
the claim, two evidences with sources (equation; van Helmont), and
one reasoning sentence.

**P24.** Human impact: a town drains a wetland to build houses.
Name one producer, one consumer, and one decomposer affected, and
for each, state whether its matter supply, its energy supply, or
both are disrupted — with one reason each. Then propose one
response the town could take, and state what it would restore.
