# Answer Key — Unit 03: Photosynthesis, Respiration, and Matter–Energy Flow

**Adult only.** Every lesson item, practice item, quiz question,
assessment task, and project criterion is solved below; open
responses give a sample proficient response plus acceptable
variation. Reconciled against the lessons 2026-10-25: each key was
solved independently, then checked against the lesson's teaching.

## Lesson 01

**Guided practice**
1. Word equation: carbon dioxide + water + light energy → sugar
   (glucose) + oxygen. Symbol equation: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy
   → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. Reactants = left side (CO₂, H₂O, light energy);
   products = right side (sugar, oxygen). Light energy is the
   circled input.
2. 6O₂ holds 6 × 2 = 12 oxygen atoms. Product side: glucose holds 6
   oxygen atoms, plus 12 from 6O₂ = 18 total. (Matches the reactant
   side: 12 from 6CO₂ + 6 from 6H₂O = 18.)
3. No. The plant will use up the jar's carbon dioxide, and a sealed
   jar supplies no more. Photosynthesis slows and stops when CO₂
   runs out, even with water and light still available. (Accept:
   "stops once the CO₂ is used up" — the jar's air is finite.)

**Independent practice**
1. Mistake 1: soil is not a reactant — the reactants are carbon
   dioxide (from air) and water. Mistake 2: sunlight is not a
   material the plant builds from — it is the energy input.
   Corrected sentence: "Plants make food from carbon dioxide and
   water, using light energy."
2. A leaf looks green because chlorophyll absorbs red and blue
   light and reflects green light, and reflected light is what
   reaches our eyes. This does not waste the plant's favorite
   light: the plant's useful colors are the absorbed ones (red and
   blue); green is the color it uses least, so reflecting it costs
   little.
3. Sugar: stays in the plant — used now, stored, or built into new
   molecules. Oxygen: released through the leaf's openings into
   the air.

**Application:** Two glucose molecules need 12 CO₂ molecules:
double the equation (12CO₂ + 12H₂O → 2C₆H₁₂O₆ + 12O₂).

**Exit check:** 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂,
reactants left, products right. "Photosynthesis stores light energy
as chemical energy in sugar." Pigment: chlorophyll. Organelle:
chloroplast.

## Lesson 02

**Guided practice**
1. Ruled out: "the tree's mass came from the soil" — the soil lost
   only ~2 ounces. Left standing: "the mass came from elsewhere —
   water and, as the equation shows, carbon dioxide from the air."
2. Three fates with examples (accept any correct set): used now —
   energy for root growth; stored — starch in a potato tuber; built
   into new molecules — cellulose in a tree trunk's wood.
3. The error is equating height with growth. The dark seedling
   stretches tall and pale (reaching for light it cannot find) while
   making almost no sugar, because without light no light energy
   enters the equation and little chlorophyll is maintained. It is
   taller but weaker — spending its seed's stored food, not
   building new mass from photosynthesis.

**Independent practice**
1. No. Fertilizer supplies small amounts of minerals (such as
   nitrogen) that help the plant use its sugar, but minerals are
   not the bulk of plant mass. Van Helmont's numbers stand: the
   soil lost ~2 ounces while the tree gained ~164 pounds, so the
   bulk mass came from air (CO₂) and water, not from anything
   added to the soil.
2. Sample CER: "Most of a tree's wood comes from the air. Van
   Helmont's willow gained about 164 pounds in five years while
   its soil lost only about 2 ounces. Since the photosynthesis
   equation's only carbon input is carbon dioxide from the air,
   and the soil cannot supply 164 pounds it never lost, the wood's
   carbon must have come from the air." (Accept any paragraph with
   claim + the numbers + equation reasoning.)
3. From the seed's own stored food: the seed contains starch, and
   the sprouting plant respires that starch to build its first
   shoot before its leaves can photosynthesize.

**Application:** Foil-leaf prediction: in a week the foil-covered
patch looks paler/yellower than the uncovered part, because no
light reaches it, so no photosynthesis happens there and
chlorophyll is not maintained. Paper alternative (P21): the sunny
seedling stays green and sturdy; the dark one stays pale and
spindly.

**Exit check:** "Most of a plant's dry mass comes from carbon
dioxide in the air, not from soil." "A plant stores extra sugar as
starch, and builds cell walls out of cellulose." Fates: used now,
stored, built into new molecules.

## Lesson 03

**Guided practice**
1. Photosynthesis arrow: sugar + oxygen flow toward respiration.
   Respiration arrow: carbon dioxide + water (+ released energy)
   flow back toward photosynthesis. The two equations share their
   substances in opposite directions.
2. Breathing is the body moving gases in and out (oxygen in,
   carbon dioxide out) through lungs or body surfaces. Cellular
   respiration is the chemical rearrangement inside cells that
   breaks food apart and releases energy. Different places,
   different jobs.
3. The oxygen level rises (in sunlight, photosynthesis usually
   outruns respiration, so oxygen is produced faster than it is
   used). Respiration does not stop — it continues all day — but
   it is outpaced in light.

**Independent practice**
1. The rising dough proves the yeast cells are alive and respiring
   (releasing gas). The gas is carbon dioxide.
2. Both cells release energy: the muscle cell respires glucose for
   movement, and the leaf cell respires glucose for its own living
   costs. The leaf cell surprises people because they think plants
   only photosynthesize — but plants respire day and night, and
   photosynthesize only in light.
3. C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy. Counts: carbon 6 = 6;
   hydrogen 12 = 12; oxygen 18 (6 + 12) = 18 (6 + 12). Balanced.

**Application:** The claim is false. Respiration is limited by
the food available, by the cell's actual energy needs, and by the
cell's machinery — not by oxygen alone. Extra oxygen the cells
cannot use is simply breathed back out. More oxygen does not create
more sugar to burn.

**Exit check:** "Photosynthesis stores energy into sugar;
respiration releases energy out of sugar." Organelle: mitochondria.
"Plants respire only at night" is FALSE — plants respire day and
night; they photosynthesize only in light.

## Lesson 04

**Guided practice**
1. Accept any web with the five organisms plus decomposers and at
   least six food→eater arrows, e.g.: grass → rabbit, grass →
   mouse, rabbit → fox, mouse → fox, rabbit → hawk, mouse → hawk,
   all → decomposers (fungi, bacteria).
2. Carbon journey: air (CO₂) → grass by photosynthesis (fixed into
   sugar/cellulose) → rabbit by eating → decomposer by eating dead
   matter/decomposition → air by respiration/decomposition
   releasing CO₂. Same atom throughout.
3. The error: energy cannot circle back. The fox's waste heat and
   the energy spent on movement never become sunlight again, so no
   arrow can return energy to the grass. Correction: "Energy flows
   one way — sunlight → grass → rabbit → fox → heat out — while
   matter cycles."

**Independent practice**
1. Decomposers recycle matter: they break dead organisms and wastes
   into carbon dioxide, water, and minerals that producers reuse.
   They cannot recycle energy: the energy in dead matter leaves as
   heat during decomposition and respiration and cannot be
   re-collected as light or new food.
2. Accept any two justified consequences, e.g.: consumer — minnows
   or other algae-eaters decline (their food is gone); decomposer —
   decomposers first increase (more dead algae to break down), then
   decline as the dead matter runs out. Each must cite an arrow
   (algae → consumer; dead algae → decomposer).
3. Boundary lists (accept defended variation): IN: sunlight
   (energy). OUT: heat. CYCLES INSIDE: carbon, oxygen, water,
   minerals. Rainfall is best placed as "crosses in, then cycles":
   rain enters from outside the garden system and its water then
   cycles inside.

**Application:** Short term: rabbits increase (fewer foxes eating
them). Longer term: grass decreases (more rabbits grazing it).
Backfire (accept any sensible one): overgrazed grass collapses,
then rabbits crash from hunger and disease; or rabbits damage crops
and gardens more than the foxes ever did.

**Exit check:** "Matter cycles." "Energy flows one way." Labels:
oak tree — producer; deer — consumer; mushroom — decomposer; wolf
— consumer; algae — producer.

## Lesson 05

**Guided practice**
1. Strengths (any three): shows both flows in one picture; shows
   the system boundary (sunlight in, heat out); links the two
   equations; makes the cycle visible. Limits (any three): hides
   where processes happen (chloroplasts vs. mitochondria); leaves
   out decomposers; makes flows look instant and perfectly
   balanced; shows no time scale or rates; hides that most energy
   leaves as heat at every step.
2. It explains the energy story well (sunlight in, heat out) but
   cannot explain the matter story at all: without carbon dioxide
   arrows it cannot show where a body comes from or where it goes.
3. Changes: (1) make all energy arrows one-headed (plant → animal
   → heat out, never back); (2) add a decomposer box that returns
   carbon dioxide and minerals from dead matter to the plant.

**Independent practice**
1. Sample CER: "Photosynthesis is the process that brings new
   matter and new energy into nearly every ecosystem. The equation
   shows plants turning carbon dioxide, water, and light into sugar
   and oxygen, and van Helmont's tree gained 164 pounds while its
   soil lost only 2 ounces. Because the equation's only carbon
   input is air, the tree's mass came from the air — and because
   animals eat plants or eat plant-eaters, that plant-made sugar is
   the starting point of nearly every food web. Matter then cycles
   while energy flows one way out as heat." (Score with the
   Session 14 checklist: claim + 2 evidences + reasoning + hedge.)
2. The plants survive — for a time, and in principle indefinitely
   at small scale. Sunlight crosses the boundary; inside, plants
   photosynthesize by day and respire by night, and decomposers
   return carbon dioxide and minerals from dead leaves, so matter
   cycles without animals. (Accept "survive, but more slowly /
   limited by decomposition" — animals are not required for the
   cycle.)
3. Deep-sea hydrothermal vents: their ecosystems run on chemical
   energy from Earth's interior (chemosynthesis), not sunlight.
   Hence "almost every," not "every."

**Application:** Sample model card (cycle diagram): shows both
flows and the boundary clearly; hides organelle locations and
decomposers; cannot answer "how fast does carbon move?" (no rates
or time scale).

**Exit check:** Both equations as in Lessons 01 and 03. Boundary:
in — sunlight; out — heat. "Photosynthesis brings new matter and
new energy into an ecosystem; respiration spends the stored energy
and returns the matter (carbon dioxide and water)."

## Practice bank

**P1.** Words: carbon dioxide + water + light energy → sugar
(glucose) + oxygen. Symbols: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆
+ 6O₂. Reactants left, products right. The sugar's energy came from
light energy captured by chlorophyll.
**P2.** Carbon: left 6 × 1 = 6, right 6 (in C₆H₁₂O₆). Hydrogen: left
6 × 2 = 12, right 12. Oxygen: left 6 × 2 + 6 × 1 = 18, right 6 + 6 × 2
= 18. Balanced.
**P3.** Unbalanced: carbon 1 vs. 6, hydrogen 2 vs. 12, oxygen 3 vs.
8. Corrected: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂.
**P4.** Photosynthesis drops sharply: green light is mostly
reflected, not absorbed, so little light energy is captured.
Chlorophyll's useful colors are red and blue.
**P5.** Growth: the plant fixed CO₂ and water into sugar and new
molecules. Rising oxygen: photosynthesis releases O₂ as a
by-product. In a dark room for a week: photosynthesis stops,
respiration continues, so oxygen falls and carbon dioxide rises
while the plant slowly loses mass.
**P6.** Sample 5–7 sentences: "Chlorophyll is the green pigment
that captures light energy. It sits inside chloroplasts, mostly in
leaf cells. With that energy, the plant builds sugar from carbon
dioxide and water. The sugar stores the light energy as chemical
energy. Later, the plant (or an animal that eats it) breaks the
sugar apart in cellular respiration, releasing that energy for
work. So photosynthesis fills the pantry that respiration spends."
(Accept any 5–7 sentences covering chlorophyll, chloroplasts, and
the respiration link.)
**P7.** Words: food (sugar) + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water +
released energy. Symbols: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy.
The energy was stored in the sugar by photosynthesis.
**P8.** Counterexamples (any two): a potted plant grows and stays
warm in the dark (it respires stored starch); yeast dough rises
(yeast cells respire and release CO₂); a seed sprouts underground
(it respires seed starch before any leaves exist). Each proves
respiration happens without movement or breathing.
**P9.** Table: photosynthesis — inputs CO₂, water, light; outputs
sugar, oxygen; energy stored; chloroplast. Respiration — inputs
sugar, oxygen; outputs CO₂, water, energy; energy released;
mitochondrion.
**P10.** Faster breathing (more oxygen in, more CO₂ out); feeling
warm (released energy becomes heat); third (accept any): sweating,
faster heartbeat, or thirst — each tied to faster energy release.
**P11.** Carbon dioxide and water left as gases: the plant respired
its stored sugar all night, releasing CO₂ (and water) to the air.
The mass did not vanish — it left as gas.
**P12.** Without yeast, no yeast cells respire in the dough, so no
CO₂ is released and the dough does not rise. (The "missing
reactant" framing is a trap: the dough has sugar, but nobody is
there to respire it.)
**P13.** Air CO₂ → grass by photosynthesis → rabbit by eating →
fox by eating → decomposer by eating dead matter → air by
respiration/decomposition releasing CO₂.
**P14.** Rabbits: ~100 units (10% of 1,000). Foxes: ~10 units (10%
of 100). Approximate because transfer varies: not all grass is
eaten, not all eaten food becomes growth, health and activity
differ, and much energy leaves as heat at every step.
**P15.** Accept any pond web with the six organisms, ≥8 food→eater
arrows, and every organism connected (e.g., algae → snail, algae →
minnow, snail → turtle, minnow → heron, minnow → turtle, snail →
heron, all dead matter → decomposers, decomposers → water/soil
nutrients).
**P16.** Immediate: minnows and snails lose food and decline.
Delayed: herons and turtles decline (their prey declined), while
decomposers first spike on dead algae, then fall. Each consequence
must cite an arrow chain.
**P17.** IN: sunlight (and rainfall, which then cycles). OUT: heat.
CYCLES INSIDE: carbon, water, oxygen, minerals. Rainfall defense:
rain crosses into the garden from outside, then its water cycles
inside (soil → plant → air → rain again) — so "in, then cycles" is
the honest placement.
**P18.** Error: energy cannot circle back to the grass — heat lost
at each step never becomes sunlight again. Energy's path is a
one-way line (sun → producers → consumers → heat out); matter's
path is a circle (air → plant → animal → decomposer → air).
**P19.** Errors: (1) plants do not make food from soil — from CO₂,
water, and light; (2) animals do not get energy directly from the
sun — from food. Rewrite (sample): "Plants make food from carbon
dioxide and water using light energy. Animals get energy by eating
plants or other animals. Decomposers return dead matter to the
soil, water, and air for reuse."
**P20.** Explains well: the photosynthesis equation; where plant
food and oxygen come from. Cannot explain: respiration; food webs
or energy loss; decomposers or matter cycling. Unanswered unit
question: how do animals get energy, and where does energy go?
**P21.** (a) Seedling A photosynthesized more: 9 broad green leaves
vs. 4 small pale ones, sturdy upright stem vs. thin bending stem.
(b) B is taller because it stretched toward light it could not
find (etiolation), spending seed stores without making sugar — tall
but thin, pale, and weak. (c) It does not prove a general rule: two
plants only, no replication, no dry-mass measurements, and
conditions (pot size, soil, seed size) are not shown controlled. A
real experiment would replicate many seedlings, control water/soil/
seed size, measure dry mass, and repeat.
**P22.** Light terrarium: photosynthesis outruns respiration, so
oxygen rises, carbon dioxide cycles, plants grow. Dark terrarium:
no photosynthesis, respiration only — plants spend starch, release
CO₂, yellow, and die. The dark terrarium ends with more CO₂,
because nothing re-fixes it into sugar.
**P23.** Outline: claim — "Photosynthesis is the entry point for
matter and energy into a meadow ecosystem." Evidence 1 — the
equation (CO₂ + water + light → sugar + O₂). Evidence 2 — van
Helmont's numbers (tree +164 lb, soil −2 oz). Reasoning — because
the equation's only carbon input is air and every food chain
starts with a producer, all of the web's matter and energy traces
back to photosynthesis.
**P24.** Accept any ecologically sensible set, e.g.: producer —
reeds/cattails: matter and water supply disrupted (habitat drained).
Consumer — frogs/herons: energy supply disrupted (food web
collapses). Decomposer — wetland fungi/bacteria: matter cycling
disrupted (no wet dead matter to break down). Response — restore
part of the wetland or build a retention pond: restores water,
habitat, and the matter cycle. (Score the reasoning, not the exact
species.)

## Week-14 quiz

1. 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂; reactants left,
   products right.
2. Carbon: 6 × 1 = 6. Oxygen: 6 × 2 = 12.
3. From carbon dioxide in the air (plus water); the plant fixed air
   CO₂ into sugar and then into wood and leaves.
4. Used now (energy for growth/repair); stored (starch in roots,
   seeds, tubers); built into new molecules (cellulose for cell
   walls, proteins for new cells).
5. Words: food (sugar) + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water +
   released energy. Organelle: mitochondria.
6. FALSE. Plants respire day and night; they photosynthesize only
   in light.
7. Grass → rabbit → fox (arrows food → eater). Matter is the one
   that cycles (atoms conserved, reused); energy flows one way
   (leaves as heat, never recovered).
8. Short term: rabbits increase (fewer foxes eating them). Longer
   term: grass decreases (more rabbits grazing). Each justified by
   the rabbit–fox and rabbit–grass arrows.
9. Error: energy cannot circle back — the fox's spent energy leaves
   as heat and never becomes sunlight again. Correction: "Energy
   flows one way from the sun through the web and out as heat."
10. Sample CER (claim underlined, evidence circled, reasoning
    bracketed by the learner): "Photosynthesis is the entry point
    for energy into a meadow. The equation shows plants turning
    carbon dioxide, water, and light into sugar and oxygen. Because
    every food chain starts with a producer, all of the meadow's
    energy traces back to that sugar." (Score: claim + evidence +
    reasoning present = full marks.)

## Unit assessment

**A1.** Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ +
6O₂. Respiration: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy. Reactants
left, products right, both balanced.
**A2.** Oxygen, reactant side: 12 (from 6CO₂) + 6 (from 6H₂O) = 18.
Product side: 6 (in glucose) + 12 (from 6O₂) = 18. Equal — balanced.
**A3.** Sample: "The numbers rule out soil as the mass source —
the soil lost only 2 ounces. They leave open water as a
contributor, since van Helmont watered the tree. The equation adds
the missing piece: the only carbon input is CO₂ from the air, so
the tree's carbon mass came from the air, not from water or soil."
**A4.** In spring the plant breaks stored starch back into sugar
(fate 2 spent): some sugar is respired now for energy (fate 1) and
some is built into new shoot cells (fate 3). New shoots can grow
before new leaves photosynthesize much because the pantry was
filled last summer.
**A5.** Changes (any two, each with an equation): the plant grows
paler/weaker and loses mass (no photosynthesis, so no new sugar;
respiration spends stored sugar); the air around it gains CO₂ (and
water) because respiration continues without photosynthesis to
re-fix the CO₂.
**A6.** Breathing moves oxygen in and carbon dioxide out through
the lungs; cellular respiration is the chemical rearrangement
inside cells that releases energy from food. A resting person still
respires because living cells always need energy (heartbeat,
warmth, repair, brain activity).
**A7.** Accept any garden web with the six organisms, ≥8 food→eater
arrows, and correct role labels (lettuce producer; caterpillar,
snail, bird, thrush consumers; fungi decomposer).
**A8.** Accept any four-stop atom journey with processes, e.g.:
air CO₂ → lettuce by photosynthesis → caterpillar by eating → bird
by eating → fungi by decomposition → air by respiration releasing
CO₂.
**A9.** Caterpillars: ~50 units (10% of 500). Birds: ~5 units (10%
of 50). Rough because transfer varies with health, waste, activity,
and how much is actually eaten and converted to growth.
**A10.** Immediate: caterpillars/snails increase (fewer thrushes
eating them). Delayed: lettuce decreases (more grazers), and birds
that compete with thrushes may increase — each justified by a web
arrow.
**A11.** Score with the Session 14 checklist (claim; ≥2 evidences;
reasoning; "almost every" hedge; energy/matter directions correct).
A paragraph without reasoning scores at most half, even if its
facts are right. Sample proficient paragraph: see Lesson 05 I1 key.
**A12.** Score the honesty, not the choice: one genuine strength,
two genuine limits, one unanswerable question (e.g., cycle diagram
— strength: shows both flows and the boundary; limits: hides
organelle locations, omits decomposers; cannot answer: how fast
does carbon move?).
