# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Reproduction, Inheritance, and Genetic Variation

**Adult only.** This guide supports the lessons; worked solutions
are in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md). Pacing is Sessions 1–16 in
the unit [README](README.md).

## Lesson-by-lesson guidance

### Lesson 01 — Two ways to make offspring

- **Watch for:** "asexual = no parent" (correct: exactly one
  parent); "variation = looking different because of the
  environment" (correct that distinction is Lesson 05; variation
  here means differences in inherited information); "sexual is
  better because it makes more offspring" (correct: sexual gives
  variation, not necessarily more offspring; asexual is often
  faster).
- **The illustration:** accept either panel order as long as the
  reasoning names parent count and information source. The runners
  are drawn above the soil line for clarity — do not let learners
  read rootedness or plantlet counts off the image as data.
- **Reteach prompt:** "How many parents put in genetic information
  here — one, or two? That answer alone sorts the case."

### Lesson 02 — Mitosis and meiosis

- **Watch for:** the two swaps — "mitosis halves" and "meiosis
  copies." The fix is physical: make the learner build both strip
  models rather than re-reading the definitions. Also watch for
  "meiosis loses information" (correct: each gamete carries one
  complete set, one from each pair).
- **Numbers that must be automatic by Session 6:** pea mitosis
  14 → 14, 14 (two cells, identical); pea meiosis 14 → 7, 7, 7, 7
  (four cells, varied); fertilization 7 + 7 = 14.
- **Reteach prompt:** "Show me with the strips. Did you copy first
  (mitosis) or split the pairs without copying (meiosis)?"

### Lesson 03 — Genes, proteins, and mutations

- **Watch for:** "every mutation is harmful"; "mutations happen on
  purpose / because the organism needs them"; "gene = trait"
  (skipping the protein step). Every judgment must name the
  environment, or it is incomplete — return it for revision.
- **Boundary:** do not teach protein-synthesis mechanisms, specific
  mutation types, or molecular detail. The chain stays conceptual:
  gene change → possibly different protein → possibly different
  trait → judged in an environment.
- **Reteach prompt:** "Trace the chain out loud: what changed in
  the gene, what changed in the protein, what changed in the trait
  — and in which environment are you judging it?"

### Lesson 04 — Punnett squares and probability

- **Watch for:** "dominant = more common" (dominance is expression
  in a heterozygote, not frequency); "the square predicts this one
  seed" (it predicts group proportions); "Rr is wrinkled" (Rr is
  round — one dominant allele is enough); forgetting that RR × rr
  gives all Rr, all round.
- **Coin simulation:** 40 pairs will rarely land exactly on
  30 round / 10 wrinkled. Treat a result like 27/13 or 33/7 as a
  successful demonstration of chance variation, not an error.
- **Reteach prompt:** "Read each box aloud: which allele came from
  each parent? Now count the boxes — the ratio is a count, not a
  feeling."

### Lesson 05 — Genes, environment, and reproductive success

- **Watch for:** the Lamarckian error ("acquired traits are
  inherited"); "taller = healthier" (the low-light plants are the
  counterexample: taller, paler, thinner); behavior/structure
  explanations that never state the mechanism ("it helps
  reproduction" without protection, attraction, transport, or a
  better germination site).
- **The investigation:** check the log at Session 12, not only at
  Session 14 — a missing week of measurements cannot be
  reconstructed honestly. Learners who missed measurements use the
  labeled practice dataset and say so.
- **Reteach prompt:** "Passed in genes, or gained in life? And for
  the behavior: how, exactly, does it change the odds?"

## Session 14 CER revision checklist

Before the quiz, check the learner's closing CER paragraph (Lesson
05 / project Part 3) against: claim names both factors; evidence
cites at least two group means with numbers; reasoning explains
that the variety pattern repeats across lights and the light
pattern repeats across varieties; health judgment uses color and
sturdiness, not height alone. Any missing element is a same-day
revision, not a deduction carried into the assessment.

## Formative quiz (Session 15) — reteach map

- Q1 wrong → Lesson 01, illustration comparison.
- Q2 wrong → Lesson 02, rebuild both strip models.
- Q3–Q4 wrong → Lesson 03, chain tracing with a new modeled
  mutation.
- Q5–Q7 wrong → Lesson 04, rebuild Rr × Rr and Rr × rr with cards.
- Q8 wrong → Lesson 05 sorting test ("passed in genes, or gained
  in life?").
- Q9 wrong → Lesson 05 Worked example 1; recompute the four means.
- Q10 wrong → Lesson 05 mechanism rule: name the protection or
  attraction, not just "it helps."

## Unit assessment (Session 16) — scoring notes

Part A is 2 points per item; give 1 point when the process is
right but a number or a reason is missing, 0 when the process is
wrong. Part B1 means are 12.0, 16.0, 9.0, and 13.0 cm; variety
effect: C beats D by 3.0 cm in both conditions; light effect:
+4.0 cm in shade for both varieties. Accept any clearly stated
equivalent (for example, "shade plants are 4 cm taller in both
varieties"). Part B2 needs all four elements (claim, evidence,
reasoning, labels) for full points; a paragraph that claims only
one factor cannot score above 3/6.

## Project scoring

Use the rubric in [project.md](project.md) (20 points). The
walkthrough is the most informative part: a learner who can explain
all three parts in their own words has understood the unit even if
one mean is miscalculated. Record the portfolio score separately
from the assessment score; do not average them into a single grade
without telling the learner which is which.

## Accessibility and safety notes

- All lessons offer an oral, pointing, drawing, or model-building
  response mode; the illustration and the Punnett diagram both
  carry text alternatives, so no objective depends on seeing an
  image.
- Investigation safety: adult supervision throughout; no tasting
  of seeds or plants; hand-washing after soil handling; allergy
  check before starting; no unattended lamps; no total-darkness
  growing for the full two weeks. The practice dataset is an equal
  alternative, never a penalty.
- Sensitivity: learners are never asked about their own or their
  family's traits, and human examples stay at the general level
  described in the unit README.
