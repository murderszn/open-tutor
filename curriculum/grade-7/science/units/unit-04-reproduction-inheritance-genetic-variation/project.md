# Project — Variation Portfolio

**Type:** investigation + modeling portfolio · **Sessions:** 11
(launch), 12 (data work), 13 (workshop), 14 (final measurements)
· **Adult-supervised throughout.**

## Question

How does variation arise, how can it be predicted, and how do genes
and environment together shape what actually grows?

## What you will deliver

A portfolio with three parts, plus a walkthrough (spoken or written,
learner's choice):

1. **Reproduction model diagram** — one species (plant or animal,
   not a person, and never yourself or your family) shown two ways:
   how it reproduces asexually (if it can) or, if it cannot, how a
   species that can does so — and how it reproduces sexually. Label
   the number of parents and whether offspring information is
   identical or varied in each case.
2. **Prediction page** — a Punnett square for a single-gene trait
   in your species (or in peas, if your species has no simple
   single-gene trait you can model honestly), the genotype and
   phenotype ratios, and the results of a 40-pair coin-toss
   simulation compared with the square's expectation.
3. **Growth investigation report** — the two-variety,
   two-condition investigation (or the practice dataset below),
   with a data table, group means, a variety-effect sentence, a
   light-effect sentence, a health judgment that uses color and
   sturdiness as well as height, and a CER paragraph answering
   whether genes, environment, or both influence growth.

## Investigation steps (adult-led)

1. Choose two seed varieties (A and B) and plant five seeds of each
   in full (bright indirect) light and five of each in low light:
   four groups of five, 20 plants total. Label every pot.
2. Water all groups the same amount; keep everything else the same
   except variety and light. Record the setup: variety names,
   planting date, light descriptions, watering routine.
3. Measure height in centimeters every three days for two weeks.
   At each measurement also record leaf color (green / pale green /
   yellow) and stem sturdiness (sturdy / thin / floppy) for each
   group. An adult checks the setup and the log.
4. On day 14, find each group's mean height (add the five heights,
   divide by 5). Compare: variety A vs. B in the same light (the
   variety effect), and the same variety across lights (the light
   effect).
5. Write the health judgment: does the tallest group look the
   healthiest? Use color and sturdiness, not height alone.

**Safety and alternatives.** Adult supervision throughout. Do not
taste seeds or plants; wash hands after handling soil; check for
seed or pollen allergies before starting; never leave a lamp
unattended and never grow plants in total darkness for the full
two weeks. If plants are unavailable, use the practice dataset
below — it teaches the same objectives, with no penalty, and the
report must say "practice dataset" wherever it uses those numbers.

## Practice dataset (labeled fictional — not measurements anyone made)

Day-14 heights in centimeters, five plants per group:

| Group | Heights (cm) | Mean |
|---|---|---|
| Variety A, full light | 18, 20, 19, 21, 17 | 19.0 |
| Variety A, low light | 24, 26, 25, 27, 23 | 25.0 |
| Variety B, full light | 14, 15, 13, 16, 14 | 14.4 |
| Variety B, low light | 19, 20, 18, 21, 19 | 19.4 |

Low-light plants in this dataset are also recorded as paler and
thinner than full-light plants of the same variety. Learners using
their own plants replace this table with their own measurements and
observations.

## Rubric (adult-scored; 20 points)

| Criterion | Proficient (full points) | Developing (partial) | Beginning (little/no) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reproduction model (5 pts) | Both routes labeled with parent count and identical/varied information; one real example each | One route correct, or parent count missing | Routes confused or unlabeled |
| Punnett prediction + simulation (5 pts) | Square correct; genotype and phenotype ratios correct; 40-pair simulation recorded and compared with expectation, difference explained as chance | Square correct but simulation missing or unexplained | Square incorrect or ratios missing |
| Growth data and means (4 pts) | Table complete; all four means correct (own data or practice dataset, labeled); variety and light effects each stated with numbers | Means partly correct or one effect missing | No table or means incorrect |
| Health judgment + CER (4 pts) | Health uses color and sturdiness, not height alone; CER claims both factors influence growth, cites means as evidence, and reasons from them | CER claims both factors but evidence thin, or health uses height alone | No CER or claim contradicts the data |
| Walkthrough (2 pts) | Learner explains all three parts in their own words and answers one follow-up question | Explains two parts | Cannot explain the portfolio |

**Readiness signal:** 16/20 or above with no criterion at
"Beginning" indicates readiness for Unit 05 (variation becomes the
raw material for natural selection). Below that, reteach the
weakest criterion with the teacher-guide prompts before moving on.
