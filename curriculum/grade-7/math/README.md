# Grade 7 Mathematics

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are delivered;
the eight units are planned, not yet written. See [scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained for
one grade-7 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 7 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-7 math course organized around proportional
reasoning (unit rates with fractional ratios, testing proportionality, constant
of proportionality in tables, graphs, and equations), operations with all
rational numbers (sign rules grounded in number-line models, complex fractions,
terminating and repeating decimals), percent problems (discounts, markups, tax,
tips, commissions, percent increase/decrease and error, simple interest),
linear expressions with rational coefficients (expand, factor, rewrite to reveal
structure), multi-step equations (px + q = r and p(x + q) = r) and inequalities
(px + q > r or < r) graphed and interpreted, scale drawings and informal
geometric constructions (triangle uniqueness conditions), circle area and
circumference, area/surface area/volume of figures built from triangles,
quadrilaterals, polygons, cubes, and right prisms, angle-equation problems
(supplementary, complementary, vertical, adjacent), random sampling and
comparative inference (visual overlap, mean absolute deviation), and chance
processes (probability models, long-run frequency, compound events, tree
diagrams, simulation). Sessions are about 45 minutes, four per week, blending
number-line and area models, concrete manipulatives (two-color counters,
fraction bars, dice, spinners, rulers, protractors), and independent written
practice that the adult reviews the same day.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Add and subtract rational numbers using number-line diagrams; explain the
   sign rules with opposite-quantities contexts (debts, temperatures below
   zero) and distance-as-absolute-value.
2. Multiply and divide rational numbers by applying properties of operations
   (distributive property justifies (−1)(−1) = 1); convert quotients of
   integers to terminating or repeating decimals by long division.
3. Solve multi-step real-world and mathematical problems with all four
   operations on rational numbers, including complex fractions, and check
   reasonableness by estimation.
4. Compute unit rates from ratios of fractions, including unlike units, and
   state results with correct units.
5. Decide whether two quantities are in a proportional relationship by testing
   for equivalent ratios in a table and by checking whether the graph is a
   straight line through the origin; distinguish proportional relationships
   from other relationships.
6. Identify the constant of proportionality (unit rate) in tables, graphs,
   equations, diagrams, and verbal descriptions; write the proportional
   relationship as an equation; explain what the points (0, 0) and (1, r)
   mean in context.
7. Solve multi-step percent problems: percent increase and decrease,
   discounts, markups, sales tax, tips, commissions, fees, and percent
   error.
8. Compute simple interest and explain it as proportional growth per period.
9. Add, subtract, factor, and expand linear expressions with rational
   coefficients using properties of operations; rewrite an expression in a
   different form to show how quantities relate (for example,
   a + 0.05a = 1.05a shows a 5% increase is multiplication by 1.05).
10. Solve multi-step real-life problems posed with positive and negative
    rational numbers in any form (whole numbers, fractions, decimals);
    convert between forms as appropriate and assess reasonableness with
    mental computation and estimation.
11. Solve word problems leading to equations of the forms px + q = r and
    p(x + q) = r with specific rational numbers; compare the algebraic
    solution to an arithmetic solution by naming the operations used.
12. Solve word problems leading to inequalities of the forms px + q > r or
    px + q < r; graph the solution set and interpret it in the context of
    the problem.
13. Solve scale-drawing problems: compute actual lengths and areas from a
    scale drawing, and reproduce a scale drawing at a different scale.
14. Draw geometric figures (freehand, with ruler and protractor, and with
    technology) from given conditions; for triangles, decide whether three
    measures determine a unique triangle, more than one, or no triangle.
15. Use facts about supplementary, complementary, vertical, and adjacent
    angles in multi-step problems to write and solve simple equations for
    an unknown angle.
16. Find the circumference and area of circles from radius or diameter; give
    an informal derivation of the relationship between circumference and
    area of a circle.
17. Solve real-world and mathematical problems involving area, volume, and
    surface area of two- and three-dimensional objects composed of
    triangles, quadrilaterals, polygons, cubes, and right prisms.
18. Describe the two-dimensional figures that result from slicing
    right rectangular prisms and right rectangular pyramids (cross-sections).
19. Judge whether a sample is representative of a population; explain why
    random sampling tends to produce representative samples that support
    valid inferences.
20. Draw inferences from random-sample data about a population; generate
    multiple samples of the same size to gauge variation in estimates.
21. Compare two numerical data distributions by visual overlap; express the
    difference between centers as a multiple of a measure of variability
    (mean absolute deviation); draw informal comparative inferences with
    measures of center and variability from random samples.
22. Assign probabilities as numbers between 0 and 1 with likelihood
    language (near 0 unlikely, near 1/2 neither, near 1 likely).
23. Approximate a chance event's probability by its long-run relative
    frequency, and predict approximate frequencies from a given
    probability.
24. Build uniform probability models and frequency-based (possibly
    non-uniform) models; compare model probabilities with observed
    frequencies and explain discrepancies.
25. Find probabilities of compound events using organized lists, tables,
    tree diagrams, and simulations; design a simulation (for example, with
    random digits) to approximate a compound-event probability.

## For the guiding adult

Grade-7 math converts grade-6 fluency into proportional, rational-number, and
algebraic power: proportional relationships get tested (not assumed),
negatives extend to all four operations through number-line reasoning, and
equations/inequalities solve multi-step word problems before they become
procedure. Read the scope-and-sequence before starting; it lists safe
household materials, accessibility supports, and the diagnostic, midyear, and
review weeks that keep pacing humane. Fluency work is strategy-based — no
timed speed tests. Each future unit will include its own teacher guide and
answer key. Proportionality testing, signed-number multiplication, and
sampling inference are new in this grade — all deserve slow, concrete
introductions, and "distinguish proportional from non-proportional" should
stay visual (tables, graphs through the origin) long before it becomes
definition.
