# Lesson 02 — Factor and Rewrite for Structure

**Goal:** You will factor linear expressions and rewrite
expressions in different forms to show how quantities relate.
**Time:** 45 minutes · **Materials:** paper, pencil, calculator for checking only
**Unit:** [Unit 04](../README.md) · **Objective 1** · **Standards 7.EE.A.1–2**

## Readiness check (3 min)

1. Expand 6(2x + 3). What do you get?
2. What number divides both 12 and 18? What is the largest such number?
3. Write 5% as a decimal. Compute a + 0.05a by combining like terms.

## Teaching

**Factoring** is expanding in reverse: write an expression as a
product. To factor 12x + 18, find the **greatest common factor
(GCF)** of the terms — the largest number (and any shared variable)
that divides every term. The GCF of 12x and 18 is 6. Divide each
term by 6: 12x ÷ 6 = 2x; 18 ÷ 6 = 3. So 12x + 18 = **6(2x + 3)**.
Always check by expanding: 6(2x + 3) = 12x + 18. ✓

Rewriting also reveals structure. Two expressions are
**equivalent** if they always give the same value for the same
variable. Sometimes one form tells the story better than another:

- a + 0.05a = 1.05a: "a price plus 5% of itself" is the same as
  "1.05 times the price" — a 5% increase (Unit 03's key rewriting).
- x − 0.20x = 0.80x: "a price minus 20% of itself" is "0.80 times
  the price" — a 20% discount.
- 4x + 12 = 4(x + 3): "four groups of x plus 12" is "four groups of
  (x + 3)" — useful when x + 3 is one meaningful quantity.

Choose the form that answers the question being asked. To compute
a discounted price, 0.80x is fastest. To see the four equal groups,
4(x + 3) is clearest. Neither form is "more correct"; they are
equivalent, and a good mathematician switches between them.

**Worked example 1.** Factor 12x + 18, and check by expanding.

GCF of 12x and 18 is 6. 12x ÷ 6 = 2x; 18 ÷ 6 = 3.
So 12x + 18 = **6(2x + 3)**.
Check: 6(2x + 3) = 12x + 18. ✓ Also test x = 2: 24 + 18 = 42;
6 × 7 = 42. ✓

**Worked example 2.** Rewrite each expression and say what it shows.

(a) p + 0.10p = **1.10p**: a 10% increase — multiply by 1.10.
(b) m − 0.25m = **0.75m**: a 25% decrease — multiply by 0.75.
(c) 2x + 6 + 3x − 2: combine like terms → **5x + 4**. The
simplified form shows the total grows by 5 for each unit of x,
starting from 4.

**Worked example 3.** Show that (1/2)(10x + 8) = 5x + 4, two ways.

Method A (expand): (1/2) × 10x = 5x; (1/2) × 8 = 4. So it equals
5x + 4. Method B (factor the right side): 5x + 4 has GCF 1, but
multiplying both terms by 2 inside a (1/2) factor gives
(1/2)(10x + 8). Check with x = 2: left (1/2)(28) = 14;
right 10 + 4 = 14. ✓

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** Factor 8x − 12.
*Hint: what is the GCF of 8x and 12?*

**G2.** Rewrite y + 0.1y in one-term form, and say what percent
change it shows.

**G3.** Simplify 3a + 9 − a, then factor the result if possible.
*Hint: combine the a-terms first.*

## Independent practice

**I1.** Factor 15x + 10.
**I2.** Rewrite p − 0.25p in one-term form, and say what percent
change it shows.
**I3.** Simplify 6x − 4 + 2x, then factor the result.
**I4.** Simplify 2(x + 3) + x, and explain what the simplified
form shows.

## Applied task

A shop sells a notebook for n dollars. A gift set contains 3
notebooks plus a $4.50 pen, and the shop writes its price two
ways: 3n + 4.50 and 3(n + 1.50). Are the two prices always equal?
Show why, and say which form you would use to find the price
when n = 6.50 (compute it both ways as a check).

## Exit check

Factor 9x + 6. Check your answer by expanding and by testing x = 4.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** read 6(2x + 3) as "six groups of
  two x plus three" before factoring anything.
- **Alternate response mode:** use tiles to show 12x + 18 as 6
  equal groups before writing the factored form.
- **Extension:** factor (3/4)x + 3/2, and explain why the GCF
  is 3/4. (Answer in the key.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: factoring out a common factor that is not
the greatest (12x + 18 → 2(6x + 9) is true but unfinished);
reading 1.05a as "increase by 105%"; treating factored and
expanded forms as different values. Answers: see
[answer key](../answer-key.md), Lesson 02.
