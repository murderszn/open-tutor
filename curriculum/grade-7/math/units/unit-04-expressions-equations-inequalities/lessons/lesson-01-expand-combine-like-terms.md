# Lesson 01 — Expand and Combine Like Terms

**Goal:** You will expand linear expressions with rational
coefficients and combine like terms to write simpler equivalent
expressions.
**Time:** 45 minutes · **Materials:** paper, pencil, calculator for checking only
**Unit:** [Unit 04](../README.md) · **Objective 1** · **Standards 7.EE.A.1–2**

## Readiness check (3 min)

1. Multiply: 3 × (4 + 5). Now multiply 3 × 4 + 3 × 5. What do you notice?
2. Add: 2x + 5x. (Hint: 2 apples + 5 apples.)
3. Compute (1/2) × 6 and 0.5 × 6. Are they the same? Why?

## Teaching

An **expression** is a combination of numbers, variables, and
operations with no equals sign: 3x + 5, for example. A **term** is a
part added or subtracted; 3x + 5 has two terms. The number in front
of a variable is its **coefficient** (in 3x, the coefficient is 3).
**Like terms** have exactly the same variable part: 2x and 5x are
like terms; 2x and 2x² are not, and 2x and 2 are not.

Two tools simplify expressions. The **distributive property** says
a(b + c) = ab + ac: multiply the outside number by *every* term
inside. **Combining like terms** says you may add or subtract the
coefficients of like terms: 2x + 5x = 7x, because 2 of something
plus 5 of the same thing is 7 of that thing.

Watch the signs. Distributing a negative multiplies every inside
term by that negative, and subtracting an expression means
distributing −1 across it: −(x − 5) = −x + 5, not −x − 5.

**Worked example 1.** Expand 3(2x + 1/2).

Multiply 3 by each inside term: 3 × 2x = 6x; 3 × (1/2) = 3/2.
So 3(2x + 1/2) = **6x + 3/2**.
Check with x = 2: left side 3(4 + 1/2) = 3 × 4.5 = 13.5;
right side 6 × 2 + 3/2 = 12 + 1.5 = 13.5. ✓ They match.

**Worked example 2.** Simplify −2(3x − 4) + 5x.

Distribute −2: −2 × 3x = −6x; −2 × (−4) = +8. That gives
−6x + 8 + 5x. Now combine like terms −6x and 5x: −6x + 5x = −x.
Answer: **−x + 8**.
Check with x = 1: left side −2(3 − 4) + 5 = −2(−1) + 5 = 7;
right side −1 + 8 = 7. ✓

**Worked example 3.** Simplify (2/3)x + 1/4 + (1/3)x − 3/4.

Group like terms: (2/3)x + (1/3)x = (3/3)x = x. Constants:
1/4 − 3/4 = −2/4 = −1/2. Answer: **x − 1/2**.
Check with x = 3: left side 2 + 1/4 + 1 − 3/4 = 2.5;
right side 3 − 1/2 = 2.5. ✓

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** Expand 4(x − 3).
*Hint: 4 times x, and 4 times −3.*

**G2.** Simplify 2x + 3 + 5x − 1.
*Hint: group the x-terms, then group the numbers.*

**G3.** Expand −(1/2)(4x + 6).
*Hint: multiply each inside term by −1/2; watch the sign of 6.*

## Independent practice

**I1.** Expand 5(2x − 1).
**I2.** Simplify 3x − 2 + 4x + 7.
**I3.** Expand 2(0.5x + 4).
**I4.** Simplify −(x − 5) + 2x.

## Applied task

A rectangle has length 2x + 3 and width x − 1 (units in cm).
Write its perimeter as a simplified expression, then check it
for x = 5.

*Perimeter = 2 × length + 2 × width. Expand each part before
combining.*

## Exit check

Expand 3(x + 2/3). Show the check for x = 3.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** say each expression aloud before
  writing ("three times the quantity two x plus one half").
- **Alternate response mode:** build 3(2x + 1/2) with algebra
  tiles or counters before writing symbols.
- **Extension:** simplify 2(3x − 1) − 3(x + 4) and explain why
  the x-coefficient ends up as 3. (Answer in the key.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: distributing to only the first inside term
(3(2x + 1/2) → 6x + 1/2); forgetting that −(x − 5) flips both
signs; treating 2x and 2 as like terms. Answers: see
[answer key](../answer-key.md), Lesson 01.
