# Lesson 04 — Equations of the Form px + q = r

**Goal:** You will solve word problems leading to equations of
the form px + q = r, check by substitution, and compare the
algebraic solution with an arithmetic one.
**Time:** 45 minutes · **Materials:** paper, pencil, calculator for checking only
**Unit:** [Unit 04](../README.md) · **Objective 3** · **Standard 7.EE.B.4.a**

## Readiness check (3 min)

1. Solve x + 5 = 12. What did you do to both sides?
2. Solve 3x = 21. What did you do to both sides?
3. If 2x + 3 = 11, what should 2x equal? Why?

## Teaching

An **equation** states that two expressions are equal. The
**solution** is the value of the variable that makes the
statement true. Think of a balance scale: the two sides weigh
the same, so whatever you add, subtract, multiply, or divide on
one side, you must do on the other — or the scale tips.

![AI-generated illustration of a level balance scale with small parcels on the left pan and one larger parcel on the right pan](../assets/balance-scale-equation-activity.png)

*Caption: AI-generated illustration of a level balance scale.
The scale is level, so the two sides are equal — that is what
the = sign means. The parcels are a picture of balance only;
every number in the activity below comes from the table, not
from counting parcels.*

**Text-only alternative (the activity's numbers):** A delivery
cart holds 3 identical boxes plus a 5-lb tool bag, and the cart
weighs 32 lb in total. The boxes weigh the same unknown amount
each. Question: how much does one box weigh?

To solve px + q = r, undo the operations in reverse order
using **inverse operations**: first undo the + q (subtract q
from both sides), then undo the × p (divide both sides by p).
Then **check by substitution**: put the answer back into the
original equation and confirm both sides are equal.

**Worked example 1.** Solve 3x + 5 = 20.

Subtract 5 from both sides: 3x = 15. Divide both sides by 3:
x = **5**. Check: 3 × 5 + 5 = 20. ✓
Arithmetic solution (no algebra): start from 20, undo the +5
to get 15, undo the ×3 to get 5. Same operations, same order —
algebra just writes them down as steps on the equation.

**Worked example 2.** Solve (1/2)x − 3 = 4.

Add 3 to both sides: (1/2)x = 7. Multiply both sides by 2
(the inverse of × 1/2): x = **14**. Check: (1/2) × 14 − 3 =
7 − 3 = 4. ✓
Arithmetic solution: 4 + 3 = 7; 7 × 2 = 14. Again the same
undoing sequence.

**Worked example 3.** Solve −2x + 7 = 1. (Watch the sign.)

Subtract 7 from both sides: −2x = −6. Divide both sides by
−2: x = **3**. Check: −2 × 3 + 7 = −6 + 7 = 1. ✓
Common slip: dividing −6 by 2 instead of −2 gives x = −3,
which fails the check (−2 × (−3) + 7 = 13, not 1). The check
catches it.

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** Solve 4x − 9 = 11, and check by substitution.
*Hint: add 9 first, then divide by 4.*

**G2.** Solve 2x + 7 = 19, and write its arithmetic solution
in one sentence.

**G3.** Solve −3x + 4 = 22, and check by substitution.
*Hint: the division is by −3, not 3.*

## Independent practice

**I1.** Solve 5x + 2 = 27.
**I2.** Solve (3/4)x + 1 = 10.
**I3.** Solve −x + 8 = 3.
**I4.** Solve 6x − 11 = 25.

## Applied task

**Image activity.** Look again at the balance scale above and
the text table: 3 identical boxes plus a 5-lb tool bag weigh
32 lb in total. Write the equation, solve it, and check it.
Then answer: if a fourth identical box were added to the
cart, what would the new total weight be?

## Exit check

Solve 7x − 3 = 25 by both the algebraic steps and a one-sentence
arithmetic solution. Check by substitution.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** translate each word problem
  into "___ times the unknown, plus ___, equals ___" before
  writing symbols.
- **Alternate response mode:** act out the balance with a
  real scale or tiles: remove the same weight from both pans.
- **Extension:** a plumber charges a $45 visit fee plus $30
  per hour, and a bill totals $165. Write and solve the
  equation, and explain what each number in your equation
  means. (Answer in the key.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: undoing in the wrong order (dividing
before removing q); dropping a negative sign when dividing;
skipping the substitution check. Answers: see
[answer key](../answer-key.md), Lesson 04.
