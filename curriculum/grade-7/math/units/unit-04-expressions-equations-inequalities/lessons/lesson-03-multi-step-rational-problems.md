# Lesson 03 — Multi-Step Problems with Rational Numbers

**Goal:** You will solve multi-step real-life problems with
positive and negative rational numbers in any form, converting
between forms and checking reasonableness by estimation.
**Time:** 45 minutes · **Materials:** paper, pencil, calculator for checking only
**Unit:** [Unit 04](../README.md) · **Objective 2** · **Standard 7.EE.B.3**

## Readiness check (3 min)

1. Compute −3.5 + 7.25 without a calculator.
2. Write 2/3 as a decimal rounded to two places. Is it exact?
3. Estimate 3 × $12.25 before computing it exactly.

## Teaching

A multi-step problem mixes forms — whole numbers, fractions,
decimals, negatives — and mixes operations. Work it in four
moves: (1) name what each number means and its sign; (2) convert
forms only when it helps (decimals for money, fractions for
exact recipe parts); (3) compute step by step, keeping signs
visible; (4) **estimate first or last** and compare: if the
estimate and the exact answer disagree wildly, a sign or a
decimal point slipped.

Estimation is not guessing. Round each number to something easy,
compute mentally, and ask: "Should the answer be near this?"
For money, round to whole dollars. For a temperature, round to
whole degrees. A reason check catches most errors before they
reach an answer.

**Worked example 1.** A bank account holds $85.40. Three
withdrawals of $12.25 each are made. What is the balance?

Step 1: withdrawals are subtractions: 3 × $12.25 = $36.75.
Step 2: 85.40 − 36.75 = **$48.65**.
Reason check: round first — $85 − 3 × $12 = $85 − $36 = $49.
$48.65 is near $49. ✓ (Rounding down the withdrawals slightly
lowers the estimate's subtraction, so $48.65 just under $49
makes sense.)

**Worked example 2.** The morning temperature is −3.5°F. It
rises 7.25°F by noon, then falls 2.75°F by evening. What is the
evening temperature?

−3.5 + 7.25 = 3.75. Then 3.75 − 2.75 = **1.0°F**.
Reason check: round — −4 + 7 − 3 = 0. The exact answer 1.0°F
is near 0. ✓ Also note the story: the rise (7.25) is bigger
than the fall (2.75) plus the morning deficit (3.5), so the
evening temperature should be just above zero — and it is.

**Worked example 3.** A recipe needs 2/3 cup of oats per batch.
How many cups are needed for 2.5 batches? Give the answer as a
fraction and as a decimal, and say which form is more useful
for measuring.

2.5 = 5/2. So (2/3) × (5/2) = 10/6 = **5/3 cups = 1 2/3 cups
≈ 1.67 cups**.
Reason check: 2/3 is about 0.67; 0.67 × 2.5 is about 1.67. ✓
The fraction 1 2/3 cups is exact; the decimal 1.67 is rounded.
For measuring with a 1/3-cup scoop, the fraction form is more
useful (five 1/3-cup scoops); for a decimal scale, 1.67 cups is.

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** An account holds $45.60. Two withdrawals of $8.75 each
are made. Find the balance, with an estimate first.
*Hint: estimate $46 − 2 × $9 first.*

**G2.** Compute (−1/2) × 4 + 9. Explain the sign of the first
term before computing.
*Hint: (−1/2) × 4 is negative — how negative?*

**G3.** A walker climbs 12.5 m, descends 7.75 m, then climbs
3.25 m. What is the net change in height? Estimate first.

## Independent practice

**I1.** A gift card holds $120. Four purchases of $15.50 each
are made. How much remains?
**I2.** Compute −4.25 + 6.5, and explain in one sentence why
the answer is positive.
**I3.** Five pieces of ribbon are each 3/4 yard long. How many
yards in total? Give a fraction and a decimal.
**I4.** A $48.90 bill is split equally among 3 people. How much
does each pay? Estimate first, then compute exactly.

## Applied task

A fundraiser sells 24 tickets at $6.50 each and pays a $38
venue fee. How much money does the fundraiser keep? Show the
estimate (24 × $6.50 ≈ 25 × $6 = $150, minus about $40) and the
exact computation, and say whether the estimate supports the
exact answer.

## Exit check

Compute −6 + 2.5 × 4. Explain why multiplication comes before
addition, and give a one-line estimate check.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** underline the operation words
  (withdrawal, rises, falls, each) before writing any numbers.
- **Alternate response mode:** draw a number line for the
  temperature problem (Worked example 2) instead of writing a
  vertical sum.
- **Extension:** a stock price falls $2.75 on Monday, rises
  $1.50 on Tuesday, and falls $0.25 on Wednesday, starting from
  $18.00. Find Friday's opening price and the total change.
  (Answer in the key.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: adding a withdrawal instead of
subtracting it; converting 2/3 to 0.66 and treating it as exact;
estimating after computing and forcing agreement. Answers: see
[answer key](../answer-key.md), Lesson 03.
