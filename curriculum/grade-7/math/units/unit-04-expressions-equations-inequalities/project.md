# Project — Budget Challenge

**Unit:** [Unit 04](README.md) · **Objectives 1, 3, 4** · **Standards 7.EE.A.1–2, 7.EE.B.4**
**Session:** 13 (45 minutes, plus finishing time before Session 16)
**Submitted with:** Session 16 assessment

This project expands the legacy
[`../../assignments/inequalities-budget-challenge.md`](../../assignments/inequalities-budget-challenge.md)
task, which asked for six inequalities but gave no expressions,
no equation, no graph, and no rubric. All money below is
fictional practice data. No real family spending is used or
recorded.

## The situation

You are planning a shopping trip with a fixed budget. Pick one
budget target: **$120, $200, or $300**. You will plan purchases
in two groups: a **base plan** (items you will definitely buy)
and **extras** (identical items you may add while staying
within budget).

## What to submit

1. **Base-plan expression.** List each base-plan item with its
   price, write the total cost as an expression (for example,
   3(25) + 2(20)), and simplify it to a single dollar amount.
   Your base-plan total must be less than your budget.
2. **Exact-spend equation.** Choose a price for one identical
   extra item. Write and solve an equation of the form
   base + p × n = budget (or base + p(n + q) = budget) that
   spends the budget *exactly*, if your numbers allow it. If
   your numbers do not allow an exact spend, say so, show the
   equation, and explain what its solution means (for example,
   "n = 3.7, so no whole number of extras spends exactly
   $200").
3. **Two limit inequalities.** Write and solve two inequalities
   for your trip: (a) extras alone must fit in the money left
   after the base plan (≤); (b) a second constraint of your
   choice — for example, the number of extras is at least 2
   ("I want at least two"), or the total including a $10 fee
   stays under budget. Interpret each solution in a sentence.
4. **One graph.** Graph one of your inequalities on a number
   line, with the correct open or closed circle and arrow
   direction, and label what the variable counts.
5. **Recommendation paragraph (4–6 sentences).** Which extras
   will you actually buy, how many, what the final total is,
   and one reason your plan is safe (for example, the money
   left over, or the whole-number check on the inequality).
6. **Reflection (3–4 sentences).** Which part was hardest —
   the expression, the equation, or the inequalities — and why?
   Name one check (substitution, boundary test, or estimate)
   that caught or could catch an error in your work.

## Rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | Full marks | Partial | Missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base-plan expression, simplified correctly (4) | Expression with all items, correct total | Minor arithmetic slip, method sound | No expression or wrong total with no work |
| Exact-spend equation, solved and checked (4) | Correct equation, solution, substitution check; or a correct "no exact spend" explanation | Equation right, check missing | No equation, or unsolved |
| Two inequalities, solved and interpreted (6) | Both correct, with sentence interpretations | One correct, or interpretations missing | Neither solved |
| Graph correct (3) | Right circle type, direction, labels | Circle or direction wrong | No graph |
| Recommendation + reflection (3) | Totals match the algebra; reflection names a real difficulty and a check | Totals inconsistent, or reflection generic | Missing |

## Worked sample (adult reference only — not a learner answer)

See the [answer key](answer-key.md), Project sample: a $200
budget, a $140 base plan, the equation 140 + 20n = 200 (n = 3),
and the inequality 140 + 20n ≤ 200 (n ≤ 3, so at most 3 extras).
Learners choose their own budget and items; any correct plan
with matching algebra earns full marks.
