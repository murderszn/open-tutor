# Unit 04 — Expressions, Equations, and Inequalities

**Grade:** 7 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #34](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/34), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

An equation is a balance: whatever you do to one side, you do to the
other, or the scale tips. An inequality is the same balance with a
direction: one side is heavier, and the graph shows every value that
keeps it that way. This unit turns the rational-number fluency of
Unit 01 and the percent rewriting of Unit 03 (a + 0.05a = 1.05a) into
real algebra: expand, factor, and rewrite linear expressions with
rational coefficients; solve multi-step problems in any number form;
solve equations of the forms px + q = r and p(x + q) = r and compare
the algebraic route with an arithmetic one; and solve and graph
inequalities of the forms px + q > r or px + q < r, including the
rule that multiplying or dividing by a negative number flips the
inequality sign.

## Essential question

When a story gives you an unknown quantity, how do you write it as
an equation or inequality, solve it without breaking the balance,
and check that the answer makes sense in the story?

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–03 and the diagnostic weeks:

- The four operations on rational numbers, including negatives,
  fractions, and decimals (Unit 01); sign rules, especially that
  (−1)(−1) = 1
- The distributive property and properties of operations as
  computation strategies (Unit 01)
- Percent rewriting as multiplication, e.g. a + 0.05a = 1.05a
  (Unit 03) — the first rewriting-for-structure example
- Estimation and reason checks: round, compute mentally, compare
  (Units 01–03 routine)

No geometry is required. Angle equations wait for Unit 05.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Add, subtract, factor, and expand linear expressions with
   rational coefficients using properties of operations, and
   rewrite an expression in a different form to show how quantities
   relate (track objective 9; 7.EE.A.1–2).
2. Solve multi-step real-life problems posed with positive and
   negative rational numbers in any form, converting between forms
   as appropriate and assessing reasonableness with mental
   computation and estimation (track objective 10; 7.EE.B.3).
3. Solve word problems leading to equations of the forms
   px + q = r and p(x + q) = r with specific rational numbers, and
   compare the algebraic solution to an arithmetic solution by
   naming the operations used (track objective 11; 7.EE.B.4.a).
4. Solve word problems leading to inequalities of the forms
   px + q > r or px + q < r, graph the solution set, and interpret
   it in the context of the problem (track objective 12; 7.EE.B.4.b).

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**,
grade 7, Expressions and Equations domain, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/Math](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/)
(codes and descriptions cross-checked against the official domain
pages during the A00 audit, 2026-10-05, and the framework page
re-confirmed 2026-11-01; no state adoption or accreditation
claimed). Descriptions below are paraphrases of the official
wording.

| Unit objective | Standard | Official description (paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7.EE.A.1 | Apply properties of operations to add, subtract, factor, and expand linear expressions with rational coefficients. |
| 1 | 7.EE.A.2 | Understand that rewriting an expression in different forms can shed light on the problem and how the quantities in it are related (for example, a + 0.05a = 1.05a means a 5% increase is multiplication by 1.05). |
| 2 | 7.EE.B.3 | Solve multi-step real-life problems posed with positive and negative rational numbers in any form; convert between forms as appropriate; assess reasonableness using mental computation and estimation. |
| 3 | 7.EE.B.4.a | Solve word problems leading to equations of the form px + q = r and p(x + q) = r, where p, q, and r are specific rational numbers; compare an algebraic solution to an arithmetic solution, identifying the sequence of operations used in each approach. |
| 4 | 7.EE.B.4.b | Solve word problems leading to inequalities of the form px + q > r or px + q < r, where p, q, and r are specific rational numbers; graph the solution set and interpret it in the context of the problem. |

Conventions fixed by this unit (adult, note them before Session 1):
an equation solution is always checked by substitution into the
original equation; an inequality solution is checked with the
boundary value and one test value on each side where the story
allows it; inequality graphs use an open circle when the boundary
is not included (>, <) and a closed circle when it is (≥, ≤);
money answers are rounded to the nearest cent only at the final
step; all prices, budgets, and stories are fictional practice data.

## Accessibility and safety

All activities are paper-based; the image activity ships with a
text-only table carrying the same numbers, and the inequality
diagrams ship with a written description of every graph (open or
closed circle, arrow direction). Expressions and equations can be
built with algebra tiles or two-color counters for learners who
benefit from concrete models. No lab materials, no hazards, no
accounts, no learner data leaves the household. Budgets are
fictional practice data, so no family spending information is
required or recorded.
