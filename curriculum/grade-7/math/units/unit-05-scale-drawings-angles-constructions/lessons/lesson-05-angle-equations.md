# Lesson 05 — Writing Equations for Unknown Angles

**Goal:** You will write and solve simple equations for unknown angles
using complementary, supplementary, and vertical angle facts.
**Time:** 45 minutes · **Materials:** paper, pencil
**Unit:** [Unit 05](../README.md) · **Objective 3** · **Standard 7.G.B.5**

## Readiness check (3 min)

1. Solve x + (x + 20) = 90. (2x = 70, x = 35.)
2. Solve 3x + 10 = 5x − 30. (40 = 2x, x = 20.)
3. Two angles are complementary. One is 55°. What is the other? (35°.)

## Teaching

Lesson 04 gave you the facts; this lesson turns them into equations.
The pattern is always the same, in three steps:

1. **Name the relationship.** Are the angles complementary (add to
   90°), supplementary (add to 180°), or vertical (equal)? The
   diagram — or the words "on a straight line," "in a right angle,"
   "opposite" — tells you.
2. **Write the equation.** Complementary: A + B = 90. Supplementary:
   A + B = 180. Vertical: A = B.
3. **Solve with Unit 04 tools, then answer the question asked.**
   Almost always the question asks for the *angle measures*, not just
   x. Substitute x back into each expression and check the sum.

**Worked example 1.** Two complementary angles are x and x + 20.
Find both angles.

Relationship: complementary, so they add to 90°.
Equation: x + (x + 20) = 90 → 2x + 20 = 90 → 2x = 70 → x = 35.
Angles: 35° and 35° + 20° = **55°**. Check: 35 + 55 = 90. ✓

**Worked example 2.** Two vertical angles measure 3x + 10 and
5x − 30 (in degrees). Find x and the angle measure.

Relationship: vertical, so the measures are equal.
Equation: 3x + 10 = 5x − 30 → 10 + 30 = 5x − 3x → 40 = 2x → x = 20.
Angle: 3(20) + 10 = **70°**. Check with the other expression:
5(20) − 30 = 70. ✓ Both give 70°, as vertical angles must.

**Worked example 3.** Two supplementary angles measure 2x + 5 and
x + 25 (in degrees). Find both angles.

Relationship: supplementary, so they add to 180°.
Equation: (2x + 5) + (x + 25) = 180 → 3x + 30 = 180 → 3x = 150 →
x = 50. Angles: 2(50) + 5 = **105°** and 50 + 25 = **75°**.
Check: 105 + 75 = 180. ✓

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** Two complementary angles are x and x + 10. Find both angles.
*Hint: x + (x + 10) = 90.*

**G2.** Two vertical angles measure 2x + 5 and x + 35. Find x and the
angle measure.
*Hint: vertical means equal — set the expressions equal.*

**G3.** Two supplementary angles are x and 2x + 30. Find both angles.
*Hint: x + (2x + 30) = 180.*

## Independent practice

**I1.** Two complementary angles measure x + 5 and 2x − 20. Find both
angles.
**I2.** Two vertical angles measure 4x − 15 and 2x + 25. Find x and the
angle measure.
**I3.** Two supplementary angles measure x + 40 and 3x − 20. Find both
angles.
**I4.** Two adjacent angles on a straight line measure 2x + 10 and
x + 50. Find both angles.

## Applied task

A straight angle is split into three angles measuring x, 3x, and 60°.
(a) Write and solve an equation for x. (b) List all three angle
measures and check that they add to 180°.

## Exit check

Two complementary angles: one is twice the other. Find both angles.
(Start with x + 2x = 90.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: stopping at x instead of finding the angle
measures — every key entry here lists both, and the adult should
insist on the substitution step. A second error: using 90° for a
straight-line pair (or 180° for a right-angle pair); the diagram
check — "what do they sit on?" — comes before any algebra. Answers:
see the [answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-05).
