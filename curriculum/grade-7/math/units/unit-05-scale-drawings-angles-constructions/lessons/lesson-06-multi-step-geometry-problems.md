# Lesson 06 — Multi-Step Geometry Problems

**Goal:** You will combine scale drawings, triangle conditions, and
angle facts to solve multi-step problems, and find errors in
faulty reasoning.
**Time:** 45 minutes · **Materials:** paper, pencil, ruler, protractor
**Unit:** [Unit 05](../README.md) · **Objectives 1–3** ·
**Standards 7.G.A.1–2, 7.G.B.5**

## Readiness check (3 min)

1. Scale 1 cm = 2 m. A line drawn 5 cm is really how long? (10 m.)
2. A triangle has angles 40° and 70°. What is the third angle? (70°.)
3. Two angles add to 180°. One is 2x + 10 and the other is x + 20.
   What equation finds x? ((2x + 10) + (x + 20) = 180.)

## Teaching

This lesson puts the unit together. A multi-step problem usually
hides two or three single-step problems inside it: read the whole
problem, list what is given and what is asked, and do the steps in
order — scale first, then shape facts, then angle facts (or the
reverse, as the problem demands). Write the step you are on before
you compute it; that one habit prevents most multi-step errors.

**Worked example 1.** A triangular garden bed is drawn with sides
3 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm. The scale is 1 cm = 1.5 m. (a) What are the
real side lengths? (b) What is the real perimeter? (c) What is the
real area? (d) Is the real triangle a right triangle?

(a) Multiply each drawn side by 1.5: 3 × 1.5 = **4.5 m**,
4 × 1.5 = **6 m**, 5 × 1.5 = **7.5 m**.
(b) Perimeter: 4.5 + 6 + 7.5 = **18 m**.
(c) The drawn 3-4-5 triangle is right-angled (3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25
= 5²), and scaling keeps it right-angled, so the legs are 4.5 m and
6 m: area = ½ × 4.5 × 6 = **13.5 m²**.
(d) Check: 4.5² + 6² = 20.25 + 36 = 56.25, and 7.5² = 56.25. Equal,
so **yes, right-angled**. ✓ (Angles are never scaled: the drawn
right angle is a real right angle.)

**Worked example 2.** A triangle is drawn with two angles 35° and
65°. What is the third angle — in the drawing, and in the real
figure the drawing represents?

Third angle: 180° − 35° − 65° = **80°**. The real figure's angles are
the same: 35°, 65°, 80°. Scaling changes lengths only; angle measures
are identical in the drawing and the real figure.

**Worked example 3 (error analysis).** A scale is 1 cm = 3 m. A
learner says a line drawn 4 cm is 7 m long, "because 4 + 3 = 7."
What is the error, and what is the correct length?

The learner added the scale factor instead of multiplying by it. The
scale is a proportional relationship, not an addition: actual =
4 × 3 = **12 m**. (Reason check: 4 cm at 1 cm = 3 m must be longer
than 4 m — and 12 m is, while 7 m is barely longer than 4 m.)

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** A rectangle is drawn 5 cm by 3 cm. The scale is 1 cm = 2 m.
What is its actual area?
*Hint: actual sides first (10 m by 6 m), then multiply.*

**G2.** A triangle has angles 42° and 58°. What is the third angle?
*Hint: angles sum to 180°; scaling does not change them.*

**G3.** A triangle is drawn with sides 5 cm, 12 cm, 13 cm. The scale
is 1 cm = 2 m. What are the real side lengths and the real perimeter?
*Hint: multiply each side by 2, then add.*

## Independent practice

**I1.** A square is drawn with side 2.5 cm. The scale is 1 cm = 4 m.
What is its actual area?
**I2.** Two complementary angles: one is 4 times the other. Find both
angles.
**I3.** A triangle is drawn with sides 8 cm, 15 cm, 17 cm. The scale
is 1 cm = 0.5 m. What are the real side lengths? Is the real triangle
right-angled? (Check 4² + 7.5² against 8.5².)
**I4.** Two adjacent angles on a straight line measure x and 3x − 20.
Find both angles.

## Applied task

A park plan uses 1 cm = 10 m. It shows a rectangular lawn drawn
6 cm by 4 cm and a triangular flower bed drawn with sides 3 cm, 4 cm,
5 cm. (a) What is the lawn's actual area? (b) What are the flower
bed's real side lengths and perimeter? (c) The bed's drawn angles
measure about 37°, 53°, and 90°. What are the real bed's angles, and
why are they the same?

## Exit check

A map uses 1 cm = 15 km. A lake is drawn as a rectangle 2 cm by
1.5 cm. (a) What are the lake's actual dimensions? (b) What is its
actual area?
