# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Scale Drawings, Angles, and Geometric Constructions

**Grade 7 · Track weeks 20–23 · Sessions 1–16 (45 minutes each)**

## Pacing notes

- **Session 1 (L1):** the direction question — page to world or world
  to page — is the unit's keystone. Spend the first 10 minutes on it
  with two quick examples before the garden activity; the image
  activity itself takes about 15 minutes.
- **Session 3 (L2):** the squared-factor idea needs a concrete
  demonstration: draw a 2 cm × 2 cm square and a 4 cm × 4 cm square
  on graph paper and count squares (4 vs. 16). Do this before Worked
  example 1.
- **Session 5 (L3):** budget 10 minutes for the triangle-spotting
  warm-up (it is the first outdoor task in the track) and 15 minutes
  for hands-on construction with strips of paper — the failing
  construction (3, 4, 8 cm) teaches the triangle inequality better
  than any explanation.
- **Session 7 (L4):** teach the pairs by their sums (90° / 180°) and
  their positions (opposite / next to). The SVG diagram is the
  reference for the rest of the unit — keep it visible.
- **Session 9 (L5):** insist on the three-step pattern (name the
  relationship, write the equation, substitute back). Refuse answers
  that stop at x.
- **Sessions 11/13 (project):** Session 11 is drawing; Session 13 is
  computing and writing. Learners who finish drawing early in
  Session 11 may start the measurement table.

## Misconception clinic

| Misconception | What you see | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Adding the scale factor | "4 cm at 1 cm = 3 m is 7 m" | The scale is a proportion, not an addition. Ask: "Is the real thing longer than the drawing by a little, or by a factor?" |
| Dividing for the actual length | Actual < drawn | Page → world multiplies; world → page divides. The real object is bigger than its scale drawing. |
| Scaling area by the linear factor | "96 cm² × 2 = 192 m²" | Scale the sides first, then multiply; or square the factor. Count graph-paper squares. |
| Scaling angles | "The real angle is twice the drawn angle" | Angles are never scaled. A protractor reading is the same on the drawing and the real figure. |
| "Adjacent means supplementary" | Any next-to pair called supplementary | Adjacent is position; supplementary is a sum. Ask both questions: "What do they add to?" and "What do they sit on?" |
| Stopping at x | "x = 35" with no angle measures | The question asks for angles. Substitute back and check the sum (90° or 180°). |
| "Three measures = one triangle" | SSA or AAA called unique | SSA can give two triangles (compass arc crosses twice); AAA fixes shape, not size. |

## Reteach triggers (from the week-23 quiz)

- **Q3 or Q10 wrong (scale area):** reteach Lesson 02 with the
  graph-paper squares demonstration; drill "scale the sides first."
- **Q4 or Q5 wrong (triangle conditions):** reteach Lesson 03 with
  paper strips; drill the triangle inequality as a three-check habit.
- **Q6–Q8 wrong (angle pairs):** reteach Lesson 04 from the SVG;
  drill complement = 90 −, supplement = 180 −, vertical = equal.
- **Q9 wrong (angle equation):** reteach Lesson 05's three steps;
  the usual failure is the setup (wrong sum) or stopping at x.
- **Two or more sections weak:** use Session 14 for a second pass
  over Lessons 02 and 05 before the assessment; do not rush into
  Session 16.

## Readiness evidence

The learner is ready for Unit 06 when they can: compute an actual
area from a scale drawing without prompting about the squared factor;
state the triangle inequality and apply it to a new triple; set up an
angle equation from a diagram description without being told which sum
to use; and explain in one sentence why a scale drawing's angles are
unchanged. If any of these is missing, reteach that lesson before
Unit 06 (circles will reuse the same scale-area reasoning).

## Differentiation and supports

- **Concrete first:** paper strips for triangle inequality; graph
  paper for area scaling; a protractor with large markings.
- **Technology option:** GeoGebra constructions (see the
  [resource pack](resource-pack.md)) for learners who struggle with
  compass control — the uniqueness judgment is the same.
- **Reading access:** word problems available as adult read-aloud;
  vocabulary defined in context (see the unit README glossary).
- **Response modes:** scribed or oral responses for reflections and
  exit checks; the project change note may be dictated.
- **Extension:** construct *both* triangles for an SSA case with a
  compass and measure how they differ; the golden-ratio enrichment
  (`assignments/golden-ratio.md`, adult previews interactives) as an
  optional scale-and-proportion investigation.
- **Extra time:** no timed speed tests; chunk the P1–P24 bank into
  sets of 4–6.
