# Answer Key — Unit 05: Scale Drawings, Angles, and Geometric Constructions

Every lesson exercise, practice item, quiz question, and assessment
question is solved here, independently of the lesson text. Worked
examples are solved in the lessons; their answers are re-checked
below. Never place this key beside a student question.

## Lesson 01

**Readiness check:** 1. 25% = 0.25 = 1/4. 2. 10% of 80 = 8 (both
ways: (10/100) × 80 = 8; 0.10 × 80 = 8). 3. The price rose $10.

**Worked examples (re-check):** Ex. 1: 3.5 × 5 = 17.5 km. Ex. 2: bed
9 × 2 = 18 m long, 2 × 2 = 4 m wide. Ex. 3: (a) 4.5 × 3 = 13.5 ft;
(b) 6 ÷ 3 = 2 in.

**Guided practice:** G1. 6 × 4 = **24 m**. G2. 7.5 × 2 = **15 ft**.
G3. 36 ÷ 6 = **6 cm**.

**Independent practice:** I1. 5.5 × 8 = **44 km**. I2. 2.5 × 3 =
**7.5 m**. I3. 54 ÷ 9 = **6 in**. I4. (a) 12 × 2 = **24 m** long,
8 × 2 = **16 m** wide. (b) 2 × 2 = **4 m** per side.

**Applied task:** (a) 3 × 1 = **3 m** long, 2 × 1 = **2 m** wide.
(b) The friend added the scale factor instead of multiplying: 1 cm
stands for 1 m, so a 3 cm drawing stands for 3 × 1 = 3 m — addition
has no role in scaling.

**Exit check:** 4 × 7 = **28 m**.

## Lesson 02

**Readiness check:** 1. 15 cm². 2. 20 m. 3. 16.

**Worked examples (re-check):** Ex. 1: actual 20 m × 12 m, area
**240 m²** (check: 15 × 16 = 240). Ex. 2: garden **6 cm × 4 cm**;
bed **4.5 cm × 1 cm**; shed **1 cm × 1 cm**. Ex. 3: Mia's error is
scaling area by the linear factor 2; correct factor is 2² = 4, area

**Guided practice:** G1. Actual 12 m × 18 m, area **216 m²**. G2.
Actual side 10 m, area **100 m²**. G3. **6 cm × 4 cm**.

**Independent practice:** I1. Actual 35 m × 10 m, area **350 m²**.
I2. 20 × 3² = 20 × 9 = **180 m²**. I3. **5 cm × 3 cm**. I4. Drawn
**4.5 cm × 1 cm**; actual area 18 × 4 = **72 m²**.

**Applied task:** (a) Actual 20 m × 12 m, area **240 m²**. (b) New
drawn size **4 cm × 2.4 cm** (20 ÷ 5 = 4; 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4); smaller
because each centimeter now stands for more meters. (c) The banner's
actual area does not change when the drawing changes — it is still
240 m². Dividing by 5 confuses the drawing's scale with the object's
area.

**Exit check:** Actual 18 m × 12 m, area **216 m²**.

## Lesson 03

**Readiness check:** 1. 180°. 2. Isosceles. 3. 90°.

**Warm-up:** adult checks the four sketches, classifications, and
evidence sentences; no single answer. The copied triangle must record
its scale.

**Worked examples (re-check):** Ex. 1: 5 + 6 = 11 > 7 ✓ — exists,
**unique** (SSS). Ex. 2: 3 + 4 = 7 < 8 — **no triangle**. Ex. 3:
**unique** (ASA); third angle 180 − 50 − 60 = **70°**. Ex. 4: SSA —
**more than one** triangle (the swinging side meets the base twice).

**Guided practice:** G1. 4 + 9 = 13 > 12 ✓ (and 4 + 12 > 9,
9 + 12 > 4) — exists, **unique**. G2. ASA — **unique**; third angle
180 − 30 − 90 = **60°**. G3. 2 + 5 = 7 < 9 — **no triangle**.

**Independent practice:** I1. 6 + 8 = 14 > 10 ✓ — exists,
**unique**; right-angled: 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100 = 10² ✓. I2.
**Many** — three angles fix the shape only; similar triangles of any
size fit. I3. SAS — **unique**; isosceles (two 8 cm sides). I4.
4 + 5 = 9 < 10 — **no triangle**.

**Applied task:** (a) 2 m: 3 + 2 = 5 > 4 ✓ — **works**. 7 m:
3 + 4 = 7, not > 7 — **no** (degenerate, lies flat). 8 m:
3 + 4 = 7 < 8 — **no**. (b) The two short boards must reach across
the long one; at 7 m they just meet flat, and beyond 7 m they fall
short.

**Exit check:** 9 + 12 = 21 > 15 ✓ — exists, **unique** (also right:
9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225 = 15²).

## Lesson 05

**Readiness check:** 1. x = 35. 2. x = 20. 3. 35°.

**Worked examples (re-check):** Ex. 1: x = 35; angles **35°, 55°**.
Ex. 2: x = 20; angle **70°** (both expressions give 70). Ex. 3:
x = 50; angles **105°, 75°**.

**Guided practice:** G1. x + (x + 10) = 90 → x = 40; angles **40°,
50°**. G2. 2x + 5 = x + 35 → x = 30; angle 2(30) + 5 = **65°**.
G3. x + (2x + 30) = 180 → x = 50; angles **50°, 130°**.

**Independent practice:** I1. (x + 5) + (2x − 20) = 90 → 3x = 105 →
x = 35; angles **40°, 50°**. I2. 4x − 15 = 2x + 25 → x = 20; angle
4(20) − 15 = **65°**. I3. (x + 40) + (3x − 20) = 180 → x = 40;
angles **80°, 100°**. I4. (2x + 10) + (x + 50) = 180 → x = 40;
angles **90°, 130°**.

**Applied task:** (a) x + 3x + 60 = 180 → 4x = 120 → x = 30.
(b) Angles **30°, 90°, 60°**; 30 + 90 + 60 = 180 ✓.

**Exit check:** x + 2x = 90 → x = 30; angles **30°, 60°**.

## Lesson 06

**Readiness check:** 1. 10 m. 2. 70°. 3. (2x + 10) + (x + 20) = 180.

**Worked examples (re-check):** Ex. 1: (a) **4.5 m, 6 m, 7.5 m**;

**Guided practice:** G1. Actual 10 m × 6 m, area **60 m²**. G2.

x + (3x − 20) = 180 → 4x = 200 → x = 50; angles **50°, 130°**.

**Applied task:** (a) Actual lawn 60 m × 40 m, area **2400 m²**.
(b) Bed sides **30 m, 40 m, 50 m**; perimeter **120 m**. (c) The

(b) 30 × 22.5 = **675 km²**.
