# Project — Scale-Drawing Studio

**Sessions 11 and 13 (plus finishing time at home if needed) · 20 points**
Rebuilds the legacy
[`geometry-scale-drawing-studio.md`](../../assignments/geometry-scale-drawing-studio.md)
with a rubric, a worked exemplar, a triangular feature, and a
protractor angle check.

## The task

Design a room — a bedroom, a studio, or a game room — and draw it to
scale. You are the designer; the drawing is the plan a builder would
follow, so every length on it must be honest.

## Steps

1. **Choose the room and the scale.** Pick a real or imagined
   rectangular room (for example, 12 ft by 10 ft, or 5 m by 4 m).
   Choose a scale that fits on one sheet of graph paper — for
   example, 1 cm = 1 ft, or 1 cm = 0.5 m — and write it at the top
   of the drawing.
2. **Draw the room.** Draw the room rectangle to scale. Label each
   side with its drawn length *and* its actual length.
3. **Place the furniture.** Draw at least four pieces as rectangles
   (bed, desk, shelf, rug — your choice), each to the same scale,
   and label each with its drawn and actual dimensions.
4. **Add the triangular feature.** Include one triangular feature — a
   reading nook, a corner shelf, a triangular rug — with all three
   side lengths given. Test it with the triangle inequality and
   record the test.
5. **Check one corner.** Measure one room corner with a protractor
   and record the angle. Is it a right angle (90°)? Say what the
   measurement tells you about your drawing.
6. **Compute.** Find the room's actual perimeter and actual area, and
   the actual area of one furniture piece. Show each step: drawn
   length → actual length → perimeter or area.
7. **Write the change note.** Propose one change that improves the
   layout (move a piece, resize the rug, open a path) and explain in
   2–4 sentences why it is an improvement, using a measurement from
   your drawing as evidence.

## Deliverables

- The scale drawing (one sheet, labeled, with the scale written on it)
- A measurement table: every drawn length and its actual length
- The perimeter, area, and triangle-inequality calculations, with work
  shown
- The corner-angle measurement and what it shows
- The change note (2–4 sentences)

## Worked exemplar (adult reference; learners design their own room)

Room 12 ft by 10 ft at 1 cm = 1 ft → drawn 12 cm by 10 cm.
Perimeter: 2 × (12 + 10) = **44 ft**. Area: 12 × 10 = **120 ft²**.
Bed 6 ft by 3.5 ft → drawn 6 cm by 3.5 cm; area 6 × 3.5 = **21 ft²**.
Triangular nook with sides 3 ft, 4 ft, 5 ft: 3 + 4 = 7 > 5 ✓,
3 + 5 = 8 > 4 ✓, 4 + 5 = 9 > 3 ✓ — a real triangle (and right-angled:
3² + 4² = 25 = 5²). Corner measured **90°** — a right angle, as a
rectangle's corner should be. Change note: move the desk from the
north wall to the east wall so it no longer blocks the window; the
desk is 4 ft wide and the east wall has 10 ft, leaving 6 ft clear.

## Rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | 5 / 4 / 3 points | Partial | Missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scale consistency (5) | One scale used for every length; drawn and actual lengths both labeled and correct | One or two lengths off-scale or unlabeled (3) | No consistent scale (0) |
| Measurements and calculations (5) | Perimeter, room area, and one furniture area all correct with work shown | One calculation wrong or work missing (3) | Two or more wrong, or no work (0) |
| Triangular feature and angle check (4) | Triangle tested with the inequality; corner measured and interpreted | Triangle drawn but not tested, or angle measured without interpretation (2) | No triangle or no angle check (0) |
| Completeness and labels (3) | Four furniture pieces, all labeled, scale written on the drawing | Three pieces, or some labels missing (2) | Fewer than three pieces, or no scale (0) |
| Change note (3) | One change proposed with a measurement-based reason (2–4 sentences) | A change with a vague reason (2) | No change, or no reason (0) |

**Scoring note for the adult:** a drawing that is careful but has one
arithmetic slip can still earn full marks on scale consistency if the
method is right — score the method, then note the slip for reteach.
