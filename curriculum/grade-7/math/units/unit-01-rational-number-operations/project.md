# Project — The Temperature Ledger

**Type:** week-long data investigation (Sessions 11, 13, 14; submitted at
Session 16) · **Objectives:** 1, 2, 3
**Adult role:** help read the thermometer or weather source; check the
data table for completeness; do not compute for the learner.

## The task

Real weather lives on both sides of zero. For seven consecutive days you
will keep a **temperature ledger**: each day's high and low, the daily
swing, and how the highs move from day to day. Then you will turn the
ledger into a short report with a graph and a written conclusion.

**Dataset A (preferred): real observations.** With an adult, record your
town's actual daily high and low for seven days. Use a household
thermometer (read together at the same times each day) or any free,
no-account weather source the adult chooses. Pick °F or °C and use it all
week — do not mix scales.

**Dataset B (alternative): fictional Harbor Town week.** Use this
original fictional dataset if real collection isn't possible. All
temperatures are °C.

| Day | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| High (°C) | 3 | −1 | −6 | −2 | 4 | 7 | 2 |
| Low (°C) | −4 | −8 | −12 | −9 | −3 | 0 | −5 |

## Steps

1. **Build the ledger table (Session 11).** Columns: Day | High | Low |
   Daily swing (High − Low) | Change in high from previous day. Leave the
   last column blank for Day 1 (there is no previous day).
2. **Collect (Sessions 11–14).** Dataset A: record highs/lows daily with
   the adult. Dataset B: copy the table above.
3. **Compute (Sessions 13–14).** For each day, the daily swing as a
   distance expression. Then: the largest single-day swing; the largest
   rise and largest drop in the high from one day to the next; the mean
   high and mean low for the week (write non-terminating means with bar
   notation); the total change from Day 1's high to Day 7's high.
4. **Graph (Session 14).** On graph paper or a drawn grid, plot each
   day's high (dots) and low (crosses) against a vertical number line.
   Connect the highs with line segments. Label the zero line "freezing
   (0°C)" or "32°F" as appropriate.
5. **Write (Session 14).** A 4–6 sentence conclusion using at least four
   of these words correctly: *opposite, absolute value, mean, swing,
   change, distance, negative.* Answer: was the week overall warming or
   cooling? What was the most extreme day, and how do you know?

## Deliverables

1. The completed ledger table (all seven days, all columns).
2. Computation work showing every operation — estimates or number-line
   checks beside at least three items.
3. The graph with labeled axes and zero line.
4. The written conclusion.

## Rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Proficient | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Data completeness | All 7 days, both temps, no gaps; units labeled | 6 days complete or one minor gap | 4–5 days or missing units | Fewer than 4 days |
| Computation accuracy | All swings, changes, means correct with work shown | 1–2 errors, work shown | 3–4 errors or missing work | Mostly incorrect or no work |
| Rational-number reasoning | Signs, opposites, and absolute value used correctly and explained | Correct use, thin explanation | Sign errors present | No signed-number reasoning |
| Graph | Correct vertical number line, all points plotted, zero line labeled | Minor plotting/label slips | Axes unlabeled or several misplots | Missing or unreadable |
| Written conclusion | 4+ vocabulary words used correctly; warming/cooling claim backed by data | 3 words correct; claim present | 1–2 words; claim unsupported | Missing or off-topic |

**Scoring:** 18–20 = exceeds; 14–17 = meets; 10–13 = approaching (revise
the computation section and resubmit); below 10 = reteach with the adult
using Lessons 02–03, then redo.
