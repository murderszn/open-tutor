# Lesson 03 — Subtracting Rational Numbers

**Goal (student-readable):** I can rewrite any subtraction as adding the
opposite, and I can find the distance between two rational numbers with
absolute value.
**Time:** 45 minutes · **Materials:** number line, paper, pencil.

## Readiness check (5 min)

Rewrite each as an addition you already know how to do: (a) 9 − 4;
(b) −3 − 5; (c) 2 − (−6). (Answers: 9 + (−4); −3 + (−5); 2 + 6.) If (c)
felt strange — subtracting a negative turning into adding — that strangeness
is exactly what today explains.

## Teaching

**Subtraction = adding the opposite.** p − q = p + (−q), always. Why?
Subtracting q undoes adding q, and adding the opposite of q also undoes
adding q — both are the "undo" of the same journey, so they must be equal.
This single idea turns every subtraction into an addition you can solve on
the number line.

**The four sign cases, one method.** Rewrite, then journey:

- 8 − 3 = 8 + (−3): start at 8, move 3 left → 5.
- −8 − 3 = −8 + (−3): start at −8, move 3 left → −11.
- 8 − (−3) = 8 + 3: start at 8, move 3 right → 11.
- −8 − (−3) = −8 + 3: start at −8, move 3 right → −5.

Notice the famous one: **subtracting a negative moves you right** — it
increases the number. "Minus a negative is a plus" is not magic; it is the
opposite-of-an-opposite.

**Distance as absolute value.** The distance between p and q on the number
line is |p − q| (equivalently |q − p|). Distance ignores direction, so the
absolute value strips the sign. Example: the distance between −4 and 7 is
|−4 − 7| = |−11| = 11. Check on the line: from −4 to 0 is 4, from 0 to 7
is 7, total 11. ✓

**Fractions and decimals.** Same method, more careful arithmetic:
1/2 − (−3/4) = 1/2 + 3/4 = 2/4 + 3/4 = 5/4. And 2.5 − 4.1 = 2.5 + (−4.1):
signs differ, 4.1 wins → −(4.1 − 2.5) = −1.6.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** −12 − (−20).

Rewrite: −12 + 20. Start at −12, move 20 right → 8. **Answer: 8.**
Story check: removing a $20 debt from someone who owes $12 leaves them $8
ahead. ✓

**Example 2.** Find the distance between −2.5 and 3.5 two ways.

Method A (absolute value): |3.5 − (−2.5)| = |3.5 + 2.5| = |6| = 6.
Method B (number line): from −2.5 to 0 is 2.5; 0 to 3.5 is 3.5; total 6.
**Distance: 6.** Both methods agree — and Method B is your estimation
check for Method A.

**Example 3.** 3/8 − 5/6.

Rewrite: 3/8 + (−5/6). Common denominator 24: 9/24 + (−20/24) = −11/24.
**Answer: −11/24.** Reason check: 5/6 ≈ 0.83 > 3/8 = 0.375, so the result
must be negative and roughly −0.45; −11/24 ≈ −0.458. ✓

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. Rewrite as addition, then compute: (a) 5 − 9; (b) −5 − 9; (c) 5 − (−9);
   (d) −5 − (−9). *(Hint: change the sign of the second number only.)*
2. Find the distance between: (a) −10 and 4; (b) −3 and −9; (c) 2.5 and
   −1.5. *(Hint: |larger − smaller| works, but |p − q| works in any order.)*
3. (a) 7/10 − (−3/5); (b) −4.2 − 1.8; (c) 0 − (−6.25).
4. At 6 a.m. the temperature is −5°C; by 2 p.m. it is 7°C. How many
   degrees did it rise? Write the distance expression.
5. Explain why 3 − (−4) = 7 using the "undo" idea from the lesson, not just
   the rule.

## Independent practice

6. Compute: (a) −14 − 6; (b) 14 − (−6); (c) −14 − (−6); (d) −14 + 6.
   Then write one sentence comparing (a) and (c).
7. Compute: (a) −5/6 − 1/3; (b) 9/4 − (−7/4); (c) −3.75 − (−1.25);
   (d) 2.08 − 5.2.
8. The Dead Sea surface is about −430 meters (430 m below sea level); the
   peak of a nearby hill is 120 meters above sea level. What is the
   elevation difference between the hilltop and the Dead Sea surface?
9. **Error analysis.** Priya says −8 − (−3) = −11 "because subtracting
   makes it more negative." Identify her error, show the correct rewrite,
   and give the correct answer.
10. Fill the missing number: (a) __ − 7 = −2; (b) −9 − __ = −4;
    (c) 1/2 − __ = −1/4.

## Application

**Account audit.** A bank statement shows: starting balance −$15 (overdrawn
$15), a −$8 fee charged, then a $30 deposit. Write one expression using
subtraction and addition, compute the ending balance, and state whether
the account is still overdrawn. Then verify by rewriting every subtraction
as adding the opposite.

## Exit check

1. Rewrite −6 − (−11) as an addition and compute it.
2. What is the distance between −7 and −7? What does that tell you about
   |p − q| when p = q?

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading/language access:** color-code the rewrite step — circle the
  subtraction sign and the second number's sign in red, then rewrite in
  blue.
- **Alternate response mode:** for distance problems, draw the number-line
  segment and count the hops; the drawing counts as the solution method.
- **Extension:** Prove that |p − q| = |q − p| for any rational numbers p,
  q, using the number line (distance has no direction). Then explain why
  p − q ≠ q − p in general.

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- The double negative is the unit's hardest habit: −5 − (−2) routinely
  becomes −7. Drill the rewrite step aloud ("minus negative two becomes
  plus two") until it is automatic.
- Learners confuse "distance between" with "difference": emphasize that
  distance is always ≥ 0 because of the absolute value, while p − q can be
  negative.
- Watch for rewriting errors like 5 − −3 → 5 + −3 (sign left on the 3).
  The rule changes the *subtraction sign to addition* and *the sign of the
  second number* — both must flip.
