# Unit 01 — Rational Number Operations

**Grade:** 7 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #34](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/34), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Bank balances, temperatures below zero, depths under the sea — all of these
need numbers that can go below zero. In this unit the learner extends the
four operations to rational numbers: adding and subtracting on the number
line, explaining why "minus a negative" gives a positive, justifying the
multiplication sign rules with properties of operations, converting integer
quotients to terminating or repeating decimals, and solving multi-step
real-world problems — with estimation checks guarding every answer.

## Essential question

How do the properties of operations let us predict what happens when we
compute with negative numbers?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the grade 6 track:

- Place-value, fraction/decimal equivalence, and the four operations on
  **non-negative** fractions and decimals
- Integer addition/subtraction basics on the number line (re-taught here,
  not assumed)
- Order of operations; using a variable-free expression to model a situation
- Writing a worked solution with a reason check (track routine)

No algebra is required. Equations of the form px + q = r wait for Unit 04.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Add and subtract rational numbers in any form using number-line diagrams;
   explain the sign rules through opposite-quantities contexts (debts,
   temperatures below zero) and describe distance between two rational
   numbers as the absolute value of their difference.
2. Multiply and divide rational numbers, justify the sign rules with
   properties of operations (including a distributive-property argument for
   (−1)(−1) = 1), and convert quotients of integers to terminating or
   repeating decimals by long division.
3. Solve multi-step real-world and mathematical problems with all four
   operations on rational numbers, including complex fractions, and check
   reasonableness with estimation and a number-line sketch.

## Vocabulary

rational number · integer · opposite (additive inverse) · absolute value ·
distance on the number line · terminating decimal · repeating decimal ·
complex fraction · sign rule · distributive property · estimation · reason
check

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**,
grade 7, The Number System domain, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/Math](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/)
(codes and descriptions cross-checked against the official domain pages
during the A00 audit, 2026-10-05; no state adoption or accreditation
claimed). Descriptions below are paraphrases of the official wording.

| Unit objective | Standard | Official description (paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7.NS.A.1.a | Describe situations in which opposite quantities combine to make 0; a number and its opposite sum to 0 (additive inverses). |
| 1 | 7.NS.A.1.b | Interpret p + q as the point reached by moving a distance of |q| from p, right for positive q and left for negative q; interpret sums of rational numbers in real-world contexts. |
| 1 | 7.NS.A.1.c | Understand subtraction as adding the additive inverse (p − q = p + (−q)); the distance between two rational numbers on the number line is |p − q|. |
| 1 | 7.NS.A.1.d | Apply properties of operations as strategies to add and subtract rational numbers. |
| 2 | 7.NS.A.2.a | Multiplication extends to rational numbers by keeping the properties of operations — especially the distributive property — leading to (−1)(−1) = 1 and the signed-multiplication rules; interpret products in real-world contexts. |
| 2 | 7.NS.A.2.b | Integer division (non-zero divisor) always yields a rational number; −(p/q) = (−p)/q = p/(−q); interpret quotients in real-world contexts. |
| 2 | 7.NS.A.2.c | Apply properties of operations as strategies to multiply and divide rational numbers. |
| 2 | 7.NS.A.2.d | Convert a rational number to a decimal by long division; know the decimal terminates in 0s or eventually repeats. |
| 3 | 7.NS.A.3 | Solve real-world and mathematical problems involving the four operations with rational numbers. |

lessons are Sessions 1, 3, 5, 8, 10, and 12; the other sessions are guided
practice, reference reading, the project, review, and assessments. The
project runs across Sessions 11, 13, and 14 and is submitted with the
Session 16 assessment.
