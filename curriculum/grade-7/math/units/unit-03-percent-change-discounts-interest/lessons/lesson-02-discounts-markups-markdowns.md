# Lesson 02 — Discounts, Markups, Markdowns

**Goal:** You will solve discount, markup, and markdown problems two
ways — subtract or add the change, and multiply by (1 − r) or (1 + r).
**Time:** 45 minutes · **Materials:** paper, pencil, calculator for checking only
**Unit:** [Unit 03](../README.md) · **Objective 2** · **Standard 7.RP.A.3**

## Readiness check (3 min)

1. Find 25% of $48.
2. A $20 item is 10% off. How much is the discount in dollars?
3. Write the multiplier for a 30% decrease.

## Teaching

A **discount** (or **markdown**) lowers a price: you pay (1 − r) of
the original. A **markup** raises a price above cost: the seller
charges (1 + r) of the cost. In both cases the percent acts on the
**original** price (or the cost), and the two methods below always
agree — use both as a check.

> Method A: change = r × original; new price = original ± change.
> Method B: new price = original × (1 ± r). (Minus for a discount,
> plus for a markup.)

**Worked example 1.** A backpack costs $48 and is 25% off. What is the
sale price?

Method A: discount = 0.25 × 48 = $12; sale price = 48 − 12 = **$36**.
Method B: 48 × (1 − 0.25) = 48 × 0.75 = **$36**. Both agree. ✓

**Worked example 2.** A shop buys a mug for $12 and marks it up 50%.
What is the selling price?

Markup = 0.50 × 12 = $6; selling price = 12 + 6 = **$18**.
Multiplier check: 12 × 1.50 = 18. ✓ A 50% markup does not mean "add
50 dollars" — it means add 50% *of the $12 cost*.

**Worked example 3.** A coat costs $150 and is marked down 40%. What
is the sale price?

Discount = 0.40 × 150 = $60; sale price = 150 − 60 = **$90**.
Multiplier check: 150 × 0.60 = 90. ✓

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** A $90 jacket is 30% off. What is the sale price?
*Hint: multiplier 0.70.*

**G2.** A game costs a shop $40. The shop marks it up 25%. What is the
selling price?
*Hint: markup is 25% of $40, not $25.*

**G3.** A $200 television is marked down 15%. What is the sale price?
*Hint: 15% of 200 is 30.*

## Independent practice

**I1.** A $64 book is 25% off. What is the sale price?
**I2.** A toy costs a shop $30. The shop marks it up 60%. What is the
selling price?
**I3.** A $120 chair is 10% off. What is the sale price?
**I4.** The shoes in the image activity are $80 with 30% off. A second
store sells the same shoes for $60 with no discount. Which is cheaper,
and by how much?

## Applied task

A store advertises "Everything 25% off!" A learner says a $80 item
will cost $55 "because 25 off 80 is 55." (a) What is the correct sale
price? (b) Explain the learner's mistake: what does "25% off" subtract
— 25 dollars, or 25% of the price?

## Exit check

A $40 item is 25% off. What is the sale price? Show both methods.
