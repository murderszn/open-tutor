# Lesson 01 — Percent Increase and Decrease

**Goal:** You will compute percent increase and decrease, name the
original amount as the base, and rewrite a change as multiplication by
(1 + r) or (1 − r).
**Time:** 45 minutes · **Materials:** paper, pencil, calculator for checking only
**Unit:** [Unit 03](../README.md) · **Objective 1** · **Standard 7.RP.A.3**

## Readiness check (3 min)

1. Write 25% as a decimal and as a fraction in lowest terms.
2. Compute 10% of 80 two ways: (10/100) × 80 and 0.10 × 80.
3. A price rises from $50 to $60. By how many dollars did it rise?

## Teaching

A **percent** is a rate per 100: 25% means 25 per 100, or 25/100 = 0.25.
A **percent change** compares a change to the **base** — the amount you
started from, the *original* amount. The rule never changes:

> percent change = (change) ÷ (original) × 100%

The change is new − old. If the result is positive, the quantity
increased; if negative, it decreased. The denominator is always the
**original**, never the new amount. That one choice is where most
percent mistakes live.

There is a faster form that says the same thing. An increase of r
means: new = old + r × old = old × (1 + r). A decrease of r means:
new = old × (1 − r). The number (1 + r) or (1 − r) is the
**multiplier**. A 5% increase has multiplier 1.05 — which is exactly
why a + 0.05a = 1.05a, the rewriting from the scope-and-sequence.

**Worked example 1.** A shirt costs $40. Its price rises to $50. What
is the percent increase?

Change = 50 − 40 = $10. Base = original = $40.
Percent increase = 10 ÷ 40 × 100% = 0.25 × 100% = **25%**.
Multiplier check: 40 × 1.25 = 50. ✓

**Worked example 2.** A bike helmet costs $80. Its price falls to $60.
What is the percent decrease?

Change = 80 − 60 = $20 (use the size of the drop). Base = original =
$80. Percent decrease = 20 ÷ 80 × 100% = **25%**.
Multiplier check: 80 × 0.75 = 60. ✓
Notice: going back from $60 to $80 would be a 20 ÷ 60 = 33.3%
increase, not 25% — because the base changed. Same $20, different
base, different percent. Always ask: percent *of what*?

**Worked example 3.** A school club has 120 members. Membership grows
by 15%. How many members are there now?

Method A (add the change): 15% of 120 = 0.15 × 120 = 18; 120 + 18 =
**138**. Method B (multiplier): 120 × 1.15 = **138**. Both agree.
Reason check: 15% is between 10% (12) and 20% (24), so the growth
should be between 12 and 24 — 18 is. ✓

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** A score rises from 50 to 65. Find the percent increase.
*Hint: change first, then divide by the original 50.*

**G2.** A plant's height falls from 200 cm to 170 cm after pruning.
Find the percent decrease.
*Hint: the base is 200, not 170.*

**G3.** A $84 game is marked up 25%. What is the new price?
*Hint: try the multiplier 1.25.*

## Independent practice

**I1.** A temperature falls from 90°F to 72°F. Find the percent decrease.
**I2.** A quiz score rises from 45 to 54. Find the percent increase.
**I3.** A town's population grows from 400 to 460. Find the percent increase.
**I4.** A $250 bicycle is discounted by 20% (a decrease). What is the sale price?

## Applied task

A cafeteria serves 240 lunches a day. After a menu change it serves
300. (a) What is the percent increase? (b) The manager says "lunches
rose by 60, and 60 is 20% of 300, so the increase was 20%." Explain the
manager's error in one or two sentences.

## Exit check

A $50 item falls to $40. What is the percent decrease? Show the change,
the base, and the division.
