# Lesson 06 — Multi-Step Percent Problems

**Goal:** You will solve multi-step percent problems with successive
percents, analyze the classic "up then down" error, and check every
answer against the story.
**Time:** 45 minutes · **Materials:** paper, pencil, calculator for checking only
**Unit:** [Unit 03](../README.md) · **Objectives 1–5** · **Standard 7.RP.A.3**

## Readiness check (3 min)

1. $100 increased by 20%, then decreased by 20%: guess the result
   before computing. (Keep the guess; you will test it below.)
2. A $80 item is 25% off, then 10% tax applies. What is the price
   after the discount?
3. Write the multiplier for a 10% increase followed by a 10% decrease
   as a single product.

## Teaching

**Successive percents** act one after another, and each acts on the
result of the previous step — the base changes every step. That is why
percents do not add, subtract, or cancel the way plain numbers do.
The safe method: work step by step, writing the base and the
multiplier at each step, and finish with a reason check (is the final
number above or below the start, and by roughly how much?).

**Worked example 1.** A $100 item rises 20%, then falls 20%. What is
the final price?

Step 1: 100 × 1.20 = $120. Step 2: 120 × 0.80 = **$96**.
Not $100! The 20% fall acts on $120 (a $24 drop), while the 20% rise
acted on $100 (a $20 rise). Combined multiplier: 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96,
so the item ends 4% below where it started.

**Worked example 2.** An $80 jacket is 25% off, then 10% sales tax
applies. What is the final price?

Step 1 (discount): 80 × 0.75 = $60. Step 2 (tax on $60):
0.10 × 60 = $6. Final price = 60 + 6 = **$66**.
Common error: adding the percents (−25% + 10% = −15%) gives
80 × 0.85 = $68 — wrong, because the tax acts on the discounted $60,
not on the original $80. (The two multipliers commute, so
discount-then-tax and tax-then-discount give the same $66 total; the
receipt order, discount first, is the real-world convention.)

**Worked example 3 (error analysis).** Mia says: "20% off $50, then
20% tax, brings it back to $50, because −20% + 20% = 0." Is she right?

No. Step 1: 50 × 0.80 = $40. Step 2: tax = 0.20 × 40 = $8; final =
**$48**. Mia added percents that act on different bases ($50 and
$40). The correct combined multiplier is 0.80 × 1.20 = 0.96, not 1.00.

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** A $200 item rises 10%, then falls 10%. What is the final price?
*Hint: 200 → 220 → ?*

**G2.** A $60 game is 50% off, then a $5 fee is added. What is the
final price?
*Hint: the fee is dollars, not a percent — add it after the discount.*

**G3.** A $500 deposit earns 4% simple interest per year for 2 years.
What is the total amount?
*Hint: I = Prt, then add to the principal.*

## Independent practice

**I1.** A $120 bag is 25% off, then 8% sales tax applies. Find the
final price.
**I2.** A $1,000 deposit earns 5% simple interest per year for 3 years.
Find the interest.
**I3.** Estimate 70, actual 56. Find the percent error.
**I4.** A $40 item rises 25%, then falls 25%. Find the final price.

## Applied task

Two stores sell the same backpack. Store A lists it at $100 with 30%
off. Store B lists it at $90 with 20% off. (a) Compute both sale
prices. (b) Which store is cheaper, and by how much? (c) A friend says
"Store A is better because 30% is bigger than 20%." Explain why the
bigger percent does not automatically win, in one or two sentences.

## Exit check

A $50 item rises 10%, then falls 10%. What is the final price? Show
both steps and the combined multiplier.
