# Lesson 03 — Tax, Tips, Commissions, Fees

**Goal:** You will solve multi-step problems with sales tax, tips,
commissions, and fees, stating which base each percent acts on.
**Time:** 45 minutes · **Materials:** paper, pencil, calculator for checking only
**Unit:** [Unit 03](../README.md) · **Objective 3** · **Standard 7.RP.A.3**

## Readiness check (3 min)

1. Find 8% of $40.
2. A $50 bill with a 20% tip: what is the tip amount?
3. A $90 item is 25% off. What is the discounted price?

## Teaching

Four money percents, four different bases — the base is the whole
skill of this lesson:

- **Sales tax** acts on the price you actually pay (after any
  discount). Tax = rate × discounted price.
- **Tip (gratuity)** acts, in this unit, on the **pre-tax** amount.
  (Some people tip on the after-tax total; this unit fixes the
  pre-tax convention so answers can be checked. A problem that wants
  another base will say so.)
- **Commission** acts on the total sales amount: commission = rate ×
  sales.
- **Fee** acts on the base price it is attached to: fee = rate ×
  price, then add.

Work one step at a time, write the base for each percent, and never
add percents that act on different bases.

**Worked example 1.** A meal costs $40 before tax. Tax is 8%. The
diners leave a 20% tip on the pre-tax amount. What is the total?

Tax = 0.08 × 40 = $3.20, so the after-tax bill is $43.20.
Tip = 0.20 × 40 = $8.00 (base: pre-tax $40, not $43.20).
Total = 40 + 3.20 + 8.00 = **$51.20**.
Reason check: tax + tip = $11.20 on a $40 meal, about 28% — the total
should be a bit over $51. ✓

**Worked example 2.** A salesperson earns a 6% commission on an $850
sale. How much is the commission?

Commission = 0.06 × 850 = **$51**. The base is the $850 sale; there
is no second step.

**Worked example 3.** Shoes cost $120, are 25% off, and then carry 6%
sales tax. What is the final price?

Step 1 (discount): 120 × 0.75 = $90.
Step 2 (tax on the discounted price): 0.06 × 90 = $5.40.
Final price = 90 + 5.40 = **$95.40**.
Common error: taxing the original $120 (0.06 × 120 = $7.20) would give
$97.20 — wrong, because the tax base is the price actually paid.

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** A $50 meal has 10% tax and a 15% tip on the pre-tax amount.
Find the total.
*Hint: tax $5.00, tip $7.50 — then add both to $50.*

**G2.** A salesperson earns 4% commission on a $1,250 sale. Find the
commission.
*Hint: one step: 0.04 × 1250.*

**G3.** A $200 concert ticket carries an 8% fee. What is the final price?
*Hint: fee $16.*

## Independent practice

**I1.** A $30 meal has 5% tax and a 20% tip on the pre-tax amount. Find
the total.
**I2.** A salesperson earns 8% commission on a $625 sale. Find the
commission.
**I3.** A $150 jacket is 20% off, then 5% sales tax applies. Find the
final price.
**I4.** A $90 game carries a 10% fee. What is the final price?

## Applied task

You have $75 for dinner. The meal you want costs $60 before tax. Tax
is 6% and you plan a 20% tip on the pre-tax amount. (a) What is the
total? (b) Can you afford it? If not, by how much are you over?

## Exit check

A $50 item has 8% sales tax. What is the final price? Show the tax
amount and the base it acts on.
