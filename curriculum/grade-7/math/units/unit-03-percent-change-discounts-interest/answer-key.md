## Lesson 01 — Percent increase and decrease

**Readiness:** 1. 0.25 = 1/4. 2. $8. 3. $10.
**G1.** Change 15; 15 ÷ 50 = **30% increase**.
**G2.** Change 30; 30 ÷ 200 = **15% decrease**.
**G3.** 84 × 1.25 = **$105**.
**I1.** 18 ÷ 90 = **20% decrease**. **I2.** 9 ÷ 45 = **20% increase**.
**I3.** 60 ÷ 400 = **15% increase**. **I4.** 250 × 0.80 = **$200**.
**Applied:** (a) 60 ÷ 240 = **25% increase**. (b) The manager divided
by the new amount (300) instead of the original (240); the base for a
percent increase is always the original.
**Exit:** Change $10, base $50, 10 ÷ 50 = **20% decrease**.

## Lesson 02 — Discounts, markups, markdowns

**Readiness:** 1. $12. 2. $2. 3. 0.70.
**WE check:** $48 → $36; $12 → $18; $150 → $90 (all in lesson).
**Image activity:** (a) Backpack 48 × 0.75 = **$36**; bottle
20 × 0.90 = **$18**; shoes 80 × 0.70 = **$56**. (b) Total paid =
36 + 18 + 56 = **$110**. (c) Original total $148; saved $38;
38 ÷ 148 ≈ **25.7%**. (d) No: the overall percent is not the average
of the rates, because each rate acts on a different price; the
weighted result is 25.7%, not 21.7%.
**G1.** 90 × 0.70 = **$63**. **G2.** 40 × 1.25 = **$50**.
**G3.** 200 × 0.85 = **$170**.
**I1.** **$48**. **I2.** **$48**. **I3.** **$108**.
**I4.** The image-activity shoes cost $56, which is **$4 cheaper**
than the second store's $60.
**Applied:** (a) **$60**. (b) "25% off" subtracts 25% *of the price*
($20), not 25 dollars; 80 − 20 = 60, not 55.
**Exit:** 40 × 0.75 = **$30** (also 40 − 10 = 30).

## Lesson 04 — Simple interest

**Readiness:** 1. $20. 2. 0.5 years; 1.5 years. 3. $560.
**Chart activity:** (a) I = 200 × 0.05 × 4 = $40; 200 + 40 = **$240**.
(b) I = 200 × 0.05 × 10 = $100; balance **$300**. (c) Yearly growth
$20; doubling the rate doubles r × P, the yearly dollar growth.
**G1.** I = $36; total **$336**. **G2.** I = **$100**.
**G3.** I = **$15** (t = 0.5).
**I1.** I = $180; total **$1,080**. **I2.** I = **$270**.
**I3.** I = **$15** (t = 0.75). **I4.** Both earn **$120** — equal;
3% × 4 years and 4% × 3 years give the same product.
**Applied:** t = 150 ÷ (1000 × 0.05) = **3 years**. Check:
1000 × 0.05 × 3 = $150. ✓
**Exit:** I = 400 × 0.05 × 2 = **$40**.

## Lesson 05 — Percent error

**Readiness:** 1. 10. 2. 25%. 3. 15.
**G1.** 15 ÷ 75 = **20%**. **G2.** 1 ÷ 50 = **2%**.
**G3.** 40 ÷ 160 = **25%**.
**I1.** 6 ÷ 36 ≈ **16.7%**. **I2.** 3 ÷ 300 = **1%**.
**I3.** 8 ÷ 80 = **10%**. **I4.** Both **10%** — over- and
underestimates of the same size have the same percent error.
**Applied:** (a) 50 ÷ 500 = **10%**. (b) The editor is wrong: the
denominator is the actual crowd (500), not the estimate (450);
50 ÷ 450 ≈ 11.1% uses the wrong base.
**Exit:** Difference 5, denominator 50; 5 ÷ 50 = **10%**.

## Lesson 06 — Multi-step percent problems

**Readiness:** 1. Guess tested below: **$96**, not $100.
2. $60. 3. 1.10 × 0.90 = 0.99.
**G1.** 200 → 220 → **$198**. **G2.** 30 + 5 = **$35**.
**G3.** I = $40; total **$540**.
**I1.** 90 × 1.08 = **$97.20**. **I2.** **$150**.
**I3.** 14 ÷ 56 = **25%**. **I4.** 40 → 50 → **$37.50**.
**Applied:** (a) A $70, B $72. (b) **Store A by $2**. (c) The percent
acts on the list price; 30% of $100 ($30 off) beats 20% of $90
($18 off) here, but a bigger rate on a much smaller base can lose —
compare dollars, not just rates.
**Exit:** 50 → 55 → **$49.50**; combined multiplier 1.10 × 0.90 = 0.99.

## Project — Back-to-School Bundle Comparison

**Store A:** original $148; discount $37.00; discounted $111.00;
tax $6.66; fee $5.00; final **$122.66**.
**Store B:** original $138; discount $20.70; discounted $117.30;
tax $7.04 (7.038 rounded); fee $11.73; final **$136.07**.
Cheaper: **Store A by $13.41**. Note the teaching point: Store B
started cheaper ($138 vs. $148) but finished more expensive, because
its smaller discount and percent fee outweighed its lower list prices.
**Savings interest:** principal $122.66 at 4% for 3 years:
I = 122.66 × 0.04 × 3 = $14.7192 → **$14.72**.
**Recommendation (acceptable):** Store A, citing both final totals
and the fee/discount comparison; any reason-check note showing a
second-method verification of at least one step earns full marks for
that criterion.
