# Project — Back-to-School Bundle Comparison

**Unit:** [Unit 03](README.md) · **Objectives 1–4** · **Standard 7.RP.A.3**
**Sessions:** 11 and 13 (45 minutes each) · **Submitted with:** Session 16 assessment

## The situation (fictional practice data)

Two stores sell back-to-school bundles. Every price below is fictional
practice data, not a real store price. Tax is 6% and applies to the
discounted price. Each store also charges a handling fee, given below.

**Store A — "Ready Pack":** backpack $48, water bottle $20, shoes $80.
Discount: 25% off the whole bundle. Handling fee: $5 (flat, not a
percent).

**Store B — "Value Pack":** backpack $45, water bottle $18, shoes $75.
Discount: 15% off the whole bundle. Handling fee: 10% of the
discounted price.

## Session 11 — Part 1: compute both bundles

1. For each store, find the bundle's original total (sum of the three
   items).
2. Apply the store's discount to find the discounted price.
3. Add 6% sales tax on the discounted price.
4. Add the store's handling fee (Store A: $5 flat; Store B: 10% of the
   discounted price).
5. Record each store's final total in a table with one row per step:
   original total, discount amount, discounted price, tax, fee, final
   total.

## Session 13 — Part 2: savings comparison and recommendation

6. **Savings account comparison.** Suppose you put the cheaper
   bundle's final total into a savings account paying 4% simple
   interest per year. How much interest would it earn in 3 years?
   (Use the cheaper final total as the principal.)
7. **Recommendation.** Write a short paragraph (4–6 sentences):
   which store you recommend, the final totals as evidence, and one
   reason the bigger discount percent did not automatically decide the
   winner (or did — defend it with the numbers).
8. **Reason check.** For each store, verify one step a second way
   (for example, discounted price by subtracting the discount and by
   multiplying by (1 − r)). Note any step where the two ways
   disagreed and how you fixed it.

## Deliverables

- A step table for both stores (Part 1, steps 1–5).
- The savings-account computation (Part 2, step 6).
- The recommendation paragraph (Part 2, step 7).
- The reason-check note (Part 2, step 8).

## Rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | Points | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Original totals | 2 | Both bundles summed correctly ($148 and $138) |
| Discounts | 4 | Both discount amounts and discounted prices correct |
| Tax | 4 | 6% applied to each discounted price, not the original |
| Fees | 4 | Store A $5 flat; Store B 10% of its discounted price |
| Final totals | 2 | Both final totals correct and compared |
| Savings interest | 2 | Correct principal (cheaper total), I = Prt, correct interest |
| Recommendation + reason check | 2 | Evidence-based recommendation; a genuine second-method check noted |

Partial credit: one arithmetic slip in a step loses only that step's
points if later steps correctly use the learner's own earlier value
and the error is caught in the reason check.
