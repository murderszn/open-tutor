# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Percent Change, Discounts, and Simple Interest

**Unit:** [Unit 03](README.md) · **Track weeks:** 11–14 · **Standard 7.RP.A.3**

## Pacing notes

- **Week 11 (Sessions 1–4):** Lesson 01's base question ("percent of
  what?") is the unit's load-bearing idea — do not rush it. Lesson 02
  needs both methods every time (subtract the change *and* multiply by
  (1 − r)); learners who only use one method hide base errors.
- **Week 12 (Sessions 5–8):** Lesson 03 is the heaviest lesson: four
  money percents with four bases. Teach the bases table on the board
  before any computation. Lesson 04's chart makes "same dollars every
  year" visible; point at the equal bar steps, not just the formula.
- **Week 13 (Sessions 9–12):** Lesson 05 is short; use the freed time
  for the misconception clinic in Session 10. Lesson 06 should feel
  like a payoff, not new content — every problem recombines Lessons
  01–05.
- **Week 14 (Sessions 13–16):** Session 13 finishes the project;
  Session 14 is retrieval, not re-teaching. If the quiz (Session 15)
  shows a class-wide base error, reteach Lesson 01's denominator rule
  before the assessment rather than after it.

## Misconception clinic

| What learners say | The error | The repair |
|---|---|---|
| "25% off $80 is $55" | Subtracts 25 dollars, not 25% | 25% *of $80* is $20; 80 − 20 = 60 |
| "Up 20%, down 20% = back to start" | Adds percents on different bases | 100 → 120 → 96; multipliers 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96 |
| "Tip on the total with tax" | Wrong base for the tip | This unit's convention: tip on pre-tax; name the base first |
| "Tax on the original price" | Wrong base for the tax | Tax acts on the discounted price actually paid |
| "Percent error ÷ estimate" | Wrong denominator | Error is measured against the truth (actual), not the guess |
| "6 months = 6 years in I = Prt" | Time-unit slip | Convert months to years first: 6 months = 0.5 |
| "Simple interest grows by the same percent" | Confuses dollars with percent | Same *dollars* every year; the percent of the growing balance shrinks |

## Reteach triggers

- **Percent-change items (quiz Q1, Q5, Q10) below 70%:** return to
  Lesson 01; drill "change, base, divide" as three spoken steps before
  any numbers are written.
- **Discount/markup items wrong by a round-dollar amount:** the
  learner subtracted the rate as dollars; re-teach Lesson 02's
  "percent of the price" with a hundred-grid.
- **Multi-step items right in step 1, wrong later:** the base changed
  and the learner did not; require a written base label on every line.
- **Interest items with t in months:** require the conversion line
  ("6 months = 0.5 years") before the substitution line.
- **Percent-error items using the estimate as denominator:** re-teach
  with Lesson 05's rope example (50 vs. 40) and the question "error
  compared to what — the guess, or the truth?"

## Differentiation

- **Support:** hundred-grids for percent-of-a-number; a base-label
  template ("Base = ___; Rate = ___; Change = ___"); play money for
  discount steps; calculator allowed for the final multiplication
  only, never for choosing the operation.
- **Extension:** find the original price from a sale price and a
  discount rate (for example, $72 after 25% off → $96); compare
  simple interest at two rates for the same goal; compute the
  single multiplier for three successive changes; write a "receipt"
  problem with a discount, a fee, and tax in the learner's own words
  (fictional prices only).
- **Accessibility:** all image/chart numbers are duplicated in
  text tables; money work can be dictated to the adult; the project
  accepts a handwritten table and paragraph.

## Scoring notes

- Quiz and assessment: one point per item (assessment Q12: one point
  for the $100 computation, one for the explanation — score out of 13
  and note it, or score Q12 as one item; be consistent and tell the
  learner which before they start).
- Project: use the 20-point rubric in [project.md](project.md).
  Partial credit follows the learner's own earlier values when the
  method is right — a carried arithmetic slip is not a method error.
- Conventions to state before Session 1: tip on pre-tax; tax on
  discounted price; months converted to years; rounding to cents at
  the final step only.
