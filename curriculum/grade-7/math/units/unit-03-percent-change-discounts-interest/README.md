# Unit 03 — Percent Change, Discounts, and Simple Interest

**Grade:** 7 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #34](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/34), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A price that drops 20% and then rises 20% does not come back to where it
started. A bank balance that grows by the same dollars every year is not
growing by the same percent. This unit teaches the learner to solve
multi-step percent problems the way Unit 02 taught them to test
proportional relationships: name the base, write the multiplier, and
check the answer against the story. Discounts, markups, sales tax, tips,
commissions, fees, percent increase and decrease, percent error, and
simple interest are all the same idea — a percent of a clearly named
whole — applied in different shops, kitchens, and bank accounts.

## Essential question

When a percent acts on a price, a bill, or a bank balance, what is it a
percent *of* — and how does the answer change when the base changes?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2), the grade 6 track, and
Units 01–02:

- Percent as a rate per 100; converting percents, decimals, and
  fractions (grade 6; re-taught in Lesson 01, not assumed fluent)
- The four operations on rational numbers, including decimals and
  fractions (Unit 01)
- Unit rates, equivalent ratios, and the constant of proportionality
  (Unit 02); percent problems here are proportional relationships with
  a base of 100
- Writing a worked solution with a reason check (track routine)

No algebra beyond substitution is required. Equations here are only of
the forms new = old × (1 ± r), I = Prt, and percent error =
|estimate − actual| / actual. Equations of the forms px + q = r wait for
Unit 04.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Compute percent increase and decrease, name the original amount as
   the base, and rewrite a change as multiplication by (1 + r) or
   (1 − r) (track objective 7; 7.RP.A.3).
2. Solve discount, markup, and markdown problems, including successive
   percents applied to changing bases (track objective 7; 7.RP.A.3).
3. Solve multi-step problems involving sales tax, tips, commissions,
   and fees, stating which base each percent acts on (track objective
   7; 7.RP.A.3).
4. Compute simple interest with I = Prt, find the total amount, and
   explain simple interest as the same dollar growth every period
   (track objective 8; 7.RP.A.3).
5. Compute percent error as |estimate − actual| / actual and explain
   why the denominator is the actual value, not the estimate (track
   objective 7; 7.RP.A.3).

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**,
grade 7, Ratios and Proportional Relationships domain, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/Math](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/)
(codes and descriptions cross-checked against the official domain pages
during the A00 audit, 2026-10-05, and re-confirmed 2026-10-25; no state
adoption or accreditation claimed). Descriptions below are paraphrases
of the official wording.

| Unit objective | Standard | Official description (paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 2, 3, 5 | 7.RP.A.3 | Use proportional relationships to solve multi-step ratio and percent problems. Examples: simple interest, tax, markups and markdowns, gratuities and commissions, fees, percent increase and decrease, percent error. |
| 4 | 7.RP.A.3 | Same standard, simple-interest example: interest = principal × rate × time, with time in years. |
| 1–5 (supporting) | 7.RP.A.2 | Proportional relationships as the underlying structure: a percent is a rate per 100, so a percent of a base is a proportional relationship with constant of proportionality r. |

Conventions fixed by this unit (adult, note them before Session 1):
tips are computed on the pre-tax amount unless a problem says
otherwise; sales tax is computed on the discounted price, not the
original price; time in I = Prt is always in years (months are
converted: 6 months = 0.5 years); money answers are rounded to the
nearest cent only at the final step. All prices, rates, and bills in
lessons and assessments are fictional practice data, not real store
prices.

## Accessibility and safety

All activities are paper-based; the image activity ships with a
text-only price table carrying the same numbers, and the interest chart
ships with a data table. Money work can be done with play money or a
hundred-grid for learners who benefit from concrete models. No lab
materials, no hazards, no accounts, no learner data leaves the
household. The project uses copied prices, never photographs of people
or store interiors. Prices are fictional practice data, so no family
spending information is required or recorded.
