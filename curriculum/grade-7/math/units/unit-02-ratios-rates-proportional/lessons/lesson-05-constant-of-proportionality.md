# Lesson 05 — Constant of Proportionality and y = kx

**Goal:** You will find the constant of proportionality in tables,
graphs, equations, and descriptions; write y = kx; and interpret (0, 0)
and (1, r).
**Time:** 45 minutes · **Materials:** paper, pencil, ruler, calculator for checking only
**Unit:** [Unit 02](../README.md) · **Objective 3** · **Standard 7.RP.A.2.b–d**

## Readiness check (3 min)

1. Table: (2, 10), (3, 15), (5, 25). What single number turns every x
   into its y?
2. What does the point (1, r) mean if r is "dollars per pound"?
3. Write "the cost y is 4 times the weight x" as an equation.

## Teaching

Once a relationship passes the proportionality test, one number describes
it completely: the **constant of proportionality**, usually called k.
It is the unit rate — the value of y when x = 1 — and every y in the
relationship equals k times its x:

> y = kx, where k = y ÷ x for any pair (x ≠ 0)

**Finding k in a table:** divide any y by its x. Lemonade table:
6 ÷ 2 = 3, so k = 3 cups of water per cup of lemon juice, and y = 3x.

**Finding k in a graph:** read the y-value at x = 1 — the point (1, r).
In the Lesson 04 SVG, the line passes through (1, 2.5), so k = 2.5 and
y = 2.5x. You can also use any other point: (4, 10) → 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5. ✓

**Finding k in an equation:** in y = kx, k is the number multiplying x.
y = 0.4x has k = 0.4. (Watch out: y = x + 2 is not proportional at all —
no k exists.)

**Finding k in a description:** "Apples cost $1.80 per pound" → k = 1.80
dollars per pound, y = 1.8x where x is pounds and y is dollars.

**Interpreting the special points:**
- **(0, 0):** zero of one quantity means zero of the other. No apples,
  no cost. This is *why* proportional graphs pass through the origin.
- **(1, r):** one unit of x corresponds to r units of y — r *is* the
  unit rate, the constant of proportionality itself.

**Worked example 1.** A table shows gallons of paint (x) and area covered
in square feet (y): (2, 700), (3, 1050), (5, 1750). k = 700 ÷ 2 = 350
sq ft per gallon; equation: y = 350x. The point (1, 350) means 1 gallon
covers 350 square feet. (0, 0) means 0 gallons cover 0 square feet.

**Worked example 2.** A graph of distance vs. time passes through
(0, 0), (2, 130), (5, 325). k = 130 ÷ 2 = 65 miles per hour; d = 65t.
The point (1, 65) is the speed — this is exactly the unit rate from
Lesson 01, now with a name and an equation.

**Worked example 3 (non-example).** y = 3x + 4. Test x = 1: y = 7;
x = 2: y = 10. Quotients: 7 ÷ 1 = 7, 10 ÷ 2 = 5 — not equal, so no
single k exists. The "+4" (a flat fee, a starting head start) breaks
proportionality, exactly like the taxi.

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** Table (4, 28), (6, 42), (9, 63). Find k with units, write y = kx,
and interpret (1, k).
*Hint: 28 ÷ 4. What are the units of the two columns?*
**G2.** A graph passes through (0, 0) and (3, 7.5). Find k and write the
equation. What does (1, k) mean in a "pounds of coffee vs. dollars"
story?
*Hint: 7.5 ÷ 3 = 2.5.*
**G3.** Description: "A printer prints 12 pages per minute." Identify k,
write the equation (p = pages, m = minutes), and explain (0, 0).

## Independent practice

**I1.** Table (5, 45), (8, 72), (11, 99): k, equation, meaning of (1, k).
**I2.** A graph through (0, 0), (4, 3): k, equation. (Fractions allowed.)
**I3.** Equation y = (2/3)x: what is k? What does it mean if x is hours
and y is miles?
**I4.** Description: "Elena earns $13.50 per hour." k? Equation (e = earnings,
h = hours)? Meaning of (0, 0)?
**I5.** A table shows (2, 6), (4, 14), (6, 22). Does a constant of
proportionality exist? Justify with quotients.
**I6.** **Explain your reasoning.** Two proportional relationships have
equations y = 2.5x and y = 0.4x. On the same graph, which line is
steeper? What does "steeper" mean about the unit rate? What would the
graph of y = x look like between them?

## Application

A bulk store sells rice: the sign says "$0.90 per pound." (a) Write the
equation (c = cost, p = pounds). (b) What is the constant of
proportionality, with units? (c) The store also sells a 20-lb bag for
$16. Is the 20-lb bag proportional to the sign's rate? Use k to decide,
and compute how much you save or overpay versus the sign rate.

## Exit check

A car's odometer readings: after 2 hours, 110 miles; after 3 hours,
165 miles; after 5 hours, 275 miles (all from a full tank, constant
speed, starting at 0). (a) Find k with units and write d = kt.
(b) What do (0, 0) and (1, k) mean for this trip? (c) Predict the
distance after 7 hours.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading access:** highlight the multiplier in each representation —
  circle k in the table math, the equation, and the (1, r) point.
- **Alternate response:** build y = kx with a function-machine drawing:
  x goes in, "× k" happens, y comes out.
- **Extension:** show that if y = kx, then the graph's slope between any
  two points is k — compute (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1) for two rows of a table
  and confirm it equals k. (This previews Unit 04's structure talk.)

## Teacher note

Learners often find *a* quotient and call it k without checking every
row — require the Lesson 03 test first, then k. Others confuse k with x:
"the constant is 3" for y = 3x is right, but ask "3 *what*?" until units
are automatic. I6's steepness question builds intuition that k controls
the line's tilt — revisit in Unit 04. Answer key:
[../answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-05).
