# Lesson 06 — Multi-Step Proportional Problems

**Goal:** You will solve multi-step problems that combine unit rates,
proportionality testing, and y = kx — and check every answer for
reasonableness.
**Time:** 45 minutes · **Materials:** paper, pencil, calculator for checking only
**Unit:** [Unit 02](../README.md) · **Objectives 1–3** · **Standards 7.RP.A.1–2**

## Readiness check (3 min)

1. y = 4x. What is k? What is y when x = 7?
2. Table (3, 9), (5, 15), (8, 24): proportional? k?
3. A 10-lb bag of potatoes costs $6. Unit price?

## Teaching

Multi-step problems chain the unit's tools: find a unit rate, test or
assume proportionality, write y = kx, solve, and reason-check. The
reason check is not decoration — it catches the two most common errors:
dividing in the wrong order and assuming proportionality where none
exists.

**Worked example 1.** A paint store mixes 5 gallons of blue for every
2 gallons of white (a custom shade). (a) How many gallons of white are
needed for 20 gallons of blue? (b) What is the constant of
proportionality (white per blue)? (c) Write the equation.

(a) Unit rate: 2 ÷ 5 = 0.4 gallons of white per gallon of blue. For
20 gallons blue: 20 × 0.4 = 8 gallons of white. (b) k = 0.4 gal white
per gal blue. (c) w = 0.4b. Reason check: the original ratio 5:2 means
less white than blue — 8 < 20 ✓; and 8/20 = 2/5 ✓ equivalent.

**Worked example 2 (the bulk-discount twist).** A store sells flour:
2-lb bag $3.00; 5-lb bag $7.00; 10-lb bag $13.00. (a) Unit prices?
(b) Is price proportional to weight? (c) Which bag is the best value?

(a) 3.00 ÷ 2 = $1.50/lb; 7.00 ÷ 5 = $1.40/lb; 13.00 ÷ 10 = $1.30/lb.
(b) The unit rates differ (1.50 ≠ 1.40 ≠ 1.30), so price is **not**
proportional to weight — the store gives a bulk discount, which breaks
the constant rate. (c) The 10-lb bag at $1.30/lb. Note: "not
proportional" does not mean "bad deal" — here it means a *better* deal
for big buyers.

**Worked example 3.** A recipe for 6 servings uses 1.5 cups of broth.
You need 15 servings. (a) Broth needed? (b) Write the equation
(b = broth, s = servings) and identify k.

(a) k = 1.5 ÷ 6 = 0.25 cups per serving. For 15: 15 × 0.25 = 3.75 cups
(3 and 3/4 cups). (b) b = 0.25s. Reason check: 15 servings is 2.5 times
6 servings, so broth should be 2.5 × 1.5 = 3.75 ✓ — scaling both sides
by the same factor is the heart of proportional reasoning.

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** A car uses 9 gallons to travel 234 miles. (a) Miles per gallon?
(b) Write m = kg. (c) How far on 15 gallons?
*Hint: 234 ÷ 9 = 26. Reason-check (c) against (a).*
**G2.** Coffee: 12-oz bag $9.60; 16-oz bag $11.20. (a) Unit prices.
(b) Proportional? (c) Better value?
*Hint: compare 9.60/12 and 11.20/16 exactly.*
**G3.** A map scale: 1 inch = 25 miles. Two cities are 3.5 inches apart
on the map. Actual distance? Write the equation first.

## Independent practice

**I1.** A printer prints 84 pages in 6 minutes. (a) Pages per minute.
(b) Equation p = km. (c) Time to print 350 pages (nearest minute)?
**I2.** Yogurt: 6-oz cup $1.20; 32-oz tub $4.80. Unit prices?
Proportional? Better value? Explain the store's likely reason for the
price difference in one sentence.
**I3.** A recipe uses 3/4 cup of sugar for 8 muffins. How much sugar for
20 muffins? Give the answer as a fraction and a decimal.
**I4.** A runner's splits: 2 miles in 17 minutes, 5 miles in 42.5
minutes. (a) Is time proportional to distance? (b) k? (c) Predict 10
miles.
**I5.** A 5-lb bag of apples costs $6.25. (a) Unit price. (b) If the
price were proportional, what should 8 lb cost? (c) The store actually
charges $9.60 for 8 lb. Proportional or not? How much do you save?
**I6.** **Error analysis.** Leo solves "4 workers build a wall in 6
hours; how long for 8 workers?" with a proportion: 4/6 = 8/x, x = 12
hours. Explain why proportional reasoning does *not* apply here, and
give the correct answer with reasoning.

## Application

Plan a class party's lemonade (Lesson 03's 1:3 juice-to-water ratio) for
40 cups total. (a) How many cups of juice and water? (b) Juice costs
$0.50 per cup. Total juice cost? (c) If 40 cups serve 80 small cups,
write the equation relating total cups t to servings s, and find k.

## Exit check

A delivery driver is paid $0.65 per mile plus a $12 daily base fee.
(a) Make a table for 0, 20, 40, 60 miles. (b) Is pay proportional to
miles? Prove it with the table test. (c) The driver says "my per-mile
rate is $0.65, so it's proportional." Explain the flaw in that claim.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading access:** for each problem, write "Find k first / Test
  first / Just compute" before starting — naming the strategy prevents
  the wrong-tool error (see I6).
- **Alternate response:** solve with a double number line instead of an
  equation; both are accepted if the reason check is shown.
- **Extension:** the "workers and hours" problem (I6) is an *inverse*
  proportion (more workers, less time). If 4 workers take 6 hours, the
  product 4 × 6 = 24 worker-hours is constant. Write the equation h =
  24/w and graph it — notice it is *not* a straight line. (Inverse
  proportion is enrichment only.)

## Teacher note

I6 is the unit's most important trap: not every "two quantities" story
is proportional, and cross-multiplication without a proportionality test
is how learners go wrong. Celebrate the correct "not proportional"
verdict as loudly as a correct computation. The exit check's (c) targets
the "a rate exists, therefore proportional" confusion — the base fee is
the tell. Answer key: [../answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-06).
