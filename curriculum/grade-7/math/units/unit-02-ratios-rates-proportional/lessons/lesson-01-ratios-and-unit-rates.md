# Lesson 01 — Ratios and Unit Rates

**Goal:** You will compute unit rates from ratios of fractions and always
state the units.
**Time:** 45 minutes · **Materials:** paper, pencil, calculator for checking only
**Unit:** [Unit 02](../README.md) · **Objective 1** · **Standard 7.RP.A.1**

## Readiness check (3 min)

1. Simplify 12/18.
2. Compute (3/4) ÷ (1/2). Show the reciprocal step.
3. A car travels 120 miles in 2 hours. How many miles does it travel in
   1 hour? What are the units of your answer?

## Teaching

A **ratio** compares two quantities: "3 cups of flour for every 2 cups of
sugar" is the ratio 3:2, or 3/2. A **rate** is a ratio that compares
quantities with *different* units: "120 miles per 2 hours." A **unit rate**
is a rate with a denominator of 1: "60 miles per 1 hour," usually written
"60 miles per hour" or "60 mph."

To find a unit rate, divide the first quantity by the second:

> unit rate = (quantity A) ÷ (quantity B), in "A per B"

The phrase **per** means "for each one": miles *per* hour means miles for
each one hour. Always keep the units attached — a bare number is not a
rate.

When the ratio itself uses fractions, the unit rate is a **complex
fraction** — a fraction whose numerator or denominator is a fraction.
Divide exactly as you learned in Unit 01: multiply by the reciprocal.

**Worked example 1.** A recipe uses 3/4 cup of sugar for every 1/2 batch
of cookies. What is the unit rate in cups of sugar per batch?

Divide: (3/4) ÷ (1/2) = (3/4) × (2/1) = 6/4 = 3/2 cups per batch.

So the unit rate is **3/2 cups of sugar per 1 batch** (1.5 cups per
batch). Reason check: half a batch needs 3/4 cup, so a whole batch needs
twice that — 3/2 cups. The answer is bigger than 3/4, which makes sense.

**Worked example 2.** A painter earns $45 for 2.5 hours of work. What is
the hourly rate?

45 ÷ 2.5 = 18, so **$18 per hour**. Reason check: 2 hours at $18/hr is
$36, plus a half hour ($9) is $45. ✓

**Worked example 3.** A printer prints 5/6 of a page in 1/3 minute. What
is the printing rate in pages per minute?

(5/6) ÷ (1/3) = (5/6) × 3 = 15/6 = 5/2 = 2.5 pages per minute.
Reason check: in 1/3 minute it prints less than a page (5/6), so in a
full minute — three times as long — it prints about 2.5 pages. ✓

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** A cyclist rides 7/8 mile in 1/4 hour. Find the speed in miles per
hour.
*Hint: divide 7/8 by 1/4. The reciprocal of 1/4 is 4.*

**G2.** A 5/2-pound bag of rice costs $6. Find the unit price in dollars
per pound.
*Hint: 6 ÷ (5/2) = 6 × (2/5).*

**G3.** Water flows at 9 gallons every 2/3 minute. Find the rate in
gallons per minute. Then explain in one sentence why your answer is
larger than 9.

## Independent practice

**I1.** A hiker walks 4/5 mile in 1/6 hour. Speed in miles per hour?
**I2.** A recipe calls for 2/3 cup of oil for every 3/4 cup of vinegar.
Find cups of oil per cup of vinegar.
**I3.** A machine makes 150 parts in 2.5 hours. Parts per hour?
**I4.** 3/8 inch of rain falls in 1/4 hour. Inches per hour?
**I5.** A phone plan costs $27.50 for 5 GB of data. Dollars per GB?
**I6.** **Error analysis.** Maya says the unit rate for "2/3 mile in
1/6 hour" is (1/6) ÷ (2/3) = 1/4 mile per hour. What did she do wrong,
and what is the correct rate?

## Application

A trail mix recipe uses 1/2 cup of raisins for every 3/4 cup of peanuts.
You want to make a large batch with 3 cups of peanuts. Use a unit rate
to find how many cups of raisins you need, and show your reason check.

## Exit check

A car uses 3/5 gallon of gas to travel 1/4 of the way to grandma's
house. What is the gas mileage in miles per gallon? (Assume the whole
trip is 60 miles.) Explain what "per gallon" tells you here.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading access:** read each problem aloud and underline the two
  quantities and their units before computing.
- **Alternate response:** build the unit rate with a bar model — draw
  the given amount, then scale the picture to "1" of the second
  quantity.
- **Extension:** find the unit rate for "5/6 mile in 2/9 hour" two ways:
  with the reciprocal method and by scaling both quantities to whole
  numbers first. Show both give 15/4 mph.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: dividing in the wrong order (see I6) — learners
often put the "1" quantity first. Insist on the sentence frame "___
per ___" *before* dividing: the quantity after "per" is the divisor.
Another: dropping units, so "4" could mean anything — require units on
every answer from day one. Answer key: [../answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-01).
