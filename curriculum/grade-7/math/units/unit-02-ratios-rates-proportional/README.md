# Unit 02 — Ratios, Rates, and Proportional Relationships

**Grade:** 7 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #34](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/34), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A recipe that always tastes the same no matter the batch size, a store shelf
where the bigger box is not always the better deal, a graph that is a
straight line through the origin — this unit teaches the learner to compute
unit rates from fractional ratios, to **test** whether two quantities are
really proportional (equivalent ratios in a table; a straight-line graph
through the origin), and to name the constant of proportionality and write
the relationship as y = kx, interpreting what (0, 0) and (1, r) mean in
context.

## Essential question

How can you tell whether two quantities really change in the same proportion —
and what does the "per one" value tell you about everything else?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2), the grade 6 track, and Unit 01:

- Fraction/decimal equivalence and the four operations on non-negative
  fractions and decimals (U01 extended these to all rational numbers)
- Simplifying fractions; dividing by a fraction (multiply by the reciprocal)
- Reading values from tables; plotting ordered pairs on a coordinate plane
  (re-taught in Lesson 04, not assumed fluent)
- Writing a worked solution with a reason check (track routine)

No algebra beyond substitution is required. Equations of the form px + q = r
wait for Unit 04; here equations are only the form y = kx.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Compute unit rates from ratios of fractions, including ratios with
   unlike units, and state every result with correct units (track
   objective 4; 7.RP.A.1).
2. Decide whether two quantities are in a proportional relationship by
   testing for equivalent ratios in a table and by checking whether the
   graph is a straight line through the origin; distinguish proportional
   relationships from non-proportional ones and explain the verdict
   (track objective 5; 7.RP.A.2.a).
3. Identify the constant of proportionality (unit rate) in tables, graphs,
   equations, diagrams, and verbal descriptions; write the proportional
   relationship as an equation y = kx; explain what the points (0, 0) and
   (1, r) mean in context (track objective 6; 7.RP.A.2.b–d).

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**,
grade 7, Ratios and Proportional Relationships domain, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/Math](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/)
(codes and descriptions cross-checked against the official domain pages
during the A00 audit, 2026-10-05; no state adoption or accreditation
claimed). Descriptions below are paraphrases of the official wording.

| Unit objective | Standard | Official description (paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7.RP.A.1 | Compute unit rates associated with ratios of fractions, including ratios of lengths, areas, and other quantities measured in like or different units. |
| 2 | 7.RP.A.2.a | Decide whether two quantities are in a proportional relationship, e.g. by testing for equivalent ratios in a table or graphing on a coordinate plane and observing whether the graph is a straight line through the origin. |
| 3 | 7.RP.A.2.b | Identify the constant of proportionality (unit rate) in tables, graphs, equations, diagrams, and verbal descriptions of a proportional relationship. |
| 3 | 7.RP.A.2.c | Represent proportional relationships by equations (of the form y = kx in this unit). |
| 3 | 7.RP.A.2.d | Explain what a point (x, y) on the graph of a proportional relationship means in terms of the situation, with special attention to the points (0, 0) and (1, r) where r is the unit rate. |

Percent applications of proportional reasoning (7.RP.A.3) are Unit 03's
work; this unit builds the testing and constant-of-proportionality
machinery that Unit 03 relies on.

## Accessibility and safety

All activities are paper-based; the image activity ships with a text-only
data table carrying the same numbers. Graph work can be done by dictating
plotted points to the adult or by using the SVG's built-in data table.
No lab materials, no hazards, no accounts, no learner data leaves the
household. The project uses copied prices, never photographs of people or
store interiors.
