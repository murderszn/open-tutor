## Lesson 01 — Ratios and unit rates

**Readiness:** 1. 2/3. 2. (3/4) × (2/1) = 6/4 = 3/2. 3. 60 miles per hour.

**Guided:** G1. (7/8) ÷ (1/4) = (7/8) × 4 = 28/8 = 7/2 = **3.5 miles per
hour**. G2. 6 ÷ (5/2) = 6 × (2/5) = 12/5 = **$2.40 per pound**. G3.
9 ÷ (2/3) = 9 × (3/2) = 27/2 = **13.5 gallons per minute**; it is larger
than 9 because 2/3 minute is *less* than a minute, so the per-minute
rate must be bigger.

**Independent:** I1. (4/5) ÷ (1/6) = 24/5 = **4.8 miles per hour**. I2.
(2/3) ÷ (3/4) = 8/9 **cup of oil per cup of vinegar**. I3. 150 ÷ 2.5 =
**60 parts per hour**. I4. (3/8) ÷ (1/4) = 3/2 = **1.5 inches per hour**.
I5. 27.50 ÷ 5 = **$5.50 per GB**. I6. Maya divided the wrong way round —
she computed (time) ÷ (distance) instead of (distance) ÷ (time). Correct:
(2/3) ÷ (1/6) = (2/3) × 6 = **4 miles per hour**.

**Application:** Unit rate = (1/2) ÷ (3/4) = 2/3 cup raisins per cup
peanuts. For 3 cups peanuts: 3 × (2/3) = **2 cups raisins**. Reason
check: 3 cups is 4 × (3/4), so raisins scale by 4: 4 × (1/2) = 2 ✓.

**Exit:** 1/4 of the trip = 15 miles used 3/5 gallon. 15 ÷ (3/5) = 25
**miles per gallon**. "Per gallon" = the miles you get for each one
gallon of gas.

## Lesson 02 — Rates with unlike units

## Lesson 03 — Testing proportionality with tables

**Independent:** I1. 2.5, 2.5, 2.5 → **proportional**. I2. 4, 4.5,

**Application:** Plan A: 15, 15, 15 → **proportional**. Plan B: 20, 15,

**Exit:** Sitter 1: 12, 12, 12 → **proportional**. Sitter 2: 30, 20,

**Independent:** I1. **Proportional** (line through origin). I2. **Not

**Exit:** (a) Yes — 7 ÷ 2 = 3.5, 14 ÷ 4 = 3.5, 21 ÷ 6 = 3.5, all on one

## Lesson 05 — Constant of proportionality

**Guided:** G1. 28 ÷ 4 = 7; 42 ÷ 6 = 7; 63 ÷ 9 = 7 → **k = 7** (units: y

**y = 2.5x is steeper**; steeper means a larger unit rate (more y per
