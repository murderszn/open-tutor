# Project — Cedar Hollow Trade Fair

**Week 23 · S3 (investigation share, second half) · plus planning in Week 22, S4 and building in Week 23, S2 · Unit 05**

## What you will make

A pretend market stall at the **Cedar Hollow Trade Fair**: you are
a producer for a day. Your stall has four parts:

1. **A stall plan** — the good you produce, the three kinds of
   resources it needs, and what you specialize in.
2. **A price and profit card** — your price, your cost per item,
   your profit per item, and your profit if you sell 10 items.
3. **A trade** — you trade with one other stall (the adult plays a
   second stall, or a classmate does) and record what each side
   gave and got, and why both sides are better off.
4. **An interdependence map** — a drawing that shows your stall
   connected to at least two other places or stalls, with arrows
   labeled with what travels each way, plus one sentence about what
   would happen to your stall if one trade partner stopped trading.

## Materials

- Paper/poster board, pencils, crayons or markers (adult may scribe
  captions).
- Play coins and bills (or paper tokens) and play "goods" cards for
  the trade. No real money is used.
- The market illustration
  ([assets/cedar-hollow-market-day.png](assets/cedar-hollow-market-day.png))
  or its text-only alternative (Lesson 02), and the trade map
  ([assets/trade-map-cedar-hollow.svg](assets/trade-map-cedar-hollow.svg))
  or its text-only alternative (Lesson 06).
- The lemonade-stand card (Lesson 04) as a worked profit example.

## Steps

1. **Choose a good** (Week 22, S4): pick something a grade-3
   producer could make with simple resources — for example, paper
   bracelets (beads + string), apple slices in a bag (apples + bag),
   or bookmarks (card + ribbon). Tell the adult why you picked it
   in one sentence. The good must be something you can explain
   making, not something you would need a factory for.
2. **List the resources** (Week 23, S2): name at least one natural
   resource, one human resource (a worker and their skill), and one
   physical resource (a tool) your good needs. Check: does each
   resource really go into making the good?
3. **Set the price and work out the profit** (Week 23, S2): choose
   a price in whole dollars or simple cents (for example, $1.00).
   Work out the cost per item by adding its parts, then the profit
   per item (price − cost), then the profit for 10 items sold
   (10 × profit per item). Show all three steps on the card.
   *Worked example (fictional practice data):* paper bracelets —
   beads 30¢ + string 10¢ = cost 40¢ each. Price $1.00. Profit per
   bracelet $1.00 − $0.40 = $0.60. Profit for 10 bracelets:
   10 × $0.60 = $6.00.
4. **Make the trade** (Week 23, S2–S3): trade with the other stall.
   Record what you gave, what you got, and one sentence for each
   side saying why that side is better off. Check: did both sides
   gain? If one side would lose, change the trade — never force a
   match.
5. **Draw the interdependence map** (Week 23, S3): your stall in
   the middle, arrows to at least two other stalls or places
   (Sunnyvale, Hilltop, the Mill Town, or the other stall), each
   arrow labeled with what travels that way. Add one sentence:
   "If ___ stopped trading with me, ___ would happen to my stall."
6. **Present for 3 minutes** (Week 23, S3): read your stall plan,
   show your price and profit card, describe your trade, and show
   your map. The adult asks one question: "What would happen to
   your stall if your trade partner stopped trading?"

## Deliverables

- The stall plan (good, three resource kinds, specialization
  sentence).
- The price and profit card (price, cost per item, profit per item,
  profit for 10 items — all three steps shown).
- The trade record (what each side gave and got; why both sides
  are better off).
- The interdependence map (at least two labeled connections; one
  "if trading stopped" sentence).
- A 3-minute presentation (spoken, drawn, or both).

## Rubric (adult-scored; 12 points)

| Criterion | 3 — Strong | 2 — Developing | 1 — Starting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stall plan (3) | Good is makeable with simple resources; one natural, one human, one physical resource named correctly; specialization sentence explains the focus | Good is makeable; resources named but one kind is missing or mis-sorted | Good needs a factory or magic; resources missing or mostly wrong |
| Price and profit (3) | Price, cost per item, profit per item, and profit for 10 items are all correct, with all three steps shown | Math is right for the per-item profit but the 10-item total is missing or wrong | Price minus cost is confused with revenue, or the arithmetic is wrong in more than one step |
| Trade (3) | Both sides' gains are named and both sides are better off; the record shows what was given and got | A trade happened but only one side's gain is explained, or one side's gain is weak | No trade, or one side clearly loses and the learner did not notice |
| Interdependence map (3) | At least two labeled connections with correct directions; the "if trading stopped" sentence names a real effect on the stall | Two connections but a label or direction is wrong, or the sentence is vague ("it would be bad") | Fewer than two connections, or no "if trading stopped" sentence |

**Readiness signal:** 10–12 points with correct profit math means
the learner is ready for Unit 06's movement-and-perspectives work.
Below 7 points, or a wrong profit calculation, means re-teaching
Lesson 04's profit steps and Lesson 02's resource sorting before
the unit assessment.

## Safety and privacy

- Stalls use pretend goods and play money only; no real money is
  spent, and no real food is sold or shared.
- Presentations are shared only with the guiding adult and the
  learner's class group, never published from this track.
- All Cedar Hollow material is labeled fictional whenever it is
  displayed.
