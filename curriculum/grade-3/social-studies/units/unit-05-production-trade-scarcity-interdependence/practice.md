# Practice Sets — Unit 05

**Grade 3 Social Studies · Weeks 20–23.** The adult may scribe. Every
item is solved in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md). All Cedar Hollow
material is fictional practice material, not real economics. All
prices are fictional practice data.

## Week 20 — Set A: Scarcity, choice, incentives (after Lesson 01)

- **A1.** June has $5.00. She can buy a storybook for $5.00 OR a
  jump rope for $3.00 and keep $2.00.
  (a) Name one benefit and one cost of buying the book.
  (b) Name one benefit and one cost of buying the jump rope.
  (c) If June picks the jump rope, what is her opportunity cost?
- **A2.** Ana's bakery has 6 loaves this morning, and 10 customers
  each want one loaf. (a) Why is this scarcity? (b) What must the
  customers or Ana do because of it?
- **A3.** Sort each nudge into *positive incentive* or *negative
  incentive*, and tell what choice it nudges.
  (a) The library gives a sticker to every learner who reads five
  books this month.
  (b) The library charges a fine when a book is returned late.
  (c) Milo's stand gives a free cup to anyone who brings back five
  empty cups.
- **A4.** The class has $10.00. Choice A: buy 2 soccer balls at
  $5.00 each. Choice B: buy seeds and soil for the school garden
  for $6.00 and keep $4.00.
  (a) What is the total cost of Choice A? Show the multiplication.
  (b) How much money does Choice B keep?
  (c) If the class picks Choice B, what is the opportunity cost?
- **A5.** Error hunt: Sam says, "I picked the book, so I gave up
  nothing — I have the book!" What is wrong, and how do you know?
- **A6.** Name one positive incentive and one negative incentive
  from your own week, and tell what choice each one nudged. (There
  is no single right pair — your examples must be real and the
  nudged choice must match the incentive.)

## Week 21 — Set B: Resources, specialization, trade, money (after Lessons 02–03)

- **B1.** Sort each item into *natural resource*, *human resource*,
  or *physical resource*. Give one reason for two of your sorts.
  Wheat · baker · clay oven · wool · weaver · loom · water · flour
  mill.
- **B2.** Use the market illustration (Lesson 02) or its text-only
  alternative. For each stall, name the good it sells and one
  resource it uses.
  (a) Bakery stall. (b) Farm stand. (c) Weaving stall.
- **B3.** Milo buys 3 apples at 25¢ each and pays with a $1.00
  bill. (a) How much does he spend? (b) What is his change? Show
  the subtraction.
- **B4.** A loaf of bread costs $2.00. June pays with a $5.00 bill.
  (a) What is her change? (b) Check your answer by adding the
  change back to the price.
- **B5.** Ana wants a basket that costs $1.00. The basket-maker
  wants apples, not bread. Ana has bread.
  (a) Explain the barter problem in two steps.
  (b) Explain how money fixes it.
- **B6.** Rosa sells 2 pieces of cloth at $5.00 each. (a) How much
  money does she take in? (b) She spends it on 8 apples at 25¢
  each. How much did the apples cost? (c) How much money is left?

## Week 22 — Set C: Profit (after Lesson 04)

Milo's stand (fictional practice data): price $1.00 per cup; cost
per cup 40¢ (lemon 25¢ + sugar 10¢ + cup 5¢); profit per cup 60¢.

- **C1.** Milo sells 10 cups on a cool day. (a) Money in?
  (b) Total cost? (c) Profit? Show each step.
- **C2.** Milo sells 20 cups on a hot day. (a) Money in?
  (b) Total cost? (c) Profit? Show each step.
- **C3.** Milo raises his price to $1.50 a cup and sells 8 cups.
  (a) Money in? (b) Total cost? (c) Profit?
  (d) Did the higher price earn more profit than the hot day at
  $1.00 (C2)? Explain using the numbers.
- **C4.** Sugar gets expensive: 20¢ a cup instead of 10¢.
  (a) What is the new cost per cup? (b) What is the new profit
  per cup at a $1.00 price? (c) What is Milo's profit if he sells
  10 cups at the new cost?
- **C5.** Error hunt: Sam says, "Milo took in $20.00 on the hot
  day, so his profit is $20.00." What is wrong, and how do you
  know?
- **C6.** Milo can sell 10 cups at $1.00 (profit $6.00) or 16 cups
  at $0.75 a cup. Work out the money in, total cost, and profit
  for the 16-cup plan, and tell which plan earns more profit.

## Week 23 — Set D: Outside effects and interdependence (after Lessons 05–06)

- **D1.** The town builds a new park. Sort each effect into
  *buyer/seller (the deal itself)*, *external benefit*, or
  *external cost*. Give one reason for two of your sorts.
  (a) Children have a safe place to play.
  (b) The town pays builders to build the park.
  (c) The bakery across the street sells more bread to park
  visitors.
  (d) The house next door hears children shouting on Saturday
  mornings.
  (e) The town pays workers to mow and clean the park, using tax
  money from everyone in town.
  (f) Visitors enjoy the park.
- **D2.** A woodworking shop opens next to the school. (a) Name one
  external benefit of the shop. (b) Name one external cost of the
  shop. (c) Name one way to shrink the external cost.
- **D3.** Use the trade map
  ([assets/trade-map-cedar-hollow.svg](assets/trade-map-cedar-hollow.svg))
  or its text-only alternative (Lesson 06).
  (a) What does Cedar Hollow send to Sunnyvale?
  (b) What does Hilltop send to Cedar Hollow?
  (c) If Sunnyvale has a bad year for oranges, name two people or
  places in Cedar Hollow who feel it, and tell how each one feels
  it.
- **D4.** A pencil has wood, graphite (the black middle), rubber,
  and metal. (a) Why can't one person make a whole pencil alone?
  (b) What does the pencil show about interdependence?
- **D5.** The railroad station opened in Cedar Hollow in 1910
  (Unit 03 timeline). (a) Name one way the railroad changed trade
  in Cedar Hollow. (b) Name one group that was helped and one
  group that was hurt by the change.
- **D6.** Your class has a pet hamster, and its wheel squeaks
  during the next-door class's quiet reading time. (a) Is the
  squeak an external cost? Why or why not? (b) Name one fix, and
  tell what it changes.
