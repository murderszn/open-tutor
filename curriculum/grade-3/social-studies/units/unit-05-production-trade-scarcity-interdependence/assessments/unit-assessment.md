# Unit Assessment — Unit 05: Production, Trade, Scarcity, and Interdependence

**Grade 3 Social Studies · Week 23, S4 · 30 points · adult-scored.**
The adult may read questions aloud and scribe. All Cedar Hollow
material is fictional practice material; all prices are fictional
practice data. Every question is solved in
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md). Do not hand the answer key to
the learner.

Milo's stand (fictional practice data): price $1.00 per cup; cost
per cup 40¢ (lemon 25¢ + sugar 10¢ + cup 5¢); profit per cup 60¢.
Market prices (fictional): apple 25¢ · loaf of bread $2.00 · piece
of cloth $5.00.

## Section A — Ideas (8 pts, 2 pts each)

1. Scarcity means:
   (a) there is nothing left at all.
   (b) there is not enough of something for everyone to have all
   they want.
   (c) everything is free.
   (d) nobody wants anything.
   Circle the best answer.
2. A *positive incentive* is something that:
   (a) forces a person to choose.
   (b) makes a choice more attractive.
   (c) makes a choice less attractive.
   (d) has nothing to do with choices.
   Circle the best answer.
3. Why do people specialize? Write one sentence using the words
   *specialize* and *trade*.
4. What is the difference between barter and money? Write two
   sentences: one about barter, one about money.

## Section B — A choice, weighed (6 pts)

5. The class has $10.00. Choice A: buy 2 soccer balls at $5.00
   each. Choice B: buy seeds and soil for the school garden for
   $6.00 and keep $4.00.
   (a) Complete the chart with one benefit and one cost for each
   choice. (4 pts — 1 pt per cell)

   | Choice | One benefit | One cost |
   |---|---|---|
   | A: soccer balls | | |
   | B: garden | | |

   (b) If the class picks Choice B, what is the opportunity cost?
   (2 pts)

## Section C — Money and profit (8 pts)

6. June buys 2 loaves of bread at $2.00 each and pays with a $5.00
   bill. (a) How much do the loaves cost? (b) What is her change?
   Show both steps. (2 pts)
7. Milo sells 10 cups of lemonade on a cool day. (a) How much
   money does he take in? (b) What is his total cost? (c) What is
   his profit? Show all three steps. (3 pts)
8. Milo raises his price to $1.50 a cup and sells 8 cups.
   (a) How much money does he take in? (b) What is his total cost?
   (c) What is his profit? (d) Did the higher price earn more
   profit than the 20-cup hot day at $1.00 (profit $12.00)? Answer
   yes or no, and explain in one sentence. (3 pts)

## Section D — Outside effects and the trade web (8 pts)

9. The town builds a new park. Sort each effect into *external
   benefit* or *external cost*. (4 pts — 1 pt each)
   (a) Children have a safe place to play.
   (b) The house next door hears noise on Saturday mornings.
   (c) The bakery across the street sells more bread.
   (d) The town pays workers to mow and clean the park with tax
   money.
10. Use the trade map
    ([`../assets/trade-map-cedar-hollow.svg`](../assets/trade-map-cedar-hollow.svg))
    or its text-only alternative (Lesson 06).
    (a) What does Cedar Hollow send to Sunnyvale? (1 pt)
    (b) What does Hilltop send to Cedar Hollow? (1 pt)
    (c) If Sunnyvale has a bad year for oranges, explain in two
    sentences how Cedar Hollow feels it. (2 pts)
