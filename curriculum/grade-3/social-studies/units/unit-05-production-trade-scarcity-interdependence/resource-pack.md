# Resource Pack — Unit 05: Production, Trade, Scarcity, and Interdependence

**Goal (one sentence):** Give the guiding adult short,
grade-3-appropriate references and search routes that reinforce
scarcity and choice, incentives, resources and specialization,
trade and money, profit, external effects, and interdependence.
**Age fit:** grade 3 (ages 8–9); the adult previews every video and
co-views with the learner. Nothing here replaces the unit's own
lessons; videos and references supplement them.

**Fact-check record (2026-11-06):** the EconEdLink "Toys for Me"
lesson page was read 2026-11-06: it is an elementary lesson on
scarcity and choice ("This OR That, not This AND That"), with
opportunity-cost language matching Lesson 01. The EconEdLink
"Lemon Squeeze" lemonade-stand lesson was read 2026-11-06: it
builds supply and demand schedules around a lemonade stand and
names profit as the reason a seller supplies more at higher prices;
it is above grade 3 in full, so it is adult-side background for
Lesson 04 only. The Federal Reserve Education Kiddynomics page was
confirmed via the EconEdLink lesson's own link 2026-11-06: a
five-lesson curriculum for ages 3–5, so it is adult-side and
younger-band, useful only for its "Betty Bunny Wants Everything"
wants/scarcity read-aloud idea. The U.S. Mint Coin Classroom
introduction was read 2026-11-06: the Mint's education content is
targeted at kindergarten through grade 4, with games and lesson
plans about coins — it supports Lesson 03's money work. The
University of Nebraska Omaha Model Elementary Curriculum Guide
(K–6) was read 2026-11-06: its grade-2 benchmarks name
specialization ("people concentrate their production on fewer kinds
of goods and services than they consume") and interdependence
("greater specialization leads to increasing interdependence"),
matching Lessons 02 and 06 at the adult-planning level. C3
Framework codes are carried from the track audit's 2026-10-03
verification, not re-verified in this run. The unit's Cedar Hollow
prices, costs, and trades are original fictional practice data and
are never presented as real economic facts.

## Web references (reputable, free, no account)

1. **Council for Economic Education (EconEdLink) — "Toys for Me: A
   Lesson on Choice"** —
   https://econedlink.org/resources/toys-for-me/
   *Why good:* elementary lesson on scarcity and choice — wants
   must be prioritized, every choice has an opportunity cost —
   matching Lesson 01's language and the "This OR That" framing.
   *Read 2026-11-06; adult previews; free, no account.*
2. **Council for Economic Education (EconEdLink) — "Lemon Squeeze:
   The Lemonade Stand"** —
   https://preview2.econedlink.org/resources/lemon-squeeze-the-lemonade-stand/?view=teacher
   *Why good:* the lemonade-stand lesson behind Lesson 04's model:
   taste tests, supply and demand schedules, and the profit reason
   a seller supplies more at higher prices. *Read 2026-11-06;
   adult-side only — its schedules are above grade 3; the unit
   uses its own simpler profit model. The link is EconEdLink's
   preview mirror for this lesson page; adult previews.*
3. **Federal Reserve Education — Kiddynomics: An Economics
   Curriculum for Young Learners** —
   https://www.federalreserveeducation.org/teaching-resources/economics/curriculum/kiddynomics-an-economics-curriculum-for-young-learners-lessons-for-grades-pre-k-k
   *Why good:* five-lesson curriculum for ages 3–5 from the
   Federal Reserve's education program, including "Betty Bunny
   Wants Everything" on wants and scarcity. *Confirmed via
   EconEdLink's link 2026-11-06; adult-side only — younger band
   than grade 3; the adult borrows the read-aloud idea, not the
   lessons. Free, no account.*
4. **U.S. Mint — Introducing the U.S. Mint Coin Classroom** —
   https://www.usmint.gov/news/inside-the-mint/introducing-the-us-mint-coin-classroom
   *Why good:* the Mint's own introduction to its Coin Classroom —
   coins, coin production, and collecting for kindergarten through
   grade 4, with games and lesson plans — supporting Lesson 03's
   coin-counting and change work. *Read 2026-11-06; adult
   previews; free, no account.*
5. **University of Nebraska Omaha, Center for Economic Education —
   Model Elementary Curriculum Guide for K–6** —
   https://www.unomaha.edu/college-of-business-administration/center-for-economic-education/teacher-resources/curriculum-planning-guide.php
   *Why good:* adult-side planning benchmarks for the elementary
   grades, including specialization and interdependence benchmarks
   that match Lessons 02 and 06, and market-game ideas for buyers
   and sellers finding a price. *Read 2026-11-06; adult-side
   reference, not learner reading. Free, no account.*
6. **National Council for the Social Studies — C3 Framework
   standards page** — https://www.socialstudies.org/standards/c3
   *Why good:* the framework home for the grades 3–5 indicators

   verification; no state adoption or alignment certification is
   claimed.*
