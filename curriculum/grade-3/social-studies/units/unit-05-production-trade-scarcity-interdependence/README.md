# Unit 05 — Production, Trade, Scarcity, and Interdependence

**Grade:** 3 (ages 8–9) · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track issue:** [#21](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/21)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 20–23 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, 30–35 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

Why can't we have everything we want — and how do people still get
what they need? This unit answers that question with economics a
grade-3 learner can touch. Learners **compare the benefits and
costs of individual choices** and explain what a choice gives up
(D2.Eco.1.3-5); **identify positive and negative incentives** that
change what people choose (D2.Eco.2.3-5); **identify the human,
physical, and natural resources used to produce goods and services**
(D2.Eco.3.3-5); **explain why people and businesses specialize and
trade** (D2.Eco.4.3-5); **explain how money makes exchange easier**
(D2.Eco.5.3-5); **explain how profits influence sellers** by working
a real lemonade-stand model (D2.Eco.7.3-5); **identify external
benefits and costs** — effects on people who are not the buyer or the
seller (D2.Eco.8.3-5); and **explain how trade creates economic
interdependence** and how new connections change a place's economic
patterns (D2.Eco.14.3-5; D2.Geo.7, 11.3-5). The unit's project —
**Cedar Hollow Trade Fair** — asks the learner to run a pretend
market stall: choose a good to produce, name its resources, set a
price, compute the profit, trade with another stall, and draw the
web that connects the stalls to each other and to other places.

## The fictional town — read this first

**Cedar Hollow is a made-up town created for this track. It is not a
real place, and its market, lemonade stand, and trade partners are
practice material, not real history or real economics.** Every price,
cost, and trade in this unit is labeled *fictional practice data*
wherever it appears. The adult says this out loud at the start of
Week 20 and again whenever a new scenario is introduced: "We are
practicing economics on a pretend town, so that when we later look
at a real town, we already know how to ask good questions." The
numbers are small and round on purpose, so the learner's job is
economic reasoning, not hard arithmetic. Real-world economics in
this unit is limited to the Resource Pack references; no real town's
economy is described or invented here.

**Cedar Hollow market (fictional practice data):**

| Stall | Seller (fictional) | Sells | Made from / made with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bakery | Ana the baker | Loaves of bread, $2.00 each | Wheat (natural resource), flour mill and clay oven (physical resources), farmer, miller, baker (human resources) |
| Farm stand | Ben the farmer | Apples, 25¢ each; carrots and cabbage | Soil, sun, rain (natural resources), baskets and tools (physical resources), farmer (human resource) |
| Weaving stall | Rosa the weaver | Folded cloth, $5.00 a piece | Wool from Hilltop sheep (natural resource), wooden loom (physical resource), weaver (human resource) |

**Trade partners (fictional):** Sunnyvale, a warm town to the south
that grows oranges Cedar Hollow cannot grow; Hilltop, a high town
to the north that raises sheep for wool. Cedar Hollow trades bread
and apples for oranges and wool.

**Lemonade stand (fictional practice data, Milo's stand):** one cup
sells for $1.00. One cup costs 40¢ to make: lemon 25¢ + sugar 10¢ +
cup 5¢. Profit on one cup is 60¢ ($1.00 − $0.40).

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Compare** the benefits and costs of an individual choice and
   **explain** what is given up by choosing it — the opportunity
   cost (D2.Eco.1.3-5).
2. **Identify** positive and negative incentives in a scenario and
   **predict** how each incentive changes the choice a person makes
   (D2.Eco.2.3-5).
3. **Identify** the human, physical, and natural resources used to
   produce a good or service, and **explain** why people specialize
   in one kind of work and trade for the rest (D2.Eco.3, 4.3-5).
4. **Explain** how money makes exchange easier than barter, **count**
   money to $5.00, and **make change** for a purchase (D2.Eco.5.3-5).
5. **Compute** profit as price minus cost and **predict** how a price
   change or a cost change affects a seller's profit and choices
   (D2.Eco.7.3-5).
6. **Identify** external benefits and costs of a change — effects on
   people who are neither buyer nor seller — and **explain** how
   trade makes places depend on each other and how a new road,
   railroad, or market changes a place's economic patterns
   (D2.Eco.8, 14.3-5; D2.Geo.7, 11.3-5).

## Essential question

*We cannot have everything we want — so how do people decide, make,
trade, and depend on each other to get what they need?*

## Vocabulary

**Scarcity** — there is not enough of something for everyone to
have all they want. · **Want** — something you would like to have.
· **Need** — something you must have to live, learn, and stay
healthy (food, water, shelter, clothing). · **Good** — a thing you
can buy (bread, an apple, a cloth). · **Service** — work someone
does for you (baking, fixing, teaching). · **Producer** — a person
who makes a good or provides a service. · **Consumer** — a person
who buys or uses a good or service. · **Benefit** — a good thing
that comes from a choice. · **Cost** — what you give up, pay, or
lose because of a choice. · **Opportunity cost** — the best thing
you give up when you make a choice. · **Incentive** — something
that nudges a person toward or away from a choice (a reward, a
sale price, a fine). · **Resource** — something used to make a
good or provide a service: **natural resources** (things from
nature: wheat, wool, water, wood), **human resources** (workers and
their skills), **physical resources** (tools, ovens, looms, mills).
· **Specialize** — to focus on making one kind of good or doing
one kind of work, and trade for the rest. · **Trade** — to exchange
goods or services with someone. · **Barter** — trading a good or
service directly for another good or service, with no money. ·
**Money** — coins and bills that everyone accepts in exchange. ·
**Price** — the amount of money a good or service costs. ·
**Revenue** — all the money a seller takes in from sales. ·
**Profit** — the money left after a seller pays the costs:
profit = price − cost (for one item) or revenue − total cost. ·
**External benefit** — a good effect on people who are not the
buyer or the seller. · **External cost** — a bad effect on people
who are not the buyer or the seller. · **Interdependence** — places
and people depending on each other because each has something the
others need.

## Accessibility and safety

- All image tasks have a text-only alternative; the assessed skill is
  reasoning, never eyesight or handwriting — the adult may scribe.
- Money work uses play coins and bills or paper tokens; no real
  money changes hands in any activity, and no learner spends real
  money.
- The Trade Fair uses pretend goods and play money only. Any
  real-world market visit is adult-supervised, observation-only, and
  stays private — no learner names, photos, addresses, or schedules
  enter this repository.
- The lemonade-stand investigation is a paper model. A real
  lemonade stand is optional, adult-supervised, and outside this
  unit's assessed work; food-safety rules (clean hands, clean water,
  adult handling of any shared food) apply if the adult chooses it.
- No real learner names, photographs, schedules, or locations are
  published in this repository.
