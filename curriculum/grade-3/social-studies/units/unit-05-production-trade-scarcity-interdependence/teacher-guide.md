# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Production, Trade, Scarcity, and Interdependence

**For the guiding adult. Do not hand this guide or the answer key to
the learner.** Student pages are the lessons, practice sets, project,
and assessments. This guide covers session flow, likely
misconceptions, and reteach signals. All Cedar Hollow material is
fictional practice material — say so at the start of Week 20 and
whenever a new scenario appears. No real money is used in any
activity.

## Session-by-session guidance

### Week 20 — Scarcity, choice, incentives

- **S1 (Lesson 01):** Start with the fiction label. Keep the
  benefit/cost chart visible all week. The "what did you give up?"
  question is the unit's most reusable tool — use it whenever a
  choice is discussed.
- **S2 (Practice A):** Do not correct mid-task. Collect errors for
  the exit discussion; the most common error is calling the money
  spent the opportunity cost (A1, A5). Re-teach: the opportunity
  cost is the *best thing given up*, not the dollars.
- **S3 (Incentive detective):** Give the learner three short
  scenarios and ask for each: positive or negative incentive, and
  what choice it nudges. Use the library sticker, the late fine,
  and Milo's free-cup offer from practice set A, or invent local
  ones (a shop sale, a chore reward). Keep each scenario to two
  sentences.
- **S4 (Review):** Quick check: "Scarcity means…? Opportunity cost
  means…? Positive incentive means…?" Any hesitation on
  opportunity cost means repeating Lesson 01's worked example 1
  before Week 21.

### Week 21 — Specialization, trade, money

- **S1 (Lesson 02):** Read the AI-illustration label aloud. The
  bread chain (wheat → flour → bread) is the week's anchor — return
  to it whenever "who made this?" comes up. The Cannot-tell step
  from Unit 03 is reused in the application; do not skip it.
- **S2 (Practice B):** The resource sort (B1) is the week's hardest
  idea. The rule to repeat: "Natural = from nature; human = workers
  and skills; physical = tools and buildings." Money is *none* of
  the three — it is a trading tool, not a resource for making.
- **S3 (Lesson 03, investigation session):** This lesson is taught
  in the investigation slot because the trade simulation *is* an
  investigation. Use play coins throughout; let the learner hold
  and count them. Do not introduce the simulation until the two
  worked examples are done — the simulation tests the idea, it does
  not teach it.
- **S4 (Review):** Re-teach the barter problem with the basket
  example: two trades without money, one sale and one purchase
  with it. Any confusion between "money makes things" and "money
  buys things" means repeating the worked example 2 before Week 22.

### Week 22 — Profit

- **S1 (Lesson 04):** Keep the lemonade-stand card visible all
  week: price $1.00, cost 40¢, profit 60¢. Every profit question
  starts from one cup, then multiplies. The $1.50/8-cup example is
  the unit's key insight — let the learner be surprised by it.
- **S2 (Practice C):** Watch the arithmetic, not just the idea:
  8 × $1.50 = $12.00 and 8 × 40¢ = $3.20 are the two most common
  slips (C3). Insist on the two-step check: money in, then cost,
  then profit.
- **S3 (Lemonade-stand investigation):** Run the stand on paper:
  cool day (10 cups), hot day (20 cups), higher price (8 cups at
  $1.50), sugar price rise. Record revenue, cost, and profit in a
  three-row table for each case. A real lemonade stand is optional,
  adult-supervised, and outside the assessed work.
- **S4 (Quiz + project planning):** Give the quiz first, then let
  the learner choose a Trade Fair good. A learner who scores below
  7 on the quiz should still plan a project — but re-teach Lesson
  01 or 04 in Week 23, S2 before presenting.

### Week 23 — Outside effects, interdependence

- **S1 (Lesson 05):** The three-column chart is the lesson's core.
  Stress that costs are not only money: lost quiet and bad smells
  are real costs. The tax link (Unit 04) needs one explicit
  sentence: the park's upkeep is paid with taxes, so everyone pays
  a little.
- **S2 (Practice D + project building):** Check every project's
  profit card before the learner presents. A wrong profit card
  presented with confidence is harder to fix than a wrong one
  caught in building.
- **S3 (Lesson 06 + project share):** Teach Lesson 06's trade web
  and pencil trace first (about 15 minutes), then the Trade Fair
  presentations (about 10 minutes). Keep presentations to 3
  minutes. Ask one question per presentation: "What would happen
  to your stall if your trade partner stopped trading?"
- **S4 (Unit review + assessment):** Review with the market
  illustration and the trade map visible. Do not introduce new
  material in S4.

## Misconception watch (all weeks)

| Misconception | Correction move |
|---|---|
| Scarcity means nothing is left | Not enough for *all wants* — 6 loaves for 10 customers is scarcity with loaves still on the shelf (Lesson 01) |
| The best choice has no cost | Every choice gives something up; list costs first (Lesson 01) |
| Opportunity cost is the money spent | Opportunity cost is the *best thing given up* — the book, not the $5.00 (Lesson 01) |
| An incentive forces you | Incentives nudge; people still choose (Lesson 01) |
| The baker made the bread alone | Bread needs a chain: farmer, miller, baker, plus tools and nature (Lesson 02) |
| Trade means someone loses | A voluntary trade makes both sides better off — each gives what they have and gets what they want (Lesson 02) |
| Money is a resource for making bread | Money is a tool for *trading*; wheat, oven, and baker make bread (Lessons 02–03) |
| Barter is bad | Barter is not bad, only harder when wants do not match (Lesson 03) |
| $1.00 is 10 cents | A dollar is 100 cents — always count in cents first (Lesson 03) |
| Profit is the money taken in | Profit is money in *minus* cost; revenue is the money taken in (Lesson 04) |
| A higher price always means more profit | Fewer buyers can shrink the day's profit — $8.80 < $12.00 (Lesson 04) |
| Only money counts as a cost | Lost quiet, lost time, and bad smells are real costs (Lesson 05) |
| If the seller did not mean it, it does not count | Effects count whether or not they were intended (Lesson 05) |
| Depending on others is a weakness | Interdependence gives everyone more; the answer to risk is more partners, not no partners (Lesson 06) |
| One town makes everything in my home | The pencil trace proves it: four materials, four places (Lesson 06) |

## Readiness and reteach signals

- **Ready to move on (per session):** the learner states the
  session goal in their own words and passes the exit check.
- **Reteach Lesson 01 if:** the learner cannot name an opportunity
  cost or calls the money spent the opportunity cost after Week 20,
  S4.
- **Reteach Lesson 03 if:** the learner cannot make change from
  $1.00 for a 75¢ purchase or confuses barter with money after
  Week 21, S4.
- **Reteach Lesson 04 if:** the learner calls revenue the profit,
  or cannot work out the 10-cup profit, after Week 22, S2.
- **Reteach Lesson 05 if:** the learner cannot sort an effect into
  the three columns after Week 23, S2.
- **Assessment bands:** 26–30 ready for Unit 06; 20–25 re-teach the
  weakest part in the midyear-review catch-up time before Unit 06;
  below 20 re-teach Lessons 01, 03, and 04 in small steps before
  Unit 06.
- **Quiz bands:** 9–10 ready to move on; 7–8 re-teach the missed
  idea in Week 23, S2; below 7 re-teach Lessons 01 and 04 before
  the unit assessment.

## Differentiation

- **Reading/language access:** read all cards and scenarios aloud;
  use sentence starters from each lesson; accept oral, pointing,
  and drawing responses; scribe when writing is not the assessed
  skill.
- **Extension:** Lesson 01 (a real choice from the learner's week,
  with its own benefit/cost chart); Lesson 02 (trace a second good
  backwards); Lesson 03 (price three new goods and work out change
  from $5.00); Lesson 04 (15-cup day: $9.00 profit); Lesson 05
  (propose a fix for an outside cost and re-sort); Lesson 06 (add
  a fourth trade partner to the map).
- **Pacing:** if a session runs long, cut the extension, never the
  exit check. If the unit runs short, add a second incentive
  scenario in Week 20, S3 or a second product trace in Week 23, S3.
