# Lesson 04 — Places Change, and People Cluster

**Week 10 · S1 (concept launch) · 30–35 minutes · Unit 02**

## Student goal

*I can describe one way a place changed over time — and explain why people
cluster in some parts of a region and not others.*

## Materials

- Paper, pencil; two-column "THEN / NOW" chart.
- The dataset table below (from `resources/us_states.csv`, on `main`;
  values are **approximate practice data**, rounded for this activity):

| State | People per square mile* |
|---|---|
| New Jersey | ~1,065 |
| Illinois | ~221 |
| Montana | ~7 |
| Alaska | ~1 |

\*Column `population_density_per_sq_mi` — people per square mile, rounded to
whole numbers for this task.

## Readiness check (2 min)

"Think of the oldest building or oldest photograph you know from our
community. What is one thing that looks *different* about our community
today than in that old picture?" (New buildings, new roads, fewer fields —
anything showing change.)

## Teaching (explicit explanation, 10 min)

**Places change over time.** Both parts of a place can change:

- **Environmental characteristics change:** a forest is cleared for farms; a
  river is dammed and a lake appears; a marsh is drained and becomes dry
  land.
- **Cultural characteristics change:** a dirt path becomes a paved road; a
  general store becomes a row of shops; a small village becomes a busy town.

And the changes connect: when people **modify** an environment (Lesson 02),
the place changes — and the changed place then changes how people live.

**Population** means all the people who live in a place. **Distribution**
means where those people are — spread out or **clustered** (bunched
together).

Look at the dataset table. New Jersey has about **1,065 people per square
mile** — crowded. Alaska has about **1 person per square mile** — very
spread out. Why? Because **environmental and cultural characteristics shape
where people cluster**:

- People cluster where there is **work** (cities, ports, factories), **flat
  buildable land**, **mild climate**, and **water and resources** — the same
  three settlement reasons from Lesson 01, scaled up.
- People spread out where the land is **steep, very cold, very dry**, or far
  from jobs and services — or where the culture *wants* space, like big
  cattle ranches in Montana.

So distribution is not an accident: it is settlement thinking (water + land
+ resources + work) drawn across a whole region.

**Vocabulary in context:** *population* — all the people in a place.
*Distribution* — where people are clustered or spread out. *Cluster* —
bunched close together.

## Worked example 1 — Then and now (modeled reasoning, 5 min)

Adult draws the THEN/NOW chart and thinks aloud:

"**THEN (about 100 years ago):** a small farming village sits by a river —
ten houses, big wheat fields, one dirt road, everyone farms.
**NOW:** the same spot is a town — two hundred houses, shops along a paved
road, the fields are smaller, and a railroad runs past the river.

What changed, and why? *Environmental change:* some farmland became streets
and house lots. *Cultural change:* the railroad (physical capital!) brought
traders and jobs, so more families moved in — the population grew and
clustered near the station. Cause-and-effect chain: railroad built →
traders and jobs come → families move in → town grows → more houses and
shops replace fields."

"Notice the circle: people modified the place (railroad), and the modified
place changed people's lives (new jobs). That circle is the whole unit in
one story."

## Worked example 2 — Reading the density table (5 min)

Adult thinks aloud with the table:

"New Jersey: 1,065 people per square mile. Alaska: 1 person per square mile.
That is a *thousand-times* difference. Why?

New Jersey is small, flat-ish, near the ocean, with huge cities and ports —
work, water, flat land, mild climate: people cluster. Alaska is enormous,
with tall mountains, long freezing winters, and far fewer cities — the
environment is harsh and jobs are fewer: people spread out.

Now Montana (7) vs. Illinois (221). Montana has big open ranches and few big
cities — culture *and* environment spread people out. Illinois has Chicago,
flat farm country, and lake/river transport — work and water cluster people.
Same country, different distributions — because characteristics differ."

Then the learner's turn: "Which two states in our table are the *most*
crowded? Which is the *least*?" *(Most: New Jersey, then Illinois. Least:
Alaska.)* "Name one environmental reason Alaska is so spread out."
*(Mountains / long cold winters / huge empty areas — any one.)*

## Guided practice (with prompts/hints, 6 min)

**G1.** THEN/NOW: "A forest hillside (then) becomes terraced farm fields
(now)." Name one environmental change and one cultural change. Then give
the cause-and-effect chain in two links. *(Environmental: forest → fields.
Cultural: people now farm there / new farm village. Chain: people needed
more food → cut terraces → fields feed more families.)*

**G2.** "Two regions: Region X has a warm climate, a river port, and flat
farmland. Region Y is a high, dry plateau with no river. Where will people
cluster? Give two reasons." *(Region X — water + flat land + port jobs /
mild climate.)*

**G3.** Error hunt: "A classmate says, 'Alaska has few people because people
don't like Alaska.' Improve the explanation." *(Give environmental and
cultural reasons instead: vast mountains, very cold long winters, fewer
cities/jobs — and note some people love Alaska; 'few people' is about
characteristics, not liking.)*

## Independent practice (6 min)

**I1.** Draw a THEN/NOW pair for a made-up place: THEN — a riverside
fishing village (5 houses, boats, one dock). NOW — the same place 80 years
later (draw at least three changes). Label each change **E** (environmental)
or **C** (cultural) and write one sentence: "The biggest change happened
because…". *(Adult checks three changes labeled and a sensible because.)*

**I2.** "A new bridge is built across a wide river (like our valley
picture). Predict: will people cluster more on both sides now? Why?"
*(Yes — crossing is easy, so trade, visits, and building spread to both
sides; this is modification → distribution.)*

## Application — Our community's distribution (4 min)

With the adult: "Where do people cluster in our community — near what
street, river, station, or shops? Where is it emptiest? Does that match our
three settlement reasons?" (Adult records; feeds the project.)

## Exit check

"Name one way a place can change over time, and explain in one sentence why
New Jersey is more crowded than Alaska." (Success: a real change +
environmental/cultural reason for the density difference.)

## Supports

- **Reading/language access:** adult reads the table aloud; "1,065 vs. 1"
  can be shown with dot clusters (many dots vs. one dot) for a visual feel
  of density.
- **Alternate response mode:** THEN/NOW may be drawn only; the "because"
  sentence may be dictated.
- **Extension:** "Find our own state's row in `resources/us_states.csv`
  (columns: `name_common`, `population_density_per_sq_mi`). Where does our
  state sit between Alaska (1) and New Jersey (1,065) — and does its
  environment explain it?" (Dataset task: name columns and units aloud.)

## Misconceptions to watch

- **"Crowded = bad, spread out = good."** Distribution is not a grade —
  cities cluster people near work; ranches spread people near land. Each
  fits its characteristics.
- **"Change only goes one way (small → big)."** Towns can also shrink when a
  mine closes or a river shifts — change is not always growth.
- **Reading the table as exact census counts.** These are rounded
  *approximate* practice numbers; real census figures move every year.
