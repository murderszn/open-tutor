# Lesson 02 — Adapting to the Environment and Modifying It

**Week 8 · S1 (concept launch) · 30–35 minutes · Unit 02**

## Student goal

*I can tell the difference between adapting to an environment and modifying
it — and I can explain one cause and one effect of a modification.*

## Materials

- Paper, pencil; the valley illustration from Lesson 01 (for the closing
  re-look).
- Two-column chart: **ADAPT** | **MODIFY** (adult draws it).

## Readiness check (2 min)

"Last week we learned people build near water, flat land, and resources.
But what if the environment is *not* comfortable — too cold, too wet, too
steep? What are two things people could do?" (Accept anything; sort answers
later into the two columns.)

## Teaching (explicit explanation, 10 min)

When the environment does not fit people perfectly, people do one of two
things.

**Adapt** means *changing yourself* so the environment works for you. You do
not change the place — you change how you live in it.

- It is freezing outside → you wear a heavy coat. (You changed; the cold
  stayed cold.)
- The sun is fierce → you build houses with thick walls and small windows.
- The ground floods every spring → you build your house on tall stilts.

**Modify** means *changing the environment* so it works better for you. You
change the place.

- The river blocks your road → you build a **bridge**. (The river changed;
  you can now cross.)
- The land is too steep for farming → you cut flat **terraces** (steps) into
  the hillside.
- Water does not reach your fields → you dig a **canal** or an irrigation
  ditch.
- Floods threaten the town → you build a **levee** (a raised wall of earth)
  along the river.

Here is the key idea for the whole week: **every modification has effects,
and effects have their own effects.** Geographers call these **cause-and-
effect chains**. A cause is *why something happened*; an effect is *what
happened as a result*.

Example chain: *Cause:* the town builds a bridge over the river → *Effect:*
people and goods cross easily → *Further effect:* more traders visit →
*Further effect:* the town grows and needs more houses.

Modifications can also have effects people did *not* want. A levee stops a
flood (wanted) but can also make flooding worse downstream (unwanted). Good
thinking means asking: "What might happen *next*?"

**Vocabulary in context:** *adapt* — change how you live. *Modify* — change
the place. *Cause* — why it happened. *Effect* — what happened.

## Worked example 1 — Sort it (modeled reasoning, 5 min)

Adult reads each; thinks aloud while placing it in the ADAPT or MODIFY
column:

1. "A family in a hot desert builds a house with thick mud walls that stay
   cool inside." → "Did the family change the desert? No — they changed
   their *house* to fit the desert. **ADAPT.**"
2. "Farmers dig a long ditch to carry river water to dry fields."
   → "Did they change themselves or the land? They changed the land — water
   now flows somewhere new. **MODIFY.**"
3. "A town on a river builds a tall earth wall (a levee) so spring floods do
   not wash through the streets." → "The river and the land changed — the
   water is blocked. **MODIFY.**"
4. "Children in a snowy town wear boots, mittens, and heavy coats to walk
   to school." → "The snow is still there — the children changed what they
   wear. **ADAPT.**"
5. "People cut steps (terraces) into a steep hill and plant rice on each
   flat step." → "The hill itself was reshaped. **MODIFY.**"

Tricky-note (say it out loud): "A warm coat is adapt; a heated house starts
to look like modify — heating changes the air *inside*, but the outside
environment is untouched. When it is unclear, ask: *did the place itself
change?*"

## Worked example 2 — A cause-and-effect chain (5 min)

"Let's trace one modification all the way." Adult draws:

**The town digs a canal from the river to dry fields.**
→ *Effect 1:* fields get water, so farmers grow more food.
→ *Effect 2:* with more food, more families can live in the town — the
   settlement grows.
→ *Effect 3 (maybe unwanted):* the river downstream has less water, so the
   next town's boats sit low and its fishermen catch less.

"Notice: the same canal has a good effect for one town and a bad effect for
another. That is why geographers ask *'and then what?'* — twice."

Then the learner's turn with a hint: "The town builds a **bridge** across
the river (like the one in our valley picture). Give me one effect." *(People
cross easily.)* "And then what?" *(More visitors/traders; the town grows;
people on both sides visit each other.)*

## Guided practice (with prompts/hints, 6 min)

**G1.** Sort into ADAPT or MODIFY: *build a dam · wear rain boots · plant
crops that need little water · pave a road over a marsh · build houses on
stilts · dig a well*. *(Modify: dam, pave road, dig well. Adapt: rain boots,
drought-tough crops, stilts.)* Hint for "dig a well": "Did the ground
change, or did the person change?"

**G2.** Error hunt: "A classmate says a levee is adapting to floods. Is the
classmate right? Fix the sentence." *(Wrong — a levee changes the river's
path, so it is modifying. Correct: 'Building a levee is modifying the
environment to hold back floods; moving houses to high ground would be
adapting.')*

**G3.** Build a chain: "Farmers cut terraces into a steep hill." Give one
effect, then one further effect. *(Effect: they can farm flat steps and grow
more food. Further: more food feeds more people / the town has food to
trade. Accept any sensible two-link chain.)*

## Independent practice (6 min)

**I1.** Write or dictate two of your own examples — one adapt, one modify —
about a place that gets *very heavy rain*. *(E.g., adapt: wear raincoats,
build steep roofs so rain runs off; modify: dig drainage ditches, build a
dam.)*

**I2.** Pick one modification from G1. Write its cause-and-effect chain with
*three* links (modification → effect 1 → effect 2). *(Adult checks the links
connect logically.)*

## Application — Re-look at the valley (4 min)

Show the Lesson 01 illustration again: "Find one modification the people in
this valley already made." *(The wooden footbridge; the fenced field; the
water wheel; the boats.)* "Find one adaptation." *(Accept: houses clustered
on the flat land near the river — settling where the environment already
suits people is a kind of adapting; or: the field's crops are planted where
soil and water are.)* "What is one effect of the bridge?" *(People/goods
cross; trade grows.)*

## Exit check

"Tell me one way to adapt to a cold place and one way to modify a dry
place — and for the modification, one effect." (Success: adapt = change
people/clothes/houses; modify = change the place; effect named.)

## Supports

- **Reading/language access:** adult reads everything aloud; the two-column
  chart is drawn large and kept visible all week.
- **Alternate response mode:** sorting can be done by pointing or moving
  word cards; chains can be dictated.
- **Extension:** "Find a real modification in our community (a bridge, a
  dam, a drained field, a leveled hill). Who wanted it, and what is one
  wanted and one unwanted effect?" (Feeds the Week 8 S3 investigation.)

## Misconceptions to watch

- **"Modifying is bad and adapting is good."** Neither is automatically
  good or bad — a bridge helps everyone cross; a dam can flood a forest.
  Judge each by its effects.
- **"An effect is always the end."** Effects become causes of new effects —
  insist on the "and then what?" second link.
