# Teacher Guide — Unit 02

**For the guiding adult.** Student materials never contain answers; all
solutions live in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## Session-by-session guidance

**Week 7 — Settlement.** S1: teach Lesson 01 with the valley illustration —
read the AI-illustration label aloud the first time; the think-aloud in
Worked example 1 *is* the lesson, so perform it slowly and point. S2:
practice set A; insist that every reason is tied to something visible (no
"because it's nice"). S3: the local inquiry — walk or use photos: "Where is
our water? Is our land flat? What resource is nearby?" Record answers; they
return in the project. S4: review + vocabulary check (settlement,
environment, natural resource); keep the learner's ideal-settlement sketch
for the project.

**Week 8 — Adapt/modify.** S1: Lesson 02 — keep the two-column chart posted
all week; the "did the place itself change?" test resolves every sort
dispute. S2: practice set B; B1's rice paddies are deliberately borderline —
credit either sort *with a reason*. S3: the modification investigation —
learner picks one real local modification (bridge, dam, leveled hill,
drained field) and traces two effects with you. S4: review; re-teach the
two most-missed sorts before the quiz week.

**Week 9 — Resources.** S1: Lesson 03 — the three boxes stay posted next to
last week's chart; run the bread chain with real word cards if you have
them. S2: practice set C; C3 (money) is the week's key insight — don't rush
it. S3: the community resource map — a supervised walk is best; the
photograph-set alternative works when a walk isn't possible (take 6–8 photos
of local resources beforehand). S4: formative quiz (12 pts), then project
work time (scouting + resource plan). Record quiz gaps — they set the Week
10 S4 review agenda.

**Week 10 — Change + distribution.** S1: Lesson 04 — the density table lands
best with the dot-cluster visual (many dots vs. one dot). Name the CSV
column and units aloud; stress *approximate*. S2: practice set D; D5 is the
self-check — let the learner find their own gaps, then target them. S3:
project share — 3-minute presentations, two adult follow-up questions each;
score with the rubric the same day. S4: review driven by quiz + D5 gaps,
then the unit assessment (30 pts).

## Misconception watch (all lessons)

- **Environment doesn't matter anymore (pipes/trucks).** Counter: water,
  food, and materials still come *from* environments; most big cities still
  sit on rivers, coasts, or lakes. The link changed shape; it didn't vanish.
- **Modifying = bad, adapting = good.** Neither is automatic — judge each by
  its effects (Lesson 02, G-tasks).
- **Money is a resource kind.** Money *buys* resources; it isn't natural,
  human, or physical capital (Lesson 03, C3).
- **An effect is the end of the story.** Effects become causes — always ask
  "and then what?" twice (Lessons 02, 04).
- **Crowded = bad / spread out = good; change = growth.** Distribution fits
  characteristics; towns shrink too (Lesson 04).
- **The density table is exact.** It's rounded, approximate, and moves
  yearly — the *pattern* (NJ ≫ AK) is the lesson, not the digits.

## Readiness and re-teaching signals

- **Ready to move on:** the learner names the three settlement reasons
  unprompted, sorts adapt/modify at ≥80%, and names all three resource kinds
  with an example each (quiz + D5).
- **Re-teach settlement reasons:** return to Lesson 01's Worked example 1;
  re-do it with the learner narrating and the adult pointing.
- **Re-teach adapt/modify:** replay the "did the place itself change?" test
  on five new examples; keep the chart visible another week.
- **Re-teach resource kinds:** re-run the bread chain with the learner
  placing the cards; then the chair chain from memory.
- **Re-teach distribution:** re-draw the dot clusters; compare only two
  states (NJ vs. AK) until the pattern is solid.
- **Quiz below 8/12:** re-teach the missed objective's lesson before Week
  10 S4; use parallel practice items, not the same ones.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** scribe everything; accept pointing/drawing/dictation; pre-
  teach vocabulary with the picture cues; shorten independent practice to 3
  items (keep the error hunts — they're the highest-value items).
- **Extension:** the per-lesson extensions (desert-town comparison; local
  modification field study; pencil-factory trace; own-state CSV lookup);
  plus: "Design a settlement for the *desert* half of Unit 01's picture —
  what must you adapt, what must you modify, and what breaks first?"

## Pacing notes

The 16 sessions assume ~30 minutes. If a session runs long, move its
overflow into the next S2 (practice sessions absorb spillover by design).
Never cut S4 reviews — they are where the adult records gaps. The project
steps are deliberately spread across four weeks; do not let them pile into
Week 10.
