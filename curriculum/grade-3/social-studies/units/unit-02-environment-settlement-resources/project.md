# Project — Plan a Settlement

**Unit 02 investigation · Weeks 7–10 · about 2.5 hours total, in small
pieces · presented in Week 10, S3**

## The challenge

You are the founder of a brand-new town. Using the valley map below (or the
[`river-valley-settlement.png`](assets/river-valley-settlement.png)
illustration as your valley), choose the **best site** for the town, explain
your choice with evidence, and present your plan to the adult.

## Materials

- The valley illustration (AI-generated teaching picture — a made-up
  valley, not a real place) *or* a hand-drawn valley map with: a river, a
  forest, hills/mountains, and an area of flat land.
- Paper, pencil/crayons; the three resource boxes from Lesson 03.
- The adult (audience for the presentation; may scribe).

## The valley (text alternative to the picture)

A river curves through a valley. On the **right**, a tall dark-green forest
covers a hillside. In the **distance**, gray rocky mountains rise with snowy
tops. The land near the river is **flat and grassy**. There is already a
small wooden footbridge and a fenced farm field on the flat land. Two wooden
boats float on the river, and a water wheel turns beside it.

## Steps

1. **Scout (Week 7, S3–S4, ~20 min).** Study the valley. Mark **three
   candidate sites** for the town (A, B, C) — for example: by the river bend,
   at the forest edge, on the flat grass near the field.
2. **Weigh (Week 8, S2–S3, ~20 min).** For each site, list: water? flat
   land? resources nearby? Then answer: does this site need people to
   **adapt**, or would it need them to **modify** the environment? Pick your
   winner and write down **why the other two lost** (one reason each).
3. **Resource plan (Week 9, S3–S4, ~30 min).** For your chosen site, name
   **three resources** the town will use — one natural, one human, one
   physical capital — and say what each one is *for*. Then name **one
   modification** the town will build (bridge, well, terrace, levee…) and
   trace its cause-and-effect chain with two links.
4. **Draw the plan (Week 10, S1–S2, ~30 min).** Draw your settlement on the
   valley: houses, the modification, farms or docks — with a title, labels,
   and a key (Unit 01 map habits return). Mark where people will cluster
   and where they will stay spread out, with one reason.
5. **Present (Week 10, S3, ~10 min).** Present the plan orally (3 minutes):
   the site, the three reasons, the three resources, the modification and
   its effects. The adult asks two follow-up questions.

## Deliverables

1. The three-site scouting notes (water / land / resources for A, B, C +
   why two lost).
2. The resource plan (one natural + one human + one physical-capital
   resource with uses; one modification + two-link effect chain).
3. The drawn settlement map (title, labels, key; cluster/spread-out marked
   with a reason).
4. The 3-minute oral presentation.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Good | 2 — Developing | 1 — Starting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Site choice with evidence** (10 pts) | Site chosen; **three** environmental reasons, each tied to something visible on the map | Three reasons, one vague or untied to the map | Two reasons only | One reason or no map evidence |
| **Why others lost** (6 pts) | Both rejected sites have a specific environmental reason | One rejected site explained well | Rejections are vague ("bad") | No comparison |
| **Three resource kinds** (10 pts) | One natural, one human, one physical-capital resource, each with a clear *use*; all sorted correctly | All three named and sorted, one use vague | One kind mis-sorted or missing | Two or more kinds missing |
| **Modification + effects** (8 pts) | One real modification; **two** logical effect links ("and then what?" twice) | Modification + one effect link | Modification with no effects | No modification |
| **Map** (4 pts) | Title, labels, key; cluster/spread-out marked with a reason | Two of the three map parts | Map drawn, parts missing | No usable map |
| **Presentation** (2 pts) | Clear 3-minute talk covering site, reasons, resources, effects | Covers most parts | Reads notes only / under 1 minute | No presentation |

**Scoring:** 36–40 exceeds · 30–35 meets · 24–29 approaching (re-teach the
weak criterion with its lesson) · below 24 — redo the scouting and resource
plan with adult support before the unit assessment.

## Adult notes

- The assessed skills are **reasoning and evidence use**, not drawing skill
  or handwriting — scribe freely.
- Accept any defensible site: a forest-edge site defended with timber +
  water-carrying reasoning can outscore a riverside site with weak reasons.
- If the learner's modification has an unwanted effect, credit it — that is
  Lesson 02 thinking at its best.
- Keep the map and notes: they are portfolio evidence for the track's final
  review (Week 36).
