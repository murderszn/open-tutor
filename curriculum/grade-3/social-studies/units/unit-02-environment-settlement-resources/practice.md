# Practice Sets — Unit 02

Short, purposeful practice after each week's lesson. Every item has a worked
solution in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md). The adult may scribe; the
assessed skill is the thinking, not the handwriting.

## Week 7 — Settlement reasons (after Lesson 01)

**A1.** In [`river-valley-settlement.png`](assets/river-valley-settlement.png)
(or its text-only alternative in `assets/README.md`): list **five** things in
the picture that helped the town grow there, and name the need each one meets
(water, flat land, or a resource). *(River: water/drink/fish/travel. Flat
grassy land: building/farming. Forest: timber. Mountains: stone. Fenced
field: farm soil/food. Boats: fishing/travel. Bridge: crossing. Water wheel:
river power. Any five with sensible needs.)*

**A2.** "A traveler can build one cabin: (a) beside a clean stream in a flat
meadow, or (b) on a windy, rocky hilltop with no water. Which is the better
settlement spot, and why — two reasons?" *(a — water nearby; flat land for
building.)*

**A3.** True or false, with a reason: "A settlement needs flat land more
than it needs water." *(False — people can terrace slopes or build on hills,
but everyone needs water daily; water is the harder need to replace.)*

**A4.** Error hunt: "A classmate points at the mountains in the picture and
says, 'Nobody would ever settle near mountains — they're useless.' Write
two sentences correcting the classmate." *(Mountains give stone and timber;
people settle near them for mining, forests, and water from mountain
streams — the town in the picture uses the mountains' stone.)*

**A5.** Draw and label: your Lesson 01 ideal-settlement sketch is missing
its labels. Add arrows and label **water**, **flat land**, and **one natural
resource**. *(Adult checks all three present.)*

**A6.** "Long ago, towns grew along rivers because there were no water
pipes. Today cities have pipes. Does that mean rivers don't matter to cities
anymore? Explain in two sentences." *(They still matter: drinking water
comes from rivers/lakes, rivers carry goods, and most old river cities are
still big — pipes loosened the link; they didn't erase it.)*

## Week 8 — Adapt vs. modify; cause and effect (after Lesson 02)

**B1.** Sort into **ADAPT** or **MODIFY**: *build houses on tall stilts in a
floodplain · dig an irrigation ditch · wear a sun hat in the desert · cut a
road through a hill · plant rice in flooded paddies · build a dam across a
river*. *(Adapt: stilts, sun hat. Modify: ditch, road, paddies are
borderline — accept MODIFY (reshaped land) or ADAPT (crop choice) with a
reason; dam.)*

**B2.** "A town builds a **levee** along its river." Write the chain with
three links: modification → one wanted effect → one unwanted effect.
*(Wanted: streets stay dry in floods. Unwanted: floodwater pushes harder
downstream / the river can't spread rich soil on fields. Accept sensible.)*

**B3.** Error hunt: "A classmate says, 'Wearing boots in the rain is
modifying the environment.' Fix it in one sentence, then give a real
modification for a rainy place." *(Wrong — boots change the person, so it's
adapting. Real modification: dig drainage ditches / build covered walkways.)*

**B4.** Two effects, one modification: "Farmers cut **terraces** into a
steep hill." Name one effect that helps the farmers and one effect that
could hurt the hill's wildlife. *(Helps: flat steps to grow food. Hurts:
forest cleared — animals lose homes; accept sensible.)*

**B5.** "The valley town builds a second, bigger bridge." Give the
cause-and-effect chain with *two* further effects ("and then what?" twice).
*(Crossing is easy → more traders visit → town grows / more houses built.
Accept any logical two-link chain.)*

**B6.** Which is it — and why does it matter? "A desert town builds its
houses half-underground to stay cool." *(Adapt — the desert is unchanged;
the people changed how they shelter. It matters because adaptations don't
risk unwanted effects on the place the way modifications can.)*

## Week 9 — Three kinds of resources (after Lesson 03)

**C1.** Sort into **NATURAL / HUMAN / PHYSICAL CAPITAL**: *coal · miner's
skill · mine elevator · wheat · farmer · tractor · river · fishing boat ·
captain's knowledge of good fishing spots*. *(Natural: coal, wheat, river.
Human: miner's skill, farmer, captain's knowledge. Physical capital: mine
elevator, tractor, fishing boat.)*

**C2.** "A town makes wool sweaters." Build the three-box chain: one
natural, one human, one physical-capital resource. *(Natural: sheep/wool,
grass, water. Human: shepherd's/shearer's/knitter's skill. Physical capital:
shears, looms, knitting machines, shop building.)*

**C3.** Error hunt: "A classmate sorted *money* as a fourth kind of
resource. Explain in two sentences why money is not one of the three
kinds." *(Money buys resources but is none of the three: it isn't from
nature, it isn't a skill, it isn't a tool for making things.)*

**C4.** Mixed-up chain: "Trees → sawmill → tables." Name the kind of each:
*trees, the sawmill workers' skills, the saws, the sawmill building, the
tables*. *(Trees: natural. Workers' skills: human. Saws + building: physical
capital. Tables: product — which becomes a resource when sold.)*

**C5.** Look at the valley illustration again. Find **one physical-capital
resource** the town built and explain which natural resource it depends on.
*(Bridge → depends on the river / timber; water wheel → river's moving
water; boats → river + timber; fenced field → soil. One pair with the link
explained.)*

**C6.** "A settlement has rich soil and skilled farmers but no tools — not
even a plow." What is missing, and what breaks in the bread chain?
*(Physical capital is missing; planting/harvesting/grinding/baking at scale
breaks — the human and natural resources can't do the job alone.)*

## Week 10 — Change over time; distribution (after Lesson 04)

**D1.** THEN/NOW in words: "A riverside village (then): 8 houses, a dock,
corn fields. The same place (now): 300 houses, shops, a paved road, a
railroad bridge, fewer fields." Name **two** environmental changes and
**two** cultural changes. *(Environmental: fields → house lots/streets;
riverbank changed by the bridge. Cultural: dirt path → paved road; village
→ town with shops; railroad added.)*

**D2.** Using the Lesson 04 density table (NJ ~1,065; IL ~221; MT ~7; AK
~1 people per square mile): "Which state would feel most crowded to visit?
Which would feel emptiest? Give one environmental reason for each."
*(Most crowded: New Jersey — small, flat-ish, coastal cities. Emptiest:
Alaska — vast, mountainous, long cold winters.)*

**D3.** "A new dam makes a lake where a valley village stood. The villagers
move to higher ground and build a new town." Is the lake an environmental
change, a cultural change, or both? Explain. *(Both: the landscape changed
— water where land was (environmental) — because people built the dam
(cultural/modification).)*

**D4.** Predict with reasons: "A gold mine opens in dry, empty hills. What
happens to the population distribution nearby over ten years?" *(People
cluster near the mine — jobs pull workers; shops, houses follow; a
settlement grows where almost nobody lived — distribution follows work +
resources.)*

**D5.** Self-check: without looking, write the three settlement reasons, the
adapt/modify difference in one sentence each, and the three resource kinds
with one example each. *(Adult checks against Lessons 01–03; gaps drive the
S4 review.)*

**D6.** Error hunt: "A classmate says, 'Places only change when they get
bigger.' Give a counterexample." *(A mining town shrinking when the mine
closes; a village abandoned when its river dries — change isn't always
growth.)*
