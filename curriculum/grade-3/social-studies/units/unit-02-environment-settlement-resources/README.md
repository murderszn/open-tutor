# Unit 02 — Environment, Settlement, and Resource Use

**Grade:** 3 (ages 8–9) · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track issue:** [#21](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/21)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 7–10 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, 30–35 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

Why do people build towns where they do? This unit answers that question
with evidence. Learners **explain** how environmental characteristics —
fresh water, flat land, climate, and natural resources — shape where
settlements grow (D2.Geo.8.3-5); **distinguish** adapting to an environment
from modifying it and **explain** probable causes and effects of one
modification (D2.Geo.4.3-5; D2.His.14.3-5); **identify** natural, human, and
physical-capital resources in a production chain (D2.Eco.3.3-5); and
**explain** how places change over time and how environmental and cultural
characteristics shape where people cluster (D2.Geo.5.3-5; D2.Geo.6.3-5). The
unit's project — **Plan a Settlement** — asks the learner to choose a site
for a new town on a valley map, justify it with three environmental reasons,
name three kinds of resources it will use, and present the plan. The
settlement-reasoning habit and the cause-and-effect chain return in Units 03,
05, and 08.

## Prerequisites (developmental expectations; re-taught here as needed)

- Read a simple map with title, key, symbols, labels, and a compass rose;
  name the four cardinal directions (Unit 01; diagnostic Weeks 1–2).
- Describe a place's environmental characteristics (heat, rain, landforms,
  plants/animals) from maps and photographs (Unit 01, Lesson 03).
- Sort objects by one or two attributes; explain a choice with one or two
  reasons in speech or 2–3 written sentences (grade-2 entry expectations).
- Weigh a simple benefit/cost choice (grade-2 entry expectations; the
  production-chain work in Lesson 03 leans on this).

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Explain** with map and photograph evidence how three environmental
   characteristics (fresh water, flat land, a natural resource) shape where
   people build settlements (D2.Geo.8.3-5).
2. **Distinguish** adapting to an environment from modifying it, and
   **explain** one probable cause and one probable effect of a human
   modification (D2.Geo.4.3-5; D2.His.14.3-5).
3. **Identify** natural, human, and physical-capital resources in a simple
   production chain (e.g., bread: wheat, oven, baker) and **explain** how a
   settlement uses each kind (D2.Eco.3.3-5).
4. **Describe** one way a place changed over time and **explain** why people
   cluster in some areas of a region and not others (D2.Geo.5.3-5;
   D2.Geo.6.3-5).

## Essential question

*Why do people build towns where they do — and what happens when people and
places change each other?*

## Vocabulary

**Settlement** — a place where people live and build homes together (a
village, town, or city). · **Environment** — everything around a place:
water, land, air, weather, plants, and animals. · **Natural resource** —
something from nature that people use (water, trees, stone, soil, fish). ·
**Human resource** — the work and skills people bring (a baker, a builder, a
teacher). · **Physical capital** — tools, machines, and buildings people use
to make things (an oven, a boat, a bridge). · **Adapt** — to change how you
live so an environment works for you (wear warm clothes in the cold). ·
**Modify** — to change the environment itself (dig a canal, build a bridge).
· **Population** — all the people who live in a place. · **Distribution** —
where people are spread out or clustered; who lives where. · **Cause** — why
something happened. · **Effect** — what happened as a result. · **Climate** —
the usual weather of a place over a long time.
