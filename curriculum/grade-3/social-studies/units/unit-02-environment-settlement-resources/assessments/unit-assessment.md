# Unit Assessment — Unit 02

**30 points · about 30 minutes · adult may scribe · covers all four
objectives**

Every task is solved in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md). Open responses
carry sample reasoning and acceptable variation. This is a **draft
classroom assessment, not a standardized or validated test.**

## Part 1 — Settlement reasoning (8 pts)

**1. (3 pts)** Study
[`river-valley-settlement.png`](../assets/river-valley-settlement.png).
A new family wants to build their house in this valley. Give them **three**
pieces of advice about *where* to build, each tied to something visible in
the picture.
*(Text-only alternative: wide blue river, two wooden boats, footbridge, six
red-roofed houses on flat grass, fenced crop field, water wheel, dark-green
forest on the right hill, snowy gray mountains behind. Sample: build on the
flat grass near the river (flat land + water); stay close to the forest for
firewood (resource); don't build on the steep mountain slope (hard to build,
far from water). Any three sensible, picture-tied.)*

**2. (2 pts)** "Two empty valleys. Valley A: a slow river, flat meadows,
and a forest. Valley B: steep rocky slopes, no river, thin soil." Which
valley gets the town? Give **two** environmental reasons.
*(Valley A — water from the river; flat land for building/farming; forest
for timber. Any two.)*

**3. (3 pts)** "A mining town grows in dry, rocky hills because a rich
copper mine is found there." Does this *break* the settlement rule (water +
flat land + resources)? Explain in two or three sentences.
*(No — it follows the rule's deeper logic: the valuable resource (copper)
outweighs the missing water and flat land, so people adapt (haul water,
terrace the slope) or modify (dig wells, build roads). The rule predicts
*trade-offs*, not a ban.)*

## Part 2 — Adapt, modify, cause and effect (8 pts)

**4. (2 pts)** Sort each as **ADAPT** or **MODIFY**, with one phrase of
reasoning:
a) A town paves a road across a marsh.
b) Farmers plant a rice variety that survives floods.
*(a) MODIFY — the marsh itself was changed. (b) ADAPT — the farmers changed
their crop choice; the floods continue.)*

**5. (3 pts)** "A village digs an irrigation canal from the river to dry
fields." Draw or write the chain with **three** links: the modification →
effect 1 → effect 2.
*(Sample: canal dug → fields watered, more food grown → more families fed,
village grows / surplus traded. Accept any three logical links.)*

**6. (3 pts)** "A coastal town builds a **seawall** to stop storm waves."
Name one wanted effect and one unwanted effect, then finish: "The town
should also think about…" (one further consequence).
*(Wanted: streets/houses protected from waves. Unwanted: beach in front of
the wall washes away / waves hit harder next door. Further: where the water
goes instead / who pays to maintain it. Accept sensible.)*

## Part 3 — Resources (7 pts)

**7. (3 pts)** "A fishing village." Name **one natural**, **one human**, and
**one physical-capital** resource the village uses, and say what each is
for.
*(Natural: fish / seawater. Human: fishers' knowledge of good spots.
Physical capital: boats / nets / dock. Each with a use.)*

**8. (2 pts)** A classmate says: "The *most important* resource kind is
natural resources, because the other two come from nature too." Do you
agree? Take a position and defend it in two or three sentences.
*(Either position accepted if defended. Sample agree: without nature's
materials there is nothing to work with. Sample disagree: materials are
useless without skilled people and tools — a forest is not lumber until
someone with a saw arrives. Credit the reasoning, not the side.)*

**9. (2 pts)** "A bakery's delivery truck breaks down for a week." Which
resource kind broke, and which link of the bread chain fails?
*(Physical capital broke; the delivery/transport link fails — bread can't
reach stores. Accept: distribution link.)*

## Part 4 — Change and distribution (7 pts)

**10. (3 pts)** THEN (60 years ago): "Milltown: one paper mill on the river,
40 houses, a dirt main street." NOW: "Milltown: the mill closed 20 years
ago, 15 houses, a quiet paved street, an old stone bridge." Describe **one**
environmental change and **one** cultural change, and explain in one
sentence why the town shrank.
*(Environmental: riverbank/mill site changed; fewer houses on the land.
Cultural: mill closed, main street quieter. Shrinking: the jobs left, so
families moved away — distribution follows work.)*

**11. (2 pts)** Density table (approximate people per square mile): New
Jersey ~1,065 · Illinois ~221 · Montana ~7 · Alaska ~1. "A family wants wide-
open space and few neighbors. Which state fits best, and give one
environmental reason?" *(Alaska (or Montana) — vast land, mountains/long
winters/few cities keep people spread out. Either with a real reason.)*

**12. (2 pts)** In two or three sentences, answer the unit's essential
question: *"Why do people build towns where they do — and what happens when
people and places change each other?"*
*(Sample: people build where water, flat land, resources, and work meet;
then they adapt to what they find and modify what doesn't fit — and every
modification changes the place, which changes people's lives in turn.
Credit any answer naming settlement reasons AND the two-way change.)*

## Scoring

27–30 exceeds · 22–26 meets · 17–21 approaching (re-teach the weak objective
with its lesson, then re-assess with parallel items) · below 17 — revisit
Lessons 01–04 guided practice before the project share.
