## Lesson 01 (guided + independent)

- **G1.** River → water (drink, fish, travel). Fenced field → flat farm soil
  (food). Forest → timber (building, firewood). Mountains → stone
  (building). *Reasoning: each visible feature is matched to a daily need.*
- **G2.** Accept: trees are a resource to harvest, not ground to waste;
  building among tall trees is dark and hard; flat open land near water is
  easier to build and farm on. *Reasoning: settlement uses resources
  without destroying the source unnecessarily.*
- **G3.** Evidence: the six houses sit on flat grass by the river (not on
  the mountains); the crop field is on flat soil by the water; the bridge
  and water wheel show people positioning themselves to *use* the river.
  *Reasoning: placement itself is the evidence.*
- **I1.** Check three labeled arrows: water, flat land, one natural
  resource. *Reasoning: labels prove the learner transferred the three
  reasons to a new sketch.*
- **I2.** Must name two environmental reasons (e.g., "near the river for
  water" + "flat ground for houses"). *Accept dictated sentences.*

## Lesson 02

- **G1.** Modify: dam, paved road, dug well (the ground/land changed).
  Adapt: rain boots, drought-tough crops, stilts (the people changed how
  they live). *Note on "dig a well": the ground changed → modify, even
  though the purpose is to get water.*
- **G2.** Wrong: a levee reshapes the river's path → **modify**. Corrected
  sentence: "Building a levee is modifying the environment to hold back
  floods; moving houses to higher ground would be adapting." *Reasoning:
  the test is always 'did the place itself change?'*
- **G3.** Sample: terraces cut → farmers can plant on flat steps and grow
  more food → the extra food feeds more families (or is traded). *Credit any
  two links that connect logically.*
- **I1.** Rainy place — adapt examples: raincoats, steep roofs, raised
  floors. Modify examples: drainage ditches, dams, covered walkways.
  *Credit any two, correctly sorted.*
- **I2.** Three-link chain, e.g., dam built → river held back, steady water
  → fields watered year-round → bigger harvests feed a growing town.
  *Check that each link follows from the previous one.*

## Lesson 03

- **G1.** Natural: sunlight, river water, timber. Human: teacher, nurse's
  skill, truck driver. Physical capital: school building, bridge, hammer.
  *Reasoning: nature-given → natural; skill/work → human; built-to-make →
  physical capital.*
- **G2.** The oven was built by people to bake with → physical capital. The
  fire inside it being "natural" is irrelevant to what the *oven* is.
- **G3.** Natural: timber. Human: builders' skills. Physical capital: saws,
  hammers (accept: the finished houses as built shelter). *Reasoning: each
  box filled with the right kind.*
- **I1.** Check the sort logic for the chosen product, e.g., pencil: wood

  capital). *Don't grade product facts; grade the sorting.*
- **I2.** Natural: timber/stone. Human: builders' skills. Physical capital:

## Project rubric — scoring notes

- **Site choice (10):** 4 = three map-tied reasons; 3 = three reasons, one
  vague; 2 = two reasons; 1 = one/no evidence.
- **Why others lost (6):** scale 4/3/2/1 per rubric; credit specific
  environmental rejections.
- **Resource kinds (10):** all three kinds correctly sorted with uses = full;
  deduct per mis-sort or missing kind.
- **Modification + effects (8):** full = real modification + two logical
  links; unwanted effects earn credit, not penalties.
- **Map (4):** title + labels + key + cluster/spread-out reason.
- **Presentation (2):** covers site, reasons, resources, effects.
