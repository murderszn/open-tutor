**For the guiding adult only.** Every item below was solved independently

## Lesson 01 — Seven continents and five oceans

*Guided practice*
1. Atlantic. The ocean east of North America is the Atlantic Ocean.
2. Antarctica; the Southern Ocean surrounds it.
3. No — imaginary. It marks Earth's middle; places near it get strong,
   direct sun all year.
4. Accept any landmark-based answer, e.g., "west of the Pacific, east of
   Europe."
5. Flat map (all at once); globe (true round shape).
*Exit check:* the seven names in any order: Africa, Antarctica, Asia,
Australia, Europe, North America, South America. A flat map cannot show
Earth exactly because Earth is round — flattening stretches or squishes
something.

## Lesson 02 — Map scale

*Guided practice*
1. Accept the learner's own words for "each map-centimeter stands for the
   stated real distance."
2. Method: map cm × scale value. (Adult checks the multiplication.)
3. Neighborhood map. The country map has no room for a street at its scale.
4. State map — it shows both cities and the roads between them; the
   neighborhood map cannot show the other city.
5. Tell them: small-scale maps cannot show every small thing; the house is
   too tiny to fit at country scale — the map is not wrong, the job is
   different.
*Exit check:* 4 cm × 2 km = **8 km**. The block map shows more detail
(large scale = small area, big detail).

## Lesson 03 — Location and environment

*Guided practice*
1. Dry clues (any three): dunes, one small pond, few plants, camel, bright
   bare sun. Wet clues (any three): river, dense layered trees, ferns,
   macaw.
2. Amazon: near the equator. Sahara: north of the equator, northern Africa.
3. Strong sun + lots of rain → rainforest. Strong sun + almost no rain →
   desert.
4. Corrected: "The Sahara gets very little rain, but oases hold fresh
   water, and camels, fennec foxes, and about 1,200 plant species live
   there — life adapts to dryness." (Britannica Kids, checked 2026-10-10.)
5. It is an AI-generated teaching illustration, not a photograph; it shows
   typical features, not one real place. Real deserts also include rock
   plains, mountains, and towns.
*Exit check:* both get strong sun; the difference is rain — steady rain
near the equator makes the Amazon wet and green; almost no rain makes the
Sahara dry and sandy. Full credit names heat AND water.

## Lesson 04 — Asking geographic questions

*Guided practice*
1. (b) — needs evidence and explanation.
2. Accept: "How do people in the Sahara get food in such a dry place?" or
   equivalent why/how question needing evidence.
3. Accept any on-topic smaller question (travel, food, homes, school,
   work in the rainforest).
4. "What is the biggest desert?" is a fact lookup. Improved: "Why did the
   Sahara become a desert while the Amazon stayed wet?" — asks for reasons.
5. Questions tell you what evidence to look for.
*Exit check:* a compelling question needs evidence and has no one-word
answer. Accept any on-topic supporting question (e.g., "What do desert
animals drink?").
