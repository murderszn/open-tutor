# Resource Pack — Unit 01: Maps, Regions, Continents, and Geographic Inquiry

**Goal (one sentence):** Give the guiding adult short, grade-3-appropriate
videos, references, and search routes that reinforce the seven continents
and five oceans, map scale, the Sahara/Amazon region comparison, geographic
questions, and the four U.S. regions.
**Age fit:** grade 3 (ages 8–9); the adult previews every video and
co-views with the learner.

**Fact-check record (2026-10-10):** continent/ocean names verified against
[Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/assembly/view/148401)
("Earth has seven continents: Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Australia (Oceania),
Europe, North America, and South America") and
[NOAA](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/howmanyoceans.html) (five
recognized oceans: Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic, Southern/Antarctic) —
both opened 2026-10-10 during the sibling Grade 2 U01 delivery. Sahara facts
verified against [Britannica Kids "Sahara"](https://kids.britannica.com/kids/article/Sahara/346205)
(checked 2026-10-10: largest hot desert on Earth, ~3.32M sq mi, northern
Africa, hot days and cool nights, very little rain). Amazon facts verified
against the Learn Bright rainforest video (opened 2026-10-10: world's
largest rainforest, nine South American countries, tropical, four forest
layers). U.S. regions verified against the Census Bureau system as
summarized by the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/hus/sources-definitions/geographic-region.htm)
(opened 2026-10-10): four regions — Northeast, Midwest, South, West.

   for young children; matches Lesson 01's naming goal. **Caveat:** the song
   says "Oceania" and "the Antarctic" — the unit teaches **Australia** and
   the **Southern Ocean** per Britannica/NOAA; the adult names the

## Web references

1. **NOAA — "How many oceans are there?"** —
   https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/howmanyoceans.html
   *Why good:* the authoritative source for the unit's five-ocean list
   (Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic, Southern); short, plain language.
   Best for the adult's background and for answering "why five?" *Opened
   and verified 2026-10-10; free, no account.*
2. **Britannica Kids — "Continents" (Students level)** —
   https://kids.britannica.com/students/assembly/view/148401
   *Why good:* states the seven-continent list the unit teaches. *Opened
   and verified 2026-10-10; free content visible without login.*
3. **National Geographic Education — "Map" (reference)** —
   https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/map/
   *Why good:* explains what maps are, symbols/keys, and scale — the adult
   background for Lessons 01–02. General-audience reading level: adult
   reads and adapts; not assigned to the learner. *Opened and verified
   2026-10-10; free, no account.*
4. **CDC — "Geographic division or region"** (summarizes the U.S. Census
   Bureau system) —
   https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/hus/sources-definitions/geographic-region.htm
   *Why good:* short, plain page listing the four Census regions and which
   states sit in each — the exact system behind the dataset's `subregion`
   column and Lesson 05's sort. Adult background. *Opened and verified
   2026-10-10; free, no account.*
5. **Britannica Kids — "Sahara" (Kids level)** —
   https://kids.britannica.com/kids/article/Sahara/346205
   *Why good:* Kids reading level states the Sahara is the largest hot
   desert on Earth, ~3.32M sq mi in northern Africa, with hot days, cool
   nights, and rare rain — the unit's desert facts. The adult may read
   the introduction aloud. *Checked 2026-10-10; free content visible
   without login.*
6. **Easy Science for Kids — "Facts about Sahara Desert for kids"** —
   https://easyscienceforkids.com/sahara-desert-facts-for-kids-video/
   *Why good:* kid-written-level fast facts (only two permanent rivers —
   the Nile and the Niger; dunes up to 590 feet; goats and camels as the
   most domesticated animals) plus an embedded kids' video. Adult previews
   for age fit. *Checked 2026-10-10; free, no account.*
