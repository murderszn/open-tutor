# Project — Region Explorer Report

**Type:** investigation with a built product · **Time:** Weeks 5–6 (about
3 sessions plus project share) · **Materials:** paper or poster board,
crayons/markers, a ruler, the unit's generated illustration, a U.S. map or
world map, the `us_states.csv` dataset (adult side)

## The mission

You are a **region explorer**. Pick **one region** — one of the four U.S.
regions (Northeast, Midwest, South, West) or one world region you studied
(the Sahara region or the Amazon region). Build a **Region Explorer report**
with four parts:

1. **A scaled map** of one place in your region (a state, a city, or a
   landmark area): title, key, compass rose, labels, and a scale bar.
2. **An environment description** (4–6 sentences): heat, rain, plants,
   animals — each claim backed by a piece of evidence (map, photograph,
   video, or the unit illustration).
3. **Your questions**: the compelling question from Lesson 04 (or a new one
   about this region) plus two supporting questions.
4. **A "why it is like this" explanation** (2–3 sentences): how the region's
   location connects to its environment.

## Steps

1. **Choose** your region (adult approves) and one place inside it.
2. **Ask**: write your compelling + supporting questions first — they steer
   the investigation.
3. **Gather**: collect at least two pieces of evidence (map + one more:
   photograph, video, dataset row, or illustration observation).
4. **Map**: draw the scaled map. Measure twice; label everything.
5. **Describe**: write the environment description, citing each piece of
   evidence ("I know it is dry because…").
6. **Explain**: write the location → environment explanation.
7. **Share**: present the report to the adult (3 minutes: map, one
   environment claim with evidence, your compelling question).

## Deliverables

- One labeled, scaled map (all five map parts).
- One environment description (4–6 sentences, evidence-cited).
- One compelling question + two supporting questions.
- One location → environment explanation (2–3 sentences).

## Rubric (adult scores; 12 points)

| Criterion | 3 — Strong | 2 — Developing | 1 — Starting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Map (scale habit) | All five parts; scale bar readable and used for one estimate | Parts present; scale bar decorative or estimate missing | Missing 2+ parts |
| Environment description | 4–6 sentences; every claim tied to evidence | Sentences present; some claims lack evidence | Few sentences; no evidence |
| Questions | Compelling (why/how, needs evidence) + 2 on-topic supporting | Compelling but vague, or supporting questions wander | Fact lookups only |
| Location → environment | Names location AND rain/heat to explain the environment | Names one of the two | No connection made |

**Safety/accessibility:** all work is desk-based; no field trip required. The
adult scribes on request; drawing, pointing, and oral presentation are full
response modes. Learners who chose the Sahara or Amazon reuse the Lesson 03
text-only alternative instead of the picture if needed.
