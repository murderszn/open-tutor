# Unit 01 — Maps, Regions, Continents, and Geographic Inquiry

**Grade:** 3 (ages 8–9) · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track issue:** [#21](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/21)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 3–6 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, 30–35 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

This unit builds the geographic thinking the rest of the year reuses: naming and
locating Earth's **seven continents** and **five oceans**; reading and using
**map scale** to move between neighborhood, state, and country views of the same
place; explaining how a place's **location** (including latitude and distance
from the equator) connects to its **environmental characteristics**; asking a
**compelling geographic question** with supporting questions; and describing the
four **U.S. Census regions** (Northeast, Midwest, South, West) by sorting real
state data into regions with map evidence. The unit's project — a **Region
Explorer report** — asks the learner to map one place at scale, describe its
environment with evidence, and explain how location connects to environment.
The scale habit and the region-comparison routine return in Units 02, 05, 06,
and 08.

## Prerequisites (developmental expectations; re-taught here as needed)

- Read a simple map with title, key, symbols, labels, and a compass rose;
  name the four cardinal directions (grade-2 entry expectations; diagnostic
  Weeks 1–2 probed these).
- Sort and compare objects by one or two attributes; describe familiar places
  orally and in writing (2–3 sentences).
- Multiply and divide by 10 and 100 within grade-3 math expectations
  (used for simple scale-bar estimating, with adult support).
- Dictate sentences for an adult to scribe; draw and label with clear intent.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Name** Earth's seven continents and five oceans, **locate** them on a
   blank outline map, and **describe** how two different world maps show the
   same Earth differently (D2.Geo.1.3-5).
2. **Use** a map scale (scale bar) to compare the same place at different
   scales and **estimate** a real distance from a map measurement
   (D2.Geo.3.3-5).
3. **Explain** with maps, photographs, and the unit's illustration how a
   place's location relates to its environmental characteristics — heat, rain,
   and plant/animal life (D2.Geo.2.3-5).
4. **Ask** a compelling geographic question and at least two supporting
   questions about a world region (D1.2.3-5).
5. **Describe** the four U.S. Census regions and **sort** states into regions
   using the dataset's `subregion` column and map evidence; **explain** why
   environmental characteristics vary among world regions (D2.Geo.10.3-5).

## Essential question

*Why are some places hot and dry while others are wet and green — and how can
a map help us find out?*

## Vocabulary

**Continent** — one of Earth's seven very large pieces of land. · **Ocean** —
one of Earth's five very large areas of water. · **Equator** — the imaginary
line around Earth's middle; places near it get strong, direct sun all year.
· **Latitude** — how far north or south of the equator a place is. ·
**Map scale** — what a map uses to show that it is much smaller than the real
place (for example, "1 centimeter = 10 kilometers"). · **Scale bar** — the
little ruler-like drawing on a map that shows distance. · **Region** — an area
of land whose places share something in common, like location, climate, or
landforms. · **Desert** — a dry region that gets very little rain. · **Oasis**
— a fertile spot in a desert where fresh water is available. · **Rainforest**
— a very wet forest near the equator with dense trees and lots of wildlife.
· **Canopy** — the top layer of a rainforest's trees. · **Compelling
question** — a big, interesting question that needs evidence to answer. ·
**Supporting question** — a smaller question that helps answer a compelling
question.
