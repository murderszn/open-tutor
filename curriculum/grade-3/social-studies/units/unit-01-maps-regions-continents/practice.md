# Practice Sets — Unit 01

Short, purposeful practice after each week's lessons. Every item has a worked
solution in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md). The adult may scribe; the assessed
skill is the thinking, not the handwriting.

## Week 3 — Continents, oceans, and map parts

**A1.** Write the seven continent names from memory, then check against the
globe and fix any spelling. (Success: all seven, correctly placed on the
outline map.)

**A2.** "I am thinking of an ocean. It touches North America on the west and
Asia on the east." Which ocean? What is the biggest ocean? *(Pacific)*

**A3.** Label the equator on your outline map. Then answer: is Brazil
(Amazon) near the equator or far from it? Is Egypt (Sahara) north or south
of the equator? *(near; north)*

**A4.** Error hunt: a classmate's map labels Australia as "Oceania" and the
Southern Ocean as "the Antarctic Ocean." The unit teaches **Australia** and
the **Southern Ocean** — write one sentence explaining the difference to
your classmate. *(Both names describe the same places; we use Australia and
Southern Ocean.)*

**A5.** Globe vs. flat map: list one job each tool does best, and one thing
each one cannot do. *(Globe: true shape; cannot show all at once. Flat map:
whole Earth at once; stretches/squishes something.)*

## Week 4 — Map scale

**B1.** A map's scale bar says `1 cm = 5 km`. The library and the school are
6 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they really? *(30 km)* Show the
multiplication.

**B2.** Same place, three maps: you need to find your street. Which map do
you use — neighborhood, state, or country? You need to drive to another
city in your state. Which map now? Explain each choice. *(neighborhood;
state — match the scale to the job)*

**B3.** True or false, with a reason: "A small-scale map shows a small area."
*(False — small-scale = large area, little detail.)*

**B4.** Measure-then-estimate: on the state map (adult gives the scale),
measure two cities and estimate the real distance. Record: map cm → scale →
real distance. *(Adult checks arithmetic; assessed skill is the method.)*

**B5.** Finish your scaled map of a familiar place: title, key, compass rose,
labels, and a scale bar. Trade with the adult: can they use your scale bar
to estimate one real distance on your map? *(Peer-check of the scale habit.)*

## Week 5 — Location, environment, and questions

**C1.** Evidence sort: put each clue under **Sahara** or **Amazon** — *sand
dunes; scarlet macaw; oasis; river; camel; canopy; fennec fox; daily rain*.
*(Sahara: dunes, oasis, camel, fennec fox. Amazon: macaw, river, canopy,
daily rain.)*

**C2.** "Two places both get strong sun. One is wet and green; the other is
dry and sandy." In one or two sentences, explain how that is possible.
*(Sun is only half the story — rainfall decides; near-equator steady rain
vs. dry sinking air.)*

**C3.** Compelling or check? Label each: (a) "How many oceans are there?"
(b) "Why does the Amazon get so much rain?" (c) "What is the capital of
Brazil?" (d) "How do people in the Sahara find water?" *(Checks: a, c.
Compelling: b, d.)*

**C4.** Take your compelling question from Lesson 04. List the two sources
you would check first and what you hope each one tells you. *(e.g., a map —
where the region is; a video — what daily life looks like)*

**C5.** Text-only alternative practice (for the illustration): without
looking at the picture, describe the desert half and the rainforest half
from the written description in Lesson 03. *(Checks the text alternative
carries the same information.)*

## Week 6 — Regions and review

**D1.** Sort: Maine, Texas, California, Ohio, Florida, Oregon. Write the
region for each and the dataset's `subregion` value you used. *(Maine:
Northeast; Texas: South; California: West; Ohio: Midwest; Florida: South;
Oregon: West.)*

**D2.** "The dataset's `region` column says 'Americas' for every state. Can
you use it to find the U.S. region?" *(No — use the `subregion` column.)*

**D3.** One-sentence region portraits: write one environmental sentence for
each of the four regions (heat/rain/landforms). *(Adult checks against
Lesson 05.)*

**D4.** Retrieval: name the seven continents and five oceans again, from
memory, in under two minutes. *(Spaced retrieval of Week 3.)*

**D5.** Unit self-check: which objective (1–5 in the unit README) feels
strongest? Which needs one more practice round before the assessment?
*(Learner rates; adult plans the Week 6 S3 review around the answers.)*
