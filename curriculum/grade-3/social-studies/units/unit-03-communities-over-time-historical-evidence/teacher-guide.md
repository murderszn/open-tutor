# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Communities over Time and Historical Evidence

**For the guiding adult. Do not hand this guide or the answer key to
the learner.** Student pages are the lessons, practice sets, project,
and assessments. This guide covers session flow, likely
misconceptions, and reteach signals. All Cedar Hollow material is
fictional practice material — say so at the start of Week 11 and
whenever a new source appears.

## Session-by-session guidance

### Week 11 — Timelines

- **S1 (Lesson 01):** Start with the fiction label. Keep the six
  event cards visible all week. The "what had to exist first?"
  question is the unit's most reusable tool — use it whenever
  ordering stalls.
- **S2 (Practice A):** Do not correct mid-task. Collect errors for
  the exit discussion; the most common error is swapping 1895 and
  1910 (mill vs. station). Re-teach with the cause chain: workers
  need jobs (mill) before goods need fast transport (station).
- **S3 (Personal timeline investigation):** Use only adult-approved,
  private family events (first day of school, a move, a new sibling).
  Nothing from this activity enters the repository or the exhibit.
  If the learner has no shareable events, use the life of a pet or
  a plant instead — the skill is sequencing, not biography.
- **S4 (Review):** Quick check: shuffle the six cards and ask for
  first/last/just-before/just-after. Any hesitation on 1895 vs. 1910
  means repeating S2 before Week 12.

### Week 12 — Then and now

- **S1 (Lesson 02):** Stress the year on every "then" claim. If the
  learner says "long ago people...," ask "Which year do you mean?"
  until the habit forms.
- **S2 (Practice B):** Watch for one-sided answers ("everything was
  worse"). The application task's two-sided rule applies to all
  comparisons this week.
- **S3 (Then/now comparison task):** Require one cited source per
  row of the chart — the illustration, a lesson fact, or a practice
  source. "I just know" is not evidence.
- **S4 (Review):** Re-teach the "past was not one time" point with
  the 1880 vs. 1950 contrast: same town, 70 years apart, very
  different daily life.

### Week 13 — Sources

- **S1 (Lesson 03):** Read the AI-illustration label aloud. The
  Cannot-tell step is the lesson's core — do not skip it for time.
  Learners who master Cannot-tell rarely fall for "one picture
  proves everything" later.
- **S2 (Practice C):** The primary/secondary sort (C2, C6) is the
  week's hardest idea. The rule to repeat: "It depends on the
  question." A 1995 label is secondary for 1895 facts and primary
  for 1995 opinions.
- **S3 (Lesson 04, investigation session):** This lesson is taught
  in the investigation slot because comparing sources *is* an
  investigation. Read both sources aloud slowly; let the learner
  follow the chart. Do not reveal the answer key's chart before the
  learner builds their own.
- **S4 (Quiz + project planning):** Give the quiz first, then let
  the learner choose a project change. A learner who scores below 6
  on the quiz should still plan a project — but re-teach Lesson 03
  or 04 in Week 14, S2 before presenting.

### Week 14 — Claims

- **S1 (Lesson 05):** The word "probable" needs explicit permission:
  learners often think it means "guessing." Reframe: "Probable means
  the evidence points this way, even though we were not there."
- **S2 (Practice D + project building):** Check every claim for
  evidence match before the learner builds the exhibit. A mismatched
  claim rebuilt now saves a failed presentation later.
- **S3 (Project share):** Keep presentations to 3 minutes. Ask one
  kind question per presentation; model the question yourself first
  ("What does your second source add that the first one does not?").
- **S4 (Unit review + assessment):** Review with the timeline strip
  and the two bridge sources visible. Do not introduce new material
  in S4.

## Misconception watch (all weeks)

| Misconception | Correction move |
|---|---|
| Close on the timeline = close in years | Always check the numbers, not the gaps (Lesson 01, rule 2) |
| The past was one time | Attach a year to every "then" claim (Lesson 02) |
| Everything long ago was worse | Require one good-then and one better-now per comparison (Lesson 02 application) |
| Old means true | Old sources can be wrong or one-sided; compare sources (Lesson 03) |
| A picture tells everything | Cannot-tell step on every image task (Lesson 03) |
| Primary is always better | "Best depends on the question" (Lesson 03, C2/C6) |
| Different means one is lying | Facts vs. feelings; perspectives differ honestly (Lesson 04) |
| The newspaper is neutral | Audience and purpose shape every source (Lesson 04) |
| One good source is enough | Town-wide claims need more than one source (Lessons 04–05) |
| One cause, one effect | Chains branch: the bridge had a happy and a sad effect (Lesson 05) |
| A claim is an opinion | A claim with matching evidence is an argument (Lesson 05) |
| Probable means guessing | Probable means evidence-backed but not certain (Lesson 05) |

## Readiness and reteach signals

- **Ready to move on (per session):** the learner states the
  session goal in their own words and passes the exit check.
- **Reteach Lesson 01 if:** the learner cannot order the six events
  or confuses 1895/1910 after Week 11, S4.
- **Reteach Lesson 03 if:** the learner cannot name the five source
  kinds or cannot complete a Cannot-tell entry after Week 13, S2.
- **Reteach Lesson 05 if:** the learner's project claim evidence
  does not match the claim after guided correction.
- **Assessment bands:** 26–30 ready for Unit 04; 20–25 re-teach the
  weakest part in midyear review week; below 20 re-teach Lessons 01,
  03, and 05 in small steps before Unit 04.

## Differentiation

- **Reading/language access:** read all sources aloud; use sentence
  starters from each lesson; accept oral, pointing, and drawing
  responses; scribe when writing is not the assessed skill.
- **Extension:** Lesson 01 (invent a labeled pretend event), Lesson
  03 (primary/secondary depends on the question), Lesson 04
  (usefulness depends on the question, D2.His.13.3-5), Lesson 05
  (summarize a secondary source's claim; write two-source-plus-map
  questions).
- **Pacing:** if a session runs long, cut the extension, never the
  exit check. If the unit runs short, add a second then/now area
  (food, clothing) in Week 12, S3.
