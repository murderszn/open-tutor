# Formative Quiz — Week 13 (10 points)

**Grade 3 Social Studies · Unit 03.** The adult may read questions
aloud and scribe answers. All Cedar Hollow material is fictional
practice material. Solutions are in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md)
— do not hand the key to the learner.

**1. (1 pt)** Put these in chronological order: mill opens (1895) ·
first homes (1880) · bridge replaces ferry (1950).

**2. (1 pt)** Name the five kinds of historical sources taught in
Lesson 03.

**3. (1 pt)** Which source kind best answers "Where was the ferry
dock?" — artifact, photograph, map, document, or oral account? Give
one reason.

**4. (2 pts)** Study the main-street illustration from Lesson 03
(or read its text-only alternative): "A wide dirt road runs down the
middle of a small old-time town. On both sides stand wooden
storefronts with porches and blank wooden signs. A brown horse pulls
a wooden wagon loaded with large sacks along the road. On the
right-hand store porch sit wooden barrels and crates. An old-style
lamp post stands at the left corner. Small houses and green hills
stand in the distance. No people are shown, and no sign has words on
it."
(a) Name two clues that this shows travel long ago.
(b) Name one thing this picture cannot tell you, and why.

**5. (2 pts)** Read these two short fictional source lines about the
1950 bridge:
- Gazette (fictional): "A great crowd gathered on Saturday morning
  as the first wagon crossed the new bridge. Bells rang and children
  waved flags."
- Mara's letter (fictional): "Father drove the ferry for twenty
  years, and now the ferry will stop."
(a) Name one thing both lines agree on.
(b) Name one way the two lines differ in what they emphasize.

**6. (1 pt)** A museum label written in 1995 describes the mill that
opened in 1895. For the question "What happened at the mill in
1895?" is the label a primary or a secondary source? Why?

**7. (1 pt)** What does the word "audience" mean for a historical
source? Give one clue from the Gazette line in question 5 that shows
its audience.

**8. (1 pt)** True or false, with a reason: "If two sources describe
the same event differently, one of them must be lying."
