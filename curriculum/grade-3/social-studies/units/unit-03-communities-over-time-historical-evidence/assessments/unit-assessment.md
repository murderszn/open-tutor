# Unit Assessment — Unit 03: Communities over Time and Historical Evidence (30 points)

**Grade 3 Social Studies · Week 14, S4.** The adult may read
questions aloud and scribe answers. All Cedar Hollow material is
fictional practice material. Solutions and scoring guidance are in
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) — do not hand the key to the
learner.

## Part 1 — Timelines (6 pts)

Cedar Hollow timeline (fictional): 1880 first homes · 1895 mill
opens · 1910 railroad station · 1928 brick school · 1950 bridge
replaces ferry · 1995 mill becomes a museum.

**1. (2 pts)** List all six events in chronological order with their
years.

**2. (2 pts)** The brick school opened in 1928 and the bridge opened
in 1950. Was the school already open when the bridge opened? How many
years earlier? Show your subtraction.

**3. (2 pts)** A child born in 1910 turned 18 in 1928. Name one
Cedar Hollow event from their childhood (before age 18) and one from
the year they turned 18. Were the two events the same year? In one
sentence, explain how one person's life can still connect two events
18 years apart.

## Part 2 — Then and now (6 pts)

**4. (3 pts)** Complete the chart. Each row needs one "then" fact
with a year and one "now" fact.

| Area | Then (year: ___) | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Crossing the river | | |
| Sending news to another town | | |
| School | | |

**5. (3 pts)** "Then, people crossed by ferry and waited in rain;
now, people cross by bridge in any weather." Is this a good
comparison? Give two reasons for your answer, and name the evidence
for the "then" side.

## Part 3 — Sources (10 pts)

**6. (2 pts)** Name the five kinds of historical sources and match
each to one question it answers best:
(a) What did a ferry ticket look like?
(b) Where was the ferry dock?
(c) What did the main street look like in 1910?
(d) How many children attended the brick school in 1928?
(e) What did the ferry worker's daughter remember about river
mornings?

**7. (4 pts)** Read this new fictional practice source — a diary
line written by a mill worker in 1895: "Today the mill opened. I
start work at sunrise. My hands will be tired, but my family will
have bread."
(a) Is this a primary or a secondary source for studying the mill
opening? Why? (1 pt)
(b) Who is the most likely audience for a diary line like this?
Give one clue from inside the source. (1 pt)
(c) Name one thing this source can tell us and one thing it cannot
tell us. (2 pts)

**8. (4 pts)** Read the two fictional bridge sources again (full
texts in Lesson 04 and practice set D):
- Gazette: the crowd celebrated, bells rang, children waved flags;
  the mayor said people could now cross in any weather.
- Mara's letter: her father drove the ferry for twenty years and now
  the ferry will stop; she will miss the river mornings.
(a) Name two things the sources agree on. (2 pts)
(b) Infer the audience and purpose of EACH source, with one clue
from inside the source for each inference (four clues total).
(2 pts)

## Part 4 — Causes, effects, and claims (8 pts)

**9. (3 pts)** Event: the railroad station opens in 1910. Give one
probable cause, one probable effect, and one question about the
people who shaped this change. (One point each.)

**10. (5 pts)** Write a claim about the bridge (1950) using the
frame: "I claim ___. My evidence is ___ and ___."
- Claim is clear and about the bridge (1 pt).
- First evidence piece matches the claim (1 pt).
- Second evidence piece matches the claim and comes from a different
  source than the first (2 pts).
- One question your evidence cannot answer (1 pt).

## Scoring guide (adult use)

- **26–30:** Ready for Unit 04. The learner sequences, compares
  sources, and argues from evidence at the grade-3 entry level.
- **20–25:** Mostly ready; re-teach the weakest part (timeline
  order, source kinds, or claim matching) in the midyear review week.
- **Below 20:** Re-teach Lessons 01, 03, and 05 in small steps before
  Unit 04; use the practice sets again with adult guidance.
- Open responses accept any answer matching the answer key's
  acceptable variation; a well-reasoned answer that differs in
  wording but not in content earns full credit.
