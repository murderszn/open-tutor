# Project — Mini-Exhibit: One Change, Two Pieces of Evidence

**Week 14 · S3 (investigation share) · plus planning in Week 13, S4
and Week 14, S2 · Unit 03**

## What you will make

A mini-exhibit about **one change** in Cedar Hollow (fictional town):
a poster, a short talk with drawings, or a small display the adult
helps arrange. The exhibit has four parts:

1. **A timeline strip** showing your change in chronological order
   with at least two neighboring events (before and after).
2. **A claim** about your change, in the frame: "I claim ___."
3. **Two pieces of evidence** from two different kinds of sources
   (for example, one document and one map, or one oral account and
   one photograph/illustration). Each piece of evidence must match
   the claim.
4. **A "What we still wonder" card** with one question your evidence
   cannot answer.

## Materials

- Paper/poster board, pencils, crayons or markers (adult may scribe
  captions).
- The Cedar Hollow timeline and the Lesson 04 sources (reprinted in
  [practice.md](practice.md), set D).
- The main-street illustration
  ([assets/cedar-hollow-main-street.png](assets/cedar-hollow-main-street.png))
  or its text-only alternative (see Lesson 03).

## Steps

1. **Choose a change** (Week 13, S4): the mill opening (1895), the
   railroad station (1910), the brick school (1928), or the bridge
   (1950). Tell the adult why you picked it in one sentence.
2. **Place it on the timeline** (Week 14, S2): draw a strip with your
   event plus the event just before and just after it. Label the
   years.
3. **Ask a changemaker question** (Week 14, S2): "Who shaped this
   change, and who was helped or hurt by it?" Write it on your
   exhibit.
4. **Pick two evidence pieces** (Week 14, S2): each from a different
   source kind. Write or dictate one sentence per piece saying what
   it shows. Check: does each piece actually support your claim? If
   not, change the claim or the evidence — never force a match.
5. **Build the exhibit** (Week 14, S2–S3): poster, talk with
   drawings, or display. Keep captions short; the adult scribes if
   writing is not the assessed skill.
6. **Present for 3 minutes** (Week 14, S3): read your claim, show
   your two evidence pieces, and read your "What we still wonder"
   card. Classmates (or the adult) ask one kind question each.

## Deliverables

- The timeline strip (with years and order correct).
- The claim, in the claim frame.
- Two evidence pieces, labeled with their source kinds.
- The "What we still wonder" card.
- A 3-minute presentation (spoken, drawn, or both).

## Rubric (adult-scored; 12 points)

| Criterion | 3 — Strong | 2 — Developing | 1 — Starting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timeline order (3) | Event placed correctly with before/after neighbors and years | Event placed correctly but a neighbor or year is missing | Order or years wrong |
| Claim (3) | Clear claim about the change, in the claim frame | Claim present but vague ("it was important") | No claim, or claim is just an opinion with no frame |
| Evidence match (3) | Two pieces, two different source kinds, both clearly support the claim | Two pieces but one kind repeated, or one piece weakly matches | One piece only, or pieces do not support the claim |
| Honest limits (3) | "What we still wonder" names a real gap the evidence cannot answer | A wonder card is present but answerable from the evidence | No wonder card, or the learner claims the evidence proves everything |

**Readiness signal:** 10–12 points with a correct timeline means the
learner is ready for Unit 04's deliberation work. Below 7 points, or
a wrong timeline order, means re-teaching Lesson 01's ordering rules
and Lesson 05's claim frame before the unit assessment.

## Safety and privacy

- Exhibits are shared only with the guiding adult and the learner's
  class group, never published from this track.
- The personal-timeline work from Week 11 stays private and is not
  part of this exhibit.
- All Cedar Hollow material is labeled fictional whenever it is
  displayed.
