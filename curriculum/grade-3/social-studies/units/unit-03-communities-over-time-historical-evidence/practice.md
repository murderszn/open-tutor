# Practice Sets — Unit 03

**Grade 3 Social Studies · Weeks 11–14.** The adult may scribe. Every
item is solved in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md). All Cedar Hollow
material is fictional practice material, not real history.

## Week 11 — Set A: Sequencing (after Lesson 01)

Cedar Hollow timeline (fictional): 1880 first homes · 1895 mill
opens · 1910 railroad station · 1928 brick school · 1950 bridge
replaces ferry · 1995 mill becomes a museum.

- **A1.** Put these four events in chronological order: bridge
  replaces ferry (1950) · first homes (1880) · railroad station
  (1910) · mill opens (1895).
- **A2.** Which event happened just before the brick school (1928)?
  Which happened just after it?
- **A3.** True or false, with a reason: "The mill became a museum
  before the railroad station opened." Explain using years.
- **A4.** A child born in Cedar Hollow in 1895 turned 15 in 1910.
  Name one event from their childhood and one from their teenage
  years, and tell whether those two events happened "at the same
  time" in the overlapping-lives sense.
- **A5.** Error hunt: Sam ordered the events as 1880 homes, 1910
  station, 1895 mill, 1928 school, 1950 bridge, 1995 museum. What is
  wrong, and how do you know?
- **A6.** The town library can show only three events on a poster.
  Pick three that show the biggest changes, and give one reason for
  each pick.

## Week 12 — Set B: Then and now (after Lesson 02)

- **B1.** Complete the chart with one "then" fact and one "now" fact
  for each row. Name the year for each "then" fact.

  | Area | Then (year: ___) | Now |
  |---|---|---|
  | Travel across the river | | |
  | School | | |
  | Sending news to another town | | |

- **B2.** Which sentence is the best comparison? Explain why the
  other two are weak.
  1. "Travel then was bad and travel now is good."
  2. "Then, people crossed by ferry and waited in rain; now, people
     cross by bridge in any weather."
  3. "People then had no cars, so they knew nothing about travel."
- **B3.** Look at the main-street illustration (Lesson 03) or its
  text-only alternative. Name two clues that the picture shows travel
  long ago, not travel today.
- **B4.** Give one way school is better now than in 1928, and one way
  school in 1928 was good. (There is no single right pair — your
  reasons must be honest, not "everything was worse.")
- **B5.** Error hunt: "Nobody in the past ever traveled fast, and
  everybody today travels by car." What is wrong with the words
  "nobody" and "everybody" here?
- **B6.** Compare 1880 and 1950 (not past vs. present): name one
  thing that changed between those two years in Cedar Hollow, and
  one thing that stayed the same.

## Week 13 — Set C: Source kinds (after Lesson 03)

- **C1.** Match each question to the best source kind (artifact,
  photograph, map, document, oral account). Give one reason per match.
  1. Where was the ferry dock?
  2. What did the ferry worker's daughter feel when the ferry stopped?
  3. What did a ferry ticket look like?
  4. How many children attended the brick school in 1928?
  5. What did the main street look like in 1910?
- **C2.** Sort these into primary and secondary for the question
  "What happened when the bridge opened in 1950?": (a) Mara's letter
  written the week the bridge opened; (b) a museum label written in
  1995 about the bridge; (c) the Gazette story printed the week the
  bridge opened; (d) a history book chapter written in 2020 about
  Cedar Hollow. Explain each sort in one sentence.
- **C3.** Use the main-street illustration (or its text-only
  alternative). Complete Observe–Reflect–Question–Cannot-tell with
  one entry each:
  - Observe: one thing you notice.
  - Reflect: what you think it means.
  - Question: one thing you wonder.
  - Cannot tell: one thing this picture cannot tell you.
- **C4.** A learner says, "This old photograph tells us everything
  about Cedar Hollow in 1910." Write a kind correction in two
  sentences.
- **C5.** You want to know what goods the mill made in 1895. Which
  source kind would you try first, and which kind would probably not
  help? Explain both choices.
- **C6.** The museum label from 1995 says the mill was "the heart of
  the town." For the question "What did people in 1995 think about
  the mill?" is the label a primary or a secondary source? Why does
  the question change the answer?

## Week 13 — Set D: Two sources, one event (after Lessons 04–05)

Read both fictional practice sources again before answering. They
are repeated here so this page works on its own.

**Source A — The Cedar Hollow Gazette (fictional), the week the
bridge opened:** "THE BRIDGE IS OPEN! A great crowd gathered on
Saturday morning as the first wagon crossed the new bridge over the
river. Bells rang and children waved flags. 'Now we can cross in any
weather,' said the mayor. 'No more waiting for the ferry in the
rain.' The bridge was built with strong steel and wood, and it will
carry wagons, horses, and people safely across the river for many
years to come."

**Source B — Letter from Mara, the ferry worker's daughter, to her
cousin (fictional), the week the bridge opened:** "Dear Cousin, The
bridge opened on Saturday. Father drove the ferry for twenty years,
and now the ferry will stop. We stood on the bank and watched the
first wagon cross. Father smiled, but later he was quiet. He does not
know what work he will do now. I will miss the river mornings, when
the mist sat on the water and Father called out to the passengers he
knew by name. Your cousin, Mara"

- **D1.** Name two things both sources agree on, and two things only
  one source mentions.
- **D2.** Infer audience and purpose for each source, giving one
  clue from inside the source for each inference (four clues total).
- **D3.** Build a cause/effect chain for the bridge: one probable
  cause, the event, and two probable effects (one happy, one sad).
- **D4.** Write a claim about the bridge using the frame: "I claim
  ___. My evidence is ___ and ___." Then name one question your
  evidence cannot answer.
- **D5.** A learner says, "Source B proves the bridge was a bad
  idea." Explain in two or three sentences why one source cannot
  prove a whole-town claim.
- **D6.** Which source is more useful for each question? Explain in
  one sentence each.
  1. How did the crowd feel on opening day?
  2. What happened to the ferry worker's job?
  3. What did the mayor promise about the bridge?
