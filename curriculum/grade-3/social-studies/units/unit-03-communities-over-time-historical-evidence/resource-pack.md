# Resource Pack — Unit 03: Communities over Time and Historical Evidence

**Goal (one sentence):** Give the guiding adult short,
grade-3-appropriate references and search routes that reinforce
timelines, then/now comparison, kinds of historical sources,
primary vs. secondary sources, and comparing two sources about one
event.
**Age fit:** grade 3 (ages 8–9); the adult previews every video and
co-views with the learner. Nothing here replaces the unit's own
lessons; videos and references supplement them.

**Fact-check record (2026-10-24):** the Library of Congress
Observe–Reflect–Question routine is confirmed by multiple
teacher-facing descriptions read 2026-10-24: the Reading Rockets
article "Using primary sources in the classroom" describes the LOC
Primary Source Analysis Tool's Observe prompts ("What do you notice
first?", "What people and objects are shown?") and its guides for
photographs, oral histories, and maps; the Colorín Colorado article
confirms the tool's observe/reflect/question structure and the
primary-vs.-secondary distinction (secondary sources are "accounts
or interpretations of events created by someone without firsthand
experience"); the OSLIS Elementary pages confirm the working
definitions used in Lesson 03 (primary = created at the time of the
event; secondary = analyzes or interprets an event afterwards).
C3 Framework codes are carried from the track audit's 2026-10-03
verification, not re-verified in this run. The unit's Cedar Hollow
sources are original fictional practice texts and are never presented
as real historical documents.

## Web references (reputable, free, no account)

1. **Library of Congress — Getting Started with Primary Sources:
   Primary Sources and Standards** —
   https://www.loc.gov/programs/teachers/getting-started-with-primary-sources/primary-sources-and-standards/
   *Why good:* the LOC teachers program's own page on primary
   sources and standards, including links to the analysis tool and
   teacher guides behind Lesson 03's routine. *Check note
   2026-10-24:* the page's URL is confirmed via the Library's own
   teacher-resources FAQ answer (ask.loc.gov), but a direct text
   fetch of the LOC page returned an access block on 2026-10-24 and
   a live-browser check returned no result in this run; the adult
   must open and preview it before use. Do not cite page contents beyond what the
   FAQ and the Reading Rockets/Colorín Colorado descriptions
   confirm.
2. **Reading Rockets — "Using primary sources in the classroom"** —
   https://www.readingrockets.org/blogs/sound-it-out/using-primary-sources-classroom
   *Why good:* elementary-focused explanation of the LOC Teacher's
   Guides and Analysis Tools, with Observe prompts and guidance for
   photographs, oral histories, and maps. *Snippet read 2026-10-24;
   adult previews; free, no account.*
3. **Colorín Colorado — "Primary Sources, the Library of Congress,
   and English Learners"** —
   https://www.colorincolorado.org/article/primary-sources-library-congress-and-english-learners
   *Why good:* clear primary-vs.-secondary definitions, the
   observe/reflect/question structure, and language-access tips
   (partners, word banks, sentence frames) that match this unit's
   supports. *Snippet read 2026-10-24; adult previews; free, no
   account.*
4. **OSLIS Elementary — "Primary vs. Secondary Sources"** —
   https://elementary.oslis.org/secondary/learn-to-research/plan/plan-possible-sources/primary-sources
   *Why good:* elementary-level definitions with examples (letters,
   diaries, photographs vs. books and articles that interpret them)
   that match Lesson 03's wording. OSLIS is Oregon's school-library
   information system. *Snippet read 2026-10-24; adult previews;
   free, no account.*
5. **National Council for the Social Studies — C3 Framework
   standards page** — https://www.socialstudies.org/standards/c3
   *Why good:* the framework home for the grades 3–5 indicators this
   unit teaches; adult-side reference for the codes in the unit
   README. *Carried from the track audit's 2026-10-03 verification;
   no state adoption or alignment certification is claimed.*
