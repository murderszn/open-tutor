# Project — Our Class Council Proposal

**Week 17, S4 (planning) · Week 18, S2 (building) · Week 18, S3
(presentation) · Unit 04**

## What you will make

A **Class Council Proposal** for a real problem in your classroom or
school: a poster, a short speech with drawings, or both. The proposal
has five parts:

1. **The problem**, in one sentence, with one piece of evidence that
   it is real (something you observed: "Three books were left on the
   floor this week and one got stepped on").
2. **Who it affects**, and how — at least two groups (for example,
   readers and the librarian; players and the playground monitor).
3. **The proposed policy** — one to three fair rules that handle the
   problem, each with a reason (a responsibility it creates and a
   freedom it protects, as in Lesson 03).
4. **The steps to make it happen** — the policy process from Lesson
   06 (notice, talk and listen, propose, decide by class vote, try
   it, check), with a named way to check whether it worked (what you
   will count or observe, and after how long).
5. **A 3-minute presentation** — read the problem, the policy, and
   the check plan; answer one kind question from the class or the
   adult.

The problem must be a real classroom or school problem the class can
actually act on. It must not name, blame, or embarrass any child or
adult, and it must not ask the class to contact a real official.
Proposals stay in the classroom; nothing is sent anywhere from this
project.

## Materials

- Paper/poster board, pencils, crayons or markers (adult may scribe
  captions).
- The deliberation-steps card and the policy-steps strip from
  Lessons 05–06.
- Practice sets B and D (fair-rule frames and policy steps).

## Steps

1. **Choose a problem** (Week 17, S4): brainstorm three classroom or
   school problems, then pick one the class can act on. Tell the
   adult why you picked it in one sentence, and name your one piece
   of evidence.
2. **Talk and listen** (Week 18, S2): ask at least two people how
   the problem affects them. Write or dictate one sentence per
   person. If two people disagree about the fix, write down both
   views — disagreement is evidence, not a problem.
3. **Draft the policy** (Week 18, S2): write one to three rules.
   Check each rule with Lesson 03's test: is it fair (a reason that
   connects to safety, fairness, or learning)? Does it name a
   responsibility and a protected freedom?
4. **Plan the check** (Week 18, S2): state what you will count or
   observe (for example, "ruined books per week") and when you will
   check (for example, "after two weeks"). A policy without a check
   plan is unfinished.
5. **Build the poster or speech** (Week 18, S2): problem, who it
   affects, policy, steps, check plan — in that order, in short
   captions. The adult scribes if writing is not the assessed skill.
6. **Present for 3 minutes** (Week 18, S3): read the proposal, then
   take one kind question. After the presentation, the class votes
   (Lesson 05 procedure) on whether to try the policy — and the
   result, including a "no," is recorded honestly.

## Deliverables

- The problem sentence with one piece of evidence.
- Who it affects (at least two groups, with how).
- The proposed policy (1–3 fair rules, each with a reason).
- The steps to make it happen, including the check plan.
- A 3-minute presentation (spoken, drawn, or both).

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | 10 — Strong | 7 — Good | 4 — Starting | 0 — Missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem and evidence (10)** | Real classroom/school problem in one clear sentence, with a specific observed piece of evidence. | Problem is clear; evidence is vague ("things are messy"). | Problem is unclear or is about one named person. | No problem or evidence. |
| **Policy clarity and fairness (10)** | 1–3 rules, each fair, each with a responsibility and a protected freedom named. | Rules are mostly fair; reasons are thin for one rule. | Rules are unfair, missing reasons, or do not address the problem. | No policy. |
| **Steps and participation (10)** | Full policy steps (talk and listen, propose, vote, try, check); at least two people's views included. | Steps are mostly complete; one view or one step missing. | Steps are out of order or missing the vote or the check. | No steps. |
| **Presentation and listening (10)** | Clear 3-minute presentation in order; answers one question kindly and accurately; listened fairly during discussion. | Presentation is clear but out of order, or the question answer is thin. | Presentation is hard to follow, or the learner interrupts or dismisses questions. | No presentation. |

**Scoring note for the adult:** a class vote of "no" does not lower
the score — the proposal is judged on its reasoning, fairness, and
process, not on winning. A proposal that names a losing view fairly
scores higher on participation than one that hides disagreement.
