# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Local Government, Participation, and Public Services

**Grade 3 · Social Studies · Weeks 15–18.** This guide is for the
guiding adult. Learner pages are the lessons, practice sets, project,
and assessments; this guide and the answer key stay with the adult.

## Before Week 15 — name the real local setup

Cedar Hollow is fictional, and real local governments vary. Before
Lesson 02, spend five minutes naming the real setup for your town,
city, or county, in one sentence each: who leads it (mayor, town
supervisor, or city manager), what the elected group is called
(council, board, or commission), and which services the town itself
runs versus the county or school district. If a title differs from
Cedar Hollow's, say so out loud ("Our town has a town supervisor
instead of a mayor; the job is the same kind of job") and use the
real title from then on. Two cautions:

- Some real mayors vote on the council or break ties. If yours does,
  say so when teaching Lesson 02, and treat the lesson's "the council
  votes" as "the council votes, and the mayor votes only to break a
  tie" for your town. The unit's practice answers follow the
  fictional Cedar Hollow setup.
- Do not ask the learner to contact a real official, attend a real
  meeting alone, or share personal information. Any real-world
  look-up (for example, finding a town's real mayor through USA.gov)
  is done by the adult; see the resource pack.

## Session-by-session guidance

### Week 15 — Levels and officials

- **S1 (Lesson 01).** Teach the "how big is the place?" test before
  the sorting. If the learner sorts by fame ("the President is most
  important, so national"), re-teach: importance is not the test,
  coverage is.
- **S2 (Practice set A, items A1–A4).** A1 is the core check. A4
  (President and the park) is the misconception probe — discuss it
  aloud even if the answer is right.
- **S3 (Lesson 02).** Show the town-hall illustration large, or read
  its text-only alternative slowly. Ask the picture question before
  teaching the job cards, so the learner discovers the council's
  listening job. Then run the matching worked example.
- **S4 (Review; Practice set A, items A5–A8).** Re-run the pattern
  sentence ("council decides, mayor carries out, departments do the
  work, clerk writes it down") from memory. If the learner still
  swaps mayor and council, keep the job cards face-up for Week 16.

### Week 16 — Rules and laws

- **S1 (Lesson 03).** Drill the three-question test (who made it?
  is it written in law? does it apply to everyone here?) on two
  examples before the sort. Name the Constitution only as "the
  country's most important rules" — do not teach amendments, court
  cases, or ratification dates at this grade.
- **S2 (Practice set B, items B1–B4).** B4 (write a fair rule) is the
  session's real product; accept spoken or dictated rules. Check
  every rule for both parts: a responsibility and a protected
  freedom.
- **S3 (Investigation; Practice set B, items B5–B8).** The learner
  traces one classroom rule, one school rule, and one community law
  to the group that made it. Keep it to groups, not named people:
  "our class," "the school," "the state government."
- **S4 (Review).** Re-teach the key distinction if needed: a law is
  not a "big rule" — who made it is the test. Preview the budget
  strip for Week 17 so the numbers are familiar before Lesson 04.

### Week 17 — Taxes and deciding together

- **S1 (Lesson 04).** Use real coins or counters for the $10 tracing
  example; do not let the rounding (25% of 10 = 2.5) become the
  lesson. The point is that every dollar lands in exactly one pile.
  Say plainly that the $100 budget is fictional practice material.
- **S2 (Practice set C, items C1–C4).** C4 is the judgment item:
  there is no single right answer to "is this a good trade?" — score
  the reason, not the yes/no. C5–C8 can move to S4 if time runs
  short.
- **S3 (Lesson 05).** Pick the real classroom decision in advance
  and make sure you can carry it out. Run the five steps strictly:
  everyone speaks once before anyone speaks twice. If the group
  cannot finish, record where it stopped and finish in S4 — do not
  let one voice decide by default.
- **S4 (Formative quiz, 10 points; project planning).** The quiz
  checks Weeks 15–17 only. Use it diagnostically: a missed Q1 or Q2
  means re-teach levels or officials before the assessment; a missed
  Q4 means re-teach budget addition. Then plan the project problem
  (Project step 1).

### Week 18 — Participation and change

- **S1 (Lesson 06).** Be honest about age: children cannot vote in
  elections yet. Name what they can do now (speak up with adult help,
  help, serve in school roles) so the lesson does not promise a power
  the learner does not have.
- **S2 (Practice set D; project building).** D4 is the age-honesty
  check. Then build the proposal (Project steps 2–5). Refuse any
  proposal that names or blames a child or adult; redirect to a
  problem about a thing or a routine ("books left on the floor"),
  not a person.
- **S3 (Project share).** Keep presentations to 3 minutes. One kind
  question each. Then run the class vote on whether to try the
  policy, and record the result honestly — a "no" is a valid outcome
  and does not lower the project score.
- **S4 (Unit review; unit assessment, 30 points).** Review with the
  pattern sentences (levels test, council/mayor pattern, tax chain,
  deliberation steps, policy steps) before the assessment, not with
  new content.

## Misconception watch (whole unit)

- **"The President runs everything."** Re-teach Lesson 01's coverage
  test. Local and state governments have their own leaders and jobs.
- **"The mayor does all the work."** Re-teach Lesson 02's pattern:
  council decides, mayor carries out, departments do the work.
- **"Services are free."** Re-teach Lesson 04's chain: paid in
  advance, together, through taxes.
- **"A law is a big rule."** Re-teach Lesson 03's three-question
  test: who made it is the test.
- **"Losing a vote means unfairness."** Re-teach Lesson 05: fairness
  is being heard, an open count, and a chance to check and retry.
- **"Children cannot participate."** Re-teach Lesson 06: speaking up
  (with adult help), helping, and school service are participation
  now; election voting waits until 18.

## Readiness and reteach signals

- **Ready for the assessment:** the learner sorts 8 needs by level
  with at most one error, states the mayor/council distinction
  without prompting, and completes the tax chain in order.
- **Reteach levels (Lesson 01)** if the learner sorts by importance
  or fame rather than by coverage. Re-teach with the sorting cards,
  not with more explanation.
- **Reteach rules vs. laws (Lesson 03)** if the learner calls every
  strict rule a law. Re-teach with the three-question test on three
  fresh examples.
- **Reteach budgets (Lesson 04)** if the learner's five amounts do
  not total $100. Re-teach addition with coins before re-teaching
  any budget idea.
- **Reteach deliberation (Lesson 05)** if the learner treats a vote
  as "everyone must agree" or treats losing as unfairness. Re-run a
  small, low-stakes decision (which book to read) with the steps
  card visible.

## Safety and boundaries

- Investigations are observation- and discussion-based. No field
  trips, interviews with strangers, or contact with real officials
  are required or expected from the learner.
- Proposals and presentations stay in the classroom or home. Nothing
  is mailed, posted, or published from this unit.
- Keep all learner work, names, and schedules in private storage —
  never in this repository.

## Scoring summary

- Formative quiz (Week 17): 10 points. Diagnostic only; do not grade
  it as a final mark.
- Unit assessment (Week 18): 30 points. Part A 10, Part B 10,
  Part C 10. Part C, item C2 is scored on reasoning and completeness
  (problem, rule with reason, check plan), not on whether the class
  adopts the policy.
- Project: 40 points by the rubric in [project.md](project.md).
  Judge reasoning, fairness, and process — not winning the vote.
