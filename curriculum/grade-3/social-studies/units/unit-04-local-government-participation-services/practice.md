# Practice Sets — Unit 04: Local Government, Participation, and Public Services

**Grade 3 · Social Studies · Weeks 15–18.** The adult may scribe;
answers may be spoken, pointed to, or drawn. Every item is solved in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md). All Cedar Hollow names, officials, and
budget figures are **fictional practice material**.

## Practice set A — Levels and local officials (Week 15)

**A1.** Sort each need to its level — LOCAL, STATE, or NATIONAL.
Write one letter (L, S, or N) beside each:

1. Fixing a pothole on Maple Street in Cedar Hollow.
2. Printing the money used in every state.
3. Making the rules for driver's licenses in one state.
4. Buying new swings for Cedar Hollow Park.
5. Keeping the country safe from attack.
6. Repaving a highway that crosses a whole state.
7. Running story hour at the Cedar Hollow Library.
8. Making an agreement with another country.

**A2.** Match each job to the official or group that does it. Write
the letter of the job beside the official:

Officials: (a) Mayor Ana Ruiz · (b) Town council · (c) Town clerk

Jobs: (1) Votes on local rules and the budget. (2) Leads town
meetings and carries out the council's decisions. (3) Keeps town
records and meeting notes.

**A3.** Match each need to the department that handles it in Cedar
Hollow. Write the department's name:

Departments: road crew · fire department · library · parks crew

1. A tree branch fell on the playground slide.
2. Books are needed for a school project.
3. A street sign fell down in a storm.
4. Smoke is coming from a kitchen window.

**A4.** True or false? "The President is in charge of Cedar Hollow's
park." Explain your answer in one sentence.

**A5.** Look at the town-hall illustration
([assets/cedar-hollow-town-hall.png](assets/cedar-hollow-town-hall.png))
or use its text-only alternative in Lesson 02. Answer:

1. Who is speaking, and who is listening?
2. Name the three public services you can spot through the windows.
3. Why does it matter that residents can speak at council meetings?
   (One sentence.)

**A6.** Fill in the pattern sentence: "The council ______, the mayor
______ ______, the departments ______ ______ ______, and the clerk
______ ______ ______." (Use: decides · carries out · do the work ·
writes it down.)

**A7.** Cedar Hollow's council wants a new rule about park hours.
Put these steps in order: (a) The parks crew puts up the new park
signs. (b) The council discusses and votes. (c) The mayor carries
out the decision. (d) The clerk writes the decision down.

**A8.** Error-analysis: Sam says, "The mayor votes on local rules
alone, so the council is not needed." Is Sam right? Correct him in
two sentences.

## Practice set B — Rules, laws, and fair rules (Week 16)

**B1.** Sort each statement into RULE or LAW. Use the three-question
test (who made it? is it written in law? does it apply to everyone in
that place?):

1. Raise your hand before speaking in class.
2. Stop at a red light.
3. Put your shoes by the door at home.
4. Do not take something that belongs to someone else (stealing).
5. Return library books on time.
6. Wear a seat belt in a car.

**B2.** For each rule below, name one responsibility it creates and
one freedom it protects:

1. "In Cedar Hollow Park, dogs must stay on a leash."
2. "In class, listen while someone else is speaking."
3. "Return what you borrow, in the shape you borrowed it."

**B3.** Which of these is a fair rule? Which is unfair? Give a reason
for each:

1. "Take turns on the swings, five minutes each, so everyone gets a
   turn."
2. "Only children with blue backpacks may use the swings."
3. "Walk in the hallway so nobody gets hurt."

**B4.** Write a fair classroom rule of your own, in this frame: "Our
rule: ___. The responsibility is ___. The freedom it protects is
___."

**B5.** The Constitution is the country's most important set of
rules. Name two things it does, from Lesson 03: (1) it says how the
government is ______; (2) it protects people's ______.

**B6.** A town council makes a local law that says "nobody may speak
at council meetings." Can that law stand? Why or why not? (Hint: think
about the basic rules that stand above smaller ones.)

**B7.** Trace one classroom rule, one school rule, and one community
law to the group that made it. Fill in the table:

| Rule or law | Who made it? |
|---|---|
| (your classroom rule) | |
| (your school rule) | |
| Stop at a red light | |

**B8.** Error-analysis: Maya says, "A law is just a really big rule."
Correct her in two sentences, using the three-question test.

## Practice set C — Taxes, budgets, and spending choices (Week 17)

Cedar Hollow's pretend budget (fictional practice numbers) for
$100: Roads $30 · Fire department $25 · Library $15 · Parks $15 ·
Town hall and records $15.

**C1.** How much goes to each service? Copy the table and fill it in
from the budget strip. Then add the five amounts and show that they
total $100.

**C2.** Answer with arithmetic shown:

1. Roads plus fire department together: $___.
2. Library plus parks together: $___.
3. If the council moves $5 from parks to the library, the new parks
   amount is $___ and the new library amount is $___.

**C3.** A toy costs $10 and the sales tax is $1. How much does the
buyer pay in total? Who keeps the $10, and where does the $1 go?

**C4.** The council is asked to cut the library from $15 to $5 and
give the $10 saved to roads. Answer:

1. What are the new library and roads amounts?
2. What is gained? What is lost?
3. Is this a good trade? Give one reason for your answer.

**C5.** Trace one pretend tax dollar: list the four links of the
chain in order (people pay taxes → ___ → ___ → ___).

**C6.** Name two kinds of tax from Lesson 04, and give one example of
each in your own words: (1) ______ tax; (2) ______ tax.

**C7.** A resident says, "I never use the park, so I should not pay
park tax." Answer kindly in two sentences, using the idea that taxes
pay for services for everyone together.

**C8.** Error-analysis: Tom adds the budget as
30 + 25 + 15 + 15 + 15 = 110. Show the correct addition, step by step,
and name Tom's mistake.

## Practice set D — Deciding together, participation, and change (Week 18)

**D1.** Match each situation to the best procedure — CONSENSUS,
VOTE, or LEADER DECIDES — and give one reason:

1. A fire alarm rings.
2. The class picks a name for its reading corner.
3. The town council must set the budget by Friday, and residents
   disagree.
4. Two friends choose which game to play, and both must enjoy it.

**D2.** Put the five deliberation steps in order: (a) Share reasons.
(b) State the problem. (c) Check how it worked. (d) Listen to
everyone. (e) Decide.

**D3.** "I want the bench" is a wish. Rewrite it as a reason, using
the frame: "I think ___ because ___."

**D4.** Name four ways people participate in a democracy. For each
one, say whether a 9-year-old can use it now, or must wait until
older:

1. Vote in an election.
2. Speak up (with an adult's help).
3. Help by volunteering.
4. Serve on a student council.

**D5.** Match each problem to a participation form that fits it
best — VOTE, SPEAK UP, HELP, or SERVE — and give one reason:

1. Litter in the park after weekends.
2. Choosing the next mayor (for adults).
3. A younger child needs help learning to read.
4. The library needs someone to run story hour every week.

**D6.** Put the six policy steps in order: (a) Decide (vote).
(b) Notice a problem. (c) Check whether it worked. (d) Talk and
listen. (e) Try it. (f) Propose a policy.

**D7.** A new library rule — "quiet voices for homework help, silent
reading in the back room" — was tried for a month. Visits rose, and
noise complaints did not. Should the library keep the rule, change
it, or drop it? Give one reason, using the check step.

**D8.** Error-analysis: Lee says, "Once a rule is made, it can never
change, and losing a vote means the process was unfair." Correct both
parts of Lee's sentence, in two sentences.
