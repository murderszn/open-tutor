# Answer Key — Unit 04: Local Government, Participation, and Public Services

**For the guiding adult only.** Every practice item, quiz question,
assessment task, and project criterion is solved or scored here.
Open responses carry sample reasoning and acceptable variation; any
answer that meets the stated test counts. All Cedar Hollow details
are **fictional practice material**.

## Lesson exit checks and applications (samples)

- **Lesson 01 exit:** local, state, national (closest to home
  first). Application: tell the cousin to call the town or city —
  trash pickup is a local service.
- **Lesson 02 exit:** mayor — leads meetings, carries out the
  council's decisions; council — votes on local rules and the
  budget; the council votes. Application: the council votes on
  library hours and budget; the mayor carries the decision out; the
  library staff runs the longer hours; the clerk writes it down.
- **Lesson 03 exit:** sample rule — raise your hand; sample law —
  stop at a red light; the telling question is "Who made it — our
  group, or a government?" Application: the line-leader rule is fair
  if it names a responsibility (wait for your turn) and a protected
  freedom (everyone gets a turn, not just the loudest or fastest).
- **Lesson 04 exit:** budget; services. Application: taxes pay for
  services for everyone together — the speaker uses roads and fire
  protection even if they do not use the library.
- **Lesson 05 exit:** state the problem, listen, share reasons,
  decide, check. Application: losing a vote is not the same as being
  ignored — everyone was heard, the count was open, and the group
  can check and vote again.
- **Lesson 06 exit:** any two of: speak up with an adult's help;
  help by volunteering; serve on student council; practice voting in
  class. Application sample: messy cubbies → help first, by tidying
  and labeling, because the problem is small and fixable without a
  new rule.

## Practice set A

- **A1:** 1. L · 2. N · 3. S · 4. L · 5. N · 6. S · 7. L · 8. N.
  Reasoning: sort by coverage — one town, one state, the whole
  country.
- **A2:** (a) Mayor → (2) leads meetings, carries out decisions.
  (b) Council → (1) votes on local rules and the budget.
  (c) Clerk → (3) keeps records and meeting notes.
- **A3:** 1. parks crew · 2. library · 3. road crew · 4. fire
  department.
- **A4:** False. The President leads the national government; a
  town park is a local government's job.
- **A5:** 1. The resident with the raised hand (red hair) is
  speaking; the mayor and council are listening. 2. Fire department
  (fire truck), library (book cart), parks (park tree). 3. Sample:
  "So the council hears residents' needs before it discusses and
  votes." Any sentence naming listening-before-deciding counts.
- **A6:** "The council **decides**, the mayor **carries out**, the
  departments **do the work**, and the clerk **writes it down**."
- **A7:** Order: (b) council discusses and votes → (d) clerk writes
  the decision down → (c) mayor carries out the decision → (a)
  parks crew puts up the new signs. (The clerk's record step may
  also be placed at the end; what matters is (b) first and (a)
  last — the vote precedes the work.)
- **A8:** Sam is wrong. The council votes on local rules; the mayor
  carries out the council's decisions and does not make local laws
  alone. (Adult note: in some real towns a mayor also votes to break
  a tie — if your town works that way, say so; the fictional Cedar
  Hollow answer stands for this unit.)

## Practice set B

- **B1:** 1. RULE · 2. LAW · 3. RULE · 4. LAW · 5. RULE · 6. LAW.
  Test: who made it, is it written in law, does it apply to everyone
  in that place?
- **B2:** Samples — 1. Responsibility: dog owners hold the leash and
  control their dog. Freedom protected: everyone's freedom to walk
  and picnic without being chased. 2. Responsibility: wait and listen
  while someone speaks. Freedom protected: everyone's freedom to be
  heard. 3. Responsibility: return the item in good shape. Freedom
  protected: everyone's freedom to borrow next. Any pair that names
  a real responsibility and a real protected freedom counts.
- **B3:** 1. Fair — everyone gets a turn; the reason (fair sharing)
  connects to fairness. 2. Unfair — backpack color has nothing to do
  with safety or fairness; it takes a freedom away for no good
  reason. 3. Fair — the reason (nobody gets hurt) connects to
  safety.
- **B4:** Sample: "Our rule: take turns choosing the read-aloud book.
  The responsibility is to wait for your turn and listen to others'
  choices. The freedom it protects is that everyone gets a book they
  like sometimes." Any fair rule with both parts counts.
- **B5:** (1) set up (how the government is set up / how government
  works); (2) freedoms (people's freedoms, such as speech).
- **B6:** No, it cannot stand as a total ban. The Constitution
  protects freedoms such as speech, and it stands above smaller
  rules; a council may set fair speaking-time rules, but it may not
  ban residents from speaking entirely. (Adult note: this is a
  grade-3 simplification of First Amendment principles, not legal
  advice.)
- **B7:** Sample table — classroom rule (for example, "raise your
  hand") → our class (teacher and learners); school rule (for
  example, "walk in the hallway") → the school; "Stop at a red
  light" → the state government (traffic laws). Any group that
  actually made the named rule counts.
- **B8:** Sample correction: "A law is not just a big rule. A law is
  a rule made by a government, written down, and enforced for
  everyone in that place — who made it is the test, not how big or
  strict it is."

## Practice set C

- **C1:** Roads $30 · Fire $25 · Library $15 · Parks $15 · Town hall
  $15. Addition: 30 + 25 = 55; 55 + 15 = 70; 70 + 15 = 85; 85 + 15 =
  100.
- **C2:** 1. $30 + $25 = **$55**. 2. $15 + $15 = **$30**. 3. New parks
  $15 − $5 = **$10**; new library $15 + $5 = **$20**. Total still
  $100 (check: 30 + 25 + 20 + 10 + 15 = 100).
- **C3:** Total **$11**. The shop keeps the $10 price; the $1 tax
  goes to the government.
- **C4:** 1. New library **$5**; new roads **$40**. 2. Gained: $10
  more for roads. Lost: $10 from the library — two-thirds of its
  $15 budget; story hour and new books are at risk. 3. Open: either
  yes or no counts **if** the reason names both the gain and the
  loss (for example, "No, because losing story hour costs more than
  $10 of road work is worth" or "Yes, because the roads are dangerous
  and the library can share books with the school for a while").
- **C5:** People and businesses pay taxes → the government collects
  the money → the council votes a budget → departments spend it on
  services.
- **C6:** (1) **Property** tax — paid by people who own land or a
  building, based on its value. (2) **Sales** tax — a small extra
  amount added when you buy something.
- **C7:** Sample: "Taxes pay for services for everyone together.
  You may not use the park, but you use roads and fire protection,
  and your neighbors use the park — sharing the cost keeps every
  service there when anyone needs it." Any kind answer naming shared
  payment counts.
- **C8:** Correct addition: 30 + 25 = 55; 55 + 15 = 70; 70 + 15 = 85;
  85 + 15 = 100. Tom's mistake: he added an extra 10 (his total is
  exactly 10 too high — as if one $15 were counted as $25, or a $10
  were added that is not in the budget). The lesson: re-add a budget
  before voting on it.

## Practice set D

- **D1:** 1. **Leader decides** — safety cannot wait for a vote.
  2. **Vote** (consensus also accepted with a reason) — nobody is in
  danger, and everyone will live with the name, so fairness matters
  more than speed. 3. **Vote** (council vote after deliberation) —
  the decision must be recorded by a deadline after residents are
  heard. 4. **Consensus** — both friends must enjoy the game, so the
  decision must be acceptable to both.
- **D2:** (b) State the problem → (d) Listen to everyone → (a)
  Share reasons → (e) Decide → (c) Check how it worked.
- **D3:** Sample: "I think we should buy the bench **because**
  grandparents need a place to sit while children play." Any
  "I think ___ because ___" sentence with a real reason counts.
- **D4:** 1. Vote in an election — **wait** (until 18). 2. Speak up
  with an adult's help — **now**. 3. Help by volunteering — **now**.
  4. Serve on a student council — **now** (school role).
- **D5:** 1. **Help** (speak up also accepted with a reason: asking
  the council for more trash cans) — litter can be picked up now, or
  reported for more cans. 2. **Vote** — elections choose officials.
  3. **Help** — tutoring a younger child is volunteering. 4. **Serve**
  — running story hour weekly is taking a community job.
- **D6:** (b) Notice a problem → (d) Talk and listen → (f) Propose
  a policy → (a) Decide (vote) → (e) Try it → (c) Check whether it
  worked.
- **D7:** **Keep** the rule. The check step shows the policy worked:
  visits rose and noise complaints did not. Keep checking; if the
  result changes, deliberate again.
- **D8:** Sample correction: "Rules and laws can change, through the
  policy steps — notice, talk, propose, vote, try, check. And losing
  a fair vote does not mean the process was unfair: everyone was
  heard, the count was open, and the group can check and vote
  again."

## Lesson 05 deliberation (record)

The class's real deliberation (Lesson 05, S3) has no fixed answer.
Record in private notes: the problem chosen, the reasons offered on
each side, the procedure used, the decision, and the check date. A
deliberation counts as complete if every participant spoke or passed
freely, at least one reason was given on each side, and the decision
and check plan were stated aloud.

## Project scoring (40 points)

Score with the rubric in [project.md](project.md): problem and
evidence 10, policy clarity and fairness 10, steps and participation
10, presentation and listening 10. A class vote of "no" does not
lower the score. A proposal that fairly names a losing view scores
higher on participation than one that hides disagreement.
