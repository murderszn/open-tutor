# Resource Pack — Unit 04: Local Government, Participation, and Public Services

**Goal (one sentence):** Give the guiding adult short,
grade-3-appropriate references and search routes that reinforce the
three levels of government, local officials and public services,
rules vs. laws, taxes and budgets, deliberation, and participation.
**Age fit:** grade 3 (ages 8–9); the adult previews every video and
co-views with the learner. Nothing here replaces the unit's own
lessons; videos and references supplement them.

**Fact-check record (2026-10-31):** Ben's Guide to the U.S.
Government (U.S. Government Publishing Office) "Journey Person"
(ages 9–13) page was opened and read 2026-10-31: it lists Learning
Adventures on "Federal Versus State Government," "Branches of
Government," "How Laws are Made: The Language of the Law," and
"Election Process." The page is aimed at ages 9–13, slightly above
grade 3, so the adult previews and adapts it; it is an adult
reference, not assigned learner reading. USA.gov's "State and local
governments" and "State and local elections" pages were confirmed
by search snippets read 2026-10-31: they describe state/local
officials (governors, mayors, county executives, councils), local
services, and election types; the adult uses them to name the real
local setup, never to have the learner contact an official. The
iCivics elementary page was confirmed by a search snippet read
2026-10-31: it describes K–5 units introducing local, state, and
national government and navigating local government; the adult must
check account and access requirements before use, since they were
not verified in this run. C3 Framework codes are carried from the
track audit's 2026-10-03 verification, not re-verified in this run.
All Cedar Hollow budget figures are fictional practice numbers, not
a real town's budget.

## Web references (reputable, free, no account unless noted)

1. **Ben's Guide to the U.S. Government — Journey Person
   (ages 9–13) Learning Adventures** (U.S. Government Publishing
   Office) —
   http://bensguide.gpo.gov/learning-adventures-14more%3Fid%3D38%26age%3Dben9_13
   *Why good:* the official kids' civics site's 9–13 level, with
   adventures on federal vs. state government, branches, how laws
   are made, and elections that back Lessons 01–03 and 06.
   *Check note 2026-10-31:* page opened and read; adult previews and
   adapts for grade 3 (ages 8–9). Free, no account.
2. **USA.gov — State and local governments** —
   https://www.usa.gov/state-local-governments
   *Why good:* the U.S. government's official portal page for
   finding state and local government services, governors, and local
   contacts — the adult's tool for naming the real local setup in
   the teacher guide. *Snippet read 2026-10-31; adult use only;
   free, no account.*
3. **USA.gov — State and local elections** —
   https://www.usa.gov/state-local-elections
   *Why good:* official explanation of state and local election
   types and officials (governors, mayors, county councils, state
   legislators) that backs Lesson 06's voting discussion.
   *Snippet read 2026-10-31; adult use only; free, no account.*
4. **iCivics — Elementary Civics & Social Studies Lessons, Games,
   Videos** —
   https://vision.icivics.org/elementary-social-studies/
   *Why good:* nonpartisan K–5 civics curriculum whose elementary
   units introduce local, state, and national government and
   navigating local government — a teacher-planning reference for
   Lessons 01–02. *Snippet read 2026-10-31; adult must check account
   and access requirements before use; do not assume free
   no-account access.*
5. **Elephango — "The U.S. Government: From Big to Small"**
   (local and state government explainer) —
   https://www.elephango.com/index.cfm/pg/k12learning/lcid/12137/Local_and_State_Government

   wording. *Snippet read 2026-10-31; adult previews; check access
   before assigning.*
