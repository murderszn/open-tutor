# Lesson 02 — Local Officials and Public Services

**Week 15 · S3 (investigation) · 30–35 minutes · Unit 04**

## Student goal

*I can name what a mayor, a town council, and three public-service
departments do — and I can match a community need to the department
that handles it.*

## Materials

- The town-hall illustration
  ([`../assets/cedar-hollow-town-hall.png`](../assets/cedar-hollow-town-hall.png))
  or its text-only alternative (below).
- Job cards (adult prepares): MAYOR, COUNCIL, ROAD CREW, FIRE
  DEPARTMENT, LIBRARY, PARKS CREW, TOWN CLERK.

> **Text-only alternative for the illustration:** Five people sit at
> a long table at the front of a meeting room: in the middle, the
> mayor, wearing a sash and a star badge; on either side, four
> council members. In front of them sit six residents, including a
> child with a notebook and an older man in a wheelchair. One
> resident, a woman with red hair, stands with her hand raised to
> speak. Through the tall windows behind the table you can see a red
> fire truck, a cart full of library books, and a big green park
> tree. Question for the picture: "Who is speaking, and who is
> listening? What three public services can you spot through the
> windows?" (Answers: the resident with the raised hand is speaking;
> the mayor and council are listening; the fire truck = fire
> department, the book cart = library, the tree = parks.)

> **AI-illustration label (read aloud):** "This picture was made by a
> computer to help us practice. It is not a photograph of a real

## Readiness check (2 min)

"Last lesson we learned that local government is closest to home.
Name two local jobs you remember." (Streets, parks, library, fire
protection, trash pickup.) "Today we meet the people who do those
jobs in our pretend town."

## Teaching (explicit explanation, 10 min)

In Cedar Hollow — remember, a pretend town — local government has
people with different jobs:

- **Mayor Ana Ruiz** leads town meetings, carries out the council's
  decisions, and speaks for the town. The mayor does not make local
  laws alone.
- **The town council** (Ben Carter, Priya Patel, Tom Okafor, Grace
  Liu) discusses problems, votes on local rules called
  **ordinances**, and votes on the town's spending plan, the
  **budget**. Council members are elected by the town's voters.
- **Departments** do the daily work. A department is a part of a
  government with one special job:
  - **Road crew** — fixes potholes, plows snow, keeps street signs
    up.
  - **Fire department** — puts out fires, rescues people, teaches
    fire safety.
  - **Library** — lends books, runs story hour, helps people find
    information.
  - **Parks crew** — mows, plants, fixes playground equipment.
  - **Town clerk** — keeps town records and meeting notes, so
    decisions are written down and can be checked later.

A **public service** is work a government does for everyone. You do
not pay the fire department each time it comes; the town pays for it
from taxes, which you will learn about in Lesson 04. The pattern to
remember: **the council decides, the mayor carries out, the
departments do the work, and the clerk writes it down.**

**Vocabulary in context:** *mayor* — leader of a town or city.
*Council* — elected people who vote on local rules and spending.
*Ordinance* — a local law. *Budget* — a spending plan. *Department*
— one part of government with one special job. *Public service* —
work done for everyone.

## Worked example 1 — Reading the town-hall picture (modeled reasoning, 5 min)

The adult thinks aloud: "Who is at the front table? Five people —
the mayor in the middle and four council members. They are listening,
not talking. Who is talking? The woman with her hand raised — a
resident who wants to speak. That is how a council meeting works:
residents speak, the council listens, then the council discusses and
votes. Now look through the windows. I see a fire truck, a book cart,
and a park tree. Those are three public services the town provides:
fire protection, the library, and parks. The picture is telling us
that the people at the table are responsible for services you can
see right outside the window."

## Worked example 2 — Matching a need to a department (modeled reasoning, 5 min)

"A storm knocks a tree branch onto Cedar Hollow's playground slide.
Who handles it? First, what kind of job is it? Fixing playground
equipment. Which department's special job is playgrounds? The parks
crew. So: the parks crew clears the branch and fixes the slide. Does
the council do the fixing? No — the council votes the money for
parks, the mayor makes sure the work happens, and the parks crew does
it. If the branch had blocked the road instead, the job would belong
to the road crew. Same storm, different department — because the
departments have different special jobs."

## Guided practice (5 min)

Adult and learner together, using the job cards:

1. "The town needs a new rule about dogs in the park. Who votes on
   it?" (The council — it votes on ordinances.)
2. "A fire starts in a kitchen. Who comes?" (The fire department.)
3. "Someone wants to borrow a book about trains." (The library.)
4. Error-analysis: the adult says, "The mayor fixes potholes
   herself, right?" and asks the learner to correct it. (No — the
   road crew fixes potholes; the mayor carries out the council's
   decisions and speaks for the town.)

## Independent practice (4 min)

Learner completes practice set A, items A5–A8 (see
[practice.md](../practice.md)). The adult does not correct mid-task;
errors are discussed in the exit check.

## Application (3 min)

"Cedar Hollow's library wants longer Saturday hours. Who would need
to agree, and what would each person do?" (The council votes on the
library's budget and hours; the mayor carries out the decision; the
library staff runs the longer hours; the clerk writes the decision
down. Any answer that keeps "council decides, mayor carries out,
department does the work" counts.)

## Exit check (1 min)

"Name one job of the mayor and one job of the council — and tell me
which one votes on local rules." (Mayor: leads meetings / carries
out decisions. Council: votes on local rules and the budget. The
council votes.) If the learner mixes them up, re-teach with the
pattern sentence: "Council decides, mayor carries out."

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** keep the job cards face-up; let the learner answer by
  pointing to a card or to a person in the picture instead of
  writing.
- **Reading/language access:** the adult reads the picture
  description aloud; "department" is explained as "one team's special
  job," like a job on a sports team.
- **Extension:** the learner invents one more Cedar Hollow
  department (for example, a water department or an animal shelter
  team), names its special job, and explains which council decision
  it would need (money, a rule, or both).

## Misconceptions to watch

- **"The mayor does everything in town."** The mayor leads and
  carries out decisions, but departments do the work and the council
  votes.
- **"A council is one person."** A council is a group. That is the
  point: several elected people decide together.
- **"Public services are free."** They feel free at the moment of
  use, but the town pays for them from taxes (Lesson 04).
