# Grade 3 Social Studies

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are delivered;
the eight units are planned, not yet written. See [scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained for
one grade-3 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 3 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-3 social studies course organized around
geographic inquiry — maps, regions, continents, and how people use place;
environment and settlement; communities over time through historical evidence;
local government, participation, and public services; production, trade,
scarcity, and interdependence; cultures, perspectives, migration, and global
communities; rights, responsibilities, fairness, and historical change; and a
regional comparative inquiry ending in a civic capstone.

Sessions are about 30 minutes, four per week: a concept/introduction session, a
skills-practice session, an investigation or application session, and a
review/check session each week. Work is map- and evidence-based: learners draw
and read maps, sequence events, compare sources, weigh economic choices, and
practice classroom deliberation. Independent written practice is short and
reviewed by the adult the same day.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. **Do geographic inquiry:** construct maps and graphic representations of
   familiar and unfamiliar places; use maps, photographs, and other
   representations to explain how location connects to environmental
   characteristics; use maps of different scales to describe cultural and
   environmental characteristics. (C3 D2.Geo.1–3.3-5)
2. **Explain people–environment connections:** describe how culture influences
   how people modify and adapt to environments; explain how places change over
   time; describe how environmental and cultural characteristics shape where
   people settle; explain how settlements relate to natural resources; explain
   why environmental characteristics vary among world regions. (C3 D2.Geo.4–6,
   8, 10.3-5)
3. **Explain movement and interdependence:** describe how cultural and
   environmental characteristics affect the movement of people, goods, and
   ideas; describe how economic activity patterns change through interaction
   with nearby and distant places. (C3 D2.Geo.7, 11.3-5)
4. **Work with historical evidence:** build and use chronological sequences;
   compare life in past time periods to life today; summarize how different
   kinds of historical sources explain past events; compare sources about the
   same past event; infer a source's audience and purpose from the source
   itself; explain probable causes and effects; use evidence to develop a claim
   about the past. (C3 D2.His.1, 2, 9–11, 13, 14, 16.3-5)
5. **Take perspectives seriously:** explain why people in the same historical
   period held different perspectives; explain how historical context connects
   to people's perspectives; identify the beliefs, experiences, and values
   behind their own and others' points of view on civic issues. (C3 D2.His.4,
   5.3-5; D2.Civ.10.3-5)
6. **Understand government:** distinguish the responsibilities and powers of
   government officials at different levels and branches; examine the origins
   and purposes of rules, laws, and key U.S. constitutional provisions; explain
   how groups make rules that create responsibilities and protect freedoms;
   explain the origins, functions, and structure of systems of government,
   including those in the U.S. and state constitutions. (C3 D2.Civ.1, 3–5.3-5)
7. **Participate as a citizen:** explain how a democracy relies on people's
   responsible participation and what that implies for individuals; describe how
   people benefit from and are challenged by working together; apply civic
   virtues and democratic principles in school settings; use deliberative
   processes in group decisions; compare decision-making procedures; explain
   how rules and laws change society and how people change rules and laws;
   explain how policies are developed to address public problems; illustrate
   historical and contemporary ways people changed society. (C3 D2.Civ.2, 6, 7,
   9, 11–14.3-5)
8. **Reason economically:** compare benefits and costs of choices; identify
   incentives that influence decisions; identify human, physical, and natural
   resources used to produce goods and services; explain why people and
   businesses specialize and trade; explain money's role in exchange; explain
   how profits influence sellers; identify external benefits and costs; explain
   how government pays for the goods and services it provides; explain how
   trade creates economic interdependence among nations. (C3 D2.Eco.1–5, 7, 8,
   12, 14.3-5)
9. **Inquire and argue from evidence:** identify disciplinary concepts behind
   compelling and supporting questions; explain how supporting questions help
   answer compelling questions; determine which sources will help; gather
   information from multiple sources using origin, structure, and context;
   distinguish fact from opinion to judge credibility; identify evidence from
   multiple sources; develop claims and construct arguments and explanations
   with reasons, sequence, examples, and details; present summaries to others.
   (C3 D1.2–1.5, D3.1–3.4, D4.1–4.3.3-5)
10. **Take informed action:** use deliberative and democratic procedures to
    decide on and act on a classroom or school civic problem; explain
    strategies people use to address local problems and predict possible
    results. (C3 D4.6–4.8.3-5)

Standards source: the College, Career, and Civic Life (C3) Framework for Social
Studies State Standards (National Council for the Social Studies), grades 3–5
band. Codes and descriptions were verified against the framework's 3–5
indicator tables (opened and read 2026-10-03). **No state adoption,
accreditation, or alignment certification is claimed.** The 3–5 band spans
three grades; this track teaches each indicator at the grade-3 entry level.
Indicators the framework defers to later bands (D2.His.7–8, 9–12; D2.His.15,
6–8) are not taught here.

## Accessibility and safety

- Maps and diagrams always pair color with symbols and labels (never
  color-only); every image carries alt text and a text-only description.
- Map work offers oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative options; the adult
  scribes written explanations when writing is not the skill being measured.
- Tables are screen-reader-friendly; videos (in later unit Resource Packs) will
  require captions or transcripts.
- Investigations are observation- and interview-based; any community contact is
  adult-supervised and stays private — no learner names, photos, addresses, or
  schedules enter this repository. Capstone presentations are shared only with
  the guiding adult's approval, never published from this track.
