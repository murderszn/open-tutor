# Starter Lesson — Test how ramp height changes a roll

**Status:** draft supplement. **Time:** three 30-minute sessions. **Goal:** Plan a simple investigation, record repeat trials, and use a data pattern to explain a claim.

## Adult preparation and readiness

Use a toy car/ball, a short board or stiff cardboard ramp, books to raise one end, and tape measure or equal paper strips. Work on a clear floor away from stairs and people; adult sets height and supervises. Readiness: learner can compare distances as longer/shorter.

## Teach and model

Explain: a test is fair when only the factor in the question changes. Here, change ramp height; keep the same object, ramp, release point, and surface. Release without a push. Measure from the lower end of the ramp to the object’s stopping point each time.

**Worked example 1:** Low ramp distances 28, 30, 29 cm. The runs are close to one another (28–30 cm), suggesting a repeatable pattern for this small test.

**Worked example 2:** Higher ramp distances 41, 43, 42 cm. All three exceed the low-ramp distances in the example. These sample results do not guarantee what a learner will measure.

## Pacing and tasks

Session 1: adult models fair-test choices; guided tasks 1–2. Session 2: learner runs and records trials under supervision. Session 3: compare values, error check, and write a claim.

1. **Guided:** State the question: Does raising the ramp change how far the object rolls?
2. **Guided:** Identify changed factor and two conditions to keep steady.
3. **Independent with adult supervision:** Record three trials for low and high settings.
4. **Independent:** Compare each high-ramp distance with the low-ramp results.
5. **Adult-guided error check:** One high trial uses a different car. Explain how this could affect the comparison.
6. **Application:** Make a claim with two measured values and one limitation.

**Sample answer checks:** Changed = ramp height; same = car, release point/surface; sample low values 28–30 cm, high 41–43 cm; different car introduces another change. Actual data may differ; claim must match data.

## Exit check and product

Prompt: “What did your data suggest, and what did you hold constant?” Product: data table, labeled setup sketch, and claim-evidence sentence.

## Review rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fair test | Changes ramp height only | Names one control | Adult demonstrates matched setup |
| Data | Records repeated trials and units | Records with help | Use preprinted sample table |
| Claim | Cites results and a limitation | Gives conclusion without evidence | Use sentence frame |

Use a ball, wider ramp, accessible floor/table setup, measurement strips, or adult scribing. Adult must prevent falls and ensure ramp is low/stable. Do not interpret practice data as universal law; one setup offers limited evidence.
