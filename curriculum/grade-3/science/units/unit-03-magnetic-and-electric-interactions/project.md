# Project — The Magnet Latch (and No-Touch Keeper)

**Time:** two 30-minute sessions (plan in Week 12, build/test/improve
in Week 13) plus the Week 14 showcase · **Adult-supervised throughout**

## The challenge

Choose **one** design to build:

- **Option A — Magnet latch:** a latch that keeps a small box shut
  when the box is tipped, but lets an adult open it with one hand.
- **Option B — No-touch keeper:** a device that keeps two toy cars
  from touching when they roll toward each other.

Both options use the same science: opposite poles attract (latch),
like poles repel (keeper).

## Materials

Small cardboard box with a lid (Option A) or two toy cars and a flat
track made of cardboard (Option B); two to four bar magnets; tape;
string; cardboard scraps; ruler; science journal; pencil. No glue
unless the adult allows it; no wall outlets; no batteries in learner
builds. Small magnets are counted back into their container at the
end of every session.

## Steps

1. **Define the problem (one sentence).** Write the job and the tool.
   Example for Option A: "Design a magnet latch that keeps this box
   shut when tipped, but lets an adult open it with one hand."
2. **Write three criteria and two constraints.** Criteria must be
   testable. Example criteria for Option A: (1) the lid stays shut
   when the box is tipped upside down; (2) an adult can open the lid
   with one hand; (3) the latch uses only the allowed materials.
   Example constraints: at most two magnets; tape and string only.
   Get the adult's approval before building.
3. **Sketch the prototype.** Label where each magnet goes and which
   poles face each other: opposite poles facing for the latch
   (attract), like poles facing for the keeper (repel). Show the gap
   the keeper keeps between the cars, if you chose Option B.
4. **Build the prototype.** Tape the magnets in place. Check the
   poles before taping: test each pair by hand first, because tape
   hides the ends.
5. **Test against every criterion, twice.** For Option A: tip the box
   upside down (twice), open it with one hand (twice). For Option B:
   roll the cars toward each other (twice) and measure the closest
   gap with the ruler. Record pass or fail for each criterion, each
   time.
6. **Improve one thing.** Find the failure point from the tests — the
   place or moment the design failed — and change exactly one thing:
   move a magnet, add a second magnet pair, shorten the string, or
   change the gap. Test again, twice, and record the new result.
7. **Present.** In the Week 14 showcase, demonstrate the design,
   state the defined problem and criteria, report the test results
   (including the failure and the improvement), and make a claim:
   "My latch works because ___," supported by at least two test
   results.

## Deliverables

- Defined problem sentence, three criteria, two constraints (journal).
- Labeled sketch with magnet positions and facing poles.
- Test table: each criterion, two trials before improvement, the
  improvement made, two trials after improvement.
- A short claim talk at the showcase, citing at least two test
  results.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Full marks (2) | Partial (1) | Not yet (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defined problem | One sentence names the job and the magnet tool | Job named, tool missing, or two sentences | No defined problem |
| Criteria and constraints | 3 testable criteria and 2 constraints, adult-approved | Criteria present but one untestable, or a constraint missing | No criteria, or none testable |
| Sketch | Magnet positions and facing poles labeled correctly for the chosen option (attract for latch, repel for keeper) | Positions shown, poles missing or one pole wrong | No sketch, or poles reversed for the option |
| Testing | Every criterion tested twice, results recorded, one-variable improvement tested twice | Tested once, or improvement untested, or results missing | No recorded tests |
| Claim | Claim cites at least two test results and names the failure point and improvement | Claim cites one result, or improvement unnamed | No evidence cited |
| Safety and care | Magnets counted back, no mouthing, adult supervised all building | Minor lapse corrected by adult | Unsafe handling continued after correction |

**Score:** 10–12 = ready to move on; 7–9 = ready with one reteach
(see teacher guide); below 7 = rebuild with adult support, retesting
one criterion at a time. Scores are adult-assessed, never published.
