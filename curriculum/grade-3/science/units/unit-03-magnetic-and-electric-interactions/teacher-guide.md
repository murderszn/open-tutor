# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Magnetic and Electric Interactions

**For the guiding adult only.** This guide stays separate from
learner pages. Solutions for every item are in
[answer key](answer-key.md).

## What "ready to move on" looks like

The learner is ready for Unit 04 when they can, without prompting:
sort a new set of objects by testing; predict all four pole pairs
before testing; state both strength rules (closer is stronger; two
magnets are stronger than one) with data; explain a static pull as a
noncontact force that is not magnetism; and define a latch problem
with testable criteria. Quiz readiness is 7 of 10; assessment
readiness is 10 of 12 (item 3 needs all four pairs).

## Session-by-session notes

- **Session 1 (Lesson 01):** Do the sort before naming the rule.
  Learners who guess from shininess or weight need the coin/foil
  counterexample most. Keep the cardboard-barrier demo short; it
  previews Lesson 03.
- **Session 3 (Lesson 02):** Mark magnet ends before the lesson if
  the set is unmarked — a dot sticker on one end of each magnet.
  Teach from the marks and the tests, never from paint colors.
- **Session 5 (Lesson 03):** Read rulers from zero; a common error is
  reading from 1 cm. Run the distance test on a flat table, not on a
  slope. The practice numbers (4 cm; 6 vs. 11 clips) are fictional —
  say so out loud, and treat the learner's own trials as the real
  evidence.
- **Session 7 (project plan):** Reject untestable criteria kindly and
  immediately ("be nice" cannot be tested). Approve the plan before
  any tape is cut.
- **Session 9 (Lesson 04):** Check latex allergy first; if in doubt,
  use the comb alternative for the whole lesson. Humidity changes
  static results day to day — accept the observed ordering and do
  not force a textbook ordering.
- **Session 11 (Lesson 05):** The keeper option (repel) is the harder
  build; steer learners who struggled with poles to the latch
  (attract), which is easier to test.
- **Session 15 (re-check stations):** four stations — (1) sort six
  new objects, (2) predict four pole pairs, (3) distance ordering
  cards, (4) compass near each magnet end. The learner predicts and
  explains 3 of 4 to pass the station round.

## Misconceptions and reteaching

| Misconception | Correction | Reteach with |
|---|---|---|
| All metals / all shiny things are magnetic | Only some metals (iron, steel, nickel, cobalt) are strongly attracted; aluminum foil and most coins are not | Re-sort with foil and a coin, testing each |
| Color decides the pole | Paint is a choice; marks and tests decide | Dot/plain code on unmarked magnets |
| A magnet has one working end | Both ends act; the pair decides attract or repel | Pole-pair table, all four pairs |
| Bigger always beats closer | Distance dominates: a small magnet close up out-pulls a big magnet far away | Distance test at 1 cm vs. 10 cm |
| Static electricity is magnetism | No magnet involved; static pulls light nonmagnetic things (paper, hair) | Balloon vs. magnet on paper bits and a clip |
| Criteria are wishes | Criteria must be checkable by a test | Rewrite "be nice" as a testable criterion together |

## Reteach paths

- **Sort errors:** back to Lesson 01 with four objects only; the
  adult tests alongside, narrating each result.
- **Pole-pair errors:** back to Lesson 02 guided practice with
  dot/plain magnets; predict-test-record for two pairs per day until
  all four are right twice running.
- **Distance/strength errors:** re-run Lesson 03's two real trials
  with the adult sliding the magnet; graph the chain counts as clip
  drawings.
- **Static confusion:** re-run Lesson 04 with the comb alternative;
  contrast directly: magnet on a clip (pulls), balloon on a clip
  (does not), balloon on paper bits (pulls).
- **Design criteria weak:** co-write one criterion together using
  the frame "when I ___, the latch must ___," then let the learner
  write the other two alone.

## Review game (Session 8)

Pole-pair cards: the adult shows a pole pair (N–N, S–S, N–S, S–N) and
the learner calls "attract" or "repel" before the adult tests it.
Then "what changed, what happened?": the adult names a change
("I moved the magnet farther away," "I rubbed the balloon more
times," "I flipped the magnet") and the learner names the effect.
8 rounds; 6 correct passes.

## Safety checklist (every session)

- Small magnets and paper clips counted out and counted back.
- Nothing mouthed; nothing near younger children unsupervised.
- Balloons: adult-inflated only, allergy checked, away from faces,
  popped pieces discarded at once.
- Electromagnet demo (Lesson 03 extension): adult-only, one AA
  battery, 30 seconds at most, stop if warm, never a wall outlet.
- Magnets kept away from screens, bank cards, and medical devices.

## Differentiation reminders

Oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative responses are always
accepted; the adult scribes on request. Sentence frames are in the
unit README. The no-balloon alternative and the table variants cover
the balloon and movement needs. Keep sessions to about 30 minutes
with a movement break between sessions.
