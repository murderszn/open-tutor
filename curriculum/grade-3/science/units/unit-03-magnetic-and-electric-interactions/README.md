# Unit 03 — Magnetic and Electric Interactions

**Grade:** 3 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#19](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/19)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 11–14 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 30 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

Some forces need no touching at all. A magnet can pull a paper clip
toward it through the air, and two magnets can push each other apart
before they ever meet. A balloon rubbed on a sweater can lift hair and
pick up tiny bits of paper without touching them either. This unit
teaches the learner to notice, test, and explain those
**noncontact** forces: what magnets attract, how **poles** decide
whether magnets **attract** or **repel**, how **distance** and the
number of magnets change the strength of the pull, how **static
electricity** behaves, and how magnet ideas can solve a real problem —
a latch that keeps a box shut, or a keeper that stops two moving
objects from touching.

The unit's investigation — testing how distance and magnet number
change a magnet's pull — is a one-variable fair investigation that
reuses Unit 01's fair-test routine. The unit's design project defines
a simple magnet problem with clear criteria, then builds, tests, and
improves a solution, following the scope-and-sequence for Weeks
11–14.

## Prerequisites (from Units 01–02; re-taught here as needed)

- Asking a testable question; measuring length in centimeters starting
  from zero; recording data in labeled tables.
- Planning a fair test: one variable changed, everything else the
  same, three or more trials; making a claim with evidence and a
  limitation.
- Force language from Unit 02: a force is a push or a pull, with a
  direction and a relative strength (stronger vs. weaker, never
  numbers for force size).
- Science safety practices: adult supervises every investigation;
  small magnets and paper clips stay away from the mouth; the adult
  checks latex allergies before any balloon work and handles balloon
  inflation; nothing is put near a screen, a bank card, or a medical
  device.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Test objects with a magnet and sort them as **magnetic** or
   **nonmagnetic**, and describe a magnetic force as a push or a pull
   that acts without contact (3-PS2-3).
2. Predict whether two magnets will **attract** or **repel** from
   which **poles** face each other, and explain that flipping a
   magnet changes the direction of the interaction (3-PS2-3).
3. Ask **cause-and-effect questions** about magnetic interactions and
   carry out a **one-variable fair test** of how distance, or the
   number of magnets, changes the strength of the pull (3-PS2-3;
   assessment boundary honored).
4. Ask and test **cause-and-effect questions** about **static
   electricity**: a charged balloon lifting hair and paper, and what
   changes that effect (3-PS2-3; static electricity only).
5. **Define a simple magnet design problem** with criteria for
   success and constraints on materials, and build, test, and improve
   a solution (3-PS2-4; 3–5 band connection 3-5-ETS1-1).

## Essential question

*How can something push or pull without touching — and how can I use
that to solve a problem?*

## Vocabulary

**Magnet** — an object that pulls magnetic materials toward it and
pushes or pulls other magnets without touching them. ·
**Magnetic** — attracted to a magnet (for example, steel paper clips).
· **Nonmagnetic** — not attracted to a magnet (for example, a plastic
spoon or a wooden block). · **Attract** — pull toward. ·
**Repel** — push away. · **Pole** — one end of a magnet; every magnet
has a **north pole** and a **south pole**. · **North pole / south
pole** — the two ends of a magnet; opposite poles attract, like poles
repel. · **Noncontact force** — a force that acts without the objects
touching. · **Distance** — how far apart two objects are. ·
**Strength** — how strong a force is; compared as *stronger* or
*weaker* (never numbers here). · **Static electricity** — a charge
built up on an object (for example, by rubbing a balloon) that can
pull light objects toward it without touching. · **Charged** — carrying
a static-electricity charge after rubbing. · **Criteria** — what a
good solution must do. · **Constraint** — a limit on a solution, such
as materials, size, or time. · **Prototype** — a first test version
of a design, built to be tested and improved.

## Materials

Two bar magnets (labeled north/south ends if the set marks them; if
not, the adult marks one end of each magnet with a dot sticker before
Lesson 02), steel paper clips (at least 30), a small compass
(optional), a plastic spoon, a wooden block, a rubber eraser, an
aluminum-foil square, a coin, a crayon, a cotton ball, cardboard
pieces, a ruler with centimeters, a small cardboard box with a lid
(for the latch project), tape, string, balloons (adult-handled; check
for latex allergy first), a wool sweater or fleece cloth, small torn
paper bits, a hair-safe comb (optional), science journal, pencil and
crayons. All investigations are adult-supervised. Small magnets and
paper clips are never mouthed, never shared with younger children
unsupervised, and are counted back into their container at the end of
every session. Balloons are inflated only by the adult, kept away from
faces, and put away after the session; popped balloon pieces are
thrown away at once.

## Differentiation and accessibility

- Oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative response modes always
  available; the adult scribes dictated observations on request.
  Pole-pair predictions may be shown by pointing, by arranging the
  magnets, or by drawing arrows.
- Sentence frames: "The magnet ___ because ___," "The poles are ___
  and ___, so they ___," "When the distance got bigger, the pull got
  ___," "I changed ___, and ___ happened," "My latch must ___; it
  cannot ___."
- Large-print, high-contrast magnet cards; vocabulary taught with
  objects and motion first, word second; visual science word wall;
  home-language labels welcomed.
- Short sessions with movement breaks; every investigation has a
  table variant (tray sort, ruler-track slide) and a floor variant
  (large magnets on a mat). The generated image ships with a text-only
  alternative; color is never the only cue in sorts or graphs.
- Balloon work has a no-balloon alternative: a plastic comb rubbed on
  wool, or the resource-pack video plus the adult's demonstration.

## Safety

Adult supervises every investigation and previews every procedure.
Small magnets and paper clips stay away from the mouth and are
counted back into their container after each session; keep them away
from younger children, screens, bank cards, and medical devices.
Magnets are never thrown, never hammered, and never put near the eyes.
The adult checks for latex allergy before balloon work, inflates all
balloons, keeps balloons away from faces, and discards popped pieces
at once. The optional electromagnet demonstration in Lesson 03 is
adult-only: one AA battery, insulated wire, adult assembly, 30 seconds
at most, stop at once if anything feels warm. Nothing is plugged into
a wall outlet. Hands washed after handling shared magnets.
