# Unit Assessment — Unit 03: Magnetic and Electric Interactions

**For the learner, adult-supervised.** 12 items. Oral, pointing, and
drawing answers are accepted. The adult scores with the separate
[answer key](../answer-key.md). Readiness: 10 of 12 items correct,
with item 3 requiring all four pole pairs correct.

1. **Sort by testing.** Test each object with a magnet and write it
   under "Magnetic" or "Nonmagnetic": steel paper clip, iron nail,
   plastic spoon, wooden block, rubber eraser, aluminum-foil square.
2. In your own words, what do **attract** and **repel** mean?
3. **Pole pairs.** Write "attract" or "repel" for each pair facing
   each other: N–N, S–S, N–S, S–N. Then check each one with real
   magnets.
4. Two magnets are pushing apart. What is one thing you can do to make
   them pull together? Why does it work?
5. Order these pulls from strongest to weakest: (a) one magnet 6 cm
   from a clip, (b) two magnets 1 cm from a clip, (c) one magnet 1 cm
   from a clip.
6. **Practice data (fictional).** A learner slides a magnet toward a
   paper clip and records the distance where the clip first moves:
   4 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm. What distance would you report, and why is
   three trials better than one?
7. **Practice data (fictional).** One magnet holds a chain of 6 paper
   clips. Two magnets stacked hold 11. How many more clips do two
   magnets hold, and what was the one thing changed in this test?
8. A balloon rubbed 10 times lifts a few paper bits; rubbed 20 times
   it lifts more. Write one cause-and-effect sentence about this.
9. A charged balloon is held near (a) tiny paper bits and (b) a steel
   paper clip. Predict what happens in each case and explain why the
   results differ.
10. **Picture question.** Use the
    [generated image](../assets/magnet-table-investigation.png) in
    [Lesson 01](../lessons/lesson-01-magnets-attract-and-repel.md) —
    or its text-only description there: (a) Which objects are stuck
    to a magnet? (b) Which objects are lying loose? (c) The two
    magnets do not touch each other. What does that show about
    magnetic force?
11. **Design.** Write a defined problem sentence for a magnet latch
    on a box lid, plus two testable criteria and one constraint.
12. **Error-analysis.** Correct both errors and give a reason for
    each correction: (a) "All shiny things are magnetic." (b) "A big
    magnet far away always pulls harder than a small magnet close
    up."
