# Resource Pack — Unit 03: Magnetic and Electric Interactions

**Goal (one sentence):** Give the guiding adult short, grade-3-appropriate
videos, a hands-on simulation catalog entry, and references that
reinforce magnetic vs. nonmagnetic sorts, poles, distance and strength,
static electricity, and magnet design problems.
**Age fit:** grade 3 (ages 8–9); the adult previews every video and
co-views with the learner.

## Videos and interactives (verified or labeled search links)

1. **Learn Bright — "Magnets for Kids | What is a magnet, and how
   does it work?" video** —
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7HHs98PBgk0
   *Why good:* a short elementary video that names poles (from about
   1:06), three magnet types including electromagnets (from about
   2:06), and everyday magnet uses (from about 3:13), matching Lessons
   01–02 vocabulary. *Found via search 2026-10-23; adult previews
   before showing; free, no account stated on the video page.*
2. **SciShow Kids — preferred-channel search** (search link, not a
   single verified episode):
   https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=SciShow+Kids+magnets+static+electricity+balloon
   *Why good:* the channel describes its episodes as structured around
   NGSS and ideal for grades 1–3 (verified from the channel description
   in Unit 01, 2026-10-10). The adult picks one short episode on
   magnets or static electricity. *Labeled search — adult previews.*
3. **Crash Course Kids — preferred-channel search** (search link, not
   a single verified episode):
   https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Crash+Course+Kids+magnets+forces
   *Why good:* hosted for upper-elementary learners; the channel's
   force episodes use kid language for pushes, pulls, and gravity
   (episode transcripts reviewed via Nerdfighteria wiki in Unit 02,
   2026-10-17). The adult selects the episode matching the week's
   lesson. *Labeled search — adult previews.*
4. **ComPADRE catalog entry — PhET "Magnets and Electromagnets"
   simulation** —
   https://www.compadre.org/portal/items/detail.cfm?ID=10520&Relations=1
   *Why good:* the catalog entry describes the free PhET simulation
   (compass and bar-magnet interactions, electromagnet
   characteristics) and links onward to it. Caution for the adult:
   this simulation is from the Java era, so check whether it runs in
   your browser before promising it to the learner; if it does not,
   use the hands-on stations in Lessons 01–03 instead, which cover
   the same objectives. *Found via search 2026-10-23; free access
   stated in the catalog entry.*
5. **NASA Space Place** — https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/
   *Why good:* NASA's kids' site with short articles and games;
   useful for extension reading about Earth's magnetism and compasses
   (Lesson 02 extension). *Listed in the repo resource map; free, no
   account.*

## Web references

1. **NSTA — NGSS standard display for 3-PS2-4** —
   https://my.nsta.org/ngss/DisplayStandard?view=pe&id=50
   *Why good:* the National Science Teaching Association's official
   display of the performance expectation, clarification statement,
   and disciplinary core idea (PS2.B) for the design problem. Adult
   reference. *Found via search 2026-10-23.*
2. **The Wonder of Science — 3-PS2-3: Electric and Magnetic Forces**
   (mirror page) —
   https://paul-andersen-xw6e.squarespace.com/3ps23
   *Why good:* reproduces the standard's exact wording, clarification
   statement, and assessment boundary (manipulable objects; static
   electricity only). Adult reference for what to teach and what to
   leave out. *Found via search 2026-10-23; mirror of the Wonder of
   Science page — the adult checks the wording against the unit's
   standards notes.*
3. **South Dakota Department of Education — Grade 3 "Electricity and
   Magnets" standards bundle (PDF)** —
   http://DOE.SD.GOV/ContentStandards/documents/Science-Frameworks/3rd-Electricity-24.pdf
   *Why good:* the bundle page reproduces the full 3-PS2-3 and 3-PS2-4
   wording, clarification statements, and assessment boundaries used
   to corroborate this unit's standards notes. Adult reference.
   *Found via search 2026-10-23.*
4. **OKSci Framework — 2026 Grade 3: Magnets** —
   http://okscienceframework.pbworks.com/w/page/163731621/2026%20Grade%203%3A%20Magnets
   *Why good:* a teacher-facing bundle analysis restating 3-PS2-3 and
   3-PS2-4 in plain language, with student and teacher actions. Adult
   background and lesson-planning aid. *Found via search 2026-10-23.*
5. **KnowItAll (South Carolina ETV) — "Magnets: Are you attracted to
   them... or are you repelled?"** —
   https://www.knowitall.org/lessons/magnets-are-you-attracted-themor-are-you-repelled
   *Why good:* a classroom lesson page on poles, attraction and
   repulsion, and compasses, written for elementary learners and
   usable as adult background or read-aloud. *Found via search
   2026-10-23; free, no account.*
6. **Scholastic SuperScience — "Made With Magnets!"** —
   https://sn3.scholastic.com/content/classroom_magazines/superscience/issues/2021-22/020122/made-with-magnets.html
   *Why good:* a short student-facing article explaining magnetic
   fields, iron/nickel/cobalt attraction, permanent magnets, and
   electromagnets in plain language matching Lessons 01–03. *Found
   via search 2026-10-23; adult previews before assigning.*
