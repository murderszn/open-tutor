# Practice — Unit 03: Magnetic and Electric Interactions

Work with the adult. Test with real magnets wherever an item says
"test." Answers are in the separate
[answer key](answer-key.md) — do not look at them before trying.

## Week 11 — Magnet sorts and noncontact forces

1. **Sort by testing.** Test each object with a magnet and write it
   under "Magnetic" or "Nonmagnetic": steel paper clip, iron nail,
   plastic spoon, wooden block, rubber eraser, aluminum-foil square,
   crayon, cotton ball. Do not guess — every object must be tested.
2. A magnet is held near a paper clip with a thin piece of cardboard
   between them. The clip moves toward the magnet. Did the force need
   contact? Explain in one sentence.
3. Two bar magnets snap together with a click. Is the force between
   them a push or a pull, and is it called attract or repel?
4. The adult flips one magnet around. Now the magnets push apart and
   will not meet. What is this called, and what changed?
5. **Error-analysis.** A friend says, "All shiny things are
   magnetic." Use one object from item 1 to show why this is wrong.
6. **Picture questions.** Use the
   [generated image](assets/magnet-table-investigation.png) in
   [Lesson 01](lessons/lesson-01-magnets-attract-and-repel.md) (or its
   text-only description there): (a) Which objects are stuck to a
   magnet? (b) Which objects are lying loose? (c) The two magnets do
   not touch each other. What does that show about magnetic force?
7. **Magnet hunt.** Name one place at home where a magnet is already
   at work. Say whether it attracts something, repels something, or
   holds something still, and what would happen if the magnet were
   removed.
8. Complete the sentence and explain it: "A magnet pulled a paper
   clip without touching it, so magnetic force is a ___ force."

## Week 12 — Distance, strength, and cause and effect

1. **Practice data (fictional).** A learner slides a magnet toward a
   paper clip and records the distance where the clip first moves:
   4 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm. What distance would you report, and why is
   three trials better than one?
2. **Practice data (fictional).** One magnet holds a chain of 6 paper
   clips. Two magnets stacked hold 11. How many more clips do two
   magnets hold? Show the subtraction.
3. Order these pulls from strongest to weakest: (a) one magnet 6 cm
   from a clip, (b) two magnets 1 cm from a clip, (c) one magnet 1 cm
   from a clip.
4. A magnet pulls a clip from 3 cm away. Predict: will it pull the
   same clip from 9 cm away? Explain with the distance rule.
5. **Pole pairs.** Write "attract" or "repel" for each pair facing
   each other: N–N, S–S, N–S, S–N. Then check each one with real
   magnets.
6. Write two cause-and-effect questions in the form "If I change ___,
   what happens to ___?" One must be about distance, one about the
   number of magnets.
7. **Error-analysis.** A learner tests one magnet at 2 cm and two
   magnets at 8 cm, and the two magnets win. The learner concludes
   "two magnets are always stronger." Explain the error.
8. **Barrier prediction.** Predict whether a magnet can attract a
   paper clip through paper, through cardboard, and through a thin
   book — and rank the three barriers from most pull getting through
   to least. Then test each one and record whether your ranking was
   right.

## Week 13 — Static electricity and design criteria

1. A balloon rubbed 10 times lifts a few paper bits. The same balloon
   rubbed 20 times lifts more bits. What changed, and what happened?
   State the cause and the effect.
2. A charged balloon is held near (a) tiny paper bits and (b) a steel
   paper clip. Predict what happens in each case and explain why the
   results differ.
3. **Criteria check.** Which of these latch criteria are testable?
   (a) "The lid stays shut when the box is tipped upside down."
   (b) "The latch is nice." (c) "An adult can open the lid with one
   hand." For each untestable one, rewrite it so it can be tested.
4. **Error-analysis.** A friend says, "The balloon picks up paper, so
   the balloon must be a magnet." Explain why this is wrong, using
   one difference between static electricity and magnetism.
5. **No-touch keeper.** Two toy cars roll toward each other. You want
   a magnet device that keeps them from touching. Should the facing
   poles attract or repel? Draw the setup and label the poles.
6. A charged balloon held 1 cm from paper bits lifts many bits. The
   balloon is moved to 10 cm away. Predict what happens, and explain
   with the distance idea from Lesson 03.
7. A charged balloon sticks to a wall. Then the adult presses the
   balloon flat against the wall and lifts it away. Predict what
   happens to the balloon's pull now, and explain why.
8. **Latch sketch.** Sketch a magnet latch for a box lid. Label where
   each magnet goes, which poles face each other, and one test you
   would run for each of your three criteria.
