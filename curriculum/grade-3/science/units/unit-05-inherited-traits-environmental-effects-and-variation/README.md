# Unit 05 — Inherited Traits, Environmental Effects, and Variation

**Grade:** 3 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#19](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/19)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 19–22 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 30 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

A bean seed grows into a bean plant, never into a sunflower. A brown
dog with floppy ears has puppies that look a lot like her — but no
two puppies look exactly alike, and one may have pointy ears or
lighter fur. This unit teaches the learner to notice, measure, and
explain those patterns: offspring **inherit** traits from their
parents, so they resemble them; **variation** means the members of a
group are not identical; and the **environment** — water, light, food,
exercise — can change how a trait turns out. The learner's own bean
plants, planted in Unit 04 and logged ever since, are the evidence:
their heights vary from plant to plant, and a second tray grown with
half the water grows shorter. The unit ends with the learner sorting
traits into "inherited" and "environment" with evidence, and defending
the sort in an evidence talk.

The investigation reuses Unit 01's fair-test routine (one variable
changed at a time) and Unit 04's growth-log measurement ritual
(height from the soil line to the nearest centimeter, leaf count,
labeled drawing, dated entry). The fictional practice datasets in this
unit are labeled **fictional practice data** wherever they appear, so
every learner can practice the measuring and arithmetic even if their
own beans were slow or failed.

## Prerequisites (from Units 01–04; re-taught here as needed)

- Asking a testable question; measuring length in centimeters starting
  from zero; recording data in labeled tables and reading bar graphs
  and pictographs (Unit 01).
- Planning a fair test: one variable changed, everything else the
  same, repeated trials; making a claim with evidence and a
  limitation (Unit 01).
- The life-cycle pattern — birth, growth, reproduction, death — and
  the flowering-plant cycle: seed, seedling, adult plant, flower,
  seed (Unit 04).
- The bean **growth log** started in Unit 04: pots with 2–3 bean seeds
  each, height measured from the soil line to the nearest centimeter,
  leaf count, labeled drawing, dated entry. The log runs through this
  unit and Unit 06.
- Science safety practices: adult supervises every investigation;
  living things are handled gently and observed, never harmed; hands
  washed after soil work.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Explain that a **trait** is a feature of a living thing and that
   offspring **inherit** traits from their parents, so offspring
   resemble their parents but are not exactly like them (3-LS3-1).
2. Analyze data from a group of similar organisms — the bean plants'
   heights and leaf counts — to show that **variation** exists: name
   the range, the most common value, and the fair-share (mean) height
   (3-LS3-1).
3. Explain that siblings share the same parents but inherit different
   mixes of traits, using a parent–offspring table to trace which
   parent each trait came from (3-LS3-1).
4. Use evidence to explain that the **environment** can influence a
   trait: plants grown with less water grow shorter; a pet dog given
   too much food and little exercise may become overweight
   (3-LS3-2).
5. Sort traits as **inherited** or **environment-influenced** and
   defend the sort with evidence, naming one trait that is both
   (for example, height: the kind is inherited, how tall it grows
   depends on water and light) (3-LS3-1, 3-LS3-2).

## Essential question

*Why do living things look like their parents — and why are they never exactly the same?*

## Vocabulary

**Trait** — a feature of a living thing that you can observe, such as
fur color, ear shape, leaf shape, or flower color. ·
**Parent** — a living thing that produces offspring. ·
**Offspring** — the young of a living thing; a puppy is the offspring
of a dog, a seedling is the offspring of a plant. ·
**Inherit** — to receive a trait from a parent. ·
**Inherited trait** — a trait passed from a parent to its offspring. ·
**Variation** — differences among members of a group of similar
living things; no two are exactly alike. ·
**Sibling** — one of two or more offspring of the same parents. ·
**Environment** — everything around a living thing that can affect
it: water, light, soil, food, temperature, and space. ·
**Range** — the distance from the smallest value to the largest value
in a set of measurements. ·
**Most common** — the value that appears most often in a set of
measurements. ·
**Fair-share height (mean)** — the height every plant would have if
all the heights were added up and shared out equally. ·
**Stunted** — grown much shorter than usual because of poor
conditions, such as too little water. ·
**Evidence** — observations or measurements used to support a claim.

## Differentiation and accessibility

- Oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative response modes always
  available; the adult scribes dictated observations on request.
  Trait sorts may be done by placing picture cards in piles instead
  of writing.
- Sentence frames: "A trait is ___," "The puppy inherited ___ from
  its mother because ___," "The plants vary: the tallest is ___ and
  the shortest is ___," "When the water was less, the plants grew
  ___," "My evidence is ___."
- Large-print, high-contrast trait cards and data tables; vocabulary
  taught with objects and pictures first, word second; visual science
  word wall; home-language labels welcomed.
- Short sessions with movement breaks; every investigation has a
  table variant (tray measuring, card sorting) and a floor variant
  (large trait cards on a mat). The generated image ships with a
  text-only alternative; color is never the only cue in sorts or
  graphs — every bar is labeled with its number.
- If the bean plants failed or the log is missing, the labeled
  fictional Practice Tray data carries every measuring, plotting, and
  arithmetic task; no learner is blocked by a dead plant.

## Safety

Adult supervises every investigation and previews every procedure.
Living things are observed gently, never pulled up, broken, or
harmed. Hands washed after soil work and after handling shared
trays. Water is poured slowly by the adult or with the adult's hand
guiding the cup; spills are wiped at once so nobody slips. Nothing
is tasted or put in the mouth. The demo tray uses the same bean
seeds and potting soil as Unit 04 — no new chemicals, fertilizers,
or unknown materials.
