# Answer Key — Unit 05: Inherited Traits, Environmental Effects, and Variation

**For the guiding adult only.** Never place this key beside a student
question. Every item below was solved independently and reconciled
with the lesson, practice, quiz, and assessment pages. Accept
equivalent wording when the reasoning and the numbers match.

## Lesson 01 — Offspring look like their parents

**Generated-image activity:**

1. Mother dog's traits: brown fur, floppy ears, white chest patch.
2. The puppy on the left looks most like the mother: it shares all
   three traits — brown fur, floppy ears, white chest patch.
3. The puppy on the right has pointy ears. The mother does not — her
   ears are floppy. The pointy ears may have come from the father,
   who is not in the picture; the picture alone cannot tell us.
4. No. Floppy ears are shared by the mother and the left and middle
   puppies, but not the right puppy. Brown fur is shared by the
   mother and the left and right puppies, but not the middle puppy
   (light tan). The white chest patch is shared by the mother and the
   left and right puppies, but not the middle puppy. No single trait
   is shared by all three puppies and the mother — each puppy got a
   different mix.

**Guided practice:**

1. Mother's traits: brown fur, floppy ears, white chest patch. Left
   puppy: same, same, same. Middle puppy: different (light tan),
   same (floppy), different (no patch). Right puppy: same (brown),
   different (pointy), same (patch).
2. No — the middle puppy's fur is light tan, not brown like the
   mother's. It inherited a different mix; its fur color may have
   come from its father.
3. Error: "exactly like" is never true for offspring. The left puppy
   is smaller and younger, and it still has its own mix of traits —
   it matches the mother on the three traits shown, but it is a
   different dog.

**Independent practice:**

4. Drawing: mother plus three puppies, each labeled with fur color,
   ear shape, and chest patch. Accept any clear labeling that matches
   the picture (left: brown/floppy/patch; middle: light tan/floppy/no
   patch; right: brown/pointy/patch).
5. Example sentences: "The left puppy inherited its brown fur from
   its mother." / "The middle puppy's fur is light tan, different
   from its mother's brown fur." Any two sentences using **inherit**
   or **inherited** correctly are acceptable.
6. Bean-plant traits (any three): leaf shape, stem color, flower
   color if any. For each: "The seedling inherited this from its
   parent plant."
7. "Offspring look like their parents because they inherit traits
   from them, but each offspring gets a different mix from its two
   parents, so no two are exactly alike."

**Application:** Any two real parent–offspring pairs with one shared
trait and one difference each. Accept the learner's observations if
the shared trait is genuinely shared and the difference is real.

**Exit check:** The middle puppy shares floppy ears with its mother
and differs in fur color (light tan, not brown). The word is
**inherit**.

## Lesson 02 — Variation in our bean plants

**Guided practice:**

2. Shortest plant: 12 cm. Tallest: 17 cm. They are 17 − 12 = **5 cm**
   apart. (If the learner's real plants differ, use the real numbers
   and the same subtraction.)
3. Error: variation is about the whole group. The plants range from
   12 to 17 cm, so they do vary — two plants sharing the height of
   12 cm does not erase the differences across the group.

**Independent practice (Practice Tray, fictional):**

4. Day 28 log entries: accept the learner's real measurements if
   their plants were measured; otherwise the Practice Tray values
   (12, 15, 14, 17, 12 cm; 6, 8, 7, 9, 6 leaves).
5. Bar graph: five bars at 12, 15, 14, 17, 12 cm, each labeled with
   its number. Sentence: "The plants vary from 12 cm to 17 cm."
6. (a) Most common height: **12 cm** (appears twice). (b)
   Fair-share height: 12 + 15 + 14 + 17 + 12 = **70** cm;
   70 ÷ 5 = **14 cm**.
7. "Variation means differences among members of a group of similar
   living things. Our bean plants showed it: same kind, same tray,
   heights from 12 to 17 cm."

**Application:** Accept any sentence true for both groups, for
example: "Living things of the same kind vary because each one
inherits a different mix of traits." (The bean-plant variation also
includes small environment differences; accept either framing if the
learner names the mix or the differing care.)

**Exit check:** Range 12 to 17 cm, 5 cm apart. Variation means
differences among members of a group.

## Lesson 03 — Siblings are alike and different

**Guided practice:**

1. Kitten B: gray fur from the father, green eyes from the mother.
   Full sentence: "Kitten B inherited its gray fur from its father
   and its green eyes from its mother."
2. No — Kitten C still has a father, and it may carry traits we
   cannot see in this table (for example, the shape of its paws).
   Looking like one parent does not mean the other parent gave
   nothing.
3. Error: same parents do not mean same looks. The cat table shows
   three kittens with three different mixes (A: orange fur, blue
   eyes; B: gray fur, green eyes; C: orange fur, green eyes).

**Independent practice:**

4. Cat-family table: Kitten A — fur from the mother (orange), eyes
   from the father (blue). Kitten B — fur from the father (gray),
   eyes from the mother (green). Kitten C — fur from the mother
   (orange), eyes from the mother (green).
5. Drawings: three small cats labeled with the fur and eye colors
   from the table.
6. Example sentences: "Kittens A and B are alike because they are
   siblings from the same parents." / "Kittens A and B are different
   because Kitten A has orange fur and blue eyes and Kitten B has
   gray fur and green eyes." Any equivalent pair using **sibling** is
   acceptable.
7. "Two puppies from the same mother can have different fur colors
   because each puppy inherits a different mix of traits from its two
   parents."

**Application:** Accept any parent pair with two drawn siblings
showing different mixes, each trait labeled with the parent it came
from.

**Exit check:** Kitten A: fur color from the mother (orange), eye
color from the father (blue).

## Lesson 04 — The environment changes traits

**Guided practice:**

1. Tallest demo plant: **9 cm**. Shortest main-tray plant: **12 cm**.
   Difference: 12 − 9 = **3 cm**.
2. Only the water was different. Claim: "Less water made the plants
   shorter." Evidence: the tallest demo plant (9 cm) is still shorter
   than the shortest main-tray plant (12 cm).
3. Error: the demo plants are the same kind of bean — same seeds,
   same soil, same light. The difference is the water, not the kind.

**Independent practice:**

4. Two-tray table: accept the learner's real measurements; otherwise
   the fictional values (main 12, 15, 14, 17, 12 cm; demo 8, 7, 9, 6,
   8 cm; leaves main 6, 8, 7, 9, 6; demo 5, 4, 6, 4, 5).
5. "Less water made the plants **shorter** because **the tallest
   plant with half the water (9 cm) was still shorter than the
   shortest plant with full water (12 cm)**." Any equivalent claim
   with the two numbers is acceptable.
6. No — the shortness came from the dark corner, not from the
   parents. The seeds carry the bean-plant kind, and in good light
   and water the new plants grow normally.
7. Drawing: two trays side by side, tall plants on the left labeled
   12–17 cm (full water), short plants on the right labeled 6–9 cm
   (half water).

**Application:** Accept any real example where an environment change
and a trait change are both named, plus what would happen if the
environment changed back (for example, a plant leaning toward the
window would straighten if turned regularly; a pet that grew heavier
would slim down with less food and more exercise).

**Exit check:** Less water made the plants shorter — the whole demo
tray (6–9 cm) sits below the whole main tray (12–17 cm).

## Lesson 05 — Inherited or environment?

**Guided practice:**

2. A learned trick is **environment** — the dog learned it during
   its life; it was not passed from a parent. A puppy of that dog
   would still need to learn it.
3. Error: scars happen during life and are not passed on. A scarred
   horse's foals do not have scars.

**Independent practice:**

4. (a) A bird's feather color — **Inherited** (passed from parents).
   (b) A plant's leaf shape — **Inherited** (passed from the parent
   plant). (c) A cat's weight after eating too much — **Environment**
   (too much food changed it). (d) A dog's tail length —
   **Inherited** (passed from parents). (e) A bean plant's height in
   a dark corner — **Environment** (too little light made it short).
   (f) A rabbit's long ears — **Inherited** (passed from parents).
5. Example sentences: "A dog's floppy ears are inherited because the
   puppy got them from its parents." / "A plant's height after a dry
   summer is environment because too little water made it short."
6. "Height is a both trait because the plant inherits the bean-plant
   kind from its parent, and how tall it grows depends on water and
   light."
7. "Plants and animals do not get all their traits from their
   parents — for example, the demo tray plants were shorter because
   they got half the water, not because their parents were short."
   Any unit example with the environment cause is acceptable.

**Application:** Accept any one-minute talk that names the trait, the
pile, and the evidence, with a follow-up answered from evidence.

**Exit check:** Floppy ears: inherited. Height after a dry summer:
environment. Both: height — the kind is inherited, the growth
depends on water and light.

## Unit assessment

1. A trait is a feature of a living thing you can observe. Animals:
   fur color, ear shape (any two). Plants: leaf shape, flower color
   (any two).
2. Inherit means to receive a trait from a parent. Example sentence:
   "The seedling inherited its leaf shape from the parent plant."
3. Puppy on the left: shares brown fur, floppy ears, white chest
   patch — all three; differs only in size and age. Puppy in the
   middle: shares floppy ears; differs in fur color (light tan, not
   brown) and has no white chest patch. Puppy on the right: shares
   brown fur and the white chest patch; differs in ear shape (pointy,
   not floppy).
4. Fur color from the father (gray); eye color from the father (blue).
5. Each kitten inherits a different mix of traits from its two
   parents — one got orange fur from the mother, the other got gray
   fur from the father — so siblings from the same parents can look
   different.
6. (a) 12 + 15 + 14 + 17 + 12 = **70 cm**. (b) 70 ÷ 5 = **14 cm**
   fair-share height. (c) Range 12 to 17 cm; 17 − 12 = **5 cm**
   apart.
7. **6 leaves**; **2** plants have it.
8. (a) **5 cm** each week. (b) 13 + 5 = **18 cm**. (c) A prediction
   is a guess from the pattern; a measurement is what you actually
   read with the ruler.
9. (a) **9 cm**. (b) Shorter than the shortest main-tray plant by
   12 − 9 = **3 cm**. (c) Claim: less water made the plants shorter.
   Evidence: the tallest demo plant (9 cm) is still shorter than the
   shortest main-tray plant (12 cm), and only the water was different.
10. Probably tall — they inherit the bean-plant kind and get good
    soil and water. In dry soil they would be shorter, because the
    environment (water) affects how tall they grow.
11. (a) Inherited — passed from its parents. (b) Environment — the
    dry summer (too little water) made it short. (c) Inherited —
    passed from its parents. (d) Environment — the scar happened
    during the horse's life; it was not passed on. (e) Inherited —
    passed from the parent plant. (f) Environment — too much food
    changed the dog's weight.
12. Accept any unit example with the environment cause, for example:
    "The demo tray plants were shorter because they got half the
    water — same seeds, different environment — so traits do not all
    come from parents." Or: "The overweight dog's weight came from
    too much food and too little exercise, not from its parents."
