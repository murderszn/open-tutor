# Lesson 04 — The Environment Changes Traits

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** the two trays (main tray and
demo tray) or the fictional tray tables below, ruler with centimeters,
science journal, pencil

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will learn that traits are
not only inherited — the **environment** can change them too. You
will compare two trays of bean plants: one got full water, one got
half the water. Everything else was the same. What happened?

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "In Lesson 02 our bean plants varied
from 12 to 17 cm tall. They all got the same water and light. What do
you think would happen if one tray got only half the water?" Any
prediction about shorter or weaker plants shows readiness — and a
prediction is not yet evidence, which is the point of today's test.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

The **environment** is everything around a living thing that can
affect it: water, light, soil, food, temperature, and space. Some
traits are inherited from parents — fur color, leaf shape, flower
color. But how a trait turns out can depend on the environment. A
plant can inherit the bean-plant kind from its parent and still grow
short if it does not get enough water. The kind is inherited; the
height is shaped by the environment too.

Scientists test this with a fair test: grow two groups the same way,
change only one thing, and compare. In our Two-Tray Test the adult
planted a second tray — the **demo tray** — with the same kind of
bean seeds, the same soil, the same light, and the same care. The
only change: the demo tray got **half the water**. One variable
changed, everything else the same — the Unit 01 fair-test routine.

**Worked example 1 — the two trays at Day 28 (fictional practice data):**

> Adult shows this table, labeled **fictional — not our plants**:
>
> | Tray | Heights at Day 28 (cm) | Leaves |
> |---|---|---|
> | Main tray (full water) | 12, 15, 14, 17, 12 | 6, 8, 7, 9, 6 |
> | Demo tray (half water) | 8, 7, 9, 6, 8 | 5, 4, 6, 4, 5 |
>
> Adult thinks aloud: "Main tray: shortest 12 cm, tallest 17 cm.
> Demo tray: shortest 6 cm, tallest 9 cm. The tallest demo plant is
> 9 cm. The shortest main-tray plant is 12 cm. So the tallest plant
> with half water is still shorter than the shortest plant with full
> water — by 12 − 9 = **3 cm**. Every demo plant is shorter than
> every main-tray plant. Only the water was different. So my claim
> is: **less water made the plants shorter**. My evidence is the two
> height ranges. The plants are **stunted** — grown much shorter
> than usual because they did not get enough water."

**Worked example 2 — the overweight dog:**

> Adult reads the case: "A pet dog is given too much food and very
> little exercise, and it becomes overweight. Is its weight an
> inherited trait? No. The dog inherited its fur color and ear shape
> from its parents, but its weight changed because of its
> environment — too much food, too little exercise. If that dog has
> puppies and the puppies eat the right amount and run and play, the
> puppies will not be overweight just because their parent was. The
> environment changed the parent's trait; it did not rewrite what the
> parent passes on."

Key idea to say plainly: **The environment can change how a trait
turns out. Less water makes plants shorter. Too much food and too
little exercise can make a dog overweight. The trait is still the
living thing's own — but the environment shaped it.**

## Measuring activity

Measure the demo tray now (Day 14 if planted in Week 19 — adjust to
the learner's real dates) and compare with the main tray's log entries
from when those plants were the same age. Record both trays in one
table: date, height, leaves. If the demo tray was not planted, use the
fictional Day-28 table above and say so — the comparison still works
as data analysis, and the adult notes in the journal that the numbers
are practice data, not the learner's own measurements.

## Guided practice (adult coaches)

1. Adult and learner read the two-tray table together and find the
   tallest demo plant and the shortest main-tray plant. Learner does
   the subtraction (12 − 9 = 3 cm) out loud.
2. Adult asks: "What was the only thing different between the two
   trays?" Learner answers: the water. "So what can we claim, and
   what is our evidence?" Learner states the claim with the evidence.
3. **Error-analysis:** Adult says, "The demo plants are shorter, so
   they must be a different kind of bean." Learner explains the
   error: same seeds, same kind — the difference is the water, not
   the kind.

## Independent practice

4. Complete the two-tray table in the journal: heights and leaf
   counts for both trays, labeled with the date.
5. Write or dictate the claim in the frame: "Less water made the
   plants ___ because ___." Fill both blanks with the evidence.
6. Answer in a full sentence: "A bean plant grew short in a dark
   corner. Its seeds are planted in good light and water. Will the new
   plants be short and pale too? Why or why not?"
7. Draw the two trays side by side at Day 28: tall plants on the
   left (full water), short plants on the right (half water), each
   labeled with its height range.

## Application

Environment hunt: with the adult, find one living thing at home whose
trait was shaped by its environment (for example, a houseplant that
leaned toward the window, a plant that grew pale in a dark corner, a
pet that grew heavier after a holiday of extra treats). Describe what
changed in the environment and what changed in the living thing — and
say what would happen if the environment changed back.

## Exit check

Adult asks: "The tallest plant with half the water is 9 cm. The
shortest plant with full water is 12 cm. What does that tell you
about water and height?" (Less water made the plants shorter — the
whole demo tray is below the whole main tray.)

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** compare only the tallest plant in each tray first,
  then the shortest; use the subtraction with counters or a number
  line; the adult reads the table aloud.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the taller tray, draw the two
  trays at their heights, or sort plant cards into "full water" and
  "half water" piles by height.
- **Extension:** design a light test — same seeds, same soil, same
  water, one tray in bright light and one in a dim corner. Name the
  one variable that changes, what you would measure, and what you
  would predict. (Do not run it yet — it is a plan, and the adult
  decides whether to run it in Unit 06.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: "the short plants are a different kind" and
"whatever happens to a parent is passed to its babies" (the
overweight-dog and dark-corner cases exist to break the second one).
Also watch for "the demo tray got less water AND less light" — the
test changed only the water; the light was the same for both trays,
and the adult should say so before the lesson. If the learner's real
demo tray shows a smaller difference than the fictional table, accept
the observed ordering and do not force the textbook ordering — the
claim is judged on the real evidence, not on matching the practice
numbers. See [answer key](../answer-key.md) for Lesson 04 solutions
and the misconception notes.
