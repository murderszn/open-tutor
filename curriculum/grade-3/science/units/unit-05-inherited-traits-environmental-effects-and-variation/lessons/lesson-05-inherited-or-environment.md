# Lesson 05 — Inherited or Environment?

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** trait cards (from the project
page or drawn), science journal, pencil

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will sort traits into two
piles — **inherited** and **environment** — and defend your sort
with evidence. You will also find a trait that belongs in both piles
at once.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "Name one trait a puppy inherits
from its mother, and one trait that the environment can change."
Any inherited example (fur color, ear shape) and any environment
example (height with little water, weight with too much food) shows
readiness.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

Here is the sorting rule. Ask two questions about a trait:

1. **Was it passed from a parent?** If yes, it is an **inherited**
   trait. Fur color, ear shape, leaf shape, and flower color are
   inherited — the offspring got them from a parent.
2. **Did the environment change how it turned out?** If yes, it is an
   **environment** trait. A plant's height after a dry summer, a
   dog's weight after too much food, and a scar on a horse's leg are
   environment traits — they happened during the living thing's life,
   not at birth.

Some traits need both questions answered yes. Height is the best
example: a bean plant inherits the bean-plant kind from its parent,
and how tall it grows depends on water and light. So height is
**both** — inherited and environment. When a trait is both, say so
and explain both parts. That is the strongest answer in science: not
one pile, but a reasoned "both."

**Worked example 1 — sorting six traits:**

> Adult shows six trait cards and sorts them aloud:
>
> | Trait | Sort | Why |
> |---|---|---|
> | A cat's striped fur | Inherited | Passed from its parents; the stripes were there from birth. |
> | A plant's height after a dry summer | Environment | The dry summer (too little water) made it short. |
> | A dog's floppy ears | Inherited | Passed from its parents. |
> | A scar on a horse's leg | Environment | The scar happened during the horse's life; it was not passed from a parent. |
> | A bean plant's flower color | Inherited | Passed from the parent plant. |
> | A pet dog's weight after too much food | Environment | Too much food and too little exercise changed it. |
>
> Adult thinks aloud: "A scar is the trickiest card. A scar is on the
> body, but it is not inherited — a scarred horse does not have
> scarred foals. If a trait happened during life, it stays with that
> one living thing."

**Worked example 2 — height is both:**

> Adult says: "Take height. A bean plant's parent was a bean plant,
> so the seedling inherits the bean-plant kind — it will never grow
> into a sunflower. That part is inherited. But how tall it grows
> depends on water and light — our demo tray proved that: same seeds,
> half the water, shorter plants. That part is environment. So height
> is **both**: the kind is inherited, the amount of growth depends on
> the environment. When you sort height, put it in the middle and
> explain both parts."

Key idea to say plainly: **Some traits are inherited, some are shaped
by the environment, and some — like height — are both. Always give
your evidence.**

## Guided practice (adult coaches)

1. Adult and learner sort the six cards from the worked example
   together, saying the reason for each card out loud.
2. Adult asks: "A dog learned to sit when its owner says 'sit.' Is
   that inherited or environment?" Learner answers: environment — a
   learned behavior is not passed from a parent; the puppy would
   still need to learn it. (A learned trick is not inherited.)
3. **Error-analysis:** Adult says, "A horse got a scar, so its foals
   will have scars too." Learner explains the error: scars happen
   during life and are not passed on.

## Independent practice

4. Sort six new traits into Inherited / Environment / Both, with one
   reason each: (a) a bird's feather color, (b) a plant's leaf shape,
   (c) a cat's weight after eating too much, (d) a dog's tail length,
   (e) a bean plant's height in a dark corner, (f) a rabbit's long
   ears.
5. Write or dictate two sentences: one about a trait that is only
   inherited, one about a trait that is only environment, each with
   its evidence.
6. Explain in a full sentence: "Why is height a 'both' trait?"
7. Answer in a full sentence: "A friend says plants and animals get
   all their traits from their parents. Use one example from this
   unit to explain why that is not the whole story."

## Application

Evidence talk: the learner picks one trait from the sort and gives a
one-minute talk to the adult — the trait, the pile, and the evidence.
The adult asks one follow-up question ("How do you know?" or "What
would change your mind?"), and the learner answers with evidence,
not with "because I think so."

## Exit check

Adult holds up two cards — "a dog's floppy ears" and "a plant's
height after a dry summer" — and asks the learner to sort each one
and give one reason. Then: "Name one trait that is both, and explain
why." (Ears: inherited. Height after drought: environment. Both:
height — kind inherited, growth depends on water and light.)

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** sort three cards instead of six; the adult reads each
  card aloud; sort by placing cards in labeled piles instead of
  writing.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the pile, draw the trait in
  the right column, or act out the environment cause (pouring little
  water, eating too much).
- **Extension:** find a trait that is hard to sort (for example, a
  bird's song — partly inherited, partly learned from other birds)
  and explain why it is hard. The adult confirms that some traits
  are genuinely hard to sort, and "both, with a reason" is a good
  scientific answer.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: "if it is on the body, it must be inherited"
(the scar and the learned trick exist to break this) and "a trait
belongs in exactly one pile" (height breaks this — accept "both with
a reason" as the strongest answer). Watch for learners sorting the
overweight dog as inherited because "the dog's parent was fat too" —
repeat that the puppies' weight depends on their own food and
exercise, not on their parent's weight. No human examples are used.

misconception notes.
