# Lesson 01 — Offspring Look Like Their Parents

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** the generated dog-family
picture (below), science journal, pencil, crayons

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will learn three new science
words: **trait**, **offspring**, and **inherit**. You will look at a
dog family and find out which traits the puppies got from their
mother — and why no puppy looks exactly like her.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "In Unit 04 you planted beans and
watched them grow. A bean seed grows into a bean plant, not into a
sunflower. Why do you think a baby living thing grows into the same
kind of living thing as its parent?" Any answer about babies being
like their parents, or seeds growing into the same kind of plant,
shows readiness.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

A **trait** is a feature of a living thing that you can observe. Fur
color is a trait. Ear shape is a trait. Leaf shape is a trait.
Flower color is a trait. If you can see it, describe it, or measure
it, it may be a trait.

A **parent** is a living thing that produces young. The young are
called **offspring**. A puppy is the offspring of a dog. A seedling
is the offspring of a plant. A kitten is the offspring of a cat.

When an offspring receives a trait from a parent, scientists say the
offspring **inherits** that trait. **Inherit** means *to receive a
trait from a parent*. A puppy inherits its fur color from its
parents. A bean seedling inherits its leaf shape from the plant that
made the seed.

Here is the big pattern of this unit: **offspring look like their
parents, but they are never exactly the same as their parents.**
They get some traits from one parent and some traits from the other
parent, so each offspring is a different mix. That is why puppies
from the same mother can look different from each other.

**Worked example 1 — a bean plant family:**

> Adult points to the learner's bean plants (or a drawing of one):
> "This bean plant grew from a bean seed. The seed came from an
> adult bean plant. The seedling has the same kind of leaves as its
> parent — long, pointed leaves — because it inherited the leaf shape
> from its parent. It will grow into a bean plant, not a sunflower,
> because it inherited the bean-plant kind from its parent. But it
> may grow taller or shorter than its parent, and it may have more or
> fewer leaves. Same kind, same leaf shape — but not an exact copy."

**Worked example 2 — a cat family, told as a story:**

> Adult tells the story: "A mother cat has orange fur and green
> eyes. A father cat has gray fur and blue eyes. Their kitten has
> orange fur and blue eyes. Which parent did the kitten's fur color
> come from? The mother — orange fur. Which parent did the eye color
> come from? The father — blue eyes. The kitten inherited one trait
> from each parent. It looks like both of them, and exactly like
> neither of them."

Key idea to say plainly: **Offspring inherit traits from their
parents, so they look like their parents — but each offspring gets a
different mix, so no two are exactly alike.**

**Text-only alternative:** A large brown mother dog sits in the
middle of a light-green field. She has floppy ears that hang down,
and a white patch on her chest. In front of her sit three puppies in
a row. The puppy on the left is brown, has floppy ears, and has a
white patch on its chest. The puppy in the middle is light tan, has
floppy ears, and has no white patch on its chest. The puppy on the
right is brown, has pointy ears that stand up, and has a white patch
on its chest. The father dog is not in the picture.

Questions:

1. Name the mother dog's three traits: fur color, ear shape, and
   chest patch.
2. Which puppy looks most like the mother? Which traits does it share
   with her?
3. Which puppy has pointy ears? Does the mother have pointy ears?
   Where might the pointy ears have come from? (Hint: the father is
   not in the picture.)
4. Is there a trait that all three puppies share with the mother?
   Explain your answer.

## Guided practice (adult coaches)

1. Adult and learner list the mother dog's traits together (brown
   fur, floppy ears, white chest patch) and check each puppy against
   the list, saying "same" or "different" for each trait.
2. Adult asks: "The middle puppy is light tan. Is its fur color the
   same as the mother's?" Learner answers no, then explains that the
   puppy inherited a different mix — it may have gotten its fur color
   from its father.
3. **Error-analysis:** Adult says, "The left puppy looks exactly
   like its mother, so it inherited everything from her." Learner
   explains the error: the puppy is smaller, and it still has its own
   mix — and "exactly like" is never true for offspring.

## Independent practice

4. Draw the dog family in the journal: mother plus three puppies,
   each labeled with fur color, ear shape, and chest patch (drawn or
   written).
5. Write or dictate two sentences: one naming a trait a puppy
   inherited from its mother, one naming a trait that is different
   from the mother's, each using the word **inherit** or
   **inherited** correctly.
6. Name three traits of the learner's bean plants (leaf shape, stem
   color, flower color if any) and say for each one: "The seedling
   inherited this from its parent plant."
7. Answer in a full sentence: "Why do offspring look like their
   parents but never exactly like them?"

## Application

Trait hunt: walk around the home or garden with the adult and find
two parent–offspring pairs in real life (for example, a plant with a
seedling beside it, a pet with a photo of its parent, birds in a
garden). For each pair, name one trait the offspring shares with the
parent and one way they are different.

## Exit check

Adult points to the middle puppy and asks: "Name one trait this puppy
shares with its mother and one trait that is different — and tell me
the word for a trait passed from a parent." (Floppy ears shared;
light tan fur different; the word is **inherit**.)

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** fewer traits to track (fur color only, then add ear
  shape); the adult reads labels aloud; sorting by placing picture
  cards in piles instead of writing.
- **Alternate response mode:** point to the matching puppy, draw the
  traits, or act out floppy ears (hands hanging down) and pointy ears
  (hands up).
- **Extension:** add a fourth puppy of the learner's own design — draw
  it, choose its fur color, ear shape, and chest patch, and explain
  which parent each trait could have come from.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: "offspring are exact copies of their parents"
and "a puppy gets all its traits from the parent it looks most
like." Both fail on this picture: the left puppy matches the mother
on all three traits yet is still a different size and a different
dog, and the other two puppies show traits the mother does not have.
Keep the language at "traits passed from parents" — do not introduce
genes or DNA (outside the 3-LS3-1 assessment boundary). No human
family traits are used in this unit. See
[answer key](../answer-key.md) for Lesson 01 solutions and the
misconception notes.
