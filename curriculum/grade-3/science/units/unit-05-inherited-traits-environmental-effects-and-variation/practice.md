# Practice — Unit 05: Inherited Traits, Environmental Effects, and Variation

Work with the adult. Measure real plants wherever an item says
"measure." Items marked **fictional** use practice data, not your
plants. Answers are in the separate
[answer key](answer-key.md) — do not look at them before trying.

## Week 19 — Parent–offspring patterns

1. **Match the offspring to its parent kind.** Draw a line from each
   offspring to the kind of living thing it grows into: seedling →
   bean plant; puppy → dog; kitten → cat; tadpole → frog; caterpillar
   → butterfly. Then finish the sentence: "A seedling grows into a
   bean plant because it inherited the ___ from its parent."
2. What is a **trait**? Give one example from an animal and one
   example from a plant.
3. What does **inherit** mean? Use the word in a sentence about a
   puppy and its mother.
4. **Picture questions.** Use the
   [generated image](assets/dog-family-inherited-traits.png) in
   [Lesson 01](lessons/lesson-01-offspring-look-like-their-parents.md)
   (or its text-only description there): (a) Which puppy looks most
   like the mother, and which traits does it share with her? (b)
   Which puppy has pointy ears? (c) Is there one trait that all three
   puppies share with the mother? Explain.
5. **Cat family (fictional).** Mother cat: orange fur, green eyes.
   Father cat: gray fur, blue eyes. Kitten B has gray fur and green
   eyes. Which parent gave Kitten B its fur color, and which parent
   gave it its eye color?
6. **Error-analysis.** A friend says, "Siblings have the same
   parents, so they must all look the same." Use the cat family in
   Lesson 03 to show why this is wrong.
7. A bean seed grows into a bean plant, never into a sunflower. Did
   the seedling inherit the bean-plant kind from its parent? Explain
   in one sentence.
8. Draw a parent bean plant and its seedling offspring in the journal.
   Label one trait the seedling inherited from its parent, and one
   way the seedling is different from the parent.

## Week 20 — Variation data

Use the **Practice Tray (fictional practice data)** for items 1–6:

| Plant | Height (cm) | Leaves |
|---|---|---|
| Plant 1 | 12 | 6 |
| Plant 2 | 15 | 8 |
| Plant 3 | 14 | 7 |
| Plant 4 | 17 | 9 |
| Plant 5 | 12 | 6 |

1. What is the tallest height in the tray? What is the shortest
   height?
2. How many centimeters apart are the tallest and shortest plants?
   Show the subtraction.
3. Which height is the **most common**? How many plants have it?
4. Find the **fair-share height**: (a) add all five heights; (b)
   share the total equally among the 5 plants. Show both steps.
5. Leaves: what is the fewest number of leaves? The most? Which leaf
   count is the most common?
6. **Practice Bean 1 (fictional practice data).** Day 7 — 3 cm, 2
   leaves. Day 14 — 8 cm, 4 leaves. Day 21 — 13 cm, 6 leaves. (a)
   How many centimeters did it grow each week? (b) Predict its Day
   28 height and leaf count. (c) Explain why a prediction is not a
   measurement.
7. **Bar chart (fictional practice data).** Read the chart of the
   Practice Tray heights:
   - Plant 1: 12 cm
   - Plant 2: 15 cm
   - Plant 3: 14 cm
   - Plant 4: 17 cm
   - Plant 5: 12 cm

   (a) Which two plants are the same height? (b) How many centimeters
   taller is Plant 4 than Plant 1? Show the subtraction. (c) Which
   plant's height matches the fair-share height from item 4?
8. **Error-analysis.** A learner says, "Two plants are both 12 cm
   tall, so there is no variation in this tray." Explain the error.

## Week 21 — Environment effects

Use the **two-tray table (fictional practice data)** for items 1–2:

| Tray | Heights at Day 28 (cm) | Leaves |
|---|---|---|
| Main tray (full water) | 12, 15, 14, 17, 12 | 6, 8, 7, 9, 6 |
| Demo tray (half water) | 8, 7, 9, 6, 8 | 5, 4, 6, 4, 5 |

1. (a) What is the tallest demo-tray plant? (b) What is the shortest
   main-tray plant? (c) How many centimeters shorter is the tallest
   demo plant than the shortest main-tray plant? Show the
   subtraction.
2. Write the claim in the frame: "Less water made the plants ___
   because ___." Fill both blanks using the table as evidence.
3. A pet dog is given too much food and very little exercise, and it
   becomes overweight. Is the dog's weight an inherited trait or an
   environment effect? Explain in two sentences.
4. A bean plant grew short and pale in a dark corner. Its seeds are
   planted in good light and water. Will the new plants be short and
   pale too? Why or why not?
5. **Sort.** Write "Inherited," "Environment," or "Both" for each
   trait, with one reason each: (a) a cat's striped fur; (b) a plant's
   height after a dry summer; (c) a dog's floppy ears; (d) a scar on a
   horse's leg; (e) a bean plant's flower color; (f) a pet dog's
   weight after too much food.
6. Explain in two sentences why height is a **both** trait.
7. **Error-analysis.** A learner says, "The demo plants are shorter,
   so they must be a different kind of bean." Explain the error.
8. **Design a light test.** Same seeds, same soil, same water — one
   tray in bright light, one in a dim corner. (a) What is the one
   variable that changes? (b) What would you measure? (c) What would
   you predict?
