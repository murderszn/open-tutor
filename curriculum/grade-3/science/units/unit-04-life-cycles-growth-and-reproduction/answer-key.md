# Answer Key — Unit 04: Life Cycles, Growth, and Reproduction

**Teacher-only.** Do not place this file beside student questions.
Every item below was solved independently of the student pages and
reconciled with them. Oral, pointing, drawing, and card answers that
match the reasoning here count as correct. All growth-log numbers
marked "fictional" come from the Lesson 04 practice dataset, not
from any real plant.

## Lesson 01

- **Guided:** (1) birth, growth, reproduction, death. (2) Has a life
  cycle: bean seed, butterfly egg, adult bean plant, caterpillar.
  Never alive: rock, toy car. (3) Order: egg, caterpillar,
  chrysalis, adult butterfly; reproduction starts at the adult
  butterfly laying eggs. (4) Fixed order: egg, caterpillar,
  chrysalis, adult butterfly — the adult cannot come before the
  caterpillar grows and changes.
- **Independent:** (1) Loop labeled birth, growth, reproduction,
  death, with arrows returning to birth. (2) Accept any four
  sentences matching the bean: seed sprouts (birth), plant grows
  (growth), flower makes seeds (reproduction), one plant dies while
  its seeds carry on (death). (3) Have a life cycle: bean plant,
  butterfly, frog. Do not: rock, bicycle — never alive, no birth,
  growth, or reproduction. (4) Not right as a straight line: the
  missing piece is the loop — seeds start the cycle again.
- **Application:** Chicken: egg (birth), chick (growth), adult hen
  (reproduction: lays eggs), death of one hen while its eggs carry
  on. The loop starts again at the eggs.
- **Exit check:** birth, growth, reproduction, death; one living
  thing (any correct example) and one nonliving thing.

## Lesson 02

- **Image activity:** (1) Seedling = Panel 2; adult plant with a
  flower = Panel 4. (2) Germination just starting = Panel 2 (tiny
  sprout with two small leaves above the soil; Panel 1 is still a
  seed). (3) Fifth step: the flower's new seeds, with an arrow back
  to Panel 1.
- **Guided:** (1) seed, seedling, adult plant, flower, seed; pattern
  words: birth, growth, growth, reproduction, birth again.
  (2) Root grows down (drinks, anchors); shoot grows up (reaches
  for light). (3) Fixed: seed, seedling, adult plant, flower, seed
  — a seedling is a young plant and must grow before it is an
  adult. (4) No — reproduction has not happened until the flower
  makes seeds.
- **Independent:** (1) Loop with five labeled stages and arrows.
  (2) Germination needs water and warmth; the flower makes seeds
  that start the cycle again. (3) Part of the cycle: seed, seedling,
  flower. Not: egg, chrysalis, tadpole — those are animal stages.
  (4) Missing: the new seeds and the arrow back to the start; without
  them the cycle looks finished at the flower instead of looping.
- **Application:** Seeds sit at the reproduction/seed stage of the
  cycle. Planted in damp, warm soil, a seed germinates and grows
  into the same kind of plant.
- **Exit check:** seed, seedling, adult plant, flower, seed; the
  flower stage is reproduction.

## Lesson 03

- **Guided:** (1) egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, adult butterfly;
  pattern words: birth, growth, growth (with metamorphosis),
  reproduction. (2) egg, tadpole, froglet, adult frog; legs first
  appear as the tadpole becomes a froglet. (3) Same: both start from
  eggs, both grow, both have metamorphosis, both reproduce as
  adults. Different: butterfly stages vs. frog stages; butterfly
  flies, frog swims and jumps; chrysalis vs. tadpole/froglet stages.
  (4) Fixed: egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, adult butterfly — the
  caterpillar must eat and grow before it can change in the
  chrysalis.
- **Independent:** (1) Loop labeled egg, caterpillar, chrysalis,
  adult butterfly. (2) Accept any own-words version of "a big body
  change during growth, caterpillar to butterfly." (3) Order: egg,
  chick, adult hen, eggs; reproduction starts at the adult hen.
  (4) Disagree — one animal, one life, four stages; the caterpillar
  and the butterfly are the same living thing at different stages.
- **Application:** Accept any detail actually in the video or stage
  cards (for example, caterpillars shed skin as they grow; adult
  butterflies pump up their wings). If a detail disagrees with the
  lesson, the adult notes the disagreement and checks a second
  source rather than marking the learner wrong for noticing it.
- **Exit check:** egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, adult butterfly;
  metamorphosis = a big body change during growth.

## Lesson 04

- **Guided:** (1)–(2) Pots planted per the six steps; Day 1 entry:
  date, 0 cm, 0 leaves, labeled drawing. (3) Pencil-in-cup reading
  to the nearest centimeter from the rim. (4) Day 1 to Day 7 growth:
  3 − 0 = **3 cm**.
- **Independent:** (1) Accept any correct care routine: keep soil
  damp, keep in light and warmth, check each session. (2) 6 cm is
  right — height is measured from the soil line, not the table.
  (3) Predicted Day 28: 13 + 5 = **18 cm**, 6 + 2 = **8 leaves**; a
  prediction is not a measurement — it must be checked by measuring
  on Day 28. (4) Drawing shows stem, two leaves, labeled soil line,
  height arrow from the soil line.
- **Application:** Accept any reasoned placement (light, warmth,
  safe from knocks). Cold-night risk: a windowsill can get cold;
  the adult moves the pot away from the glass at night.
- **Exit check:** zero at the soil line; read to the nearest
  centimeter at the tallest tip; count leaves; draw and label;
  record at once. Day 1 entry complete as in Guided (1).

## Lesson 05

- **Guided:** (1) Accept any two valid sameness and two valid
  difference entries (see Practice set C key). (2) Seed-starters:
  bean seed, sunflower seed. Egg-starters: butterfly egg, frog egg,
  chicken egg. Tadpole is a middle stage, not a starting point.
  Plant vs. animal re-sort: plant — bean seed, sunflower seed;
  animal — butterfly egg, frog egg, chicken egg, tadpole.
  (3) No — a sunflower seed grows into a sunflower, not a bean
  plant; offspring are the same kind as their parents.
  (4) Error: butterflies have no flowers. Fix: the sameness is the
  four-part pattern (or starting small and growing, or
  reproducing), not flowers.
- **Independent:** (1) Accept any valid frog-vs.-bean same/difference
  pairs. (2) "Better" is not a science comparison; rewrite as a
  same/difference statement (for example, "A butterfly can fly and
  a bean plant cannot; both have birth, growth, reproduction, and
  death"). (3) Bean plant: seed, seedling, flower (full cycle adds
  adult plant and return seed). Butterfly: egg, caterpillar,
  chrysalis (full cycle adds adult butterfly). Label each cycle.
  (4) A bean plant — evidence: the offspring rule (a bean seed grows
  into a bean plant) and the plant cycle model.
- **Application:** Accept any correct plant cycle and animal cycle
  in four parts, plus one sameness and one difference.
- **Exit check:** Two sameness points (four-part pattern; start
  small and grow; reproduce; die while offspring carry on) and two
  difference points (seed vs. egg; flower vs. chrysalis; plant stays
  put vs. butterfly moves; metamorphosis vs. no body-shape change).

## Practice set C (Week 17)

1. Same (any two): four-part pattern; start small and grow;
   reproduce; die while offspring carry the kind on. Different (any
   two): seed vs. egg start; flower vs. chrysalis stages; butterfly
   metamorphosis vs. plant staying a plant; plant stays in one place
   vs. butterfly moves and flies.
2. Seed-starters: bean seed, sunflower seed. Egg-starters: butterfly
   egg, frog egg, chicken egg. Neither: tadpole (a middle stage of
   the frog, not a starting point).
3. Wrong: butterflies have no flowers; "both have flowers" is false
   as a sameness. Replacement (any true sameness): "Both have birth,
   growth, reproduction, and death" (or: both start small and grow).
4. Accept any rewrite that states a sameness or difference without
   "better" — for example: "A butterfly's cycle is different from a
   bean plant's because the butterfly moves and flies and has
   metamorphosis, while the plant stays in one place and grows in
   soil."
5. No — a sunflower seed grows into a sunflower. Offspring grow into
   the same kind of living thing as their parents.
6. Bean plant: seed, seedling, adult plant, flower, seed. Butterfly:
   egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, adult butterfly.
7. Predicted Day 28: **18 cm, 8 leaves** (13 + 5, 6 + 2). This is a
   prediction, not a measurement. Check it by measuring the plant
   on Day 28.
8. Accept any observation-based plan: keep a growth log, watch and
   date each stage, compare stage lengths. What it would show: stage
   lengths differ between kinds even though the four parts are the
   same — so the claim "same time because same parts" is not
   supported by observation.

## Unit assessment

1. A life cycle is the pattern of stages a living thing goes
   through: birth, growth, reproduction, death.
2. seed, seedling, adult plant, flower, seed. Seed = birth,
   seedling = growth, adult plant = growth, flower = reproduction.
   Death is the end of one plant's life; the loop goes on through
   its seeds.
3. egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, adult butterfly. Metamorphosis = a
   big body change during growth. A chrysalis is not sleeping: the
   body is changing inside (legs, antennae, and wings forming); a
   sleeping caterpillar would wake up still a caterpillar.
4. Missing stage: **tadpole**. The tadpole swims with a tail and no
   legs.
5. (a) 13 − 3 = **10 cm**. (b) Day 14 leaves: **4**; that is 4 − 0 =
   **4 more** than on Day 1.
6. **5 cm** is correct — height is measured from the soil line to
   the tallest tip, not from the pot rim.
7. Same (any two): four-part pattern; start small and grow;
   reproduce; die while offspring carry on. Different (any two):
   seed vs. egg start; flower vs. frog stages; plant stays in one
   place vs. frog moves; frog metamorphosis vs. plant growth without
   a body-shape change.
8. Seed-starters: apple seed, bean seed. Egg-starters: duck egg, frog
   egg. Neither: tadpole.
9. Bean plants. Rule: offspring grow into the same kind as their
   parents. Unit 05 will study which traits offspring share with
   parents and how offspring of the same parents still vary.
10. Model checklist (2 points per wheel, 4 total; half points
    allowed): bean wheel — five stages in order, labeled, loop
    arrows; butterfly wheel — four stages in order, labeled, loop
    arrows. Deduct for a missing stage, a wrong order, or missing
    loop arrows; accept drawn or card-built models equally.
11. Sample: "All life cycles share birth, growth, reproduction, and
    death, and my evidence is my bean wheel order and my log: my
    bean grew to 13 cm by Day 21 (fictional dataset)" — accept any
    claim of the shared pattern (or another true shared feature)
    with one model piece of evidence and one labeled log number
    (real or fictional, labeled as such).
12. Look, do not touch, and tell the adult. Do not move, hold, or
    take the eggs or nest. Reason: handling or moving wild eggs can
    harm them; observing without touching keeps them safe.

**Scoring guide (assessment items 1–9, 11–12):** 1 point each for a
correct answer with reasoning where asked; item 10 is scored 0–4 by
its checklist. Total 15 points: 13–15 strong; 10–12 on track, re-teach
the missed objective from the teacher guide; below 10, re-teach the
unit's highest-need objectives before Unit 05 and re-check with the
Week 18 stations.
