# Practice — Unit 04: Life Cycles, Growth, and Reproduction

**For the learner, adult-supervised.** Oral, pointing, and drawing
answers are accepted. Answers are in the separate
[answer key](answer-key.md). Each set has 8 items; the adult reviews
the set the same day. The Week 16 set uses one fictional dataset,
labeled as fictional wherever it appears — it is practice data, not
measurements from the learner's own plants.

## Week 15 — The pattern and the plant cycle

1. Put the four parts of a life cycle in order: death, birth,
   reproduction, growth.
2. Which of these have a life cycle, and which do not: bean plant,
   rock, butterfly, bicycle, frog, toy car? Give one reason for each
   "do not" choice.
3. Put the flowering plant stages in order: flower, seed, adult
   plant, seedling. Which stage is the birth part of the cycle?
4. Use the text-only alternative for the generated image in Lesson
   02 (Panel 1: bean seed in a pot of soil. Panel 2: tiny sprout
   with two small leaves. Panel 3: young plant with a stem and two
   larger leaves. Panel 4: tall plant with many leaves and one
   purple flower). Which panel shows a seedling? Which panel shows
   the flower stage?
5. A learner draws a bean cycle as: seed, seedling, flower. What two
   things are missing or wrong? Fix the cycle.
6. True or false, then fix the sentence if it is false: "A rock has
   a life cycle because it breaks into smaller pieces."
7. A bean seed sits in a dry cup on a shelf. Will it germinate?
   What is missing, and what would you change?
8. What job does the flower do in a flowering plant's life cycle?
   Answer in one sentence.

## Week 16 — Animal cycles and reading a growth log

**Fictional practice dataset (not the learner's own plants):**
Practice Bean 1 — Day 1: 0 cm, 0 leaves. Day 7: 3 cm, 2 leaves.
Day 14: 8 cm, 4 leaves. Day 21: 13 cm, 6 leaves.

1. Put the butterfly stages in order: adult butterfly, egg,
   chrysalis, caterpillar.
2. Put the frog stages in order: froglet, egg, adult frog, tadpole.
   Which stage comes right after the tadpole?
3. In your own words, what is metamorphosis? Then answer: a seedling
   grows taller and adds leaves. Is that metamorphosis? Why or why
   not?
4. Using the fictional dataset, how many centimeters did Practice
   Bean 1 grow from Day 7 to Day 14? Show the subtraction.
5. Using the fictional dataset, how many centimeters did Practice
   Bean 1 grow in total from Day 1 to Day 21? How many more leaves
   did it have on Day 21 than on Day 7?
6. A practice stem measures 6 cm from the soil line. A learner
   measured from the table and wrote 9 cm. Which measurement is
   right, and why?
7. Put the chicken cycle in order: adult hen, chick, egg, eggs. At
   which stage does reproduction start?
8. A learner says, "A caterpillar and a butterfly are two different
   animals." Agree or disagree, with one reason from the life cycle.

## Week 17 — Comparing cycles and error spotting

1. Compare a bean plant's cycle with a butterfly's cycle. Name two
   things that are the same and two things that are different.
2. Sort these by starting point — seed-starters, egg-starters, or
   neither (not a starting stage): bean seed, butterfly egg,
   sunflower seed, frog egg, chicken egg, tadpole.
3. Error spot: a learner's comparison chart says, "Same: both have
   flowers." What is wrong, and what is a true sameness statement
   to replace it with?
4. A learner says, "A butterfly's cycle is better because it can
   fly." Rewrite this as a science comparison (a same/difference
   statement) without the word "better."
5. A sunflower seed is planted. Will it grow into a bean plant?
   Why or why not? Use the offspring idea from Lesson 05.
6. Sort these mixed stages into two labeled cycles: egg, seed,
   chrysalis, seedling, caterpillar, flower, adult plant, adult
   butterfly.
7. Using the fictional dataset's pattern (5 cm more and 2 more
   leaves each week), predict Practice Bean 1's height and leaf
   count on Day 28. Is your answer a measurement or a prediction?
   How would you check it?
8. Two learners argue: "A bean cycle and a butterfly cycle must take
   the same amount of time, because both have four parts." How
   would you find out whether they are right? Name one observation
   you could make and what it would show.
