# Project — The Life-Cycle Museum

## The challenge

Build a small museum display that teaches a visitor two life cycles
and shows real growth evidence. The display has three parts: a bean
plant cycle wheel, a butterfly cycle wheel, and a growth-log
checkpoint. Then give a short talk (1–2 minutes, spoken, signed, or
dictated) that states one claim about life cycles and backs it with
evidence from the display.

## Materials

Card or paper plates (2), brass fasteners or tape, crayons, stage
cards from Lessons 02–03, the bean pots and growth log from Lesson
04, science journal, ruler with centimeters.

## Steps

1. **Bean wheel.** Draw a circle divided into five parts. Label the
   parts in order: seed, seedling, adult plant, flower, seed. Draw
   each stage and add arrows showing the loop goes around again.
2. **Butterfly wheel.** Draw a circle divided into four parts.
   Label the parts in order: egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, adult
   butterfly. Draw each stage and add arrows showing the loop.
3. **Growth-log checkpoint.** Measure the real beans (soil line to
   tallest tip, nearest centimeter), count leaves, and draw the
   plant. Add this entry to the log with today's date. If the beans
   have not sprouted yet, record 0 cm and 0 leaves honestly and
   write what you will check next time — do not invent a height.
4. **Claim sentence.** Write or dictate one sentence of this form:
   "All life cycles share ___, and my evidence is ___." The
   evidence must come from the wheels or the log (for example, a
   stage order, or a measured height).
5. **Museum talk.** Present the display to the adult (or to family).
   Point to each wheel in order, show the log entry, read the claim
   sentence, and answer one question from the listener.
6. **Keep the log going.** After this unit, the adult keeps the
   growth log alive through Units 05–06 with a short check each
   week. Bring the log to Unit 05: it becomes the variation dataset.

## Deliverables

- Two labeled cycle wheels, in correct order, with loop arrows.
- One dated growth-log checkpoint entry (height, leaf count,
  labeled drawing).
- One claim sentence with evidence from the display.
- A short museum talk.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Getting started (1) | On track (2) | Strong (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cycle order and loop | One wheel is out of order or has no loop arrows | Both wheels are in order with loop arrows | Both wheels are in order with loop arrows, and the learner can explain why the order cannot be shuffled |
| Labels and vocabulary | Some stages unlabeled or misnamed | All stages labeled with the unit's words (seed, seedling, flower; egg, caterpillar, chrysalis) | All stages labeled, and the learner uses birth, growth, reproduction, death correctly for both cycles |
| Growth-log evidence | Entry is missing a date, a height, or a drawing | Entry is complete (date, height from the soil line, leaf count, labeled drawing) | Entry is complete, and the learner explains what changed since the last entry using numbers |
| Talk and claim | Talk names stages but states no claim | Talk states a claim and points to one piece of display evidence | Talk states a claim, cites two pieces of evidence (one from a wheel, one from the log), and answers a listener question |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = strong museum display; 7–9 = on track, fix one
criterion with the adult; 4–6 = re-teach with the teacher guide
before the unit assessment. The rubric is a teaching tool, not a
grade for a report card. Oral, pointing, and drawn responses count
the same as written ones.
