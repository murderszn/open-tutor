# Unit 04 — Life Cycles, Growth, and Reproduction

**Grade:** 3 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#19](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/19)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 15–18 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 30 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

Every living thing has a life story with the same four chapters:
it is **born**, it **grows**, it **reproduces** (it makes new living
things of its own kind), and it **dies**. The details are wildly
different — a bean seed does not look like a butterfly egg, and a
caterpillar changes its whole body in a way a bean plant never does —
but the pattern is the same. This unit teaches the learner to build
and read **models** of life cycles: a flowering bean plant's cycle
(seed, seedling, adult plant, flower, seed) and an egg-laying animal's
cycle (butterfly: egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, adult butterfly; frog
as a second example). The learner plants real beans, measures their
growth in centimeters, counts leaves, and keeps a **growth log** with
labeled drawings. That log is not finished at the end of this unit:
it runs on as a background thread through Units 05–06, where it
becomes the variation dataset for inherited-traits work.

Week 18 is the track's **midyear review week**: the first growth-log
checkpoint, a cumulative re-teach of Units 01–04 highest-need
objectives, and the formative check.

## Prerequisites (from Units 01–03; re-taught here as needed)

- Asking a question that can be explored by observing; recording in
  words, drawings, and labeled tables (Unit 01).
- Measuring length in centimeters starting from zero; counting and
  recording in a table; reading a simple bar picture of data
  (Unit 01).
- Making a claim with evidence and a limitation; one variable changed
  at a time in a fair test (Unit 01; reused lightly here — growth
  logging is observation, not a fair test, and the lessons say so).
- Science safety practices: adult supervises every investigation;
  living things are handled gently and observed, never harmed; hands
  washed after soil work; nothing wild is collected or disturbed.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Name the four-part pattern of a life cycle — **birth, growth,
   reproduction, death** — and use it to describe any plant or animal
   cycle (3-LS1-1).
2. Build a model of a **flowering plant** life cycle in order — seed,
   seedling, adult plant, flower, seed — and explain that flowers make
   the seeds that start the cycle again (3-LS1-1).
3. Build a model of an **egg-laying animal** life cycle in order —
   butterfly: egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, adult butterfly — and
   explain **metamorphosis** in the learner's own words as a big
   body change during growth (3-LS1-1).
4. Plant beans, and **observe, measure, and record** growth over time
   in a growth log: height in centimeters from the soil line, leaf
   count, and a labeled drawing at each check (3-LS1-1, growth;
   SEP-3).
5. **Compare** two life cycles and name what is the **same** (the
   four-part pattern) and what is **different** (starting point,
   stages, how long stages last, whether the body changes shape)
   (3-LS1-1).
6. **Notice** that offspring grow into the same kind of living thing
   as their parents — a bean seed grows into a bean plant, a butterfly
   egg grows into a butterfly — as a bridge to Unit 05, where trait
   patterns are studied in full (3-LS3-1 preview; not assessed as a
   Unit 05 objective here).

## Essential question

*How are a bean plant and a butterfly alike — and how are their life
stories different?*

## Vocabulary

**Life cycle** — the pattern of stages a living thing goes through:
birth, growth, reproduction, and death, in a loop. ·
**Birth** — the start of a life; for plants in this unit, sprouting
from a seed; for the animals here, hatching from an egg. ·
**Growth** — getting bigger and changing over time. ·
**Reproduction** — making new living things of the same kind, so the
kind goes on. · **Death** — the end of one living thing's life; the
cycle goes on through its offspring. · **Seed** — a plant's starting
package: a baby plant plus stored food, in a coat. ·
**Germination (sprouting)** — a seed starting to grow: it takes in
water, a root grows down, a shoot grows up. ·
**Seedling** — a young plant, just sprouted, with its first leaves. ·
**Adult plant** — a full-grown plant that can flower and make seeds. ·
**Flower** — the part of an adult flowering plant that makes seeds
(with help from pollinators such as bees and butterflies). ·
**Egg** — the starting stage of the animals in this unit; a baby
animal grows inside it and then hatches. ·
**Caterpillar (larva)** — the eating-and-growing stage of a butterfly. ·
**Chrysalis (pupa)** — the resting stage in which a caterpillar's body
changes into a butterfly's body. · **Metamorphosis** — a big body
change during growth, such as caterpillar to butterfly. ·
**Tadpole** — the young, swimming stage of a frog. ·
**Froglet** — a young frog with legs and a shrinking tail. ·
**Offspring** — the young of a living thing. · **Growth log** — a
dated record of measurements and drawings of one growing thing.

## Materials

Dry untreated bean seeds for planting (from a garden supply, never
seeds saved for eating), small pots or cups with drainage holes,
potting soil, a watering can or cup, a ruler with centimeters,
a hand lens (optional), science journal, pencil and crayons, paper
plates and card for the cycle wheels, a brass fastener or a dot of
tape per wheel, printed or drawn stage cards (bean plant stages,
butterfly stages, frog stages), and the [generated image](assets/bean-growth-stages.png).
All investigations are adult-supervised. Planting seeds are not
eaten. Soil work is followed by hand washing. No wild eggs, tadpoles,
caterpillars, or nests are collected; animal cycles are learned from
the stage cards, the generated image's companion activities, and the
[resource-pack](resource-pack.md) videos, not from captured animals.

## Differentiation and accessibility

- Oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative response modes always
  available; the adult scribes dictated observations on request.
  Stage orders may be shown by arranging cards, by pointing, or by
  drawing arrows.
- Sentence frames: "A life cycle is ___," "First ___, then ___, next
  ___, last ___," "The bean and the butterfly are the same because
  ___; they are different because ___," "My plant grew ___ cm
  because ___," "The offspring of a ___ is a ___."
- Large-print stage cards; vocabulary taught with objects and motion
  first, word second; visual science word wall; home-language labels
  welcomed.
- Short sessions with movement breaks; every investigation has a
  table variant (tray planting, card sorts) and a floor variant
  (large stage cards walked in order). The generated image ships with
  a text-only alternative; color is never the only cue in sorts or
  graphs.
- If planting is not possible (no soil, no light, allergies), use the
  fictional growth-log dataset in Practice set B and the generated
  image for all measuring work; the objectives are still met by
  modeling and data reading, and the adult notes the substitution in
  the journal.

## Safety

Adult supervises every investigation and previews every procedure.
Planting seeds are never eaten; check the packet that the seeds are
untreated planting seeds. Hands are washed after soil work. Pots with
drainage sit on a tray; spills are wiped at once so floors are not
slippery. No wild animals, eggs, or nests are collected, moved, or
disturbed — if the learner finds a nest or eggs outdoors, the rule is
look, do not touch, and tell the adult. No live caterpillar or
tadpole kits are required; if the adult chooses one, the kit's own
care instructions are followed exactly and the animals are released
or rehomed only as those instructions direct.
