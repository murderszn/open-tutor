# Lesson 03 — Egg-Laying Animals: Butterflies and Frogs

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** butterfly stage cards
(egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, adult butterfly), frog stage cards
(egg, tadpole, froglet, adult frog), science journal, pencil

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will put a butterfly's
life cycle in order — **egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, adult
butterfly** — and you will learn the word **metamorphosis**: a big
body change during growth. You will also compare it with a frog's
cycle.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "Name the four parts of a life
cycle. A butterfly starts as an egg — which part is that?" Any
answer naming birth shows readiness.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

The animals in this unit start from **eggs**. A butterfly's life
cycle has four stages:

1. **Egg** (birth) — a tiny egg, often laid on a leaf. A baby
   butterfly grows inside it and hatches.
2. **Caterpillar, or larva** (growth) — the caterpillar eats leaves
   and grows bigger, shedding its skin several times as it
   outgrows it.
3. **Chrysalis, or pupa** (growth, with a big change) — the
   caterpillar hangs from a stem and forms a hard case called a
   **chrysalis**. Inside, its body changes into a butterfly's body:
   six legs, antennae, and wings.
4. **Adult butterfly** (reproduction) — the butterfly crawls out,
   pumps up its wings, and flies. It can lay eggs, so the loop starts
   again. One butterfly's life ends (death) while its eggs carry the
   cycle on.

That big body change — caterpillar to butterfly — is called
**metamorphosis**. Say it slowly: met-a-mor-pho-sis. It means the
animal's body changes shape during growth. The caterpillar does not
just get bigger; it becomes a different-looking animal that can fly.

A frog's cycle is a second egg-laying example, and it also has
metamorphosis, but the stages look different:

1. **Egg** — jelly-like eggs in water. 2. **Tadpole** — a swimming
   young frog with a tail and no legs. 3. **Froglet** — legs grow,
   the tail shrinks. 4. **Adult frog** — a frog that can lay eggs.

**Worked example 1 — ordering the butterfly:**

> Adult lays out the butterfly cards mixed up: chrysalis, egg,
> adult butterfly, caterpillar. Thinking aloud: "The egg must be
> first — that is birth. The caterpillar hatches from the egg and
> eats and grows. Then the chrysalis, where the big change happens.
> Then the adult butterfly, which lays eggs. So: egg, caterpillar,
> chrysalis, adult butterfly. If I put the adult before the
> chrysalis, the butterfly would have to lay eggs before it had
> wings — that cannot be the order."

**Worked example 2 — is a chrysalis sleeping?**

> Adult asks: "Is the chrysalis just a sleeping caterpillar?" Then
> answers: "No. Sleeping animals wake up the same. Inside a
> chrysalis the body is **changing**: legs, antennae, and wings are
> forming. That is why metamorphosis is a better word than sleeping.
> A sleeping caterpillar would wake up still a caterpillar."

## Guided practice (adult and learner together)

1. Put the butterfly cards in order and name the four-part pattern
   word for each card.
2. Put the frog cards in order. Point to the stage where legs first
   appear.
3. Name two things that are the same in the butterfly and frog
   cycles, and two things that are different.
4. Error spot: the adult orders egg, chrysalis, caterpillar, adult
   butterfly and says, "Close enough." The learner fixes it and
   explains why the caterpillar must eat and grow before the
   chrysalis.

## Independent practice

1. Draw the butterfly cycle as a loop with four labeled stages and
   arrows.
2. Write or dictate what metamorphosis means, in your own words,
   using the caterpillar as the example.
3. A chicken's cycle is: egg, chick, adult hen, eggs. Put it in
   order and name the stage where reproduction starts.
4. A learner says, "A butterfly and a caterpillar are two different
   animals." Agree or disagree, with a reason from the cycle.

## Application

Watch or listen to the SciShow Kids butterfly video from the
[resource-pack](../resource-pack.md) with the adult (or use the
stage cards if no video is available). Name one new detail the video
adds about the caterpillar stage, and one detail it adds about the
adult stage. If a detail disagrees with this lesson, tell the adult
— checking sources against each other is science, too.

## Exit check

Order the butterfly stages and say what metamorphosis means in one
sentence. (Oral, card, or drawn answers accepted.)

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** keep the stage cards on the table; let the learner
  answer by arranging cards and pointing.
- **Language access:** "larva = the young, eating stage";
  "pupa = the changing stage inside a case"; "chrysalis = a
  butterfly's pupa case." Break metamorphosis into syllables and
  clap them.
- **Extension:** compare a third egg-laying animal the learner knows
  (chicken, turtle, duck) in four stages, and predict which of its
  stages change the body most; note that the prediction is checked
  against a book or video, not assumed.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: (a) caterpillar and butterfly are different
animals — answer with the single loop: one life, four stages;
(b) chrysalis = sleeping — answer with body-change evidence;
(c) "all animals start from eggs" — correct gently: in this unit we
study egg-laying animals only; many animals start in other ways, and
those are not part of this unit's models. Answers are in the
separate [answer key](../answer-key.md).
