# Lesson 02 — A Flowering Plant's Life Cycle

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** the [generated image](../assets/bean-growth-stages.png)
(bean growth stages), bean plant stage cards (seed, seedling, adult
plant, flower, seed), science journal, pencil and crayons

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will put a flowering
plant's life cycle in order — **seed, seedling, adult plant, flower,
seed** — and you will learn how a flower starts the whole cycle
again.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "Name the four parts of a life
cycle. Which part is a seed?" Any answer naming birth for the seed
(or sprouting) shows readiness.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

A flowering plant's life cycle has five stages to draw, but they fit
the same four-part pattern:

1. **Seed** (birth) — a seed is a starting package: a tiny baby
   plant plus stored food, inside a coat. It waits until it has
   water, warmth, and a place to grow.
2. **Seedling** (growth) — the seed **germinates**, or sprouts: it
   takes in water, a root grows down, and a shoot grows up through
   the soil. The young plant with its first leaves is called a
   **seedling**.
3. **Adult plant** (growth) — the seedling keeps growing: taller
   stem, more leaves. A full-grown plant is called an **adult plant**.
4. **Flower** (reproduction) — the adult plant grows flowers. With
   help from pollinators such as bees and butterflies, flowers make
   **seeds**.
5. **Seed** again — the new seeds fall or are carried away, and the
   loop starts again. That is reproduction: the plant has made new
   plants of its own kind.

So the four-part pattern and the five-stage model fit together:
birth = seed, growth = seedling and adult plant, reproduction =
flower making seeds, death = one plant's life ending while its seeds
carry the cycle on.

**Worked example 1 — ordering the generated image:**

> Adult shows [bean-growth-stages.png](../assets/bean-growth-stages.png)
> and thinks aloud, left to right: "First panel: a bean seed in a
> pot of soil — that is the **seed** stage. Second panel: a tiny
> sprout with two little leaves — that is a **seedling**. Third
> panel: a taller plant with two big leaves — still growing toward
> adult. Fourth panel: a tall plant with many leaves and a purple
> flower — that is the **adult plant** with its **flower**. The
> flower will make new seeds, so the loop goes around again."

**Worked example 2 — what does a plant need to start?**

> Adult asks: "A bean seed sits in a dry cup on a shelf for a year.
> Does it germinate?" Adult answers: "No. A seed needs water (and
> warmth) to start. On the shelf it has neither, so it waits. That
> is why in Lesson 04 we will plant our beans in damp soil and keep
> them warm and watered — we are giving the seed what germination
> needs." Then: "A seed in damp, warm soil sprouts a root down and
> a shoot up. Root down, shoot up — roots drink, shoots reach for
> light. If a learner draws the root going up, that is the error to
> fix."

## Image activity

Look at [bean-growth-stages.png](../assets/bean-growth-stages.png)
together. (1) Point to the seedling and to the adult plant with a
flower. (2) Which panel shows germination just starting, and how can
you tell? (3) Draw the fifth step the picture does not show: the
flower's new seeds, with an arrow back to the first panel.

**Text-only alternative (same questions, no picture needed):**
Panel 1: a bean seed in a pot of soil. Panel 2: a tiny sprout with
two small leaves above the soil. Panel 3: a young plant with a stem
and two larger leaves. Panel 4: a tall plant with many leaves and
one purple flower at the top. Use these four descriptions to answer
(1)–(3) above.

## Guided practice (adult and learner together)

1. Put the five stage cards in order: seed, seedling, adult plant,
   flower, seed. Say the four-part pattern word for each card.
2. Name what the root does and what the shoot does, with a hand
   motion (root down, shoot up).
3. Error spot: the adult orders seed, adult plant, seedling, flower
   and says, "Good enough." The learner fixes it and explains why a
   seedling must come before an adult plant.
4. Answer: a flower has no seeds yet. Has reproduction happened?
   Why or why not?

## Independent practice

1. Draw the bean plant cycle as a loop with five labeled stages and
   arrows showing the order.
2. Write or dictate two sentences: what germination needs, and what
   a flower does in the cycle.
3. Sort these into "part of a flowering plant's cycle" or "not":
   seed, egg, seedling, chrysalis, flower, tadpole. Explain one
   "not" choice.
4. A learner draws a bean cycle that stops at the flower. What is
   missing, and why does it matter?

## Application

Find a fruit or vegetable at home that has seeds in it (for example,
a bean pod, a pepper, an apple — adult checks what is safe to
handle). Where do those seeds sit in the plant's life cycle, and what
would happen if one were planted in damp, warm soil?

## Exit check

Order the five stages out loud, by cards, or in a drawing — seed,
seedling, adult plant, flower, seed — and name the four-part pattern
word for the flower stage.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** keep the five cards on the table for every answer;
  allow the learner to answer by arranging cards instead of writing.
- **Language access:** "germinate = sprout, start growing";
  "seedling = baby plant with first leaves." Draw root-down/shoot-up
  arrows on every plant drawing.
- **Extension:** predict how long each stage might take for a real
  bean plant, then note that the growth log in Lesson 04 will test
  the early stages by observation — predictions are guesses until
  measured.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: (a) seeds are "dead" because they do not
move — answer: a seed holds a living baby plant and stored food, and
it starts growing when it has water and warmth; (b) the flower is
"just pretty" — answer: in the cycle, the flower's job is making
seeds; (c) roots grow up — fix with root-down/shoot-up motions.
Answers are in the separate [answer key](../answer-key.md).
