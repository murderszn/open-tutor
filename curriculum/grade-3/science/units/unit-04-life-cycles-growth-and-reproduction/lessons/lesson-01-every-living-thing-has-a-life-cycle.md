# Lesson 01 — Every Living Thing Has a Life Cycle

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** stage cards (bean seed,
bean seedling, adult bean plant, butterfly egg, caterpillar,
chrysalis, adult butterfly, plus a rock card and a toy-car card),
science journal, pencil

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will learn the four parts
of every life cycle — **birth, growth, reproduction, death** — and
you will be able to tell a living thing's life story from something
that was never alive at all.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "Name one living thing and one
thing that was never alive. How are they different?" Any answer that
separates living things (they grow, they make new ones like
themselves) from nonliving things shows readiness.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

A **life cycle** is the pattern of stages a living thing goes
through. Every life cycle has the same four parts, in this order:

1. **Birth** — the life starts. A plant starts when a seed sprouts.
   An animal in this unit starts when it hatches from an egg.
2. **Growth** — the living thing gets bigger and changes.
3. **Reproduction** — the living thing makes new living things of
   its own kind. A flower makes seeds. A butterfly lays eggs.
4. **Death** — one living thing's life ends. But the cycle does not
   end: the seeds and eggs it made start new lives. That is why we
   draw a life cycle as a **loop** (a circle), not a straight line.

Say it as a chant, with a hand motion for each part: *born* (hands
small together), *grow* (hands move apart and up), *reproduce*
(hands open out to the sides, like scattering seeds), *die* (hands
come back down and rest) — and then the loop starts again with new
births.

A rock has no life cycle. It does not grow by itself, it does not
make baby rocks, and it was never born. A toy car has no life cycle
either. If something was never alive, it has no birth, no growth, no
reproduction, and no death — so it has no life cycle at all.

**Worked example 1 — telling a butterfly's story in four parts:**

> Adult lays out the butterfly cards in a mixed-up order and thinks
> aloud: "Egg — that is the **birth** part, when the butterfly's
> life starts. Caterpillar — that is **growth**, eating and getting
> bigger. Chrysalis — still growth, but a special big change, which
> we will name in Lesson 03. Adult butterfly — it can lay eggs, so
> that is **reproduction**. And one day that butterfly dies — that
> is **death**. But its eggs hatch, so the loop starts again. Egg,
> caterpillar, chrysalis, adult: birth, growth, reproduction, death."

**Worked example 2 — is this a life cycle?**

> Adult holds up the rock card. "A rock sits on a path. It gets
> rained on. It breaks into smaller pieces. Is that a life cycle?"
> Adult answers slowly: "No. The rock was never born, it does not
> grow the way a living thing grows, and it does not make new rocks
> of its own kind. Breaking is not birth, and pieces are not
> offspring. So a rock has changes, but no life cycle." Then the
> adult holds up the bean seed card: "A seed sprouts, grows, flowers,
> and makes new seeds. That **is** a life cycle — birth, growth,
> reproduction, death, and around again."

## Guided practice (adult and learner together)

1. Chant the four parts with the hand motions, then say them
   without the adult's help.
2. Sort these six cards into "has a life cycle" and "never alive,
   no life cycle": bean seed, butterfly egg, rock, toy car, adult
   bean plant, caterpillar. Explain one sort out loud.
3. Put the butterfly cards (egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, adult) in
   order. Point to the card that shows reproduction starting, and
   say why.
4. Error spot: the adult lays out egg, adult butterfly, caterpillar,
   chrysalis and says, "Done — in order!" The learner fixes the
   order and explains what was wrong.

## Independent practice

1. Draw a loop (a circle with arrows) and label its four parts:
   birth, growth, reproduction, death.
2. Write or dictate one sentence for each part, about a bean plant.
3. Sort: bean plant, rock, butterfly, bicycle, frog. Which have a
   life cycle? Which do not? Give one reason for each nonliving
   choice.
4. A learner says, "My plant's life cycle is a straight line: seed,
   seedling, plant, dead." Is that right? Fix the picture and explain
   what is missing.

## Application

Tell the life story of a chicken (egg, chick, adult hen, eggs) in
four parts, out loud or in a drawing. Then answer: where in the story
does the loop start again?

## Exit check

Name the four parts of a life cycle in order, and name one living
thing and one nonliving thing. (Adult accepts oral, pointing, or
drawn answers.)

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** keep the four hand motions going for every answer;
  let the learner answer by pointing at cards in order.
- **Language access:** "birth = start," "growth = get bigger and
  change," "reproduction = make new ones like itself," "death = the
  end of one life." Repeat the chant in the home language first if
  that helps.
- **Extension:** name a living thing whose life cycle the learner
  already knows (a dog, a sunflower) and tell its four parts; predict
  which part takes the longest and say why that is only a guess
  until it is observed.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: (a) "nonliving things have life cycles too"
(rock breaking, car rusting) — answer with the four-part test: was
it born, does it grow by itself, does it reproduce? (b) "a life
cycle is a straight line that ends at death" — redraw as a loop and
ask what the offspring do. (c) "reproduction" is a hard word —
keep the definition short ("makes new ones of its own kind") and
never use human examples in this unit. Answers are in the separate
[answer key](../answer-key.md).
