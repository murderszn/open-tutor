# Lesson 04 — Planting Beans and Starting the Growth Log

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** dry untreated bean seeds,
pots or cups with drainage holes, potting soil, water, tray, ruler
with centimeters, science journal, pencil, plant labels

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will plant real beans and
start a **growth log**: a dated record of height, leaf count, and
drawings that you will keep going through Units 05–06.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "What does a seed need to
germinate? How do we measure a plant's height — from where to
where?" Answers naming water and warmth, and measuring from the soil
line to the tallest tip, show readiness.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

Planting is an **investigation by observation**: we will watch and
measure the same plants over time. This is not a fair test with one
changed variable — we are not comparing two treatments here. We are
recording what happens, carefully, so the log can be read later as
data. (Unit 05 will use this log to study variation; that is why
every entry must be dated and measured the same way.)

The measurement ritual, reused from Unit 01:

1. Put the ruler's zero exactly at the **soil line**.
2. Read to the **nearest centimeter** at the tallest tip of the
   plant.
3. **Count leaves** — count every leaf you can see, including tiny
   new ones, and say the count out loud before writing it.
4. **Draw** what you see and **label** it (soil line, stem, leaves).
5. **Record at once** — date, height, leaf count, drawing — before
   doing anything else.

Steps for planting (adult supervises; learner does the steps the
adult judges safe):

1. Fill each pot with soil to about 2 cm below the rim.
2. Push 2–3 bean seeds about 2 cm deep, spaced apart. Cover them
   gently.
3. Water slowly until the soil is damp, not flooded. Set the pot on
   the tray.
4. Label the pot (bean, date planted). Put it where it gets light
   and stays warm.
5. Make the Day 1 log entry: date, height **0 cm**, leaves **0**,
   and a drawing of the pot with the seeds under the soil (draw the
   seeds as if you could see through the soil, and label them
   "seeds — not yet sprouted").
6. Wash hands.

Care routine between sessions (adult's job, learner may help):
keep the soil damp, not wet; keep the pot in light; if a seed never
sprouts after two weeks, note that in the log — a missing sprout is
data, not a failure — and plant a replacement beside it, labeled
with its own planting date.

**Worked example 1 — a Day 1 entry done right:**

> Adult models in the journal: "Date: Day 1. Height: 0 cm. Leaves:
> 0. Drawing: pot, soil line labeled, three seeds drawn under the
> soil and labeled. Note: 'Seeds planted, watered, in light.'"
> Adult points out what would be wrong: writing "tall" instead of a
> number, or measuring from the table instead of the soil line.

**Worked example 2 — reading a practice log (fictional data):**

> Adult shows this practice log, labeled **fictional — not our
> plants**: "Practice Bean 1: Day 1 — 0 cm, 0 leaves. Day 7 — 3 cm,
> 2 leaves. Day 14 — 8 cm, 4 leaves. Day 21 — 13 cm, 6 leaves."
> Adult thinks aloud: "From Day 7 to Day 14 the plant grew
> 8 − 3 = **5 cm**. From Day 14 to Day 21 it grew 13 − 8 = **5 cm**.
> Leaves went up by 2 each week. The plant grew every week — the
> pattern is steady growth, not a jump and then nothing. Total
> growth from Day 1 to Day 21 is 13 − 0 = **13 cm**."
> This fictional log is the dataset Practice set B and the quiz use,
> so every learner can practice measuring and subtracting even if
> their own beans are slow.

## Guided practice (adult and learner together)

1. Plant the beans following the six steps, with the adult checking
   depth and watering.
2. Make the Day 1 entry together: date, 0 cm, 0 leaves, labeled
   drawing.
3. Practice the ruler ritual on a pencil standing in a cup: zero at
   the cup's rim, read to the nearest centimeter, record at once.
4. Read the fictional practice log above and answer: how many
   centimeters did Practice Bean 1 grow from Day 1 to Day 7?

## Independent practice

1. Write the care routine in your own words (water, light, warmth,
   check each session).
2. A practice stem measures 6 cm from the soil line. A learner
   measured from the table and wrote 9 cm. Which is right, and why?
3. Complete this fictional log row: Day 28 — the pattern of the
   practice log continues (5 cm more, 2 more leaves). What height
   and leaf count would you predict? Then explain why a prediction
   is not a measurement.
4. Draw a Day 7 entry for a bean that has sprouted: stem, two
   leaves, soil line labeled, height arrow from the soil line.

## Application

Decide where the pots will live and why: light, warmth, and a place
where they will not be knocked over. Write the decision and one
reason in the journal. If the best light is a windowsill, what is
the risk on a cold night, and what will the adult do about it?

## Exit check

State the four steps of the measurement ritual in order, and show a
complete Day 1 entry (date, height, leaves, labeled drawing).

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** the adult holds the ruler's zero at the soil line
  while the learner reads; leaf counts may be shown by pointing and
  counting aloud.
- **Language access:** "soil line = where the soil top is";
  "tallest tip = the highest point of the plant." Use the sentence
  frame: "On ___, my plant is ___ cm tall and has ___ leaves."
- **Extension:** measure twice and compare the two readings; if they
  differ, measure a third time and explain which reading to trust and
  why (the Unit 01 rule: repeat and record the careful reading).

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: (a) measuring from the table or the pot rim
instead of the soil line — re-teach the ritual every session until
it is automatic; (b) "my seed failed, so my log is ruined" — answer:
a seed that does not sprout is data; note it and plant a labeled
replacement; (c) growth-log numbers are "answers to guess" — answer:
numbers come from measuring, predictions are labeled as predictions.
Safety: planting seeds are never eaten; hands washed after soil work.
