# Lesson 05 — Comparing Life Cycles

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** bean plant stage cards,
butterfly stage cards, frog stage cards, two-column comparison
chart (Same / Different), science journal, pencil

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will compare a bean
plant's life cycle with a butterfly's life cycle and name what is
the **same** and what is **different** — and you will notice that
offspring grow into the same kind of living thing as their parents.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "Put the bean cards in order. Put
the butterfly cards in order. Which cycle has a flower, and which
has a chrysalis?" Correct orders and one correct match show
readiness.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

Comparing means looking for **sameness** and **difference** on
purpose, not just noticing whatever catches your eye. Use the
four-part pattern as the comparison frame: check each cycle against
birth, growth, reproduction, death, and then look at the details.

**What is the same** for a bean plant and a butterfly:

- Both have a life cycle with the same four parts: birth, growth,
  reproduction, death.
- Both start small (a seed, an egg) and grow bigger.
- Both reproduce: the plant makes seeds, the butterfly lays eggs.
- Both die, but their offspring carry the kind on — the loop goes
  around again.

**What is different:**

- **Starting point:** a seed vs. an egg.
- **Stages:** seed, seedling, adult plant, flower, seed — vs. egg,
  caterpillar, chrysalis, adult butterfly.
- **Body change:** the butterfly's body changes shape completely
  (metamorphosis); the bean plant grows bigger and adds leaves and
  flowers, but it never turns into a different-looking animal — it
  stays a plant.
- **Where growth happens:** the plant stays in one place and grows
  in soil; the butterfly moves, eats leaves, and flies.
- **How long stages last:** stages do not take the same time in
  every living thing. Some cycles take weeks, some take years. (Do
  not memorize numbers here — the point is that stage lengths
  differ, and a growth log is how we find out for our own beans.)

**Offspring resemble parents** — the bridge to Unit 05: a bean seed
grows into a bean plant, not into a sunflower. A butterfly egg grows
into a butterfly, not into a frog. Offspring are the same kind as
their parents. Unit 05 will look closely at *which* traits offspring
share with parents and how offspring of the same parents still vary.
Today, "same kind" is enough.

**Worked example 1 — a same/different chart done right:**

> Adult fills the chart aloud: "Same: both are born, grow,
> reproduce, and die. Same: both start from something small.
> Different: the plant starts from a seed; the butterfly starts from
> an egg. Different: the butterfly has metamorphosis; the plant does
> not change into a different kind of body. Different: the plant
> makes seeds in a flower; the butterfly lays eggs."

**Worked example 2 — sorting by cycle pattern:**

> Adult lays out six cards: bean seed, butterfly egg, frog egg,
> chicken egg, sunflower seed, tadpole. "Sort by starting point:
> seed-starters vs. egg-starters." Adult sorts: seeds — bean seed,
> sunflower seed. Eggs — butterfly egg, frog egg, chicken egg.
> "Tadpole is not a starting point at all — it is a middle stage of
> the frog. Sorting by *stage* (start, middle, end) is a different
> sort from sorting by *starting point*, and mixing the two sorts is
> the error to watch for."

## Guided practice (adult and learner together)

1. Fill a Same / Different chart for bean plant vs. butterfly with
   at least two entries on each side.
2. Sort the six cards from Worked example 2 by starting point, then
   re-sort them by "plant cycle" vs. "animal cycle."
3. Answer: a sunflower seed is planted. Will it grow into a bean
   plant? Why or why not?
4. Error spot: a learner's chart says, "Same: both have flowers."
   Fix it and explain the error.

## Independent practice

1. Write or dictate two sameness sentences and two difference
   sentences comparing a frog's cycle with a bean plant's cycle.
2. A learner says, "A butterfly's cycle is better because it can
   fly." Is "better" a science comparison? Rewrite the sentence as
   a same/difference statement.
3. Order these mixed stages into two cycles: egg, seed, chrysalis,
   seedling, caterpillar, flower. Label each cycle.
4. Predict: if a bean plant's seeds are planted, what kind of plant
   grows? What evidence from this unit supports your prediction?

## Application

Choose any two living things you know (one plant, one animal) and
tell their cycles in four parts each, then name one sameness and one
difference. This is the skill the project showcase will ask for.

## Exit check

Name two things that are the same and two things that are different
between a bean plant's cycle and a butterfly's cycle, without mixing
up stages and starting points.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** keep both sets of stage cards visible; let the
  learner answer by moving cards into Same / Different columns.
- **Language access:** "same = alike in that way";
  "different = not alike in that way." Use the frame: "They are the
  same because ___. They are different because ___."
- **Extension:** compare a third cycle (frog or chicken) with the
  bean plant, and argue which comparison is more interesting and
  why — with evidence from the stages, not from liking one animal
  more.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: (a) "better/worse" as a comparison — redirect
to same/difference evidence; (b) mixing sorting rules (starting
point vs. stage vs. plant/animal) — name the sorting rule before
sorting; (c) "offspring look exactly like parents" — correct gently:
same kind, not identical; exact trait patterns are Unit 05's work.
