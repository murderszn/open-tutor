# Answer Key — Unit 01: Fair Tests, Measurement, Data, and Explanations

Every solution below was worked independently from the learner materials
and reconciled with them. Open responses accept any answer matching the
reasoning shown. All datasets are fictional practice data.

## Lesson 01 — Testable questions (guided)

1. Testable — measure each roll distance in centimeters.
2. Not testable — "best color" is an opinion; no measurement settles it.
3. Testable — measure each plant's height.
4. Not testable — a feeling/opinion, not checkable by observing or measuring.
5. Testable — measure the water each towel absorbs.
6. Testable — count the paper clips.

## Lesson 02 — Observing and measuring (guided)

1–3. Accept the learner's actual measurements (spoon length in cm; mass of
rock in g). Check: mm reading = 10 × cm reading; every value carries its
unit; measurements started at the 0 mark.
4. Accept: water exactly at the 50 mL line, read at eye level.
5. Accept: longer object named with "about ___ cm longer" (difference of
the two measurements).

## Lesson 03 — Fair tests (guided + image activity)

1. Fair — one change: the car's color.
2. Unfair — the amount of water also changes. Fix: pour the same number of
   spoons on each towel.
3. Fair — one change: ramp height; repeated trials.
4. Fair — one change: light; controls include same water and same kind of
   plant.
5. Unfair — the ball AND the ramp height change. Fix: roll both balls from
   the same ramp height.
6. Fair — and because nothing changes, it checks that the result repeats
   (the point of repeated trials).

**Image error-analysis:** Left station — fair; the one change is the surface
(bare wood vs. green towel); height, car, and release are the same. Right
station — unfair; three things change: ramp height, surface (bare vs. blue
cloth), and the object (red car vs. yellow ball).
**Application (redesign):** any fix that leaves exactly one difference —
e.g., make both ramps the same height (then surface + object still differ…
no — must equalize two of the three). Accept: same height AND same object
with different surfaces; or same height AND same surface with different
objects; or same surface AND same object with different heights.

## Lesson 04 — Data tables and graphs (guided)

Bean-bag data: Sam 120, 125, 121; Alex 140, 138, 142.

1. Sam: 120 + 125 + 121 = **366 cm**. Alex: 140 + 138 + 142 = **420 cm**.
2. Alex's bar is longer.
3. 420 − 366 = **54 cm** more.
4. 366 ÷ 10 = 36.6 — not a whole number of stars. Round to about 370 cm →
   37 stars, and label the pictograph "approximate." (Teaching point:
   pictographs suit friendly numbers.)
5. X's at 120, 121, 125 — clustered 120–125 cm, close together: a pattern,
   so the measurement is trustworthy.
6. Missing the scale (numbers up the side) — without it no one can tell how
   big each bar is.

**Independent:** line plot — 35: XX, 36: XX, 37: X, 38: X. Cluster 35–36 cm;
most common: 35 cm and 36 cm (tie, two X's each).

## Lesson 05 — Claims (guided)

Magnet data: one magnet 6, 7, 6; two magnets 11, 12, 11.

1. One magnet: 6 + 7 + 6 = **19** clips. Two magnets: 11 + 12 + 11 =
   **34** clips.
2. 34 − 19 = **15** more clips.
3. Accept: "Two magnets pick up more paper clips than one magnet."
4. Accept two of: totals 34 vs. 19; every two-magnet trial (11, 12, 11)
   beat every one-magnet trial (6, 7, 6); difference of 15.
5. Accept one of: only 3 trials; only paper clips tested; only these two
   magnets.
6. "Always" goes beyond the evidence — the test tried only these magnets
   and paper clips.

## Practice set A

1. Testable. 2. Not testable (opinion). 3. Testable.
4. Accept: "…by putting the same spoon of sugar in warm and cold water and
   timing how long each takes to disappear."
5. Accept: "…by coloring with each brand until it runs out and comparing,"
   or "…by measuring how far each colors before wearing down."
6. Trust 24 cm — the measurement. An estimate is a guess; the measurement
   checks it.
7. No — starting at the ruler's end adds the worn/extra end length to every
   reading, so all measurements come out too long.
8. Accept any (tiny print, leaf veins, fabric weave, crumbs).

## Practice set B

1. Unfair — push AND surface change. Fix: same surface both times; only the
   push changes.
2. Fair — one change: the cup material.
3. Fair — one change: light; controls: same seeds, same wet paper, same
   count.
4. Variable: ramp height. Controls: any two of same car, same ramp, same
   surface, same release point, no pushing.
5. Accept: change = surface; keep same = car, ramp height, release (any
   two+); 3 trials each; measure roll distance in cm.
6. Accept: change = towel brand; keep same = amount of water, pour method,
   wait time (any two+); 3 trials; measure mL absorbed.
7. "Does the surface change how far the car rolls?" (bare wood vs. towel).
8. Accept any failure point + prevention, e.g.: "the car falls off the
   ramp — put books as side rails"; "the towel slips — tape it down";
   "someone bumps the table — clear the area first."

## Practice set C

Towel data: A 5, 6, 5; B 9, 8, 9.

1. 5 + 6 + 5 = **16 mL**.
2. 9 + 8 + 9 = **26 mL**.
3. Towel B; 26 − 16 = **10 mL** more.
4. 26 ÷ 4 = 6.5 — not exact. Draw 6½ sponge pictures, or 7 labeled
   "approximate."
5. X's at 5, 5, 6 — clustered 5–6 mL, close together: a pattern, so the
   measurement is trustworthy.
6. Accept, e.g.: "Towel B absorbed more water. Evidence: 26 mL vs. 16 mL;
   every B trial beat every A trial. Limitation: only 3 trials."
7. Missing: the title (what the graph shows) and the units (mL). Without
   them readers cannot tell what was measured.
8. "Always/forever" goes beyond 3 trials on two towels.

## Formative quiz (week-04_quiz.md)

1. Testable — count (or measure) how many paper clips each magnet picks up.
2. Not testable — "prettiest" is an opinion; no measurement settles it.
3. 14 cm.
4. Grams — mass is measured in grams.
5. The surface (bare wood vs. green towel).
6. Unfair — two extra changes: the ramp height and the object (car vs. ball).
7. a. 16 mL. b. 26 mL. c. Towel B; 10 mL more in total.
8. Plant A: 4 × 10 = 40 mL. Plant B: 6 × 10 = 60 mL. Plant B got 20 mL more.
9. Accept, e.g.: "Towel B absorbed more water (claim). Evidence: 26 mL vs.
   16 mL. Limitation: only 3 trials were run."
10. One trial could be a fluke or a mistake; repeats reveal a pattern and
    make the result trustworthy.

## Unit assessment (unit-assessment.md)

1. Accept any testable magnet question, e.g. "How many paper clips can this
   magnet pick up?" or "Does using two magnets pick up more paper clips
   than one?"
2. Variable and two controls consistent with the item-1 question (e.g.,
   change: number of magnets; keep same: same paper clips, same surface,
   same lifting method).
3. a. 30 mm. b. 9 cm.
4. a. centimeters. b. grams. c. milliliters.
5. Unfair — light AND water both change. Fix: water both plants the same
   and change only the light (or keep both in the same light and change
   only the water).
6. 50-cm drops: 32 + 33 + 33 = **98 cm**. 100-cm drops: 58 + 60 + 59 =
   **177 cm**.
7. Accept a bar graph with title, labels, numbered scale, and units; the
   100-cm bar is taller.
8. 177 − 98 = **79 cm** more.
9. X's cluster in the low 30s (32–33) and the high 50s (58–60). Most
   often: 33 cm (two X's).
10. Accept, e.g.: "Dropping the ball from higher makes it bounce higher
    (claim). Evidence: 177 cm total vs. 98 cm total, and every 100-cm trial
    beat every 50-cm trial. Limitation: only 3 trials with one ball."
11. a. Unfair — two things changed (ramp height and the car), so the result
    cannot be tied to one cause.
    b. Use the same car for both heights; change only the ramp height
    (keep surface, release, and trials the same).
    c. Accept any honest limitation of the learner's own project, e.g.
    only 3 trials, one car, one ramp, one room.

**Scoring:** 1 point per numbered item (multi-part items: 1 point if all
parts correct, 0.5 if partially). 10–11 = secure; 8–9.5 = ready with
light review; below 8 = re-teach per the teacher guide before Unit 02.
