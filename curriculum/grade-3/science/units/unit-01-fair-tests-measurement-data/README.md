# Unit 01 — Fair Tests, Measurement, Data, and Explanations

**Grade:** 3 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#19](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/19)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 3–6 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 30 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

This unit teaches the *practices* that power the rest of the year: how to ask
a question that can be tested, how to observe carefully and measure with
standard tools, how to run a **fair test** (change one thing, keep the rest
the same, repeat trials), and how to record data in tables, bar graphs,
pictographs, and line plots so a claim can be supported with evidence. The
unit's investigation — testing whether ramp surface changes how far a toy car
rolls — is a one-variable investigation honoring the 3-PS2-1 assessment
boundary (one variable at a time), and its fair-test routine is the one reused
in Units 02, 03, 07, and 08. The measurement skills here (centimeters and
millimeters, grams, milliliters) extend the grade-3 math track's measurement
work into real science investigations.

## Prerequisites (from grade-2 science; re-taught here as needed)

- Asking questions that can be explored by looking, touching, or testing.
- Observing with the senses and simple tools (hand lens, ruler); recording in
  words, drawings, or tallies.
- Measuring length to the nearest centimeter with a ruler.
- Comparing everyday materials by observable properties.
- Science safety practices: adult supervises every investigation; materials
  stay away from the mouth; living things are handled gently; never look
  directly at the sun.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Ask a **testable question** and explain what would be measured or observed
   to answer it (SEP-1).
2. Measure **length** to the nearest centimeter and millimeter, **mass** to the
   nearest gram, and **volume** to the nearest 10 milliliters with standard
   tools, starting from zero and recording the unit.
3. Plan and carry out a **fair investigation**: change one thing (the
   *variable*), keep everything else the same (*controls*), repeat at least
   three trials, and record data in a labeled table (3-PS2-1 assessment
   boundary; 3-5-ETS1-3; SEP-3).
4. Build and read **bar graphs**, **pictographs**, and **line plots** from
   collected data; answer "how many more" and trend questions (SEP-4).
5. Make an **evidence-based claim** supported by at least two data values and
   one limitation; judge whether someone else's test was fair and fix an
   unfair test (SEP-7).

## Essential question

*How can I tell if a test is fair, and how do my measurements and records
help me trust what I found?*

## Vocabulary

**Testable question** — a question that can be answered by observing,
measuring, or testing. · **Observation** — something noticed with the senses
or a tool. · **Measure** — find out how long, how heavy, or how much with a
tool. · **Centimeter / millimeter** — metric length units; 10 millimeters =
1 centimeter. · **Gram** — metric mass unit. · **Milliliter** — metric volume
unit. · **Estimate** — a careful guess *before* measuring. · **Trial** — one
try of a test; scientists repeat trials. · **Data** — recorded measurements
and observations. · **Fair test** — a test that changes only one thing while
everything else stays the same. · **Variable** — the one thing we change. ·
**Control** — a thing we keep the same on purpose. · **Table** — rows and
columns that hold data. · **Bar graph / pictograph / line plot** — pictures
of data. · **Pattern** — something that repeats or stays similar across
trials. · **Claim** — what we say is true because of our evidence. ·
**Evidence** — data used to support a claim. · **Limitation** — something our
test cannot prove or a reason our evidence is incomplete.

## Standards and source notes

- Reference framework: Next Generation Science Standards, grade-3 performance
  expectations and the 3–5 engineering band. The codes and descriptions below
  were corroborated with the audit's crosswalk (verified 2026-10-03 against
  the California Department of Education's official reproduction of the NGSS
  grade-3 release, "NGSS Grade Three Arranged by DCI," revised March 2015).
  No state adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification is claimed.
- **3-PS2-1** — Plan and conduct an investigation to provide evidence of the
  effects of balanced and unbalanced forces on the motion of an object. This
  unit uses only the practice portion of this PE: the ramp investigation is
  planned with **one variable at a time**, honoring its assessment boundary
  (number, size, or direction of forces varied one at a time; no quantitative
  force size — qualitative and relative only).
- **3-5-ETS1-3** — Plan and carry out fair tests in which variables are
  controlled and failure points are considered to identify aspects of a model
  or prototype that can be improved. The unit's fair-test routine — one
  variable changed, the rest controlled, trials repeated, failure points
  named — is the foundation Unit 08's engineering design cycle reuses.
- Science and engineering practices: **SEP-1** (asking questions),
  **SEP-3** (planning and carrying out investigations), **SEP-4** (analyzing
  and interpreting data), **SEP-7** (engaging in argument from evidence).
  Crosscutting concepts: **patterns** (repeated trials; graphs reveal
  patterns) and **cause and effect** (the changed variable causes the
  measured change — tested, not assumed).

## Materials

Ruler with centimeters/millimeters (or a printed centimeter ruler card),
balance scale, measuring cup or plastic graduated cylinder, hand lens,
toy car or ball, a short board or stiff cardboard ramp, books to raise the
ramp, two surface cloths (smooth paper/cardboard and a small towel), stopwatch
or timer, bean seeds (for the extension estimate activity), science journal
or notebook, pencil and crayons, tape measure or equal-length paper strips.
All investigations are adult-supervised; the adult sets ramp heights and
checks every setup.

## Differentiation and accessibility

- Oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative response modes always available;
  the adult scribes dictated observations on request.
- Sentence frames for evidence talk: "My evidence is ___," "When ___ changed,
  then ___ happened," "My test was fair because ___."
- Large-print, high-contrast data tables and word cards; vocabulary taught
  with objects and pictures first, word second; visual science word wall;
  home-language labels welcomed.
- Short sessions with movement breaks; every investigation has a table
  variant and an active variant.
- Diagrams ship with text-only alternatives; color is never the only cue in
  graphs or sorts.

## Safety

Adult supervises every investigation and previews every procedure. Ramps are
built from books and boards at table height with rolling objects kept clear
of stairs; the adult sets and checks every ramp. Materials stay away from the
mouth; hands washed after any soil or outdoor work. Weather or outdoor
alternatives are observation-only.
