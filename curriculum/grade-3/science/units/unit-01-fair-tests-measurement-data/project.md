# Project — The Great Ramp Race

**A fair-test investigation.** Sessions 10–11 of the unit pacing (plan, then
run), showcased in session 15. Adult-supervised throughout.

## Driving question

*Does the ramp surface change how far the toy car rolls?*

## Materials

Toy car or ball, a short board or stiff cardboard ramp, books to raise one
end, two surfaces (bare smooth board/cardboard and a small towel or cloth),
tape measure or ruler, science journal, pencil, crayons. The adult sets the
ramp height, checks the setup, and keeps rolling objects clear of stairs
and people.

## Steps

1. **Ask and predict.** Write the driving question in your journal. Predict
   which surface will roll farther — and why. (A prediction is a guess
   *before* the test; it is allowed to be wrong.)
2. **Plan the fair test.** With the adult, fill the plan card:
   - The **one thing I change** (variable): the ramp surface — bare board,
     then towel.
   - **Things I keep the same** (controls): the same car, the same ramp,
     the same ramp height, the same release point, released the same way
     with no push.
   - **Trials:** 3 rolls on each surface.
   - **What I measure:** distance from the bottom of the ramp to where the
     car stops, in centimeters, ruler starting at 0.
   - **Failure points:** what could go wrong? (Car falls off the ramp; the
     towel slips; someone bumps the table.) Write one prevention for each.
   The adult approves the plan before any rolling starts.
3. **Run and record.** Roll 3 trials on the bare surface, then 3 on the
   towel. Record every distance immediately in a labeled table:

   | Surface | Trial 1 | Trial 2 | Trial 3 | Total |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Bare board | ___ cm | ___ cm | ___ cm | ___ cm |
   | Towel | ___ cm | ___ cm | ___ cm | ___ cm |

4. **Graph.** Build a bar graph of the two totals: title, labeled bars,
   numbered scale, units.
5. **Claim.** Write the three-part claim: what you claim, two evidence
   numbers, one limitation.
6. **Redesign.** Name one way to make the test *even fairer or stronger*
   next time (more trials? a third surface? a longer ramp?) and explain why.

## Deliverables

1. The approved fair-test plan card (question, variable, controls, trials,
   failure-point preventions).
2. The completed data table with units.
3. The bar graph (title, labels, scale, units).
4. The three-part claim paragraph.
5. One redesign idea with a reason.

## Rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fair plan** | One variable named; 2+ controls named; 3 trials; failure point with prevention | One variable named; one control or trials missing | Adult builds the plan with the learner |
| **Data** | All 6 trials recorded with cm, ruler from 0 | Most trials recorded; units missing | Preprinted sample table used |
| **Graph** | Title, labels, numbered scale, units; bars match totals | Graph present; one element missing | Adult draws; learner reads |
| **Claim** | Answers the question with 2 evidence numbers and 1 limitation | Claim with evidence but no limitation | Sentence frame with adult help |

**Readiness to move on:** 6+ of 8 rubric points with the claim written
independently. Below that, re-teach with the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md)
before Unit 02 — the fair-test routine returns in every later unit.
