# Lesson 01 — Questions You Can Test

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** science journal, pencil/crayons, the
unit's vocabulary word cards (testable question, observation, measure)

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will learn what makes a question one
you can *test*, and you will practice turning wonderings into testable
questions.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "What is something you wonder about?"
Any wondering shows readiness. If the learner has none, model one: "I wonder
which towel soaks up more water."

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

Scientists start with wonderings, just like you. But not every wondering can
become an investigation. A **testable question** is a question you could
answer by *observing*, *measuring*, or *trying something* — and that would
give you an answer you can check.

"Which towel soaks up more water?" is testable. You could pour the same
amount of water on two towels and measure what is left. You would get an
answer.

"Which towel is prettier?" is not testable. Pretty is an opinion — people
disagree, and no measurement settles it. Opinions make interesting
conversations, not investigations.

A quick check: if you can finish the sentence "I could find out by ___" with
*looking*, *measuring*, or *trying*, the question is probably testable.

**Worked example 1 — sorted out loud, slowly:**

> Adult: "My wondering: *Does warm water melt an ice cube faster than cold
> water?* Is it testable? I ask myself: could I find out by trying? Yes — I
> could put one ice cube in warm water and one in cold water and watch. Could
> I measure something? Yes — I could time how long each cube takes to melt.
> Testable!"

**Worked example 2 — an untestable wondering, named kindly:**

> Adult: "My wondering: *Why is the sky so beautiful at sunset?* Could I find
> out by measuring? Beauty is an opinion — my neighbor might say sunrises are
> prettier. No measurement answers it. Not testable as a science question.
> But I *could* ask a testable question hiding inside it: *What colors appear
> in the sky during sunset?* That one I can answer by observing!"

Key idea to say plainly: **A testable question can be answered by observing,
measuring, or trying. Opinions cannot be settled by a test.**

## Guided practice (adult leads, learner decides)

Adult reads each wondering. The learner says "testable" or "not testable,"
and for testable ones names what could be observed or measured.

1. "Which ramp lets the car roll farther — the smooth one or the rough one?"
   *(testable — measure roll distance in centimeters)*
2. "What is the best color for a toy car?" *(not testable — opinion)*
3. "Do plants near the window grow taller than plants in the dark corner?"
   *(testable — measure plant height)*
4. "Why do I love rainy days?" *(not testable — opinion/feeling)*
5. "Which paper towel holds the most water?" *(testable — measure water
   absorbed)*
6. "How many paper clips can this magnet pick up?" *(testable — count them)*

For each testable question, have the learner finish: "I could find out by
___."

## Independent practice

The learner writes or dictates **three** testable questions of their own and,
for one of them, writes what they would observe or measure. (Adult scribes
on request.)

## Application

Look around the room. Find one wondering about something you can see right
now, and turn it into a testable question. Example: wondering "that plant
looks thirsty" becomes "How many days until the soil feels dry again?"

## Exit check

Prompt: "Tell me one testable question and one question that is not
testable. How do you know the difference?" Accept: the testable one can be
answered by observing/measuring/trying; the other is an opinion or cannot be
checked.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** Provide two pre-sorted examples to match before the learner
  sorts alone. Let the learner point to "testable" / "not testable" cards.
- **Extension:** The learner rewrites one untestable question as a testable
  one (as in worked example 2).

## Teacher note

**Likely misconception:** learners often think any question with an answer is
testable ("What is the biggest dog in the world?" — answerable by looking it
up, but not by *their own* observation or test). Affirm the distinction:
testable means *we* could find out by observing, measuring, or trying.
