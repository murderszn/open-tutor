# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Fair Tests, Measurement, Data, and Explanations

For the guiding adult. Learner-facing materials never include answers; all
solutions live in the [answer key](answer-key.md).

## What "ready to move on" looks like

The learner is ready for Unit 02 when they can, with at most light
prompting: name one variable and two controls for a new question; measure
length from the 0 mark with units; record 3 trials in a labeled table;
build a bar graph from a table; and write a claim with two evidence numbers
and one limitation. Evidence: project rubric score 6+/8, quiz 7+/10,
assessment 8+/11 parts. If the learner is below this, re-teach with the
targeted follow-ups below before Unit 02 — the fair-test routine is reused
in Units 02, 03, 07, and 08, so gaps compound.

## Session-by-session notes

- **Sessions 1–4 (Week 3):** Keep the testable-question sort brisk; the
  payoff is Lesson 03. In Lesson 02, watch every learner's first three
  measurements — the 0-mark habit is the unit's highest-leverage skill.
  The Crash Course Kids "Try Trials" video (resource pack) works best in
  session 4 *after* learners have measured, so "failure points" lands on
  experience.
- **Sessions 5–8 (Week 4):** The [fair-test stations image](assets/fair-test-stations.png)
  is the anchor of Lesson 03 — do the error-analysis together, slowly.
  Learners who can *fix* the right station (not just label it unfair) are
  ready for the project plan. Session 8's redesign relay: plant three
  unfair tests around the room (cards); the learner "fixes" each with the
  step cards.
- **Sessions 9–12 (Week 5):** Approve every project plan *before* rolling
  starts — most plans fail on "release the same way," so rehearse the
  release twice. Totals may be computed with the adult; the *graph* and
  the *claim* are the learner's work.
- **Sessions 13–16 (Week 6):** Quiz in session 13, reviewed the same day.
  Session 14 measurement stations: (1) length of a book in cm and mm,
  (2) mass of a rock in g, (3) 50 mL pour, (4) estimate-then-measure
  mystery object. Session 15 is the project showcase — the learner
  presents the graph and reads the claim aloud.

## Misconception bank

| Misconception | Re-teach move |
|---|---|
| "Fair" means nice/equal turns | Re-anchor with the step cards: in science, fair = one thing changes |
| Measuring from the ruler's end | Sticker on the 0 mark; three supervised re-measures |
| Dropping units ("it's 12") | Treat as incomplete, not wrong; ask "12 whats?" every time |
| Tallest bar = winner, no questions | Ask "what did they measure — and was the test fair?" before any conclusion |
| "Always/never" claims | Send back to the evidence: "what did you actually test?" |
| Limitation = mistake/apology | Praise limitations explicitly; model your own ("my demo used one car") |
| One trial is enough | Re-run a wobbly trial together; watch the numbers disagree |

## Differentiation

- **Support:** sentence frames ("My evidence is ___", "When ___ changed,
  then ___ happened"), pre-drawn axes, sticker X-marks, adult scribing,
  oral/pointing response modes, large-print tables.
- **Extension:** mm↔cm conversions; multi-trial range ("results spanned
  28–30 cm"); evaluating advertising claims ("absorbs twice as much —
  what evidence would you demand?"); designing a *second* fair test that
  changes a different variable.
- **Language access:** vocabulary with objects/pictures first; visual word
  wall; home-language labels welcomed.

## Safety reminders

Adult sets and checks every ramp; rolling objects clear of stairs and
people; materials away from the mouth; hands washed after soil/outdoor
work. No mains-powered equipment anywhere in this unit.
