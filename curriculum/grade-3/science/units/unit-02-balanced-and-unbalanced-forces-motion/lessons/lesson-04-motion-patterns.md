# Lesson 04 — Patterns of Motion: Watch, Measure, Predict

**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** science journal, pencil, ruler or
tape measure, stopwatch or timer, a shallow bowl and a marble or small
ball, a swing set or a string-and-weight pendulum at the table

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will learn that many motions have
a **pattern** — they repeat in a regular way — and that once you spot the
pattern you can **predict** what the object will do next.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "In Unit 01 you found patterns in data
tables. Today we find patterns in *motion*. What repeats about a swing
going back and forth?" Any answer about "it comes back" shows readiness.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

A **pattern** is something that repeats in a regular way. Many motions have
patterns, and a pattern is powerful: **if the past motion repeated
regularly, you can predict the future motion.**

Three classic grade-3 patterns (these are the examples the standard
itself names):

- **A child swinging in a swing** — forward, back, forward, back, in a
  steady rhythm.
- **A ball rolling back and forth in a bowl** — side to side to side,
  slowing a little each pass.
- **Two children on a seesaw** — up-down, up-down, trading places.

Watch the marble in the bowl with the adult. Push it gently and watch: it
rolls up one side, back down, up the other side — back and forth, back and
forth. Now **predict**: after it rolls up the left side, where will it go?
*Down and across to the right side.* Then watch and check. The pattern let
you predict.

Scientists also **measure** patterns. Count how many back-and-forth passes
the marble makes in 10 seconds. Try again: a similar count means the pattern
is steady. (We do not use the grown-up words "period" or "frequency" — the
standard says those come later. "How many passes in 10 seconds" is enough.)

**Worked example 1 — predict the swing:**

> Adult (at a swing, or with the table pendulum): "Watch three swings:
> forward, back, forward. The pattern is *forward-back, forward-back*.
> **Predict** the next move." (Learner: "Back.") "Watch — back! The pattern
> predicted it."

**Worked example 2 — measure the pattern:**

> Adult: "I will count the marble's passes for 10 seconds. Ready — 1, 2,
> 3, 4, 5, 6." Then: "I **predict** about 6 passes in the next 10 seconds
> if I push it the same way. Let's test." (Adult repeats.) "My prediction
> was close — the pattern held."

Key idea to say plainly: **Motion with a regular pattern can be predicted.
Watch, measure, find the pattern, then say what comes next — and check.**

## Guided practice (adult coaches)

1. Marble in the bowl: learner watches three passes, then **predicts** the
   next two passes before they happen. *(Key: predictions match the
   back-and-forth pattern.)*
2. Count-and-predict: learner counts passes in 10 seconds, predicts the
   count for a second 10-second run with the same push, then checks.
   *(Key: counts are close; prediction supported by the pattern.)*
3. **Error-analysis:** "The marble rolled left, then right, then left,
   then stopped. The pattern says it will roll left next." Learner
   explains the error. *(Key: the pattern broke — the marble stopped, so
   "what comes next" must be re-observed; predictions only hold while the
   pattern holds.)*

## Independent practice

4. Draw a three-frame cartoon: a swing at *forward*, *back*, *forward* —
   then draw frame 4 and label the prediction. *(Key: frame 4 shows the
   swing going back.)*
5. Read the pattern story: "A ball rolls in a bowl: left, right, left,
   right, left, ___." Fill the blank and write one sentence: "I predict
   ___ because ___." *(Key: "right"; "...because the pattern alternates
   left and right.")*
6. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "Your friend predicts the marble will keep
   going back and forth forever. Do you agree? Why or why not?"
   *(Key: No — friction (Lesson 05) slows it a little each pass; the
   pattern fades, so the prediction has a limit.)*

## Application

Pattern hunt: the learner finds one repeating motion at home (a rocking
chair, a ceiling fan on low, a bouncing ball) and journals the pattern in
words or drawings, plus one prediction and whether it came true.

## Exit check

Adult asks: "What is a pattern of motion, and what does it let you do?"
*(Answer: a motion that repeats regularly; it lets you predict what comes
next.)*

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading/language access:** prediction sentence frame — "I predict ___
  because the pattern is ___." Learner may draw the frames instead of
  writing.
- **Alternate response mode:** act the pattern with the body (step left,
  step right) before drawing it.
- **Extension:** The learner times how long the marble keeps its pattern
  before stopping, recording the count in a Unit 01-style table — a data
  table of a fading pattern.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: "predicting" means "guessing." Distinguish sharply:
a prediction uses the observed pattern as evidence; a guess does not. Tie
to Unit 01 claims: predictions are claims about the future, checked by
watching. Avoid the terms period/frequency (3-PS2-2 boundary).
