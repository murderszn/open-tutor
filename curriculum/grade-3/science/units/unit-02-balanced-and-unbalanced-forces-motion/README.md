# Unit 02 — Balanced and Unbalanced Forces and Motion

**Grade:** 3 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#19](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/19)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 7–10 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 30 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

This unit introduces the core grade-3 physics idea: every **push and pull**
is a **force**, and forces have a **direction** and a relative **strength**.
Forces acting on an object can be **balanced** (equal in size, opposite in
direction — the object keeps doing what it was doing) or **unbalanced** (a
motion **change** happens: start, stop, speed up, slow down, or change
direction). The unit's investigation — testing how the *strength* of a
launch push changes how far a toy car rolls — is a one-variable fair
investigation honoring the 3-PS2-1 assessment boundary, and it reuses Unit
01's fair-test routine verbatim. The unit also teaches **friction** as a
force that opposes motion, and how to spot a **pattern** in an object's
motion and use it to **predict** what happens next.

## Prerequisites (from Unit 01; re-taught here as needed)

- Asking a testable question; measuring length in centimeters and
  millimeters starting from zero; recording data in labeled tables.
- Planning a fair test: one variable changed, everything else the same,
  three or more trials; making a claim with evidence and a limitation.
- Science safety practices: adult supervises every investigation; materials
  stay away from the mouth; ramps are adult-checked; rolling objects stay
  clear of stairs.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Name forces as **pushes** or **pulls** and describe a force's **direction**
   and relative **strength** (stronger vs. weaker) (PS2.A).
2. Distinguish **balanced forces** (equal size, opposite directions — no
   change in motion) from **unbalanced forces** (a change in motion:
   starts, stops, speeds up, slows down, or changes direction) (3-PS2-1).
3. Plan and carry out a **one-variable fair investigation** that changes a
   force's size or direction and gathers evidence about the effect on an
   object's motion (3-PS2-1; assessment boundary honored) (SEP-3).
4. **Observe and measure** an object's motion, record its **pattern**, and
   use the pattern to **predict** what the object will do next (3-PS2-2;
   no period/frequency vocabulary) (SEP-4).
5. Make an evidence-based **claim** about forces and motion supported by at
   least two data values, and explain **friction** as a force that opposes
   motion (qualitative only) (SEP-7; cause and effect).

## Essential question

*How do pushes and pulls change the way things move — and how can I tell
what will happen next?*

## Vocabulary

**Force** — a push or a pull acting on an object. · **Push** — a force that
moves something away. · **Pull** — a force that moves something closer. ·
**Direction** — the way a force points (toward or away). · **Strength** — how
strong a force is; compared as *stronger* or *weaker* (never numbers here).
· **Balanced forces** — forces that are equal in size and opposite in
direction, so they cancel out. · **Unbalanced forces** — forces that do not
cancel; they change the object's motion. · **Motion** — how an object is
moving (still, rolling, sliding, swinging). · **Change in motion** — a
start, stop, speed up, slow down, or direction change. · **Friction** — a
force that opposes sliding or rolling when surfaces touch. · **Gravity** —
the force that pulls objects down toward Earth. · **Pattern** — a motion
that repeats in a regular way. · **Predict** — say what will happen next,
using the pattern as evidence.

## Materials

Toy car or ball, a short board or stiff cardboard ramp, books to raise the
ramp, two surface cloths (smooth paper/cardboard and a small towel), a
length of soft rope or a towel for a "team tug," a shallow bowl and a
marble or small ball, a swing set (or a string-and-weight pendulum at the
table), ruler/tape measure with centimeters, stopwatch or timer, science
journal, pencil and crayons, tape for a target zone, two or three
spring-free paperback "barriers" (soft blocks or cardboard). All
investigations are adult-supervised; the adult sets every ramp and
supervises every tug game (no neck wraps; hands on the rope only).

## Differentiation and accessibility

- Oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative response modes always available;
  the adult scribes dictated observations on request. Force-direction arrows
  may be drawn, pointed at, or acted out with the body.
- Sentence frames: "The force is a ___ because ___," "The forces are
  balanced because ___," "The motion changed because the force was
  unbalanced — the car ___," "I predict ___ because the pattern is ___."
- Large-print, high-contrast force cards; vocabulary taught with objects
  and motion first, word second; visual science word wall; home-language
  labels welcomed.
- Short sessions with movement breaks; every investigation has a table
  variant (marble in a tray) and an active variant (body-scale tug).
- The generated image ships with a text-only alternative; color is never
  the only cue in sorts or graphs.

## Safety

Adult supervises every investigation and previews every procedure. Tug
games are supervised, rope/towel only, no wrapping around necks or wrists,
and stopped immediately if anyone is pulled off balance. Ramps are built
from books and boards at table height with rolling objects kept clear of
stairs; the adult sets and checks every ramp. The adult handles the bowl
pendulum and any drop-from-height demos; nothing is thrown. Materials stay
away from the mouth; hands washed after any soil or outdoor work.
