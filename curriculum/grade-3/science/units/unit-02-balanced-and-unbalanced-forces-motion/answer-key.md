# Answer Key — Unit 02: Balanced and Unbalanced Forces and Motion

**For the guiding adult only.** Every practice item, quiz question,
assessment task, and the project sample responses are solved here. Keep
this file separate from the learner.

---

## Lesson 01

**Guided practice**

1. Door = push (away from you); drawer = pull (toward you); kick = push;
   backpack lift = pull (up, against gravity); swing push = push. Direction
   is shown by pointing.
2. Acceptable: "The force was a *push*; the direction was *toward the
   window* (or as observed); the strength was *weaker/stronger*." Both
   strengths must be compared, not numbered.
3. Error: gravity does not push things up. Correction: gravity **pulls**
   objects **down** toward Earth.

**Independent practice**

4. Three arrows: push-to-the-right arrow labeled "push"; pull-to-the-left
   arrow labeled "pull"; downward arrow labeled "gravity — pull." Arrows
   must point the described way.
5. Push (P): opening a lunchbox, pressing a doorbell, pushing a stroller.
   Pull (L): dragging a chair, pulling a sled, lifting a bucket.
6. Acceptable: "A stronger force changes motion more because the car
   rolled farther when I pushed firmly." Any observation-based completion.

## Lesson 02

**Guided practice**

1. Equal-effort tug: balanced; rope stays still. Harder-pull tug:
   unbalanced; rope moves toward the stronger side.
2. (a) balanced — gravity down, table up, equal and opposite, book still;
   (b) balanced — equal opposite pushes, box still; (c) not balanced —
   one push only; (d) not balanced — one side stronger.
3. Error: if the rope moves, the forces cannot be equal and opposite.
   Correction: balanced forces cause no change in motion, so a moving rope
   means the pulls are unbalanced (or were not truly equal).

**Independent practice**

4. Two arrows on the ball, equal length, opposite directions. Sentence:
   "The forces are balanced because they are equal in strength and
   opposite in direction."
5. All three: "The forces are balanced. The object *stays still / does
   not move*."
6. The friend is wrong. Both teams ARE pulling — equally and in opposite
   directions. The pulls cancel out, so the rope stays still. Still does
   not mean force-free.

## Lesson 03

**Guided practice**

1. (a) start; (b) stop; (c) speed up; (d) change direction; (e) slow down.
2. Error: the wall exerted a force on the ball (and friction/gravity
   played parts). Correction: a change in motion always means an
   unbalanced force acted — here, the wall's push reversed the ball.

**Independent practice**

3. Balanced: tug-of-war tie; book on a table; two equal pushes on a box.
   Unbalanced: one team winning; ball rolling after a kick; car slowing
   on grass.
4. Winning team: the rope/rope-side *starts moving* toward the stronger
   side. Rolling ball: *start* (it began moving when kicked). Slowing car:
   *slow down*.
5. Balanced drawing: ball stays still. Unbalanced drawing: ball moves
   toward the longer (stronger) arrow's direction.

## Lesson 04

**Guided practice**

1. After three observed passes, the next two predictions must follow the
   back-and-forth pattern (e.g., "right, then left").
2. Second-run count close to the first (within 1–2 passes); prediction
   supported by the steady pattern.
3. Error: the pattern broke when the marble stopped. Correction:
   predictions hold only while the pattern holds — re-observe before
   predicting again.

**Independent practice**

4. Frame 4 shows the swing moving *back* (forward, back, forward → back).
5. Blank: "right." Sentence: "I predict *right* because the pattern
   alternates left and right."
6. Disagree. Friction slows the marble a little each pass, so the pattern
   fades and stops — the prediction has a limit (a good limitation for a
   claim).

## Lesson 05

**Guided practice**

1. Bare table: less friction, easy slide. Towel: more friction, harder
   push / stops sooner. Evidence is the felt resistance or shorter slide.
2. Bumpy bike tires: more friction (grip). Polished slide: less friction
   (fast glide). Sandpaper: more friction. Ice rink: less friction.
3. Error: friction never speeds motion up. Correction: friction *opposes*
   motion — it slows things down; it helps only as grip or stopping.

**Independent practice**

4. Table: Bare board — less friction, car rolls farther. Towel — more
   friction, car rolls shorter.
5. Motion arrow points down the ramp; friction arrow points opposite
   (up the ramp), labeled "friction."
6. Smooth. "A smooth ramp has less friction opposing the motion, so the
   car keeps rolling farther." Both sentences must mention friction.

## Practice sets

### Week 7 — set A

1. (a) push, (b) pull, (c) push.
2. Arrow points toward the window, labeled "push."
3. Down; arrow drawn downward, labeled "gravity — pull."
4. Balanced — the pulls are equal in strength and opposite in direction,
   so they cancel and the rope does not move.
5. Gravity pulls down; the table pushes up (holds it up). Accept "the
   table pushes back up" or "something holds it up."
6. True. No fix needed.
7. No — the ball will not move, because the two pushes are equal and
   opposite (balanced), so they cancel out.
8. Disagree. The book has forces on it — gravity pulling down and the
   table pushing up. They are *balanced*, which is why the book stays
   still. Still ≠ force-free.

### Week 8 — set B

1. Balanced: tug-of-war tie; two equal pushes on a box. Unbalanced: one
   team winning; ball rolling after a kick.
2. Motion change: *start*. Forces: unbalanced (a push from one side only).
3. Motion change: *slow down*. The forces are unbalanced (friction/grass
   opposes the motion).
4. The left side (four pullers) pulls harder; the rope moves/slides toward
   the left.
5. Next: *back*. Pattern: forward-back alternation.
6. Blank: *right*. This is a **prediction**, not a guess — the alternating
   pattern is the evidence.
7. Error: the wall pushed back on the car. Correction: the direction change
   proves an *unbalanced* force acted — the wall's push.
8. The ball moves to the **right**, toward the longer arrow, because that
   push is *stronger*.

### Week 9 — set C

1. Bare board: less friction, car rolls farther. Towel: more friction,
   car rolls shorter.
2. Bumpy soles — more *friction* gives grip so the shoes do not slip.
3. The opposite way (opposite the direction of motion).
4. Acceptable claim: "The towel has more friction than the bare board.
   My evidence is that board rolls were 80 cm, 78 cm, 82 cm while towel
   rolls were 40 cm, 42 cm, 39 cm." Must cite two values and name the
   surface comparison.
5. Yes — fair. One variable: the surface. Kept the same (any two): the
   same car, the same ramp, the same ramp height, the same release point.
6. About 6 passes. Evidence: the first run's count — the steady pattern
   predicts a similar count.
7. Disagree. Gravity also acts (pulls the car down the ramp / toward the
   ground); the ramp pushes on the car too. Friction is one force among
   several.
8. Acceptable: names a smooth surface (e.g., "the bare board" or "waxed
   paper"), says it has *less* friction than the towel, and explains in
   two sentences that less friction opposes the motion less so the car
   rolls farther.

## Project sample responses

- **Plan:** Question: "Does a stronger launch push make the car roll
  farther?" Variable: push strength (gentle vs. firm). Controls (2+): same
  car, same ramp, same ramp height, same release point, same surface.
  Trials: 3 per strength. Measurement: cm from ramp bottom, ruler from 0.
- **Table (sample strong data):** Gentle: 45, 48, 44 cm. Firm: 95, 98,
  93 cm. All trials recorded with units.
- **Claim:** "A stronger launch push makes the car roll farther. My
  evidence is that firm pushes rolled 95–98 cm while gentle pushes rolled
  44–48 cm. One limitation is that I only tested two strengths on one
  surface."
- **Target-zone record:** "I predicted *firm* would reach the zone. Trials:
  hit, hit, miss." (Any honest record with a pre-stated prediction.)
- **Showcase:** states plan, claim with two values, zone result, and one
  redesign (e.g., "test a medium push to land in the zone more often").

## Unit assessment

**Part A (6 pts)**

1. (a) pull, (b) push. (1 pt each)
2. Right-pointing arrow labeled "push"; downward arrow labeled
   "gravity — pull." (1 pt per correct labeled arrow)
3. What stayed the same: the car and the direction (and surface — any
   correct control). What changed: push strength (gentle vs. firm). It
   tells us a stronger force changes motion more — the car rolled three
   times as far. (2 pts: 1 for same/changed, 1 for the conclusion)

**Part B (8 pts)**

4. Definition must contain: forces are *equal* in strength and *opposite*
   in direction, so they cancel / cause no change in motion. (2 pts; 1 if
   one element missing)
5. (a) gravity. (b) Balanced — the book does not move / stays still,
   which shows the down-pull and the table's up-push cancel. (1 pt each)
6. (a) Unbalanced. (b) The rope moves **left**, toward the stronger side
   (4 pullers beat 2). (1 pt each)
7. Error: the box has forces on it — pushes from both sides. Correction:
   the pushes are *equal and opposite*, so they are *balanced* and cancel
   out; that is why the box does not move. (2 pts)

**Part C (6 pts)**

8. Kicked ball: **start**. Caught ball: **stop**. (1 pt each)
9. (a) The pattern alternates left-right-left-right. (b) Next: **right**;
   evidence: the alternating pattern so far. (1 pt each)
10. A prediction uses the *observed pattern* as evidence (the marble kept
    alternating, so "right" is supported); a guess has no evidence behind
    it. (2 pts)

**Part D (4 pts)**

11. (a) The towel (rough surface) has more friction. (b) Friction points
    **opposite** the car's motion. (1 pt each)
12. One variable: the **number of people pulling** (1 vs. 2 vs. 3...).
    Keep the same (any two): the same wagon, the same pullers pulling the
    same way, the same surface/path, the same start signal. (1 pt for the
    variable, 1 pt for two controls)
