# Practice Sets — Unit 02: Balanced and Unbalanced Forces and Motion

Use the adult's judgment on response mode: writing, dictating to the adult,
drawing arrows, or acting with the body all count. Solutions and scoring
notes are in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

---

## Week 7 — Pushes, pulls, and balanced forces

1. Circle each force as a **push** or a **pull**: opening a door, pulling a
   wagon, kicking a soccer ball, lifting a laundry basket.
2. Draw an arrow showing the **direction** of the force when you push a toy
   car toward the window. Label it "push."
3. Which arrow shows gravity's pull on a falling apple — up, down, left, or
   right? Draw it and label it.
4. Two teams pull a rope equally hard in opposite directions. The rope does
   not move. Are the forces balanced or unbalanced? Explain in one sentence.
5. A book sits still on a table. Name the two balancing forces. *(Hint:
   one pulls down.)*
6. **True or false:** "A stronger push changes motion more than a weaker
   push in the same direction." Fix the sentence if it is false.
7. Draw a ball with two arrows: one push to the left and one equal push to
   the right. Will the ball move? Why or why not?
8. **Explain your reasoning:** Your friend says, "The book on the table
   has no forces on it because it is not moving." Do you agree? Use the
   word *balanced* in your answer.

## Week 8 — Unbalanced forces and predictions

1. Sort into **Balanced** or **Unbalanced**: tug-of-war tie; one tug team
   winning; two equal pushes on a box from opposite sides; a ball rolling
   after being kicked.
2. A ball sits still, then starts rolling to the right. Name the motion
   change and tell whether the forces were balanced or unbalanced.
3. A rolling car slows down on the grass. Name the motion change. What
   kind of force caused it — balanced or unbalanced?
4. Look at the unit's tug-of-war picture again
   ([assets/tug-of-war-balanced-unbalanced.png](assets/tug-of-war-balanced-unbalanced.png)).
   In the RIGHT scene, which side pulls harder, and which way does the rope
   move? **Text-only alternative:** the right scene shows four kids pulling
   left and two kids pulling right; the rope slides toward the left.
5. A swing moves: forward, back, forward, back. What comes next? Write your
   prediction and the pattern it uses.
6. A marble rolls in a bowl: left, right, left, right, left, ___.
   Complete the pattern. Then answer: is this a prediction or a guess?
   How do you know?
7. **Error analysis:** "The car changed direction when it hit the wall, so
   no force acted on it." Explain the error using the word *unbalanced*.
8. Draw a ball with one long arrow pushing right and one short arrow
   pushing left. Which way will the ball move? Explain using the word
   *stronger*.

## Week 9 — Friction, evidence, and patterns

1. Complete the table:

   | Surface | Friction (more / less) | Car rolls (farther / shorter) |
   |---|---|---|
   | Bare board | ? | ? |
   | Towel | ? | ? |

2. A designer wants shoes that do NOT slip on a wet floor. Should the
   soles be smooth or bumpy? Use the word *friction* in your answer.
3. Which way does friction point compared to the direction of motion —
   the same way or the opposite way?
4. Read the data (the adult's demonstration values from Lesson 05,
   recorded as observed data): bare board rolls: 80 cm, 78 cm, 82 cm;
   towel rolls: 40 cm, 42 cm, 39 cm. Write a claim with **two data values**
   about which surface has more friction. *(Use Unit 01's claim frame.)*
5. Was the two-surface test a fair test? Name the one variable and two
   things kept the same.
6. **Predict:** A marble rolls back and forth in a bowl 6 times in 10
   seconds. About how many passes do you expect in the next 10 seconds if
   it is pushed the same way? What is your evidence?
7. **Explain your reasoning:** "Friction is the only force acting on the
   rolling car." Do you agree? Name at least one other force.
8. Challenge: Design a ramp surface that makes a toy car roll as far as
   possible. Name your surface, say whether it has more or less friction
   than a towel, and explain your choice in two sentences.
