# Unit 03 — Informational Text: Main Idea, Structures, and Evidence

**Grade:** 3 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #20](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/20), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Stories tell you what happened. Informational text tells you what is true —
and shows you how it knows. In this unit the learner reads short original
informational passages about beavers, pumpkins, leaves, honeybees, and
beekeepers; finds each passage's main idea and the key details that support
it; follows steps in order; traces causes to effects; compares two ways of
saying something; uses headings, captions, glossaries, and indexes to find
answers fast; notices when a picture adds information the words do not; tells
the author's point of view apart from their own; and finally compares two
texts on the same topic and says which points matter most.

## Essential question

How do I know what a text is mostly about — and how do I prove it with
evidence from the text itself?

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–02 and the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–10):

- Read grade-level text aloud with accuracy and expression on repeated
  readings; self-correct with context (RF.3.4.a–c)
- Decode multisyllable words and use prefixes, suffixes, and root words to
  figure out word meanings (RF.3.3.a–c, L.3.4.b–c)
- Ask and answer who/what/where/when/why/how questions about a text
  (RI.3.1, RL.3.1)
- Recount a story and name its central message; describe characters and how
  their actions move events along (RL.3.2–3) — the same "what matters most"
  habit, now applied to facts instead of fiction
- Use a glossary or beginning dictionary to check a word's meaning
  (L.3.4.d)

Nothing in this unit assumes the learner has studied text structures before.
Lesson 01 re-teaches "main idea" from scratch: the main idea is what the
whole passage is mostly about, not the first sentence and not the most
interesting detail.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Ask and answer questions about an informational text, pointing to the
   exact words that answer them; determine the main idea of a passage,
   recount its key details, and explain how each detail supports the main
   idea (RI.3.1, RI.3.2).
2. Describe relationships in a text using time, sequence, and cause/effect
   language: put steps in order with first/next/then/finally, name a cause
   and its effect, and name how two things are alike and different
   (RI.3.3).
3. Determine the meaning of grade-3 academic and domain-specific words
   (for example *lodge*, *chlorophyll*, *colony*) from sentence-level
   context, a known word part, or a glossary entry (RI.3.4).
4. Use text features and search tools — table of contents, headings,
   captions, sidebars, glossary, index, and key words — to locate
   information on a given topic efficiently, without reading every page
   (RI.3.5).
5. Distinguish their own point of view from the author's, and use
   information from illustrations (photographs, diagrams, maps) together
   with the words to show understanding of where, when, why, and how key
   events occur (RI.3.6, RI.3.7).
6. Describe the logical connection between sentences and paragraphs
   (comparison, cause/effect, sequence); compare and contrast the most
   important points and key details of two texts on the same topic; and
   read grade 2–3 band informational texts with understanding
   (RI.3.8, RI.3.9, RI.3.10).

## Unit passages (all original, written for this unit)

Every passage in this unit was written for it. No published textbook, story,
or worksheet passage is reproduced. The facts are simplified for grade 3 but
checked against the reputable references in the
[Resource Pack](resource-pack.md); where a passage simplifies (for example,
leaf-color chemistry in one paragraph), the teacher guide says so.

- **"Beavers Build a Home"** (Lesson 01, practice, project): what a beaver
  dam and lodge are, and why beavers build them.
- **"From Seed to Pumpkin"** (Lesson 02): the steps of growing a pumpkin, in
  order.
- **"Why Leaves Change Color"** (Lesson 03): shorter, cooler days; the green
  color fading; yellow, orange, and red showing; leaves falling.
- **"Two Ways to Travel: Long Ago and Today"** (Lesson 03): comparing travel
  by horse-drawn wagon and by car.
- **"The Honeybee's Job"** (Lesson 04): a passage with headings, a caption,
  a sidebar, and a glossary entry, about jobs inside a hive.
- **"Beavers Are Remarkable Builders"** (Lesson 05): a passage whose author
  openly admires beavers — the unit's point-of-view text — paired with the
  generated pond illustration.
- **"Honeybees at Work" and "A Beekeeper's Day"** (Lesson 06, assessment):
  two short texts on the same topic, honeybees, with different most
  important points.

## 16-session pacing (track weeks 11–14; sessions 25–30 minutes)

| Session | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Week 11, S1 | Lesson 01 — Main idea and key details | Learner states a main idea in their own words and points to two supporting details |
| Week 11, S2 | Practice A — Main-idea sort (beaver passage, new short passages) | 4 of 5 main-idea choices correct, each with a detail cited |
| Week 11, S3 | Evidence hunt: reread "Beavers Build a Home"; answer who/what/where/why questions with finger-on-the-words evidence | Every answer names the sentence it came from |
| Week 11, S4 | Lesson 02 — Sequence: steps in order | Learner reorders 5 pumpkin steps correctly and retells them with first/next/then/finally |
| Week 12, S5 | Lesson 03 — Cause/effect and comparison connections | Learner names one cause and its effect, and one alike/different pair, from a passage |
| Week 12, S6 | Practice B/C — Cause/effect chains; sequence-and-cause sorting | Cause/effect matches correct; learner explains one chain in a full sentence |
| Week 12, S7 | Word workshop: domain words (lodge, dam, chlorophyll, colony) with context clues and glossary | Learner defines 4 of 5 words from context, then checks one in the glossary |
| Week 12, S8 | Lesson 04 — Text features and search tools | Learner finds 3 answers using only the table of contents, headings, and index — no full read |
| Week 13, S9 | Practice D — Text-feature scavenger hunt + illustration questions (generated pond image) | Learner answers from features alone, and names one thing the picture shows that the words do not |
| Week 13, S10 | Lesson 05 — Illustrations and the author's point of view | Learner separates "what the author thinks" from "what I think" in two sentences each |
| Week 13, S11 | Project work: "My Own Informational Page" — draft with heading, main idea, three details, one illustration plan | Draft has a heading, a main-idea sentence, and three details that support it |
| Week 13, S12 | Lesson 06 — Comparing two texts on one topic | Learner names one shared point and one point found in only one text |
| Week 14, S13 | Project finish + peer/adult read-aloud and revision | Final page read aloud; one revision made from feedback |
| Week 14, S14 | Formative quiz (week-04 quiz) | Adult scores with the answer key; reteach list written the same day |

## Differentiation and supports

- **Reading access:** the adult reads each passage aloud first; the learner
  follows along, then rereads. Any passage may be chunked into paragraphs
  with a pause-and-retell after each.
- **Response modes:** answers may be spoken, pointed to, drawn, or written.
  A learner who cannot yet write a paragraph may dictate the main-idea
  sentence while the adult scribes, then copy one line themselves.
- **Vocabulary support:** a personal word bank card for each lesson's new
  words; the adult pre-teaches *lodge*, *chlorophyll*, and *colony* before
  the passages that use them.
- **Extension:** fast finishers write a second paragraph for their project
  page, find a second text on beavers or bees in a library book, or turn a
  cause/effect chain into a labeled diagram (drawn, not copied).
- **What to reteach:** if the learner names a detail as the main idea,
  return to Lesson 01's "whole passage, not one sentence" test before moving
  on; do not let a shaky main idea ride into Lessons 05–06.

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for English Language
Arts**, grade 3, Reading: Informational Text, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/)
(framework opened and codes/descriptions cross-checked during the track
audit, 2026-10-03; no state adoption or accreditation claimed).

| Unit objective | Standard | Official description (grade 3) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | RI.3.1; RI.3.2 | Ask and answer questions to demonstrate understanding of a text, referring explicitly to the text as the basis for the answers. / Determine the main idea of a text; recount the key details and explain how they support the main idea. |
| 2 | RI.3.3 | Describe the relationship between a series of historical events, scientific ideas or concepts, or steps in technical procedures in a text, using language that pertains to time, sequence, and cause/effect. |
| 3 | RI.3.4 | Determine the meaning of general academic and domain-specific words and phrases in a text relevant to a grade 3 topic or subject area. |
| 4 | RI.3.5 | Use text features and search tools (e.g., key words, sidebars, hyperlinks) to locate information relevant to a given topic efficiently. |
| 5 | RI.3.6; RI.3.7 | Distinguish their own point of view from that of the author of a text. / Use information gained from illustrations (e.g., maps, photographs) and the words in a text to demonstrate understanding of the text (e.g., where, when, why, and how key events occur). |
| 6 | RI.3.8; RI.3.9; RI.3.10 | Describe the logical connection between particular sentences and paragraphs in a text (e.g., comparison, cause/effect, first/second/third in a sequence). / Compare and contrast the most important points and key details presented in two texts on the same topic. / By the end of the year, read and comprehend informational texts, including history/social studies, science, and technical texts, at the high end of the grades 2–3 text complexity band independently and proficiently. |
