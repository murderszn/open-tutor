# Practice — Unit 03: Informational Text

Six sets, one per lesson focus. The adult reads directions aloud; the
learner answers orally, by pointing, or in writing. Solutions are in the
[answer key](answer-key.md#practice) (adult only — do not hand the learner
the key).

## Practice A — Main idea and key details (Lesson 01)

Read this passage (original, written for this unit):

> **"The School Garden"**
>
> Our school has a garden behind the playground. In the garden, students
> grow tomatoes, carrots, and lettuce. Each class takes a turn watering
> the plants and pulling weeds. In fall, the classes pick the vegetables
> and wash them. Then the cafeteria cooks the vegetables for lunch.
> Students like eating food they grew themselves.

A1. What is the main idea of "The School Garden"? Write it in one
    sentence, in your own words.
A2. List two key details that support your main idea, and copy the words
    that prove each one.
A3. Which is the best main idea? (a) Students grow vegetables in the
    school garden and eat them for lunch. (b) Tomatoes are red.
    (c) Schools have playgrounds. Explain why the other two fail a test.
A4. Write one question about the passage that can be answered from the
    text, then answer it with evidence (point to the words).
A5. Error analysis: Pat said the main idea is "Watering plants." Why is
    Pat wrong?

## Practice B — Sequence (Lesson 02)

B1. Put these steps for planting a seed in order (write 1–5):
    (a) Water the soil. (b) Put the seed in the soil. (c) Watch the
    sprout push up. (d) Cover the seed with soil. (e) Dig a small hole.
B2. Retell the steps in order using at least three sequence words.
B3. Swap test: could you water the soil before digging the hole and
    still plant the seed the same way? Explain.
B4. Read this passage and number its steps 1–3:

    > To make a paper fan, first fold a sheet of paper back and forth
    > like an accordion. Next, pinch one end together and tape it.
    > Finally, spread the other end open like a fan.

B5. What would go wrong if you spread the paper open *before* pinching
    and taping one end? Explain.

## Practice C — Cause/effect and comparison (Lesson 03)

C1. Read this passage and name one cause and its effect:

    > The wind blew hard all night, so a tree branch fell across the
    > path. Because the path was blocked, walkers used the other path
    > in the morning.

C2. Compare a candle and a flashlight: write one sentence about how they
    are alike (name the feature) and one about how they are different
    (name the feature).
C3. "The branch fell, and then it rained." Did the falling branch cause
    the rain? Explain why or why not.
C4. From Lesson 03's leaf passage: what causes the yellow and orange
    colors to show? Answer with a signal word.
C5. Write a cause/effect sentence about something in your day, using
    "because" in one version and "so" in a second version of the same
    chain.

## Practice D — Text features and the pond illustration (Lessons 04–05)

D1. Using Lesson 04's table of contents: on which page does "Bees and
    People" begin?
D2. Using Lesson 04's index: on which pages is honey-making discussed?
D3. Look at the pond illustration in Lesson 05 (or read its text-only
    alternative). Where is the dam, and where is the lodge? Answer with
    the words *foreground* and *middle*.
D4. Name one thing the pond illustration shows that Lesson 05's passage
    does not say in words.
D5. A friend wants to know what *pollen* means. Which feature of Lesson
    04's pretend book answers that fastest, and what does it say?
D6. Build a mini table of contents for this practice page: list three
    sections (A, B, C) with a made-up page number for each.

## Practice E — Point of view (Lesson 05)

E1. Read this sentence: "Our school garden is the best garden in the
    whole town, and everyone should visit it!" Is that a fact or the
    writer's opinion? Which words tell you?
E2. Rewrite E1's sentence as a fact (remove the opinion) — what remains
    true without the writer's judgment?
E3. Read this sentence: "The school garden is behind the playground."
    Fact or opinion? Explain.
E4. Write one "The author thinks…" sentence about Lesson 05's beaver
    passage, and one "I think…" sentence of your own about beavers.
E5. Error analysis: Sam wrote, "Beavers are the most remarkable builders
    in the animal world" as a fact he learned. Correct Sam's sentence so
    it accurately reports the author's view.

## Practice F — Comparing two texts (Lesson 06)

Read this pair (original, written for this unit):

> **Text C — "Rain in the City"**
>
> When it rains in the city, water runs off roofs and streets into storm
> drains. The drains carry the water to rivers. Rain helps the city
> because it washes dust off the streets and waters the park trees.

> **Text D — "Rain on the Farm"**
>
> When it rains on the farm, water soaks into the fields. The crops drink
> the water and grow. Rain helps the farm because it gives the plants the
> water they need, but too much rain can flood a field and hurt the crop.

F1. Write the main idea of Text C and of Text D, one sentence each.
F2. Name one important point shared by both texts, with a phrase from
    each text that shows it.
F3. Name one important point found only in Text C, and one found only in
    Text D.
F4. Do the texts disagree about rain? Explain with evidence.
F5. Which is a most important point of the pair: "rain washes dust off
    city streets" or "rain gives plants the water they need to grow"?
    Explain with the "fall apart without it" test — or argue that both
    matter, one per text, and say why.
