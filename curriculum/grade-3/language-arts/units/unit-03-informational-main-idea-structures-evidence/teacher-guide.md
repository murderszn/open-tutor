# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Informational Text

**Audience:** the guiding adult only. This guide explains the teaching
moves, the misconceptions behind the most common wrong answers, and what
to reteach when. It does not contain the learner's answers — those are in
the [answer key](answer-key.md), kept separate so a learner browsing the
unit does not stumble onto solutions.

## How to teach this unit

- **Read aloud first, every time.** Grade-3 informational text is often
  harder to decode than to understand. The adult reads each passage
  aloud once while the learner follows, then the learner rereads. Never
  let decoding difficulty masquerade as a comprehension failure.
- **One skill per lesson, revisited everywhere.** Lesson 01's main idea
  returns in every later lesson (Lessons 02–06 all ask for a main idea
  before comparing or connecting). If Lesson 01 is shaky, later lessons
  will look shaky too — fix Lesson 01, not Lesson 06.
- **Evidence is a habit, not a worksheet section.** Every answer in this
  unit should end with the learner pointing to words. "Because the book
  says so" is not evidence; "because it says 'the dam slows the water'"
  is. Model the pointing until the learner does it without prompting.
- **Sessions are 25–30 minutes.** If a lesson runs long, split it at the
  guided-practice boundary and finish the next session; do not rush the
  modeled examples, which carry the teaching.

## Lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — Main idea and key details

- **Core move:** the three tests (whole-passage, too-small, too-big).
  Teach them as questions the learner asks themselves, not as rules to
  recite.
- **Common wrong answer:** "Beaver teeth are strong" or any single vivid
  detail. Response: run the whole-passage test paragraph by paragraph.
  Teeth appear in one sentence of one paragraph; a main idea must fit
  all four.
- **Common wrong answer:** copying the first sentence as the main idea.
  Response: the first sentence introduces beavers and their homes; the
  main idea must also cover the dam, the lodge, and the why (safety and
  warmth).
- **Reteach path:** if the learner cannot state a main idea after two
  tries, drop to a one-paragraph passage (Practice A, "The School
  Garden"), then rebuild to the four-paragraph beaver passage.

### Lesson 02 — Sequence

- **Core move:** the swap test. A sequence is only real if swapping two
  steps breaks the process.
- **Common wrong answer:** listing facts about pumpkins instead of steps.
  Response: ask "What happens *first*?" — facts have no first; processes
  do.
- **Common wrong answer:** copying the passage instead of retelling.
  Response: cover the passage and retell from the step cards; copying
  proves eyesight, retelling proves understanding.
- **Reteach path:** act the steps out (mime planting, sprouting, growing,
  picking) before writing anything.

### Lesson 03 — Cause/effect and comparison

- **Core move:** order is not cause. Say it explicitly, twice, with the
  leaves example: leaves change color *before* they fall, but the color
  change does not *cause* the falling — drying out does.
- **Common wrong answer:** "They are different" with no feature named.
  Response: always ask "Different in *what*?" — speed, power, size, cost.
  No feature, no credit.
- **Common wrong answer:** treating the cause as the effect when the
  signal word sits mid-sentence ("trees stop making chlorophyll because
  there is less sunlight"). Response: cover each half and ask, "Is this
  the why, or the what-happened?"
- **Reteach path:** use the learner's own life (forgot umbrella → got
  wet) before returning to passage chains.

### Lesson 04 — Text features and search tools

- **Core move:** the fast-finder routine (question → key word → contents
  or index → heading → answer). Time the learner once, for fun — the
  point is that searching beats reading everything.
- **Common wrong answer:** reading the whole passage to answer a contents
  question. Response: re-teach the routine; the skill being tested is
  *finding*, not reading.
- **Common wrong answer:** skipping captions and sidebars. Response:
  Modeled example 2 shows two facts that exist *only* in the caption and
  sidebar. Ask after every passage: "What did the caption add?"
- **Reteach path:** do the application activity with a real book the
  learner likes; motivation fixes most feature-skipping.

### Lesson 05 — Illustrations and point of view

- **Core move:** dam vs. lodge, separated in the picture. Check Guided
  practice item 1 before moving on — if the learner still mixes them up,
  the rest of the lesson's picture work is built on sand.
- **Core move:** the "author thinks / I think" frame. Require it every
  time an opinion is retold. A learner who says "beavers are remarkable"
  as a fact has not learned RI.3.6 yet, whatever else they can do.
- **Say plainly:** the pond illustration is AI-generated for this lesson.
  It is not a photograph of a real pond and it does not prove anything
  about real beavers; it illustrates the passage. Learners who cannot see
  it use the text-only alternative, which carries every fact the
  activities need.
- **Reteach path:** if point of view confuses, use the learner's own
  opinions first ("I think broccoli is…") before the author's.

### Lesson 06 — Comparing two texts

- **Core move:** the "fall apart without it" test for importance, and
  the difference between a shared *topic* ("both are about bees") and a
  shared *point* ("both say the colony's survival matters").
- **Common wrong answer:** "The texts disagree" when they merely cover
  different parts of a topic. Response: disagreement needs two claims
  that cannot both be true. Different coverage is not disagreement.
- **Common wrong answer:** listing every detail instead of weighing.
  Response: require the Venn chart first; sentences second.
- **Reteach path:** compare two things the learner knows well (two
  pets, two games) before returning to texts.

## Simplifications the adult should know

- Leaf color (Lesson 03): the passage says trees "stop making
  chlorophyll" and other colors "show through." In reality, yellow and
  orange pigments (carotenoids) are present all summer and masked by
  green; red and purple (anthocyanins) are newly made by some trees in
  fall. The passage's version is the standard grade-3 simplification and
  is accurate at that level; do not present it as the full chemistry.
- Beaver dams and lodges (Lessons 01, 05): beavers build dams to create
  deep water that protects the lodge entrance; not every beaver family
  builds both in every place. The passages describe the typical pattern,
  not a universal rule.
- Honeybee jobs (Lessons 04, 06): worker roles change with age, and the
  queen's egg-laying rate varies by season; "more than a thousand eggs in
  one day" is a peak-season figure, simplified here as in most grade-3
  texts.

## What success looks like

By the end of the unit the learner can: state a main idea in their own
words and defend it with two details; order a process and retell it;
name a cause and its effect without confusing order with cause; find an
answer with a contents page or index; say what a picture adds; keep
"the author thinks" apart from "I think"; and compare two texts by
shared points, only-one-text points, and importance. If one of those is
missing, reteach that lesson — do not move to Unit 04 hoping it will
catch up on its own.
