## Part A — One passage, many skills

Read this passage (original, written for this unit):

> **"How Rain Gets to the River"**
>
> First, rain falls on hills and fields. Next, the water runs downhill in
> small streams. Then the streams join together into a bigger river.
> Finally, the river carries the water to a lake or to the sea.
>
> Rain helps the land in two ways. It gives plants the water they need
> to grow. It also fills ponds and lakes where animals drink. But too
> much rain at once can cause a flood. When a river rises too high, the
> water spills over its banks and covers nearby fields.

A1. What is the main idea of the passage? Write it in one sentence, in
    your own words.
A2. List two key details that support your main idea, and copy the words
    that prove each one.
A3. Put the four water steps in order, using sequence words.
A4. Name one cause and its effect from the passage. Quote the signal
    word.
A5. Is too much rain good or bad for fields, according to the passage?
    Explain with evidence — the passage says both a good and a bad
    thing, so name both.

## Part B — Features and point of view

Read this heading, caption, and opinion paragraph (original, written for
this unit):

> **Heading: "The River in Spring"**
>
> *[Picture: a wide river with fast-moving water and green trees on both
> banks.]*
> **Caption:** In spring, melting snow makes the river run fast and
> high.
>
> Rivers are the most wonderful places on Earth. Nothing beats sitting
> beside a river and listening to the water. I think every town should
> have a river park.

B1. What new fact does the caption give that the heading alone does
    not?
B2. What is the author's point of view about rivers? Quote two opinion
    words or phrases.
B3. Write one "The author thinks…" sentence and one "I think…"
    sentence about rivers. They may agree or disagree.

## Part C — Comparing two texts

Read these two texts (original, written for this unit):

> **Text I — "Seeds That Fly"**
>
> Some seeds travel by wind. A dandelion seed has a fluffy white top
> that catches the wind like a tiny parachute. The wind carries the seed
> far from its parent plant, and where it lands, a new dandelion can
> grow. Traveling by wind helps the plant spread to new places.

> **Text J — "Seeds That Hitch a Ride"**
>
> Some seeds travel by hitching a ride on animals. A burr is a seed with
> tiny hooks that catch on an animal's fur. When the animal moves, it
> carries the burr to a new place. Later the burr falls off, and where
> it lands, a new plant can grow. Traveling on animals helps the plant
> spread to new places.

C1. Write the main idea of Text I and of Text J, one sentence each.
C2. Name one important point shared by both texts, with a phrase from
    each text that shows it.
C3. Name one important point found only in Text I, and one found only
    in Text J.
C4. Which is a most important point of the pair: "dandelion seeds have
    fluffy white tops" or "seeds travel to new places so new plants can
    grow"? Explain with the "fall apart without it" test.

## Part D — Write an informational paragraph

D1. Choose one topic: beavers, honeybees, pumpkins, or rain. Write a
    short paragraph (3–5 sentences) that (a) states a main idea in the
    first sentence, (b) gives two key details that support it, and
    (c) ends with a concluding sentence. Use at least one sequence word
    or one cause/effect signal word. The adult may scribe a dictated
    paragraph; the ideas must be the learner's own.

**Scoring guide (adult):** Part A: 5 items × 2 points = 10. Part B:
3 items × 2 points = 6. Part C: 4 items × 2 points = 8. Part D: 6 points
(main idea 2, two supporting details 2, sequence/cause signal word 1,
conclusion 1). Total 30. 26–30 = ready for Unit 04; 20–25 = revise the
missed skill with the matching Practice set and re-check; below 20 =
reteach Lessons 01–03 and re-assess with the quiz items first.
