# Lesson 02 — Sequence: Steps in Order

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can put steps in order and retell them with
first, next, then, and finally."
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** this lesson's passage below;
step cards the adult writes (one step per card, in mixed order); a pencil
and paper.
**Standards:** RI.3.3 (sequence), RI.3.2 (main idea and details, revisited).

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult asks: "What do you do first when you get ready for school? What
comes next? What is last?" The learner retells three steps in order. The
adult points out the order words they used — *first, then, last* — and
says: "Informational text does the same thing. When a passage explains
how something happens, the steps come in an order, and changing the order
changes the meaning. You cannot pick a pumpkin before you plant the seed."

## Explicit teaching

A **sequence** is the order in which things happen or steps are done.
Writers signal sequence with time words: **first, second, third, next,
then, after that, before, finally, in the end**. When you read steps, ask:

1. **What happens first?** Find the starting step — often the one that
   must happen before anything else can.
2. **What happens next, in order?** Follow the time words, and check that
   each step makes sense after the one before it.
3. **What happens last?** The final step usually finishes the process.
4. **Retell it.** Say the steps back in order using your own sequence
   words. If you cannot retell it, you have not followed the sequence yet.

A useful check: **swap test.** Swap two steps and ask, "Could it still
happen this way?" If the answer is no — you cannot harvest before you
plant — the order matters, and you have found a real sequence.

## Passage — "From Seed to Pumpkin" (original, written for this unit)

First, in spring, the farmer plants a pumpkin seed in the soil. The farmer
covers the seed with soil and waters it.

Next, the seed sprouts. A root grows down into the soil, and a small stem
pushes up toward the sun.

Then the plant grows into a long vine with big leaves. Yellow flowers
bloom on the vine, and bees visit the flowers.

After that, a tiny green pumpkin begins to grow where a flower was. The
pumpkin grows bigger and bigger all summer.

Finally, in fall, the pumpkin turns orange. The farmer cuts it from the
vine and carries it to market.

### Modeled example 1 — Ordering the steps

*Adult writes the five steps on cards, in mixed order, and thinks aloud:*
"Which step must come first? Planting — nothing grows before the seed is
in the soil. The words say 'First, in spring, the farmer plants.' Next:
the seed sprouts — 'Next, the seed sprouts.' Then: the vine grows and
flowers bloom — 'Then the plant grows into a long vine.' After that: the
tiny green pumpkin grows — 'After that, a tiny green pumpkin begins to
grow.' Finally: the pumpkin turns orange and is picked — 'Finally, in
fall, the pumpkin turns orange.'"

"Swap test: could the pumpkin turn orange before the flower blooms? No —
the pumpkin grows where a flower was. The order is real."

### Modeled example 2 — Retelling with sequence words

*Adult models a retelling:* "First, the farmer plants a seed in spring.
Next, the seed sprouts, with a root going down and a stem going up. Then
a vine grows, flowers bloom, and bees visit. After that, a tiny green
pumpkin grows bigger all summer. Finally, in fall, the pumpkin turns
orange and the farmer picks it."

"Notice: I did not copy the passage word for word. I kept the order and
the key facts, and I used my own sequence words. That is a retelling, and
it proves I followed the sequence. Also notice the main idea underneath
the steps: **a pumpkin grows from a seed through a series of steps, from
planting in spring to picking in fall** — the steps are the key details
that support it."

## Guided practice

Answers are in the [answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-02--sequence).

1. List the five steps of "From Seed to Pumpkin" in order, one short
   phrase each.
2. Which time word tells you the vine-and-flowers step comes after
   sprouting? Quote it.
3. Swap test: could the farmer carry the pumpkin to market before it
   turns orange? Explain why or why not, using the passage.
4. Retell the pumpkin steps in order, out loud, using at least three
   sequence words (first, next, then, after that, finally).
5. Read these mixed-up steps for making a sandwich and put them in order:
   (a) Eat the sandwich. (b) Put cheese between two slices of bread.
   (c) Take out the bread and cheese. (d) Put the sandwich on a plate.
6. What is the main idea of "From Seed to Pumpkin"? Name two steps that
   support it as key details.

## Independent practice

7. Read this short passage and number its steps 1–4 in order:

   > To wash your hands, first wet them with clean water. Next, rub soap
   > all over your hands, front and back, for about twenty seconds. Then
   > rinse the soap off with clean water. Finally, dry your hands with a
   > clean towel.

8. Write the hand-washing steps in order, in your own words, using
   sequence words.
9. Error analysis: Maya put the pumpkin steps in this order: sprout,
   plant, flower, grow, pick. Which step is out of place, and what does
   the passage say comes before it?

## Application

Choose a process you know (brushing teeth, making a bed, building with
blocks, getting ready for bed). Write or dictate its steps in order, using
at least four sequence words, then read it to the adult. The adult checks
with the swap test: can any two steps swap without breaking the process?
If yes, the order needs fixing.

## Exit check

Without looking: name the five pumpkin steps in order, and say which
sequence word goes with each step.

## Supports and extension

- **Support:** act the steps out in order (mime planting, sprouting,
  growing, picking) before writing anything; movement fixes order.
- **Alternate response mode:** order picture cards instead of writing
  sentences; point to the step cards in order while the adult reads them.
- **Extension:** write the pumpkin process as a numbered list with a
  one-sentence "what happens" and a one-sentence "why it must come at this
  point" for each step.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: (1) treating any list as a sequence — a list of
facts (beaver dam parts) has no order; a process does. (2) Copying the
passage instead of retelling — retelling in one's own words is the skill.
(3) Using "first" for every step. Insist on varied sequence words. See
the [teacher guide](../teacher-guide.md#lesson-02--sequence) and
[answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-02--sequence).
