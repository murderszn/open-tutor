# Project — "My Own Informational Page"

**Type:** investigation and writing project (Sessions 11 and 13, with
optional home research between them)
**Standards:** RI.3.1, RI.3.2, RI.3.5, RI.3.7, W.3.2 (introduce a topic,
develop with facts and details, provide a conclusion), SL.3.4 (report with
facts and details at an understandable pace).

## The task

Make one informational page about an animal, plant, place, or everyday
process you can observe or already know well (a pet, a garden plant, how
bread is made, your neighborhood park). Your page teaches a reader
something true. It must be your own words — no copying from a book or a
website.

## Materials

- Paper and pencil (or a device the adult supervises, if the learner
  types)
- The pond illustration in Lesson 05 as a model for what a good
  illustration does (shows something words do not say)
- A real book or library book about the topic, if one is at hand, for
  checking one fact — the adult helps find it
- Crayons or markers for the illustration

## Steps

1. **Choose and observe (Session 11, first half).** Pick your topic.
   Write down five facts you already know or can observe. Circle the
   three that matter most for a reader who knows nothing about it.
2. **Plan the page (Session 11, second half).** Fill this plan:
   - Title (what the page is about):
   - Main-idea sentence (what the whole page is mostly about):
   - Key detail 1:
   - Key detail 2:
   - Key detail 3:
   - Text feature I will use (heading, caption, sidebar, or glossary
     word with a definition):
   - What my illustration will show that my words do not:
3. **Check one fact (between sessions, with the adult).** Find one of
   your facts in a book or another trusted source. Write the source's
   title and the page or section where you found it. If a fact turns out
   wrong, fix it — fixing is part of the project.
4. **Draft the page (Session 13, first half).** Write a title, a
   main-idea sentence, and one short paragraph per key detail (or one
   paragraph holding all three details, if the learner writes shorter
   pieces). Add your chosen text feature and your illustration with a
   caption.
5. **Read aloud and revise (Session 13, second half).** Read the page
   aloud to the adult. The adult asks: does every detail support the
   main idea? Does the illustration add something? Is anything copied
   word for word? Make at least one revision from the feedback.
6. **Present (end of Session 13).** Present the page: read the title and
   main-idea sentence, point to each detail, and explain what the
   illustration adds. Speak in complete sentences at an understandable
   pace.

## Deliverables

1. The filled plan (step 2).
2. The fact-check note (step 3): one fact, its source title, and where in
   the source it appears.
3. The finished informational page: title, main-idea sentence, three
   supporting details, one text feature, one illustration with a
   caption.
4. A short spoken presentation (step 6), observed by the adult.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (3) | Developing (2) | Starting (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main idea | One clear sentence fits the whole page; learner can restate it | Main idea is present but partly a detail, or too broad | No main idea, or the "main idea" is a single detail |
| Key details and evidence | Three details, each supporting the main idea; learner can point to where each is shown or sourced | Two details support the main idea, or one detail drifts off-topic | Fewer than two details, or details do not support the main idea |
| Text feature | Feature is used correctly and adds information (a real caption, sidebar, or glossary entry) | Feature is present but decorative or incorrect | No text feature |
| Illustration | Illustration shows something the words do not say, with a caption that adds a fact | Illustration repeats the words only, or has no caption | No illustration |
| Accuracy and own words | Facts checked (step 3 done); all wording is the learner's own | One fact unchecked or one copied phrase remains | Facts unchecked and wording copied |
| Presentation | Complete sentences, understandable pace, points to details and illustration | Mostly complete sentences; pace rushed or too quiet in places | Single words or fragments; cannot point to details |

**Scoring:** 15–18 = ready to move on; 10–14 = revise one criterion and
re-present; below 10 = re-teach with Lesson 01 and re-draft the page.
The rubric is an adult teaching tool, not a grade to record publicly.

## Safety and boundaries

- Observe animals and plants without touching wildlife, entering water,
  or picking unknown plants. Observation from a path or window is enough.
- No photographs of real children, no names, no school or home details on
  the page. The page teaches about the topic, not about the learner.
- Any online fact-checking is adult-supervised, and the source is recorded
  by title — the learner never creates an account or posts anything.
