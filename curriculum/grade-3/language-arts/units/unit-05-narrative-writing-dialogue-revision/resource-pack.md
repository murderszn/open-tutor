# Resource Pack — Unit 05: Narrative Writing: Dialogue and Revision

## Summary

**Goal:** give the guiding adult free, no-account routes to support
grade-3 narrative writing — establishing a situation and characters,
ordering events with temporal words, writing dialogue that develops the
story, showing actions/thoughts/feelings, closing with a sense of
resolution, choosing exact words, and revising with the plan–revise–edit
routine.
**Age fit:** grade 3 (about 8–9); adult previews every video before use;
videos supplement the lessons and never replace explicit teaching or the
adult check. Checked 2026-11-06 unless noted.

## Web references

Checked 2026-11-06 (opened and read) unless noted.

1. **Reading Rockets — "Teaching Writing to Diverse Student
   Populations"**
   `https://www.readingrockets.org/article/teaching-writing-diverse-student-populations`
   *Why:* the adult-side grounding for Lessons 01 and 06 — the SPACE
   LAUNCH narrative-planning strategy (story structure + planning
   steps, with a planning sheet and peer score card) and the
   revising/editing strategies COPS/COLA, C-D-O (compare–diagnose–
   operate), and PQP (praise–question–polish), which this unit adapts
   as Praise–Question–Polish feedback. Verified live 2026-11-06.
   Free, no account. Adult reference.
2. **ReadWriteThink — "Comics in the Classroom as an Introduction to
   Narrative Structure"**
   `https://www.readwritethink.org/classroom-resources/lesson-plans/comics-classroom-introduction-narrative?tab=1`
   *Why:* a grades 3–5 lesson on plot as significant events (not a list
   of things that happen), with comic-strip storyboarding as prewriting
   and minilessons on punctuating dialogue and paragraphing dialogue —
   the direct model for this unit's story-map planning and Lesson 03's
   new-speaker-new-paragraph rule. Verified live 2026-11-06. Free, no
   account. Adult reference; the Comic Creator student interactive it
   links is optional and needs a device.
3. **Florida Center for Reading Research — Instructional Routines**
   `https://fcrr.org/reading-success/instructional-routines`
   *Why:* scripted routines matched to this unit's foundations — Third
   Grade "Comprehension: Identify Story Grammar" and "Retelling the
   Main Events of a Story in Sequence" (Lessons 01–02), and the
   vocabulary routines for shades of meaning (Lesson 04 word work).
   Verified live 2026-11-06. Free, no account. Adult reference.
4. **Common Core State Standards — English Language Arts, grade 3**
   `https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/`
   *Why:* **adult reference only** — the authoritative text of W.3.3.a–d,
   W.3.5, W.3.10, L.3.2.c, and L.3.3.a behind this unit's objectives
   and standards table. Opened and verified during the track audit,
   2026-10-03; W.3.3.a–d wording re-checked 2026-11-06; no state
   adoption or accreditation claimed. Free, no account.
5. **Library of Congress — classroom materials**
   `https://www.loc.gov/classroom-materials/`
   *Why:* the adult's route to real texts, images, and primary-source
   sets for story ideas and extension work (for example, a historic
   photograph as a story starter in the Lesson 01 application, or a
   Primary Source Set for the project when the learner wants a
   real-world spark). Verified live 2026-11-06: 210 online classroom
   materials, including 27 items for Grades 3–5. Free, no account.
   Adult reference; preview for age fit before showing anything to the
   learner.
