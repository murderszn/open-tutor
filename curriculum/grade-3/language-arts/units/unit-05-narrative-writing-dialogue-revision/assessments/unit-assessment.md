# Culminating Assessment — Unit 05: Narrative Writing

**Points:** 40 · **Time:** 40–50 minutes (may be split across two
sessions) · **Standards:** W.3.3.a–d, W.3.5, L.3.2.c, L.3.3.a
**How to use:** the adult reads directions aloud if needed. The learner
works independently. Score with the [answer key](../answer-key.md#culminating-assessment--unit-05)
the same day. This assessment is a unit check written for this unit; it
is not a standardized or validated assessment.

## Part A — Read and analyze (12 points, 2 points each)

Read the story. Then answer the questions.

### "The Lost Library Book" (original, written for this unit)

> On Tuesday after school, Theo carried a library book about volcanoes
> home in his backpack. He read it at the kitchen table, then left it
> on the couch while he went outside to play.
>
> Later, when it was time to return the book, Theo could not find it.
> He looked under his bed. He looked in the kitchen. "Oh no," he
> whispered. "It was due today!"
>
> Then his little sister June came into the room, holding something
> red behind her back. "Is this what you are looking for?" she asked.
> She had been reading the volcano book under the couch cushions.
>
> At last Theo laughed. "Thank you, June," he said. "Next time I will
> put the book back on the shelf myself."

**1.** What is the situation? Name the who, where, and when. (2 points)

**2.** List the events of the story in order, in four short phrases.
(2 points)

**3.** Which time words does the story use to signal event order? Name
two. (2 points)

**4.** Which line of dialogue shows Theo's feeling? What feeling does
it show, and what clue tells you? (2 points)

**5.** What is the closure of the story? Does it answer both closure
questions (what happened to the problem, and how does the character
feel)? Explain. (2 points)

**6.** Find one exact word in the story that does more work than a tired
word would. Quote it, name the tired word it replaces, and say why it
is better. (2 points)

## Part B — Write a closure (12 points)

Read the beginning and middle of a story. Then write its closure.

> On Friday morning, Ana planted a sunflower seed in a pot on her
> windowsill. First she patted the soil down gently. Then she watered
> it, just a little. Every day after school she checked the pot. On the
> seventh day, a tiny green shoot pushed through the soil. "It grew!"
> Ana shouted. "Look, Mama, it grew!"

**7.** Write a closure of two or three sentences for Ana's story
(12 points: 4 points for resolving the problem — what happens to the
sunflower; 4 points for saying how Ana feels, shown or named exactly;
4 points for word choice — at least two exact words, and no tired
"nice/good/happy" ending).

## Part C — Fix the dialogue (8 points, 2 points each)

Rewrite each exchange correctly: quotation marks, commas, capitals,
and a new paragraph each time the speaker changes.

**8.** ana said look a shoot mama said i see it

**9.** it grew shouted ana mama said i told you it would

**10.** will it be tall asked ana yes said mama taller than you

**11.** ana said thank you for helping me mama said you did it yourself

## Part D — Revise a weak paragraph (8 points)

Read the weak paragraph. Then revise it.

> Ana planted a seed. It was nice. The seed was small. Ana was happy.
> The plant grew. Ana went inside. It was a good day.

**12.** Rewrite the paragraph (four to six sentences) making at least
three ARMS changes: add one line of dialogue, add one time word, and
swap one tired word for an exact one. (8 points: 2 points for the
dialogue line, correctly punctuated; 2 points for the time word used
correctly; 2 points for the exact-word swap; 2 points for a closure
sentence that resolves the paragraph and says how Ana feels.)
