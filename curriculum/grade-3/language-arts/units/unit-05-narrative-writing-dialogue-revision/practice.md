# Practice — Unit 05: Narrative Writing: Dialogue and Revision

Six practice sets (A–F), one per lesson focus. The adult reads items
aloud as needed; the learner answers orally or in writing. All answers
are in the [answer key](answer-key.md). Sessions 25–30 minutes; a set
may be split across two sessions.

## Practice A — Situation and event order (Lesson 01)

1. Read the beginning. Does it answer Who, Where, When, and What? If
   one is missing, name it: "On Tuesday after school, Jamal took his
   new soccer ball to the field behind his house."
2. Write a beginning of two sentences for a story about a girl named
   Priya who finds a tiny frog in her garden. Answer Who, Where, When,
   and What.
3. Put these events in order. Number them 1–4: Priya picks up the frog
   gently. / Priya sees something small and green hop in the garden. /
   Priya puts the frog back near the pond. / Priya shows the frog to
   her grandmother.
4. Which of these is a problem that could drive a story? (a) "Priya's
   grandmother likes frogs." (b) "The frog hops away before Priya can
   show her grandmother." Explain your choice.
5. Use the "because" test: does this chain hold together? "Priya finds
   a frog. Priya eats lunch. Priya rides her bike. Priya shows the frog
   to her grandmother." Which event breaks the chain, and why?
6. Write the "what happens because of what" link for these two events:
   "The frog hops away" → "Priya is disappointed." Finish the
   sentence: "Priya is disappointed *because* ___."
7. Look at the [kite illustration](assets/kite-in-the-tree-illustration.png).
   Name the characters you can see, the setting, and one thing that
   must have happened before this picture.
8. Error analysis: Kim's beginning is: "A frog. A garden. It was
   morning." What is missing? Rewrite it as two sentences that answer
   all four questions.

## Practice B — Temporal words (Lesson 02)

1. Fill each gap with a different time word from the box. Box: *first /
   next / suddenly / finally*. "___ Priya stepped into the garden.
   ___ she saw the frog. ___ it hopped away! ___ she found it again
   near the pond."
2. Which time word is wrong, and what should replace it? "Finally,
   Priya woke up. Then she ate breakfast. First, she found the frog."
3. Choose the best time word: "Priya waited quietly. ___, the frog
   hopped onto her shoe." (a) First (b) At last (c) Suddenly
4. Write two sentences about Priya showing the frog to her grandmother.
   Use *after that* in one sentence and *later* in the other.
5. The events are in the wrong order. Rewrite them in order with time
   words: "Priya put the frog back. Priya found the frog. Priya told
   her grandmother."
6. Circle the sentence where the time word does not match its place:
   (a) "First, Priya found the frog." (b) "Later, she showed her
   grandmother." (c) "Finally, she woke up." (d) "At last, she put
   the frog back near the pond."
7. Write a four-sentence retelling of the kite story's middle (the wind,
   the tangle, Maya reaching, Leo arriving). Use a different time word
   in each sentence.
8. Error analysis: Alex wrote, "Then Priya woke up. Then she found the
   frog. Then she showed her grandmother. Then she put it back." Name
   two things Alex should change.

## Practice C — Dialogue (Lesson 03)

1. Punctuate this dialogue correctly: maya said look a frog priya
   whispered its tiny
2. Start a new paragraph each time the speaker changes. Rewrite this
   block with correct paragraphs and punctuation: "Can I hold it?" asked
   Priya. "Gently," said Grandmother. "Frogs are fragile."
3. Which line of dialogue does a job (shows a feeling or moves the
   story), and which is decoration? (a) "Hi," said Priya. "Hi," said
   Grandmother. (b) "Wait!" cried Priya. "Don't touch it yet — it
   might hop away!"
4. Write one line of dialogue for Priya that shows she is *surprised*.
   Use a said word that is not *said*.
5. Write one line of dialogue for Grandmother that moves the story:
   she tells Priya what to do next about the frog.
6. Replace the weak said words with exact ones: (a) "Look!" said Priya
   loudly. (b) "Shh," said Grandmother quietly.
7. Read this exchange. Who is speaking in each line, and how do you
   know?
   > "It's hopping toward the pond!" Priya shouted.
   >
   > "Let it go," Grandmother said. "It belongs near the water."
8. Error analysis: Sam wrote: "I found a frog" said Priya "It is green"
   said Grandmother. Name three things wrong, and rewrite it correctly.

## Practice D — Actions, thoughts, and feelings (Lesson 04)

1. Rewrite this telling sentence as a showing action: "Priya was
   excited about the frog."
2. Rewrite this telling sentence as a thought in Priya's own words:
   "Priya was worried the frog would hop away."
3. Order these feeling words from weakest to strongest: *scared,
   terrified, worried.* Then say which one fits Priya finding a frog in
   her garden, and why the strongest one does not fit.
4. Which sentence shows a feeling, and which only names it?
   (a) "Grandmother smiled and clapped her hands." (b) "Grandmother
   was happy." Explain how you can tell.
5. Write one thought for Grandmother when she sees the frog, in her own
   words. Remember: no quotation marks for thoughts.
6. Choose the exact feeling word: "When the frog hopped onto Priya's
   shoe, she felt ___." (a) furious (b) thrilled (c) exhausted. Why is
   your choice exact?
7. Show, don't tell: rewrite "Maya was nervous" as one sentence about
   what Maya's hands or voice do.
8. Error analysis: Kim wrote, "Priya thought, 'I hope the frog stays.'"
   Then she wrote, "Priya was hopeful." Which sentence shows, and which
   tells? Rewrite the telling sentence so it shows Priya's hope with an
   action.

## Practice E — Closures and exact words (Lesson 05)

1. Which ending is a closure, and which is a stop sign? (a) "Priya put
   the frog back. The end." (b) "At last Priya set the frog down near
   the pond, and it hopped away into the grass. She watched it go,
   glad she had seen it up close." Explain with the two closure
   questions.
2. Write a two-sentence closure for Priya's frog story. Resolve the
   problem and say how Priya feels.
3. Swap the tired words for exact ones: "Priya ___ (went) to the pond.
   The frog ___ (went) into the grass. She ___ (said) softly,
   'Goodbye, little frog.'"
4. Choose the exact word: "When Priya saw the frog hop away safely, she
   felt ___." (a) okay (b) relieved (c) angry. Why?
5. Read the ending. Does it pass the two closure questions? If not, fix
   it: "Priya went inside. Grandmother made lunch. The frog was gone."
6. Replace the tired words with exact ones: "The frog was very ___
   (big) for a frog. Priya ___ (got) a leaf to hold it. Grandmother
   ___ (said) a kind word."
7. Write a closing sentence for the kite story that shows what Maya
   learned — without using "learned," "lesson," or "moral."
8. Error analysis: Alex ended his story, "And then they went home and
   it was nice and they were happy. The end." Name two problems and
   rewrite it as a closure in two sentences.

## Practice F — Revision (Lesson 06)

Use the weak draft from Lesson 06 ("Maya went to the park. It was nice.
She had a kite. ...") for items 1–6. The draft is repeated in the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) for the adult's convenience.

1. **Add:** add one line of dialogue to the weak draft when Leo arrives.
   Punctuate it correctly.
2. **Add:** add two time words to the weak draft so the order is clear.
   Write the two sentences with your additions.
3. **Remove:** remove one sentence from the weak draft that does not
   belong to the story. Which sentence, and why?
4. **Move:** one event in the weak draft is in the wrong place. Which
   event, and where should it move?
5. **Swap:** swap three tired words in the weak draft for exact ones.
   List each old word and its replacement.
6. **Edit:** fix the spelling, capitals, and punctuation in this
   sentence from a learner's draft: "maya and leo pulled the string
   slowley and the kite finaly came down"
7. Give Praise–Question–Polish feedback for this draft ending: "Maya
   got the kite. Leo went home. It was good." One praise, one question,
   one polish suggestion.
8. Error analysis: Kim revised her story by fixing spelling first, then
   discovered she needed to delete a whole paragraph. What did she do
   wrong, and what is the right order of the three passes?
