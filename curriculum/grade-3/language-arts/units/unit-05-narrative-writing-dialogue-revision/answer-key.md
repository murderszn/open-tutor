# Answer Key — Unit 05: Narrative Writing: Dialogue and Revision (ADULT ONLY)

Do not hand this file to the learner. Every item in the lessons,
practice sets, quiz, and unit assessment is solved here. Open responses
accept any wording that carries the same meaning, the same evidence, and
the same craft move; the samples below show the meaning and the move
required. For dialogue and revision items, both the punctuation and the
job the dialogue does count.

## Lesson 01 — Establish a situation and introduce characters

1. Characters: Maya and her dog Biscuit. Where: the park. When:
   Saturday morning.
2. Sample: "On Saturday morning, Maya takes her new red kite to the
   park with her dog Biscuit, and the wind is already tugging at it."
   Accept any one-sentence version naming Maya, Biscuit, the park,
   Saturday morning, and the kite.
3. (b) is better. It answers all four questions: Who (Maya and
   Biscuit), Where (the park), When (Saturday morning), What (carrying
   her new red kite). (a) names only Maya and the park and one fact
   about the kite — no when, and no action.
4. In order: (1) Maya runs and the kite lifts. (2) The wind yanks the
   string. (3) The kite tangles in the oak tree. (4) Maya reaches for
   the kite. Because-test: the kite lifts *because* Maya runs; it
   tangles *because* the wind yanks it into the tree; Maya reaches
   *because* the kite is stuck.
5. (a) is a problem: something has gone wrong that the story must
   resolve. (b) is only a fact about the setting — nothing to solve.
6. Before the picture: Maya was flying the kite and the wind pulled it
   into the tree (any plausible version). After the picture: Maya — and
   Leo, who arrives — try to get the kite down, and she gets it back
   (any plausible version).
7. Sample: "On Tuesday after school, Theo carried a library book about
   volcanoes home in his backpack. He read it at the kitchen table."
   Who (Theo), Where (home / the kitchen), When (Tuesday after
   school), What (carrying and reading the library book).
8. In order: (1) Theo leaves the book on the couch and goes outside to
   play. (2) When it is time to return the book, he cannot find it.
   (3) His little sister June shows him she was reading it under the
   couch cushions. Accept any three events in this order.
9. Missing: Who (no character is named), Where (no place), and What (no
   action — "Stuff happened" says nothing). Rewrite sample: "On
   Tuesday after school, Theo carried his library book home. He read
   it at the kitchen table while his little sister played nearby."

## Lesson 02 — Temporal words and event order

1. In order: *First* (the beginning), *Then* (the next event),
   *Suddenly* (the wind — fast and surprising), *After that* (the
   tangle follows the dive). Each one tells the reader the order;
   *Suddenly* also tells the reader the wind was a surprise.
2. Sample: "First, Maya reached up for the kite. Next, Leo came along
   the path. Finally, the kite floated down." Accept any three
   different time words in the right places.
3. (b) *At last* — Maya waited, so the release comes after a wait, at
   the end of the waiting. (a) *Suddenly* is wrong: nothing sudden
   happens after a wait. (c) *First* is wrong: this is not the first
   event.
4. Fixed: "First, Maya bought the kite. Then she flew it in the park.
   Finally, it tangled in the tree." (The events were right; only the
   time words were swapped.)
5. In order, with time words: "First, the kite tangles. Then Leo
   arrives. After that, Maya pulls the string gently. Finally, the
   kite floats down." Accept any correct order with matching time
   words.
6. Two things: (1) the first sentence must not start with "Then" — the
   first event needs "First" (or another beginning word); (2) every
   sentence starts with "Then" — vary the time words.
7. Sample: "First, Maya held the string while Leo watched the
   branches. Next, the wind dropped a little. Suddenly, the tail
   slipped off the branch. Finally, the kite floated down into Maya's
   hands." The four time words appear in the required order: first,
   next, suddenly, finally.
8. The wrong time word is "Suddenly": deciding to go to the park is not
   sudden, and the decision must come *first*, not last. Corrected:
   "First, Maya and Leo decided to go to the park. Then they tied the
   kite string to the bench. Later, they untangled the tail."
9. In order: *While* Maya held the string, Leo watched the branches.
   *After that* the wind dropped. *At last* Maya pulled gently. *Then*
   the kite came free. (*While* fits because two things happen at
   once; *After that* and *At last* mark the waiting; *Then* marks the
   result.)

## Lesson 03 — Dialogue that develops the story

1. "Oh no," she whispered. "My new kite! It was my birthday kite!"
   Clues: the said word *whispered* (a quiet, worried voice), the
   words "Oh no," and the detail that it was her birthday kite — this
   kite matters to her.
2. Leo's plan: "Don't worry," he said. "We can get it down. If we pull
   the string slowly, the tail might slip off the branch." What happens
   next: Maya and Leo pull the string slowly — the plan becomes the
   next event.
3. Corrected, two paragraphs:
   > "What happened?" asked Leo.
   >
   > "The wind took it," said Maya.
   (Quotation marks, capital first words, commas inside the closing
   quotes, new paragraph for the new speaker.)
4. (b) does a job: it shows Leo's urgency (the said word *cried* and
   the word "Wait!") and it moves the story — the warning changes what
   Maya does next. (a) is decoration: greetings that change nothing.
5. Sample: "Look, Biscuit — the wind is dropping!" Maya cried. Accept
   any line showing hope with a said word other than *said*.
6. Sam's line announces the feeling ("I am worried") instead of
   showing it, and the second sentence explains the worry in a flat
   summary. Rewrite sample: "Oh no," Maya whispered. "My birthday
   kite!" (The worry shows in the words, the voice, and the detail.)
7. Sample: "We got it!" Leo cheered. "The kite is down!" Maya laughed.
   "We did it!" Leo shouted. (Said words and spoken words changed;
   punctuation rules kept.)
8. Sample:
   > "Is that your kite up in the tree?" Ms. Alvarez asked.
   >
   > "Yes," said Maya. "The wind took it."
   (Two paragraphs, correct punctuation; any sensible question and
   answer accepted.)
9. Corrected, two paragraphs:
   > Maya said, "I can reach it if I stand on the bench."
   >
   > "No, the bench is too wobbly," said Leo.
   Note for the adult: Leo is right — standing on a bench to reach is
   not safe. If the learner asks, say so: the story's solution is
   pulling the string, not climbing.

## Lesson 04 — Actions, thoughts, and feelings

1. Action: "Maya jumped and grabbed at the lowest branch" (or "Her
   shoulders sank"). Thought: "*What if it never comes down?*" (or
   "*Leo always has a plan.*"). Biscuit's action: "Biscuit barked and
   ran in circles around the tree."
2. Sample: "Leo ran to the tree, waving his arms and grinning." Accept
   any action that shows excitement and can be drawn.
3. Sample: "Maybe Leo will know what to do." Accept any thought in
   Maya's own words showing hope.
4. (c) *thrilled* is the best answer: the kite floated down safely after
   real worry, a big happy moment. (b) *glad* is also true but weaker
   — less exact. (a) *terrified* is wrong: nothing dangerous happened.
5. (b) shows a feeling; (a) only names it. "Maya laughed and spun in a
   circle" is an action you can draw; "Maya was happy" names the
   feeling with no picture.
6. Two problems: (1) thoughts do not go in quotation marks — quotation
   marks mean spoken aloud; (2) the thought announces the feeling
   ("I am worried") instead of showing it in her own words. Rewrite
   sample: Maya thought, *What if it never comes down?*
7. Sample: "Ms. Alvarez clapped and smiled from the bench." Accept any
   action showing pride.
8. Weakest to strongest: *mad → angry → furious*. Sample: "Maya was
   furious when she thought the kite would fly away forever and never
   come back." Why it fits: she believed something precious was lost
   for good — the strongest word matches a fear of permanent loss.
9. Sample: Leo thought, *Maybe if we pull the string slowly, the tail
   will slip free.* (A thought, not in quotation marks, in his own
   words.)

## Lesson 05 — Closure and word choice for effect

1. Problem resolved: the tail slips off the branch and the kite floats
   down into Maya's hands. Maya feels relieved and proud — shown by
   hugging the kite tight, laughing, and planning to check the wind
   next time.
2. (b) is a closure: it resolves the problem (the kite floats down into
   her hands) and says how Maya feels (she hugs it tight; she knows
   she will check the wind next time — wiser, relieved). (a) is a stop
   sign: "The kite came down" states the fact but not the feeling, and
   "The end" is a label.
3. Sample: "Maya *raced* to the tree. She *grabbed* the string. Leo
   *cheered*, 'Pull gently!'" Why: *raced* shows hurry; *grabbed*
   shows the action; *cheered* shows excitement. Accept any exact
   swaps with fitting reasons.
4. (b) *relieved* — the worry is over and the kite is safe; the exact
   shade for worry ending well. (a) *okay* is vague; (c) *furious* is
   the wrong feeling entirely.
5. Sample: "At last Theo found the book under the couch cushions.
   He hugged it to his chest, relieved, and promised himself he would
   put it on the shelf next time." (Resolves the problem — the book is
   found — *and* says how Theo feels: relieved, shown by hugging the
   book.) Accept any two-sentence closure answering both questions.
6. Two problems: (1) it does not resolve the problem or say how the
   characters feel — "went home," "nice day," "happy" is a summary,
   not a closure; (2) tired words (*nice, happy*) and "The end" as a
   label. Rewrite sample: "At last the kite floated down into Maya's
   hands. She hugged it tight, and Leo cheered — they had done it
   together."
7. Sample: "Maya put the kite away carefully, glad it was safe. Leo
   went home, and Biscuit slept under the tree, tired after his big
   day." (Fails the feeling question as written — "put the kite away /
   went home / slept" says nothing about how anyone feels; the fix
   adds *glad* and *tired*.)
8. Sample: "The dog *dashed* after the kite. Maya *shouted*, 'Look!'
   The kite was *huge* in the sky." (Any exact swaps accepted:
   *dashed/raced* for *went*, *shouted/cried* for *said*,
   *huge/towering* for *big*.)
9. Sample: "From then on, Maya checked the wind before she flew her
   kite." Accept any sentence showing changed behavior without
   "learned," "lesson," or "moral."

## Lesson 06 — Plan, revise, and edit

1. In order: **Plan** (map the story before drafting), **Revise**
   (ARMS — change what the story says and how it says it), **Edit**
   (fix spelling, capitals, and punctuation last).
2. (a) **Add** — add a showing action or thought. (b) **Remove** — the
   breakfast sentence does not belong. (c) **Move** — the tangling
   event must come after the flying event. (d) **Swap** — replace
   repeated "said" with exact said words.
3. Sample: Praise — "Your sentence tells the whole story in one breath
   — I can follow it." Question — "How did Maya feel when the kite
   got stuck?" Polish — "Add one line of dialogue when Leo arrives so
   I can hear what he says." Accept any one praise, one question, one
   polish suggestion.
4. Sample: "At last the kite floated down into Maya's hands. She
   hugged it tight, and Leo cheered — they had done it together."
   (Resolves the problem *and* says how the characters feel.)
5. Edited: Maya said, "Let's pull the string slowly." "OK," said Leo.
   (Capitals on *Maya* and *Leo*; comma after *said*; quotation marks
   around the exact words; period inside the closing quote; "OK"
   capitalized.) Accept the tag-first form as written.
6. Sam edited before revising: he polished sentences he then had to
   rewrite. Next time: revise first (ARMS), edit last.
7. Sample: "What happened?" asked Leo. (New paragraph, correctly
   punctuated.) Accept any dialogue line at Leo's arrival, correctly
   punctuated.
8. Remove "It was nice." It tells the reader nothing about *this*
   story — it could belong to any story.
9. Sample swaps: *went* → *raced*; *got* → *pulled*; *was sad* →
   *her shoulders sank* (showing instead of naming). Accept any three
   exact swaps with reasons.

3. In order: (1) Priya sees something small and green hop in the
   garden. (2) Priya picks up the frog gently. (3) Priya shows the
   frog to her grandmother. (4) Priya puts the frog back near the pond.

   could show her grandmother."
7. Characters: a girl (Maya) and her dog (Biscuit). Setting: a park
   with a big oak tree, a bench, and a winding path. Before the
   picture: Maya was flying the red kite and the wind pulled it into
   the tree.

## Practice B — Temporal words

1. "First, Priya stepped into the garden. Next, she saw the frog.
   Suddenly, it hopped away! Finally, she found it again near the
   pond." (Accept *Then* for *Next*.)
2. "Finally" is wrong for the first event — waking up is first. Fixed:
   "First, Priya woke up. Then she ate breakfast. Finally, she found
   the frog." (The events were right; the time words were swapped.)
3. (b) *At last* — Priya waited quietly, so the hop comes after a
   wait. (a) *First* is wrong: this is not the first event.
   (c) *Suddenly* is acceptable for surprise, but *At last* is exact
   here because the sentence emphasizes waiting.
4. Sample: "After that, Priya carried the frog to her grandmother.
   Later, they put it back near the pond."
5. In order: "First, Priya found the frog. Then she told her
   grandmother. Finally, she put the frog back."
6. (c) "Finally, she woke up." Waking up is first, not last.
7. Sample: "First, the wind yanked the string and the kite dived into
   the oak. Then Maya reached up but could not touch it. Suddenly, Leo
   came running along the path. Finally, Maya pulled the string and the
   kite floated down."
8. Two things: (1) the first sentence must not start with "Then" — the
   first event needs "First"; (2) every sentence starts with "Then" —
   vary the time words.

## Culminating assessment — Unit 05

### Part A — Read and analyze (12 points)

1. Situation: Who — Theo and his little sister June. Where — home (the
   kitchen, the couch). When — Tuesday after school. What — Theo
   carries a library book about volcanoes home and reads it. (2
   points: 1 for who/where/when, 1 for what.)
2. In order, four short phrases: (1) Theo brings the volcano book home
   and reads it at the kitchen table. (2) He leaves it on the couch
   and goes outside to play. (3) When it is due, he cannot find it
   and looks under the bed and in the kitchen. (4) June shows she was
   reading it under the couch cushions. (Accept four or five phrases
   covering the same events; the thank-you belongs to the closure.)
3. Any two of: *Later*, *Then*, *At last*. (2 points: 1 per word.)
4. "Oh no," he whispered. "It was due today!" It shows worry (panic
   about the due book). Clues: the said word *whispered* (a quiet,
   worried voice) and the words "Oh no" and "It was due today!"
5. The closure is: "At last Theo laughed. 'Thank you, June,' he said.
   'Next time I will put the book back on the shelf myself.'" Yes to
   both questions: the problem is resolved (the book is found — the
   story's problem was the lost book, and finding it resolves it) and
   Theo's feeling is shown (he *laughed* — relieved and happy) with
   what he learned (he will shelve the book himself next time).
6. Sample: *whispered* replaces *said* — it shows Theo is worried and
   speaking quietly, which plain *said* does not. Also accept *carried*
   (for *took* — shows careful carrying) or *laughed* (for *was
   happy* — shows relief). (2 points: 1 for the word, 1 for the
   reason.)

### Part B — Write a closure (12 points)

7. Sample: "Soon the shoot grew taller, and a yellow flower opened at
   the top. Ana beamed, proud that the tiny seed she had planted had
   become a sunflower." Scoring: 4 points for resolving the problem
   (the sunflower grows and flowers); 4 points for saying how Ana
   feels, shown or named exactly (*beamed, proud*); 4 points for word
   choice (at least two exact words — *beamed, proud* — and no tired
   "nice/good/happy" ending). Accept any closure meeting the three
   criteria.

### Part C — Fix the dialogue (8 points, 2 each)

8. Ana said, "Look, a shoot!" / "I see it," said Mama.
9. "It grew!" shouted Ana. / "I told you it would," said Mama.
10. "Will it be tall?" asked Ana. / "Yes," said Mama. "Taller than

### Part D — Revise a weak paragraph (8 points)

12. Sample rewrite: "First, Ana planted a tiny seed in the pot. Then
    she watered it, just a little. 'Grow, little seed,' she whispered.
    At last a green shoot pushed through the soil, and Ana cheered."
    Scoring: 2 points for the dialogue line, correctly punctuated
    ("Grow, little seed," she whispered.); 2 points for the time word
    used correctly (*First / Then / At last*); 2 points for the
    exact-word swap (*whispered* for *said*; *pushed* for *came* or
    *grew*); 2 points for a closure sentence (the shoot pushing through
    and Ana cheering — resolves the paragraph and shows how Ana feels).
    Accept any rewrite meeting the four criteria.
