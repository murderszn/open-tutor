# Lesson 02 — Temporal Words and Event Order

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can join the events of a story with time
words, so a reader always knows what happened first, next, and last."
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** paper and pencil; the event
cards from Lesson 01 (or five slips of paper with the kite-story events).
**Standards:** W.3.3.c.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult asks: "Tell me how you get ready in the morning — but you may
not use the words *first*, *next*, *then*, or *after*." The learner
tries, and it comes out muddled. "Time words are the glue. Without them,
the reader cannot tell the order. Today we collect the glue."

## Explicit teaching

**Temporal words and phrases** are words that signal event order — they
tell the reader *when* something happened in relation to something else.
The most useful ones for grade-3 stories:

- **Beginning:** *first, at first, in the morning, one Saturday, to
  start*
- **Middle:** *next, then, after that, later, soon, while, suddenly,
  all at once*
- **End:** *finally, at last, in the end, after a while*

Three rules for using them well:

1. **One time word per step is enough.** "First Maya ran. Next the
   kite lifted. Then the wind yanked the string." If every sentence
   starts with *then*, the glue stops working — vary it.
2. **The word must match the time.** *Suddenly* is for something fast
   and surprising; *later* is for something that happens after a wait;
   *finally* is only for the last thing. "Finally Maya woke up" is
   wrong if the story has just begun.
3. **A time word does not fix a wrong order.** "First the kite tangled,
   then Maya flew it" is still backwards. Check the order with the
   "because" test from Lesson 01, *then* add the time words.

## Model story — "The Kite in the Tree," Part 2 (original, written for this unit)

*Adult reads aloud; the learner follows along.*

> First, Maya ran across the grass, and the red kite lifted into the
> sky. Then Biscuit barked and chased after it. Suddenly, a gust of
> wind yanked the string, and the kite dived toward the big oak tree.
> After that, the kite's tail caught on a branch, and the red kite
> hung there, tangled and still.

### Modeled example 1 — Time words carry the order

*Adult thinks aloud:* "Four sentences, four time words: *First, Then,
Suddenly, After that*. Cover the time words and read again: 'Maya ran.
Biscuit barked. A gust of wind yanked the string. The kite's tail
caught on a branch.' The order is still there, because the events
follow each other — but the reader has to work harder. The time words
do the pointing for the reader. And *Suddenly* is doing double work: it
tells you the order *and* that the wind was a surprise."

### Modeled example 2 — The wrong time word breaks the meaning

*Adult writes:* "Finally, Maya woke up on Saturday morning. First, she
carried her kite to the park."

"Read it: *finally* says 'this is the last thing,' but waking up is the
first thing. And *first* says 'this is the first thing,' but carrying
the kite comes after waking up. Swap the words and both sentences are
true: 'First, Maya woke up on Saturday morning. Then she carried her
kite to the park.' A time word has to match where its event sits in the
story."

## Guided practice

The adult reads each item aloud; the learner answers orally or in writing.
Answers are in the [answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-02--temporal-words-and-event-order).

1. In Part 2, list the four time words or phrases in the order they
   appear, and say what each one tells the reader.
2. Add a time word to each sentence so the order is clear. Use a
   different time word each time: "___ Maya reached up for the kite."
   "___ Leo came along the path." "___ the kite floated down."
3. Which time word fits best, and why? "Maya waited for the wind to
   drop. ___, the tail slipped off the branch." (a) Suddenly (b) At
   last (c) First
4. Fix the time words (do not change the events): "Finally, Maya bought
   the kite. Then she flew it in the park. First, it tangled in the
   tree."
5. The adult reads this scrambled sequence. Put the events in order and
   join them with time words: the kite floats down / Leo arrives /
   the kite tangles / Maya pulls the string gently.
6. Error analysis: June wrote, "Then Maya woke up. Then she went to the
   park. Then she flew the kite. Then the kite tangled." What two things
   should June change? (Hint: one is about the first word, one is about
   variety.)

## Independent practice

7. Write four sentences that tell, in order, how Maya gets her kite
   back. Use a different time word in each sentence: *first, next,
   suddenly,* and *finally* — in that order.
8. Read this paragraph and circle the time word that does not fit. Then
   write the sentence correctly: "Maya and Leo tied the kite string to
   the bench. Later, they untangled the tail. Suddenly, they decided to
   go to the park."
9. Choose the best time word for each gap, from the box: *while /
   after that / at last / then*. "___ Maya held the string, Leo watched
   the branches. ___ the wind dropped. ___ Maya pulled gently. ___ the
   kite came free."

## Application

Retell the whole kite story so far — beginning (Lesson 01) plus Part 2 —
in five sentences, using at least three different time words. Read it
aloud to the adult, who checks: does every event follow from the one
before it, and does every time word match its place?

## Exit check

Name three time words for the beginning of a story, three for the
middle, and two for the end. Then explain, in your own words, why
"Finally, Maya woke up" is wrong at the start of a story.

## Supports and extension

- **Support:** keep a time-word card on the table (Beginning: first,
  at first, one Saturday; Middle: next, then, after that, later,
  suddenly; End: finally, at last, in the end). The learner points to
  a word before using it.
- **Alternate response mode:** act out the five kite events in order
  with the event cards, saying a time word before placing each card;
  or draw the events as four quick pictures in boxes labeled with time
  words.
- **Extension:** rewrite Part 2 with no time words at all, then argue
  with the adult about which version a reader follows more easily —
  and why the time-word version wins.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: (1) *then* at the start of every sentence —
praise the order, then ask for one different word per sentence. (2) A
time word can fix any order — always run the "because" test first;
*time words label order, they do not create it.* (3) *Finally* means
"a long time later" — no: it means "last of all," whatever the clock
says. See the [teacher guide](../teacher-guide.md#lesson-02--temporal-words-and-event-order)
and [answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-02--temporal-words-and-event-order).
