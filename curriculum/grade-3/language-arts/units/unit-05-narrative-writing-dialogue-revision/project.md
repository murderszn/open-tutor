# Project — "My Own Story"

**Type:** narrative writing project (Sessions 3, 9, 11–13, with drafting
and revision between sessions)
**Standards:** W.3.3.a–d; W.3.5; W.3.10; L.3.2.c; L.3.3.a; SL.3.4 (read
aloud at an understandable pace).

## The task

Write your own complete story — real or imagined — in three to five
paragraphs. It must have a beginning that sets the scene, events in an
order a reader can follow, at least one line of dialogue that shows a
feeling or moves the story, descriptions of actions, thoughts, or
feelings, time words that signal the order, and an ending that resolves
the problem and says how the main character feels. Then revise it with
the ARMS checklist, edit it, and read it aloud.

Your story may start from the [kite
illustration](assets/kite-in-the-tree-illustration.png) — what happens
next, or what happened before the picture — or from one of these
starters, or from your own idea:

- "On Saturday morning, I found a small door in the garden wall that I
  had never seen before."
- "The lost dog followed me home from the park, and I had to decide
  what to do."
- "My grandmother's old radio started playing a song nobody had heard
  in years."

## Materials

- Paper and pencil (or a device the adult supervises, if the learner
  types — W.3.6)
- The story map below (copy it onto paper)
- The kite illustration as a model for what a story picture shows
- Word-bank cards: time words (Lesson 02), said words (Lesson 03),
  feeling words and shades (Lesson 04), exact words (Lesson 05)
- The ARMS revision checklist (Lesson 06)
- A beginning dictionary (print) for editing

## Steps

1. **Choose and plan (Session 3).** Pick your story idea. Fill in the
   story map with single words or short phrases — never whole
   sentences:
   - Who (two characters):
   - Where and when:
   - What happens first:
   - What goes wrong (the problem):
   - What happens next (two events, in order):
   - How it ends (resolution + feeling):
2. **Check the plan (end of Session 3, with the adult).** The adult asks
   the four beginning questions (Who? Where? When? What?) and runs the
   "because" test on the events. Fix the plan before drafting — a
   fixed plan is cheaper than a rewritten draft.
3. **Draft (Session 9).** Write the first draft from the map: one
   paragraph for the beginning, one or two for the middle (events,
   dialogue, descriptions), one for the ending. Do not stop to fix
   spelling while drafting; keep going. A draft may be dictated while
   the adult scribes, with the learner copying one paragraph
   themselves.
4. **Revise (Sessions 11–12).** Do one ARMS pass: at least one Add, one
   Remove, one Move, and one Swap. Mark each change with a colored
   pen. Then get Praise–Question–Polish feedback from the adult (and a
   peer, if one is available in the setting) and decide which
   suggestions to take — you may refuse one with a reason.
5. **Edit (Session 12, second half).** Read the draft aloud slowly.
   Fix dialogue punctuation (Unit 04 rules), capitals, and spelling;
   check any unsure word in the beginning dictionary.
6. **Publish and share (Session 13).** Make a final copy with a title
   (capitalized per Unit 04 rules) and, if the learner wishes, a small
   drawing of one scene with a caption. Read the story aloud to the
   adult at an understandable pace. Answer one listener question about
   the story.

## Deliverables

- A completed story map (words or short phrases)
- A first draft with visible ARMS revision marks
- A final copy: title, three to five paragraphs, at least one dialogue
  exchange, at least three time words, a closure
- A read-aloud in the share circle (Session 13)

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion (8 points each) | 8 — Strong | 6 — Good | 4 — Developing | 2 — Beginning | 0 — Missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Situation and characters (W.3.3.a)** | Beginning answers Who, Where, When, and What in the first few sentences; characters are introduced clearly | Answers three of the four questions clearly | Answers two questions; reader must guess at the rest | One question answered; beginning is mostly a list of facts | No clear situation or characters |
| **Event sequence and time words (W.3.3.a, W.3.3.c)** | Three or more events in an order that unfolds naturally; three or more different time words used correctly | Events in order; two time words used correctly | Events mostly in order; one time word, or time words repeat | Events out of order, or no time words | No clear sequence of events |
| **Dialogue and descriptions (W.3.3.b)** | At least one dialogue exchange that shows a feeling or moves the story, plus an action and a thought that show character | Dialogue present and doing a job; one showing description | Dialogue present but decorative, or descriptions only name feelings | Dialogue attempted but unpunctuated or unclear who speaks | No dialogue and no descriptions of actions, thoughts, or feelings |
| **Closure and word choice (W.3.3.d, L.3.3.a)** | Ending resolves the problem and says how the character feels; three or more exact words chosen for effect | Ending resolves the problem; two exact words | Ending is a stop sign ("The end") but the problem is resolved; one exact word | Ending does not resolve the problem | No ending |
| **Revision, editing, and read-aloud (W.3.5, L.3.2.c, SL.3.4)** | Four ARMS changes visible and marked; dialogue punctuation, capitals, and spelling checked; read aloud clearly at an understandable pace | Three ARMS changes; minor editing errors remain; read aloud clearly | Two ARMS changes; several editing errors; read aloud rushed or mumbled | One ARMS change; many editing errors; read aloud hard to follow | No revision marks; not read aloud |

**Scoring note for the adult:** score the *final copy* for criteria
1–4 and the *process evidence* (marked draft, read-aloud) for criterion
5. A dictated draft scribed by the adult is scored the same way — the
ideas are the learner's. 32–40 points: ready for Unit 06 (opinion
writing, which will reuse the revision routine). 24–31: one reteach
session on the lowest criterion, then move on. Below 24: revisit the
story map and re-draft the weakest paragraph with the adult before
Unit 06.
