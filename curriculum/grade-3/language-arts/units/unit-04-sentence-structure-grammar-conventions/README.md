# Unit 04 — Sentence Structure, Grammar, and Conventions

**Grade:** 3 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #20](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/20), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Words have jobs. In this unit the learner names each job — noun, pronoun,
verb, adjective, adverb — and then uses that knowledge to build better
sentences: plurals that follow the rule and plurals that break it, verbs
that tell past, present, and future, subjects and verbs that agree,
comparisons that choose correctly between two things and among three or
more, conjunctions that join ideas, and simple, compound, and complex
sentences that say exactly what the writer means. Then the learner turns
to conventions: capital letters in titles, commas in addresses and
dialogue, possessives, spelling patterns and suffix rules, and the
beginning dictionary. The unit ends in midyear-review week with a
sentence-building project, a formative quiz, reteaching, and a
culminating assessment — and with the learner speaking in complete
sentences to explain their own choices.

## Essential question

How do I build a sentence that says exactly what I mean — and fix one
that does not?

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–03 and the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–14):

- Read grade-level text aloud with accuracy and expression on repeated
  readings; self-correct with context (RF.3.4.a–c)
- Decode multisyllable words and use prefixes, suffixes, and root words
  to figure out word meanings (RF.3.3.a–c, L.3.4.b–c)
- Ask and answer who/what/where/when/why/how questions about a text
  (RI.3.1, RL.3.1); determine a main idea and supporting details (RI.3.2)
- Use a glossary or beginning dictionary to check a word's meaning
  (L.3.4.d)
- Write short pieces with a beginning, middle, and end, and revise with
  adult guidance (W.3.3, W.3.5, from Unit 02 writing routines)

Nothing in this unit assumes the learner has studied grammar terms
before. Lesson 01 re-teaches every part of speech from scratch: a noun
names, a pronoun takes a noun's place, a verb shows action or being, an
adjective describes a noun, an adverb tells how, when, or where about a
verb.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Explain the function of nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, and
   adverbs in general and in particular sentences (L.3.1.a).
2. Form and use regular and irregular plural nouns, abstract nouns,
   regular and irregular verbs, and simple verb tenses (past, present,
   future), and ensure subject–verb and pronoun–antecedent agreement
   (L.3.1.b–f).
3. Form and use comparative and superlative adjectives and adverbs,
   choosing between them depending on what is to be modified, and use
   coordinating and subordinating conjunctions (L.3.1.g–h).
4. Produce simple, compound, and complex sentences; choose words and
   phrases for effect; and recognize and observe differences between
   the conventions of spoken and written standard English
   (L.3.1.i, L.3.3.a–b).
5. Capitalize appropriate words in titles; use commas in addresses and
   in dialogue with quotation marks; form and use possessives; spell
   high-frequency and studied words and suffix additions conventionally;
   use spelling patterns and generalizations; and consult beginning
   dictionaries to check and correct spellings (L.3.2.a–g).
6. Speak in complete sentences when appropriate to the task and
   situation in order to provide requested detail or clarification
   (SL.3.6).

Standards wording below was re-verified 2026-10-30 against the Common
Core grade 3 Language standards (L.3.1.a–i, L.3.2.a–g, L.3.3.a–b) and
Speaking and Listening standard SL.3.6; see the
[Resource Pack](resource-pack.md) for the sources checked. This unit
does not claim state adoption, accreditation, or complete alignment.

- **L.3.1.a** — Explain the function of nouns, pronouns, verbs,
  adjectives, and adverbs in general and their functions in particular
  sentences.
- **L.3.1.b** — Form and use regular and irregular plural nouns.
- **L.3.1.c** — Use abstract nouns (e.g., *childhood*).
- **L.3.1.d** — Form and use regular and irregular verbs.
- **L.3.1.e** — Form and use the simple (e.g., *I walked; I walk;
  I will walk*) verb tenses.
- **L.3.1.f** — Ensure subject–verb and pronoun–antecedent agreement.
- **L.3.1.g** — Form and use comparative and superlative adjectives
  and adverbs, and choose between them depending on what is to be
  modified.
- **L.3.1.h** — Use coordinating and subordinating conjunctions.
- **L.3.1.i** — Produce simple, compound, and complex sentences.
- **L.3.2.a** — Capitalize appropriate words in titles.
- **L.3.2.b** — Use commas in addresses.
- **L.3.2.c** — Use commas and quotation marks in dialogue.
- **L.3.2.d** — Form and use possessives.
- **L.3.2.e** — Use conventional spelling for high-frequency and other
  studied words and for adding suffixes to base words (e.g., *sitting,
  smiled, cries, happiness*).
- **L.3.2.f** — Use spelling patterns and generalizations (e.g., word
  families, position-based spellings, syllable patterns, ending rules,
  meaningful word parts) in writing words.
- **L.3.2.g** — Consult reference materials, including beginning
  dictionaries, as needed to check and correct spellings.
- **L.3.3.a** — Choose words and phrases for effect.
- **L.3.3.b** — Recognize and observe differences between the
  conventions of spoken and written standard English.
- **SL.3.6** — Speak in complete sentences when appropriate to task
  and situation in order to provide requested detail or clarification.

## Unit sentences (all original, written for this unit)

Every example sentence in this unit was written for it. No published
textbook, story, or worksheet sentence is reproduced. The recurring
example sentences describe an ordinary park afternoon, so the learner
can focus on the grammar instead of new content:

- "Maya flies her red kite."
- "The happy dog runs quickly across the grass."
- "Sam and Lin carry the picnic basket."
- "The kite rose high because the wind blew hard."

## Differentiation and supports

- **Reading access:** the adult reads each lesson's teaching text aloud
  first; the learner follows along, then rereads. Any lesson may be
  split across two sessions.
- **Response modes:** the learner may answer orally, by pointing to a
  word card, by drawing an arrow, or in writing. Sorting word cards is
  an accepted alternative to writing every answer.
- **Language access:** grammar terms are always paired with a plain job
  description ("a noun names a person, place, thing, or idea") and an
  example before the term is used alone.
- **Extension:** the learner writes an extra sentence of each type,
  finds a grammar choice in a book they are reading, or edits a second
  paragraph for a classmate's (adult-provided, anonymous) practice
  draft.
- **What "ready to move on" looks like:** the learner can name a word's
  job, fix a plural, tense, or agreement error, and build one sentence
  of each type without adult prompting — even if spelling is still
  developing.
