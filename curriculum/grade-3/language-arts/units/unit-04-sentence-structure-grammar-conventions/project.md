# Project — "My Park Paragraph"

**Goal:** write, illustrate (optional drawing), revise, and read aloud
a short paragraph about a park afternoon, using everything in this
unit — then explain two of your own grammar choices in complete
sentences.
**Time:** Sessions 12–13 (two 25–30-minute sessions), plus sharing.
**Standards:** L.3.1.a–i, L.3.2.a–g, L.3.3.a–b, SL.3.6.

## Materials

- Paper, pencil, crayons or markers (optional, for the illustration)
- Word cards from Lessons 01–03 (optional)
- A beginning dictionary (for the spelling check in step 4)

## Steps

1. **Plan (Session 12, first 10 minutes).** Choose your park afternoon:
   who is there, what the weather is like, and one thing that happens.
   List five words you will use: one noun, one verb, one adjective,
   one adverb, and one conjunction.
2. **Draft (Session 12, rest of the session).** Write a paragraph of
   5–8 sentences. Your paragraph must include:
   - one simple sentence, one compound sentence, and one complex
     sentence (label each type in the margin);
   - one comparative or superlative;
   - one line of dialogue with correct commas and quotation marks;
   - one possessive;
   - one word spelled with a suffix rule from Lesson 06
     (doubling, dropped *e*, or *y → i*).
3. **Illustrate (optional).** Draw the scene, or plan where the
   generated park picture from Lesson 01 would fit. A drawing is not
   scored; the sentences are.
4. **Revise (Session 13, first 15 minutes).** Read your paragraph
   aloud. Check it against this list and fix at least two things:
   - Does every subject agree with its verb?
   - Is every plural spelled correctly?
   - Are titles (if any), addresses (if any), dialogue, and
     possessives punctuated correctly?
   - Did you check one spelling in the dictionary?
   Write one sentence saying what you changed and why.
5. **Share (Session 13, rest of the session).** Read your paragraph
   aloud in complete sentences. Then, in complete sentences, explain
   two choices: one sentence type you used and why, and one word you
   chose for effect.

## Deliverables

- A 5–8-sentence paragraph meeting all six requirements in step 2.
- Margin labels for the simple, compound, and complex sentences.
- One revision note (what changed and why).
- A read-aloud and two spoken explanations in complete sentences.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | 4 points (strong) | 2–3 points (developing) | 0–1 point (needs support) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sentence types (10 pts: see note) | All three types present, labeled, and punctuated correctly | Two types correct, or one label/punctuation error | One or no type correct |
| Grammar: agreement, plurals, tenses (8 pts) | No errors; irregular forms correct | 1–2 errors | 3+ errors |
| Conventions: capitals, commas, possessives, spelling (10 pts: see note) | No more than 1 error | 2–3 errors | 4+ errors |
| Dialogue and possessive (4 pts) | Both present and punctuated correctly | One present, or one punctuation error | Missing, or both incorrect |
| Word choice and revision (4 pts) | A precise word chosen for effect; revision note names a real change and a reason | Word choice or revision note is vague | No word choice or revision note |
| Read-aloud in complete sentences (4 pts) | Paragraph read aloud fluently; two explanations in complete sentences | Read aloud with help, or one explanation incomplete | Not read aloud, or explanations missing |

*Scoring notes:* Sentence types are scored 10 points (all three
correct = 10; two correct = 6; one correct = 3; none = 0). Conventions
are scored 10 points (0–1 errors = 10; 2–3 errors = 6; 4+ errors = 2).
All other rows are scored 0–4 (strong = 4, developing = 2–3, needs
support = 0–1) as shown. Total: 10 + 8 + 10 + 4 + 4 + 4 = 40.

**What "ready" looks like:** 32–40 points, with all three sentence
types present. Below 32, reteach the weakest row with the matching
lesson before Unit 05.
