# Lesson 01 — Parts of Speech: What Each Word Job Does

**Goal (student-readable):** I can name the job of a noun, pronoun,
verb, adjective, and adverb in a sentence.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** word cards (optional), the
park picture in [assets](../assets/park-sentence-scene.png), paper.
**Standards:** L.3.1.a.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

The adult says: "Tell me one word that names a person, one word that
shows an action, and one word that describes something." Any sensible
answers pass. If the learner is stuck, the adult offers: "person —
teacher; action — jump; describing word — happy."

## Explicit teaching

Every word in a sentence has a job. Here are the five jobs in this
lesson, in plain words first:

- **Noun — names.** A noun names a person, place, thing, or idea.
  *Maya, park, kite, friendship.*
- **Pronoun — takes a noun's place.** Instead of repeating a name, we
  use *she, he, it, they, we, I, her, him, them*.
- **Verb — shows action or being.** A verb tells what someone does or
  what something is. *flies, runs, is, was.*
- **Adjective — describes a noun.** It tells what kind, which one, or
  how many. *red, happy, tall, three.*
- **Adverb — tells how, when, or where about a verb.** It often — but
  not always — ends in *-ly*. *quickly, yesterday, here.*

A quick test: ask "What job is this word doing **in this sentence**?"
The same word can do different jobs in different sentences. In "The
brave girl climbs," *brave* describes the girl, so it is an adjective.
In "They brave the storm," *brave* is the action, so it is a verb.
Job first, label second.

## Worked example 1 — "Maya flies her red kite."

Think it through, one word at a time:

- *Maya* — names a person → **noun**.
- *flies* — the action Maya does → **verb**.
- *her* — takes Maya's place; it points back to Maya instead of
  repeating her name → **pronoun**.
- *red* — describes the kite; it tells what kind of kite → **adjective**.
- *kite* — names a thing → **noun**.

So the sentence has nouns (*Maya, kite*), a verb (*flies*), a pronoun
(*her*), and an adjective (*red*).

## Worked example 2 — "The happy dog runs quickly across the grass."

- *happy* — describes the dog → **adjective**.
- *dog* — names an animal (a thing) → **noun**.
- *runs* — the action → **verb**.
- *quickly* — tells **how** the dog runs → **adverb**.
- *grass* — names a thing → **noun**.

Common mix-up: *happy* and *quickly* both describe, but they describe
different things. *Happy* describes a noun (the dog). *Quickly*
describes a verb (runs). If it describes a noun, it is an adjective.
If it tells how, when, or where about a verb, it is an adverb.

## Guided practice (adult helps)

Name the job of each underlined word. Hints are in parentheses.

1. **The happy dog runs quickly.**
   *happy* (describes the dog) · *dog* (names an animal) ·
   *runs* (the action) · *quickly* (tells how it runs)
2. **She carries a red kite.**
   *She* (takes a name's place) · *carries* (the action) ·
   *red* (describes the kite) · *kite* (names a thing)
3. **Sam walks slowly to school.**
   *Sam* (names a person) · *walks* (the action) ·
   *slowly* (tells how he walks) · *school* (names a place)
4. **They sang a loud song.**
   *They* (takes names' place) · *sang* (the action) ·
   *loud* (describes the song) · *song* (names a thing)

## Independent practice

Name the job of each underlined word.

1. **The tall tree sways gently.**
   *tall* · *tree* · *sways* · *gently*
2. **He reads a funny book.**
   *He* · *reads* · *funny* · *book*
3. **Birds sing sweetly in the morning.**
   *Birds* · *sing* · *sweetly* · *morning*
4. **We found a smooth stone.**
   *We* · *found* · *smooth* · *stone*

## Applied task — picture sentences

Look at the park picture in
[assets](../assets/park-sentence-scene.png) (also described in words
below the picture in [assets/README.md](../assets/README.md)).
Write two sentences about what you see. In each sentence, underline
one noun and circle one verb. Then read one sentence aloud and name
one adjective or adverb in it.

*Text-only alternative:* a girl flies a red kite in a park; a brown
dog runs beside her; a green bench stands under a tall leafy tree; a
smiling sun shines in a blue sky with three white clouds.

## Exit check

Name the job of each underlined word: **The brave girl climbs
carefully.** (*brave* · *girl* · *climbs* · *carefully*)

## Supports and extension

- **Reading access:** the adult reads each sentence aloud first.
- **Alternate response mode:** sort word cards into five labeled piles
  (noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb) instead of writing labels.
- **Extension:** find one adjective and one adverb in a book you are
  reading and explain what each one describes.
- **Error-analysis note:** if the learner calls every describing word
  an adjective, return to the test: "Does it describe a noun, or does
  it tell how/when/where about a verb?"

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: *-ly* words are always adverbs. Counterexample:
*lovely* describes a noun ("a lovely day"), so it is an adjective.
Keep the job test, not the spelling test. Full solutions are in the
[answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-01).
