# Lesson 02 — Plurals, Abstract Nouns, Verbs, Tenses, and Agreement

**Goal (student-readable):** I can make plurals, name ideas I cannot
touch, put verbs in past, present, and future, and make subjects and
verbs agree.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** word cards (optional), paper.
**Standards:** L.3.1.b–f.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

The adult asks: "What is one dog? What are two dogs? What did you do
yesterday, and what will you do tomorrow?" The learner answers in
their own words. The adult notes whether the learner already changes
the verb for yesterday and tomorrow.

## Explicit teaching

### Plurals: more than one

- Most nouns just add **-s**: *kite → kites*.
- Nouns ending in *s, x, ch,* or *sh* add **-es**: *box → boxes,
  dish → dishes*.
- Nouns ending in a consonant + *y* change *y* to **-ies**:
  *story → stories, baby → babies*.
- Some plurals are **irregular** — they do not follow the rule and
  must be learned by heart: *child → children, foot → feet,
  tooth → teeth, mouse → mice, man → men, woman → women*.
- Some nouns look the same in singular and plural: *one sheep, two
  sheep; one deer, two deer*.

### Abstract nouns: ideas you cannot touch

A **concrete** noun names something you can see or touch (*kite,
rock*). An **abstract** noun names an idea or feeling you cannot
touch: *friendship, bravery, kindness, childhood, freedom, joy, fear*.
You can feel joy, but you cannot hold it in your hand.

### Verbs and simple tenses

- **Regular** verbs add **-ed** for the past: *walk → walked*.
- **Irregular** verbs change in their own way: *go → went,
  see → saw, eat → ate, run → ran, teach → taught, catch → caught*.
- **Simple tenses:** past (already happened: *I walked*), present
  (happening now or usually: *I walk*), future (will happen:
  *I will walk*).

### Agreement: the subject and verb must match

- One subject → a singular verb: *The dog runs. She has a kite.*
- More than one subject → a plural verb: *The dogs run. They have
  a kite.*
- A pronoun must match the noun it replaces: *Maya lost her kite*
  (not *their kite*, when Maya is one person); *Sam and Lin lost
  their kite* (two people → *their*).

## Worked example 1 — plurals, sorted

| Rule | One | More than one |
|---|---|---|
| Add -s | kite | kites |
| Add -es | box | boxes |
| y → ies | baby | babies |
| Irregular — learn it | child | children |
| Same form | sheep | sheep |

Reasoning out loud: "Box ends in *x*, so I add *-es*: boxes. Baby ends
in a consonant + *y*, so *y* becomes *ies*: babies. Child is
irregular — no rule gives me *children*, so I remember it."

## Worked example 2 — tense and agreement in one story

- Yesterday: "Yesterday Maya **walked** to the park." (past, *-ed*)
- Today: "Today she **walks** to the park." (present; one girl, so
  the verb ends in *-s*)
- Tomorrow: "Tomorrow she **will walk** to the park." (future,
  *will* + base verb)

Now agreement: "The dog **runs**" (one dog → *runs*), but "The dogs
**run**" (more than one dog → *run*, no *-s*). The trick that
surprises learners: a singular verb often ends in *-s*, while a plural
verb does not.

## Guided practice (adult helps)

1. Write the plural: *peach, story, man, tooth*.
2. Circle the abstract nouns: *kindness, rock, bravery, kite*.
3. Fill in the verb: "Yesterday Sam ___ (look) at the clouds."
   "Tomorrow we ___ (play) at the park." "Today he ___ (read) a book."
4. Fix the agreement error in each sentence, and say why:
   "The birds sings." · "Maya and Sam walks home." ·
   "She have a kite."

## Independent practice

1. Write the plural: *dish, puppy, foot, deer*.
2. Name the abstract noun in each sentence: "His childhood was
   happy." · "We felt joy at the news."
3. Write the past tense: *go, catch, teach, jump*.
4. Choose the verb that agrees: "The cat (sleep / sleeps) in the
   sun." · "The cats (sleep / sleeps) in the sun." ·
   "Lin and I (is / are) friends." · "He (do / does) his best work
   in the morning."

## Applied task — three-tense kite day

Write three sentences about a kite day: one in the past, one in the
present, and one in the future. Each sentence must have a subject and
a verb that agree. Underline the verb in each sentence.

## Exit check

Choose the verb that agrees: "The children ___ (was / were) happy."
Then write the plural of *woman*.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading access:** the adult reads the rule table aloud; the
  learner points to the row that matches each word.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner says plurals and past
  tenses aloud, or sorts cards, instead of writing them.
- **Extension:** hunt for three irregular plurals or irregular past
  verbs in a book you are reading and list them with their singular
  or present forms.
- **Error-analysis note:** "childs" and "foots" are the most common
  errors — they show the learner applied a rule where English keeps an
  exception. Praise the rule, then teach the exception.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: adding *-s* always makes a plural, and adding
*-s* to a verb always makes it plural. Both are backwards in the
second case: *runs* is singular. Drill with pairs (*dog runs / dogs
run*) until the pattern is automatic. Full solutions are in the
[answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-02).
