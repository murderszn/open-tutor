# Lesson 06 — Spelling Patterns, Suffix Spelling, and the Dictionary

**Goal (student-readable):** I can spell words with suffixes, use
spelling patterns, and check a word in a beginning dictionary.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** a beginning dictionary
(print or classroom copy), paper.
**Standards:** L.3.2.e–g.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

The adult writes *hop* and asks: "What happens if I add *-ing*?
What about *hope* + *-ing*?" The learner guesses. Both answers are
accepted as guesses — the lesson teaches the rules that decide them.

## Explicit teaching

### Adding suffixes (L.3.2.e)

A **suffix** is a word part added to the end of a base word.

- **Double the final consonant** when a short word has one vowel and
  one final consonant: *run → running, swim → swimming,
  hop → hopping*.
- **Drop the silent e** before a suffix that starts with a vowel:
  *make → making, smile → smiled, hope → hoping*.
- **Change y to i** before a suffix (except *-ing*): *happy →
  happiness, cry → cried, try → tries*. Keep the *y* before *-ing*:
  *cry → crying, play → playing*.
- High-frequency words are spelled by memory and practice, not by
  rule: *said, they, because, friend, through, again, school*.

### Spelling patterns and generalizations (L.3.2.f)

- **Word families:** words that share a pattern rhyme and spell alike:
  *-ight* → *light, night, right, bright*; *-ake* → *make, cake,
  rake, take*.
- **Position-based spelling:** the /k/ sound after a short vowel is
  usually spelled *ck* (*back, sock, duck*); after a long vowel or a
  consonant it is usually *k* (*book, milk, bank*).
- **Syllable patterns:** breaking a word into syllables helps spelling:
  *hap-pi-ness, run-ning, pic-nic*.

### Using a beginning dictionary (L.3.2.g)

- Words are in alphabetical order by **entry word**.
- **Guide words** at the top of a page name the first and last entry
  words on that page. A word belongs on the page if it falls
  alphabetically between the two guide words.
- An entry shows the spelling, the part of speech, and the meaning:
  "**kite** (noun): a light toy that flies in the wind on a string."

## Worked example 1 — four suffix spellings

- *run + ing*: short vowel + one consonant → double the *n*:
  **running**.
- *make + ing*: drop the silent *e* before *-ing*: **making**.
- *happy + ness*: *y* → *i* before *-ness*: **happiness**.
- *play + ing*: keep the *y* before *-ing*: **playing**.
  (Say it as a rule: "Double, drop, or change — check the base word
  first.")

## Worked example 2 — guide words

Guide words: **kite** and **lion**. Does *ladder* belong on this page?
Alphabetical order: *kite* (ki…) < *ladder* (la…) < *lion* (li…).
Yes — *ladder* falls between *kite* and *lion*, so it is on this
page. Does *leaf* belong? *kite* (ki…) < *leaf* (le…) < *lion*
(li…) — yes, it falls between too. Does *love* belong? *love*
(lo…) comes after *lion* (li…), because *o* comes after *i* — so
no, *love* is on a later page. The adult models this letter-by-letter
check slowly: compare the second letter first, then the third.

## Guided practice (adult helps)

1. Add the suffix: *hop + ing* · *hope + ing* · *cry + ed* ·
   *happy + ness*.
2. Word family: name one more *-ake* word besides *make, cake,
   rake, take*. Then choose: "The /k/ sound after a short vowel is
   usually spelled (back / bak)."
3. Guide words **map** and **moon**: does *milk* belong on this
   page? Does *mouse* belong? Explain with letter order.
4. Spelling dictation (adult reads aloud, learner writes):
   *because, friend, through, said*.

## Independent practice

1. Add the suffix: *swim + ing* · *smile + ed* · *try + es* ·
   *funny + est*.
2. Pattern work: write a word in the *-ight* family that names a
   source of light. Then choose: "The /k/ sound after a short vowel
   is usually spelled (ck / k)."
3. Guide words **park** and **play**: does *peach* belong on this
   page? Does *pond* belong? Explain with letter order.
4. Spelling dictation (adult reads aloud, learner writes):
   *they, where, again, school*.

## Applied task — edit the paragraph

Fix every spelling error in this paragraph (there are four). Rewrite
the paragraph correctly:

"Maya is runing to the park. She is hoppeing over a puddle. Her freind
Sam carryed the kite. They had a great day."

## Exit check

Add the suffix: *happy + ness*. Then, with guide words **sun** and
**swim**, tell whether *supper* belongs on the page, and why.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading access:** the adult reads each base word aloud and
  stretches its final sound before the learner adds the suffix.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner builds suffix words with
  letter cards (double the consonant card, cover the *e* card,
  swap *y* for *i*) instead of writing.
- **Extension:** look up two words from this unit in a beginning
  dictionary and copy their entry words, parts of speech, and
  definitions; or write five words in the *-ight* family.
- **Error-analysis note:** "hoping" vs. "hopping" is the classic
  minimal pair — one *p* changes the meaning (wishing vs. jumping).
  Use it to show why the doubling rule matters.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: every suffix rule applies to every word. Teach
the order: look at the base word's ending first (silent *e*? *y*?
short vowel + one consonant?), then choose the rule. Full solutions
are in the [answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-06).
