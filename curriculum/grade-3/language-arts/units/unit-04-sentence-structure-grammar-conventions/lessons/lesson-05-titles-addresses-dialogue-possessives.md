# Lesson 05 — Titles, Addresses, Dialogue Punctuation, and Possessives

**Goal (student-readable):** I can capitalize titles, punctuate
addresses and dialogue, and show ownership with apostrophes.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** paper, a beginning dictionary
(optional for extension).
**Standards:** L.3.2.a–d.

> Note for the adult: all addresses in this lesson are fictional
> examples written for teaching commas. Never write a learner's real
> address in practice work.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

The adult asks: "If you wrote a book, what would its title be? How
would you write your town and state?" The learner answers in their
own words. The adult notes whether capitals already appear in the
title words.

## Explicit teaching

### Capital letters in titles

Capitalize the first word, the last word, and every important word.
Small joining words (*a, an, the, and, or, of, in, to*) stay lowercase
unless they are first or last: "A Day at the Park", "The Red Kite",
"Sam and the Big Dog".

### Commas in addresses

Put a comma after the street address and after the city:
"Maya lives at 12 Oak Street, Springfield, Illinois."
Read it as: street, city, state — a comma after each part except the
last.

### Commas and quotation marks in dialogue

Dialogue is a character's exact words, inside quotation marks.

- Speaker tag first: comma before the quote, capital first word
  inside the quote.
  Maya said, "Let's fly kites."
- Quote first: comma inside the closing quotation mark, then the tag,
  lowercase tag verb.
  "Let's fly kites," said Maya.
- If the quoted sentence ends there, the period goes inside the
  closing quotation mark: Maya said, "I love kites."

### Possessives: showing ownership

- One owner: add **'s**. *Maya's kite, the dog's dish.*
- More than one owner, and the plural already ends in *s*: add just
  **'**. *the dogs' toys, the girls' coats.*
- Plural that does not end in *s*: add **'s**. *the children's
  books, the men's hats.*
- Possessive pronouns never take an apostrophe: *its* (belonging to
  it) is not *it's* (it is); *their, his, hers, ours* never use one.

## Worked example 1 — title and address

- Title: "a day at the park" → "**A Day at the Park**."
  (*A* is first so it is capitalized; *at* and *the* are small words
  in the middle, so they stay lowercase; *Day* and *Park* are
  important words.)
- Address: "Maya lives at 12 Oak Street Springfield Illinois" →
  "Maya lives at 12 Oak Street**,** Springfield**,** Illinois."
  (Comma after the street, comma after the city.)

## Worked example 2 — dialogue and possessive

- Dialogue: 'Maya said lets fly kites' →
  Maya said, "Let's fly kites."
  (Comma after *said*, quotation marks around the exact words,
  capital *L*, period inside the closing quote.)
- Possessive: "the kite of Maya" → "**Maya's** kite."
  (One owner → *'s*.) "the toys of the dogs" → "the **dogs'**
  toys." (More than one dog, plural ends in *s* → just *'*.)

## Guided practice (adult helps)

1. Capitalize these titles: "a day at the park" · "the red kite".
2. Add commas to this address: "Maya lives at 12 Oak Street
   Springfield Illinois".
3. Fix the dialogue punctuation: 'Maya said lets fly kites' ·
   '"It is windy" said Sam'.
4. Rewrite with a possessive: "the kite of Maya" · "the toys of the
   dogs" · "the book of the children".

## Independent practice

1. Capitalize these titles: "sam and the big dog" ·
   "my trip to the lake".
2. Add commas to this address: "Sam lives at 8 Hill Road Riverton
   Ohio".
3. Fix the dialogue punctuation: '"We won" shouted Lin' ·
   'Lin asked, "can we play"'.
4. Choose the possessive that fits: "That is (its / it's) tail." ·
   "The (dog's / dogs') dish is full." (one dog) ·
   "The (childrens' / children's) coats are on the hooks."

## Applied task — two lines of dialogue

Write two lines of dialogue between Maya and Sam about flying a
kite. Use commas and quotation marks correctly, and include one
possessive (for example, *Sam's kite* or *Maya's string*). Read the
lines aloud, with a different voice for each speaker if you like.

## Exit check

Fix: 'sam said "i lost my kite"'. Then rewrite with a possessive:
"the hat of Lin".

## Supports and extension

- **Reading access:** the adult reads each unpunctuated sentence
  aloud with a pause where the comma belongs.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner places comma and
  quotation-mark cards on a printed sentence instead of rewriting it.
- **Extension:** write a three-line dialogue with a tag in the
  middle ("I am ready," said Maya, "for the wind."), or punctuate a
  short letter address with a ZIP-style number (fictional).
- **Error-analysis note:** *it's* vs. *its* is the most common
  possessive error. Test: "Can I replace it with *it is*? If yes,
  use *it's*. If no, use *its*."

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: every word in a title is capitalized. Teach the
small-word rule with a chant: "First, last, and important — the rest
stay small." Full solutions are in the
