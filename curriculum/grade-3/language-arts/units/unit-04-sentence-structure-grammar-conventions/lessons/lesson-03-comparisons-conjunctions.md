# Lesson 03 — Comparatives, Superlatives, and Conjunctions

**Goal (student-readable):** I can compare two things and three or
more things correctly, and I can join ideas with conjunctions.
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** word cards (optional), paper.
**Standards:** L.3.1.g–h.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

The adult asks: "Who is taller — you or me? Who is the tallest person
you know?" The learner answers in their own words. The adult listens
for *-er* with two and *-est* with a group.

## Explicit teaching

### Comparing: two things vs. three or more

- **Comparative** — compares **two** things: add *-er*, or use *more*.
  *taller, faster, more beautiful.*
- **Superlative** — compares **three or more** things, or picks the
  extreme: add *-est*, or use *most*. *tallest, fastest, most
  beautiful.*
- Short words usually take *-er / -est*: *tall → taller → tallest*.
- Longer words usually take *more / most*: *beautiful → more
  beautiful → most beautiful*.
- Some comparisons are irregular and must be learned: *good → better
  → best; bad → worse → worst*.
- The same rules work for adverbs: *fast → faster → fastest;
  quickly → more quickly → most quickly*.

The count test: "How many things am I comparing?" Two → comparative.
Three or more → superlative.

### Joining: conjunctions

A **conjunction** is a joining word.

- **Coordinating conjunctions** join two equal ideas: *and, but, or,
  so*. "Maya flies a kite, **and** Sam watches." (Both ideas could
  stand alone.)
- **Subordinating conjunctions** join a dependent idea to a main
  idea: *because, when, before, after, if, while*. "We stay inside
  **when** it rains." (*When it rains* cannot stand alone — it needs
  the main idea.)

## Worked example 1 — two kites, then three

- One kite: "Maya's kite is high."
- Two kites: "Maya's kite is **higher** than Sam's kite."
  (Two things → comparative, *-er*.)
- Three kites: "Lin's kite is the **highest** of the three."
  (Three things → superlative, *-est*, with *the*.)

With an adverb: "The dog runs **fast**. The girl runs **faster**.
The wind blows **fastest** of all." Same count test, same endings.

## Worked example 2 — joining two ideas

- Two equal ideas: "Maya flies a kite. Sam watches the clouds." →
  "Maya flies a kite, **and** Sam watches the clouds."
  (Comma before *and* when joining two full sentences.)
- A reason: "The dog ran. It saw a squirrel." →
  "The dog ran **because** it saw a squirrel."
  (*Because it saw a squirrel* cannot stand alone, so *because* is
  subordinating.)
- A time: "We will go to the park **if** the rain stops."
  (*If the rain stops* is the condition; the main idea is *we will
  go to the park*.)

## Guided practice (adult helps)

1. Choose: "Of the two kites, the red one is (higher / highest)."
   "Of the three kites, the red one is the (higher / highest)."
2. Choose: "This book is (more interesting / most interesting) than
   that one." "This is the (more / most) interesting book on the
   shelf."
3. Choose: "She sings (more beautifully / most beautifully) than her
   brother." "He runs (faster / fastest) of all."
4. Join each pair with the conjunction in parentheses:
   "It rained. We stayed home." (*because*) ·
   "Maya can fly a kite. Sam can ride a bike." (*and*)

## Independent practice

1. Choose: "The red kite is (bigger / biggest) than the blue kite."
   "The red kite is the (bigger / biggest) of the three."
2. Choose: "This puzzle is (harder / hardest) than that one."
   "It is the (harder / hardest) puzzle in the box."
3. Choose: "Her voice is (softer / softest) than mine."
   "Her voice is the (softer / softest) of the three."
4. Join each pair with a conjunction that fits:
   "Maya wanted a kite. She saved her coins." ·
   "The dog barked. It saw the cat."

## Applied task — compare and join

Write two comparison sentences about kites, dogs, or books: one
comparative (two things) and one superlative (three or more). Then
write one sentence that joins two ideas with *because*.

## Exit check

Choose: "Of the two dogs, the brown one runs (faster / fastest)."
Then join with *so*: "It is sunny. We will play outside."

## Supports and extension

- **Reading access:** the adult reads each pair aloud; the learner
  holds up two fingers for a comparative and three or more fingers
  for a superlative.
- **Alternate response mode:** sort comparison cards into "two" and
  "three or more" piles instead of writing.
- **Extension:** write the comparative and superlative of *good,
  bad, far* (*better/best, worse/worst, farther/farthest*) and use
  each in a sentence.
- **Error-analysis note:** "more taller" doubles the comparison.
  One comparison marker is enough: *taller* or *more tall* is wrong;
  *taller* is right.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: *-est* for any comparison, or *more* + *-er*
together. Return to the count test every time. Full solutions are in
the [answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-03).
