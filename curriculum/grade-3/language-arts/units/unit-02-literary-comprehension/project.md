# Project — My Fable Storybook Page

**Unit 02 · Grade 3 Language Arts · Sessions 12 and 14 (plus Session 19 presentations)**

## Goal

Create one illustrated storybook page (or a two-page spread) for an
**original fable you write yourself**: animal characters, a clear sequence
of events, and a central message (lesson or moral) that the events prove.
You will read it aloud like a storyteller and answer one evidence question
from the audience.

## Materials

- Paper (one large sheet, or two stapled), pencil, crayons or markers
- "Character Chain" organizer from Lesson 04 (reuse, don't remake)
- Optional: paper-bag or stick puppets for the Session 19 performance
- The unit's fable retellings as models (never copy their sentences)

## Steps

1. **Choose your lesson (Session 12, 10 min).** Pick one message your fable
   will teach, in "should" form: e.g., "Share what you have," "Think before
   you act," "Keep trying even when it is hard." Write it at the top of
   your plan.
2. **Choose your animals (10 min).** Pick two animal characters whose
   bodies fit the story (a beak, a shell, wings — like the fox's tongue and
   the stork's beak). Name one **trait** for each and the **action** that
   will prove it.
3. **Plan the chain (10 min).** Fill the Character Chain: trait → action →
   event for the main character, in beginning/middle/end order. Check: does
   the ending **prove** your message? If not, change the middle.
4. **Write the fable (Session 14, 20 min).** 8–15 sentences, your own words,
   in order. Read it aloud to yourself: does every sentence earn its place?
5. **Illustrate (Session 14, 15 min).** Draw the key moment — the action
   that proves the main trait. Add one sentence under the picture explaining
   what the illustration shows that the words don't (Lesson 06 thinking).
6. **Rehearse (5 min).** Read aloud with expression. Mark two places to
   pause for effect.
7. **Present (Session 19).** Read your fable to the group. The audience asks
   you **one** evidence question ("Which words show your character is
   ___?"); you answer by quoting your own text.

## Deliverables

1. The storybook page(s): original fable text (8–15 sentences) + illustration
   with caption.
2. The completed Character Chain plan.
3. The one-sentence central message, written separately at the top of the plan.
4. Oral presentation with one answered evidence question.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Exceeding (3) | Meeting (2) | Working toward (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Central message** | Message stated clearly AND two story details prove it | Message stated; one detail proves it | Message missing or story doesn't show it |
| **Character** | Trait named; two actions prove it; motivation clear | Trait named; one action proves it | Trait named but no action proves it |
| **Sequence** | Beginning/middle/end in order; events connect by cause | In order; one weak connection | Out of order or missing a part |
| **Illustration** | Shows the key action/feeling; caption names what words don't say | Illustrates a story moment; caption present | Decorative only; no caption |
| **Presentation** | Expressive read-aloud; answers evidence question with quoted words | Reads aloud; answers with story reference | Reads only; cannot answer the question |

**Scoring:** 12–15 = strong fable author; 9–11 = meeting — ready for Unit 03;
below 9 = revise one weakest row with the adult and re-present.

## Accessibility and alternatives

- The fable may be **dictated** to the adult (adult scribes exactly what the
  learner says); the learner still plans the chain and draws the
  illustration.
- Illustration may be a **collage** of cut paper shapes if drawing is hard.
- Presentation may be recorded audio instead of live.

## Teacher notes

- Originality check: the fable must not reuse sentences from the unit's
  retellings. Similar *plots* are fine (fables share plots); copied
  *wording* is not.
- If a learner's message is only a topic ("about sharing"), send them back
  to Lesson 03's "should"-shaped sentence test before they write.
