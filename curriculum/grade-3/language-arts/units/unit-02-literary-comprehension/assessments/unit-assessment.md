# Unit Assessment — Unit 02: Literary Comprehension (Character and Theme)

**Grade 3 Language Arts · Session 16 · 30–40 minutes**
Name: ______________________ Date: __________

## Part A — "The Boy Who Cried Wolf" (original retelling)

> "A shepherd boy watched his flock on the green hill. The days were long
> and quiet, so one afternoon he ran to the village shouting, 'Wolf! Wolf!
> A wolf is chasing my sheep!'
>
> The villagers dropped their work and ran up the hill with sticks and
> shovels. But there was no wolf — only the boy, laughing. 'Fooled you!'
> he cried.
>
> A week later he did it again. 'Wolf! Wolf!' The villagers ran again, and
> again there was no wolf. 'Stop wasting our time,' they grumbled.
>
> Then a real wolf came, gray and hungry, slinking toward the flock. The
> boy shouted with all his might: 'Wolf! Wolf! A REAL wolf this time!' But
> no one came. The villagers stayed in their houses, shaking their heads.
> The boy learned, too late, that no one believes a liar — even when he
> tells the truth."

**A1.** Recount the story's beginning, middle, and end in three sentences.
(RL.3.2)

**A2.** What is the boy's **trait** at the start of the story? Give one
**action** that proves it. (RL.3.3)

**A3.** How do the villagers **feel** the second time the boy shouts?
Quote one detail that shows it. (RL.3.3)

**A4.** What is the central message (moral)? List **two** key details that
carry it. (RL.3.2)

**A5.** "The days were long and quiet." What was the boy's **motivation**
for the first false alarm? (RL.3.3)

## Part B — "The Two Pots" (original fable-style story)

> "Two clay pots sat on the riverbank: one new and shiny, one old and
> cracked. When the rains came, the river rose, and both pots were swept
> into the current.
>
> 'Stay close to me!' cried the new pot, bumping against the old one.
> 'Do not bump me,' said the old pot quietly. 'You are strong and I am
> cracked. If we knock together, I will break.'
>
> The new pot laughed — a hollow, **ringing** laugh. 'I am not afraid of a
> little bump!' He knocked against the old pot again and again, showing off
> how tough he was.
>
> Crack. The old pot split in two and sank. The new pot floated on alone,
> and for the first time the river felt very wide."

**B1.** "A hollow, **ringing** laugh." Is *ringing* literal or nonliteral
here? What does it tell you about how the new pot laughed? (RL.3.4, L.3.5.a)

**B2.** What is the **new pot's** point of view about bumping the old pot?
What is the **old pot's** point of view? (RL.3.6)

**B3.** What is the central message of "The Two Pots"? (RL.3.2)

**B4.** **Compare** the boy in Part A with the new pot in Part B: what
**trait** do they share? Give one action from each story as proof. (RL.3.3,
RL.3.9)

**B5.** **Contrast** the two stories' endings: what is different about what
each character learns — or fails to learn? (RL.3.9)

## Scoring rubric

| Points | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **3 — Strong** | Correct answer + quoted/precise text evidence + clear reasoning |
| **2 — Meeting** | Correct answer + some text reference; reasoning mostly clear |
| **1 — Developing** | Partly correct or answer without text evidence |
| **0 — Not yet** | Incorrect, missing, or off-topic |

10 items × 3 = **30 points**. 24+ = strong; 18–23 = meeting (ready for
Unit 03); below 18 = targeted reteach of missed standards, then one
re-attempt of those items only.
