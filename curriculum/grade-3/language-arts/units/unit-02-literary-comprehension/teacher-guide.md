## Session-by-session notes

**Sessions 1–2 (Lesson 01 + evidence reading).** Drill the sentence frame
*"I know this because the story says…"* until it is automatic. Accept
approximate quotes early ("close to the words"); require exact quotes by
Week 9. If the learner answers from life knowledge, ask: "Show me where the
story says that."

**Sessions 3–4 (Lesson 02 + retell practice).** The five story-part cards
(characters / setting / beginning / middle / end) stay on the table all
unit — reuse them in every later session. Oral retells count fully; do not
force writing before the learner can hold the order orally.

**Sessions 5–6 (Lesson 03 + moral practice).** The "should"-shaped sentence
test is the gate: if the learner's message can't be phrased as advice
("You should…"), it is a topic or detail, not a message. Keep a running
"message wall" of one-sentence messages from each story.

**Sessions 7–8 (Lesson 04 + character chains).** Trait vs. feeling confusion
is the week's battle (see misconceptions). The fox-and-stork illustration is
an **AI-generated illustration made for this lesson** — say so; use it to
practice reading feelings from faces, not as evidence about real foxes.

**Sessions 9–10 (Lesson 05 + idiom journal).** Honor home and family idioms;
add them to the journal. The item-5 trap ("a piece of cake" + "until the
final week") teaches that context can revise meaning — revisit it in the
Week 10 review.

**Sessions 11–12 (Lesson 06 + project launch).** Three-column separation
(ME / NARRATOR / CHARACTER) on paper. Project: check every plan's message
with the "should" test before writing begins; a weak message now means a
weak fable later.

**Session 13 (compare/contrast).** Same-author framing: both retellings come
from the Aesop tradition. A Venn diagram or three-column table (theme /
setting / plot) is the deliverable; the point is that similar characters
can carry opposite lessons (Fox gives up; Crow persists).

**Session 14 (project writing).** Originality check: similar plots are fine,
copied sentences are not. Read two sentences aloud and ask "whose words are
these?" if unsure.

**Session 15 (quiz + error review).** Score the same day. Reteach misses
immediately in small groups; the error-analysis items (Practice A6, C7)
diagnose whether the miss is evidence-habit or concept.

**Session 16 (unit assessment).** 30–40 minutes, open-story (learners may
re-read). Score with the rubric; record per-standard results, not just the
total.

**Sessions 17–20 (flex).** Reteach, project presentations (Session 19),
myth read-aloud discussion (Session 18, RL.3.10), theme-sort review game and
Unit 03 preview (Session 20).

## Common misconceptions and responses

1. **Answering from memory/life instead of the text.** Response: require the
   "story says" frame; ask "which words?" until pointing becomes habit.
2. **Topic ≠ message.** ("It's about ants.") Response: the "should" test.
3. **Trait ≠ feeling.** ("Sad" as a trait.) Response: "Most of the time, or
   just right now?"
4. **Motivation ≠ action.** Response: "What was the character *hoping*
   would happen?"
5. **Memorized idiom definitions ignoring context.** Response: re-read the
   surrounding sentences; ask "does your meaning still fit *here*?"
6. **Merging points of view.** Response: three columns / three puppets;
   never accept "the story says" when the claim came from one character.

## Readiness criteria

- **Ready for Unit 03:** quiz ≥ 8/10 AND assessment ≥ 18/30, with at least
  "meeting" on one RL.3.2 message item and one RL.3.3 character item.
- **Reteach triggers:** evidence answers without quotes after Week 8 →
  re-run Lesson 01 guided practice with a new passage; message/topic
  confusion → Lesson 03 sorting strips again; trait/feeling confusion →
  Lesson 04 face-drawing support activity.
- **Extension path:** learners at "strong" early move to the lesson
  extensions and may draft a second fable with two morals for Session 19.

## Accessibility

- Every lesson lists oral, pointing, drawing, and adult-scribed alternatives;
  all are first-class responses, not lesser ones.
- The illustration's text-only alternative is printed in Lesson 04; any
  activity using the image works fully from the words alone.
- Sessions are 25–30 minutes by design; split any session across two days
  rather than rushing.
