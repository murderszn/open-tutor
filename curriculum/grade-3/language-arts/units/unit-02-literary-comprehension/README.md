# Unit 02 — Literary Comprehension: Character and Theme

**Grade:** 3 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #20](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/20), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Fables, folktales, and myths are short on the outside and deep on the
inside. In this unit the learner reads stories that teach lessons through
animal characters, asks and answers questions that point straight back to
the words on the page, retells stories in their own words, names the central
message (the lesson or moral) and the key details that carry it, describes
what characters are like and why they act the way they do, tells literal
meanings from nonliteral ones, notices when the narrator sees things
differently than the characters do, and compares two stories by the same
author about similar characters. Every reading passage in this unit is
original: Aesop-style fable retellings written for this unit in the unit
author's own words, plus original stories — never copied texts.

## Essential question

How do a character's actions show me what the character is like — and what
the story is really about?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2), the grade-2 track, and Unit 01:

- Decode multisyllable words by finding vowel sounds and breaking words into
  syllables; read grade-3 prose aloud for 2–3 minutes with accuracy and
  expression (Unit 01 retrieval warm-ups keep this live)
- Follow a story's who, what, and where; answer who/what/where questions
  about a read-aloud
- Write 2–4 legible sentences; draw and label a simple scene

Learners who cannot yet decode grade-3 prose independently may follow the
unit through adult read-alouds; every lesson names that alternative.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Ask and answer questions to demonstrate understanding of a text, referring
   explicitly to the text as the basis for answers (RL.3.1).
2. Recount stories — including fables, folktales, and myths — and determine
   their central message, lesson, or moral, explaining how key details convey
   it (RL.3.2).
3. Describe characters' traits, motivations, and feelings, and explain how
   their actions contribute to the sequence of events (RL.3.3).
4. Determine the meaning of literal and nonliteral words and phrases as used
   in text (RL.3.4, L.3.5.a); distinguish the learner's own point of view
   from the narrator's or characters' (RL.3.6); explain how a specific
   illustration contributes to what is conveyed by the words (RL.3.7).
5. Compare and contrast the themes, settings, and plots of stories written by
   the same author about the same or similar characters (RL.3.9); read and
   comprehend grade-3 literature with scaffolding as needed (RL.3.10).

## Unit reading passages (original texts written for this unit)

- **"The Tortoise and the Hare"** — original prose retelling of the Aesop
  fable (ancient public-domain plot; wording entirely the unit author's),
  Lessons 01–02.
- **"Anansi and the Talking Drum"** — an original folktale-style story with
  folktale features (trickster, repetition, explanation ending), written for
  this unit; not a retelling of any specific culture's tale, Lesson 02.
- **"The Ant and the Grasshopper"** — original prose retelling of the Aesop
  fable (ancient public-domain plot; wording entirely the unit author's),
  Lesson 03.
- **"The Fox and the Stork"** — original prose retelling of the Aesop fable
  (ancient public-domain plot; wording entirely the unit author's) plus the
  generated illustration `assets/fox-and-stork-dinner.png`, Lesson 04.
- **"The New School Play"** — an original story built around grade-3 idioms,
  Lesson 05.
- **"Why the Sky Is High"** — an original "why" story (myth-style:
  explains something in nature), written for this unit; not a retelling of
  any specific culture's myth, Lesson 06.
- **"The Boy Who Cried Wolf"** — original prose retelling of the Aesop
  fable for the formative quiz and compare/contrast session (Week 10).

## Session pacing (20 sessions, 25–30 minutes each, 4 per week)

| # | Session | Standard | Success criterion |
|---|---------|----------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Lesson 01** — Asking and answering questions with text evidence | RL.3.1 | Learner cites the text in 3 of 4 question answers |
| 2 | Read-aloud + paired reading: "The Tortoise and the Hare"; evidence-question practice | RL.3.1, RL.3.10 | Every answer names words from the text |
| 3 | **Lesson 02** — Recounting stories: characters, setting, beginning/middle/end | RL.3.2 | Oral recount covers all five story parts |
| 4 | Retell practice: "Anansi and the Talking Drum"; story-part sorting | RL.3.2 | Written or oral 5-part recount, correct order |
| 5 | **Lesson 03** — Central message, lesson, moral + key details | RL.3.2 | Message named; 2 key details explain how it is shown |
| 6 | Story session: "The Ant and the Grasshopper"; moral-finding practice | RL.3.2 | 4 of 5 moral questions correct with detail reasons |
| 7 | **Lesson 04** — Character traits, motivations, feelings; actions drive events | RL.3.3 | 3 traits named, each backed by a text detail |
| 8 | Character-chain practice: "The Fox and the Stork" + illustration | RL.3.3, RL.3.7 | Completed trait→action→event chain |
| 9 | **Lesson 05** — Literal vs. nonliteral language in stories | RL.3.4, L.3.5.a | 4 of 5 idioms explained in context |
| 10 | Reading practice: "The New School Play"; idiom journal | RL.3.4, L.3.5.a | Journal entries correct with context clues quoted |
| 11 | **Lesson 06** — Points of view + how illustrations add meaning | RL.3.6, RL.3.7 | Learner states 2 point-of-view differences correctly |
| 12 | Project session 1: launch fable storybook project | RL.3.2–3.3 | Plan and character sketch complete |
| 13 | Compare/contrast: two Aesop retellings — same author, similar characters | RL.3.9 | Venn/table of theme, setting, plot complete |
| 14 | Project session 2: write and illustrate | RL.3.2–3.3 | Draft fable with clear moral and trait evidence |
| 15 | Formative quiz (`assessments/week-04_quiz.md`) + error review | RL.3.1–3.4, 3.6 | ≥ 8/10; misses re-taught same day |
| 16 | Culminating assessment (`assessments/unit-assessment.md`) | All unit standards | Scored with rubric; ≥ "meeting" to proceed |
| 17 | Flex: reteach small group; oral/drawn responses honored | Targeted | Individual goal set |
| 18 | Flex: myth read-aloud — "Why the Sky Is High" discussion | RL.3.10 | Participation in recount and theme talk |
| 19 | Project presentations; peer asks an evidence question each | RL.3.1–3.3 | Every project presented and questioned |
| 20 | Unit review: theme-sort game; preview Unit 03 routines | RL.3.2, 3.9 | Retrieval of 4 story messages with details |

Every lesson opens with a 2-minute retrieval warm-up from Unit 01
(multisyllable word cards) before the new work begins.

## Standards notes

Standards follow the Common Core ELA Reading: Literature strand, grade 3
(RL.3.1–3.10), verified against the A00 audit crosswalk
(`scope-and-sequence.md` §3): RL.3.1 ask/answer with text reference; RL.3.2
recount + central message; RL.3.3 character traits/motivations/feelings and
actions; RL.3.4 literal vs. nonliteral meaning; RL.3.6 point of view;
RL.3.7 illustrations; RL.3.9 compare/contrast same-author stories;
RL.3.10 grade-level text. L.3.5.a covers distinguishing literal from
nonliteral meanings of words and phrases in context. This unit makes no claim
of state adoption, accreditation, or complete alignment; it is a validated
draft, not educator-reviewed.
