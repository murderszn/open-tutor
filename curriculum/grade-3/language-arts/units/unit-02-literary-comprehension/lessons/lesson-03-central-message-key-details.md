# Lesson 03 — Central Message, Lesson, and Moral: Finding What the Story Teaches

**Unit 02 · Grade 3 Language Arts · Session 5**
**Standard:** RL.3.2 (determine the central message, lesson, or moral and
explain how it is conveyed through key details in the text)
**Time:** 25–30 minutes · **Materials:** notebook, pencil, "Message or just
a fact?" sorting strips (prepared from the story below)

## Student goal

Today I will find the central message of a story — what it teaches about
life — and show the key details that carry it.

## Readiness check (2 minutes, retrieval warm-up)

Learner reads Unit 01 cards *prepare, season, future, gather* and gives a
thumbs-up/sideways/down for "I could use this word in a sentence." The adult
notes wobblers for later.

## Explicit teaching

A story's **central message** is the big idea it teaches about life. In a
fable it is often called the **lesson** or the **moral**. Two rules:

1. A message is about **people in general**, not just the characters.
   "Ants store food" is a fact about ants. **"Work now so you are ready
   later"** is a message for everyone.
2. A message must be **carried by key details** — the important events that
   show it happening. If no detail shows it, it is your idea, not the
   story's message.

Ask three questions:

- What did a character do **again and again** (a pattern)?
- What happened **because of** that pattern (a result)?
- What lesson would you give a friend based on the pattern and result?

### Worked example 1 — fact vs. message

> After reading a story about a girl who shares her lunch every day and
> makes a new friend:
>
> - ❌ "The girl had a sandwich." → a detail, not a message.
> - ❌ "Sharing is nice for Maya." → only about one character.
> - ✅ "Sharing with others can start a friendship." → about people in
>   general, carried by the repeated sharing and the new friend.

### Worked example 2 — key details carrying the message

> **Story: "The Ant and the Grasshopper"** (original retelling)
>
> "All summer, Ant carried grain to her hill. Grain by grain, day by day,
> she filled her storehouse while the sun was warm. Grasshopper watched
> from the fence, playing his fiddle. 'Why work?' he laughed. 'Dance with
> me! Summer is for singing!'
>
> Ant did not stop. 'Winter is coming,' she said. 'I am getting ready.'
> Grasshopper danced on, day after warm day.
>
> Then the cold came. Snow covered the field. Grasshopper knocked on Ant's
> door, hungry and shivering. Ant shared her stored grain with him, and as
> he ate, he said, 'Next summer, I will work beside you.'"

**Message:** *Work hard and prepare now so you are ready when hard times
come.*

**Key details that carry it:**

1. Ant carried grain "grain by grain, day by day" all summer (the pattern).
2. Grasshopper played and laughed instead of working (the contrast).
3. Winter came; Ant had food and Grasshopper did not (the result).

The ending adds a second layer: Ant **shares**, so a defensible second
message is *"Be prepared — and be kind when others were not."* Either answer
earns full marks with matching details.

## Guided practice

Read the retelling above again with the adult. Sort each strip as **M**
(message), **D** (detail), or **X** (neither):

1. "Ants live in hills."
2. "Work and prepare now so you are ready later."
3. "Ant carried grain grain by grain, day by day."
4. "Grasshopper played his fiddle all summer."
5. "Snow covered the field."
6. "Grasshopper promised to work next summer."
7. "It is better to give than to receive."
8. Which two **D** strips best carry strip 2? Explain to the adult.

## Independent practice

In your notebook:

1. Write the central message of "The Ant and the Grasshopper" in your own
   words (one sentence).
2. List **three** key details that show it, in story order.
3. Could the story also teach "Help others when you can"? Give **one**
   detail that supports this second message.
4. Write one sentence that is true about the story but is **not** its
   message, and explain why not.

## Application

Think of a real day this week. Write 3–4 sentences telling what happened
(pattern + result), then state one life lesson it taught **you**. This is
the same thinking fables use — your project fable (Session 12) will do this
with animal characters.

## Exit check

One sentence: What is the difference between a story *detail* and a story
*message*?

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** Adult reads aloud; learner draws the pattern (Ant working),
  the contrast (Grasshopper playing), and the result (winter), then dictates
  the message for the adult to scribe.
- **Alternate response mode:** Message matching — learner matches three
  printed messages to three one-paragraph story summaries.
- **Extension:** Does the story's message apply to **school**? Write one
  example of "working now to be ready later" from school life, with the
  pattern and result labeled.

## Teacher note (misconceptions)

- The #1 error is stating the **topic** ("This story is about ants and
  winter") instead of the **message**. Require a "should"-shaped sentence:
  a message tells what someone *should learn or do*.
- Accept any defensible message backed by details; fables support more than
  one reading. Reject messages with no text details, even if they sound
  wise.
