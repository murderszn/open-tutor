# Project — My Word-Parts Dictionary

**Investigating question:** Can I build a dictionary that teaches someone
else how words are built?

Across the unit (sessions 2–15), the learner builds a personal
**Word-Parts Dictionary**: a set of illustrated entry cards — one word per
card — that breaks each word into its parts, explains what each part does,
and uses the word in an original sentence. The finished dictionary is the
unit's investigation product and is assessed with the rubric below.

## Materials

Index cards or half-sheets of paper (8+), blue/yellow/red crayons or
markers (matching the [word-part puzzle image](assets/word-part-puzzle-pieces.png)
— **an AI-generated illustration**), pencil, a beginning dictionary
(print or adult-supervised digital), the unit word sets.

## Steps

1. **Collect candidate words (sessions 2–12, ongoing).** As the unit goes,
   the learner "arrests" interesting words: each must contain a prefix, a
   suffix, or both. Aim for at least 10 candidates by session 13; the
   final dictionary needs 8 finished entries.
2. **Draft entries (session 14).** For each chosen word, fill a card:
   - The word, written large, with syllable dots (e.g., *un·com·fort·a·ble*).
   - The word broken across colored pieces: **blue** under the prefix,
     **yellow** under the root, **red** under the suffix — matching the
     puzzle-piece colors.
   - Each part's job in the learner's own words ("un- = not", "-able =
     can be").
   - The whole word's meaning in the learner's own words.
   - An original sentence using the word correctly.
   - A small illustration slot: draw the meaning (a quick sketch, not
     fine art).
3. **Verify (session 14).** Check each entry against a beginning
   dictionary: is the meaning right? Fix any entry the dictionary
   contradicts, and note what the detective tools got wrong.
4. **Peer-teach rehearsal (session 15).** The learner "teaches" 3 entries
   to the adult using the puzzle-piece language: "The blue piece says…,
   the yellow piece is…, the red piece does…." The adult asks one "why"
   question per entry.
5. **Finish and file (session 15).** Complete 8+ entries, order them
   alphabetically, add a cover card with the dictionary's title and the
   "how to read an entry" key. The dictionary stays in the learner's
   private materials — it is not published.

## Deliverables

- 8+ finished entry cards, each with: syllable dots, color-coded parts,
  each part's job, the word's meaning, an original sentence, an
  illustration slot filled with a sketch.
- 1 cover card with title and entry-reading key.
- Evidence of dictionary verification (at least 2 corrected or confirmed
  entries noted).

## Rubric

| Criterion | 3 — Strong | 2 — Developing | 1 — Starting |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Word-part accuracy** | All 8+ entries break the word into correct prefix/root/suffix with correct jobs | 6–7 entries correct; 1–2 mislabeled parts | Fewer than 6 correct; pieces confused |
| **Meaning** | Every meaning is accurate and in the learner's own words; dictionary-verified | Meanings mostly accurate; 1–2 copied or vague | Several meanings wrong or missing |
| **Sentences** | All sentences use the word correctly and show its meaning | Most sentences correct; 1–2 awkward or off-meaning | Sentences misuse the word or are missing |
| **Completeness & care** | 8+ entries, alphabetical, cover card with key, illustration slots filled | 8 entries but missing cover key or sketches | Fewer than 8 entries or many parts missing |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = exceeds; 7–9 = meets; 4–6 = approaching (reteach with
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md#reteach-paths)); 1–3 = restart with adult
modeling one full entry first. The dictionary is a learning product, not a
standardized measure.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** the adult pre-selects 8 high-utility words (*unhappy,
  replay, careful, darkness, preview, helpful, disappear, readable*),
  pre-draws the colored pieces, and scribes the learner's dictated
  meanings.
- **Extension:** add an "etymology note" to 3 entries — where the part
  came from (*pre-* from Latin *before*; *-tion* words came through
  French/Latin). The adult helps verify with the dictionary's word-origin
  notes.
