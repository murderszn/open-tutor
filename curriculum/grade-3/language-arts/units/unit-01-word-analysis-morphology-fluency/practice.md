# Practice Sets A–F — Unit 01

Each set pairs with a pacing-table session. The adult reviews independent
work the same day. Answers are in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md); do not
show the key to the learner.

## Practice A — Syllable sort, clap-and-break, decodable sentences (session 2)

**A1. Syllable count sort.** Sort these 12 words by syllable count (1, 2,
or 4): *sun, picnic, basket, lemon, robot, tiger, paper, music, pencil,
celebration, garden, happy*.
**A2. Clap-and-break race.** The adult calls 8 words from the unit
multisyllable set; the learner claps the beats and says the count. Beat the
learner's own round-1 score in round 2 (self-competition only).
**A3. Find the vowels.** Circle every vowel in *napkin, pocket, follow,
silent, insect, absent*; draw the break line; read each word.
**A4. Decodable sentences.** Read aloud: "The rabbit sat in the garden."
"The tiger naps in the hot sun." "My pencil is in my pocket." "We had a
picnic in the park."
**A5. Error hunt.** The adult reads: "The *bas-ket* is full." Then:
"The *bak-set* is full." Which break is right? How do you know?
**A6. Write it broken.** Choose 4 words from A1; write each with its break
line(s) and read them to the adult.

## Practice B — Prefix sort and build-a-word workshop (session 4)

**B1. Four-pile sort.** Sort into un-, re-, dis-, pre-: *unhappy, replay,
disagree, preview, unlock, rebuild, disappear, prepay, unkind, rewrite,
dislike, preheat*.
**B2. Odd one out.** In each row, find the word that is NOT prefix + root:
(a) *untie, uncle, unlock* (b) *return, replay, ready* (c) *preview,
pretty, prepay*.
**B3. Build-a-word.** Combine a prefix card (un-, re-, dis-, pre-) with a
root card (*tie, appear, heat, kind, play, agree, view, lock*). Build 8
words; write each across drawn blue/yellow puzzle pieces.
**B4. Predict the meaning.** For 4 built words, write "prefix + root =
meaning" (e.g., *re + play = play again*).
**B5. Sentence check.** "They will *rebuild* the fence." "Please *preview*
the pictures." Does each predicted meaning fit? Write yes/no and why.
**B6. Invent-a-word.** Invent one new prefix word (e.g., *redraw* is real —
try *rejump*). Defend its meaning with the prefix rule.

## Practice C — Suffix sort and meaning-match (session 6)

**C1. Three-pile sort.** Sort into Latin chunk / -ful or -less / -ness:
*action, helpful, kindness, station, hopeless, sadness, readable,
careful, darkness, motion, fearless, happiness, decision, thankful,
payment, invisible*.
**C2. Chunk reading.** Read: *celebration, division, comfortable,
enjoyment, terrible, treatment*. Underline the Latin suffix in each.
**C3. Meaning-match.** Match each word to its meaning: *painful, careless,
darkness, movement* → "full of pain" / "without care" / "the state of
being dark" / "the act of moving".
**C4. Two-way change.** *help → helpful → helpless. care → careful →
careless.* Write what each suffix did, then use *helpful* and *helpless*
in two sentences that show the difference.
**C5. Noun-maker.** Add *-ment* or *-ness* to *enjoy, state, sad, dark*.
Write the new words and name the part of speech they became.
**C6. Read the chunk.** The adult covers the root of *television,
government, possible, agreeable*; the learner reads the suffix chunk
alone, then the whole word.

## Practice D — Word-part detective: mixed builds (session 7)

**D1. Three-piece teardown.** Break into prefix/root/suffix and give the
meaning: *uncomfortable, disagreeable, carelessness, rebuilding,
disappeared, unhappiness*.
**D2. Build from pieces.** Given blue/yellow/red cards (*un-, re- //
comfort, help, kind // -able, -ful, -ness*), build 6 real words and write
each meaning.
**D3. Which piece?** "In *replay*, which piece is the prefix?" (Blue.)
"In *darkness*, which piece carries the meaning change?" (Red.) "In
*preview*, which piece do you already know?" (Yellow — *view*.)
**D4. Sentence detective.** "The *unbreakable* toy survived the fall."
Break it (un-break-able), predict ("cannot be broken"), check the
sentence. Then: "Her *thoughtfulness* made everyone smile." (*thought* +
*-ful* + *-ness* — full of thought, as a quality.)
**D5. Trap words.** The removal test has two parts: (1) does removing the
affix leave a real word? (2) does the affix's meaning actually apply?
Explain why each fails: *under, lesson, mother, listen*.
**D6. Speed teardown.** The adult shows 8 mixed word cards; the learner
names the pieces and the meaning for each. Record the count correct.

## Practice E — Context-clue investigations (session 10)

**E1. Hidden definition.** "The **arboretum**, a garden of trees, was
quiet on Monday." What is an arboretum? What words told you?
**E2. Example clue.** "**Nocturnal** animals, like owls and bats, sleep
during the day." What does *nocturnal* mean?
**E3. Contrast clue.** "Unlike his **stingy** brother, Sam was
**generous** with his snacks." What does *generous* mean?
**E4. Affix tool.** "The baby was **fearless** near the gentle dog."
Break it, predict, check.
**E5. Root tool.** "They will **transport** the piano in a big truck."
You know *port* = carry (from *portable*). Predict, then check.
**E6. Multiple meanings.** "The **bark** was rough on the old tree."
"Do not **bark** at the mail carrier." Write what *bark* means in each
sentence and which words told you.
**E7. Case file.** Pick 2 words from "The Puzzle Box" you did not know at
first (*treacherous* is not in it — use *rusty, latch, horseshoe,
horseshoe, celebration*). For each: tool used, prediction, quoted
evidence.

## Practice F — Dictionary drills and rule-breaker games (session 12)

**F1. Entry reading.** Look up *rebuild, kindness, disappear*. For each,
write: syllable dots, stressed part, part of speech, meaning 1.
**F2. Guide words.** "The page runs from *plant* to *plate*. Is *play*
on this page? Is *please*?" Explain with ABC order.
**F3. Meaning picker.** Dictionary entry for *light* (noun: brightness;
adjective: not heavy). Which meaning fits "a light feather"? Which fits
"the light of the moon"?
**F4. Glossary race.** Using the adult-made 5-word glossary for "The
Puzzle Box," find all 5 meanings in under 3 minutes.
**F5. Rule-breaker sprint.** Read all 24 unit rule-breakers from cards;
the adult records the count read without hesitation. Repeat and beat
your own score.
**F6. See-say-spell-use.** Do all four steps for *enough, through,
thought, bought*.
**F7. Sentence writing.** Write 5 sentences, each using a different
rule-breaker correctly (*said, have, of, was, were, some, come, what,
where, again, laugh, would, could, should, friend, people, water, only,
because*).

## Retrieval openers (2 minutes, start of most sessions)

The adult picks one: (a) show 5 rule-breaker cards — read on sight;
(b) say a prefix — learner gives its meaning and one word; (c) say a
suffix — learner gives its job and one word; (d) clap the syllables of 3
unit words. Rotate daily; keep a simple tally so every word set cycles
through the unit.
