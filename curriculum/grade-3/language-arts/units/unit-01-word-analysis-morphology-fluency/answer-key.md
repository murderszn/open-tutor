# Answer Key — Unit 01: Word Analysis, Morphology, Vocabulary, Fluency

For the guiding adult only. Every item in the lessons, practice sets,
assessments, and project samples is solved here. Accept reasonable
wording that shows the same understanding.

## Lesson 01 — answer key

**Guided practice**
1. *lemon*: vowels e, o → lem | on → "lemon."
2. *tiger*: ti | ger (open first syllable, long i) → "tiger."
3. *dentist*: 2 syllables (chin drops twice: den-tist); vowels e, i prove it.
4. *magnet*: mag | net → "magnet."
5. BUTT-on is wrong: wrong stress and it does not sound like a word; check
   the vowel sounds (u, o) and re-blend: but-ton.
6. *garden*: gar | den; the a says /ar/ because of the r — the break is
   the same, only the vowel sound is r-colored.

**Independent practice**
1. pic-nic, bas-ket, nap-kin, si-lent (open i says its name).
2. in-sect, ab-sent, hap-pen, fin-ish.
3. carpet 2, pumpkin 2, sister 2, never 2.
4. *problem*: vowels o, e → prob-lem. *robot*: vowels o, o → ro-bot
   (open o says its name).
5. Two vowel sounds (a and e); every syllable has exactly one vowel sound,
   so two vowel sounds = two syllables.
6. Speed round: record count correct out of 6.

## Lesson 03 — answer key

**Guided practice**
1. *action*: suffix *-tion* ("shun"); ac-tion.
2. *division*: di-vi-sion; the *-sion* chunk says "zhun" here — still its
   own syllable.
3. *-able* = "can be": *breakable* = can be broken. *invisible*: in-
   (not) + vis + -ible (can be) = cannot be seen.
4. *helpful* = full of help; *helpless* = without help. Sample sentence:
   "The helpful neighbor carried our bags, but we felt helpless when the
   car would not start."
5. un + comfort + able = *uncomfortable* = not comfortable (not able to be
   comfortable).
6. *enjoyment*: en-joy-ment; *-ment* made it a noun (the thing resulting
   from enjoying).
7. Not exactly — *-ible* = "can be," but the root is an old Latin word, so
   in modern English *terrible* just means "very bad." Read the chunk,
   learn the whole word's meaning.

**Independent practice**
1. sta-tion, mo-tion, read-a-ble, pos-si-ble, pay-ment, treat-ment.
2. Latin: action, station, motion, decision, readable, invisible, payment,
   terrible, possible · -ful/-less: helpful, hopeless, careful, fearless,
   thankful · -ness: kindness, sadness, darkness, happiness.
3. painful → "full of pain"; careless → "without care"; darkness → "the
   state of being dark"; movement → "the act of moving."
4. *happiness, sadness* — they became nouns naming a state/quality.
5. *painful* = full of pain; *hopeless* = without hope. Sample: "The
   painful stubbed toe made him feel hopeless about the race."
6. *government*: gov-ern-ment, suffix *-ment*. *television*: tel-e-vi-sion,
   suffix *-sion*.
7. It always sounds the same ("shun"), so once you know the chunk you never
   guess it.

## Lesson 04 — answer key

**Guided practice**
1. *frigid* = very cold. Evidence: "made us zip our coats."
2. *bold* = brave, not shy. Clue type: contrast (*unlike the timid mouse*).
3. *fearless* = without fear. Check: "He was without fear on the high
   dive" — fits.
4. *portable* = able to be carried. Root *port* (carry), known from
   *transport*.
5. *observation* = the act of observing; watching closely and noticing.
   Tool 1 (*careful … of the ants*) and Tool 2 (*observe* + *-ation*) agree.
6. Tool 1 is fastest: "had stood for a thousand years" → very old. No
   other tool needed.
7. The adult used the wrong meaning of a multiple-meaning word and ignored
   the sentence. *Rough* describes the tree's covering, so *bark* here is
   the tree's skin — context picks which meaning.

**Independent practice**
1. *weary* = tired. Evidence: "sat down at once."
2. *relief* = the worried feeling going away; comfort after worry.
   Evidence: "when the test ended."
3. *prehistoric* = before written history; very ancient. (pre- = before +
   historic.)
4. *companion* = one who keeps you company; a friend alongside. (Root
   *company*.)
5. *generous* = giving, sharing. Clue: contrast with the brother who kept
   all the cookies.
6. Learner's three sentences — check each uses the intended clue type
   (hidden definition / example / contrast).
7. A first guess can be wrong (multiple-meaning words, misleading feel);
