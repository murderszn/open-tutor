# Unit 01 — Word Analysis, Morphology, Vocabulary, and Fluency

**Grade:** 3 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #20](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/20), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Big words are small words wearing costumes. In this unit the learner breaks
multisyllable words into readable parts, learns what the most common prefixes
and suffixes *mean* (not just how they look), uses Latin suffixes like *-tion*
and *-able*, figures out unfamiliar words with context clues and known roots,
looks up words in glossaries and beginning dictionaries, tames the
irregularly spelled words that refuse to follow rules, and reads grade-level
prose and poetry aloud with accuracy, rate, and expression.

## Essential question

How can knowing the parts inside a word help me read it — and understand it —
the first time I see it?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the grade-2 track:

- Blend and read one- and two-syllable words; long/short vowels and common
  vowel teams; final-e and vowel-consonant patterns
- Basic prefixes and suffixes from grade 2 (un-, re-, pre-, -ful, -less,
  -ed, -ing); compound words; grade-appropriate high-frequency words
- Reading aloud comfortably for 2–3 minutes at a stretch

The diagnostic re-teaches nothing here; Lesson 01 re-teaches multisyllable
breaking rather than assuming it is secure.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Decode multisyllable words by finding each vowel sound and breaking the
   word into readable syllables before blending them together (RF.3.3.c).
2. Identify the meaning of the most common prefixes — un- ("not" / "opposite
   of"), re- ("again"), dis- ("not" / "opposite of"), pre- ("before") — and
   predict the meaning of a new word when one is added to a known word
   (RF.3.3.a, L.3.4.b).
3. Decode words with common Latin suffixes (-tion, -sion, -able, -ible,
   -ment) and explain how derivational suffixes (-ful, -less, -ness) change a
   word's meaning (RF.3.3.a–b).
4. Determine or clarify the meaning of unfamiliar words using sentence-level
   context, a known affix on a known word, or a known root word — and verify
   with a glossary or beginning dictionary (L.3.4.a–d).
5. Read grade-appropriate irregularly spelled words and explain why they are
   "rule-breaker" words you remember rather than sound out (RF.3.3.d).
6. Read grade-level prose and poetry orally with accuracy, appropriate rate,
   and expression on repeated readings; use context to confirm or self-correct
   word recognition and understanding (RF.3.4.a–c); use new academic and
   domain vocabulary accurately in speaking and writing (L.3.6).

## Unit word sets (authored for this unit; reused across lessons and practice)

**Multisyllable (syllable-breaking):** picnic, basket, rabbit, lemon, robot,
tiger, paper, music, pencil, open, even, baby, happy, sunset, napkin,
pocket, follow, silent, magnet, dentist, absent, insect, problem, garden,
button, sister, happen, never, finish, carpet, pumpkin

**Prefixes:**
- un- ("not"/"opposite of"): unhappy, unkind, unlock, unsafe, unclear,
  unfinished, untie, unwrap
- re- ("again"): replay, rewind, rebuild, reread, rewrite, redo, reheat,
  return
- dis- ("not"/"opposite of"): disagree, disappear, dislike, dishonest,
  disapprove, disconnect
- pre- ("before"): preview, prepay, preheat, prepare, prefix, predict

**Suffixes:**
- Latin: action, station, nation, celebration, motion, division, vision,
  decision, television, readable, breakable, agreeable, comfortable,
  invisible, terrible, possible, payment, movement, treatment, enjoyment,
  statement, government
- Derivational: helpful, careful, thankful, painful (‑ful); hopeless,
  fearless, careless, helpless (‑less); kindness, sadness, darkness,
  happiness (‑ness)

**Irregularly spelled (rule-breakers):** said, have, of, was, were, some,
come, what, where, again, laugh, would, could, should, friend, people,
water, only, because, enough, thought, through, bought, does, gone

**Context/root-meaning:** agree/disagree, comfort/comfortable/uncomfortable,
help/helpful/helpless, care/careful/careless, kind/kindness, dark/darkness,
happy/unhappy/happiness, heat/preheat, build/rebuild, lock/unlock,
appear/disappear, like/dislike; root *port* (transport, portable); root
*scrib/script* (describe, prescription); temporal *before, after, during*;
spatial *between, beyond, across*

All word sets are original curriculum-authored lists; no worksheet or
published list is reproduced.

## 16-session pacing

Four sessions per week, 25–30 minutes each. Each session names its activity
and success criterion; repeated labels are never reused without a specific
new task.

| Session | Week | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | Lesson 01: Break and blend multisyllable words | Breaks 6 of 8 new multisyllable words into syllables and reads each correctly |
| 2 | 3 | Practice A: syllable sort, clap-and-break races, decodable sentences | Sorts 12 words by syllable count with ≤1 error; reads 4 sentences with ≤2 errors |
| 3 | 3 | Lesson 02: Prefixes un-, re-, dis-, pre- (with the word-part puzzle image) | Strips the prefix and names its meaning in 6 of 8 words; predicts meaning correctly in 6 of 8 |
| 4 | 3 | Practice B: prefix sort + build-a-word workshop with the three puzzle pieces | Builds 8 correct prefixed words; predicts 4 meanings accurately |
| 5 | 4 | Lesson 03: Latin suffixes and derivational suffixes (with the word-part puzzle image) | Reads and breaks 6 of 8 Latin-suffix words; explains the meaning change of 4 of 5 derivational-suffix pairs |
| 6 | 4 | Practice C: suffix sort and meaning-match | Sorts 12 suffix words correctly; matches 6 of 8 words to definitions |
| 7 | 4 | Practice D: word-part detective — mixed prefix + root + suffix | Breaks 6 of 8 mixed words into three parts; gives a sensible meaning for 6 of 8 |
| 8 | 4 | Fluency reading 1: repeated reading of "The Puzzle Box" (prose) | Reads with ≤4 errors on first read; improves accuracy and pace on the third read |
| 9 | 5 | Lesson 04: Figuring out word meanings — context, affix, root | Uses a stated strategy to infer 6 of 8 meanings; supports each with evidence |
| 10 | 5 | Practice E: context-clue investigations with unit word sets | Matches 8 of 10 meanings to evidence-based reasoning; cites the clue used |
| 11 | 5 | Lesson 05: Glossaries, dictionaries, and rule-breaker words | Finds 4 of 5 words in a beginning dictionary; reads 10 of 12 rule-breakers correctly |
| 12 | 5 | Practice F: dictionary drills + rule-breaker word games | Writes 4 correct dictionary entries (word, part of speech, meaning); spells 6 rule-breakers from memory |
| 13 | 6 | Lesson 06: Fluency with purpose — accuracy, rate, expression, self-correction | Reads the poem "Word Parts Parade" with expression marks honored; self-corrects 2+ errors using context |
| 14 | 6 | Project workshop 1: My Word-Parts Dictionary — draft 4+ entries | 4 entries each with parts, meanings, sentence, illustration slot |
| 15 | 6 | Project workshop 2 + review game: affix relay and rule-breaker bingo | Completes dictionary (8+ entries); scores ≥7/10 in the review game |
| 16 | 6 | Formative quiz ([week-04_quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md)) + fluency check | Quiz ≥ 27/31; reads "The Puzzle Box" final timing with ≤2 uncorrected errors |

Weeks 1–2 of the track are diagnostic/placement and are not part of this
unit.

## How lessons, practice, reading, investigation, and review fill the unit

Six explicit lessons (one per session on sessions 1, 3, 5, 9, 11, 13)
introduce each strategy; every lesson is followed or paired with a practice
session that applies it (sessions 2, 4, 6, 7, 10, 12). Connected reading
appears in sessions 2, 4, 8, 10, 13, and 16 (decodable sentences plus two
original passages re-read across the unit). The investigation is the
[My Word-Parts Dictionary project](project.md), built across the unit and
finished in sessions 14–15. Review is woven into sessions 7, 13, and 15;
retrieval practice opens most sessions with a 2-minute rule-breaker or
affix drill. Feedback happens the same day the adult reviews practice.
