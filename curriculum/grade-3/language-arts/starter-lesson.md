# Starter Lesson — Find the main idea and explain with details

**Status:** draft supplement. **Time:** three 30-minute sessions. **Goal:** Name an informational passage's main idea, select supporting details, and write a short explanation using evidence.

## Adult preparation and readiness

Use the original passage below. Readiness: learner can recall one fact after listening. Adult may read aloud; this is a comprehension activity, not a fluency test.

## Original passage: “Why Wetlands Matter”

> A wetland is an area where the ground is covered or soaked with water for at least part of the year. Wetland plants provide places for some animals to find food and shelter. Wetlands can also slow some floodwater by holding water like a shallow bowl. People study wetlands because water levels, plants, and animals change with the seasons. Not every wetland looks the same: some have open water, while others have grasses and muddy soil.

## Teach and model

Explain: the main idea tells what most of the passage teaches; details support or explain it. Model checking whether an idea covers several sentences.

**Worked example 1:** “Wetlands have different features” is supported by the final sentence about open water, grasses, and mud. It is a detail, not the broadest main idea.

**Worked example 2:** “Wetlands support living things and affect water” covers shelter/food and holding some floodwater. It is a reasonable main idea supported across the passage.

## Pacing and tasks

Session 1: adult reads and models main idea vs detail; guide tasks 1–2. Session 2: learner independently marks evidence in tasks 3–4; adult reviews. Session 3: write and revise tasks 5–6 and exit check.

1. **Guided:** Select a main idea: wetlands support life and hold water / all animals live in wetlands / rain always floods.
2. **Guided:** Identify one detail about animals and one detail about water.
3. **Independent:** Underline a detail that shows wetlands vary.
4. **Independent:** Mark supported or not supported: every wetland has open water; some animals find shelter in wetland plants; water levels can change seasonally.
5. **Adult-guided error check:** A reader chooses “all wetlands are open ponds.” Point to a sentence that corrects this.
6. **Application:** Write a 4–5 sentence explanation with main idea and two details.

**Answer checks:** 1) first choice; 2) plants offer food/shelter; wetlands can hold some floodwater; 3) last sentence; 4) not supported, supported, supported; 5) final sentence says some have grasses/muddy soil; 6) details must be accurate and support the main idea.

## Exit check and product

Ask learner to state the main idea and point to two details. Product: annotated passage and a revised paragraph. Sample: “Wetlands affect both animals and water. Some plants provide food and shelter. A wetland can hold some floodwater. Wetlands differ in their water and plants.”

## Review rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main idea | Selects a broad idea supported by multiple lines | Chooses with options | Reread each detail |
| Evidence | Uses two accurate details | Uses one accurate detail | Mark facts together |
| Writing | Organizes explanation and rereads for clarity | Dictates with frame | Adult scribes and learner revises orally |

Chunk the passage, define wetland with a simple verbal description, provide a sentence frame, and accept oral/dictated answers. No external text is required. This short passage does not represent all wetland types; avoid generalizing beyond its wording.
