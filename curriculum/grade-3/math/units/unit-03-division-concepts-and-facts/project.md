# Project — Fair-Share Picnic

**Sessions:** 14 (plan and build) and 15 (check, share, present)
**Time:** 30 minutes per session, plus presentation time
**Goal:** Plan a real picnic sharing plan for a group, using division for
every food, and present it so another person could run the picnic from
your plan alone.

## The job

You are planning a picnic for **24 guests at 6 tables** (4 guests per
table — check: 6 × 4 = 24 ✓). The food you must share fairly is:

- 48 sandwiches
- 72 juice boxes
- 36 apples
- 60 crackers, packed 10 crackers per bag (how many bags?)

Every guest must get the same amount of each food, and every table must
get the same number of bags. No guest is left out and nothing is left
unshared — all totals in this project share out exactly, by design.

## Deliverables

1. **Sharing plan table.** One row per food: the total, the number of
   shares (or the group size), the division equation, the quotient, and
   what the quotient counts (per guest, per table, or bags).
2. **Array drawing.** Draw the 6 tables as 6 rows and show one food's
   sharing as an array (for example, sandwiches: 6 rows with the per-table
   count in each row). Label rows, columns, and total.
3. **Fact-family card.** Pick one food's division and write all four
   family equations for it.
4. **Presentation.** Explain the plan aloud (or in writing) in this order:
   total → shares or group size → equation → quotient → what it counts →
   multiply-back check. Then answer one "what if" question from the adult
   (see below).

## "What if" questions (adult picks one for the presentation)

- What if 2 more guests arrive (26 guests)? Which foods still share
  fairly, and which do not? (Answer: none of the per-guest sharings work
  out exactly for 26 — 48 ÷ 26, 72 ÷ 26, and 36 ÷ 26 are not whole-number
  sharings — so the plan must change: add food or change the table plan.
  The learner is not expected to compute remainders, only to test with
  multiplication: no whole number times 26 gives 48, 72, or 36.)
- What if the sandwiches are packed 8 per tray instead of shared per
  guest? How many trays? (48 ÷ 8 = 6 trays.)
- What if there are 8 tables instead of 6? How many sandwiches per table?
  (48 ÷ 8 = 6 per table.)

## Materials and safety

Counters for every food (use counters, not real food, for the planning
work). Real food is optional, adult-supervised, and only food every guest
can safely eat; check allergies before any real sharing, and never share
food a guest cannot eat. No cooking, cutting, or heat is involved.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Full (3) | Partial (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sharing plan table (Objectives 1–2) | All four foods correct: total, shares/group size, equation, quotient, and meaning stated | Three foods correct, or meanings missing on one row | Two or fewer foods correct, or equations missing |
| Array drawing (Objective 2) | Rows, columns, and total labeled; drawing matches the equation | Drawing present but a label missing or one count off | No array, or array does not match any equation |
| Fact-family card (Objective 4) | All four equations correct for one food | Three equations correct | Two or fewer correct |
| Presentation + check (Objectives 5–6) | Explains in order, states what each quotient counts, multiplies back, answers the "what if" | Explains most steps, or check missing | Reads numbers only, no explanation or check |

**Scoring:** 12 = complete; 9–11 = complete with one re-teach target (name
it and revisit it in Session 16 review time); 8 or below = re-plan one
food with the adult before the unit assessment.

## Sample plan (teacher reference — not a learner answer sheet)

- Sandwiches: 48 ÷ 6 tables = 8 per table (6 × 8 = 48 ✓).
- Juice boxes: 72 ÷ 24 guests = 3 per guest (24 × 3 = 72 ✓).
- Apples: 36 ÷ 6 tables = 6 per table (6 × 6 = 36 ✓); or 36 ÷ 24 guests
  does not share out exactly — so apples are shared per table, and each
  table's 6 apples are then shared by its 4 guests only if the table
  agrees on a split (not required here). The plan must state which
  sharing it uses: per table.
- Cracker bags: 60 ÷ 10 per bag = 6 bags; 6 bags ÷ 6 tables = 1 bag per
  table (6 × 1 = 6 ✓).
- Array example: sandwiches as 6 rows × 8 per row = 48.
- Family example (sandwiches): 6 × 8 = 48, 8 × 6 = 48, 48 ÷ 6 = 8,
  48 ÷ 8 = 6.

Note for the adult: the apples row is the project's deliberate trap — a
learner who writes "36 ÷ 24 = 1 per guest with 12 left over" has spotted
a real problem, and that answer earns full credit for the row if they
then choose the per-table sharing and justify it. Do not mark the trap
wrong for noticing it.
