# Unit 03 — Division Concepts and Facts

**Grade:** 3 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #18](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/18), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Division is sharing fairly and counting the groups. If 24 muffins must feed
4 tables equally, each table gets 6 — and if muffins come in boxes of 6, then
24 muffins fill 4 boxes. It is the same division, 24 ÷ 4 = 6, answering two
different questions: *how many in each share?* and *how many shares?* In this
unit the learner shares counters one at a time around the plates, counts
groups of a fixed size, turns every division into the multiplication fact it
came from (find 32 ÷ 8 by asking what times 8 makes 32), finds unknown numbers
in multiplication and division equations, solves division word problems with
equal groups and arrays, and builds strategy-based fluency with quotients
within 100. No timed speed tests: fluency here means a reliable strategy for
every fact, not racing a clock.

## Essential question

How can sharing fairly, counting groups, and knowing multiplication facts
help us divide?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and Units 01–02:

- Equal groups and arrays as multiplication (Unit 02); products within 100,
  with secure strategies through the 6s and a working strategy for every fact
  through 10 × 10
- Skip counting by 2s, 3s, 5s, and 10s; repeated addition
- Addition and subtraction within 100; rounding to the nearest 10 (Unit 01,
  used only in retrieval warm-ups and reasonableness checks)
- Word problems with a symbol for the unknown number (Unit 02)

Each lesson's readiness check names the exact re-teach for a missing
prerequisite. This unit never asks the learner to divide by a number whose
multiplication fact they have no strategy for yet.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Interpret whole-number quotients two ways: as the number of objects in
   each share when a total is partitioned equally into a known number of
   shares, and as the number of shares when a total is partitioned into
   equal shares of a known size; write the matching division equation for a
   model or story. (3.OA.A.2)
2. Solve division word problems within 100 in situations involving equal
   groups, arrays, and measurement quantities, using drawings and equations
   with a symbol for the unknown number. (3.OA.A.3, division half)
3. Determine the unknown whole number in a multiplication or division
   equation relating three whole numbers (for example, 8 × ? = 48,
   5 = _ ÷ 3). (3.OA.A.4)
4. Understand division as an unknown-factor problem: find 32 ÷ 8 by finding
   the number that makes 32 when multiplied by 8; use fact families to move
   between the four related equations. (3.OA.B.6)
5. Divide fluently within 100 using strategies such as the relationship
   between multiplication and division; by the end of the unit, hold a
   reliable strategy for every quotient within 100 and know the quotients
   for facts through the 6s from memory. (3.OA.C.7)
6. Explain their reasoning with sharing, grouping, and fact-family language,
   and judge whether an answer is reasonable (multiply back, re-deal, or
   re-draw).

## Vocabulary (taught with objects first, word second)

division · divide · share equally · fair share · dividend · divisor ·
quotient · group · groups of · each · per · array · rows · columns ·
fact family · unknown factor · symbol for the unknown · multiply back ·
remainder-free sharing (every object placed; this unit uses only totals that
share out exactly — leftovers arrive in later work, not here)

Note on language: the adult may use *dividend*, *divisor*, and *quotient*,
but the learner is never required to produce those words to show
understanding. "The total," "the number of shares," and "how many in each
share" are always accepted.

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials
list): 100 small counters (beans, buttons, or interlocking cubes — no food
the learner is allergic to), paper plates or drawn circles for shares, index
cards, paper and crayons/pencils, a 10 × 10 multiplication chart (printed or
drawn), the generated illustration
[`assets/fair-share-picnic.png`](assets/fair-share-picnic.png) plus the
reproducible SVG [`assets/fact-family-triangle.svg`](assets/fact-family-triangle.svg),
blank fact-family cards, a number-line strip 0–120. No sharp tools; supervise
small parts. Real food is used only in the project, only with the adult, and
only food the learner can safely eat; counters work for every activity.

## How the 16 sessions fill the unit

Six lessons alone do not fill four weeks. Lessons introduce each idea once;
practice sessions (A, B, E, F) give the learner a second and third pass at
the same idea with new numbers; game sessions build strategy speed without
timing; review sessions force the learner to explain, not just answer; the
project applies all five objectives to one real plan; and the quiz and
assessment check the objectives, not the lessons. If a lesson's exit check
fails, the following practice session becomes re-teach time — see
[teacher-guide.md](teacher-guide.md) for the ready-to-move-on criteria.
