# Practice Sessions — Unit 03: Division Concepts and Facts

Each session is 30 minutes and opens with the 2-minute retrieval warm-up
from the unit pacing. The adult reviews every session the same day. Answers
are in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md#practice-sessions); do not read the key
before trying.

## Session A — Fair-share trays

*After Lesson 01. Goal: deal one at a time and check every share equal.*

1. Deal 20 counters into 5 shares. Write the equation and the quotient.
2. Deal 32 counters into 8 shares. Write the equation and the quotient.
3. Deal 42 counters into 6 shares. Write the equation and the quotient.
4. Deal 54 counters into 9 shares. Write the equation and the quotient.
5. Deal 36 counters into 4 shares. Write the equation and the quotient.
6. A class shares 48 crayons equally among 6 tables. How many crayons per
   table? Deal it, then write the equation.
7. Error analysis: a learner dealt 30 counters into 5 shares as
   8, 8, 8, 3, 3 and said "shared." Explain why this is not fair sharing,
   and re-deal it correctly.
8. Explain in one sentence why dealing one at a time guarantees fairness.

*Success criterion:* items 1–6 correct with equal shares shown; item 7's
re-deal gives 6 per share (30 ÷ 5 = 6).

## Session B — Group-count builders

*After Lesson 02. Goal: build groups of a fixed size and count the groups.*

1. Build groups of 4 from 28 counters. How many groups? Write the equation.
2. Build groups of 6 from 42 counters. How many groups? Write the equation.
3. Build groups of 7 from 49 counters. How many groups? Write the equation.
4. Build groups of 8 from 64 counters. How many groups? Write the equation.
5. Build groups of 9 from 72 counters. How many groups? Write the equation.
6. A teacher makes reading groups of 5 from 35 students. How many groups?
   Write the equation and say what the quotient counts.
7. For 36 ÷ 6 = 6, write one fair-share story and one group-count story,
   and say what the 6 counts in each.
8. Error analysis: a learner built groups of 6 from 54 counters and counted
   8 groups, then stopped with 6 counters left over. What went wrong, and
   how many groups are there really?

*Success criterion:* items 1–6 correct with the quotient's meaning stated
where a story is given; item 8 corrected to 9 groups (54 ÷ 6 = 9).

## Session C — Deal-and-count race

*Sessions 3 and 7 (game, no timer on the learner — the "race" is against
your own last round, and accuracy beats speed).*

**Round 1 — Deal race (after Lesson 01; replayed after Lesson 02 as the
group-count race).** The adult calls a total and a share count (or a group
size). The learner deals or builds it, writes the equation, and states the
meaning. Play 6 rounds; each round must be checked by multiplying back
before the next round starts. Sample rounds: 24 ÷ 4, 36 ÷ 6, 45 ÷ 5,
56 ÷ 8, 63 ÷ 7, 72 ÷ 9 (fair-share wording first, group-count wording on
the replay).

**Round 2 — Fact-family flip (after Lesson 03).** The adult shows a
fact-family card with one number covered (for example, 7, ?, 56). The
learner names the missing number and all four equations. Play 8 rounds;
sample families: (4, 8, 32), (6, 7, 42), (7, 8, 56), (9, 6, 54),
(8, 9, 72), (5, 9, 45), (7, 9, 63), (6, 9, 54).

*Success criterion:* at least 5 of 6 deal rounds and 6 of 8 flip rounds
correct, every one with a multiply-back or family check. A wrong round is
replayed, not skipped.

## Session D — Share, draw, explain review

*After Lessons 01–02. Goal: explain, not just answer.*

1. Draw 24 ÷ 6 as fair shares (6 plates). Label each plate's count and
   write the equation.
2. Draw 24 ÷ 6 as groups of 6. Label each group and write the equation.
3. Explain in two sentences why both drawings give quotient 6 but the 6
   means different things.
4. Solve 40 ÷ 5 two ways: by dealing and by thinking multiplication.
   Show both and say which you prefer and why.
5. Solve 56 ÷ 7 two ways: by group counting and by thinking
   multiplication. Show both.
6. True or false, with a reason: "Division always makes a smaller number."
   (Consider 45 ÷ 1 = 45 and 45 ÷ 45 = 1.)

*Success criterion:* drawings labeled, both meanings stated, and item 6
answered false with a counterexample (dividing by 1 leaves the total
unchanged).

## Session E — Unknown-number puzzles

*After Lesson 04. Goal: find the unknown in any spot and prove it.*

Solve each and prove it by putting the answer back into the original
equation:

1. 8 × ? = 72
2. ? × 7 = 49
3. 63 ÷ ? = 7
4. _ ÷ 6 = 8
5. 5 × ? = 45
6. ? ÷ 4 = 8
7. 81 ÷ ? = 9
8. _ ÷ 8 = 6
9. 9 × ? = 54
10. 56 ÷ ? = 8
11. Puzzle: I am thinking of a fact family. One equation is 7 × ? = 42.
    Write all four family equations with the unknown filled in.
12. Puzzle: _ ÷ 9 = 8 and 72 ÷ ? = 9 use the same family. Name the family
    and solve both.

*Success criterion:* items 1–10 correct with proofs; puzzles solved with
the full family named.

## Session F — Fluency stations

*After Lesson 06. Goal: a named strategy for every fact, checked by
multiplying back.*

**Station 1 — Think multiplication.** Solve by asking the unknown-factor
question: 36 ÷ 9, 42 ÷ 6, 54 ÷ 6, 72 ÷ 8, 81 ÷ 9, 28 ÷ 4.

**Station 2 — Skip-count the groups.** Solve by skip-counting and
counting counts: 45 ÷ 5, 63 ÷ 9, 27 ÷ 9, 48 ÷ 8, 35 ÷ 7, 24 ÷ 8.

**Station 3 — Break apart.** Solve by splitting the total into friendly
parts: 64 ÷ 8 (32 + 32), 56 ÷ 7 (28 + 28), 72 ÷ 9 (45 + 27), 48 ÷ 6
(30 + 18), 96 ÷ 8 (80 + 16), 63 ÷ 7 (35 + 28).

**Station 4 — Mixed review.** Solve any way you like, but name the
strategy and multiply back: 90 ÷ 10, 100 ÷ 10, 24 ÷ 4, 49 ÷ 7, 18 ÷ 3,
60 ÷ 6.

*Success criterion:* every station item correct with a named strategy and
a multiply-back check. Any missed item returns to its station for a
second pass before the quiz.
