## Ready to move on — what it looks like

- **After L1:** learner deals one at a time, checks every share equal, and
  multiplies back without prompting. If shares are uneven, do not advance
  to group counting — re-deal Session A until dealing is automatic.
- **After L2:** learner can answer "Do we know the number of shares, or
  the size of each group?" before computing, and states what each quotient
  counts. A bare number is not ready.
- **After L3–L4:** learner asks the unknown-factor question aloud and
  proves unknowns by substitution. If the learner still adds to undo
  division (? ÷ 7 = 6 → 13), re-teach L4's four hiding spots with the
  multiplication chart before L5.
- **After L6:** learner names a strategy for every fact and multiplies
  back. Speed is never the criterion; a slow correct answer with a named
  strategy passes, a fast bare answer does not.
- **Unit readiness:** quiz 9–10, or assessment 20–22, with the project at
  9+ rubric points. Below that, re-teach the missed objective's practice
  session, not the whole unit.

## Misconceptions and fixes

| Misconception | Fix |
|---|---|
| Dealing uneven piles and calling it sharing | Re-deal one at a time; no equation until every share is checked equal (L1). |
| Treating ÷ as subtraction (20 ÷ 4 → 16) | Rebuild as groups: 4 × 5 = 20 undoes 20 ÷ 4 = 5 (L1). |
| Answering the wrong question (shares vs. groups) | Ask the story question first, every time, before computing (L2, L5). |
| Echoing a visible number (56 ÷ 8 → 8) | Ask the unknown-factor question; put the candidate back in and check (L3). |
| Adding to undo division (? ÷ 7 = 6 → 13) | Undo with multiplication: 6 × 7 = 42 (L4). |
| Bare quotients with no meaning | Require the full sentence: "9 pencils per student" (L5). |
| "Close is correct" (54 ÷ 6 = 8) | Multiply back on every item: 8 × 6 = 48 ≠ 54 (L6). |
| Stopping a skip-count early | Count to the total, not to a familiar number; count the counts (L6). |

## Reteach paths

- **L1/L2 confusion:** return to counters for one full session; use only
  totals under 40 until dealing and grouping are each clean, then return
  to the lesson numbers.
- **L3/L4 unknowns:** keep the multiplication chart open for a full
  session; the chart is a tool, not cheating. Remove it only when the
  learner finds families without it.
- **L5 stories:** act out every story with counters before writing;
  stories the learner invents themselves stick better than stories they
  only read.
- **L6 fluency:** do not drill with a timer. Use Session F stations and
  the strategy card from L6's application; celebrate strategy choice,
  not speed.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** counters for every item; adult-scribed equations while the
  learner deals and speaks; multiplication chart always available;
  totals under 40 for re-teach.
- **Extension:** L6's 96 ÷ 8 break-apart work; the project's apples trap
  (36 shared per table vs. per guest); inventing hiding-spot puzzles for
  the adult (L4 application).
- **Language access:** "shares known" vs. "group size known" as the two
  story questions, said aloud before computing; pointing to shares and
  groups as they are counted.

## Notes on scope

- All division in this unit is exact: every total shares out with nothing
  left over. Remainders are deliberately not introduced here; a learner
  who notices the picnic apples do not split evenly per guest (project)
  has found a real boundary of this unit, and the teacher note there
  explains how to credit it.
- Quotients in the core work are one-digit numbers (dividends within 100,
  divisors 2–10). The only two-digit quotient, 96 ÷ 8 = 12, appears as an
  explicitly labeled extension in L6 and as a Session F station item;
  it previews Unit 04 and is never required for unit readiness.
- No timed tests appear anywhere in this unit, by design: the track's
  scope-and-sequence specifies strategy-based fluency for Units 02–04.
