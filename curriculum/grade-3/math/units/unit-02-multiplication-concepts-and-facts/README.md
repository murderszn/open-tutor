# Unit 02 — Multiplication Concepts and Facts

**Grade:** 3 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #18](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/18), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Multiplication is just counting equal groups quickly. A bag of 3 apples, another
bag of 3 apples, another bag of 3 apples — instead of adding 3 + 3 + 3 every
time, we learn to see "3 groups of 3" and know it is 9. In this unit the learner
builds equal groups with counters, draws arrays in rows and columns, solves word
problems with equal groups, arrays, and measurement quantities (writing equations
with a symbol for the unknown), discovers that facts turn around (4 × 6 is the
same as 6 × 4), breaks big arrays into smaller ones to learn new facts
(8 × 7 = 8 × 5 + 8 × 2), and builds fluent, strategy-based recall of products
within 100 — starting with the facts through the 6s and finishing with a working
strategy for every fact through 10 × 10. The adult names the properties
(commutative, distributive); the learner uses them.

## Essential question

How can equal groups and arrays help us find and remember multiplication facts?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2), the grade-2 track, and Unit 01:

- Rectangular arrays up to 5 × 5 as repeated addition (grade-2 bridge)
- Fluent addition and subtraction within 20; strategies within 100
- Skip counting by 2s, 5s, and 10s
- One- and two-step word problems within 100; equations with a symbol for the
  unknown
- Rounding to the nearest 10/100 from Unit 01 (used only in this unit's
  retrieval warm-ups and reasonableness checks, not as new instruction)

Each lesson's readiness check names the exact re-teach for a missing
prerequisite; no lesson assumes secure Unit 01 facts beyond rounding warm-ups.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Interpret products of whole numbers as equal groups and as arrays; write a
   matching multiplication equation for a model or story, including a symbol
   for the unknown where the problem asks for it. (3.OA.A.1)
2. Solve word problems within 100 in situations involving equal groups, arrays,
   and measurement quantities, using drawings and equations with a symbol for
   the unknown number. (3.OA.A.3)
3. Use the commutative property ("turn-around facts") and the distributive
   strategy ("break an array apart") to find products they do not yet know from
   memory. (3.OA.B.5)
4. Multiply fluently within 100 with strategies; know products through the 6s
   from memory by the end of the unit and hold a reliable strategy for every
   fact through 10 × 10. (3.OA.C.7)
5. Explain their reasoning with equal-group and array language, and judge
   whether an answer is reasonable (estimate, check by turnaround, or re-draw).

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials
list): 50+ small counters (beans, buttons, or interlocking cubes — no food the
learner is allergic to), index cards, paper and crayons/pencils, a 10 × 10
multiplication chart (printed or drawn), the generated illustration
[`assets/array-market-stand.png`](assets/array-market-stand.png) plus the
reproducible SVG [`assets/array-break-apart.svg`](assets/array-break-apart.svg),
blank fact-family cards, a number-line strip 0–120. No sharp tools; supervise
small parts.
