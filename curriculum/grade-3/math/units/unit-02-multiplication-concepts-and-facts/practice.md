# Practice Sessions — Unit 02

Six named practice sessions referenced from the [unit README](README.md)
pacing table. Each session is 30 minutes: 2-minute retrieval warm-up, 24
minutes of stations or games, 4-minute wrap-up where the learner states one
thing they can now do faster or more surely. Materials are household items;
the adult keeps score only to celebrate, never to rank.

## Session A: Equal-groups trays

**Covers:** Objective 1 (equal groups, equations, products). **Needs:**
counters, index cards, paper.

1. **Warm-up:** "Show 2 groups of 7 with counters. Equation?" (2 × 7 = 14.)
2. **Tray sort (12 min):** the adult writes products (12, 15, 18, 20, 24) on
   five cards. The learner builds each product as equal groups on a "tray"
   (a sheet of paper), choosing their own group size, then writes the
   equation. Two different groupings for 24 earns a star.
3. **Story dealer (10 min):** the learner draws a story card ("6 nests, 4
   eggs each") from a homemade deck, builds it, writes the equation, and
   states the product. Six cards, adult checks each.
4. **Wrap-up:** "Which product was hardest to build? Why?"

*Success criterion:* 4 of 5 tray products built with correct equal groups and
equations; story equations match the built groups.

## Session B: Array builders

**Covers:** Objectives 1–2 (arrays, rows × columns). **Needs:** grid paper or
plain paper, crayons, counters, the
[array-market-stand.png](assets/array-market-stand.png) illustration.

1. **Warm-up:** "Draw a 4 × 3 array from memory." (12 dots, rows labeled.)
2. **Market copy (12 min):** look at the illustration's apple tray. The
   learner draws the same 5 × 5 array on paper, labels rows and columns, and
   writes 5 × 5 = 25. Then the orange crate: 4 × 4 = 16. (Text-only
   alternative in Lesson 02 covers learners who cannot see the image.)
3. **Array auction (10 min):** the adult calls products (18, 20, 28, 36); the
   learner draws *any* array that makes that product and writes the equation.
   Bonus for two different arrays for one product (e.g., 3 × 6 and 2 × 9 for
   18).
4. **Wrap-up:** "Show me with your finger: which way do rows go?"

*Success criterion:* arrays drawn as true rectangles with straight, equal
rows; rows counted before the × in every equation.

## Session C: Skip-count race and multiplication war

**Covers:** Objectives 1, 4 (skip counting, early fluency). **Needs:** a deck
of number cards 0–10 (two sets), counters.

1. **Warm-up:** count by 3s to 30 as a race against the adult's slow
   finger-tap.
2. **Skip-count race (10 min):** the adult names a group size (2, 3, 4, 5,
   6); the learner skip counts as far as they can in 30 seconds *without a
   timer shown* — the adult just listens and notes the farthest correct
   landing. Best of three rounds per group size.
3. **Multiplication war (12 min):** each player flips two cards, multiplies
   them (counters allowed), and the higher product wins the round. The winner
   must *say* the equation aloud. First to 10 round-wins takes the game; ties
   replay.
4. **Wrap-up:** "Which fact did you win with twice? Do you know it from
   memory now?"

*Session 7 replays this session as "Array race": same war rules, but each
product must also be sketched as a quick array before the round counts.*

*Success criterion:* products found with a named strategy (skip count,
repeated add, known fact) — wild guesses replay the round.

## Session D: Draw, label, explain review

**Covers:** Objectives 1–2, 5 (representation and explanation). **Needs:**
paper, crayons.

1. **Warm-up (Unit 01 retrieval):** "Round 47 to the nearest 10; estimate
   47 + 28." (50; about 80.)
2. **Teach-back gallery (20 min):** the learner makes three "teaching
   posters," one per prompt, each with a drawing, a labeled equation, and one
   sentence of explanation:
   - "What 4 × 5 means" (equal groups)
   - "How to read an array" (rows × columns, labeled)
   - "How I check a word problem" (draw → equation with □ → solve → label)
3. **Gallery walk (6 min):** the learner presents each poster to the adult in
   60 seconds, pointing at the drawing while explaining.

*Success criterion:* all three posters have a correct drawing, a correct
labeled equation, and an explanation the adult can follow without guessing.

## Session E: Break-apart puzzles

**Covers:** Objective 3 (distributive strategy). **Needs:** paper, the
[array-break-apart.svg](assets/array-break-apart.svg) diagram, counters.

1. **Warm-up:** "You know 5 × 8 = 40. Find 6 × 8." (One more 8: 48.)
2. **Cut lab (14 min):** for each target fact (7 × 6, 8 × 7, 9 × 6, 6 × 8),
   the learner draws the array, draws the cut line, labels both pieces with
   mini-equations, and writes the total. The adult checks cuts keep rows
   whole.
3. **Mystery cut (8 min):** the adult shows a *solved* break-apart with one
   piece hidden: "8 × 9 = 8 × 5 + 8 × □. What is □? What is the total?"
   (□ = 4; 40 + 32 = 72.) Four rounds.
4. **Wrap-up:** "Which cut is your favorite for hard facts? Why?"

*Success criterion:* 3 of 4 cut labs correct with both pieces labeled and
added; no jagged cuts.

## Session F: Fact stations

**Covers:** Objective 4 (fluency within 100). **Needs:** index cards, 10 × 10
chart, timer (adult-held, never displayed to the learner).

1. **Warm-up:** oral "Know it / Strategy" sort for the 6s row.
2. **Station 1 — Card sort (8 min):** 20 fact cards (mixed 0–9). Sort into
   "Know it" and "Strategy" piles; for each Strategy card, say the strategy
   aloud while solving.
3. **Station 2 — Chart detective (8 min):** on the 10 × 10 chart, the adult
   covers 5 products with sticky notes; the learner finds each using a
   neighbor fact (turnaround, one-more-group, or break-apart) and explains
   the route.
4. **Station 3 — Teach the trick (6 min):** the learner teaches the adult one
   nines fact using "tens minus the number," with the adult playing a
   confused student who asks "why?"
5. **Wrap-up:** update the personal "Know it / Strategy" card from Lesson 06;
   count how many facts moved to "Know it" since Session 12.

*Success criterion:* at least 16 of 20 cards correct with a strategy named for
every non-memory answer; no timed pressure at any station.
