# Project — The Array Market

**Sessions:** 14 (plan and build) and 15 (check, price, and present), 30
minutes each · **Covers objectives:** 1, 2, 3, 5
**Pairs with:** [Lesson 02](lessons/lesson-02-arrays-rows-and-columns.md)
(arrays), [Lesson 03](lessons/lesson-03-multiplication-word-problems.md) (word
problems with unknowns)

## Story

The Array Market from our lessons is opening a new stall, and the learner is
the stall manager. The manager's job: design the fruit display so every tray
is a perfect array, write the stock equations, price the trays, and present
the stall plan to the "owner" (the adult). A tray that is not a true array —
ragged rows, uneven columns — cannot go on the stall.

## Materials

Paper and crayons, counters or dried beans for mock fruit, index cards,
the [array-market-stand.png](assets/array-market-stand.png) illustration for
inspiration, price tags (small paper slips). No sharp tools; supervise small
parts.

## Part 1 — Plan the stall and build the trays (Session 14)

1. **Choose 3 fruits** (real or invented) and design one tray per fruit as an
   array with at least 3 rows and 3 columns. Draw each tray, label its rows
   and columns, and write the stock equation (rows × columns = total pieces).
2. **Build** each tray with counters on the table. The adult inspects: are
   the rows straight and equal? Fix any ragged rows.
3. **Write a restocking word problem** for one tray: "The stall sold out of
   ___. The manager orders ___ trays with ___ pieces each. How many pieces?"
   Include a symbol for the unknown in the equation and solve it.
4. **Turnaround check:** pick one tray, write its turnaround equation, and
   explain in one sentence why the stock count is unchanged.

## Part 2 — Check, price, and present (Session 15, after the quiz)

1. **Break-apart audit:** the adult picks the learner's biggest tray. The
   learner breaks that array into two friendly pieces on paper (draw the cut,
   label both mini-equations, add the parts) to prove the stock count a
   second way.
2. **Price the trays:** assign each tray a per-piece price in whole cents
   (e.g., 3¢ per plum). Compute each tray's total value with multiplication
   and write it on a price tag. (Products stay within 100; the adult helps
   pick prices that do.)
3. **Present the stall (5 minutes):** walk the adult through the display —
   point at each tray, read its equation, explain one break-apart, and read
   the restocking problem aloud with its solution.
4. **Manager's note:** write 3–4 sentences: which tray was hardest to keep as
   a true array, which strategy (turnaround or break-apart) helped most, and
   one fact you now know from memory that you did not know in Session 1.

## Deliverables

1. Stall plan drawing: 3 labeled array trays with stock equations.
2. One restocking word problem with equation (symbol for the unknown), drawing,
   solution, and label.
3. Break-apart audit sheet for the biggest tray (cut drawn, both pieces
   labeled, total shown).
4. Three price tags with per-piece price and total value.
5. Manager's note (3–4 sentences).

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (3) | Developing (2) | Starting (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| True arrays | All 3 trays are true arrays: straight, equal rows and columns; rows/columns labeled | 2 true arrays; 1 has an uneven row or missing label | Arrays are scattered groups, not rectangles |
| Equations | Every tray has a correct rows × columns equation with the right product | Most equations correct; one product or factor error | Equations missing or do not match the drawings |
| Word problem | Story, drawing, equation with □/letter, solution, and label all present and consistent | One element missing or inconsistent (e.g., unlabeled answer) | Story unsolved or equation missing |
| Break-apart audit | Cut keeps rows whole; both pieces labeled with correct mini-equations; parts sum to the tray total | Cut drawn; one piece mislabeled or addition error | No valid cut, or pieces not added |
| Presentation & note | Explains one strategy clearly; note names the hardest tray, the most helpful strategy, and one new memory fact | Explanation vague; note missing one of the three reflections | Cannot explain the strategy; note missing |

**Scoring:** 13–15 = ready for Unit 03; 10–12 = solid, note one reteach item
for Session 16 warm-ups; below 10 = revisit the flagged criterion with the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) reteach map before the unit assessment.

## Adult notes

- Keep prices small (2¢–5¢ per piece) so totals stay within 100.
- If the learner's biggest tray exceeds 10 × 10, cap the drawing at 10 columns
  and note "the rest continues" — arrays bigger than 10 × 10 belong to later
  grades.
- The presentation is the assessment of Objective 5 (explaining reasoning);
  listen for equal-group and array language, not just right answers.
