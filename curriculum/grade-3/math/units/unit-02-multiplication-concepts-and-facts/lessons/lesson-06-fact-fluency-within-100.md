# Lesson 06 — Fact Fluency Within 100

**Session:** 12 · **Time:** 30 minutes · **Goal:** I can find any product
within 100 using a strategy, I know the facts through the 6s from memory, and
I have a personal plan for learning the rest.

**Materials:** index cards, 10 × 10 multiplication chart, paper and pencil.

**Retrieval warm-up (2 min):** "Break 7 × 8 into friendly pieces." (e.g.,
7 × 5 + 7 × 3 = 35 + 21 = 56.) "What is the turnaround of 8 × 7?" (7 × 8 =
56 — same answer, two strategies agreeing.)

## Readiness check (3 min)

Quick oral round — no paper, no timer: "6 × 4? 3 × 5? 2 × 9? 5 × 5? 4 × 7?"
If most answers come within a few seconds *with* a strategy named ("doubles,"
"turnaround," "break apart"), the learner is ready for fluency work. If they
are still building every fact from scratch, that is fine — this lesson is
about collecting strategies, not performing speed.

## Explicit teaching (8 min)

**Fluency** means finding the answer quickly *and* correctly *with
understanding* — it is not a race, and this track never uses timed speed
tests. A fluent mathematician has a **strategy for every fact** and **memory
for the easy ones**. Here is the strategy toolbox, in the order we learned
them:

| Strategy | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Zeros and ones | anything × 0 or × 1 | 7 × 0 = 0 (seven groups of nothing); 7 × 1 = 7 |
| Doubles | × 2, and × 4 as double-double | 6 × 4 = double 6 × 2 = 12 → 24 |
| Fives | half of the tens fact | 6 × 5 = half of 6 × 10 = 60 → 30 |
| Nines | tens minus the number | 9 × 7 = 70 − 7 = 63 |
| Turnaround | the hard order | 8 × 3 → 3 × 8 = 24 |
| Break apart | big unknowns | 7 × 8 = 7 × 5 + 7 × 3 = 56 |
| Square numbers | the diagonal | 6 × 6 = 36, 7 × 7 = 49, 8 × 8 = 64, 9 × 9 = 81 |

The memory goal for **this unit**: all products through the **6s** from memory
(everything with both factors ≤ 6, plus the strategies above for the rest).
The year-end goal (standard 3.OA.C.7) is *all* one-digit products from memory —
Units 03–04 keep building it. Make a two-column "Know it / Strategy" chart on
an index card and sort today's facts into it honestly.

## Worked example 1 — 9 × 7 with the nines strategy

1. The nines rule: 9 × *n* = (10 × *n*) − *n*.
2. 10 × 7 = 70. Subtract one 7: 70 − 7 = 63.
3. **9 × 7 = 63.**
4. Check with break-apart: 9 × 5 + 9 × 2 = 45 + 18 = 63. ✓ Two strategies,
   one answer.
5. Common slip: 9 × 7 = 70 − 1 = 69. The subtraction removes one *group of 7*,
   not one — "take away a 7, not a 1."

## Worked example 2 — 6 × 7 with break-apart

1. 6 × 7 is past the 6s memory line? No — 6 × 7 has a factor 7, so it needs a
   strategy (memory goal is both factors ≤ 6).
2. Break 7 into 5 + 2: 6 × 5 = 30, 6 × 2 = 12.
3. 30 + 12 = 42. **6 × 7 = 42.**
4. Check with turnaround + fives: 7 × 6 → 7 × 5 = 35, plus one more 7:
   35 + 7 = 42. ✓
5. Write 6 × 7 on the "Strategy" card for now; after a week of practice it
   moves to "Know it."

## Guided practice (with hints)

Name the strategy first, then solve. The adult gives the hint.

1. **4 × 9** — which strategy?
   *(Hint: nines rule — 40 − 4.)*
2. **7 × 5** — which strategy?
   *(Hint: fives — half of 70.)*
3. **8 × 6** — which strategy?
   *(Hint: break 8 into 5 + 3, or turn it around to 6 × 8 and break the 8.)*
4. **0 × 9** and **1 × 9** — explain each in equal-group words.
   *(Hint: "zero groups of 9" and "one group of 9.")*
5. **7 × 7** — find it two ways.
   *(Hint: break the second 7 into 5 + 2, then try 4 + 3, and compare the
   two routes.)*

## Independent practice

6. Solve with a named strategy: **3 × 8**, **9 × 5**, **6 × 6**, **4 × 7**.
7. Which of these do you know from memory? **5 × 6, 2 × 9, 4 × 4, 6 × 3.**
   Mark each K (know) or S (strategy).
8. Fill the missing products: 7 × 8 = __, 8 × 8 = __, 9 × 9 = __. Show the
   strategy for each.
9. True or false: **"6 × 9 = 54 because 6 × 10 = 60 and 60 − 6 = 54."**
   Explain.
10. Make your "Know it / Strategy" card for all facts with a 7, 8, or 9 in
    them (21 facts). Be honest — strategies count as success.
11. Error analysis: Dan says **"8 × 5 = 45 because 8 × 4 = 32 and you add
    8."** Find the arithmetic slip and fix it. (8 × 4 = 32; 32 + 8 = 40, not
    45.)

## Application

Teach a younger sibling, stuffed animal, or the adult one strategy from the
toolbox (your choice). Use a real fact, show the steps, and explain *why* the
strategy works — not just *how*. The adult scores your explanation with the
Lesson 06 rubric line in the [answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-06).

## Exit check

Answer these 10 facts — untimed, strategies welcome, name the strategy for at
least 4:

2 × 7 · 5 × 8 · 9 × 4 · 6 × 6 · 3 × 9 · 7 × 4 · 8 × 5 · 9 × 9 · 4 × 6 · 7 × 3

*Success criterion:* at least 8 of 10 correct, with a sensible strategy named
for the 7s/8s/9s. Wrong answers with *no* strategy named signal "still
building" — that is Session 13's job, not a failure.

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** strategy names on a visible anchor chart ("Nines:
  tens minus the number"); the learner points to the strategy before solving.
- **Alternate response mode:** oral round with counters available — build any
  fact you cannot recall within 10 seconds.
- **Extension:** learn the 7s, 8s, and 9s from memory this week using
  turnaround + break-apart as the *path* to memory (say the strategy aloud
  each time until the answer arrives before the strategy does). Prove 12 × 12
  = 144 with break-apart (12 × 10 + 12 × 2) — a peek at grade-4 thinking.

## Teacher note

Protect two things fiercely: (1) **no timed tests** — the scope-and-sequence
bans them; speed anxiety destroys the strategy habit this unit built. (2)
**"Know it" must be honest** — a learner who claims memory but silently
re-derives is fine; a learner who guesses is not. The exit check's 8/10 bar is
diagnostic, not a grade. Facts through the 6s from memory is the unit goal;
everything else is "strategy-secure," which fully meets the unit's fluency
objective. Answer key: [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-06).
