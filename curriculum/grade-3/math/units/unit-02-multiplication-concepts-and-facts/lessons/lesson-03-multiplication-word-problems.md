# Lesson 03 — Multiplication Word Problems

**Session:** 8 · **Time:** 30 minutes · **Goal:** I can solve a word problem
with equal groups, an array, or a measurement quantity by drawing it and
writing an equation with a symbol for the unknown.

**Materials:** paper and pencil, counters.

**Retrieval warm-up (2 min):** "Draw a 3 × 4 array from memory. What is the
product?" (12.) "Round 96 to the nearest 10." (100 — watch the 96 → 100
carry.)

## Readiness check (3 min)

Ask: "Write an equation with a symbol for the unknown for this story: *Mia has
some stickers. She gets 5 more. Now she has 12.*" (□ + 5 = 12, or
*n* + 5 = 12.) If the symbol-for-the-unknown habit is gone, re-teach it with
two addition stories before introducing multiplication stories.

## Explicit teaching (8 min)

Word problems hide equal groups in three costumes:

1. **Equal groups:** "4 bags with 3 apples *in each bag*." Words like *each*,
   *per*, and *every* are the giveaway.
2. **Arrays:** "3 rows with 6 chairs *in each row*." Anything lined up in rows
   and columns.
3. **Measurement quantities:** "4 jumps of 3 on the number line — where do you
   land?" or "3 ribbons, each 5 inches long." Same structure, measured units
   instead of objects.

The problem-solving steps never change:

1. **Read and retell** the story in your own words.
2. **Draw** the groups, the array, or the jumps.
3. **Write the equation** with a symbol for the unknown — □, ?, or a letter
   like *n* — standing for the answer you are finding.
4. **Solve and label** the answer ("24 crayons," not just 24).
5. **Check** by re-reading: does the drawing match the story?

## Worked example 1 — Equal groups: crayon boxes

*There are 6 boxes of crayons. Each box holds 4 crayons. How many crayons in
all?*

1. Retell: 6 groups, 4 in each group. Unknown: the total.
2. Draw: 6 small boxes with 4 dots in each.
3. Equation: **6 × 4 = □** (the □ stands for the total crayons).
4. Solve: skip count "4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24." □ = 24. Answer: **24 crayons.**
5. Check: 6 groups of 4 is 24 — draw matches, equation matches, label is on.
6. Common slip: 6 + 4 = 10. Ask: "Does 10 crayons make sense for 6 *boxes*?"
   The estimate habit (Unit 01) catches it — 6 boxes must hold more than 10.

## Worked example 2 — Measurement quantity: number-line jumps

*Leo the frog jumps 5 inches each jump. He makes 3 jumps. Where does he land?*

1. Retell: 3 jumps, 5 inches each jump. Unknown: the landing spot.
2. Draw: a number line, 3 jumps of 5.
3. Equation: **3 × 5 = *n*** (a letter works as the unknown too).
4. Solve: "5, 10, 15." *n* = 15. Answer: **15 inches.**
5. Check with repeated addition: 5 + 5 + 5 = 15. ✓
6. Notice: the units are inches, not frogs. The label follows the *jump size*,
   not the jumper.

## Guided practice (with hints)

Work through these together; the adult gives the hint, the learner draws and
writes.

1. *There are 5 plates. Each plate has 2 cookies. How many cookies?*
   *(Hint: draw the plates first, then fill each one — the equation is
   5 × 2 = □.)*
2. *A garden has 4 rows of flowers with 7 flowers in each row. How many
   flowers?*
   *(Hint: this is an array story — draw the rows, then write rows ×
   columns.)*
3. *Maya walks 6 blocks to school. Each block is the same length as 2
   house-lengths. How many house-lengths is her walk?*
   *(Hint: measurement quantity — 6 jumps of 2: 6 × 2 = ?)*
4. *There are 3 × 8 = □ eggs in egg cartons. Write a one-sentence story for
   this equation.*
   *(Hint: give the 3 a job — 3 cartons — and the 8 a job — 8 eggs each.)*
5. Error analysis: For problem 1, Noah wrote **5 + 2 = 7 cookies**. Explain
   what Noah missed and write the correct equation.
   *(Hint: how many cookies are really on 5 plates?)*

## Independent practice

6. *A baker packs 7 bags with 3 muffins in each bag. How many muffins?* Draw,
   write the equation with □, solve, and label.
7. *The classroom library has 5 shelves. Each shelf holds 8 books. How many
   books?* Draw as an array, write the equation, solve.
8. *A caterpillar crawls 4 centimeters each minute for 6 minutes. How far does
   it crawl?* (Measurement quantity — number-line jumps welcome.)
9. Write a word problem for **4 × 9 = □** about something you like. Then
   solve it.
10. *There are 2 teams with 9 players each. How many players?* Solve two ways:
    a drawing and skip counting.
11. Challenge: *A sticker book has 3 pages. Each page has 4 rows of 2
    stickers.* How many stickers in all? (Two multiplications — draw it.)

## Application

The Array Market is restocking: the manager orders **8 crates of oranges with
6 oranges in each crate**, and **5 baskets of plums with 9 plums in each
basket**. Which fruit did she order more of? Show a drawing and an equation
for each fruit, then write one sentence comparing the totals.

## Exit check

1. *There are 4 shelves with 6 plants on each shelf. How many plants?* Write
   the equation with a symbol for the unknown and solve.
2. *Sam hops 3 times, 7 inches each hop. How far?* Draw the jumps and write
   the equation.

*Success criterion:* equation uses a symbol for the unknown, drawing matches
the story, answer is labeled. If the learner solves without drawing, require
the drawing anyway — the drawing is the thinking, not decoration.

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** the adult reads the problem aloud and the learner
  retells it ("6 groups of 4") before anything is written; act out the story
  with counters.
- **Alternate response mode:** build the story with counters, point to the
  unknown, and answer orally; the adult scribes the equation.
- **Extension:** change Worked example 1 to 6 boxes of 40 crayons. Estimate
  first (Unit 01 rounding: about 6 × 40 = 240), then solve exactly (240).
  (6 × 40 = 240 — the tens-as-units bridge from Unit 01.)

## Teacher note

The most damaging habit is **keyword hunting** ("each means multiply" applied
blindly). It works here by luck and fails by Unit 04's two-step problems.
Insist on the retell-and-draw routine for every problem this week; speed comes
later. Also watch **unlabeled answers** — "24" with no unit is not a finished
answer in this unit. Answer key:
