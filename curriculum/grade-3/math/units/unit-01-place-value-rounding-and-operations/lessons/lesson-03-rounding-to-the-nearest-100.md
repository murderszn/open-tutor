# Lesson 03 — Round to the Nearest 100

**Session:** 8 · **Time:** 30 minutes · **Goal:** I can round three-digit
numbers to the nearest hundred using benchmark hundreds and the halfway
landmark.

**Materials:** number-line strip (0–1000 by hundreds), the reproducible SVG
[`rounding-number-line-10.svg`](../assets/rounding-number-line-10.svg) for the
230–260 zoom view, digit cards, paper and pencil.

## Readiness check (3 min)

Count by 100s from 100 to 900. Then: "What are the two benchmark hundreds that
348 sits between?" (300 and 400.) If the learner cannot name hundred neighbors,
do five minutes of "hundred neighbors" before rounding.

## Explicit teaching (8 min)

Rounding to the nearest **hundred** works exactly like rounding to the nearest
ten — only the landmarks change. The number lives between two **benchmark
hundreds** (300 and 400, for example). The **halfway point** is 50 into the
stretch (350 between 300 and 400). Before halfway → round down; at or past
halfway → round up.

Now the **tens digit** decides: tens 0–4 rounds down, tens 5–9 rounds up.
Exactly halfway (tens digit 5, ones anything... precisely: the last two digits
form 50) always rounds **up** — 350 → 400, 750 → 800, same agreement as last
lesson.

Three steps: (1) name the two benchmark hundreds, (2) find the halfway point
(the lower hundred + 50), (3) before halfway → down, at/past → up.

## Worked example 1 — Round 372 to the nearest 100

1. Benchmark hundreds: 300 and 400.
2. Halfway: 350.
3. 372 is past 350 → round **up**. 372 ≈ 400.
4. Digit check: tens digit 7 (5 or more) → round up. **372 rounds to 400.**

## Worked example 2 — Round 845 to the nearest 100

1. Benchmark hundreds: 800 and 900.
2. Halfway: 850.
3. 845 is before 850 → round **down**. 845 ≈ 800.
4. Digit check: tens digit 4 (4 or less) → round down. **845 rounds to 800.**
5. Edge case: **750** is exactly halfway between 700 and 800 → rounds **up**
   to 800.

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. Round **260** to the nearest 100. *(Hint: neighbors 200 and 300 — halfway
   is...?)*
2. Round **481** to the nearest 100. *(Hint: which benchmark is 481 nearer?)*
3. Round **735** to the nearest 100. *(Hint: look at the tens digit.)*
4. Round **550** to the nearest 100. *(Hint: exactly halfway — our rule?)*
5. Round **899** to the nearest 100. *(Hint: it is past 850 — how far can it
   go?)*

## Independent practice

6. Round **318** to the nearest 100.
7. Round **449** to the nearest 100.
8. Round **672** to the nearest 100.
9. Round **750** to the nearest 100.
10. Round **903** to the nearest 100.
11. Round **585** to the nearest 100.

## Application

A library has **486** picture books. The librarian wants one round number for
the newsletter. Round 486 to the nearest 100 and write the newsletter sentence.

## Exit check

1. Round **634** to the nearest 100.
2. Round **275** to the nearest 100.

*Success criterion:* both correct with the halfway point named. If 275 → 200,
the learner is rounding to the nearest *ten* by habit — have them circle the
tens digit and ask "which landmark set am I in?"

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** the adult marks the benchmarks on the number line; the
  learner places a counter on the number and walks it to the closer hundred.
- **Alternate response mode:** hold up hundred cards (300/400) instead of
  writing.
- **Extension:** explain why rounding 499 to the nearest 100 gives 500 but
  rounding 499 to the nearest 10 gives 500 too — and find a number where the
  two answers differ (e.g., 451: nearest 100 → 500, nearest 10 → 450).

## Teacher note

The halfway-to-100 cases (550, 750) are where digit-rule-only learners wobble,
because "5 rounds up" feels arbitrary without the number line. Always anchor
halfway on the line first, then confirm with the tens digit. Answer key:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-03).
