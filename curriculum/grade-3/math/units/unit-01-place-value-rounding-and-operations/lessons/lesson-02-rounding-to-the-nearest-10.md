# Lesson 02 — Round to the Nearest 10

**Session:** 5 · **Time:** 30 minutes · **Goal:** I can round two- and
three-digit numbers to the nearest ten using a number line, and explain why
the halfway number rounds up.

**Materials:** number-line strip (0–1000 by hundreds with ten subdivisions),
digit cards, the generated illustration
[`market-rounding-stand.png`](../assets/market-rounding-stand.png), paper and
pencil.

## Readiness check (3 min)

Count by 10s from 230 to 280 aloud: 230, 240, 250, 260, 270, 280. Then: "What
are the two round tens that 47 sits between?" (40 and 50.) If the learner
cannot name the tens neighbors, practice "tens neighbors" for two-digit numbers
for five minutes before rounding.

## Explicit teaching (8 min)

**Rounding** means replacing a number with the nearest "easy" number — a
**round ten** like 40, 50, 380, or 390. We round when we want a quick picture
of a quantity instead of its exact count.

Picture the number on a **number line**. The number lives between two round
tens. Find the **halfway point** — the number exactly in the middle. If our
number is **before** halfway, it is closer to the lower ten: **round down**. If
it is **at or past** halfway, it is closer to the upper ten: **round up**.

The **ones digit** tells us which side we are on: 0–4 rounds down, 5–9 rounds
up. Exactly halfway (ones digit 5) always rounds **up** — that is the convention
we use everywhere in this unit (46 → 50, 375 → 380).

Three steps every time: (1) name the two tens neighbors, (2) find the halfway
point, (3) ask: is the number before halfway, or at/past it?

## Worked example 1 — Round 46 to the nearest 10

1. Tens neighbors of 46: 40 and 50.
2. Halfway between them: 45.
3. 46 is past 45 → round **up**. 46 ≈ 50.
4. Digit check: the ones digit is 6 (5 or more) → round up. Both ways agree:
   **46 rounds to 50.**

## Worked example 2 — Round 382 to the nearest 10

1. Tens neighbors of 382: 380 and 390.
2. Halfway between them: 385.
3. 382 is before 385 → round **down**. 382 ≈ 380.
4. Digit check: the ones digit is 2 (4 or less) → round down. **382 rounds to
   380.**

Notice the hundreds digit never changes here — rounding to the nearest *ten*
only touches the tens and ones.

## Market rounding activity (with the generated illustration)

Look at the market illustration:

peaches, purple plums, and green grapes, under a hanging wooden sign with only
decorative carved swirls.](../assets/market-rounding-stand.png)

*Caption: An AI-generated illustration of a market fruit stand with five baskets
of fruit (no text or numbers in the image). Used for rounding practice.*

The farmer counted each basket this morning (counts are given in the lesson —
the picture shows the kinds of fruit, not the numbers):

- Peaches: **46**
- Apples: **73**
- Pears: **35**
- Plums: **89**
- Grapes: **64**

Round each count to the nearest 10, the way the farmer would report "about how
many" to a customer. Work the first one together (46 → 50), then round the
rest on your own. For 35 — exactly halfway — use the convention: round up.

*Text-only alternative:* The activity needs only the five counts above and the
three rounding steps. If the image is unavailable, draw five plain basket
shapes and label them with the counts.

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. Round **52** to the nearest 10. *(Hint: neighbors 50 and 60 — where is
   halfway?)*
2. Round **87** to the nearest 10. *(Hint: look at the ones digit.)*
3. Round **124** to the nearest 10. *(Hint: the hundred doesn't change.)*
4. Round **265** to the nearest 10. *(Hint: halfway — which way does our rule
   go?)*
5. Round **350** to the nearest 10. *(Hint: this one is exactly halfway.)*

## Application

A museum sign says "About 120 visitors came today." Which of these exact
counts could round to 120? Circle all that apply and explain: (a) 116 (b) 124
(c) 129.

## Exit check

1. Round **74** to the nearest 10.
2. Round **355** to the nearest 10.

*Success criterion:* both correct, with the learner able to name the halfway
point. Halfway errors (rounding 355 down) mean the convention needs another
round of number-line practice, not more digit-rule drilling.

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** the adult draws the number line and marks the number;
  the learner points to the closer ten and says "round up/down."
- **Alternate response mode:** answer with digit cards (hold up the rounded
  ten) instead of writing.
- **Extension:** list every two-digit number that rounds to 70 (65–74), and
  explain why 65 is included but 64 is not.

## Teacher note

Two traps: (1) learners who round 296 to 290 because they "round the ones down"
without checking neighbors — insist on naming the two tens neighbors first;
(2) halfway hesitation — reassure them the "5 rounds up" rule is a class
agreement, and the unit uses it consistently. Answer key:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-02).
