# Lesson 05 — Subtract Within 1000

**Session:** 11 · **Time:** 30 minutes · **Goal:** I can subtract within 1000
by ungrouping when a place is short, and check my answer with addition.

**Materials:** base-ten blocks, paper and pencil.

## Readiness check (3 min)

Mental warm-up: 13 − 8 = ? (5.) 120 − 40 = ? (80.) Then: "You have 3 rods and
need to take away 5 rods. What trade can you make?" (Trade one flat for 10
rods → 13 rods.) If the trade is unclear, do it with blocks before the lesson.

## Explicit teaching (8 min)

Subtraction undoes addition, so we work **place by place** again — but now a
place can be **short**: the top digit is smaller than the bottom digit. When
that happens we **ungroup** (borrow): trade one from the next place left and
give its ten to the short place. One hundred becomes 10 tens; one ten becomes
10 ones.

Steps: (1) start from the ones; (2) if the top digit is smaller, ungroup from
the left and add 10; (3) subtract each column; (4) **check with addition** —
add your answer to what you took away; you should get back what you started
with. Addition and subtraction are **inverse operations**: each one undoes the
other, which makes them a built-in answer checker.

Special case: **zeros**. In 500 − 274, the ones and tens are both 0. Ungroup the
5 hundreds into 4 hundreds + 10 tens, then ungroup one of those tens into 10
ones. Think of it as one smooth trade chain, left to right.

## Worked example 1 — 623 − 258

**Column:**
```
  623
− 258
-----
  365
```
Ones: 3 − 8 — too short. Ungroup one ten: tens 2 → 1, ones 3 → 13.
13 − 8 = 5. Tens: 1 − 5 — too short. Ungroup one hundred: hundreds 6 → 5,
tens 1 → 11. 11 − 5 = 6. Hundreds: 5 − 2 = 3. **623 − 258 = 365.**

**Check with addition:** 365 + 258 = 623. ✓ (Ones 5+8=13 write 3 carry 1;
tens 6+5+1=12 write 2 carry 1; hundreds 3+2+1=6.)

## Worked example 2 — 500 − 274 (zeros)

1. Ones: 0 − 4 — too short, but tens are 0 too. Ungroup from the hundreds:
   5 hundreds → 4 hundreds + 10 tens; then 10 tens → 9 tens + 10 ones.
2. Ones: 10 − 4 = 6. Tens: 9 − 7 = 2. Hundreds: 4 − 2 = 2.
3. **500 − 274 = 226.**
4. **Check:** 226 + 274 = 500. ✓

## Application

A box holds **600** crayons. **347** are taken for an art project. How many
crayons remain? Solve, then check your answer by adding.

## Exit check

1. **742 − 358 = ?**
2. Error analysis: Lee solved 742 − 358 and got **316**. Without solving from
   scratch, look at his ones column: what mistake did Lee probably make?

*Success criterion:* the exact difference is correct and the learner can name
Lee's slip (subtracting the smaller digit from the larger in the ones column,
8 − 2 = 6, instead of ungrouping).

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** the adult narrates each trade ("13 ones minus 8 ones");
  the learner writes the digits.
- **Alternate response mode:** act out the trades with blocks — physically hand
  a flat to be broken into rods — then record the column steps.
- **Extension:** 1000 − 476 = ? (524.) Explain why the answer and 476 add to
  exactly 1000, and where all three ungroupings happen.

## Teacher note

The **subtract-smaller-from-larger** habit (Lee's error) is the most stubborn
subtraction misconception at this level — learners do it column by column when
they forget which number is being taken away from which. The addition check
catches it every time; make the check a non-negotiable habit, not an optional
extra. Answer key: [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-05).
