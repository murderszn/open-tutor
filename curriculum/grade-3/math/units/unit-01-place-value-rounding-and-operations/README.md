# Unit 01 — Place Value, Rounding, and Multi-Digit Operations

**Grade:** 3 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #18](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/18), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Big numbers get friendlier when we know how they are built, when they are "close
enough," and how to combine them. In this unit the learner re-anchors three-digit
place value with hundred-flats, ten-rods, and one-cubes, reads and writes numbers
to 1000 in numeral, name, and expanded form, rounds to the nearest 10 and the
nearest 100 using number-line benchmarks, adds and subtracts fluently within
1000 (regrouping and ungrouping, checked by the inverse operation), multiplies
one-digit numbers by multiples of 10 using tens-as-units thinking, and uses
rounding to estimate and judge whether an exact answer is reasonable.

## Essential question

How do place value, rounding, and estimation help us work confidently with
three-digit numbers?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the grade-2 track:

- Three-digit place value: hundreds, tens, ones; 100 as ten tens; counting
  within 1000; reading and writing numbers to 1000 as numerals, number names,
  and expanded form
- Comparing two three-digit numbers with `>`, `=`, `<`
- Fluent addition and subtraction within 20; within 100 with place-value
  strategies; adding/subtracting within 1000 with models; mentally adding and
  subtracting 10 and 100
- One- and two-step word problems within 100; equations with a symbol for the
  unknown

The diagnostic re-teaches any insecure grade-2 skill in this unit's first two
sessions; Lesson 01 re-anchors three-digit grouping before new instruction.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Explain a three-digit number as hundreds, tens, and ones with base-ten
   models; read and write numbers to 1000 as base-ten numerals, number names,
   and expanded form. (3.NBT.A.2 foundation re-anchor)
2. Round whole numbers to the nearest 10 and to the nearest 100, using
   number-line benchmarks and the halfway rule, and explain the rounding
   decision. (3.NBT.A.1)
3. Fluently add and subtract within 1000 using strategies and algorithms based
   on place value, regrouping/ungrouping, and/or the relationship between
   addition and subtraction; check a subtraction with addition. (3.NBT.A.2)
4. Multiply one-digit whole numbers by multiples of 10 in the range 10–90
   (e.g., 9 × 80, 5 × 60) using place-value and tens-as-units strategies.
   (3.NBT.A.3)
5. Round to estimate sums and differences, and use the estimate to judge whether
   an exact answer is reasonable.

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**, grade 3,
as published at [thecorestandards.org/Math](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/).
Codes verified against the official Grade 3 Number & Operations in Base Ten
domain page on 2026-10-10 (the page renders A.2 and A.3; the A.1 rounding
expectation was cross-checked against the official standard text during the
track A00 audit, 2026-10-03). No state adoption or accreditation is claimed.
Descriptions below are paraphrases of the official expectations.

| Unit objective | Standard | What the standard expects (paraphrase) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 3.NBT.A.1 | Use place-value understanding to round whole numbers to the nearest 10 or 100. |
| 1, 3 | 3.NBT.A.2 | Fluently add and subtract within 1000 using strategies and algorithms based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction (a range of algorithms may be used). |
| 4 | 3.NBT.A.3 | Multiply one-digit whole numbers by multiples of 10 in the range 10–90 (e.g., 9 × 80, 5 × 60) using strategies based on place value and properties of operations. |

**Rounding convention used in this unit:** when a number is exactly halfway
between two round numbers, it rounds **up** (round half up: 25 → 30, 150 →
200). This matches the common U.S. elementary convention and the Wikipedia
"Rounding" reference checked 2026-10-10. The halfway cases are taught
explicitly; no other tie-breaking rule appears.

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials
list): base-ten blocks or paper hundred-flats/ten-rods/one-cubes (ten-rods can
be straws bundled in tens; flats can be 10-by-10 grids drawn on card), number
cards 100–999, a 0–999 thousand chart, a labeled number-line strip (0–1000 by
hundreds with ten subdivisions), the generated illustration
[`assets/market-rounding-stand.png`](assets/market-rounding-stand.png) plus the
reproducible SVG [`assets/rounding-number-line-10.svg`](assets/rounding-number-line-10.svg),
paper and crayons, play "order slips" for the project. No sharp tools;
supervise small parts; check food-allergen safety before using food as counters.
