# Project — Estimate-and-Check Market

**Sessions:** 14 (Part 1 — estimate) and 15 (Part 2 — exact check and
manager's note) · **Time:** 25–30 minutes each · **Addresses objectives 2, 3, 5**

## Story

You are the new manager of the market fruit stand in
[`market-rounding-stand.png`](assets/market-rounding-stand.png). Saturday's
harvest counts just came in (fictional practice data — the picture shows the
fruit kinds; the numbers below are your inventory sheet):

| Basket | Fruit | Harvest count |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Apples | 246 |
| 2 | Pears | 138 |
| 3 | Peaches | 175 |
| 4 | Plums | 204 |
| 5 | Grapes | 96 |

The owner wants two numbers: a **quick estimate** ("about how much fruit?") for
the morning announcement, and the **exact total** for the books. Your job:
round first, estimate, then compute exactly, then write a manager's note saying
whether your estimate was reasonable.

## Materials

Inventory sheet (above), number-line strip, paper and pencil, the market
illustration for reference.

## Part 1 — Round and estimate (Session 14)

1. For each basket, name the two tens neighbors and round the harvest count to
   the nearest 10. Record all five rounded numbers.
2. Add your five rounded numbers to get the **estimated total**. Show the
   addition.
3. Write one sentence for the morning announcement, e.g.: "We have about ___
   pieces of fruit today."

## Part 2 — Exact check and manager's note (Session 15)

4. Add the five **exact** harvest counts. Show the column addition (you may add
   in any order, but show every regrouping).
5. Compare: how far apart are your estimate and your exact total?
6. Write a **manager's note** (3–4 sentences): state the estimate, the exact
   total, whether the estimate was reasonable, and one thing the rounding
   changed (for example, which basket's rounding moved the total most).

## Deliverables

- Inventory sheet with rounded counts filled in
- Estimated total with addition shown
- Exact total with column work shown
- Morning-announcement sentence
- Manager's note (3–4 sentences)

## Rubric

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Solid | 2 — Developing | 1 — Starting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rounding accuracy** (Obj. 2) | All 5 counts rounded correctly, neighbors/halfway named | 4 of 5 correct | 2–3 of 5 correct | 0–1 correct |
| **Exact computation** (Obj. 3) | Exact total correct with all regrouping shown | Correct total, one regrouping step missing | Total off by a regrouping slip, work shown | No valid column work |
| **Estimation reasoning** (Obj. 5) | Estimate computed from rounded counts; difference from exact stated numerically | Estimate correct; difference stated vaguely | Estimate present but not from rounded counts | No estimate |
| **Manager's note** | 3–4 sentences: estimate, exact, reasonableness judgment, and one specific rounding effect | All four elements, one vague | Missing two elements | One sentence or less |

**Scoring:** 16 points total. 14–16 = exceeding; 11–13 = meeting; 8–10 =
approaching (re-teach rounding or regrouping per the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md)); below 8 = redo with adult support.

## Adult notes

- The exact total (859) is within 1000, so this is a fair Unit 01 task; no
  four-digit sums are required.
- If the learner's estimate differs from the exact total by more than 60,
  suspect a rounding error rather than an addition error — check Part 1 first.
- Accept any correct addition order in Part 2; the regrouping must still be
  shown column by column.
