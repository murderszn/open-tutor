# Lesson 04 — Locating Fractions on the Number Line

 **Session:** 7 · **Time:** 30 minutes · **Goal:** I can mark a fraction
a/b on a number line by counting a unit fractions from 0, including
fractions that land exactly on 1.

**Materials:** fraction strips (halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, eighths),
a 0-to-1 number line strip or a drawn number line, a marker or counter to
place on the line.

**Retrieval warm-up (2 min, Lessons 01–03 bridge):** "What is a unit
fraction?" (A fraction with numerator 1 — one equal part of the whole.)
"Count by unit fractions of fourths from 0 to 1." (1/4, 2/4, 3/4, 4/4.)
"What fraction names the whole when the whole is split into 4 equal
parts?" (4/4 — and 4/4 is the same as 1.)

## Readiness check (3 min)

Ask aloud: "Show me 1/3 on a fraction strip." (One of 3 equal parts
shaded.) "How many 1/3 pieces make the whole strip?" (3 — 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3
= 3/3 = 1.) "Point to 0 and to 1 on this number line." (0 at the left
end, 1 at the right end.) If counting unit fractions to 1 is shaky, lay
the unit-fraction pieces end to end on the number line first, then count
them aloud before marking anything.

## Explicit teaching (8 min)

A **number line** shows numbers in order, from smaller on the left to
larger on the right. We can put fractions on it the same way we put whole
numbers on it — by counting equal jumps from 0.

To locate **a/b** on a number line from 0 to 1:

1. Split the distance from 0 to 1 into **b equal parts**. The number b
   (the denominator) tells us how many equal parts to make.
2. Each part is one **unit fraction**, 1/b — one jump of that size.
3. Count **a jumps** from 0. The number a (the numerator) tells us how
   many jumps to take.
4. Mark the point where you land. That point is a/b.

The jumps are the key, not the tick marks. The tick marks are the landing
spots, and 0 is the starting spot — it is not a jump. If you count 0 as
your first jump, you will land one jump too far every time.

A fraction can land exactly on 1. When the numerator equals the
denominator — 2/2, 3/3, 4/4, 6/6, 8/8 — you have counted the whole
distance from 0 to 1, so the fraction sits on the same point as 1.
Fractions like 3/4 and 5/6 sit between 0 and 1: the more jumps you take,
the closer to 1 you land.

The same whole matters here too: the 0-to-1 stretch on your number line
must be the same length as the whole strip you are comparing it with, or
the jumps will not match.

## Worked example 1 — Locate 3/4

1. Read: 3/4 means 3 jumps of 1/4. The denominator 4 says: split 0 to 1
   into 4 equal parts.
2. Mark the ticks: 0, then 1/4, 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 (= 1). Check the parts are
   equal — lay a fourths strip beside the line if you are unsure.
3. Jump from 0: jump 1 lands on 1/4; jump 2 lands on 2/4; jump 3 lands on
   **3/4**. Mark it with a dot and label it 3/4.
4. Check: 3/4 is one jump (1/4) short of 1, so it should sit close to 1
   but not on it. ✓
5. Common slip: counting the tick at 0 as the first jump and marking 3/4
   where 4/4 belongs. Count jumps, not ticks — the first jump ends at the
   first tick *after* 0.

## Worked example 2 — Locate 5/6

1. Read: 5/6 means 5 jumps of 1/6. The denominator 6 says: split 0 to 1
   into 6 equal parts.
2. Mark the ticks: 0, 1/6, 2/6, 3/6, 4/6, 5/6, 6/6 (= 1). Six equal
   parts, six jumps to reach 1.
3. Jump from 0: 1/6, 2/6, 3/6, 4/6, **5/6**. Mark it and label it 5/6.
4. Check: 5/6 is one jump (1/6) short of 1, and 3/6 is the halfway point,
   so 5/6 should sit past halfway and close to 1. ✓
5. Notice: 5/6 is a bigger fraction than 3/4 is not the question here —
   what matters is that both were found the same way: split into equal
   parts, count jumps from 0.

## Guided practice (with hints)

Work through these together; the adult gives the hint, the learner does
the marking and labeling.

1. Locate **2/3** on a number line from 0 to 1. Label the point.
   *(Hint: how many equal parts does the denominator 3 tell you to make?
   How many jumps of 1/3 do you take?)*
2. Locate **1/2** on a number line from 0 to 1. Label the point.
   *(Hint: 1/2 is one jump of 1/2 — where is halfway between 0 and 1?)*
3. Locate **6/8** on a number line from 0 to 1. Label the point.
   *(Hint: 8 equal parts, 6 jumps. Is 6/8 past the halfway point? The
   halfway point is 4/8.)*
4. Locate **4/4** on a number line from 0 to 1. Where does it land, and
   what whole number is at that same point?
   *(Hint: count 4 jumps of 1/4 — where do you end up? What number is
   already written there?)*
5. Error analysis: Leo split 0 to 1 into 3 equal parts and marked 2/3 at
   the third tick after 0. What did Leo count wrong? Where should 2/3
   really be?
   *(Hint: Leo counted the tick at 0 as a jump. Count jumps from 0
   instead — how many jumps land on the third tick?)*

## Independent practice

6. Draw a number line from 0 to 1 split into 8 equal parts. Locate and
   label **5/8**.
7. Draw a number line from 0 to 1 split into 4 equal parts. Locate and
   label **2/4**.
8. Draw a number line from 0 to 1 split into 3 equal parts. Locate and
   label **3/3**. What whole number is at that point?
9. Draw a number line from 0 to 1 split into 8 equal parts. Locate and
   label **7/8**. How many jumps of 1/8 short of 1 is it?
10. True or false: **4/4 and 1 are the same point on the number line.**
    Explain by counting jumps.
11. Error analysis: Kim split 0 to 1 into 6 equal parts and marked 4/6 at
    the fourth tick *before* 1, counting backward from 1. What is wrong
    with counting backward from 1? Where should 4/6 really be? Show the
    jumps from 0.

## Application

Draw a number line from 0 to 1 split into 8 equal parts. Mark **3/8**
and **6/8**. Write one sentence saying which fraction is farther from 0
and how you know (count the jumps: 3 jumps of 1/8 against 6 jumps of
1/8). Then mark **8/8** and write what whole number sits at that point.

## Exit check

1. Draw a number line from 0 to 1 split into 6 equal parts. Locate and
   label **3/6**.
2. Draw a number line from 0 to 1 split into 8 equal parts. Locate and
   label **8/8**. What whole number is at that point?

*Success criterion:* both exit items correct with equal parts shown and
the mark placed by counting jumps from 0 — not by guessing a spot that
"looks about right." If the learner counts tick marks instead of jumps,
re-count the jumps together from 0 before Session 8.

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** let the learner say the jumps aloud ("one sixth,
  two sixths, three sixths…") while the adult marks the ticks; the
  learner points to the landing spot.
- **Alternate response mode:** place a counter on the number line at each
  jump instead of drawing, and answer orally; the adult records the
  written labels.
- **Extension:** locate 2/3 and 4/6 on two number lines of the same
  length. What do you notice about where they land? (They land on the
  same point — a first look at equivalence, which is Lesson 05.)

## Teacher note

The defining misconception in this lesson is **counting tick marks
instead of jumps** — treating 0 as the first jump, or counting the tick
labels as if they were the jumps. Both come from reading the number line
as a row of labels rather than a distance measured in equal jumps from 0.
The fix is physical: hop a counter along the line, one jump per unit
fraction, and say each fraction aloud as you land. A second slip to watch
is splitting 0 to 1 into unequal parts by eye; lay the matching fraction
strip beside the line until equal spacing is secure. Answer key:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-04).
