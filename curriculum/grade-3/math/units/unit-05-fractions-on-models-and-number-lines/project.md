# Project — Fraction Bakery

**Sessions:** 14 (plan and build) and 15 (check, share, present)
**Time:** 30 minutes per session, plus presentation time
**Goal:** Plan a bake-sale tray using fractions, draw the tray as a model,
mark the flavors on a number line from 0 to 1, and present the plan so
another person could set up the tray from your plan alone.

## The job

You are planning one bake-sale tray for a school fair. The tray is a
rectangle divided into **8 equal parts** (8 slices). You will fill the
tray with three flavors — chocolate, vanilla, and strawberry — and you
must decide what fraction of the tray each flavor takes.

Your plan must use these fractions:

- Chocolate: **2/8** of the tray
- Vanilla: **3/8** of the tray
- Strawberry: **1/8** of the tray

Check the total: 2/8 + 3/8 + 1/8 = 6/8 of the tray is filled. That
leaves **2/8** of the tray empty — name the leftover in your plan
(for example, "2/8 of the tray is left empty for a fourth flavor later,"
or "2/8 is saved for plain slices"). The fractions must add up to 1 or
less, and every part of the tray must be equal in size.

You will also make **one equivalent-fraction swap** and **one
comparison**:

- **Swap:** Choose one flavor's fraction and rewrite it as an
  equivalent fraction. (For example, the chocolate is 2/8 of the tray,
  and 2/8 = 1/4, so you could say "2/8 of the tray is chocolate, which
  is the same as 1/4 of the tray.")
- **Comparison:** Say which flavor takes up more of the tray and which
  takes up less. (Vanilla, 3/8, takes up more than chocolate, 2/8,
  because 3/8 > 2/8 when the parts are the same size.)

## Deliverables

1. **Tray drawing.** Draw the rectangle tray divided into 8 equal
   parts. Color or label each part with its flavor: 2 parts chocolate,
   3 parts vanilla, 1 part strawberry, and 2 parts leftover. Label the
   fraction for each flavor on the drawing.
2. **Number line.** Draw a number line from 0 to 1 divided into 8 equal
   parts. Mark and label 1/8, 2/8, 3/8, and 6/8 on it, and show which
   mark is the end of each flavor's share (chocolate ends at 2/8,
   vanilla ends at 5/8, strawberry ends at 6/8, counting from 0).
3. **Written plan.** In your own words: the tray size (8 equal parts),
   each flavor's fraction, the total filled (6/8), the leftover (2/8)
   and what it is for, the equivalent-fraction swap, and the comparison
   of two flavors.
4. **Presentation.** Explain the plan aloud (or in writing) in this
   order: tray size → each flavor's fraction → total and leftover →
   number line → equivalent-fraction swap → comparison. Then answer
   one "what if" question from the adult (see below).

## "What if" questions (adult picks one for the presentation)

- What if the tray had 4 equal parts instead of 8, and you kept the
  same amounts? What fraction would the chocolate be? (2/8 = 1/4, so
  the chocolate would be 1/4 of a 4-part tray.)
- What if vanilla took 1 more part (4/8) and the leftover shrank to
  1/8? What fraction of the tray would be filled then? (2/8 + 4/8 +
  1/8 = 7/8 filled, with 1/8 left over.)
- What if strawberry and chocolate swapped amounts — strawberry 2/8
  and chocolate 1/8? Which flavor would have more then, and how do
  you know? (Strawberry, 2/8, would have more than chocolate, 1/8,
  because 2 parts out of 8 is more than 1 part out of 8.)

## Materials and safety

Paper strips or play-dough for the tray model — **no real food is
required**. Paper and pencil are enough: draw the tray, color the
parts, and label them. Real baking is optional, adult-supervised, and
only with food every person involved can safely eat; check allergies
before any real baking, and never use food a person cannot eat. No
oven, cutting, or heat is involved in the paper/model version.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (3) | On track (2) | Getting started (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equal parts (Objectives 1–2) | Tray divided into 8 parts that are all the same size; leftover named | Tray divided into 8 parts with one or two parts visibly unequal, or leftover unnamed | Parts clearly unequal, or the tray is not divided into 8 parts |
| Fraction naming (Objectives 1–2) | All three flavors named correctly (2/8, 3/8, 1/8) and the total 6/8 and leftover 2/8 stated | Two flavors named correctly, or the total/leftover has one error | One or fewer flavors named correctly |
| Number-line placement (Objective 3) | Number line 0 to 1 in 8 equal parts; 1/8, 2/8, 3/8, and 6/8 marked and labeled in the right places | Number line drawn with marks mostly right, or one label missing or misplaced | Number line missing, not divided into equal parts, or marks in the wrong places |
| Equivalence / comparison reasoning (Objectives 4–6) | Swap is correct (for example, 2/8 = 1/4) and the comparison names the bigger flavor with a correct reason (same-size parts, more parts) | Swap or comparison is correct, but the other has an error or no reason given | Swap is wrong and the comparison is wrong or missing |

**Scoring:** 12 = complete; 9–11 = complete with one re-teach target (name
it and revisit it in Session 16 review time); 8 or below = re-plan one
flavor with the adult before the unit assessment.
