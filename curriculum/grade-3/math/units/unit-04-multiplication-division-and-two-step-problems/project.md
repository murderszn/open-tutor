# Project — Two-Step School Store

**Sessions:** 14 (plan and build) and 15 (check, share, present) ·
**Time:** 30 minutes each · **Adult role:** shopkeeper's assistant —
check the arithmetic, never supply it.

## The task

You run a pretend school store for one fair day. Your store sells
pencils (in packs of 5, and singly), erasers (in packs of 4), and
notebooks (singly). Prices, from the store sign and your own price
list, are:

- Pencil pack (5 pencils): 10¢ · Single pencil: 2¢
- Eraser pack (4 erasers): 8¢ · Notebook: 20¢

## Part 1 — Plan the stock and build the store (Session 14)

1. **Choose your stock.** Pick a number of packs and singles for each
   item (for example, "9 pencil packs"). You must stock at least two
   items, and at least one item in packs.
2. **Count your stock in two steps.** For each packed item, show:
   packs × items per pack = total items. For the whole store, show the
   total number of items across everything you stocked.
3. **Build the store.** Make a store poster: draw or build the table
   (the generated illustration
   [`assets/two-step-school-store.png`](assets/two-step-school-store.png)
   may be your model, not your poster — your poster shows *your*
   stock), write your price list, and label each item's pack size.
4. **Plan two sales.** Write two sale stories in advance: a customer
   buys some packs of one item and some singles of another (or buys
   packs and then returns some items). Each sale story must need two
   steps to answer "how many items are left?" or "how much money was
   collected?"

## Part 2 — Check, share, present (Session 15)

5. **Run the sales.** Solve both sale stories, showing both steps,
   an estimate, and a check (inverse operation or re-computation)
   for each.
6. **Present.** Explain your store to the adult in three minutes:
   what you stocked, how you counted it, what each sale did to your
   stock, and one pattern you noticed (for example, packs of 5 and
   the 5s pattern, or why your pencil-pack total is always a multiple
   of 5).
7. **Fix one mistake.** The adult picks one computation to question
   (it may be correct — defend it with your check, or find and fix
   the faulty step).

## Deliverables

- A stock table (item, packs, items per pack, total items; plus
  singles where stocked) with both steps shown
- A store poster with price list and pack sizes labeled
- Two written sale stories with two-step solutions, estimates, and
  checks
- A three-minute presentation (notes allowed)

## Rubric (12 points total)

| Criterion (0–3 each) | 3 — Strong | 2 — Solid | 1 — Started | 0 — Missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stock plan and counting | Two or more items, packs × per-pack shown for every packed item, whole-store total correct | Most counting shown, one small slip | Stock listed but pack counting missing or mostly wrong | No stock table |
| Two-step sale stories | Two stories, each genuinely two-step, both steps and units shown | Two stories, one step unclear or one unit missing | One story, or stories that are really one-step | No stories |
| Reasonableness and checks | Estimate + inverse/re-computation check for each sale, faulty step found or defended | Checks present for one sale, or estimates without checks | A check attempted but incorrect | No checks |
| Presentation | Explains stock, counting, both sales, and one pattern clearly | Explains most parts, pattern missing or vague | Reads numbers aloud without explaining them | No presentation |

**Ready:** 9–12 points, with no criterion at 0. **Re-teach:** below 9
points, or any criterion at 0 — redo that criterion's practice session
(see [teacher-guide.md](teacher-guide.md)) before Unit 05.

## Sample store (for the adult — not the learner's answer)

A learner who stocks exactly what the picture shows for pencils —
3 baskets × 3 packs = 9 packs, 9 × 5 = 45 pencils — then sells 4 packs
(4 × 5 = 20 pencils) has 45 − 20 = 25 pencils left and collects
4 × 10¢ = 40¢. Full sample solutions are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md), Project sample.
