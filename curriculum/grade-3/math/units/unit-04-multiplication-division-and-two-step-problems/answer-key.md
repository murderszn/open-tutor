# Answer Key — Unit 04: Multiplication, Division, and Two-Step Problems

**Audience:** the guiding adult only. Do not hand this file to the
learner. Every answer below was solved independently of the lesson
text and reconciled against it; where a lesson item is open-ended, an
acceptable sample is given and variations that show the same reasoning
earn full marks. All two-step answers require both steps and the unit
for full marks.

## Lesson 01 — Two steps, one story at a time

Worked examples: picture problem — Step 1: 3 × 3 = 9 packs; Step 2:
9 × 5 = **45 pencils**. Priya — Step 1: 4 × 6 = 24 new markers; Step 2:
9 + 24 = **33 markers**.

Guided: 1. 5 × 7 = 35; 35 − 13 = **22 pencils**. 2. 3 × 8 = 24;
18 + 24 = **42 stickers**. 3. 4 × 6 = 24; 24 − 9 = **15 muffins**.
4. Tom added the pack count (3) as if it were pencils. Correct:
3 × 8 = 24; 24 + 7 = **31 pencils**.

Independent: 5. 6 × 5 = 30; 30 + 8 = **38 apples**. 6. 7 × 4 = 28;
28 − 5 = **23 empty chairs**. 7. 3 × 9 = 27; 27 + 12 = **39 pages**.
8. 8 × 3 = 24; 24 ÷ 4 = **6 erasers per table**. 9. Acceptable: the
first 3 counts baskets, the second 3 counts packs per basket, and the
5 counts pencils per pack — three different kinds, so they cannot be
added; the steps are 3 × 3 = 9 packs, then 9 × 5 = 45 pencils.

Application (sample): "4 bags with 6 marbles each, shared equally
among 3 friends" — 4 × 6 = 24; 24 ÷ 3 = 8 marbles per friend.

Exit: 2 × 9 = 18; 18 + 6 = **24 crayons**.

## Lesson 03 — Which operation does each step need?

Worked examples: teams — 24 ÷ 4 = 6; 6 + 2 = **8 students per team**.
Tom — cross out 3 pens; 4 × 7 = 28; 28 − 5 = **23 pencils**.

Guided: 1. Multiply-then-subtract: 9 × 4 = 36; 36 − 10 = **26
seedlings**. 2. Divide-then-add: 56 ÷ 7 = 8; 8 + 3 = **11 cookies per
box**. 3. Multiply-then-divide: 5 × 6 = 30; 30 ÷ 6 = **5 jars per
box**. 4. Lee's Step 1 should multiply (4 × 9 = 36) and Step 2
should divide (36 ÷ 6 = **6 marbles per friend**); adding the
numbers and subtracting the friend count answers nothing the story
asks.

Independent: 5. Multiply-then-add: 3 × 8 = 24; 24 + 12 = **36
stickers**. 6. Divide-then-subtract: 45 ÷ 5 = 9; 9 − 4 = **5 counters
per group**. 7. Multiply-then-subtract: 7 × 5 = 35; 35 − 14 = **21
buns**. 8. Add-then-divide: 14 + 10 = 24; 24 ÷ 4 = **6 crayons per
table**. 9. Cross out 2 hens; 6 × 8 = 48; 48 − 20 = **28 eggs**.

Exit: Multiply-then-subtract: 5 × 8 = 40; 40 − 17 = **23 marbles**.

## Lesson 05 — Patterns in the multiplication table

Worked examples: 4 × 7 = 28 = **14 + 14** (even). 9 × 6: skip count
54; ten-minus-one 60 − 6 = **54**; 9 × 7 = 70 − 7 = 63, digits 6 + 3 = 9. ✓

Guided: 1. 4 × 8 = **32**, even: 32 = **16 + 16**. 2. 9 × 4 = 40 − 4
= **36**; 3 + 6 = 9. ✓ 3. 8 × 6 = **48** (two sets of 4 × 6 = 24).
4. Counterexample: **3 × 2 = 6** (even) or 3 × 4 = 12 — so "every
multiple of 3 is odd" fails; Jay checked only odd multiples.

Independent: 5. 4 × 6 = **24 = 12 + 12**; 4 × 10 = **40 = 20 + 20**;
both even. 6. 9 × 5 = 50 − 5 = **45** (4 + 5 = 9); 9 × 8 = 80 − 8 =
**72** (7 + 2 = 9). 7. 3, 6, 9, 12, **15, 18**; add 3 each time; next
is **21**; parity is mixed (odd, even, odd, even, …). 8. **True**:
3 × 4 = 12, 3 × 6 = 18 — both even, because an even number splits
into two equal parts and tripling keeps that split (12 = 6 + 6).
9. 6 × 7 = 42 re-sorts into two groups of 21 (three 7s each):
**21 + 21 = 42**, so 42 is even.

Exit: 4 × 8 = **32**, even, because 32 = 16 + 16.
