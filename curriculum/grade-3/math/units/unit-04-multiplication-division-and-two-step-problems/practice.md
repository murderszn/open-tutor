# Practice Sessions — Unit 04

Each session is 30 minutes: 2-minute retrieval warm-up, about 25
minutes of work, and a 3-minute "what did this session teach me?"
close. The adult reviews written work the same day. Solutions:
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md), Practice sessions.

## Session A — Step-by-step builders

*After L1. For every problem, write Step 1, Step 2, and a final
sentence with the unit.*

1. A shop has 4 boxes with 6 pencils in each box. The shop buys 11
   more pencils. How many pencils does the shop have now?
2. A class makes 9 posters with 5 stickers on each poster, then takes
   17 stickers off to reuse them. How many stickers are left on the
   posters?
3. A teacher packs 7 bags with 4 counters in each bag and shares the
   bags' counters equally among 7 groups. How many counters does each
   group get?
4. A baker bakes 5 trays with 8 rolls on each tray, then sells 9
   rolls. How many rolls are left?
5. A shelf holds 3 rows with 10 books in each row. The librarian adds
   14 books. How many books are on the shelf now?
6. Error fix: A learner solved "6 packs of 5 pencils, give 7 pencils
   away" as 6 × 5 = 30, then 30 + 7 = 37. Name the faulty step and
   give the correct answer.

## Session B — Letter-for-unknown puzzles

*After L2. Write each equation with a letter before solving, and prove
each answer by substitution or the inverse operation.*

1. 8 × n = 72. Find n.
2. n ÷ 4 = 7. Find n.
3. Two steps: p = 5 × 9; n = p − 16. Find p, then n.
4. Write 6 × ? = 54 with the letter n, then find n.
5. Find n: n = 3 × 8 + 5. (Remember: multiply before adding.)
6. Story to equations: A cook makes 7 boxes with 6 muffins in each
   box, then shares all the muffins equally among 6 tables. Let p be
   the muffins made and n the muffins per table. Write both equations
   and solve.

## Session C — Step-sort race

*Game, after L1/L2. Cut the six stories apart (or number them). First
sort them by shape — multiply-then-add, divide-then-add,
multiply-then-subtract, add-then-divide, multiply-then-divide, or
extra-number — then solve. Speed never beats a named shape: a sorted
card with no shape label scores no point.*

1. A farmer grows 4 rows with 7 carrots in each row, then picks 6
   more carrots. How many carrots does she have?
2. Forty-eight beads are shared equally into 6 necklaces. Then 5 more
   beads are added to each necklace. How many beads are on each
   necklace now?
3. A shop stocks 9 shelves with 3 cans on each shelf, then sells 8
   cans. How many cans are left?
4. A class collects 16 red buttons and 24 blue buttons, then shares
   all the buttons equally among 5 bags. How many buttons are in each
   bag?
5. A baker makes 5 trays with 6 buns on each tray, then packs the buns
   equally into 3 boxes. How many buns are in each box?
6. Extra number: A farmer picks 8 rows with 4 plants in each row and
   also feeds 2 dogs. She sells 13 plants. How many plants are left?

## Session D — Read, model, solve review

*Review, after L1–L2. These items ask for explanations, not just
answers. A bare number earns no mark here.*

1. Retell the steps, in order, for this problem: "A class has 3 boxes
   with 8 crayons in each box, plus 7 loose crayons. How many crayons
   in all?" Solve it as you retell.
2. Find both faults: A learner solved "5 bags of 6 marbles, share
   equally among 3 friends" as 5 + 6 = 11, then 11 − 3 = 8. What
   should each step have been, and what is the correct answer?
3. Write a story that fits these equations, then solve: a = 4 × 9;
   n = a − 10.
4. Explain with units: A shop has 6 packs with 5 pencils in each pack
   and shares all the pencils equally among 5 tables. How many pencils
   does each table get, and why is the second step division rather
   than subtraction?

## Session E — Reasonableness checkers

*After L4. For each claim, give an estimate, a verdict (reasonable /
not reasonable), and the corrected answer when the claim is wrong.*

1. Claim: 7 × 9 − 20 = 53.
2. Claim: 81 ÷ 9 + 7 = 16.
3. Claim: 4 × 8 + 19 = 61.
4. Claim: 72 ÷ 8 + 6 = 15.
5. Claim: 6 × 7 + 28 = 80.
6. A learner estimates 9 × 5 − 13 as "about 40" (50 − 10) and then
   finds the exact answer 32. Is the estimate reasonable for an
   estimate? Explain what "close enough" means here.

## Session F — Pattern detective stations

*After L5. Work in stations; each station needs three examples and an
explanation, not just one answer.*

1. Station "Always even?": Compute 4 × 3, 4 × 5, and 4 × 8. Are all
   three even? Split each answer into two equal addends.
2. Station "Nines": Compute 9 × 3, 9 × 7, and 9 × 9 with the
   ten-minus-one method. Do the digits of each answer add to 9?
3. Station "Doubling": If 4 × 5 = 20, what is 8 × 5? If 4 × 9 = 36,
   what is 8 × 9? Explain each with groups.
4. Station "False pattern": A learner claims "the product of two odd
   numbers is even." Give a counterexample and explain why the claim
   fails.
5. Station "Turn-around": Show 7 × 6 = 6 × 7 with an array, then use
   6 × 8 = 48 to find 8 × 6 without recomputing.
6. Station "Fives": List 5 × 1 through 5 × 6. What pattern do the
   ones digits follow? Explain using 10 × n: 5 × n is half of
   10 × n.

## Session G — Midyear fluency stations

*Week 18 cumulative review, after L6. Mix of Units 01–04. Bring the
Lesson 06 Practice pile; Station 1 is that pile.*

1. Fluency set — name a strategy for each: 7 × 8, 56 ÷ 7, 9 × 4,
   36 ÷ 9, 6 × 6, 54 ÷ 6.
2. Two-step: A shop has 8 boxes with 6 pens in each box, then sells
   19 pens. How many pens are left? Estimate first.
3. Rounding (Unit 01): Round 47 and 83 to the nearest 10, estimate
   47 + 83, then compute exactly. Was the estimate reasonable?
4. Rounding (Unit 01): Round 386 and 214 to the nearest 100, estimate
   386 + 214, then compute exactly.
5. Unknowns (Unit 03): Find n in 8 × n = 56 and in n ÷ 9 = 6.
6. Patterns (L5): Show 9 × 6 = 54 with ten-minus-one and the digit-sum
   check; show 4 × 7 = 28 is even with two equal addends.
7. Two-step: Sixty-three stickers are shared equally among 7 friends.
   Each friend then buys 4 more stickers. How many stickers does each
   friend have now?
8. Two-step: A hall has 5 tables with 7 students at each table. Ten
   students leave in total. How many students are left?
