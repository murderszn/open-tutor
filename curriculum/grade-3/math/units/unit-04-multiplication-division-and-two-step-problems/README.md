# Unit 04 — Multiplication, Division, and Two-Step Problems

**Grade:** 3 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #18](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/18), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Most real problems take two steps. A shopkeeper first counts how many
packs are on the table, and only then counts the pencils inside them. A
team captain first shares players equally, and only then adds the late
arrivals. In this unit the learner slows down on purpose: read the story,
name the hidden first question, solve one step at a time, write an
equation with a letter for the unknown, and check whether the answer is
reasonable before calling it done. Week 18 is the track's midyear review,
so the unit also gathers Units 01–04 fluency — multiplication and division
within 100, rounding as an estimation tool, and the patterns that make the
multiplication table predictable instead of a list to memorize blind.

## Essential question

How do I break a hard problem into two smaller steps I already know how
to solve — and how do I know my answer makes sense?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and Units 01–03:

- Multiplication as equal groups and arrays; products within 100, with a
  strategy for every fact through 10 × 10 (Unit 02)
- Division as fair shares and group counts, and as an unknown-factor
  problem; quotients within 100 (Unit 03)
- Unknown whole numbers in multiplication and division equations in any
  position (Unit 03; extended here to two-step equations)
- Addition and subtraction within 100; rounding to the nearest 10 and 100
  (Unit 01 — reused here as the estimation tool for reasonableness)
- One-step word problems with a symbol for the unknown (Units 02–03)

Each lesson's readiness check names the exact re-teach for a missing
prerequisite. This unit never asks for a two-step problem whose single
steps the learner cannot already do alone.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Solve two-step word problems using the four operations, posed with
   whole numbers and having whole-number answers; model each step with a
   drawing or equation and write equations with a letter standing for the
   unknown quantity. (3.OA.D.8; 3.OA.A.3)
2. Determine the unknown whole number in a multiplication or division
   equation relating three whole numbers, in any position, and prove it by
   substitution or by the inverse operation. (3.OA.A.4)
3. Assess the reasonableness of a two-step answer using mental computation
   and estimation strategies including rounding, and correct an
   unreasonable answer by finding the faulty step. (3.OA.D.8)
4. Identify arithmetic patterns in the multiplication table (for example,
   4 times a number is always even; the digits of a 9s product add to 9)
   and explain them using properties of operations. (3.OA.D.9)
5. Multiply and divide fluently within 100 using strategies such as the
   relationship between multiplication and division and properties of
   operations; by the end of the unit, hold a reliable strategy for every
   fact within 100 and know an increasing share of products from memory.
   (3.OA.C.7)

## Vocabulary (taught with objects first, word second)

two-step problem · step · hidden question · equation · letter for the
unknown · estimate · reasonable · check · inverse operation · pattern ·
even · odd · equal addends · product · quotient · factor · multiple ·
turn-around (commutative) fact · break apart (distributive) · array

Note on language: the adult may use *commutative* and *distributive*,
but the learner is never required to produce those formal terms. "Turn
it around" and "break it apart" are always accepted, as is naming the
letter ("let n be the answer") in the learner's own words.

## How the 16 sessions fill the unit

Six lessons alone do not fill four weeks. Lessons introduce each idea
once; practice sessions (A, B, E, F, G) give the learner a second and
third pass at the same idea with new numbers; the game session builds
operation-choosing speed without timing; review sessions force the
learner to explain each step, not just answer; the project applies all
five objectives to one store plan; and the quiz and assessment check
the objectives, not the lessons. If a lesson's exit check fails, the
following practice session becomes re-teach time — see
[teacher-guide.md](teacher-guide.md) for the ready-to-move-on criteria.
Week 18 (Sessions 13–16) doubles as the track's midyear review: Session
13 is cumulative fluency, Session 15's quiz is formative, and Session 16
checks Units 01–04 readiness for Unit 05.
