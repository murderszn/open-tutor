# Lesson 03 — Which Operation Does Each Step Need?

**Session:** 7 · **Time:** 30 minutes · **Goal:** I can decide, for
each step of a two-step problem, whether to multiply, divide, add, or
subtract — and ignore numbers the story does not need.

**Materials:** counters, operation cards (×, ÷, +, −), index cards with
the six practice stories cut apart (optional).

**Retrieval warm-up (2 min):** "A story says '3 bags of 6 apples each.'
Which operation finds all the apples?" (Multiply: 3 × 6 = 18.) "A story
says '18 apples shared equally among 3 bags.' Which operation?" (Divide:
18 ÷ 3 = 6 per bag.)

## Readiness check (3 min)

Ask aloud: "24 counters are shared equally into 4 groups. Operation?"
(Divide: 24 ÷ 4 = 6.) "You have 24 counters and get 4 more. Operation?"
(Add: 24 + 4 = 28.) "Same numbers, different stories, different
operations — what told you which to use?" (The story's question: sharing
vs. getting more.) If the learner answers from the numbers instead of
the story, slow down and act out every story with counters today.

## Explicit teaching (8 min)

Do not pick operations from the numbers. Pick them from **what the
story is doing**:

| The story is… | Try… | Ask yourself |
|---|---|---|
| making equal groups, or groups of a known size | multiply | "How many groups? How many in each?" |
| sharing equally, or counting equal groups | divide | "How many in each share? How many groups?" |
| putting two amounts together, or getting more | add | "What is the total now?" |
| taking some away, or finding how many are left / how many more | subtract | "What remains? What is the difference?" |

Three traps to watch for:

1. **The hidden first question decides Step 1.** If the story asks for
   a total but only gives groups, Step 1 must *build* that total
   (multiply) before Step 2 can use it.
2. **Extra numbers are decorations.** A story may mention 3 pens while
   asking about pencils. Cross out numbers the question never uses —
   but only *after* reading the question, never before.
3. **Order matters in subtraction and division.** 30 − 12 is not
   12 − 30, and 30 ÷ 5 is not 5 ÷ 30. Say what each number counts before
   writing the equation.

Worked pattern: most two-step problems in this unit are one of four
shapes — multiply then add, multiply then subtract, divide then add,
or add then divide. Naming the shape ("this is multiply-then-subtract")
before computing prevents most wrong-operation errors.

## Worked example 1 — Divide, then add

Twenty-four students are split equally into 4 teams. Then 2 new
students join each team. How many students are on each team now?

1. Read the question first: students *per team now*.
2. Hidden first question: how many students per team at the start?
3. Step 1 (divide — sharing equally): 24 ÷ 4 = **6 students per team**.
4. Step 2 (add — more join each team): 6 + 2 = **8 students per team**.
5. Answer: **8 students** on each team now.
6. Check the shape: divide-then-add. A common slip is 24 + 2 = 26
   (adding before sharing), which answers a different question — how
   many students in all, before teams — not students per team.

## Worked example 2 — The extra number

Tom buys 4 boxes with 7 pencils in each box. He also owns 3 pens. He
gives 5 pencils away. How many pencils does Tom have left?

1. Read the question first: pencils left. Pens are never asked about.
2. Cross out the extra number: 3 pens — decoration. Do not use it.
3. Step 1 (multiply — boxes into pencils): 4 × 7 = **28 pencils**.
4. Step 2 (subtract — giving away): 28 − 5 = **23 pencils**.
5. Answer: **23 pencils** left. Shape: multiply-then-subtract.
6. If you had used the pens (28 + 3 − 5 = 26), you would be counting
   pens as pencils — same-kind rule broken.

## Guided practice (with hints)

Name the shape first (e.g., "multiply-then-add"), then solve.

1. A gardener plants 9 rows with 4 seeds in each row, then pulls out
   10 seedlings. How many seedlings are left?
   *(Hint: rows into seeds first, then take away.)*
2. Fifty-six cookies are shared equally among 7 boxes. Then 3 more
   cookies are added to each box. How many cookies are in each box now?
   *(Hint: share first — Step 1 is division.)*
3. A shop has 5 shelves with 6 jars on each shelf. The jars are packed
   equally into 6 boxes. How many jars are in each box?
   *(Hint: build the total first, then share it.)*
4. Error analysis: For "4 bags of 9 marbles, share equally among 6
   friends," Lee wrote 4 + 9 = 13, then 13 − 6 = 7. What two mistakes
   did Lee make?
   *(Hint: Step 1 should build a total from groups; Step 2 should share
   that total, not subtract the number of friends.)*

## Independent practice

5. Mia has 3 packs of 8 stickers and buys 12 more stickers. How many
   stickers does she have now? Name the shape.
6. Forty-five counters are shared equally into 5 groups. Then 4
   counters are removed from each group. How many counters are in each
   group now? Name the shape.
7. A baker makes 7 trays of 5 buns, then sells 14 buns. How many buns
   are left? Name the shape.
8. A teacher has 14 red crayons and 10 blue crayons. She shares all
   the crayons equally among 4 tables. How many crayons does each
   table get? Name the shape. (Step 1 here is addition, not
   multiplication — the crayons are already counted, not grouped.)
9. Cross out the extra number, then solve: A farmer picks 6 baskets
   with 8 eggs in each basket and also feeds 2 hens. She sells 20 eggs.
   How many eggs are left?

## Application

Take one independent-practice story and change exactly one thing — the
question — so that the operation shape changes too (for example, ask
"how many in all?" instead of "how many per group?"). Solve both
versions and explain to the adult why the same numbers now need
different operations.

## Exit check

A shop has 5 bags with 8 marbles in each bag. Seventeen marbles are
taken out for a display. How many marbles are left? Name the shape
and show both steps.

**Teacher note:** the exit story is multiply-then-subtract. Likely
misconception — using the first operation that "feels right" for the
whole problem. Answers: [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md), Lesson 03.
