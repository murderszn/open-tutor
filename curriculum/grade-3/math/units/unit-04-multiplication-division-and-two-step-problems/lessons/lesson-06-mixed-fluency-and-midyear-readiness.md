# Lesson 06 — Mixed Fluency and Midyear Readiness

**Session:** 12 · **Time:** 30 minutes · **Goal:** I can solve a
mixed set of multiplication, division, and two-step problems by choosing
a strategy for each fact, and I can name what I still need to practice
before Unit 05.

**Materials:** multiplication chart (available, not required), counters,
fluency cards from Units 02–03.

**Retrieval warm-up (2 min):** "7 × 8 — what strategy?" (Break apart:
7 × 4 + 7 × 4 = 28 + 28 = 56, or double 7 × 4.) "56 ÷ 7 — what
strategy?" (Unknown factor: what times 7 makes 56? 8.) "Name one fact
you now know from memory." (Any true product within 100, stated with
its strategy if it is not yet memorized.)

## Readiness check (3 min)

Ask aloud: "Solve 6 × 7 and say your strategy." (42 — e.g., 6 × 5 + 6 × 2
= 30 + 12.) "Solve 42 ÷ 6 and say your strategy." (7 — 6 × 7 = 42.)
"Solve a two-step problem out loud, naming both steps." (Any Lesson 01
problem, restated.) If a strategy cannot be named, the fact is not yet
fluent — it goes on the practice list, not on the memorized list. That
distinction is the point of today.

## Explicit teaching (8 min)

**Fluency** means: for every fact within 100, you have a strategy you
can use correctly without help. **Memory** (knowing it instantly) is
the end-of-Grade-3 goal for products of two one-digit numbers
(3.OA.C.7); it is not required today. What *is* required today is
honesty about which facts are memorized, which need a strategy, and
which still need the chart.

The strategy toolkit, all from Units 02–04:

- **Turn around:** 8 × 3 = 3 × 8 = 24.
- **Break apart:** 7 × 8 = 7 × 4 + 7 × 4 = 56.
- **Unknown factor:** 56 ÷ 8 = 7 because 8 × 7 = 56.
- **Doubling / halving patterns:** 4 × 8 = 32, so 8 × 8 = 64.
- **Ten-minus-one (9s):** 9 × 7 = 70 − 7 = 63.
- **Two-step method:** hidden question first, one step at a time,
  estimate and check last.

Midyear self-check (do this aloud, without shame — it is a map, not a
grade): sort ten fluency cards into three piles — *Know it* (instant),
*Strategy* (correct with a named strategy), *Practice* (chart or help
needed). The *Practice* pile is next week's Session 13 work, not a
failure. Every learner in Grade 3 has a *Practice* pile in December;
the unit's job is to make it smaller, not to pretend it is empty.

## Worked example 1 — Breaking apart 7 × 8

1. The fact: 7 × 8 = ?. It is not yet memorized — that is fine.
2. Choose a strategy: break 8 into 4 + 4.
3. Compute: 7 × 4 = 28; 7 × 4 = 28 again; 28 + 28 = **56**.
4. Check: turn it around — 8 × 7 should also be 56. 8 × 4 + 8 × 3 =
   32 + 24 = 56. ✓ Two different strategies, same answer: trusted.
5. Record honestly: 7 × 8 moves from *Practice* to *Strategy* today.
   It becomes *Know it* only when it comes instantly three sessions
   running.

## Worked example 2 — A mixed two-step, start to finish

A shop has 7 boxes with 8 pens in each box. It sells 29 pens. How many
pens are left? Estimate, then solve, then check.

1. Read the question first: pens left.
2. Hidden first question: how many pens at the start? Step 1:
   7 × 8 = 56 (break apart: 7 × 4 + 7 × 4 = 28 + 28).
3. Step 2: 56 − 29 = **27 pens** left.
4. Estimate: 7 × 8 = 56, about 60 to the nearest 10; 29 is about 30;
   60 − 30 = 30. Expect about 30; 27 is in that neighborhood. ✓
5. Inverse check: 27 + 29 = 56. ✓ and 56 ÷ 7 = 8. ✓
6. Answer: **27 pens** left.

## Guided practice (with hints)

Name the strategy before computing, for every item.

1. 9 × 6 — strategy and answer.
   *(Hint: ten-minus-one: 10 × 6 − 6.)*
2. 72 ÷ 9 — strategy and answer.
   *(Hint: unknown factor — what times 9 makes 72?)*
3. 8 × 7 — two different strategies, same answer.
   *(Hint: break apart, and doubling from 4 × 7.)*
4. Two-step: A baker makes 6 trays of 7 rolls, then sells 18 rolls.
   Estimate, solve, and check with the inverse.
   *(Hint: estimate 6 × 7 ≈ 40, minus about 20, expect about 20.)*

## Independent practice

5. Solve with a named strategy each: 6 × 9, 48 ÷ 8, 7 × 7, 63 ÷ 7.
6. Two-step: A class has 4 shelves with 9 books on each shelf. The
   class gives 15 books away. How many books are left? Estimate first.
7. Two-step: Ninety stickers are shared equally among 9 friends. Each
   friend then gives 2 stickers to a new student. How many stickers
   does each friend have now?
8. Pattern check: is 4 × 9 even? Prove it with two equal addends, then
   use the 9s digit-sum check on the same answer.
9. Sort ten facts (adult picks from Units 02–03 cards) into *Know it*,
   *Strategy*, and *Practice* piles. Write the *Practice* pile down —
   it is your Session 13 target list.

## Application

Teach-back: teach the adult one fact from your *Strategy* pile as if
the adult had never seen it — show the strategy, not just the answer.
The adult must be able to solve a second, similar fact using only your
explanation. If they cannot, your explanation needs the missing step.

## Exit check

Solve 8 × 6 and 48 ÷ 6, naming one strategy for each. Then solve this
two-step problem: 3 packs of 9 pencils, minus 11 pencils given away.
How many pencils are left?

**Teacher note:** likely misconception — claiming memorization for
facts still solved by slow counting, or hiding the *Practice* pile.
