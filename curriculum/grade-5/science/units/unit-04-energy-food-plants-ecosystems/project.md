# Project — Ecosystem Energy & Matter Model

**Unit 04 · Sessions 11–12 (Week 17) · two ~40-minute sessions, plus the Lesson 05 draft**

## The task

Build a labeled model of one ecosystem — a meadow, a pond, a forest, or a garden — that shows **both** stories from this unit:

1. **The energy story:** a flow chart (or drawn diagram) tracing energy from the sun through at least one food chain of three or more living things, with arrows pointing toward each eater.
2. **The matter story:** a loop diagram showing matter moving among plants, animals, decomposers, and the environment, with arrows that return to a producer.
3. **A written explanation** (about half a page, or dictated to the adult): one paragraph tracing the energy back to the sun, one paragraph tracing matter through the decomposer step and back to a plant, and one sentence stating the flow-versus-cycle contrast.

You may build the model as a poster, a labeled drawing, or a diorama drawing on paper. If you use the meadow picture from Lesson 05, your model must add something new — a second chain, a different ecosystem, or a complete matter loop the picture does not draw for you.

## Materials

Poster paper or a large sheet, pencils and colored pencils, science notebook (for the Lesson 05 draft), the leaf-litter observation notes from Lesson 04. The adult supervises any outdoor collection; nothing is tasted, and wild plants and mushrooms are drawn or described, never collected for eating.

## Steps

### Session 11 — Build the model

1. Choose your ecosystem and list its living things: at least two producers, at least two consumers in one chain, and at least one decomposer.
2. Draw the energy chain first: sun → producer → consumer → consumer, arrows toward the eater. Label each living thing with its role (producer, herbivore, carnivore, omnivore, decomposer).
3. Draw the matter loop on the same model in a different color: plant → animal → decomposer → soil/air/water → plant. Check that the loop closes — it must return to a producer.
4. Draft the written explanation from your Lesson 05 "life story in two tracings."

### Session 12 — Present and revise

1. Present your model to the adult: trace the energy chain aloud, then trace the matter loop aloud.
2. The adult asks two challenge questions (for example: "What happens if the decomposers vanish?" or "Where did the top eater's energy originally come from?"). Answer with evidence from the unit.
3. Revise one thing on the model based on the feedback — a corrected arrow, a missing decomposer step, or a clearer label — and note the revision on the back of the model.

## Deliverables

- The labeled model (energy chain + matter loop, both visible and distinguishable).
- The written explanation (energy paragraph, matter paragraph, contrast sentence).
- A one-line revision note describing what you changed in Session 12 and why.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (3) | Good (2) | Needs work (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Energy model (5-PS3-1)** | Chain of 3+ living things from the sun; all arrows toward the eater; four body uses named in the explanation | Chain present with the sun; one arrow reversed or one body use missing | Chain missing the sun, or arrows point from eater to food |
| **Matter model (5-LS2-1)** | Loop closes back to a producer; decomposer step named; non-food matter (air, water, soil materials) named as plant input | Loop present but stops at soil, or decomposer step unnamed | No loop — a straight line, or no decomposer step at all |
| **Argument from evidence (5-LS1-1)** | Explanation cites at least one weighing or observation fact (willow numbers, leaf-bag numbers, or the learner's own leaf-litter notes) for the air-and-water claim | Claim stated but evidence is vague ("plants grow from air") with no number or observation | Claim missing, or "plants eat soil" stated without correction |
| **Flow-versus-cycle contrast** | One clear sentence: energy flows one way and does not return to the sun; matter cycles and returns to producers | Contrast attempted but one half is wrong (energy as a cycle, or matter as a line) | No contrast sentence, or both halves confused |
| **Communication and revision** | Labels readable; roles labeled; revision note names a real change and its reason | Labels mostly readable; revision note vague | Labels missing or unreadable; no revision note |

**Scoring:** 13–15 = ready to move on; 10–12 = ready with a short reteach on the weakest criterion (see teacher guide); below 10 = reteach that criterion with the matching lesson before the unit assessment.

## Accessibility and alternatives

The learner may dictate the explanation while the adult writes it, build the model with picture cards instead of drawings, or present orally with the model as a visual aid. If outdoor collection is impossible, the learner models an ecosystem from the Lesson 05 image and the resource pack, with the same required parts.
