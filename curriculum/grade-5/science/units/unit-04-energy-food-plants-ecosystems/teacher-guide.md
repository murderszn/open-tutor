# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Energy in Food, Plants, and Ecosystems

**For the adult/teacher. Do not hand this file to the learner before the lessons — it contains solutions, misconception notes, and scoring guidance. The learner's answer key is the separate `answer-key.md`, used only for scoring after the learner has tried the work.**

## Unit at a glance

- **Standards:** 5-PS3-1 (energy in food was once sunlight), 5-LS1-1 (plants grow chiefly from air and water), 5-LS2-1 (matter movement among plants, animals, decomposers, environment).
- **Boundaries held throughout:** no molecular explanations of photosynthesis; no mass/weight distinction (scale readings are called weight); energy language stays at the four body uses (repair, growth, motion, warmth); deep-ocean vent food chains are named only as a later exception, not taught.
- **The one idea to protect:** energy flows one way; matter cycles. If a learner leaves the unit with only one sentence, it should be that contrast — and the unit returns to it in Lessons 03 and 05, the project, the quiz, and the assessment.

## Materials (whole unit)

Potted plant or seedling (optional, Lesson 01 application), kitchen scale (grams), science notebook, pencils and colored pencils, poster paper (project), clear lidded container or tray, fallen leaves, garden soil, spray bottle, gloves, hand lens. The adult collects leaf litter and checks the Lesson 04 container setup.

## Safety (non-negotiable)

- The adult supervises every investigation and handles the Lesson 04 container setup.
- **No tasting** of investigation materials; **never eat wild mushrooms, berries, or plants** — the mushrooms in the generated image are an illustration of decomposers, not food.
- Wash hands after handling soil or leaf litter; wear gloves for leaf-litter handling.
- Wild animals are observed, never approached or handled. Do not grow mold indoors on bread or other food (spores can irritate lungs); the unit observes outdoor leaf litter instead.
- **No-outdoor-access alternative:** the adult collects a tray of leaf litter for indoor observation, or the learner works from the Lesson 04 practice dataset and the Lesson 05 image/text alternative. The same evidence (softening, darkening, breaking into pieces, measured weight loss with matter moved, not vanished) is taught either way.

## Session notes

| Session | Watch for | If it goes wrong |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (L01) | Learners who accept "164 pounds" but still say "from the soil" | Re-do the subtraction aloud: 164 lb gained vs. 1/8 lb lost. Ask: "Could 1/8 of a pound of soil become 164 pounds of tree?" |
| 3 (L02) | Arrows drawn from eater to food | Point at the eater: "Who receives the energy?" Arrows point to the receiver. Re-draw one chain together. |
| 5 (L03) | Matter loop stopping at soil | Ask: "Then where do new plants get their matter?" Add the return arrows to a plant together. |
| 7 (L04) | Predictions stated after peeking at results | Record predictions before the container is examined. A wrong prediction is praised as useful evidence (Lesson 04, Worked example 2). |
| 9 (L05) | Mushrooms labeled as producers | Ask: "Do mushrooms make food from sunlight, or do they break down dead material?" The second answer is the decomposer role. |
| 11–12 (Project) | Energy chain and matter loop drawn in the same color, confusing the two stories | Require two colors (suggested: yellow for energy, green for matter) and check the loop closes. |
| 13–14 (Midyear review) | Learners reciting unit titles instead of claims | Require the format: claim + one evidence fact, for each of Units 01–04 (see below). |
| 15–16 (Quiz, assessment) | Quiz used as a final grade | The quiz is formative by design; use its thresholds to reteach, then assess. |

## Midyear review (Sessions 13–14) — claim + evidence bank

Each learner states one claim with evidence from each earlier unit, then corrects one earlier misconception:

- **Unit 01:** a fair test changes one thing and repeats trials (evidence: the learner's own fair-test design, or the damp-vs-dry leaf test in Assessment A10).
- **Unit 02:** matter is made of particles too small to see (evidence: sugar "vanishing" in water while the mixture's weight stays the same).
- **Unit 03:** the total weight of matter is conserved through changes of form (evidence: sealed-bag ice 120 g → melted water 120 g).
- **Unit 04:** plants grow chiefly from air and water; energy flows one way; matter cycles (evidence: willow numbers; food-chain and matter-loop models).

Reteach trigger: if a learner cannot state a Unit 01–03 claim with evidence, re-run that unit's key investigation in miniature (one weighing, one fair-test design) before the unit assessment.

## Misconceptions and reteach steps

| Misconception | What to say / do |
|---|---|
| "Plants eat soil." | Re-do the willow subtraction (164 lb vs. ~2 oz). Then ask where a potted plant's soil would go if the plant ate it — the pot would empty, and it does not. |
| "Van Helmont proved water alone." | Name what he did not test: air. His experiment rules out soil; it does not separate water from air. Modern claim: chiefly air **and** water. |
| "Food-chain arrows point from eater to food." | "Arrows show energy moving. Who receives it?" Re-draw with the eater at the arrowhead. |
| "The top predator has the most energy." | Energy is used at every step (motion, warmth, staying alive), so less passes on each time. The producer end holds the most. No numbers needed. |
| "Dead things disappear." | Unit 03 rule + Lesson 04 graph: matter moves (soil, air, decomposers' bodies). Ask "what left the system?" for every weight drop. |
| "Soil is the end of the matter story." | Ask where new plants get matter. Close the loop back to a producer. |
| "Mushrooms are producers." | Producers make food from sunlight. Mushrooms break down dead material — the decomposer role. |
| "Energy cycles back to the sun." | Energy is used for motion, warmth, growth, repair. New energy enters from the sun; matter is what cycles. |
| "Decomposers don't matter / are just gross." | Remove them in thought: dead matter piles up, soil stops receiving returned matter, new plants run short. The loop breaks. |

## Differentiation

- **Reading/language access:** read tables and chains aloud; accept pointing, drawing, or picture-card arrangements for any labeling task.
- **Alternate response modes:** dictated explanations (adult scribes), drawn models, oral presentations for the project.
- **Extensions:** food webs with six or more living things (Lesson 02), the air-role research note (Lesson 01), the animal-waste loop (Lesson 03), species-safe decomposer drawings (Lesson 04). Extensions are optional and never prerequisites for the assessment.

## Reteach triggers (by objective)

- **Objective 1 (5-LS1-1) weak:** re-teach Lesson 01 with a second weighing case (Practice P1-3) before the assessment.
- **Objective 2 (5-PS3-1) weak:** re-teach Lesson 02 arrow direction with two new chains; drill the four body uses as a chant or card sort.
- **Objective 3 (5-LS2-1) weak:** re-teach Lesson 03 with the learner's own loop drawn in two colors; check the loop closes.
- **Objective 4 (contrast) weak:** re-teach Lesson 05 with the line-versus-circle gesture: energy = line from the sun, matter = circle back to the plant.
- **Objective 5 (midyear revision) weak:** run the claim + evidence bank above orally, one unit at a time, before the assessment.

## Project scoring note

Use the rubric in `project.md`. The most common weakness is a matter loop that stops at soil — score that as "Good" at best on the matter criterion and require the return arrows in the Session 12 revision. A model that copies the Lesson 05 image exactly, with nothing added, cannot score "Strong" on the energy or matter criteria: the task requires a second chain, a different ecosystem, or a complete loop the picture does not draw.
