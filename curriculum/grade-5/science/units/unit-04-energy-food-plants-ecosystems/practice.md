# Practice — Unit 04: Energy in Food, Plants, and Ecosystems

Work with the adult on the first items of each set, then try the rest on your own. All numbers below are labeled practice-dataset values, not your measurements, unless a question tells you to weigh something yourself. Answer keys and worked solutions are in `answer-key.md` — do not open that file until you have tried the set.

## Set P1 — Plants grow chiefly from air and water (Lesson 01; Session 2)

**P1-1.** A willow shoot weighs 5 pounds and grows to 169 pounds in five years. Its soil loses about 2 ounces. How many pounds did the tree gain?
**P1-2.** In your own words, why does a soil loss of only about 2 ounces rule out the soil as the main source of the tree's growth?
**P1-3.** A potted plant weighs 10 grams at planting and 90 grams after two months. Its dried soil weighs 300 grams at the start and 297 grams at the end. State the claim (did the growth come chiefly from the soil?) with both numbers as evidence.
**P1-4.** Van Helmont concluded the tree grew from water alone. Name one thing his experiment did not test, and explain why "water alone" is incomplete.
**P1-5.** A learner says, "Plants eat soil the way I eat food." Write a two-sentence correction using the willow numbers.
**P1-6 (retrieval — Unit 03).** A sealed bag of ice weighs 120 grams. After the ice melts, the sealed bag of water weighs 120 grams. State the Unit 03 claim and the evidence.

## Set P2 — Energy-flow diagrams (Lesson 02; Session 6)

**P2-1.** Draw: sun → grass → mouse → owl. Label the producer and each consumer, and check your arrow directions.
**P2-2.** A snake eats a frog that ate flies that ate algae. Write the full chain starting from the sun, and write one sentence tracing the snake's food energy back to the sun.
**P2-3.** Name the four body uses of food energy, and give one example of each for a dog.
**P2-4.** A learner draws arrows pointing from the owl to the mouse to the grass. Explain the error and redraw the chain correctly.
**P2-5.** A bear eats berries and salmon. Draw both of the bear's food chains. What is the combined diagram of many chains in one ecosystem called?
**P2-6.** A learner says, "The owl has the most energy because it is at the top." Explain why the producer end of the chain holds the most energy instead.

## Set P3 — Matter-cycle models (Lesson 03; Session 6)

**P3-1.** Draw the four-step matter loop for a garden: tomato plant → caterpillar → bird → decomposers → soil/air/water → tomato plant. Label each step.
**P3-2.** In one sentence, explain what "matter that is not food is changed by plants into matter that is food" means, using air and water as examples.
**P3-3.** A learner's model ends at "soil." Explain what is missing and add the missing arrows.
**P3-4.** A dead tree falls in a forest. Name three places its matter can go over the next year, and name the living things that move it there.
**P3-5.** A garden's compost is spread on the beds each spring. Explain how last year's dead plants help this year's plants grow, using the loop.
**P3-6.** Write two sentences that keep the energy story and the matter story apart: one sentence about energy flow, one about matter cycling, for the same food chain.

## Set P4 — Decomposers and decay data (Lesson 04; Session 10)

Use the Lesson 04 practice dataset (leaf bag: Week 0 = 40 grams, Week 2 = 34 grams, Week 4 = 27 grams) unless a question gives new numbers.

**P4-1.** How many grams did the leaf bag lose from Week 0 to Week 4? Show the subtraction.
**P4-2.** Name three places the lost leaf matter could have gone.
**P4-3.** A second leaf bag starts at 60 grams and weighs 45 grams after four weeks. How many grams left the bag? Is the matter gone? Explain.
**P4-4.** A learner says, "The leaves rotted, so their matter is gone forever." Write a two-sentence correction using the decomposer loop.
**P4-5.** Why is growing mold indoors on bread a bad classroom investigation, and what does this unit do instead? (Answer: mold spores can irritate lungs and spread; the unit observes outdoor leaf litter and uses a labeled practice dataset.)
**P4-6 (retrieval — Unit 01).** A learner tests whether damp leaves decay faster than dry leaves, but changes the leaf type, the container, and the water at the same time. Explain why this is not a fair test, and name the one thing she should change.

## Set P5 — Mixed review and error analysis (Lessons 01–05; Session 10)

**P5-1.** A tree gains 80 pounds while its soil loses 1 ounce. State the claim and the evidence in one sentence.
**P5-2.** Draw a meadow energy chain with four living things (not grass → rabbit → fox). Trace the top eater's food energy back to the sun in one sentence.
**P5-3.** Draw a matter loop for a pond (algae → small fish → heron → decomposers → water/mud → algae). Check that your arrows return to a producer.
**P5-4 (error analysis).** A learner labels mushrooms as producers because they grow from the ground. Explain the error in two sentences.
**P5-5 (error analysis).** A learner draws the energy story as a circle returning to the sun. Explain why energy flows one way while matter cycles, using the meadow picture as your example.
**P5-6 (retrieval — Unit 02).** A learner says, "Sugar dissolved in water is gone because you can't see it." Use Unit 02's particle-model idea (particles too small to see) and Unit 03's weighing idea to correct her in two sentences.
