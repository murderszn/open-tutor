# Lesson 02 — Energy in Food Was Once Sunlight

**Unit 04 · Session 3 (Week 15) · ~40 minutes · Investigation lesson**

## Goal

You will draw food-chain models that show the energy in an animal's food was once energy from the sun, and you will name the four things animals use that energy for.

## Materials

Science notebook, pencil, colored pencils if available. No investigation materials are needed today: this lesson builds models from the food-chain cases below.

## Readiness check (3 minutes)

Yesterday you argued that a tree's growth material comes chiefly from air and water. But a tree also needs **energy** to grow. Where does a plant's energy come from, and where does a rabbit's energy come from? Say one idea for each. Today's lesson connects them into a single chain.

## Teaching

### Part A — The sun starts nearly every food chain

A **producer** is a living thing that makes its own food using sunlight energy — green plants are the producers you know best. A **consumer** is a living thing that gets its energy by eating other living things. Consumers that eat plants are **herbivores**; consumers that eat other animals are **carnivores**; consumers that eat both plants and animals are **omnivores**.

A **food chain** is a model — a diagram — showing one path of energy through living things. The arrows in a food-chain diagram point in the direction the **energy moves**, which means each arrow points **toward the eater**:

**sun → grass → rabbit → fox**

Read it like this: sunlight energy is captured by the grass as the grass grows; the rabbit eats the grass and takes in some of that energy; the fox eats the rabbit and takes in some of that energy. The arrows point from food to eater, never the other way.

The energy in the rabbit's food — and then in the fox's food — **was once energy from the sun**. That is the claim of NGSS 5-PS3-1, and it is true for nearly every food chain on Earth. (Deep-ocean vent communities are a rare exception scientists study later; they are not part of this grade-5 unit.)

### Part B — What animals do with food energy

Food gives animals two things at once: **materials** for body repair and growth, and **energy** for motion and for maintaining body warmth. The four body uses to memorize are:

1. **Body repair** — fixing a scraped knee, replacing worn-out body parts.
2. **Growth** — a baby rabbit becoming a full-size rabbit.
3. **Motion** — running, hopping, swimming, flying.
4. **Maintaining body warmth** — staying warm on a cold day (animals that keep a steady body temperature use food energy to do this).

**Worked example 1 — tracing a chain and naming the uses.** Chain: sun → pond algae → small fish → heron. The heron eats the small fish. The energy in the fish was once sunlight energy captured by the algae. The heron uses that energy to fly (motion), to grow, to repair feathers, and to stay warm. A common error is to draw the arrows backward (heron → fish → algae). Backward arrows say the energy flows from the eater into its food, which is the opposite of what happens: the eater *receives* the energy, so arrows point *to* the eater.

**Worked example 2 — a chain with an omnivore.** A bear eats berries (a plant) and also eats fish (an animal). Draw two chains, not one: sun → berry bush → bear, and sun → algae → small fish → bear. The bear appears in both chains because an omnivore eats at more than one step. A food **web** is what you get when you combine all the chains in one ecosystem — many overlapping paths instead of a single line. You will build one in the project.

## Guided practice

Work with the adult. Use the chains below.

**L2-G1.** Draw: sun → clover → grasshopper → sparrow. Label each living thing producer, herbivore, carnivore, or omnivore, and check that every arrow points toward the eater.
**L2-G2.** A rabbit eats grass all day. Name all four body uses its food energy supports, and give one rabbit example for each (for example, motion: hopping away from the fox).
**L2-G3.** A learner draws: sun ← grass ← rabbit ← fox (arrows pointing away from the fox). Explain the error and redraw the chain correctly.

## Independent practice

**L2-I1.** Draw a food chain with four living things from a meadow (not the grass–rabbit–fox chain). Label the producer and each consumer, and write one sentence tracing the energy back to the sun.
**L2-I2.** A fox eats a rabbit. Where did the energy in the rabbit's body come from, step by step, back to the sun? Write the chain and one sentence of explanation.
**L2-I3 (error analysis).** A learner says, "The fox has the most energy in the chain because it is the biggest and strongest." Explain why this is wrong: energy is lost at each step (used for motion, warmth, and staying alive), so the producer end of the chain holds the most energy, not the top eater. (You do not need numbers for this — the direction is what matters.)

## Application

Write down one meal you ate recently. Trace each item back as far as you can toward the sun: for a plant food, the chain is short (sun → plant → you); for an animal food, add the animal's food step. Draw at least one complete chain for your meal and label yourself as the omnivore at the end.

## Exit check

Answer in one or two sentences: *A hawk eats a mouse that ate seeds. Where did the hawk's food energy originally come from, and what are two things the hawk uses it for?*

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading/language access:** the adult reads the chain examples aloud; the learner may build chains with picture cards or drawings instead of writing names.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may dictate the chain while the adult writes it, or act out the chain (sun → plant → eater) with gestures.
- **Extension:** pick one ecosystem from the resource pack and draw its food web (at least six living things, at least three chains sharing one producer). Bring it to the project sessions.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: arrows pointing from eater to food, and "the top predator has the most energy." Both are addressed in the worked examples. See the teacher guide for reteach steps. Answer-key reference: Lesson 02 section of `../answer-key.md`.
