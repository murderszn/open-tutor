# Lesson 03 — Matter Moves Among Plants, Animals, Decomposers, and the Environment

**Unit 04 · Session 5 (Week 16) · ~40 minutes · Investigation lesson**

## Goal

You will build a model showing how matter moves among plants, animals, decomposers, and the environment — and you will explain how matter that is not food becomes food through plants.

## Materials

Science notebook, pencil, colored pencils if available. No investigation materials are needed today: this lesson builds models from the cases below, and the hands-on decomposer observation starts in Lesson 04.

## Readiness check (3 minutes)

In Lesson 02 you traced **energy** from the sun to a fox. Today you trace **matter** — the actual stuff. When a rabbit eats grass, grass-matter becomes rabbit-matter. When the rabbit dies, where does rabbit-matter go? Say one idea. If your answer is "it disappears," remember Unit 03: matter does not vanish; it moves or changes form.

## Teaching

### Part A — The matter loop

A **decomposer** is a living thing — such as fungi (mushrooms) and tiny bacteria — that breaks down dead plants and animals into materials that return to the soil, air, and water. An **ecosystem** is a community of living things together with the air, water, and soil around them.

Here is the matter loop for a meadow, in four steps:

1. **Plants make food-matter.** A plant takes in matter that is **not food** — air, water, and decomposed materials in the soil — and, using sunlight energy, changes it into plant matter: leaves, stems, roots. This is the step Lesson 01 proved indirectly: the tree's new matter came chiefly from air and water, not from the soil.
2. **Animals eat plant-matter (or other animals).** A rabbit eats grass; grass-matter becomes rabbit-matter — some for growth and repair, some passed out as waste.
3. **Decomposers break down dead matter.** When the grass, the rabbit, or the fox dies, decomposers break their remains down. Their bodies' matter moves into the soil, the air, and the water — and into the decomposers' own bodies as they grow.
4. **Plants use it again.** New plants take up that returned matter from air, water, and soil, and the loop continues.

Notice the key sentence from NGSS 5-LS2-1: matter that is **not food** (air, water, decomposed materials in soil) is **changed by plants into matter that is food**. Plants are the only step in the loop that turns non-food matter into food-matter. Without plants, the loop has no way back to food.

### Part B — Matter cycles; energy does not

Lesson 02's energy story and today's matter story sound similar but end differently:

- **Energy** flows **one way**: sun → plant → animal → animal. At each step some energy is used for motion, warmth, and staying alive, so it does not all pass on — and it never flows back to the sun.
- **Matter** **cycles**: the same matter can be plant-matter, then rabbit-matter, then soil-matter, then plant-matter again, many times over.

**Worked example 1 — the fallen leaf.** A maple leaf falls in autumn. Fungi and tiny soil animals break it down over winter. By spring the leaf is gone as a leaf — but its matter is not gone. Some of it is now in the fungi's bodies, some has become rich dark soil, and some has moved into the air and water. A new maple seedling takes up matter from that soil, air, and water and grows new leaves. Draw this as a loop with arrows: leaf → decomposers → soil/air/water → seedling → new leaf. A common error is to draw the loop as a straight line ending at "soil." Soil is not the end: plants pull matter back out of soil, air, and water, so the arrows must return to a plant.

**Worked example 2 — the dead fish in a pond.** A fish dies and sinks. Bacteria and other decomposers break its body down; its matter moves into the water and the pond mud. Algae (producers) take up matter from the water and grow; a small fish eats the algae. The matter that was once fish-body is now, in part, algae-body and then small-fish-body. The energy story is separate: the energy that once powered the dead fish does not cycle back — new energy comes from the sun to the algae. Keep the two stories apart in your model: label energy arrows and matter arrows differently (for example, yellow arrows for energy flow, green arrows for matter movement).

## Guided practice

Work with the adult.

**L3-G1.** Draw the four-step matter loop for a meadow, using grass, a rabbit, a fox, and mushrooms. Label each step 1–4 from Part A and check that your arrows return to a plant.
**L3-G2.** A learner draws: grass → rabbit → fox → soil (stop). Explain what is missing and add the missing step.
**L3-G3.** In one sentence each, answer: (a) What matter that is not food do plants change into food? (b) What living things return dead matter to the environment?

## Independent practice

**L3-I1.** Draw a matter-cycle model for a pond: algae, small fish, heron, bacteria (decomposers). Show the loop returning to the algae.
**L3-I2.** A garden's dead plants are composted and the compost is spread on the garden. Explain, using the loop, how last year's dead plants help this year's plants grow.
**L3-I3 (error analysis).** A learner says, "When a rabbit dies and rots away, its matter is gone forever." Use the Unit 03 rule (matter is conserved; it moves or changes form) and the decomposer step to explain why this is wrong, and name two places the rabbit's matter can go.

## Application

Look at a compost bin, a leaf pile, or a patch of soil with fallen leaves (adult supervises; wash hands after). Find one sign that matter is moving rather than vanishing — for example, leaves that are thinner, darker, or broken into pieces, or soil that is darker under the leaves. Record the sign and write which loop step it shows.

## Exit check

Answer in one or two sentences: *A fox dies in a meadow. In your own words, trace its matter back to a plant, naming the decomposer step.*

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading/language access:** the adult reads the four steps aloud; the learner may build the loop with picture cards or drawings instead of writing sentences.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may dictate the loop while the adult writes it, or build it as a circle of labeled drawings.
- **Extension:** add a second loop to your model showing what happens to animal waste (droppings) — decomposers break it down too, and its matter also returns to soil, air, and water.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: "dead things disappear," "soil is the end of the chain," and mixing up the energy story with the matter story. All three are addressed in the worked examples. See the teacher guide for reteach steps. Answer-key reference: Lesson 03 section of `../answer-key.md`.
