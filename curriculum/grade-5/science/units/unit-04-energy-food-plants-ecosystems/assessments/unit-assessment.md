# Unit Assessment — Unit 04: Energy in Food, Plants, and Ecosystems

**Culminating assessment · Session 16 (Week 18) · ~40 minutes · 12 tasks**

Answer in the notebook. The adult scores with `answer-key.md`. Open responses are scored on the reasoning shown, not on matching one exact sentence: the key gives the required elements for each task.

## Tasks

**A1 (5-LS1-1 — argument).** Write a short paragraph (3–4 sentences) arguing that plants get the materials they need for growth chiefly from air and water, not from the soil. Use the van Helmont numbers (5-pound shoot, 169-pound tree, soil loss of about 2 ounces) and name one limit of his experiment.

**A2 (5-LS1-1 — new case).** A sunflower seedling weighs 3 grams. After a summer it weighs 400 grams. Its pot of soil lost 5 grams. State the claim and the evidence (two numbers), and explain why the soil cannot be the chief source.

**A3 (5-PS3-1 — model).** Draw a food chain with four living things from a pond (not the meadow grass–rabbit–fox chain). Label the producer and each consumer, draw the arrows correctly, and write one sentence tracing the top eater's food energy back to the sun.

**A4 (5-PS3-1 — body uses).** Name all four things animals use food energy for, and give one example of each for a horse.

**A5 (5-LS2-1 — model).** Draw a matter-cycle model for a forest: oak tree → caterpillar → bird → decomposers → soil/air/water → oak tree. Label each step and check that your arrows return to a producer. Then write one sentence explaining what plants do in the loop that no other step does.

**A6 (5-LS2-1 — prediction).** Suppose all decomposers vanished from a meadow overnight. Predict two things that would happen over the following year, and explain each with the matter loop.

**A7 (5-PS3-1, 5-LS2-1 — image task).** Use the Lesson 05 meadow picture or its text alternative. (a) Name one producer, one consumer, and one decomposer in it. (b) Draw the energy chain sun → grass → rabbit → fox. (c) Draw the matter loop starting from the fallen leaves and returning to the grass.

**A8 (error analysis).** A learner labels the mushrooms in the meadow picture as producers "because they grow in the ground like plants." Explain the error in two sentences.

**A9 (error analysis).** A learner draws the energy story as a circle that returns to the sun. Explain why this is wrong, and state the correct contrast between the energy story and the matter story.

**A10 (midyear — Unit 01 fair test).** A learner wants to test whether damp leaves decay faster than dry leaves. She sets up two leaf containers that differ only in dampness (same leaf type, same container, same temperature, same time). Is this a fair test? Explain, naming the one thing changed and two things kept the same.

**A11 (midyear — Unit 02 particle model).** A learner stirs sugar into water. The sugar seems to vanish. Use the particle-model idea (matter is made of particles too small to see) and a weighing argument to explain where the sugar went.

**A12 (midyear — Units 03–04 combined).** Two weight drops: (a) a fizzing mixture in an open cup weighs less after fizzing; (b) a leaf bag weighs 13 grams less after four weeks outdoors. What single principle explains both drops, and what is different about the two cases? (Hint: in one case matter left quickly as a gas; in the other, matter moved slowly into soil, air, and decomposers' bodies.)

## Scoring (adult use)

Each task is scored 0–2: 2 = all required elements present and correct; 1 = partly correct or one required element missing; 0 = missing or fundamentally wrong. Total out of 24.

- **20–24:** objective met; move on to Unit 05 with the project feedback noted.
- **15–19:** objective mostly met; reteach the missed tasks' lessons briefly (teacher guide) and re-check with one practice set.
- **Below 15:** reteach the weakest objectives with the matching lessons before Unit 05; keep the assessment as a diagnostic, not a final grade.
