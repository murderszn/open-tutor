# Answer Key — Unit 04: Energy in Food, Plants, and Ecosystems

**For the adult. Every item in the lessons, practice sets, quiz, and assessment is solved here. Items were solved independently from the questions and then reconciled with the lesson text. Accept equivalent wording when the required elements are present.**

## Lesson 01 — Plants grow chiefly from air and water

- **L1-G1.** Plant gain = 62 − 2 = **60 grams**.
- **L1-G2.** Soil loss = 500 − 498 = **2 grams**.
- **L1-G3.** Claim: the plant's new growth did **not** come chiefly from the soil. Evidence: the plant gained 60 grams while the soil lost only 2 grams — far too little to supply 60 grams of growth.
- **L1-I1.** Gain = 400 − 3 = **397 grams**; soil lost 5 grams. Claim: the growth did not come chiefly from the soil. Evidence: 397 grams gained, 5 grams of soil lost.
- **L1-I2.** Wrong: the soil actually lost only about 2 ounces, not 164 pounds. The small loss shows the soil supplied almost none of the tree's 164-pound gain; the growth material came chiefly from elsewhere (water added, and air).
- **L1-I3.** Too **low**: the fallen leaves' matter was never weighed, so the measured final tree weight understates the tree's total growth.

## Lesson 02 — Energy in food was once sunlight

- **L2-G1.** Sun → clover (producer) → grasshopper (herbivore) → sparrow (carnivore in this chain). All arrows point toward the eater.
- **L2-G2.** Repair: healing a cut or replacing worn feathers; growth: a young rabbit becoming full-size; motion: hopping away from a fox; warmth: staying warm on a cold day. (Any sensible rabbit example for each use is acceptable.)
- **L2-G3.** Error: the arrows are reversed — they point from the eater toward its food. Correct chain: sun → grass → rabbit → fox.
- **L2-I1.** Sample acceptable chain: sun → dandelion → caterpillar → robin. Labels: dandelion producer, caterpillar herbivore, robin carnivore in this chain. Sentence: the robin's food energy was once sunlight energy captured by the dandelion.
- **L2-I2.** Chain: sun → grass → rabbit → fox. Explanation: the fox's food energy came from the rabbit, whose food energy came from grass, which captured sunlight energy.
- **L2-I3.** Wrong: energy is used at each step (motion, warmth, staying alive), so less energy passes on each time. The producer end holds the most energy; the top eater holds the least. Size and strength do not decide energy.

## Lesson 03 — Matter moves among plants, animals, decomposers, and the environment

- **L3-G1.** Steps: (1) grass makes food-matter from air, water, and soil materials using sunlight; (2) rabbit eats grass — grass-matter becomes rabbit-matter; (3) fox eats rabbit; when grass, rabbit, or fox dies, mushrooms and other decomposers break the remains down; (4) matter returns to soil, air, and water, and new grass takes it up. Arrows must return to the grass.
- **L3-G2.** Missing: the return step — matter from the soil/air/water must be taken up by a new plant. Add: soil/air/water → grass, closing the loop.
- **L3-G3.** (a) Air, water, and decomposed materials in the soil. (b) Decomposers (fungi, bacteria, and small soil animals).
- **L3-I1.** Loop: algae → small fish → heron → decomposers (bacteria) → water/mud → algae. Arrows return to the algae.
- **L3-I2.** Last year's dead plants were broken down by decomposers; their matter returned to the soil, air, and water; this year's plants take up that matter and grow. The loop, not new matter from nowhere, supplies the garden.
- **L3-I3.** Wrong: matter is conserved — it moves or changes form, it does not vanish. The rabbit's matter goes into decomposers' bodies, into the soil, and into the air and water (any two named places are acceptable).

## Lesson 04 — Decomposers at work

- **L4-G1.** Week 0 → Week 2: 40 − 34 = **6 grams**. Week 2 → Week 4: 34 − 27 = **7 grams**. The second two-week period lost more.
- **L4-G2.** Any three of: decomposers' bodies (as they ate and grew), the soil underneath (dark crumbly material), the air (as gas).
- **L4-G3.** Sample revision: "Dead leaves do change: in four weeks the leaves softened, darkened, broke into pieces, and lost 13 grams, because decomposers broke them down."
- **L4-I1.** 50 − 38 = **12 grams** left the bag. Two places: soil, air, or decomposers' bodies (any two).
- **L4-I2.** Without decomposers, dead matter piles up instead of breaking down; matter stops returning to soil, air, and water; new plants eventually run short of material. Decomposers keep the loop running.
- **L4-I3.** Unfair: drying removes water, so the weight loss measures water loss, not decomposition. Fair redo: keep water conditions the same for both weighings, or compare two bags kept the same way, one with decomposers present and one kept clean — changing only the decomposer condition (Unit 01 fair test).

## Lesson 05 — Energy flows, matter cycles (image activity)

- **L5-G1.** Producer: grass or wildflowers. Herbivore: rabbit. Carnivore: fox. Decomposer: mushrooms (fungi).
- **L5-G2.** Chain: sun → grass → rabbit → fox, arrows toward each eater. Sentence: the fox's food energy was once sunlight energy captured by the grass.
- **L5-G3.** Loop: fallen leaves → mushrooms/soil life (decomposers) → soil, air, and water → grass and flowers. The arrows close back at the grass.
- **L5-I1.** No. A food chain describes what an animal eats over time, not one moment. The fox's body was built from the energy and matter of animals like the rabbit, whether or not it is hunting in the picture.
- **L5-I2.** Two predictions (any two sensible ones): dead leaves and dead animals pile up; soil stops receiving returned matter; new grass eventually runs short of material; the meadow's matter loop breaks.
- **L5-I3.** Error: mushrooms do not make their own food from sunlight, so they are not producers. They get energy and matter by breaking down dead material — the decomposer role.

## Practice Set P1 — Air and water

- **P1-1.** 169 − 5 = **164 pounds**.
- **P1-2.** 2 ounces is 1/8 of a pound — far too little to supply 164 pounds of tree. If the tree ate soil, the soil would have lost about as much as the tree gained.
- **P1-3.** Gain = 90 − 10 = **80 grams**; soil loss = 300 − 297 = **3 grams**. Claim: the growth did not come chiefly from the soil (80 grams gained, 3 grams lost).
- **P1-4.** He did not test air's role — the tree sat in open air for five years and air was never weighed. "Water alone" ignores the air contribution; the modern claim is chiefly air **and** water.
- **P1-5.** Sample correction: "Plants do not eat soil. A willow gained 164 pounds while its soil lost only about 2 ounces, so the growth material came chiefly from air and water."
- **P1-6.** Claim: the total weight of matter stays the same when ice melts. Evidence: 120 grams before, 120 grams after.

## Practice Set P2 — Energy-flow diagrams

- **P2-1.** Sun → grass (producer) → mouse (herbivore) → owl (carnivore). Arrows toward each eater.
- **P2-2.** Sun → algae → flies → frog → snake. Sentence: the snake's food energy was once sunlight energy captured by the algae.
- **P2-3.** Repair, growth, motion, maintaining body warmth. Dog examples (any sensible ones): repair — healing a cut paw; growth — a puppy becoming full-size; motion — running; warmth — staying warm in winter.
- **P2-4.** Error: arrows reversed. Correct: sun → grass → mouse → owl (arrows toward the eater).
- **P2-5.** Chains: sun → berry bush → bear; sun → algae → small fish → salmon → bear (or sun → algae → small fish → bear, if the salmon step is simplified — either is acceptable if arrows and roles are correct). The combined diagram is a **food web**.
- **P2-6.** Energy is used at each step, so less passes on each time. The producer end holds the most energy; the top eater holds the least.

## Practice Set P3 — Matter-cycle models

- **P3-1.** Loop: tomato plant (1: makes food-matter from air, water, soil materials) → caterpillar (2: eats plant) → bird (3: eats caterpillar) → decomposers (4: break down dead matter) → soil/air/water → tomato plant. Arrows return to the tomato plant.
- **P3-2.** Sample: plants take in air, water, and decomposed soil materials — things that are not food — and turn them into plant matter, which is food for animals.
- **P3-3.** Missing: the return arrows from soil/air/water back to a plant. Add: soil/air/water → grass.
- **P3-4.** Three places (any three): decomposers' bodies, the soil, the air, the water. Movers: fungi, bacteria, worms, and other small soil animals.
- **P3-5.** Decomposers broke last year's plants down; their matter returned to soil, air, and water; this year's plants take it up and grow.
- **P3-6.** Sample: "Energy flows one way from the sun through the grass to the rabbit and does not return to the sun. Matter cycles: grass-matter becomes rabbit-matter, and when the rabbit dies, decomposers return its matter to the soil, air, and water for new grass."

## Practice Set P4 — Decomposers and decay data

- **P4-1.** 40 − 27 = **13 grams**.
- **P4-2.** Decomposers' bodies, the soil underneath, the air (any three of these; all three are the expected answers).
- **P4-3.** 60 − 45 = **15 grams** left the bag. No, the matter is not gone: it moved into soil, air, and decomposers' bodies.
- **P4-4.** Sample correction: "Rotted leaves are not gone. Decomposers broke them down, and their matter moved into the soil, the air, and the decomposers' own bodies — that is why the soil underneath gets darker."
- **P4-5.** Mold spores can irritate lungs and spread indoors, so growing mold on bread is unsafe for a classroom. Instead the unit observes outdoor leaf litter (adult-supervised) and uses a labeled practice dataset.
- **P4-6.** Not a fair test: she changed three things at once (leaf type, container, water). A fair test changes one thing — dampness — and keeps everything else the same.

## Practice Set P5 — Mixed review and error analysis

- **P5-1.** Claim: the tree's growth did not come chiefly from the soil. Evidence: 80 pounds gained, 1 ounce of soil lost.
- **P5-2.** Sample: sun → clover → mouse → hawk. Sentence: the hawk's food energy was once sunlight energy captured by the clover.
- **P5-3.** Loop: algae → small fish → heron → decomposers (bacteria) → water/mud → algae. Arrows return to the algae.
- **P5-4.** Error: growing in the ground does not make a producer. Mushrooms do not make food from sunlight; they break down dead material, so they are decomposers.
- **P5-5.** Energy does not cycle: it flows one way from the sun through producers and consumers and is used for motion, warmth, growth, and repair — it never returns to the sun. Matter cycles: the same matter returns through decomposers to soil, air, and water and back into plants. In the meadow picture, the sun → grass → rabbit → fox path is a flow, while the fallen-leaves → mushrooms → soil → grass path is a loop.
- **P5-6.** Correction: the sugar did not vanish — it broke into particles too small to see (Unit 02) and spread through the water. Weighing proves it: the mixture weighs the same as the water plus the sugar weighed separately (Unit 03 conservation).

## Week 18 Quiz

- **Q1.** The tree's growth did not come chiefly from the soil (164 pounds gained vs. about 2 ounces of soil lost).
- **Q2.** No. Evidence: the plant gained 60 grams (62 − 2) while the soil lost only 2 grams.
- **Q3.** Arrows point **toward the eater**, because arrows show the direction energy moves, and the eater receives the energy.
- **Q4.** Body repair, growth, motion, maintaining body warmth.
- **Q5.** Producer: grass or wildflowers. Herbivore: rabbit. Carnivore: fox. Decomposer: mushrooms.
- **Q6.** Missing: the return step — matter from soil, air, and water must be taken up by a new plant (soil/air/water → grass).
- **Q7.** 40 − 27 = **13 grams** left the bag. No, it did not vanish: it moved into the soil, the air, and decomposers' bodies (any two).
- **Q8.** Energy flows one way from the sun through producers and consumers and does not return to the sun. Matter cycles among plants, animals, decomposers, and the environment and returns to producers.
- **Q9.** Chains: sun → berry bush → bear; sun → algae → small fish → salmon → bear (simplified salmon step acceptable). Combined diagram: a **food web**.
- **Q10.** Claim: the total weight of matter stays the same when ice melts. Evidence: 120 grams before, 120 grams after.

## Unit Assessment

- **A1.** Required elements: claim (chiefly air and water, not soil); evidence (5 lb → 169 lb = 164 lb gain; soil loss ~2 oz); one limit (fallen leaves unweighed, or air never tested). Full paragraph scores 2; missing evidence or limit scores 1.
- **A2.** Gain = 400 − 3 = **397 grams**; soil lost 5 grams. Claim: growth did not come chiefly from the soil — 5 grams of soil cannot supply 397 grams of plant.
- **A3.** Sample: sun → pond algae → water flea → small fish → heron. Labels: algae producer; water flea, small fish, heron consumers. Sentence tracing the heron's food energy back to sunlight captured by the algae. Any pond chain with four living things, correct arrows, and labels is acceptable.
- **A4.** Repair: healing a cut; growth: a foal becoming a full-size horse; motion: galloping; warmth: staying warm in winter. (Any sensible horse example per use.)
- **A5.** Loop: oak → caterpillar → bird → decomposers → soil/air/water → oak, arrows returning to the oak. Sentence: plants alone turn non-food matter (air, water, decomposed soil materials) into food-matter; every other step only moves or breaks down matter that is already food.
- **A6.** Two predictions (any two sensible ones with loop reasoning): dead matter piles up; soil stops receiving returned matter; new plants run short of material; the matter loop breaks.
- **A7.** (a) Producer: grass or wildflowers; consumer: rabbit or fox; decomposer: mushrooms. (b) Sun → grass → rabbit → fox, arrows toward each eater. (c) Fallen leaves → mushrooms/soil life → soil, air, water → grass, arrows closing the loop.
- **A8.** Mushrooms do not make food from sunlight, so they are not producers. They get energy and matter by breaking down dead material — the decomposer role.
- **A9.** Wrong: energy does not return to the sun; it is used for motion, warmth, growth, and repair at each step. Correct contrast: energy flows one way from the sun; matter cycles back to producers through decomposers.
- **A10.** Yes, a fair test: one thing changed (dampness) and the rest kept the same (leaf type, container, temperature, time — any two named kept-same factors are acceptable).
- **A11.** The sugar broke into particles too small to see and spread through the water (Unit 02 particle model). It is still there: the mixture weighs the same as the water plus the sugar weighed separately (Unit 03 conservation).
- **A12.** Single principle: matter is conserved — when a measured weight drops, matter **left the system**; it did not vanish. Difference: the fizzing cup loses matter quickly as escaping gas, while the leaf bag loses matter slowly, moved by decomposers into soil, air, and their own bodies over weeks.
