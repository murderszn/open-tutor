# Lesson 04 — More Evidence, and One Non-Example

**Unit 03 · Session 7 (Week 12) · ~40 minutes · Investigation lesson**

## Goal

You will test two more mixings — milk and vinegar, and sand and water — and sort all four of the unit's mixings into "new substance" and "no new substance" using the evidence rules from Lesson 03.

## Materials

Kitchen scale (grams), clear cups, milk, vinegar, sand, water, spoon, coffee filter or cloth, safety glasses or goggles, science notebook. The adult pours all vinegar and supervises filtering. No tasting during investigations.

## Readiness check (3 minutes)

Yesterday's rule: a gas that the mixing itself produces is evidence of a new substance. Today there is no fizz in either test — so what other evidence counts? Re-read the four clues from Lesson 03 and predict: which clue will milk and vinegar show, and which will sand and water show?

## Teaching

### Part A — Milk and vinegar: a new solid appears

The adult pours vinegar into a cup of milk and stirs. White lumps — **curds** — form in a watery liquid (**whey**). The milk no longer looks, pours, or behaves like milk.

> **What you observe:** a solid that was not there before. Filtering the mixture keeps the curds on the filter — but the curds are not milk, and the liquid is not milk either. Neither starting substance can be recovered unchanged.

Claim: **milk + vinegar makes a new substance.** Evidence: a new solid (curds) formed, and the milk's properties changed in a way separating cannot undo. This is the framework's second "does form new substances" example, paired with baking soda and vinegar.

### Part B — Sand and water: still sand, still water

Stir sand into water. The sand sinks. Pour the mixture through a filter: the sand stays on the filter and the water passes through. Dry the sand: it is sand again, with the same color, hardness, and graininess Unit 02 used to identify it.

> **What you observe:** no gas, no new solid, no temperature change, no lasting color change — and both starting substances come back unchanged.

Claim: **sand + water does NOT make a new substance.** It is a **mixture** (Unit 02's word): the parts keep their own properties and can be separated. This is the framework's own non-example, and it is the control case for the whole unit: mixing alone never proves a new substance — the evidence does.

### Part C — The full sort

| Mixing | Gas? | New solid? | Can parts be recovered unchanged? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baking soda + vinegar | Yes | No | No | New substance |
| Milk + vinegar | No | Yes (curds) | No | New substance |
| Sand + water | No | No | Yes (filter) | Mixture — no new substance |
| Sugar + water | No | No | Yes (evaporate the water) | Mixture — no new substance |

**Worked example 1 — using the recovery test.** Claim: *milk + vinegar made a new substance, but sand + water did not.* Evidence: filtering sand and water returns sand and water unchanged; filtering milk and vinegar returns curds and whey, neither of which is a starting substance. Reasoning: if you can get the parts back unchanged, nothing new was made; if you cannot, something new was.

**Worked example 2 — the baking-powder extension (teacher demo or adult-previewed source).** Baking powder is a mixture of baking soda, cream of tartar (a dry acid), and cornstarch. Add water: it fizzes. Test each pair with water — only baking soda + cream of tartar fizzes. Conclusion: those two are the reacting pair, and their reaction makes a gas, just like baking soda + vinegar. This is the American Chemical Society's grade-5 "Exploring Baking Powder" investigation (teacher background; see the resource pack). It shows the evidence rules working on a third case without any new rule.

## Guided practice

Work with the adult. Use the sort table in Part C.

**L4-G1.** A mixture fizzes, then the fizz stops, and filtering does not recover the starting powder. Verdict and evidence?
**L4-G2.** A mixture settles into layers and both layers can be poured off unchanged. Verdict and evidence?
**L4-G3.** Why is sand and water the most important row in the table, even though "nothing happened"? (Hint: what would you conclude if every mixing counted as a new substance?)

## Independent practice

**L4-I1.** Salt + water: the salt dissolves, and evaporating the water leaves salt again. Verdict and evidence? Is dissolving a new-substance change?
**L4-I2.** A learner mixes two clear liquids, the mixture turns yellow, and filtering does not restore the clear liquids. Verdict and evidence? Which clue did you use?
**L4-I3 (error analysis).** A learner says, "Mixing always makes a new substance, because the mixture looks different from the parts." Use sand and water to show why "looks different" is not enough evidence.

## Application

Make a four-card evidence sort for your household (adult previews): one card per mixing from Part C. On the back of each card, write the verdict and the one best piece of evidence. Trade cards with the adult: can they sort your cards correctly from the evidence alone?

## Exit check

Answer in one or two sentences: *Milk and vinegar make curds. Sand and water stay sand and water. Which mixing made a new substance, and what is your evidence?*

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading/language access:** the sort table is kept visible during all practice; "curds" is shown with a picture-word card.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may sort physical samples (filtered sand vs. curds in cups, adult-labeled) instead of writing verdicts.
- **Extension:** run the baking-powder pair tests from Part C's worked example 2 (adult supervises; cream of tartar and cornstarch from the baking aisle). Record which pair fizzes and state the claim.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: "any mixing makes a new substance" and "dissolving makes a new substance" (salt water corrects this: the salt returns). The recovery test is the reteach for both. Answer-key reference: Lesson 04 section of `answer-key.md`.
