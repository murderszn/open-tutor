# Unit 03 — Matter Conservation and Chemical-Change Evidence

**Grade:** 5 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#27](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/27)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 11–14 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 40 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

Everything in this unit answers one question: **when matter changes, does any of it go away — and how can you tell whether something new was made?** The learner first weighs matter through changes that look like loss: ice that melts, sugar that "vanishes" into water. In both cases the scale tells the same story — the weight before and after is the same, even when the eye says otherwise. Then the unit turns to mixing: baking soda and vinegar fizz and make a gas, milk and vinegar make solid curds, but sand and water stay sand and water. The learner learns to sort changes by **evidence of a new substance** — a gas that was not there, a solid that was not there, a color or temperature change that separating cannot undo — and to explain the one honest puzzle: in an open cup, a fizzing mixture weighs *less* afterward, because the gas escaped. In a sealed bag it does not. Conservation is about all the matter, not just the matter still in the cup.

Boundary (held throughout): explanations stay **observable and qualitative**. The unit does not teach an atomic-scale mechanism for reactions, does not name atoms or molecules, and does not distinguish mass from weight — the scale reading is called **weight**, per the 5-PS1 assessment boundaries. Gas is treated as matter because it takes up space, pushes, and (when trapped) adds weight.

## Prerequisites (re-taught here as needed)

- Unit 01 habits: ask a testable question, run a fair test (change one thing, repeat trials), record in labeled tables, graph quantities, and revise a claim when new evidence arrives. Unit 03 opens with the weigh-and-graph routine from Unit 01.
- Unit 02 ideas: matter is made of particles too small to see; materials are identified by observable properties; a mixture keeps the properties of its parts. Lesson 04 reuses the Unit 02 property tests to tell "still the same stuff" from "new stuff."
- Measuring weight to the nearest gram on a kitchen scale (Unit 01 measuring practice, reused here).
- Science safety practices: the adult supervises every investigation, pours vinegar, and enforces eye protection for fizzing mixtures and **no tasting** during investigations.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Measure and graph quantities** to show that the total weight of matter is conserved when water is frozen and melted — the before, frozen, and melted weights are equal within scale error (5-PS1-2).
2. **Measure and graph quantities** to show that the total weight of matter is conserved when sugar dissolves in water — the dissolved mixture weighs the same as water plus sugar weighed separately (5-PS1-2).
3. **Conduct investigations** to determine whether mixing two substances results in new substances, citing at least one piece of evidence (gas formed, new solid formed, temperature change, or a color change that separating cannot undo) for each verdict (5-PS1-4).
4. **Distinguish a new substance from a mixture** by predicting and testing whether the parts can be recovered unchanged (filtering, evaporating, or settling) — sand and water can; baking-soda-and-vinegar products and milk curds cannot (5-PS1-4, with 5-PS1-3 property reasoning from Unit 02).
5. **Explain the open-container result** honestly: when gas escapes an open mixture, the measured weight drops because matter left the system; in a sealed container the weight stays the same — and use that reasoning to defend a conservation claim with evidence (5-PS1-2, science practice: constructing explanations).

## Essential question

*When matter melts, dissolves, or fizzes, is anything lost — and how do you know whether something new was made?*

## Standards and source notes (verified 2026-10-24)

- **NGSS 5-PS1-2** — "Measure and graph quantities to provide evidence that regardless of the type of change that occurs when heating, cooling, or mixing substances, the total weight of matter is conserved." Clarification: examples of reactions or changes include phase changes, dissolving, and mixing that forms new substances. Assessment boundary: distinguishing mass and weight is not included. (Source: NGSS 5-PS1; crosswalk carried from the track audit, scope-and-sequence §7; wording re-confirmed 2026-10-24 against the OKScience Framework bundle analysis for grade 5 "Changes to Matter," which reproduces the 5-PS1-2/5-PS1-4 pair.)
- **NGSS 5-PS1-4** — "Conduct an investigation to determine whether the mixing of two or more substances results in new substances." Official examples: combinations that do **not** produce new substances include sand and water; combinations that **do** include baking soda and vinegar, and milk and vinegar. This unit uses exactly those examples. (Source: NGSS 5-PS1; track audit crosswalk, scope-and-sequence §7.)
- **DCI support (teacher background):** "The amount (weight) of matter is conserved when it changes form, even in transitions in which it seems to vanish" (PS1.A) and "No matter what reaction or change in properties occurs, the total weight of the substances does not change" (PS1.B). Common grade-5 misconceptions listed by the OKScience bundle — "gas makes things lighter," "when matter dissolves or evaporates, it ceases to exist," "a chemical reaction always happens when two substances are combined" — are addressed directly in Lessons 02, 04, and 05 and in the teacher guide.
- **ACS Inquiry in Action, grade 5, chapter 3** (American Chemical Society, opened 2026-10-24): Lesson 3.2, "Exploring Baking Powder," confirms that baking soda + cream of tartar (a dry acid) + water produces carbon dioxide gas, and that the baking-soda-and-vinegar reaction is the same kind of acid reaction. Lesson 04's baking-powder extension follows this source; it is teacher background and an optional extension, not a required learner reading.
- **Practices:** planning and carrying out investigations; analyzing and interpreting data (weighing and graphing); constructing explanations from evidence; using mathematics (bar graphs of before/after weights).

## Safety and materials

All investigations use household materials (ice, water, sugar, salt, sand, baking soda, vinegar, milk, baking powder, cream of tartar, clear cups, sealable bags, kitchen scale, safety glasses or goggles). The **adult pours all vinegar**, checks that bags seal fully before any fizzing investigation, and enforces **eye protection for every fizzing mixture** and **no tasting** during investigations. Fizzing mixtures are never sealed in rigid containers and never heated. If a learner cannot do the vinegar investigations, the teacher guide gives observation alternatives (adult demo, video, or the sealed-bag image activity) that teach the same evidence.
