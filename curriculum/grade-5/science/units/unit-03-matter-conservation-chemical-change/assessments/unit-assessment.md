# Culminating Unit Assessment — Unit 03: Matter Conservation and Chemical-Change Evidence

**Grade 5 Science · ~40 minutes · 12 items + 1 writing task · Adult-assessed; this assessment is not a standardized or validated test.**

Answer in your notebook. All tables are labeled practice data. Solutions and scoring guidance are in `../answer-key.md` (teacher file).

## Part 1 — Conservation (5-PS1-2)

**A1.** A sealed bag holds 140 g of ice. The ice melts. What does the bag weigh now? Explain why in one sentence.

**A2.** A cup holds 125 g of water. A learner adds 35 g of salt and stirs until it dissolves. What does the cup weigh now? Show the addition. Is the salt gone? Explain with the numbers.

**A3.** Use this practice-data table:

| Trial | Before | After | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | 95 g ice | 95 g water | Sealed bag |
| Y | 95 g ice | 88 g water | Open plate, spill |

Trial Y lost 7 g. Does trial Y disprove conservation? Explain using the word "system."

**A4.** Draw a bar graph for trial X (two bars: before, after). Then write one sentence saying what the equal bar heights show.

## Part 2 — New substances (5-PS1-4)

**A5.** Baking soda + vinegar: gas forms, leftover liquid changed. New substance or not? Name the evidence.

**A6.** Milk + vinegar: curds form. New substance or not? Name the evidence.

**A7.** Sand + water: filtered, both parts recovered unchanged. New substance or not? Name the evidence.

**A8.** A learner mixes two liquids. The mixture turns blue and stays blue after filtering, and the original liquids cannot be recovered. New substance or not? Name the evidence and the clue you used.

**A9.** Shaken soapy water makes bubbles, but the water stays soapy water and the bubbles stop when shaking stops. New substance or not? Explain why bubbles alone are not enough evidence.

## Part 3 — Sealed vs. open (5-PS1-2)

**A10.** A sealed bag of baking soda and vinegar weighs 300 g before mixing and 300 g after. An open cup of the same mixture weighs 260 g before and 244 g after. (a) How many grams of gas escaped from the open cup? (b) Does the open-cup result break conservation? Explain in one sentence.

**A11.** An open cup of milk and vinegar weighs 175 g before mixing and 175 g after. Why did this open cup stay the same while the open cup in A10 dropped? (Hint: what is different about the two reactions?)

**A12.** A learner claims, "The open cup in A10 proves that chemical changes destroy matter." Write a two-sentence correction using the sealed-bag result.

## Part 4 — Writing task (claim-evidence-reasoning)

**A13.** A friend says, "When sugar dissolves, it disappears, and when baking soda fizzes, matter is destroyed — things just vanish." Write a short paragraph (4–6 sentences) that corrects both halves of her claim. Use at least two weight facts from this unit (for example, a dissolving weight and a sealed-bag weight) and the words **evidence** and **system**.

**Scoring guidance (teacher use):** Parts 1–3: 1 point per item (A10 counts 2: one for the subtraction, one for the explanation), 13 points total. Part 4: 3 points — 1 for correcting dissolving, 1 for correcting fizzing, 1 for using weight evidence and the system idea. Total 16. 14–16 = secure; 11–13 = review one missed topic; 10 or fewer = reteach with the teacher guide and re-assess with new numbers.
