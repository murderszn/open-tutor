# Project — Conservation Detective: Two Changes, One Claim

**Unit 03 · Sessions 11–12 (Week 13) · ~40 minutes each · Investigation + report**

## The challenge

You are a detective, and your suspects are two changes. One is a **conservation change**: freezing/melting or dissolving, where the weight should stay the same. The other is a **mixing test**: a pair that either makes a new substance or does not. Your job: weigh both, graph both, and write one claim for each — with evidence a skeptic can check.

## Choose your two investigations

**Investigation A — pick one conservation change:**

- A1: Freeze and melt — sealed bag of ice, weighed before and after melting (Lesson 01).
- A2: Dissolve — water + sugar (or salt), weighed separately and as a mixture (Lesson 02).

**Investigation B — pick one mixing test:**

- B1: Baking soda + vinegar (expect a new substance: gas evidence).
- B2: Milk + vinegar (expect a new substance: curd evidence).
- B3: Sand + water (expect no new substance: separation evidence).

The adult approves your choices, pours all vinegar, checks eye protection for B1/B2, and supervises all weighing.

## Materials

Kitchen scale (grams), sealable bags, clear cups, ice or water + sugar/salt, the B-choice materials, coffee filter or cloth (for B3), safety glasses or goggles, science notebook, bar-graph template.

## Steps

1. **Plan (10 minutes).** For each investigation, write: the question, the prediction (with numbers where you can predict them), what you will keep the same (fair test), and what counts as evidence.
2. **Weigh and run (40 minutes across both sessions).** Weigh before, change, weigh after. Record every number in a labeled table — including surprises. If a spill or escape happens, record it honestly; do not redo the number to make it "look right."
3. **Graph (15 minutes).** Draw one bar graph per investigation: before bar vs. after bar, labeled with grams. Equal bars support conservation; for Investigation B, add an evidence note (gas, curds, or separation) beside the graph.
4. **Write the report (25 minutes).** Two short sections, one per investigation. Each section has: **Claim** (one sentence), **Evidence** (the weights and observations), **Reasoning** (why the evidence supports the claim, including the system rule if anything left the container).
5. **Present (10 minutes).** Read one section aloud to the adult. The adult plays skeptic: "How do you know nothing vanished?" and "How do you know something new was made?" Answer from your numbers and observations, not from memory of the lessons.

## Deliverables

- Two labeled before/after tables (one per investigation).
- Two labeled bar graphs.
- A two-section claim-evidence-reasoning report (about one page total; dictation or drawing allowed for the graph labels).
- A one-sentence unit summary: what the two investigations together prove about matter.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (3) | Good (2) | Needs work (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Measuring and graphing** | Before and after weights recorded for both investigations; both graphs labeled with grams and matching the tables | Weights recorded for both; one graph incomplete or mislabeled | Weights missing or graphs do not match the tables |
| **Conservation claim (A)** | Claim states weight stayed the same with both numbers cited; system rule used if anything left the container | Claim correct but evidence incomplete (one number missing) | Claim wrong, or no evidence cited |
| **New-substance verdict (B)** | Verdict correct with the right evidence named (gas, curds, or separation) and the recovery test mentioned | Verdict correct but evidence vague ("it changed") | Verdict wrong, or based on "looks different" alone |
| **Reasoning and honesty** | Reasoning links evidence to claim in both sections; spills/escapes reported honestly instead of hidden | Reasoning present in one section; one surprise unreported | Claims stated without reasoning, or numbers altered to fit predictions |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = ready for the Week 14 review; 7–9 = revise one section with the adult (usually the reasoning); 4–6 = redo one investigation with the adult, focusing on the before weight and the system rule.

## Safety and alternatives

The adult pours vinegar, checks bag seals, and enforces eye protection for fizzing mixtures and no tasting. If vinegar investigations are not possible, use the observation alternative: the adult runs the demo while the learner records, or the learner completes Investigation B from the sealed-bag image activity (Lesson 05) plus the Lesson 04 sort table, with the same report format.
