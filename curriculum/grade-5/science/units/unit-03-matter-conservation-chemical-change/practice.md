# Practice — Unit 03: Matter Conservation and Chemical-Change Evidence

Work with the adult where an investigation is involved. All tables in this file are labeled practice data unless a task tells you to measure yourself. Solutions are in `answer-key.md` (teacher file — do not read it before finishing).

## Set P1 — Phase-change conservation (Lesson 01)

All rows are practice data.

| Trial | Before | After | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1-1 | 90 g ice | 90 g water | Sealed bag |
| P1-2 | 130 g ice | 130 g water | Sealed bag |
| P1-3 | 100 g ice | 94 g water | Open plate, spill |

**P1-1.** Draw a bar graph for trials P1-1 and P1-2 (two bars each: before, after). What pattern do you see?
**P1-2.** State the conservation claim for trial P1-2 in one sentence with both numbers.
**P1-3.** Trial P1-3 lost 6 g. Does it break the claim? Explain, using the word "system."
**P1-4.** A learner freezes liquid water (150 g) overnight. Predict the ice weight and the melted weight. Explain why both predictions are the same number.
**P1-5 (error analysis).** "Ice weighs more than water because ice is solid and hard." Use any row above to show why hardness does not decide the weight.

## Set P2 — Dissolving conservation (Lesson 02)

**P2-1.** Water 140 g + sugar 25 g = ? Predict the mixture weight. What would a reading of 165 g prove?
**P2-2.** Water 200 g + salt 40 g = ? If the mixture weighs 240 g, is the salt gone? Explain with the numbers.
**P2-3.** A cup of sugar water (180 g) is left open. A week later it weighs 150 g and sugar grains are visible. Did the sugar vanish and come back? Explain what left and what stayed, using Lesson 02's evaporation reasoning.
**P2-4.** Two cups: cup A has 100 g water + 10 g sugar (110 g total); cup B has 100 g water + 30 g sugar (130 g total). Which cup has more dissolved sugar, and how do the weights show it?
**P2-5.** A learner says dissolving and melting are the same change because "both make solids disappear." Give one way they are alike (weight conserved, form changes) and one way they differ (melting changes solid to liquid by heating/cooling; dissolving spreads one substance through another).

## Set P3 — New-substance evidence (Lessons 03–04)

Decide "new substance" or "no new substance" and name the evidence for each.

**P3-1.** Baking soda + lemon juice: fizzing gas, leftover liquid changed. Verdict?
**P3-2.** Sand + oil: sand sinks, oil floats, both recoverable unchanged. Verdict?
**P3-3.** Two clear liquids mix, turn pink, and filtering does not restore the clear liquids. Verdict?
**P3-4.** Powder + water: no fizz, no new solid, no temperature change; evaporating the water returns the powder. Verdict?
**P3-5.** Milk + vinegar: white curds form; the milk cannot be recovered. Verdict?
**P3-6 (error analysis).** "Every mixture that looks different from its parts is a new substance." Give the counterexample from Lesson 04 and explain why "looks different" is not enough.

## Set P4 — Sealed vs. open reasoning (Lesson 05)

All rows are practice data.

| Run | Before | After | System |
|---|---|---|---|
| P4-1 | 250 g (sealed bag) | 250 g | Nothing left |
| P4-2 | 210 g (open cup) | 198 g | Gas escaped |
| P4-3 | 175 g (open cup, milk + vinegar) | 175 g | Nothing left (no gas formed) |

**P4-1.** How many grams of gas escaped in run P4-2? Show the subtraction.
**P4-2.** Why did run P4-3 stay at 175 g even though it was open? (Hint: did any gas form?)
**P4-3.** A learner says run P4-2 disproves conservation. Use run P4-1 to answer her in two sentences.
**P4-4.** Predict: the same reaction as P4-2 runs in a sealed bag starting at 210 g. What is the after weight? Explain.
**P4-5.** A fizzy drink loses 5 g when opened (gas escapes). The sealed bottle weighed 520 g before opening. How much did the drink + bottle weigh right after opening, and where did the 5 g go?

## Set P5 — Mixed review (all lessons)

**P5-1.** Sort these into "weight conserved" or "new substance?" — note that the two questions are different: (a) ice melting, (b) baking soda + vinegar, (c) sugar dissolving, (d) sand + water. Answer both questions for each change.
**P5-2.** A sealed bag holds 120 g of ice. After melting it holds 120 g of water. After the water evaporates over days (bag opened by the adult), the bag holds 0 g of water. Did conservation fail at the last step? Explain with the system rule.
**P5-3.** Design a fair test: does warm vinegar fizz faster than cold vinegar with baking soda — and does the sealed weight stay the same in both? Write the question, what you keep the same, what you change, and what you will weigh.
**P5-4.** Explain the whole unit in four sentences: one for melting, one for dissolving, one for new substances, one for the open cup.
**P5-5.** Which single investigation from this unit would you show a friend to prove that "gone from sight" does not mean "gone from existence"? Name it and give its two numbers.
