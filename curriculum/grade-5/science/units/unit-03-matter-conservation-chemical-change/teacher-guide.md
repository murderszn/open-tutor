# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Matter Conservation and Chemical-Change Evidence

**For the adult. Do not hand this file to the learner before assessments — it discusses answers and reteach moves. Student questions live in the lessons, `practice.md`, and `assessments/`; solutions live in `answer-key.md`.**

## Unit at a glance

Weeks 11–14, 16 sessions of ~40 minutes. The unit teaches two performance expectations and one honest complication: 5-PS1-2 (weight conserved through heating, cooling, and mixing), 5-PS1-4 (mixing sometimes makes new substances), and the sealed/open distinction that keeps the first claim true when a gas escapes. The most common failure mode is a learner who memorizes "weight stays the same" and then treats the open-cup drop as a contradiction. Lesson 05 exists to prevent that; do not skip it.

## Materials checklist (per learner, adult-supervised)

Kitchen scale (grams, 1 g resolution), sealable plastic bags, clear cups, ice, water, sugar, salt, sand, baking soda, vinegar, milk, baking powder, cream of tartar, cornstarch (extension), coffee filter or cloth, spoon, towel, safety glasses or goggles, science notebook, bar-graph template. Vinegar is adult-poured throughout. Fizzing mixtures are never sealed in rigid containers and never heated.

## Misconceptions and responses

| Misconception (from the OKScience grade-5 bundle list and unit experience) | Where it appears | Response |
|---|---|---|
| "When matter dissolves or evaporates, it ceases to exist." | Lessons 02, 05 | Weigh it: 180 g + 20 g = 200 g. Then name what left the system (evaporated water) vs. what stayed (sugar). |
| "Gas makes things lighter." | Lessons 03, 05 | The sealed bag stays at 250 g while puffed with gas. Gas has weight when it stays in the system; an open cup drops only because gas left. |
| "A chemical reaction always happens when two substances are combined." | Lesson 04 | Sand and water: the control case. Mixing alone proves nothing; evidence (gas, new solid, lasting change, failed recovery) decides. |
| "Physical changes are irreversible." | Lesson 01 | Melted ice refreezes; dissolved salt returns by evaporation. Reversibility is not the test — evidence of a new substance is. |
| "Bubbles always mean a new substance." | Lesson 03 | Shaken soapy water: bubbles are trapped air, not a produced gas. Ask: does gas keep forming from the mixture, and can the parts be recovered unchanged? |
| "Hard things weigh more." | Lesson 01 | Ice (hard) and water (liquid) weigh the same 120 g. Hardness is a property, not a weight. |
| "The scale is wrong when the numbers disagree with my eyes." | Lessons 01–02 | Trust the measurement, then explain the disagreement with the particle model (particles too small to see) or the system rule (matter left). |

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

- **Lesson 01:** the sealed bag is the whole lesson. If the first run uses an open plate and spills, keep the result — it becomes the unfair-comparison example for Lesson 05 instead of a failed lesson.
- **Lesson 02:** do not let "dissolved = gone" survive the lesson. The exit check (25 g + 175 g = 200 g) is the minimum evidence; a learner who cannot answer it needs the bar-drawing alternative before Lesson 03.
- **Lesson 03:** run the soapy-water contrast the same day if possible. Without it, "bubbles = new substance" hardens into a rule and Lesson 04's sand-and-water control loses its force.
- **Lesson 04:** the recovery test is the unit's most transferable idea. Name it explicitly ("can we get the parts back unchanged?") and reuse it in the project and the assessment.
- **Lesson 05:** the image activity works from the text alternative alone — never require the image. If the bag strains during the sealed demo, stop, open it away from faces (adult only), and use the image + the practice-data table instead.

## Reteach triggers and follow-ups

- **Quiz 9–10:** proceed to the unit assessment.
- **Quiz 7–8:** revisit the missed lesson's guided practice (same numbers, new order) and re-check with the exit check before the assessment.
- **Quiz 6 or fewer:** reteach by topic: missed Q1–Q3 → Lessons 01–02 weighing routine with fresh numbers; missed Q4–Q6/Q9 → Lesson 04 sort table with new examples (lemon juice + baking soda, oil + water); missed Q7–Q8/Q10 → Lesson 05 sealed/open pair with the image activity. Re-quiz with new numbers before the assessment.
- **Assessment 10 or fewer (of 16):** reteach the weakest part (Part 1 → Lessons 01–02; Part 2 → Lessons 03–04; Part 3 → Lesson 05; Part 4 → project report structure) and re-assess with new numbers and a new writing prompt.
- **Project 4–6:** redo one investigation with the adult, focusing on the before weight and the system rule; the report may be dictated.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** picture-word evidence cards (gas cloud, solid lump, thermometer, color drop); bar-drawing instead of written addition; dictated reports; the text alternative for the Lesson 05 image.
- **Extension:** the baking-powder pair tests (Lesson 04), the warm-vs.-cold vinegar fair test (practice P5-3), the evaporation follow-up (Lesson 02 extension), and the open-cup milk-and-vinegar prediction (Lesson 05 extension).
- **Observation alternatives:** adult demo with learner recording; the sealed-bag image activity; ACS Lesson 3.2 video (adult-previewed) for the baking-powder extension. All teach the same evidence; none requires tasting or unsupervised pouring.

## Safety recap

Adult pours vinegar; eye protection for every fizzing mixture; no tasting during investigations; flexible bags only for sealed fizzing (small amounts, adult stops if the bag strains); no heating of fizzing mixtures; wash hands after handling. If any material is unavailable, use the observation alternative — do not substitute an unknown household chemical.
