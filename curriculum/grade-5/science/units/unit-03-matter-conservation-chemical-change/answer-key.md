# Answer Key — Unit 03: Matter Conservation and Chemical-Change Evidence

**Teacher file. Every item in the lessons, `practice.md`, `assessments/`, and `project.md` is solved here independently of the teaching text; where the teaching text and this key disagree, re-solve before teaching. Accept equivalent wording that cites the same numbers and evidence.**

## Lesson 01 — Freeze and melt

- **L1-G1.** Trial A: "The weight stayed the same when the ice melted: 85 g before, 85 g after." Trial B: same claim with 140 g / 140 g.
- **L1-G2.** Trial C does not prove melting destroys matter: 8 g of water spilled off the plate, so matter left the system before the after weighing. The comparison is unfair; a sealed bag would keep all the water in the system.
- **L1-G3.** Two bars of equal height (85 g). Equal height shows the weight did not change when the form changed.
- **L1-I1.** Claim: the weight stayed the same when the ice melted. Evidence: 200 g before, 200 g after.
- **L1-I2.** Hardness does not decide weight: the 120 g of hard ice and the 120 g of liquid water weigh the same. The property changed (solid to liquid); the amount of matter did not.
- **L1-I3.** She is not right. The 6 g is spilled water that left the system, not destroyed matter. Redo fairly: melt the ice in a sealed bag or deep bowl so all the water stays in the weighing.
- **Exit check.** The bag should weigh 110 g, because melting changes the form but not the amount of matter, and nothing left the sealed bag.

## Lesson 02 — Dissolving

- **L2-G1.** Trial A: 150 g + 10 g = 160 g; claim: the weight stayed the same (the mixture weighs what water + sugar weighed). Trial B: 150 g + 30 g = 180 g; same claim.
- **L2-G2.** Trial C: the cup spilled during stirring, so sugar water left the system before weighing. The 150 g result measures what stayed in the cup, not all the matter.
- **L2-G3 (sample).** "The sugar is not gone. The water weighed 180 g and the sugar weighed 20 g, and the mixture weighs 200 g — the sugar's weight is still there, even though its particles are too small to see now that they are spread through the water."
- **L2-I1.** Predicted mixture weight: 120 g + 15 g = 135 g. A reading of 135 g proves the salt is still there (its weight is in the total) even though it cannot be seen.
- **L2-I2 (sample).** Stirring spreads the sugar's tiny particles among the water's particles, so no grains are visible; but the particles are still in the cup, so the scale still weighs them.
- **L2-I3.** Her claim is wrong: pouring half the mixture into another cup moved half the matter to the second cup; it did not vanish. The two cups together still hold 200 g (100 g + 100 g).
- **Exit check.** No, the sugar is not gone: 175 g + 25 g = 200 g, and the mixture weighs 200 g, so the sugar's weight is still present.

## Lesson 03 — Baking soda and vinegar

- **L3-G1.** New substance. Evidence: gas formed that was not there before, and the leftover liquid is changed and cannot be filtered back into vinegar and baking soda.
- **L3-G2.** No new substance. Evidence: bubbles stop when shaking stops; the water is still soapy water — the bubbles are trapped air, not a gas produced by mixing.
- **L3-G3.** The error is treating the gas as added from outside. The gas was produced from the substances already in the cup; nothing was added. Its weight was already counted in the before weight.
- **L3-I1.** No new substance. Evidence: no fizz, no color change, and the sand can be rinsed and recovered unchanged.
- **L3-I2.** The comparison is a fair test (only vinegar vs. water changed). It proves the vinegar + baking soda pair makes a new substance while the water + baking soda pair does not — the vinegar is the difference that matters.
- **L3-I3 (sample).** (a) The gas keeps forming after mixing starts, long after any trapped air would have escaped. (b) After the fizz stops, the starting substances are changed — the leftover liquid is not plain vinegar — so something new was made.
- **Exit check.** Yes: mixing made a new substance, evidenced by the gas that formed and was not present before mixing.

## Lesson 04 — More evidence and the non-example

- **L4-G1.** New substance. Evidence: gas formed during mixing, and filtering does not recover the starting powder.
- **L4-G2.** No new substance (a mixture). Evidence: the parts settle into layers and can be poured off unchanged.
- **L4-G3.** Sand and water is the control case: it proves that mixing alone does not make a new substance. Without it, a learner could claim every mixture is a new substance, and the evidence rules would have nothing to test against.
- **L4-I1.** No new substance. Evidence: the salt dissolves but returns unchanged when the water evaporates — dissolving is a mixture change, not a new-substance change.
- **L4-I2.** New substance. Evidence: a lasting color change that filtering cannot undo — the color clue, with the recovery test confirming it.
- **L4-I3.** "Looks different" is not enough: sand stirred into water looks different from dry sand and plain water (cloudy mixture), yet filtering recovers both unchanged — so it is a mixture, not a new substance.
- **Exit check.** Milk + vinegar made a new substance (curds formed); sand + water did not (both parts recoverable unchanged).

## Lesson 05 — Sealed bag and open cup

- **L5-Q1.** Before mixing: 250 g. After mixing: 250 g.
- **L5-Q2.** What changed: the bag puffed up with gas (the mixture fizzed). What stayed the same: the scale reading, 250 g — and all the matter, still inside the sealed bag.
- **L5-Q3.** The bag puffed up because mixing produced a gas, which takes up space inside the sealed bag.
- **L5-G1.** Row A: supports conservation — nothing left the system and the weight stayed at 250 g. Row B: supports conservation once the system is understood — the weight dropped because gas left the open system, not because matter was destroyed. Row C: supports conservation — no gas formed, nothing left, weight stayed at 180 g.
- **L5-G2.** 210 g − 198 g = 12 g of gas escaped.
- **L5-G3.** A rigid sealed jar is unsafe because the produced gas builds pressure with nowhere to go; it could burst. Use a flexible bag with a small amount only (adult supervises, stops if the bag strains), or run the reaction open.
- **L5-I1.** She is wrong on both counts: the puffed-up bag shows something did change (gas formed), while the unchanged 300 g shows the total weight stayed the same. Change and conservation happened together.
- **L5-I2.** Escaped gas: 160 g − 149 g = 11 g. Sealed, the after weight would be 160 g (the gas would stay in the system and on the scale).
- **L5-I3.** Yes, the drink lost matter when opened: the escaping gas (the hiss) left the drink system, so the opened drink weighs less than the sealed one. The gas still exists — it joined the air.
- **Exit check.** No, conservation is not broken: the sealed bag kept all its matter (250 g), while the open cup lost 12 g of gas to the air (210 g − 198 g). The difference is what left the system, not what was destroyed.

## Practice sets

### P1 — Phase-change conservation

- **P1-1.** Each pair of bars is equal height (90 g / 90 g; 130 g / 130 g). Pattern: melting does not change the weight.
- **P1-2.** "The weight stayed the same when the ice melted: 130 g before, 130 g after."
- **P1-3.** No. The 6 g is water that spilled off the plate — matter that left the system before the after weighing.
- **P1-4.** Ice: 150 g. Melted: 150 g. Both predictions are 150 g because freezing and melting change form, not the amount of matter, and the sealed system loses nothing.
- **P1-5.** Any row disproves it: e.g., 90 g of hard ice and 90 g of liquid water weigh the same, so hardness does not set the weight.

### P2 — Dissolving conservation

- **P2-1.** 140 g + 25 g = 165 g. A reading of 165 g proves the sugar's weight is still in the cup.
- **P2-2.** 200 g + 40 g = 240 g. No, the salt is not gone: 240 g includes the salt's 40 g.
- **P2-3.** No. The water evaporated (left the cup as vapor and joined the air); the sugar stayed behind and reappeared as grains. Nothing vanished and came back — the water left and the sugar remained.
- **P2-4.** Cup B has more dissolved sugar: 130 g total vs. 110 g total, with the same 100 g of water, so cup B holds 30 g of sugar vs. cup A's 10 g.
- **P2-5 (sample).** Alike: both conserve weight while the form changes. Different: melting changes one substance's form by heating/cooling; dissolving spreads one substance's particles through another substance.

### P3 — New-substance evidence

- **P3-1.** New substance — gas evidence (fizzing), same acid reaction as baking soda + vinegar.
- **P3-2.** No new substance — both parts recoverable unchanged; layering/separation evidence.
- **P3-3.** New substance — lasting color change that filtering cannot undo.
- **P3-4.** No new substance — no evidence clue, and the powder returns by evaporation (a mixture).
- **P3-5.** New substance — new solid (curds) evidence.
- **P3-6.** Counterexample: sand + water looks different (cloudy) when mixed, but filtering recovers sand and water unchanged — so "looks different" cannot be the test; evidence of a new substance is.

### P4 — Sealed vs. open

- **P4-1.** 210 g − 198 g = 12 g of gas escaped.
- **P4-2.** Milk + vinegar makes curds, not gas, so nothing left the open cup; the measured weight had no reason to drop.
- **P4-3 (sample).** "The sealed bag stayed at 250 g, so the same kind of reaction conserved its weight. The open cup dropped only because its gas escaped into the air."
- **P4-4.** After weight: 210 g. Sealed, the gas stays in the system, so the scale still weighs it.
- **P4-5.** Right after opening: 520 g − 5 g = 515 g. The 5 g of gas joined the air outside the bottle.

### P5 — Mixed review

- **P5-1.** (a) Ice melting: weight conserved; not a new substance. (b) Baking soda + vinegar: weight conserved in a sealed system; yes, a new substance (gas). (c) Sugar dissolving: weight conserved; not a new substance (a mixture). (d) Sand + water: weight conserved; not a new substance (a mixture).
- **P5-2.** No. The adult opened the bag, so the water vapor left the system by evaporation. Conservation covers all the matter; once matter leaves the system, the measured weight drops for that reason, not because matter was destroyed.
- **P5-3 (sample).** Question: does warm vinegar fizz faster than cold vinegar, and is the sealed weight the same in both? Keep the same: amounts of baking soda and vinegar, bag, scale. Change: vinegar temperature only. Weigh: before and after for each run; expect equal before/after within each run even if the fizz speed differs.
- **P5-4 (sample).** "Melting changes ice to water without changing its weight. Dissolving spreads sugar through water without changing the total weight. Mixing can make a new substance, shown by gas, a new solid, or a lasting change. If gas escapes an open cup, the measured weight drops — because matter left, not because matter vanished."
- **P5-5 (sample).** The dissolving investigation: 180 g of water + 20 g of sugar = 200 g of mixture — the sugar cannot be seen, but its 20 g is still on the scale.
