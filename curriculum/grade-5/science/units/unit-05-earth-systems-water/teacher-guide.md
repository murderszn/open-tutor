# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Earth Systems, Spheres, and Water Distribution

**For the adult. Do not hand this file to the learner before assessments — it discusses answers and reteach moves. Student questions live in the lessons, `practice.md`, and `assessments/`; solutions live in `answer-key.md`.**

## Unit at a glance

Weeks 19–22, 16 sessions of ~40 minutes. The unit teaches two performance expectations and one rounding discipline: 5-ESS2-1 (two-sphere interaction models), 5-ESS2-2 (water amounts and percentages in reservoirs), and the habit of stating rounded numbers with their source. The most common failure mode is a learner who memorizes "69 frozen" and then applies it to *all* Earth's water instead of to the *fresh* water. Lessons 04 and 05 exist to prevent that; do not let the learner quote a water number without saying "of all water" or "of the fresh water." The second most common failure is modeling three or four spheres at once; the two-at-a-time rule is enforced in every lesson, the project, and the assessment.

## Materials checklist (per learner, adult-supervised)

Science notebook, pencils and crayons, four sphere word cards, 100 small counters (buttons, beans, or paper squares), two or three labeled jars or boxes, 1-liter measuring jug, teaspoon, clear jar with lid, small pebbles or gravel, potting soil, moss or small seedlings the adult has identified as suitable, spray bottle or spoon, shallow tray or baking pan, sand or soil, cup of water, book (for the mountain model), towel, printed water-data tables, bar-graph templates, printed map or globe. Blue food coloring is optional and adult-handled.

## Misconceptions and responses

| Misconception | Where it appears | Response |
|---|---|---|
| "A glacier is rock because it is solid / a snowball is geosphere because it is hard." | Lesson 01 | Solid does not mean rock. A glacier is frozen water — hydrosphere. Sort by what it is made of, not how hard it feels. |
| "A cloud is water, so it is hydrosphere." | Lesson 01, Lesson 03 | The water inside a cloud is hydrosphere, but the cloud as moving air and weather is atmosphere in this unit. When a part could fit two spheres, say so and pick the one that matters for the question. |
| "The ocean controls the weather everywhere." | Lesson 02 | The ocean steadies *coastal* climate. Inland weather has other drivers. "Everywhere" is the error — ask what shapes weather far from any ocean. |
| "Water destroys rock." | Lessons 02–03 | Water moves rock (waves, wind-blown sand) or dissolves it (limestone caves). The material goes somewhere — follow it. Nothing is destroyed. |
| "Mountains make rain out of nothing / the far side gets no water at all." | Lesson 03 | Mountains squeeze water out of air that is already wet; they do not create water. The far side gets *less* water, not none. |
| "The blue planet means mostly fresh water." | Lesson 04 | Nearly all of Earth's water is salty ocean (97 of 100 parts). Blue means water, not fresh water. |
| "71 percent water-covered means 71 percent fresh." | Lesson 04 | Coverage and fresh share are two different questions. Coverage counts all water, including the salty ocean. |
| "69 parts of all Earth's water are frozen." | Lesson 05 | 69 is parts **of the fresh water**. Of all water, frozen fresh water is only about 2 parts in 100. Make the learner say "of the fresh water" every time. |
| "Lakes and rivers hold most of the fresh water." | Lesson 05 | They hold about 1 part in 100 of it. The mistake is easy because drinking water comes from lakes and rivers — the water we use most is the water there is least of. |
| "The atmosphere holds most fresh water, so it should be on the graph." | Lessons 04–05, assessment C4 | The atmosphere's share is tiny (0.04% of fresh water in the USGS table) and the framework's boundary excludes it from the reservoirs graphed. Say the boundary out loud. |

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

- **Lesson 01:** the two-at-a-time rule is the unit's load-bearing idea. Enforce it from this lesson onward. The coral-reef note (living coral = biosphere, reef rock = geosphere) is the first "one thing, two spheres" case — accept either answer with the note.
- **Lesson 02:** run the beach tray model the same day if possible. Without it, "moving water moves land" stays abstract. The limestone-cave example is slow-change evidence — connect it to the Unit 03 idea that some evidence takes a very long time to form.
- **Lesson 03:** the book-and-spray model is a model, not the real thing. Say out loud one way it differs from real mountains (real air climbs over the top; the spray mostly stops at the book). Do not let "the far side gets no water" survive the lesson.
- **Lesson 04:** the 97 / 3 rounding must be stated every time the numbers appear — the exact USGS figure is 96.54 percent oceans. Watch for the fresh-water bar drawn at 30 by accident (that is Lesson 05's ground-water number, not this lesson's).
- **Lesson 05:** the image has no sun drawn — do not let the learner claim the sun is in the picture; the sun's role as the energy behind weather belongs to Unit 06. If the image is unavailable, the text-only alternative carries the whole activity — never require the image.

## Reteach triggers and follow-ups

- **Quiz 9–10:** proceed to the unit assessment.
- **Quiz 7–8:** revisit the missed lesson's guided practice (same numbers, new order) and re-check with the exit check before the assessment.
- **Quiz 6 or fewer:** reteach by topic: missed Q1–Q2 → Lesson 01 sphere sort with new features; missed Q3–Q4 → Lessons 02–03 two-sphere pairs with new examples; missed Q5–Q6 → Lesson 04 100-cups counting; missed Q7–Q9 → Lesson 05 fresh-water split with counters; missed Q10 → Lesson 05 exit check rewritten as a paragraph. Re-quiz with new numbers before the assessment.
- **Assessment 21–26 (of 30):** review the missed part with the adult (Part A → Lesson 01; Part B → Lessons 02–03; Part C → Lessons 04–05; Part D → Lesson 05; Part E → Lesson 05 image activity), then re-check with that lesson's exit check.
- **Assessment 20 or fewer:** reteach the weakest part and re-assess with new numbers and a new scene before Unit 06.
- **Project 4–6:** redo one part with the adult, focusing on the two-spheres-at-a-time rule and the bar labels; the report may be dictated.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** sphere word cards with a picture on each; bar-drawing with stacked counters instead of drawn bars; dictated reports and sentences; the text-only alternative for the Lesson 05 image; the 1,000 mL demonstration poured by the adult while the learner counts.
- **Extension:** the ocean-current and mangrove research tasks (Lesson 02), the real rain-shadow place task (Lesson 03), the town-water-source task (Lesson 04), and the glacier-water / well-water research task (Lesson 05).
- **Observation alternatives:** adult demo with learner recording; adult-supplied neighborhood photos or the generated river-valley image for the safari; the jar photo-log alternative; the USGS pages as read-alouds. All teach the same evidence; none requires unsupervised outdoor work.

## Safety recap

Adult supervises every outdoor observation and every jar step; prepares a clean unchipped container and fresh potting mix; washes hands with the learner after soil and outdoor work. Use only plants the adult has identified as suitable; no tasting of plants or soil. Keep the jar in bright indirect light, never hot direct sun. If mold appears, the adult removes the affected material; discard the setup if mold persists. If any material is unavailable, use the observation alternative — do not substitute an unknown household material.
