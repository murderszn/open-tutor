# Project — My Neighborhood Earth System

**Unit 05 · Sessions 11–12 (Week 21) · ~40 minutes each · Investigation + report**

## The challenge

You are a field scientist for your own neighborhood. Your job has three parts: (1) go outside and find **four real two-sphere interactions** happening near your home; (2) build a small **Earth in a Jar** model and watch two spheres interact inside it for three days; (3) draw the **water-distribution poster** — the two bar graphs from Lessons 04–05 — and explain in one paragraph why most fresh water is hard for people to use.

The field safari and the jar build are adapted from the legacy assignment `curriculum/grade-5/stem/assignments/earth-spheres-investigation.md` (audit: "Review for U05"); its Scratch portions are optional enrichment and are not part of this project. The observation-vs.-inference habit comes from Lesson 01.

## Part A — Sphere safari (outdoors, adult-supervised)

Take your field notebook outside for 20 minutes. Find and describe **four real two-sphere interactions** — each one exactly two spheres. Write one observation sentence (what you saw) and one inference sentence (what you think it means) for each, and label which is which.

Starter examples (find your own — do not copy these unless you actually see them):

- **Interaction 1:** Rain washing mud into a storm drain — **hydrosphere → geosphere**.
- **Interaction 2:** A robin pulling an earthworm from garden soil — **biosphere → geosphere**.
- **Interaction 3:** Wind shaking leaves off a tree — **atmosphere → biosphere**.
- **Interaction 4:** Puddle water shrinking into warm afternoon air — **hydrosphere → atmosphere**.

## Part B — Earth in a Jar (three-day model)

Build a small planted jar to watch two spheres interact. The adult prepares a clean, unchipped jar and fresh potting mix, and supervises every step.

**Materials:** a clean clear jar or clear bottle with a lid, small pebbles or gravel, potting soil, moss or small seedlings the adult has identified as suitable, water, a spray bottle or spoon, science notebook.

**Steps:**

1. **Layer 1 (drainage):** put 1 inch of small pebbles at the bottom (geosphere).
2. **Layer 2 (soil):** add 2–3 inches of potting soil (geosphere).
3. **Layer 3 (life):** plant 2–3 small seedlings or moss gently into the soil (biosphere).
4. **Layer 4 (water and air):** mist lightly with 2–3 tablespoons of water (hydrosphere), leaving air space at the top (atmosphere).
5. **Close it:** fit the lid on, then place the jar in bright indirect light. Check that it does not become hot.
6. **Observe for three days:** each day, write down what you see — water drops on the glass, soil dampness, how the plants look — and name the two spheres in each change you notice. (The full water-cycle explanation of what you see is Unit 06; here you name the two spheres and describe the change.)

**Three-day log:**

| Day | What I observed (observation) | Two spheres in this change | What I think it means (inference) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (2 hours in the light) | | | |
| 2 | | | |
| 3 | | | |

**Safety:** use only plants the adult has identified as suitable; **do not taste plants or soil**; wash hands after soil work; keep the jar in bright indirect light, never hot direct sun. If mold appears, the adult removes the affected material; discard the setup if mold persists. If a jar cannot be built, use the observation alternative: the adult supplies dated photos of a jar over three days, and the learner completes the same log from the photos.

## Part C — Water-distribution poster

Draw both bar graphs from Lessons 04–05 on one poster:

- **Graph 1 — All Earth's water (parts out of 100):** Ocean (salt water) 97, Fresh water 3.
- **Graph 2 — Fresh water only (parts out of 100):** Frozen in glaciers and ice caps 69, Underground (ground water) 30, Lakes, rivers, and other surface water 1.
- Label every bar with its number and write the source under each graph: "USGS Water Science School, rounded to whole parts."
- Add a title and one paragraph (3–4 sentences) explaining why most fresh water is hard for people to use, using the 69 / 30 / 1 numbers.

## Deliverables

- A field log with 4 two-sphere interactions, each with an observation and an inference, labeled.
- A jar model with labeled layers and a completed 3-day observation log.
- A water-distribution poster with both labeled bar graphs and the explanation paragraph.
- A one-sentence unit summary: what the three parts together prove about Earth's systems and water.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (3) | Good (2) | Needs work (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sphere safari observations** | 4 real interactions, each exactly two spheres, with observation and inference labeled and correct | 4 interactions, but one pair wrong or one observation/inference label swapped | Fewer than 4 interactions, or pairs of three or four spheres, or observations and inferences mixed up |
| **Earth-in-a-Jar model and log** | Jar built with labeled layers; 3-day log complete; every change named with its two spheres | Jar built; log complete but one day missing a sphere pair or a change undescribed | Jar missing, or log incomplete, or changes described without sphere pairs |
| **Water-distribution poster** | Both graphs drawn with correct bars (97 / 3 and 69 / 30 / 1), all bars labeled, source written | Both graphs drawn but one bar wrong or unlabeled, or source missing | One graph missing, or bars do not match the numbers |
| **Explanation and honesty** | Paragraph explains why most fresh water is hard to use with the 69 / 30 / 1 numbers; surprises and failed guesses reported honestly | Paragraph correct but missing one number, or one surprise unreported | Paragraph missing, or claims fresh water is easy to get, or numbers invented |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = ready for the Week 22 review; 7–9 = revise one part with the adult (usually the paragraph or one graph); 4–6 = redo one part with the adult, focusing on the two-spheres-at-a-time rule and the bar labels.

## Safety and alternatives

The adult supervises the outdoor safari and every jar step, prepares the container and potting mix, and enforces hand-washing and no tasting. If the learner cannot go outside, use the observation alternative: the adult supplies photos of the neighborhood, or the learner completes the safari from the generated river-valley image (`assets/river-valley-spheres.png`) and its text-only alternative, with the same log format. If the jar cannot be built, use the photo-log alternative above. All alternatives teach the same evidence.
