# Practice Sets — Unit 02

Five practice sets matched to the session plan. Each set mixes retrieval from
Unit 01 with the new particle, property, and mixture ideas. The adult checks
work against `answer-key.md` (§AK-P).

## Set A — Evidence for tiny particles (after Lesson 01; Session 2)

1. Sort these into **observation** or **inference**: (a) "The balloon felt
   firm." (b) "Air is made of tiny particles." (c) "The plunger pushed back
   out." (d) "There is empty space between air's particles."
2. Explain in two sentences why "I can't see the air" is **not** evidence by
   itself, but **is** part of the evidence when combined with the syringe
   push-back.
3. Retrieval (Unit 01): list the four steps of a fair test, in order.
4. A friend says, "The air escaped, so it was never real." Write the
   two-sentence reply you would give, using the word *push*.
5. Draw and label: what you think the air's particles look like inside the
   squeezed syringe vs. the released syringe. (Two small sketches; effort and
   spacing differences count, not art.)

## Set B — Particle models (after Lesson 02; Sessions 3–4)

1. Match each model description to solid, liquid, or gas: (a) "particles
   spread far apart, filling the container"; (b) "particles packed tightly in
   neat rows"; (c) "particles close together but jumbled and sliding."
2. A drawing shows liquid particles in perfect neat rows like a solid. Name
   **two observations** that drawing would wrongly predict.
3. **Error analysis:** "Mara drew gas particles clustered at the bottom of the
   jar. She said heavy things sink." Explain Mara's error using the balloon
   evidence, then describe the corrected drawing.
4. Predict with a model: you pour 200 mL of juice from a tall glass into a
   wide bowl. What does the particle model say about the juice's volume? What
   about its shape? Then check with real water and a measuring cup.
5. Retrieval (Unit 01): you measure the classroom table as 120 cm. How many
   meters is that? Show the conversion.
6. Draw the mystery-state bag from Lesson 02's application from memory, then
   compare with your lesson drawing and note one improvement.

## Set C — Properties (after Lesson 03; Sessions 5–6)

1. Complete the property table for two new samples the adult provides (keep
   the two-property rule — no naming from one test).
2. **Explain your reasoning:** "Powder X is white, dissolves completely, and
   looks glassy under the hand lens. Powder Y is white, clouds the water, and
   looks dull." Identify each as far as you honestly can, and name the two
   decisive properties for each call.
3. A classmate identifies a powder as baking soda because "it's white." Write
   the correction you would give, using the two-property rule.
4. Design a property test (no tasting, adult supervises) that could tell
   **sand** from **sugar** without using water. Explain which property it uses.
5. Retrieval (Unit 01): your bar graph shows plant heights of 5, 8, and 6 cm.
   Which plant grew tallest, and by how many centimeters more than the
   shortest?

## Set D — Mixtures (after Lesson 04; Sessions 7–8)

1. Draw particle models for: (a) a mixture of marbles and sand in a jar;
   (b) the same jar after you pour it through a sieve. Label what is caught
   and what passes.
2. **Predict, then test:** the adult mixes dry rice and dry flour. Draw the
   model, predict whether a kitchen sieve separates them, then test and write
   whether the prediction was supported.
3. For each pair, say *mixture* or *not a mixture* and give the property
   evidence: (a) trail mix; (b) a rusted nail; (c) salt stirred into soup;
   (d) a glass of milk.
4. **Separation plan:** your little brother mixes your bead collection (big
   wooden beads + tiny seed beads) into one box. Write a separation plan
   using only household tools, with the property each step exploits.
5. Retrieval (Unit 01): write a testable question about mixtures that a fair
   test could answer. (Example frame: "Does ___ separate faster with ___?")

## Set E — Dissolving and revision (after Lesson 05; Sessions 9–10)

1. **Evidence chain, written:** prediction → test → observation → conclusion,
   for the sugar-water weighing test. All four links required.
2. Your friend says, "The sugar is gone — I can't see it." Give the two
   evidence-based replies from Lesson 05 (weighing, evaporation), each in one
   sentence.
3. **Revision sentence:** complete the frame for your own Week-7 drawing:
   "Before I drew ___. The evidence ___ showed ___. Now I draw ___."
4. Predict: the adult stirs a spoonful of salt into water. Will the mixture
   weigh more than the water alone, less, or the same? Explain with particles
   *before* weighing, then weigh and compare.
5. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "Evaporation proved the sugar was still there."
   Is this sentence fully accurate? Improve it: what did evaporation actually
   show, and what did it *not* show? (Hint: it showed the sugar's particles
   remained — it did not show *how* water's particles left.)
6. Retrieval (whole unit): without looking, list the five properties in the
   identification toolkit and one material each property helped identify.
