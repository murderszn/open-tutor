# Unit 02 — Particle Models, Properties, and Mixtures

**Grade:** 5 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#27](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/27)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 7–10 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 40 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

Everything in this unit answers one question: **what is stuff made of, and how can we tell one kind of stuff from another?** The learner first gathers evidence that matter is made of **particles too small to be seen** — air pumped into a ball makes it firmer, air squeezed in a syringe pushes back, sugar stirred into water seems to vanish but its particles are still there. From that evidence the learner builds **particle models**: drawings that explain how solids hold their shape, liquids pour and take the shape of their container, and gases spread out to fill whatever holds them.

The second half of the unit turns models into tools for **identifying materials by their observable properties** — color, hardness, reflectivity, response to a magnet, and solubility — and for explaining **mixtures**: why sand and water can be separated but keep behaving like sand and water, and why sugar and water make a mixture that no filter can undo. The unit ends by revising the learner's first particle drawings in light of dissolving evidence, rehearsing the Unit 01 model-revision habit on new ground.

Boundary (held throughout): explanations stay **observable and qualitative**. The unit does not teach an atomic-scale mechanism for evaporation or condensation, does not name atoms or molecules as the particles, and treats **density** as out of scope for material identification (per the 5-PS1 assessment boundaries).

## Prerequisites (re-taught here as needed)

- Unit 01 habits: ask a testable question, run a fair test (change one thing, repeat trials), record in labeled tables, and **revise a model** when new evidence arrives. Unit 02 opens with the fair-test routine and closes with model revision, so these are retrieved, not assumed.
- Measuring volume to the nearest 10 mL with a graduated cylinder (used in the dissolving demos).
- Science safety practices: the adult supervises every investigation and previews every mixture for eye/skin safety; nothing near the mouth; non-mercury tools only.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Build a particle model** (labeled drawing) of a solid, a liquid, and a gas that shows particles too small to be seen packed tightly and orderly (solid), close but jumbled (liquid), and spread far apart (gas) — and explain what each model predicts (5-PS1-1).
2. **Cite evidence** that matter is made of particles too small to be seen: balloon inflation, syringe air compression, and dissolving (5-PS1-1).
3. **Identify unknown materials** by planning and carrying out observations of **color, hardness, reflectivity, magnetic response, and solubility**, combining at least two properties before naming a material (5-PS1-3).
4. **Explain a mixture** with a particle model showing that the mixed materials keep their own properties, and predict which mixtures can be separated by a filter or by evaporation (5-PS1-1, 5-PS1-3).
5. **Revise a scientific model** after new evidence (the dissolving investigation), naming what changed in the drawing and why (science practice: developing and using models).

## Essential question

*How can we explain what we observe about materials — what they are and how they behave — with particles we cannot see?*

## Standards and source notes (verified 2026-10-18)

- **NGSS 5-PS1-1** — "Develop a model to describe that matter is made of particles too small to be seen." Official examples of supporting evidence include *adding air to expand a basketball, compressing air in a syringe, dissolving sugar in water, and evaporating salt water* — this unit's three anchor investigations. (Source: NGSS performance expectations, 5-PS1 "Matter and Its Interactions"; crosswalk carried forward from the track audit's verified NGSS check, scope-and-sequence §7.)
- **NGSS 5-PS1-3** — "Make observations and measurements to identify materials based on their properties." Official examples of identifiable materials include *baking soda and other powders, metals, minerals, and liquids*; properties include *color, hardness, reflectivity, response to magnetic forces, and solubility*; **density is not intended as an identifiable property**. Lessons 03–04 follow this list exactly. (Source: NGSS 5-PS1; verified in track audit.)
- **Assessment boundary (held):** the atomic-scale mechanism of evaporation/condensation is not taught, and unseen particles are not defined for learners. (NGSS 5-PS1 assessment boundary; scope-and-sequence §7.)
- **Practices:** developing and using models; planning and carrying out investigations; analyzing and interpreting data (from Unit 01, extended here).

## Safety and materials

All investigations use household materials (balloon, plastic syringe without needle, clear cups, water, sugar, salt, sand, baking soda, flour, bar magnet, hand lens). The **adult previews every mixture** for eye and skin safety, pours nothing near the face, and enforces **no tasting** during investigations. Syringe: oral/medicine syringe with no needle, used only to push air.
