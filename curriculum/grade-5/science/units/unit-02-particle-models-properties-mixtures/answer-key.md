# Answer Key — Unit 02

**For the adult only.** Every solution below was worked independently and
reconciled against the student wording on 2026-10-18. Accept any wording that
carries the same reasoning; the "acceptable variation" notes mark where more
than one answer is defensible.

## AK-1 · Lesson 01

**AK-1.1 — table + claim.** Acceptable table:

| What I did | What I observed |
|---|---|
| Blew air into the balloon | It got bigger, rounder, firmer; it pushed back when squeezed |
| Pushed the plunger with the tip covered | The trapped air took up less space; the plunger pushed back out |

Claim (one sentence): *Air is matter made of tiny particles too small to see —
it takes up space and pushes back, but no pieces are visible.* Acceptable
variation: any sentence linking *takes up space / pushes* with *too small to
see*.

## AK-2 · Lesson 02

**AK-2.2 — which model is it?**
- a. Juice kept the same amount → **liquid**: particles close and jumbled; they
  rearrange (new shape) but stay close (same volume).
- b. Smell reached every corner → **gas**: particles spread far apart, moving
  through the whole room.
- c. Block kept its shape → **solid**: particles packed tightly in order; they
  cannot slide past each other.

**AK-2.3 — mystery-state bag.** Criteria: water drawn in the lower half with
particles close/jumbled; air drawn in the upper half with particles spread far
apart; both regions labeled; one sentence explaining the boundary (e.g.,
"water's particles stay close so it sits at the bottom; air's particles spread
so they fill the top").

## AK-3 · Lesson 03

**AK-3.1 — mystery liquid.** Acceptable three-test plan: (1) pour test — oil
pours more slowly/thickly than water; (2) food-coloring drop — color mixes
through water but beads/sits in oil; (3) fingertip feel (adult supervises) —
oil feels slippery, water does not. Identification: the easy-pouring,
color-mixing liquid is water; cite two properties. Accept any two honest
properties.

**AK-3.2 — property riddles.**
- a. Gray + shiny + hard + magnetic → **iron/steel** (e.g., an iron nail). The
  magnetic response is decisive; aluminum looks similar but is not magnetic.
- b. White + dull + does not dissolve + clouds water → **flour**.
- c. Clear + pours easily + dissolves salt → **water**.

## AK-4 · Lesson 04

**AK-4.1 — rice + sand.** Model first: two particle sizes mixed. Prediction:
**yes, separable** — a kitchen sieve's holes are smaller than rice grains but
larger than sand grains, so rice is caught and sand passes. Test should confirm;
if the learner's sieve fails, the key point is the *reasoning from the model*,
not the kitchenware.

**AK-4.2 — paper clips + sand.** Step 1: hold the magnet over the spill — the
clips jump to it (**magnetic response**); lift them away. Step 2: pour the
remaining sand through a sieve if needed to check nothing else is mixed in
(**size**). Explanation must name the property each step exploits.

## AK-5 · Lesson 05

**AK-5.1 — salt water.** Prediction: the mixture will weigh **the same** as
water + salt separately. After weighing: prediction supported — the salt's
particles are still in the water, too small to see. (Small differences from
spills are recorded honestly, not hidden.)

## AK-P · Practice sets

**Set A.** 1: (a) observation (b) inference (c) observation (d) inference.
2: "I can't see it" alone proves nothing — many unreal things are unseen. With
the push-back, it becomes evidence: something *unseen* is *pushing*, so it must
be made of unseen pieces. 3: fair-test steps — testable question; change one
variable; keep the rest the same; repeat trials (≥3); record in a table.
4: "The air pushed the plunger back out — nothing can't push. It was real; it
just left the balloon." 5: squeezed sketch shows particles closer together;
released sketch shows them spread — spacing difference is what counts.

**Set B.** 1: (a) gas (b) solid (c) liquid. 2: the drawing wrongly predicts
that the liquid would (i) keep its shape when moved instead of pouring, and
(ii) that its particles cannot slide past each other. 3: Mara's error — the
balloon shows air fills the *whole* container, so gas particles must be spread
throughout, not piled; "heavy things sink" misapplies solid behavior to a gas.
Fix: redraw particles spread through the jar with wide gaps. 4: volume stays
200 mL (same particles, still close); shape becomes the bowl's; the check with
real water should confirm. 5: 1.2 m (120 ÷ 100). 6: criteria — both states
shown, water low and jumbled, air spread; one genuine improvement noted.

**Set C.** 1: criteria — table complete for both samples; no identification
from a single property. 2: X = **sugar** (dissolves completely + glassy under
the lens); Y = **flour** (clouds water + dull). Both calls cite two properties.
3: acceptable correction — "White is one property, and all four powders are
white. Run a second test (solubility, magnet, or hand lens) before naming it."
4: without water — hand-lens test: sugar forms glassy, sparkly cube-like
crystals; sand grains are irregular, tan, and dull. Property: reflectivity /
crystal shape (color also differs). 5: tallest 8 cm; shortest 5 cm; difference
3 cm.

**Set D.** 1: (a) big dots + tiny dots mixed, labeled; (b) sieve drawn as a row
of holes — marbles caught above, sand below. 2: prediction — yes, separable
(rice grains larger than sieve holes, flour passes); test and honest
supported/not-supported note. 3: (a) trail mix — mixture (nuts stay crunchy,
raisins stay chewy; can pick apart). (b) rusted nail — **not** a mixture (rust
is a new substance; the iron's properties are gone where rusted). (c) salt in
soup — mixture (salt's particles remain; evaporation would recover them).
(d) milk — mixture (water + fats/proteins; cream can be separated). 4: plan —
pour through a colander/large-hole sieve: big wooden beads are caught (size),
seed beads pass; property exploited is **size**. 5: acceptable if testable with
a fair test, e.g., "Does sand settle out of water faster than flour does?"
Must name what would be measured.

**Set E.** 1: full chain — prediction: if sugar's particles remain, the mixture
weighs water + sugar; test: weigh each, stir, reweigh; observation: weights
match; conclusion: sugar's particles are still there, too small to see.
2: "The cup weighed the same as the water and sugar separately, so the sugar
is still in there." / "After the water evaporated, sugar crystals were left
behind." 3: criteria — old drawing described, evidence named, new drawing
described. 4: prediction — same (water + salt); weighing confirms; salt's
particles remain. 5: improved sentence — "Evaporation left sugar crystals
behind, showing the sugar's particles were in the water all along; it does not
show *how* the water's particles left." 6: color, hardness, reflectivity,
magnetic response, solubility — each with one honest example from the unit.

## AK-Q · Week-10 quiz (20 points)

1. (3) Liquid model: particles close together but jumbled/sliding, labeled;
   1 point for arrangement, 1 for spacing, 1 for label.
2. (2) **b**.
3. (4) Two properties, e.g., solubility (salt dissolves; flour clouds/does not)
   and reflectivity under the hand lens (salt sparkles; flour is dull). 2
   points per property with correct expectations.
4. (3) a. True. b. False — a filter cannot separate sugar from water; sugar's
   particles are too small and spread out to be caught. c. False — dissolved
   sugar's particles are still there (weighing/evaporation evidence).
5. (3) The filter's holes are smaller than sand grains but bigger than water's
   particles, so sand is caught and water passes — the model predicts sorting
   by size.
6. (5) Best identification: **flour** (dull + does not dissolve; 3 points with
   two cited properties). One more test (2 points): any honest test — e.g.,
   compare side-by-side with a known flour sample (control), test hardness vs.
   the penny, or re-check crystal shape under the lens. Accept all defensible
   proposals.

## AK-UA · Unit assessment (50 points)

1. (9) Three drawings (2 each): solid — tight, orderly; liquid — close,
   jumbled; gas — far apart. Prediction sentences (1 each): solid keeps shape;
   liquid pours but keeps volume; gas fills container / can be squeezed.
2. (4) Observed: the balloon got bigger, rounder, and firmer, and pushed back
   when squeezed (2). Claim: air is matter made of particles too small to see
   (1). Why evidence: it shows air takes up space and pushes — "nothing"
   cannot push (1).
3. (4) Water's particles are close and jumbled so they can slide past each
   other — the water takes the bowl's shape — but the particles stay close, so
   the volume does not change.
4. (4) a. The balloon/air fills its whole container (and smells spread through
   rooms) — gas particles spread out; they do not pile at the bottom (2).
   b. Redraw particles spread through the whole jar with wide gaps between
   them (2).
5. (3) One correct sentence per word, e.g.: "Color is a *property* we used to
   tell the powders apart." / "The sugar will *dissolve* when I stir it into
   water." / "Trail mix is a *mixture* because the nuts and raisins keep their
   own properties."
6. (9) a. **Q** is flour — dull/powdery and does not dissolve (clouds water);
   two properties cited (3). b. Hand-lens appearance: P shows sparkly crystals,
   R shows fine grains — reflectivity/crystal shape distinguishes them (2).
   c. P is most likely **salt**: sparkly crystals plus complete dissolving
   matches salt's profile (4 for two-sentence reasoning). *Acceptable
   variation:* **sugar** is defensible — it also dissolves — if the learner
   argues from crystal shape (sugar's glassy grains vs. salt's cubic sparkle);
   score the reasoning, not just the name.
7. (8) a. Drawing: water particles close/jumbled with sugar particles spread
   among them; both labeled (4). b. No — sugar's particles are too small and
   spread out to be caught by the filter (2). c. Evaporation — water's
   particles leave and sugar's particles stay behind as crystals (2).
8. (9) a. Two investigations: weighing (mixture weighed the same as water +
   salt separately) and evaporation (salt crystals left behind); what each
   showed, 2 points each (4). b. Revised model + revision sentence naming the
   old model, the evidence, and the new model (3). c. "Turned into water" is
   wrong by properties: the recovered crystals are white/gritty/crystalline
   like salt, not like water — and the mixture weighs more than water alone,
   which it would not if the salt had become water (2).
