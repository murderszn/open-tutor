# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Particle Models, Properties, and Mixtures

**For the adult.** Student-facing pages never link here except where labeled.
Answer key: `answer-key.md` (separate file — never beside a student question).

## Unit at a glance

Weeks 7–10, 16 sessions, ~40 minutes each. Two NGSS performance expectations:
**5-PS1-1** (model: matter is made of particles too small to be seen) and
**5-PS1-3** (identify materials by properties). The unit's spine is
**model → evidence → revision**: Lesson 01 builds the particle claim from
balloon/syringe evidence, Lesson 02 turns it into drawings, Lessons 03–04 use
properties and mixtures as new modeling ground, and Lesson 05 forces a genuine
revision when dissolving evidence arrives.

## Materials checklist (whole unit)

Balloon; plastic oral syringe **with no needle** (adult checks); clear cups and
spoons; water; sugar; salt; sand (clean play sand); baking soda; flour;
cornstarch (optional, for the project); bar magnet; hand lens; penny; balance
or kitchen scale (grams); coffee filters or paper towels + funnel; shallow
dish; science notebook; pencils and one colored pencil (for revisions).

## Safety (non-negotiable)

- The adult previews **every** powder and mixture for eye/skin safety.
- **No tasting** in any investigation — state the rule at the start of every
  session that uses powders or liquids.
- Keep faces away from powder cups; sweep spills, never blow on powders (flour
  dust irritates).
- Syringe is for air only, never pointed at eyes; the adult holds the tip seal
  for younger learners.
- Solubility-test water is discarded, never drunk.

## Lesson-by-lesson misconceptions

- **L01:** "Air is nothing" → counter with push-back evidence. "The syringe
  got emptier" → redirect to *space taken up*, not amount.
- **L02:** Gas particles drawn piled at the bottom → counter with the balloon
  (air fills all of it) and the smell-spreads observation. Liquid particles in
  neat rows → counter with pouring. Never let learners write "atoms" — the
  unit keeps *particles* qualitative.
- **L03:** Single-property identification ("it's white, so it's sugar") →
  enforce the two-property rule without exception, including in the project.
  Do not allow the vinegar-fizz test — it belongs to Unit 03.
- **L04:** "Dissolved sugar is gone" → park it, don't crush it; Lesson 05 is
  designed to resolve it with evidence. "Filtering changed the sand back" →
  the sand never changed; the filter sorted by size.
- **L05:** If the scale shows a big discrepancy, do error analysis together
  (spills, wet spoon) — honest data beats a perfect number. If evaporation
  yields no crystals, use the backup dish and discuss *why* the attempt failed.
  Do not introduce "mass is conserved" as vocabulary — Unit 03 names it.

## Reteach triggers and targeted follow-ups

| Signal | Reteach | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Quiz < 12, or assessment Q1/Q3 weak | Lesson 02 arrangements | Rebuild models with beans on a tray; re-do the draw-then-compare with the reference image |
| Quiz Q4 / assessment Q7 wrong | Lesson 04 mixtures | Re-run the two-mixture filter test; redraw filter-as-holes models |
| Assessment Q6 weak | Lesson 03 properties | New unknown pair with the adult running tests while the learner records |
| Assessment Q8 weak | Lesson 05 dissolving | Re-weigh a fresh sugar-water batch together, narrating the evidence chain aloud |
| Project rubric "Report honesty" ≤ 2 | — | Conference: model an uncertainty paragraph aloud, then the learner rewrites theirs |

## Readiness for Unit 03

The learner is ready when they can (a) draw all three particle arrangements
from memory with a correct prediction each, (b) identify an unknown powder
with two cited properties, and (c) state the revision sentence for the
sugar-water model. Unit 03 (matter conservation and chemical change) builds
directly on the weighing and evaporation evidence from Lesson 05 — keep the
learner's evidence board; Unit 03's first session re-opens it.

## Project scoring notes

A wrong identification with sound two-property reasoning and an honest
uncertainty paragraph may score 3 on Identifications — the rubric rewards
evidence habits over lucky guesses. If two powders are genuinely
indistinguishable with the toolkit (e.g., sugar vs. salt both dissolve and
sparkle), full credit goes to the learner who *names the limit* and proposes
the next test (stronger magnification of crystal shape).
