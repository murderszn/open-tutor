## Part 2 — Properties and mixtures (open notebook, 26 points)

**6. Identify the powders.** The table shows property tests on three unknown
white powders. (9 points)

| Powder | Hand lens | Magnet | In water |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | sparkly crystals | no pull | dissolves completely |
| Q | dull, powdery | no pull | clouds the water, does not dissolve |
| R | fine white grains | no pull | dissolves completely |

a. Which powder is most likely **flour**? Cite two properties. (3)
b. P and R both dissolve. Name **one** property from the table that
   distinguishes them, and state the difference. (2)
c. From the unit's list (baking soda, salt, sugar, flour), give P's most
   likely identity and explain your reasoning in two sentences. (4)

**7. Mixture models.** (8 points)

a. Draw a particle model of **sugar + water after stirring** (sugar dissolved).
   Label the sugar particles and the water particles. (4)
b. Could a filter separate this mixture? Explain with particles in one
   sentence. (2)
c. Name **one** way this mixture *could* be separated, and what the particle
   model says would be left behind. (2)

**8. The full story.** A student stirs salt into water, watches it "vanish,"
and says, "The salt is gone." (9 points)

a. Describe **two** investigations from this unit that prove the salt is still
   there, and state what each one showed. (4)
b. Draw a revised particle model of the salt water and write the
   revision sentence: *what changed in the model, and what evidence forced
   the change?* (3)
c. Why is "the salt turned into water" the wrong explanation? Answer with
   properties. (2)
