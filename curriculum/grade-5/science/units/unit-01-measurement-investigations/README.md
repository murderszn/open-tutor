# Unit 01 — Models, Measurement, and Controlled Investigations

**Grade:** 5 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#27](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/27)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 3–6 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 40 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

This unit teaches the *practices* that power the rest of the year: how to ask a
question science can answer, how to measure **length, mass, volume, and
temperature** with standard tools (and convert within the metric system), how to
run a **fair test** (change one thing, keep the rest the same, repeat trials),
and how to show data in tables and graphs so a claim can be trusted. The
learner also builds their first **scientific models** — labeled diagrams that
explain what was observed — and learns to revise a model when new evidence
arrives. The unit introduces the 3–5 engineering-band design vocabulary
(**criteria** and **constraints**) and the unit's **investigation-report
format** — the same report format every later unit's investigation will use.

This unit carries the grade-5 **math measurement connection** from the
scope-and-sequence: metric conversions within one system (5.MD.1 — for example,
5 cm = 0.05 m; 2.5 L = 2,500 mL), and graphing measured quantities
(measure-and-graph practice that supports 5-PS1 in Units 02–03). Conversions
here are mathematical practice in a science context, not a separate math
lesson; the grade-5 math track teaches the operations, this unit puts them to
work on real measurements.

## Prerequisites (re-taught here as needed)

- Asking questions that can be explored by observing or testing.
- Planning simple fair investigations (one variable changed, repeated trials).
- Measuring length to the nearest centimeter with a ruler; recording in
  labeled drawings, tallies, and simple tables.
- Science safety practices: the adult supervises every investigation;
  materials stay away from the mouth; living things are handled gently; never
  look directly at the sun.

The unit's early sessions (tool stations, the measuring ritual, the
question-sorting game) re-teach these explicitly rather than assuming they are
secure — per the scope-and-sequence, Weeks 1–2 only probed entry points.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Ask a **testable question** and name what would be measured or observed to
   answer it (science practice: asking questions).
2. Measure **length** to the nearest millimeter, **mass** to the nearest gram,
   **volume** to the nearest 10 milliliters, and **temperature** to the
   nearest degree Celsius with standard tools; **convert** within the metric
   system (mm/cm/m/km, g/kg, mL/L); record equivalents in **two-column
   tables** (5.MD.1; science practice of careful measurement).
3. Plan and carry out a **fair investigation**: change one thing (the
   **variable**), keep everything else the same (**controls**), run at least
   three **trials**, and record data in a labeled table (3-5-ETS1-3; science
   practice of planning and carrying out investigations). The fair-test
   routine introduced here is reused in Units 02, 03, and 08.
4. Display collected data in **bar graphs** and **line graphs**; answer
   "how many more," comparison, and trend questions from a graph (science
   practice of analyzing data).
5. Build a **model** (a labeled diagram or description) that explains an
   observation, **revise** the model after new evidence, **define a simple
   design problem** with criteria and constraints (3-5-ETS1-1), and write a
   complete **investigation report** in the unit's format.

## Essential question

*How do careful questions, careful measurements, and honest data displays help
us trust what a test found?*

## Vocabulary

**Testable question** — a question that can be answered by observing,
measuring, or testing. · **Observation** — something noticed with the senses
or a tool. · **Inference** — an idea about what an observation *means*; it goes
beyond what was directly seen. · **Measure** — find out how long, how heavy,
how much, or how warm with a tool. · **Millimeter / centimeter / meter /
kilometer** — metric length units; 10 mm = 1 cm, 100 cm = 1 m, 1,000 m =
1 km. · **Gram / kilogram** — metric mass units; 1,000 g = 1 kg. ·
**Milliliter / liter** — metric volume units; 1,000 mL = 1 L. · **Degree
Celsius (°C)** — metric temperature unit. · **Convert** — rewrite a
measurement in a different unit of the same system. · **Estimate** — a careful
guess *before* measuring. · **Trial** — one try of a test; repeated trials
show patterns and catch mistakes. · **Data** — recorded measurements and
observations. · **Fair test** — a test that changes only one thing while
everything else stays the same. · **Variable** — the one thing changed. ·
**Control** — a thing kept the same on purpose. · **Data table** — rows and
columns that hold data. · **Two-column table** — a table pairing equivalent
measurements (unit-conversion record). · **Bar graph / line graph** — displays
of data that make comparisons and trends visible. · **Trend** — a pattern in
how data changes. · **Claim** — what we say is true because of evidence. ·
**Evidence** — data used to support a claim. · **Model** — a drawing, object,
or description that represents and helps explain something real; models are
revised when new evidence arrives. · **Criteria** — what a successful solution
must do. · **Constraints** — limits on materials, time, or cost. ·
**Investigation report** — the unit's written record of a fair test:
question, plan, data, graph, claim, and revised model.

## Standards and source notes

- Reference framework: **Next Generation Science Standards**, the 3–5
  engineering band and grade-5 performance expectations, from the track
  audit's crosswalk (verified 2026-10-04 against the California Department of
  Education's official reproduction of the NGSS grade-5 release, "NGSS Grade
  Five Arranged by DCI," revised March 2015; cross-checked with
  nextgenscience.org). No state adoption, accreditation, or alignment
  certification is claimed.
- This unit introduces the **practices** thread of the year: **3-5-ETS1-1**
  (define a simple design problem with criteria for success and constraints
  on materials, time, or cost) and **3-5-ETS1-3** (plan and carry out fair
  tests in which variables are controlled and failure points are considered).
  It also introduces the **measure-and-graph practice** used by 5-PS1-2 in
  Unit 03. No grade-5 disciplinary performance expectation is assessed here;
  the practices thread is assessed instead.
- Math connection: **5.MD.1** — convert among different-sized standard
  measurement units within a given measurement system (the metric conversions
  in Lessons 02 and the project).
- Practice datasets in lessons are **fictional practice data**, clearly
  labeled, not observations the learner made. Real investigations (the tool
  stations, the project) use the learner's own measurements.

## 16-session pacing (4 × ~40 min per week)

Sessions rotate: **investigation lesson → measuring-and-modeling practice →
reading-and-talk about evidence → review game**, per the scope-and-sequence.

| Week | Sessions | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1–3 | **Lesson 01** — questions science can answer; observation vs. inference. S1: lesson. S2: observation-station practice (tool tour with the generated image; labeled diagrams). S3: reading-and-talk — observation vs. inference; question-sorting review game. |
| 4 | 4–6 | **Lesson 02** — measuring with standard tools; metric conversions. S4: lesson. S5: measuring practice + two-column conversion tables. S6: reading-and-talk — the metric story; estimate-then-measure game. |
| 5 | 7–9 | **Lesson 03** — fair tests. S7: lesson. S8: plan-your-own-fair-test practice. S9: reading-and-talk — claims need evidence; then **Lesson 04** preview talk. |
| 5–6 | 10–12 | **Lesson 04** — bar and line graphs; models and model revision. S10: lesson. S11: graphing practice from the learner's own ramp data. S12: model-revision talk and practice. |
| 6 | 13–14 | **Lesson 05** — design problems (criteria/constraints) and the investigation-report format. S13: lesson + project launch. S14: project trials and data. |
| 6 | 15–16 | Review week: S15 — measurement-and-graphing challenge, fair-test redesign game, **formative quiz**. S16 — project report finish, **unit assessment**, showcase talk. |

The unit's written load: short responses (8–12 tasks) reviewed the same day,
with oral, drawn, and manipulative response options always available.
