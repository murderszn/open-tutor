# Teacher Guide — Unit 01

**Grade 5 Science · Unit 01 — Models, Measurement, and Controlled Investigations**
Status: draft. For the guiding adult. Student materials never contain answers;
all solutions live in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## How to use this unit

- Sessions are ~40 minutes, 4 per week. The pacing table in the unit
  [README](README.md) names every session; this guide adds per-lesson
  teaching notes.
- Every lesson's written load is 8–12 short tasks, reviewed the same day.
  Oral, drawn, dictated, and manipulative responses always count.
- The **investigation-report format** (Lesson 05) is the year's backbone —
  insist on all six parts from the project onward; Units 02, 03, and 08
  reuse it.
- Safety: you supervise every investigation and preview every procedure.
  Non-mercury thermometers are adult-read; nothing goes near the mouth;
  water spills are wiped immediately; the rolling lane is cleared before
  ramp trials.

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — Testable questions; observation vs. inference

- Push every "testable" claim to **name the measurement**. "We could test
  it" is not enough; "we would measure ___ in ___" is the bar.
- The mystery-bag moment (guided practice #2) is the lesson's anchor —
  don't skip it. If the learner peeks, restart with a new object.
- **Watch for:** learners treating inferences as wrong. Affirm inferences
  as useful starting ideas; the mistake is stopping at them.

### Lesson 02 — Measuring tools; metric conversions

- Demo **zeroing** explicitly at each station; most week-4 errors trace back
  to starting at the ruler's end or an unzeroed scale.
- Keep the conversion wall card up all year (10 mm = 1 cm; 100 cm = 1 m;
  1,000 m = 1 km; 1,000 g = 1 kg; 1,000 mL = 1 L).
- **Multiply-vs-divide anchor:** "smaller unit → more of them → multiply."
  Have the learner say it aloud before each conversion.
- The generated image ([`assets/measurement-station.png`](assets/measurement-station.png))
  is used in the readiness check's tool-detective activity. It is an
  AI-generated illustration (not a lab photo); the cylinder and thermometer
  markings are not readable, so never ask the learner to read values from
  the picture — use it for tool identification only.

### Lesson 03 — Fair tests

- The #1 misconception: learners name the **measured result** as the
  variable ("the variable is how far it rolls"). Drill: the variable is what
  **you** change; the measurement is what **happens**.
- Run the ramp demo yourself in guided practice #3 while the learner
  measures — clean data here pays off in Lesson 04's graphing.
- If the learner's Lesson-03 application plan changes two things, don't fix
  it for them: ask "if the result changes, which change caused it?" and let
  them revise.

### Lesson 04 — Bar and line graphs; model revision

- Insist on **averaging trials** before graphing; let the learner graph raw
  trials once so they feel why averages read better.
- Check the **scale** before bars are drawn — uneven scales are the week's
  most common honest-display error.
- Celebrate model revision out loud. The learner who revises is doing the
  unit's most important thinking.

### Lesson 05 — Criteria, constraints, investigation reports

- Drill the criterion/constraint split with "can I test it?" (criterion) vs.
  "is it a limit?" (constraint).
- The report is not decoration — **the test without the record doesn't
  count**. Require all six parts in the project.
- Session 13 launches the project; Session 14 runs the real trials. Guard
  that time — rushed trials produce the year's weakest data habit.

## Readiness signals and reteach

**Ready to move on** (end of Session 16): the learner names a variable and
two controls for a new question without prompting; converts within the metric
system with the multiply/divide anchor; reads a bar graph's trend and states
one claim the data support *and* one they don't; writes or dictates a
complete six-part report with a genuine model revision.

**Reteach triggers:** if the unit assessment's Part 3 (fair tests/data)
scores below ~70%, re-run Lesson 03's guided practice with a new question
before Unit 02 — every later unit assumes the fair-test routine. If
conversions are shaky, add two 10-minute conversion warm-ups in Unit 02's
first week (Unit 02 leans on measurement again).
