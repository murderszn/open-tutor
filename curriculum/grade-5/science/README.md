# Grade 5 Science

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are delivered;
the eight units are planned, not yet written. See [scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained for
one grade-5 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository. All investigations are
adult-supervised; see the safety practices in the scope-and-sequence.

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-5 science course organized around scientific
practices and modeling; the particle model of matter, observable properties,
and mixtures; conservation of weight and evidence of chemical change; energy
flow from the sun through plants to animals; matter cycling in ecosystems;
Earth-system sphere interactions and water distribution; the water cycle and
community resource stewardship; gravity and observable sky patterns; and a
capstone engineering-design sequence on ecosystem and Earth-system problems.
Sessions are about 40 minutes, four per week, and rotate across an
investigation lesson, data-and-modeling practice, science reading-and-talk,
and review — with short written responses, drawings, and hands-on tasks, and
explicit adult directions for every investigation.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Plan and carry out controlled investigations: ask a testable question,
   change one variable at a time, repeat trials, measure and graph quantities,
   and use evidence to build explanations and compare models (3-5-ETS1-3).
2. Develop particle models showing matter is made of particles too small to be
   seen; identify materials by observable properties — no density, no
   atomic-scale mechanisms (5-PS1-1, 5-PS1-3).
3. Measure and graph to show the total weight of matter is conserved through
   heating, cooling, and mixing; determine whether mixing produces new
   substances (5-PS1-2, 5-PS1-4).
4. Model energy flow from the sun into plants and into animals' food; argue
   plants get growth materials chiefly from air and water; model matter
   movement among plants, animals, decomposers, and the environment — no
   molecular explanations (5-PS3-1, 5-LS1-1, 5-LS2-1).
5. Model how two Earth spheres interact; describe and graph water amounts and
   percentages in Earth's reservoirs (5-ESS2-1, 5-ESS2-2).
6. Model the water cycle to explain where usable water comes from; obtain and
   combine information about how communities protect Earth's resources and
   environment (5-ESS3-1).
7. Support with evidence that Earth's gravity pulls objects down toward its
   center; argue star brightness differences come from relative distances;
   graph shadow, day/night, and seasonal star patterns — no star sizes, no
   causes of seasons (5-PS2-1, 5-ESS1-1, 5-ESS1-2).
8. Complete an engineering design cycle on an ecosystem or Earth-system
   problem: criteria and constraints, solution comparison, fair tests with
   failure points considered (3-5-ETS1-1, 3-5-ETS1-2, 3-5-ETS1-3).
