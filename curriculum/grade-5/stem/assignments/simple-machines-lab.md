## 🛠️ Mission Background: How Simple Machines Give Humans Superpowers!

Did you know humans built the Great Pyramids, giant castles, and massive sailing ships thousands of years before engines, electricity, or bulldozers were invented? How did they lift stones weighing 5,000 pounds?

They used **Simple Machines**! Simple machines don't reduce the total amount of *work* you have to do, but they make work feel much easier by changing:
1. The **amount of force** you have to push or pull.
2. The **direction** of the force.
3. The **distance** over which you apply the force.

```
       Work = Force x Distance   (W = F x d)

   Heavy Lifting:   [ HUGE FORCE ] x [ small distance ]
   Simple Machine:  [ SMALL FORCE ] x [ HUGE DISTANCE ]
```

## 🧰 The Six Classic Simple Machines

| Machine | How It Works | Everyday Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Inclined Plane** | A slanted flat surface that lets you roll or slide objects up instead of lifting straight up. | Wheelchair ramp, slide, driveway hill |
| **2. Lever** | A rigid bar pivoting on a fixed point called a **fulcrum**. | Seesaw, crowbar, scissors, baseball bat |
| **3. Pulley** | A grooved wheel with a rope looping through it that changes the direction of pull. | Flagpole rope, crane, window blinds |
| **4. Wheel & Axle** | A large wheel locked to a smaller rod (axle); turning the wheel multiplies force on the axle. | Doorknob, steering wheel, bicycle pedal |
| **5. Wedge** | Two inclined planes joined back-to-back that push things apart or split them. | Axe blade, kitchen knife, doorstop, chisel |
| **6. Screw** | An inclined plane wrapped in a spiral around a central cylinder. | Wood screw, jar lid, corkscrew, lightbulb base |

## 🔬 Your Engineering Challenge

You will construct, test, and measure at least **TWO different working simple machines** using materials in your home or workshop:

### Option A: The Inclined Plane Test
*   **Build**: Prop a flat wooden board, cardboard track, or stiff binder at two different heights:
    *   Setup 1 (Steep): $30\\text{ cm}$ high.
    *   Setup 2 (Gentle): $15\\text{ cm}$ high.
*   **Test**: Pull a loaded toy truck or weighted plastic container up both ramps.
*   **Measure**: Compare how much effort is needed. Which ramp feels easier to pull? Which ramp makes the car travel farther?

### Option B: The First-Class Lever Balance
*   **Build**: Use a stiff 12-inch or 18-inch ruler, a round marker or wooden dowel as the fulcrum, and coins or small blocks as weights.
*   **Test 1 (Equal arms)**: Put the fulcrum right in the center (6 inches). Put 4 coins on one end. How many coins on the other end balance it?
*   **Test 2 (Mechanical Advantage)**: Move the fulcrum close to the load (at 3 inches). Now how many coins on the long side does it take to lift the 4 coins on the short side?

### Option C: The Single or Double Pulley Rig
*   **Build**: Use an empty thread spool, a pencil through the center as an axle, and string looped over the spool.
*   **Test**: Lift a small water bottle by pulling DOWN on the string instead of lifting straight UP.
