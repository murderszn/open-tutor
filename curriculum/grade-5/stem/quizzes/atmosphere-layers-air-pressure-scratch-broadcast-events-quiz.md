### Questions

**1.** What is **air pressure**? Why is air pressure higher at sea level (the beach) than at the top of a tall mountain?

**2.** Name the lowest layer of the atmosphere where all our weather, clouds, and airplanes fly.

**3.** What protective layer in the stratosphere shields all living things on Earth from dangerous solar ultraviolet (UV) radiation?

**4.** Why do your ears "pop" when you ride in an elevator up a skyscraper or fly in an airplane?

**5.** In Scratch, what is a **Broadcast Message**? Give an everyday analogy of how broadcasting works (like a teacher making an announcement over the school intercom).

**6.** Look at these two Scratch scripts on two different sprites:
- **Sprite 1 (Button):**
  ```scratch
  when this sprite clicked
  broadcast [Start Rocket v]
  ```
- **Sprite 2 (Rocket):**
  ```scratch
  when I receive [Start Rocket v]
  glide (2) secs to x: (0) y: (150)
  ```
What happens on screen when the player clicks the button?

**7.** **Diagram Challenge:** Draw a column representing Earth's atmosphere. Label the surface, the Troposphere, and the Stratosphere. Draw a cloud and a plane in the correct layer!

**8.** What are the two most common gases that make up Earth's clean, dry air? (Hint: Oxygen is only about $21\%$!).

## 🔑 Parent Answer Key (For Educator)

<details>
<summary>Click to expand Answer Key</summary>

1. **Air Pressure**:
   - Air pressure is the weight of the blanket of air pressing down on Earth's surface due to gravity.
   - At sea level, there is a whole column of heavy air pressing down from above. At the top of a mountain, there is less air above you, so pressure is lower.

2. **Weather Layer**:
   - The **Troposphere**.

3. **UV Shield**:
   - The **Ozone Layer** (in the stratosphere).

4. **Ear Popping**:
   - As altitude rises, outside air pressure drops quickly. The air trapped behind your eardrum pushes outward until the pressure equalizes through your Eustachian tubes with a "pop".

5. **Scratch Broadcast**:
   - A broadcast block sends a secret signal/message from one sprite that any other sprite can listen for and react to with a `when I receive` block.
   - Analogy: The principal speaking on the loudspeaker—all classrooms hear it at once and can start their fire drill or activity.

6. **Script Execution**:
   - When the button is clicked, it sends out the message. The rocket immediately receives the signal and glides upward to `y: 150` over 2 seconds.

7. **Atmosphere Diagram**:
   - Ground level at bottom $\to$ Troposphere ($0–12\text{ km}$, contains clouds, birds, weather, planes) $\to$ Stratosphere ($12–50\text{ km}$, contains ozone layer).

8. **Gases in Air**:
   - **Nitrogen** ($pprox 78\%$) and **Oxygen** ($pprox 21\%$). (The remaining $1\%$ is argon, carbon dioxide, and water vapor).
