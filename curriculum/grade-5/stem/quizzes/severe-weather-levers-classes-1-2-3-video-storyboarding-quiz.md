### Questions

**1.** What is a **weather front**? What kind of stormy weather usually happens when a cold front pushes rapidly into warm, moist air?

**2.** Explain the difference between a **Tornado Watch** and a **Tornado Warning**. Which one means you must take shelter immediately?

**3.** What is a **lever**? Name the three key parts of any lever system:
- F: _________________ (The pivot point)
- E: _________________ (The push or pull you apply)
- L: _________________ (The object you are moving)

**4.** Identify which class of lever these tools are (Class 1, Class 2, or Class 3):
- (a) A playground seesaw (Fulcrum in the middle): _________________
- (b) A wheelbarrow (Load in the middle): _________________
- (c) A fishing rod or tweezers (Effort in the middle): _________________

**5.** If you are trying to pry open a stuck paint can lid with a screwdriver:
- Where should you place the fulcrum (close to the lid or far away) to make it easiest to pry open?

**6.** In video production, what is a **storyboard**? Why do filmmakers and YouTube creators sketch out storyboards before turning on the camera?

**7.** **Diagram Challenge:** Draw a First-Class lever (like a seesaw or crowbar). Clearly label the **Fulcrum**, the **Load**, and the **Effort**.

**8.** What safety rules should your family follow if severe lightning is flashing outdoors during a thunderstorm?

## 🔑 Parent Answer Key (For Educator)

<details>
<summary>Click to expand Answer Key</summary>

1. **Weather Fronts**:
   - A boundary separating two distinct air masses of different temperatures and densities.
   - A cold front pushing into warm air creates sudden updrafts, forming cumulonimbus thunderheads, heavy downpours, strong gusts, and thunderstorms.

2. **Watch vs. Warning**:
   - Tornado Watch: Conditions are favorable for tornadoes to develop in the area; be alert.
   - Tornado Warning: A tornado has actually been spotted or detected by Doppler radar; take shelter immediately!

3. **Lever Anatomy**:
   - Fulcrum (the fixed pivot point).
   - Effort (the input force applied).
   - Load (the resistance weight to be moved).

4. **Lever Classes**:
   - (a) Seesaw: Class 1 (Fulcrum in middle).
   - (b) Wheelbarrow: Class 2 (Load in middle).
   - (c) Fishing rod / Tweezers: Class 3 (Effort in middle).

5. **Prying Lever Advantage**:
   - Close to the lid! Placing the fulcrum close to the load creates a long effort arm, multiplying your prying force.

6. **Storyboarding**:
   - A sequence of drawings/comic-style panels representing planned camera shots, actions, and dialogue.
   - Saves time, prevents forgotten shots, organizes lighting/props, and ensures clear storytelling.

7. **Lever Diagram**:
   - Bar resting on triangle fulcrum in center; Load weight on one end; downward Effort arrow on opposite end.

8. **Lightning Safety**:
   - "When thunder roars, go indoors!" Stay inside an enclosed building or vehicle; avoid open fields, tall solitary trees, bodies of water, and metal fences.
</details>
