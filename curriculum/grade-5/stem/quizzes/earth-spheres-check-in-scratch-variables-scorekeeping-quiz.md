### Questions

**1.** Review your Earth Spheres Investigation project: How did you observe the water cycle inside your closed terrarium jar? What formed on the glass?

**2.** In science, what is the difference between an **observation** and an **inference**?
- (a) "The grass is wet." $\to$ (Observation or Inference?)
- (b) "It must have rained last night." $\to$ (Observation or Inference?)

**3.** What is a **variable** in computer programming? Why is a variable like a labeled storage box?

**4.** Look at this Scratch script for a Coin sprite:
```scratch
when I receive [Coin Collected v]
change [Score v] by (1)
play sound [Coin Sound v]
hide
```
- What happens to the `Score` variable when this block runs?
- Why is it important to reset `set [Score v] to (0)` when the green flag is clicked?

**5.** If you want a player to start with 3 lives and lose a life every time they bump into an alien, what Scratch block should you put inside `if <touching [Alien v]?>`?

**6.** Why do plants in a sealed terrarium not run out of oxygen or carbon dioxide? Explain the partnership between photosynthesis and plant respiration.

**7.** **Diagram Challenge:** Sketch a picture of your terrarium jar. Label the gravel, charcoal, soil, plant, and water droplets on the glass walls.

**8.** What is one tip for speaking clearly and holding audience attention when presenting science on YouTube?

## 🔑 Parent Answer Key (For Educator)

<details>
<summary>Click to expand Answer Key</summary>

1. **Terrarium Water Cycle**:
   - Moisture evaporated from soil and plant leaves, condensed into tiny water droplets on the cooler glass ceiling/walls, and trickled down ("rained") back into the soil.

2. **Observation vs. Inference**:
   - (a) Observation (directly gathered using the senses).
   - (b) Inference (a logical conclusion based on evidence and reasoning).

3. **Variables in Code**:
   - A variable is a named storage container in computer memory that holds a value (number or text) that can change while the program runs.
   - Like a labeled storage bin: the label tells you what's inside (`Score`), and you can put different numbers in it anytime.

4. **Score Handling in Scratch**:
   - The `Score` variable increases by 1 point, plays a sound, and hides the coin.
   - Resetting to zero at the green flag ensures the new game starts fresh and doesn't keep points from the previous game!

5. **Losing a Life**:
   - `change [Lives v] by (-1)` (or `set [Lives v] to (Lives - 1)`).

6. **Gas Balance in Terrarium**:
   - During the day, plants use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to make food and release oxygen (photosynthesis). At night, plants consume a little oxygen and release carbon dioxide (cellular respiration), recycling the gases indefinitely.

7. **Terrarium Diagram**:
   - Diagram should display clear layers: bottom gravel $	o$ charcoal $	o$ soil $	o$ plant $	o$ condensation on walls.

8. **YouTube Presentation Tip**:
   - Look directly into the camera lens (eye contact with viewer); speak with high energy and enthusiasm; use visual props close up.
</details>
