## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-5 language arts course organized around reading
fluency and morphology with precise vocabulary; literary theme, character
comparison, perspective, and genre; informational reading across two or more main
ideas, text structures, multiple accounts, and source-based evidence; grammar
through conjunctions, perfect verb tenses, correlative conjunctions, and sentence
combining; conventions of series punctuation, introductory-element and direct-address
commas, titles of works, and grade-appropriate spelling; narrative writing with
pacing, dialogue, description, and sensory detail; opinion writing with logically
ordered, evidence-backed reasons and counter-consideration; short multi-source
research with paraphrase, source lists, and citation foundations; and a poetry,
multimedia presentation, and revised-portfolio capstone. Sessions are about
35–40 minutes, four per week, blending modeled reading, word study, guided and
independent written practice, discussion, and presentation — every formative check
reviewed by the adult the same day.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Read unfamiliar multisyllabic words accurately using morphology and syllabication; read grade-level prose and poetry orally with accuracy, appropriate rate, and expression; self-correct with context (RF.5.3, RF.5.4).
2. Clarify unknown words with context clues, Greek and Latin affixes and roots, and dictionaries/glossaries/thesauruses; interpret similes and metaphors; explain idioms, adages, and proverbs; use synonyms, antonyms, and homographs; acquire academic words signaling contrast, addition, and logical relationships (L.5.4, L.5.5, L.5.6).
3. In literature: quote text accurately to explain meaning and inferences; determine theme and summarize; compare characters, settings, or events; explain chapter/scene/stanza structure; describe how point of view shapes events; analyze visual and multimedia elements; compare stories in the same genre (RL.5.1–5.7, RL.5.9, RL.5.10).
4. In informational text: quote text accurately; determine two or more main ideas and explain supporting details; explain relationships among individuals, events, ideas, and concepts; compare text structures across texts; analyze multiple accounts; locate answers across sources; match reasons and evidence to points; integrate several texts (RI.5.1–5.10).
5. Write narratives with situation, narrator/characters, natural event sequence, dialogue, description, pacing, transitions, sensory detail, and a fitting conclusion (W.5.3).
6. Write opinion pieces with a clear stance, logically grouped and ordered reasons backed by facts and details, clause-level transitions, and a concluding statement, drawing evidence from texts (W.5.1, W.5.9).
7. Conduct short multi-source research; summarize and paraphrase with source lists; write informative texts with a clear focus, logical grouping, formatting, precise vocabulary, and conclusions; keyboard two pages in a sitting with guidance (W.5.2, W.5.6, W.5.7, W.5.8).
8. Use grade-5 conventions (conjunctions, prepositions, interjections; perfect tenses; correlative conjunctions; series, introductory, and direct-address punctuation; title punctuation; spelling) and speaking skills (prepared discussion, summarizing texts and speakers with evidence, multimedia presentations in formal English) (L.5.1–5.3; SL.5.1–5.6).
