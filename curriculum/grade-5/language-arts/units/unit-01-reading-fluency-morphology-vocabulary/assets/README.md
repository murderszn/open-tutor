- **Generation tool:** Muse structured media pipeline (`media.generate_image`), 2026-10-11. Output inspected by the worker before linking.
- **Learning purpose:** Lesson 02 (prefixes) launch activity — a concrete visual metaphor for morphology: words are built from parts that stack in a fixed order. Used in a guided question (which block comes first when the tower "reads" left to right; which block carries the core meaning; where the blue block attaches).
- **Factual review (2026-10-11):** Image renders three blocks — red on top, natural wood in the middle, blue on the bottom — beside an open blank notebook and a yellow pencil on a wooden desk. No text appears anywhere in the image (nothing to misspell or mislabel); no people, faces, or identifying features. The color/position mapping (red = prefix/front, natural = base word or root/middle, blue = suffix/end) is stated in the lesson text, not baked into the image, so the image cannot contradict the teaching. No lab data, measurements, maps, or historical claims are depicted. Approved for use.
- **Caption (used in lesson-02):** *Word-part blocks (AI-generated illustration): every long word is a tower of parts — a prefix (red) at the front, a base word or root (natural wood) in the middle, a suffix (blue) at the end.*
- **Alt text:** Three wooden toy blocks stacked on a desk: a red block on top, a natural-wood block in the middle, a blue block on the bottom, beside an open blank notebook and a yellow pencil.
- **Text-only alternative:** Picture three toy blocks stacked on a desk: red on top, plain wood in the middle, blue on the bottom. Read the tower left-to-right as a word: the red block is the PREFIX (attaches at the beginning and changes the word's meaning), the plain-wood block is the BASE WORD or ROOT (carries the core meaning), and the blue block is the SUFFIX (attaches at the end and can change the meaning or the part of speech).
- **Known limitations:** Colors are a redundant cue only — the lesson also states positions (first/middle/last) so color-vision differences do not block the activity. The metaphor is positional, not etymological: real prefixes/suffixes attach horizontally within a written word, which the lesson states explicitly when "tipping the tower on its side."
