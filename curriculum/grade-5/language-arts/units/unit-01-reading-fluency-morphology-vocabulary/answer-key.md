# Answer Key — Unit 01: Reading Fluency, Morphology, and Precise Vocabulary

**For the guiding adult only.** Every lesson practice item, practice-set
task, quiz question, assessment task, and the project rubric is solved
here. Learner-facing pages link here by pointer only — never copy
answers into learner view.

## Lesson 01

**Guided practice**
1. Read-1 error count varies by learner. Record the number and the
   type of each error (skipped word / swapped word / misread). Do not
   correct mid-read; the tally is the data for Read 3.
2. For each error: point to the word, name the type, and say what
   letter or chunk tricked you (e.g., "I swapped *quiet* for *quite* —
   the *qu* start tricked me").
3. Smoothness read: pauses should land on commas ("At dusk, | children
   carried..."), periods (full stop), the em-dash pause in dialogue
   tags, and the question mark lift in "Was it the highest?"
4. The calling voice is correct: the mayor addresses a crowd, and the
   exclamation marks demand energy. Flat reading ignores the author's
   stage directions.
5. Accept any honest, specific improvement: fewer errors, steadier
   speed, pauses at punctuation, or a voice that matched the meaning.

**Independent practice**
1. Fourth-read tally; the count should drop from Read 1. If it does
   not, the passage may be too hard — drop to one paragraph and
   echo-read (see teacher guide).
2. Strong answers: the comma after "At dusk" (pause); the exclamation
   in "make a wish before the flame burns low!" (energy); the question
   mark in "Was it the highest?" (lift the voice). Each mark tells the
   reader to stop, pause, lift, or add energy.
3. Sample reasoning: "The second read is smoother because my brain
   already knows the hard words from the first read, so it can spend
   its effort on phrasing instead of decoding. The punctuation map is
   also built — I know where to pause before I get there."
4. The error is *smell* for *hear*: the sentence says the town was
   quiet — quiet connects to hearing, not smelling. Fix: "hear the
   water." The learner must stop the adult, name the swap, and supply
   *hear* using the meaning.
5. Record both tallies; accept any specific causal sentence ("I got
   smoother because I knew where the commas were" / "...because I
   fixed *shimmered* on the first read").

**Exit check:** ≤3 uncorrected errors, audible comma/period pauses, the
mayor's line read with a calling voice, and the trio named (accuracy,
rate, expression).

## Lesson 02

**Launch activity (word-part blocks image):** Tipped on its side to
read left to right: the red block comes FIRST — it is the **prefix**
(attaches at the beginning and changes the word's meaning). The
natural-wood (middle) block carries the word's core meaning — it is
the **base word or root**. The blue block attaches at the END — it is
the **suffix** (attaches at the end; can change meaning or part of
speech). Accept "first/middle/last" position answers even without
color names.

**Guided practice**
1. *preview* = *pre-* ("before") + *view* ("look at") → "look at
   before." (Movie previews show the film before it comes out.)
2. *dis-* flips *appear* to its opposite: *disappear* = "vanish from
   sight, cease to be visible."
3. *impossible* = *im-* (the in- "not" disguise, spelled *im-* before
   *p*) + *possible* ("able to happen") → "not able to happen."
4. *understand*: the meaning test fails — "stand under" does not mean
   "comprehend." The parts no longer assemble into the meaning, so
   treat *understand* as a base word, not a prefix word.
5. The adult's error: *re-* here is "again," not "backwards."
   *rebuild* = "build again." The sentence decides which re- sense
   fits; "build backwards" makes no sense for a wall.

**Independent practice**
1. *rewrite* = re- (again) + write → "write again." *misjudge* =
   mis- (wrongly) + judge → "judge wrongly." *preheat* = pre-
   (before) + heat → "heat before." *disappear* = dis- (opposite) +
   appear → "vanish from sight."
2. *illegal* = il- (in- "not" disguise before *l*) + legal → "not
   legal." *overtake* = over- (above/beyond) + take → "go past, pass."
   *undercook* = under- (too little) + cook → "cook too little."
   *uncertain* = un- (not) + certain → "not certain."
3. Sample sentences (any correct use accepted): "Please *reread* the
   directions before you start." / "The magician made the coin
   *disappear*." / "Don't *overcook* the pasta."
4. Sample reasoning: "Knowing *mis-* means 'wrongly' lets me split
   *miscalculate* before I even sound it out — I expect the *mis*
   chunk and its sound, and I already know half the meaning, so the
   word breaks itself instead of being one long blur."
5. Sort: *im-* (disguise before m/p): impatient, immature. *re-*:
   replay. *un-*: unfair. *ir-* (disguise before r): irregular.
   *pre-*: preview. *mis-*: misplace. *over-*: overhead. *dis-*:
   disconnect.

**Exit check:** *irresponsible* = *ir-* (in- "not" disguise before *r*)
+ *responsible* → "not responsible." *overestimate* = *over-* ("too
much") + *estimate* → "estimate too high."

## Lesson 03

**Guided practice**
1. *fearless* = *fear* + *-less* ("without") → adjective: "without
   fear."
2. *enjoyment* = *enjoy* + *-ment* → noun: "the result of enjoying."
   Proof of noun: "the enjoyment" / "their enjoyment" — *the* fits in
   front.
3. *invent* + *-ion* → *invention* (no final *e* to drop). "The act of
   inventing." Noun.
4. *suddenly* = *sudden* + *-ly* → adverb: "in a sudden way." It
   answers "how?" (how did it happen?), not "what kind?"
5. The adult's error: *-ful* makes adjectives, not nouns. *hopeful* =
   adjective ("a hopeful child" — describes the noun *child*).

**Independent practice**
1. *powerful* = power + -ful → adjective, "full of power." *hopeless*
   = hope + -less → adjective, "without hope." *flexible* = flex +
   -ible → adjective, "can be flexed/bent." *bravely* = brave + -ly
   (drop the e) → adverb, "in a brave way."
2. *movement* = move + -ment (drop the e) → noun, "the act of moving."
   *kindness* = kind + -ness → noun, "the quality of being kind."
   *decision* = decide + -sion (drop the e; d→s spelling) → noun, "the
   act of deciding." *actor* = act + -or → noun, "one who acts."
3. *happy* + *-ness* → *happiness* (y→i). *run* + *-er* → *runner*
   (double the n). *celebrate* + *-tion* → *celebration* (drop the e).
   *fear* + *-less* → *fearless* (no change).
4. Sample reasoning: "-ful and -less are a matched opposite pair, so
   one base gives you two opposite meanings for free — *careful* vs.
   *careless*. If we only had -ful, we'd have to say 'not careful'
   with two words every time, and the sentence would be slower and
   less exact."
5. Sample sentences (any correct use with the part of speech labeled
   correctly): "The *teacher* (noun) explained the rule." / "She ran
   *quickly* (adverb) to catch the bus." / "The instructions were
   *readable* (adjective)." / "Their *kindness* (noun) surprised me."

**Exit check:** *thoughtless* = thought + -less → adjective, "without
thought." *payment* = pay + -ment → noun, "the act/result of paying."

## Lesson 04

**Guided practice**
1. *dictate* = *dict-* ("say") + *-ate*; dic-tate; "to say the words
   (for someone to write down)."
2. *scrib-* = "write": *describe* = "write down about (something)";
   *prescribe* = *pre-* ("before") + *scrib-* ("write") → "write
   before, order in writing" (as a doctor does).
3. *construction* = *con-* ("together") + *struct-* ("build") +
   *-tion* ("act of"); con-struc-tion; "the act of building together."
4. "Throw back" + *ject* ("throw") → *reject* = *re-* ("back") +
   *ject* ("throw").
5. The adult's error: *photo-* is "light," not "far" — *tele-* is
   "far." *photograph* = *photo-* ("light") + *graph* ("write") =
   "light-writing," a picture written by light.

**Independent practice**
1. *telegraph* = *tele-* ("far") + *graph* ("write"); tel-e-graph;
   "writing sent far" (old-time texting). *portable* = *port-*
   ("carry") + *-able* ("can be"); port-a-ble; "can be carried."
2. *subtract* = *sub-* ("away, under") + *tract* ("pull"); sub-tract;
   "pull away" (take away). *transform* = *trans-* ("across, change")
   + *form* ("shape"); trans-form; "change shape."
3. *struct-* words: construct, structure, instruct, destruction. What
   they share: all concern building — or, for *destruction*,
   un-building what was built. (Accept: "they're all about building
   things, putting them together or taking them apart.")
4. Sample reasoning: "*spect-* passes the meaning test in all three
   words — 'look/see' fits *inspect* (look into), *spectator* (one who
   looks), and *spectacle* (something to look at). But 'carry' makes
   no sense inside *important* — the letters *port* are there by
   accident. That's why the meaning test matters: letters alone will
   trick you, but the meaning never lies."
5. Speed round: record count correct out of 8.

**Exit check:** *inject* = *in-* ("into") + *ject* ("throw"); in-ject;
"throw into" (as medicine is injected into an arm). *export* = *ex-*
("out") + *port* ("carry"); ex-port; "carry out" (goods carried out of
a country).

## Lesson 05

**Part A — guided practice**
1. *frigid*: inference clue (no labeled type) — "we could see our
   breath" tells you the air is bitterly cold. Meaning: extremely cold.
2. *reluctant*: contrast clue — signal word *however*; her friends are
   eager, she is the opposite. Meaning: unwilling, hesitant.
3. *debris*: example/restatement clue — the dashes set off the list
   (broken branches, scattered papers, overturned bench). Meaning:
   scattered wreckage, rubbish.
4. The adult's error: *after* the loud parade flips the meaning — the
   sentence contrasts with loud. *tranquil* = calm, peaceful. The clue
   was misread; *after* is the signal.

**Part A — independent practice**
1. *luminous*: restatement/definition clue — "glowed green in the dark
   hallway" restates it. Meaning: giving off light, glowing.
2. *quarrel*: inference (cause/effect) clue — started over a book,
   ended with slammed doors. Meaning: an angry argument, a fight.
3. *astonished*: contrast clue — "they had expected the island to be
   barren." Meaning: greatly surprised, amazed.
4. Sample reasoning: "A contrast clue is stronger when the opposite
   word is one I already know — *unlike the kind keeper, the cruel
   captain* tells me *cruel* fast. It's weaker when I don't know the
   other word either. A definition clue is the most direct of all, but
   authors don't always give one, so contrast is my backup."

**Part B — guided practice**
1. *perish*: verb. Sense 1 "to die"; sense 2 "to cease to exist,
   disappear." "The old custom will perish" → sense 2 (a custom can't
   die like a living thing; it ceases to exist).
2. "The enormous dog barked" — keep: fits, stronger than *big*.
   "The colossal dog barked" — keep grammatically, but note it sounds
   exaggerated/comic; *enormous* is the safer precise choice. Neither
   breaks the sentence (both describe size).
3. Shade ladder, quietest → loudest: *content → happy → delighted →
   ecstatic*.
4. *However* (contrast — the sentences disagree: cheap, but weak).

**Part B — independent practice**
1. *murmur*: noun. The fitting sense is "a low, indistinct sound (as
   of people talking)" — "a murmur ran through the crowd."
2. Sample upgrades with swap-and-check: "She *glanced* at the test"
   (quick look — swap-and-check: *stared* would mean a long fixed
   look; *glanced* fits a nervous check). "It was an *excellent*
   plan" (swap-and-check: *reliable* also fits but says less;
   *excellent* is the stronger precise pick). "He *stammered* sorry"
   (swap-and-check: *murmured* is too quiet for an apology that needs
   to be heard; *stammered* shows nerves and fits).
3. Ladder: *cool → chilly → cold → frigid → freezing*. Strongest rung:
   *freezing*. Sample sentence: "The freezing wind cut straight
   through my jacket."
4. Sample reasoning: "Meaning #1 is just the most common meaning, not
   the one your sentence needs. *Steep* sense 1 is 'having a sharp
   slope' and sense 2 is '(of prices) too high.' 'The tickets were
   steep' needs sense 2 — grabbing sense 1 would make you picture a
   slanted ticket, which is nonsense. The sentence picks the number."

## Practice sets

**A1.** Slashes should land on punctuation and meaning breaks (after
"At dusk," before "and make a wish," at periods). If slashes land
mid-phrase, re-mark together.
**A2.** The command voice matches: the mayor is addressing the whole
town at a celebration. Sample: "I chose the command voice because the
exclamation marks and the crowd setting show the mayor is calling out,
not whispering."
**A3.** Planted errors (adult's choice): the learner must stop the
adult, name the error type (skip/swap/misread), and supply the correct
word.
**B1.** *in-*: incorrect (before *c*), independent (before *d*).
*im-*: immature (before *m*), impatient (before *p*). *il-*:
illogical (before *l*). *ir-*: irresponsible (before *r*). The next
letter forces the spelling.
**B2.** *rebuild* = "build again" (bare: "build"). *disagree* = "not
agree" (bare: "agree"). *oversleep* = "sleep too much" (bare:
"sleep"). *misspell* = "spell wrongly" (bare: "spell").
**B3.** Real prefixes: *unhappy* (un-), *restart* (re-), *restore*
(re- "back" + store — "bring back"), *preview* (pre-). Fakes: *uncle*
(*cle* is not a word), *previous* (*vious* is not a word).
**C1.** *darkness* = dark + -ness → noun, "the state of being dark":
"The darkness fell quickly." *teacher* = teach + -er → noun, "one who
teaches": "The teacher smiled." *quickly* = quick + -ly → adverb, "in
a quick way": "She ran quickly." *readable* = read + -able →
adjective, "can be read": "The sign was readable."
**C2.** *hopeful* (no change — the *e* stays before *-ful*);
*carried* (y→i); *swimmer* (double the *m*); *celebration* (drop the
*e*).
**C3.** -able: breakable, washable, comfortable. -ible: visible,
terrible, flexible. Honest rule: there is no sound rule — same sound,
two spellings; memorize the common ones and verify with a dictionary.
**D1.** *dict-*: dictate ("say the words"), predict ("say before"),
verdict ("say the truth/decision" — *ver-* "true" is a bonus root),
dictionary ("book of what is said/words"). *port-*: transport ("carry
across"), portable ("can be carried"), import ("carry in"), export
("carry out").
**D2.** "See far" → *television* (*tele-* far + *vis* see — "far
seeing"; *vis* is a bonus root). "Write before" → *prescribe*
(*pre-* before + *scrib* write). "Pull toward" → *attract* (*at-*
toward + *tract* pull). "Say before" → *predict* (*pre-* before +
*dict* say).
**E1.** *arid*: restatement/definition — "where rain almost never
falls" defines it. Meaning: dry, without rain. *elated*: restatement
— "had never been happier" restates it. Meaning: very happy,
overjoyed.
**E2.** *novel* (noun): "a long fictional story" — "She finished the
novel in two days." *novel* (adjective): "new, original" — "It was a
novel idea." (Name the part of speech and the sense used each time.)
**E3.** Sample rewrite: "The violent storm roared. She hurried to the
window and stared out. 'That's perfect,' she exclaimed." Defenses
(swap-and-check in writing): "*stared*, not *glanced* — 'glanced out'
sounds too casual for someone watching a storm; the sentence needs
intent looking." / "*exclaimed*, not *murmured* — 'murmured' is too
quiet for this excited sentence; read aloud, 'murmured' breaks the
energy."

## Week-04 quiz

1. Accuracy, rate, expression. Expression = reading with a voice that
   matches the meaning — punctuation is the author's stage directions
   (period = stop, comma = pause, question = lift, exclamation =
   energy).
2. Stop; look back at the word; use the letters and the meaning to fix
   it (*shimmered*, not *shivered*); reread the sentence; keep going.
   Strategy: self-correction — using context to confirm or self-correct
   (RF.5.4.c).
3. *mis-* ("wrongly") + *understand* + *-ing* → "the act of
   understanding wrongly."
4. *in-* (inactive), *im-* (impossible), *il-* (illegal), *ir-*
   (irregular). Same "not" prefix; the next letter picks the spelling.
5. No. The meaning test fails: *cle* is not a word, so *un-* cannot be
   a prefix here — the letters are coincidence.
6. *fear* + *-less* ("without") → adjective: "without fear."
7. Noun. The suffix *-tion* ("act or state of") changed the verb
   *celebrate* (an action) into the noun *celebration* (the thing
   itself).
8. *trans-* ("across") + *port-* ("carry") + *-tion* ("act or state
   of," makes a noun) → "the act of carrying across." (The *-a-* is a
   connecting vowel; accept answers that note it.)
9. *import*: real — *im-* ("in") + *port-* ("carry") = "carry in,"
   which is exactly what importing is. *important*: fake — "carry"
   makes no sense inside "important"; the letters are coincidence.
10. Drop the *e* (*navigate* → *navigation*); double the consonant
    (*run* → *runner*); change *y* to *i* (*happy* → *happiness*).

## Unit assessment

**Part 1 — word-part analysis (8 pts)**
1. *irreversible* = *ir-* (in- "not" disguise before *r*) + *revers*
   (from *reverse*, "turn back") + *-ible* ("can be") → "cannot be
   turned back, cannot be reversed."
2. *overestimate* = *over-* ("too much") + *estimate* → "to estimate
   too high."
3. *carelessness* = *care* + *-less* ("without") + *-ness* ("state
   of") → noun: "the state of being without care."
4. *illegible* = *il-* (in- "not" disguise before *l*) + *leg*
   ("read") + *-ible* ("can be") → "cannot be read."
5. *prediction* = *pre-* ("before") + *dict* ("say") + *-tion* ("act
   of") → noun: "the act of saying before; a foretelling."
6. *reconstruct* = *re-* ("again") + *con-* ("together") + *struct*
   ("build") → "to build together again."
7. *portable* = *port-* ("carry") + *-able* ("can be") → adjective:
   "can be carried."
8. *misinformed* = *mis-* ("wrongly") + *inform* + *-ed* → "wrongly
   informed; given wrong information."

**Part 2 — vocabulary in context (6 pts)**
9. *ascend*: restatement/inference — the em-dash phrase "all two
   hundred of them" (the stairs) plus "to light the great lamp" at the
   top shows upward movement. Meaning: to climb up, go upward.
10. *perilous*: example/cause-effect — "waves crashed against the
    rocks," "one slip on the icy steps could be fatal." Signal words:
    the storm description; *could be fatal*. Meaning: dangerous,
    full of risk.
11. *fretful*: contrast — signal word *Unlike*; the keepers are "calm
    and steady," so the sailors are the opposite. Meaning: worried,
    anxious, restless.
12. *illuminate*: word parts — *il-* (in-, intensifier) + *lumin*
    ("light") → "to fill with light"; context — "its beam would
    illuminate the black water for miles." Meaning: to light up.
13. *testament*: noun. Fitting sense: "something that serves as proof
    or evidence" (the logbook is proof of patience, duty, light).
    Rejected senses: a legal will, or the Old/New Testament — neither
    fits a keeper's weather logbook. (1 pt: part of speech + correct
    sense + one ruling-out reason.)
14. Sample rewrite: "a proof of patience, duty, and light." Judgment
    (either defended answer earns full marks): "proof" is stronger for
    plain clarity but weaker for solemn tone — *testament* sounds more
    formal and honoring, which fits a tribute sentence. Accept the
    reverse judgment if defended ("proof is stronger because it says
    exactly what it means").

**Part 3 — fluency self-check (4 pts):** 1 point per box checked
*with written evidence* (a named fixed word, an agreed rate note, a
named expressive sentence, a named self-correction). An unchecked box
or a checked box with no evidence earns 0 for that box.

**Part 4 — precise-word writing (6 pts)**
15. a. "The keeper **climbed** the stairs." (shade: walked → climbed;
    swap-and-check: *strolled* is too casual for 200 tower stairs,
    *marched* is too military — *climbed* fits the effort.)
    b. "The storm was **violent**." (swap-and-check: *enormous*
    describes size, but the sentence needs force — *violent*/*fierce*
    is precise; *huge* would be vague-for-vague.)
    c. "She **recorded** that the lamp was lit." (swap-and-check: a
    logbook entry is a recording — *murmured* is too quiet and
    *announced* too public; *recorded* matches the keeper's routine.)
    (1 pt per sentence: precise word + named shade/swap + written
    swap-and-check.)
16. Contrast: "The work was lonely; **however**, the keepers were proud
    of it." (Also accept: "**Although** the work was lonely, the
    keepers were proud of it.") Addition: " **Moreover**, the light
    saved countless ships from the reef." (Also accept *in addition* /
    *furthermore* with a correct lighthouse sentence.) (1 pt for the
    combined sentence with a correct contrast signal; 2 pts for the
    original addition sentence used correctly — 1 for the signal word,
    1 for the sentence making sense.)

## Project rubric

Score each criterion 1–4 per the [rubric](project.md#rubric); total
20.

- **Word collection:** 4 = 20+ words, every entry has a real source
  sentence (spot-check 5 against the learner's actual reading). 3 =
  16–19 words, nearly all sourced. 2 = 10–15 words or several missing
  sources. 1 = fewer than 10 or context-free words.
- **Word-part analysis:** 4 = every entry broken in the right order
  with each piece's meaning named; at least one fake/tricky word
  caught by the meaning test (a corrected first attempt still earns
  the 4 — revision is the skill). 3 = most entries correct, minor
  piece errors. 2 = frequent errors (wrong root, missed assimilated
  spelling). 1 = parts missing or merely guessed.
- **Clue use:** 4 = clue type + signal words correct for every entry.
  3 = correct for most. 2 = types named but often wrong. 1 = no types
  named.
- **Verification:** 4 = part of speech + sentence-correct numbered
  sense recorded for every entry, with a guessed-vs.-verified note.
  3 = present for most, sense choice mostly right. 2 = lookups
  attempted but senses often wrong ("sense 1 because it's first" caps
  this at 2). 1 = no verification.
- **Precise-word upgrades:** 4 = 5 upgrades, each genuinely more
  precise *in its sentence*, with a sensible written swap-and-check.
  3 = 5 upgrades, mostly precise. 2 = fewer than 5, or swaps that
  break their sentences. 1 = no upgrades or vague-for-vague swaps.

**Bands:** 18–20 exemplary · 14–17 proficient · 10–13 developing
(reteach the weakest criterion, then revise) · below 10: revisit
Lessons 02–05 with the teacher guide before revising.
