# Unit 01 — Reading Fluency, Morphology, and Precise Vocabulary

**Grade:** 5 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #28](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/28), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Fluent readers are word builders. In this unit the learner reads
grade-level prose and poetry aloud with accuracy, appropriate rate, and
expression, rereading to smooth out the rough spots and self-correcting
when the words stop making sense. The same weeks crack open how English
words are built: prefixes that flip meaning, suffixes that change a
word's job, and Greek and Latin roots that repeat across hundreds of
words. The learner hunts unknown words with context clues, verifies them
in dictionaries and thesauruses, and trades vague words for precise
ones. Every strategy feeds the next: faster accurate reading, surer word
solving, sharper word choice.

## Essential question

How does knowing how words are built make me a smoother, more exact
reader and writer?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the grade-4 language
arts track:

- Decode two- and three-syllable words using letter sounds and common
  syllable patterns; common prefixes (un-, re-, dis-, pre-) and suffixes
  (-ful, -less, -ed, -ing, -tion); Latin suffixes (-tion, -sion, -able,
  -ible, -ment)
- Read grade-level text orally with developing accuracy, rate, and
  expression; reread a passage to improve smoothness
- Use sentence-level context clues (definitions, examples, restatements)
  to guess unknown words; beginning dictionary routines; notice shades of
  meaning among related words
- Ask and answer questions about stories and informational texts

The diagnostic re-teaches nothing here; Lesson 02 re-teaches the prefix
set explicitly rather than assuming it is secure, and Lesson 01
re-teaches the fluency trio (accuracy, rate, expression) with the
self-correction routine.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Read grade-level prose and poetry aloud with **accuracy**,
   appropriate **rate**, and **expression** on successive readings; use
   context to **confirm or self-correct** word recognition and
   understanding, rereading as necessary (RF.5.4.b, RF.5.4.c).
2. Decode unfamiliar multisyllabic words by combining letter-sound
   knowledge, syllabication patterns, and morphology — roots and affixes
   (RF.5.3.a).
3. Use common, grade-appropriate Greek and Latin **prefixes, suffixes,
   and roots** as clues to the meaning of unfamiliar words (L.5.4.b).
4. Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown words using **context
   clues** (definitions, examples, restatements, comparisons,
   cause/effect), then **verify** meanings with print or digital
   dictionaries, glossaries, and thesauruses (L.5.4.a, L.5.4.c).
5. Choose and use **precise, grade-appropriate academic and
   domain-specific words** — including words that signal contrast,
   addition, and other logical relationships, and related words linked
   by synonym/antonym relationships — in speaking and writing (L.5.5.c,
   L.5.6).

## Unit word sets (authored for this unit; reused across lessons and practice)

**Fluency passages (original, written for this unit):** "The Night of
the Lanterns" (prose, ~130 words, used for repeated reading in Lesson
01); "The Thunderhead" (poem, used for phrasing and expression in
practice sessions).

**Prefixes (meaning → example words):**
- un- ("not, opposite of"): unhappy, unfair, unlock, uncertain
- re- ("again, back"): reread, rewrite, rebuild, replay
- pre- ("before"): preview, prepay, preheat, prehistoric
- dis- ("not, opposite of"): disagree, disappear, distrust, disconnect
- mis- ("wrongly, badly"): misplace, misunderstand, misjudge,
  miscalculate
- in-/im-/il-/ir- ("not" — spelling changes to match the next letter):
  inactive, impossible, illegal, irregular
- over- ("too much, above"): overcook, overflow, overtake, overhead
- under- ("too little, below"): undercook, undervalue, underground,
  underestimate

**Suffixes (meaning; part of speech it makes):**
- -ful ("full of" → adjective): careful, joyful, hopeful, thankful
- -less ("without" → adjective): careless, fearless, hopeless, endless
- -able/-ible ("can be" → adjective): readable, breakable, visible,
  flexible
- -ly ("in that way" → adverb): quickly, quietly, bravely, suddenly
- -ment ("act, state, or result of" → noun): enjoyment, movement,
  payment, agreement
- -tion/-sion ("act or state of" → noun): action, celebration,
  decision, invention
- -ness ("state or quality of" → noun): kindness, darkness, happiness,
  weakness
- -er/-or ("one who" → noun): teacher, runner, inventor, actor

**Greek/Latin roots (meaning → example words):**
- graph- ("write"): autograph, photograph, paragraph, biography
- tele- ("far"): telephone, television, telescope, telegraph
- photo- ("light"): photograph, photon, photosynthesis
- port- ("carry"): transport, portable, import, export
- dict- ("say"): dictate, predict, dictionary, verdict
- scrib/script- ("write"): describe, prescribe, manuscript,
  inscription
- struct- ("build"): construct, structure, instruct, destruction
- spect- ("look, see"): inspect, respect, spectacle, spectator
- tract- ("pull, draw"): attract, subtract, tractor, traction
- form- ("shape"): transform, inform, format, formula
- ject- ("throw"): reject, eject, project, inject

**Context-clue practice words (solved, never memorized cold):**
reluctant, frigid, luminous, quarrel, debris, tranquil, astonish,
perish

**Precise-word upgrade pairs:** said → murmured / exclaimed /
stammered; big → enormous / colossal; walked → strolled / marched;
looked → glanced / stared; happy → delighted / content; good →
excellent / reliable; tired → exhausted / drowsy

**Academic words (collect and use):** analyze, demonstrate, contrast,
contribute, observe, predict, summarize, significant, precise, affect,
effect, however, although, moreover, similarly, in addition

All word sets are original curriculum-authored lists; no worksheet or
published list is reproduced. All reading passages in this unit are
original and written for it; no published text is reproduced.

## 16-session pacing

Four sessions per week, ~40 minutes each. Each session names its
activity and success criterion; repeated labels are never reused without
a specific new task.

| Session | Week | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | Lesson 01: The fluency trio — accuracy, rate, expression; the three-read routine with "The Night of the Lanterns" | Three reads recorded; third read shows fewer errors or smoother phrasing than the first; learner names what improved |
| 2 | 3 | Practice A: Repeated reading of the poem "The Thunderhead"; slash-mark phrasing | Reads the poem with ≤3 errors by the third read; pauses land on punctuation; phrasing marks match meaning units |
| 3 | 3 | Lesson 02: Prefixes that flip meaning (un-, re-, pre-, dis-, mis-) | Breaks and defines 8 of 10 prefix words; writes 3 original sentences using them correctly |
| 4 | 3 | Practice B: Assimilated prefixes (in-/im-/il-/ir-), over-/under-; prefix sort | Sorts 16 words by prefix with ≤2 errors; explains the spelling change in 4 assimilated forms |
| 5 | 4 | Lesson 03: Suffixes — meaning and part of speech (-ful, -less, -able/-ible, -ly, -ment, -tion/-sion) | For 6 of 8 words: names the part of speech and the assembled meaning; builds 4 correct words from bases + suffixes |
| 6 | 4 | Practice C: -ness, -er/-or; spelling-change rules (drop e, double consonant, y→i); suffix ladder | Spells 8 of 10 suffixed forms correctly; names each new part of speech |
| 7 | 4 | Lesson 04: Greek and Latin roots (graph, tele, photo, port, dict, scrib/script, struct, spect, tract, form, ject) | Breaks and reads 6 of 8 unpracticed words using root + syllables; assembles sensible meanings |
| 8 | 4 | Practice D: Mixed word-part review (affix auction) + partner timed fluency read | Mixed word-part set ≥80%; partner read shows improvement from first to second read |
| 9 | 5 | Lesson 05, Part A: Context-clue types — definition, example, restatement, contrast | Names the clue type and meaning for 6 of 8 words |
| 10 | 5 | Practice E: Context-clue investigations cross-checked with word parts | 8 of 10 meanings solved with the clue type named; 4 cross-checked against prefix/root/suffix meanings |
| 11 | 5 | Lesson 05, Part B: Dictionary/thesaurus routines, shades of meaning, precise academic words | Reads a dictionary entry correctly (headword, part of speech, numbered sense) for 4 of 5 words; picks the sentence-correct numbered sense 4 of 5 times; upgrades 4 vague sentences with precise words |
| 12 | 5 | Practice F: Shade-of-meaning ladders, thesaurus swap-and-check, contrast/addition signal words | Builds 3 five-word intensity ladders; swaps 4 thesaurus words and defends or rejects each; uses 3 signal words (however, moreover, although) correctly |
| 13 | 6 | Project workshop 1: Word Detective Field Journal — setup; collect first 8 words from own reading, break into prefix/root/suffix | 8 journal entries with parts labeled and an initial meaning guess |
| 14 | 6 | Project workshop 2: Verify entries (clue → parts → dictionary); write 5 precise-word upgrade sentences | Every entry shows guessed vs. verified meaning; 5 upgrade sentences replace vague words with precise ones |
| 15 | 6 | Review: morphology relay game + fluency poetry slam (reads "The Thunderhead" for an audience) | Relay team ≥7/10; poem read aloud with ≤2 uncorrected errors and punctuation-honored expression |
| 16 | 6 | Formative quiz ([week-04_quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md)) + fluency self-check | Quiz ≥8/10; self-check checklist completed with evidence for each box |

Weeks 1–2 of the track are diagnostic/placement and are not part of this
unit.

## How lessons, practice, reading, investigation, and review fill the unit

Five explicit lessons (sessions 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 — Lesson 05 is taught
in two parts across sessions 9 and 11) introduce each tool; each is
paired with a practice session that applies it (sessions 2, 4, 6, 8, 10,
12). Connected reading appears in every session: the fluency passages
are reread across the unit, morphology words come from authentic
sentences, and the project journal collects words from the learner's own
reading. Retrieval practice runs through the mixed review (session 8)
and the relay (session 15); the week-04 quiz (session 16) is formative,
not final — the unit assessment measures the objectives independently.
Feedback is same-day on practice, and the teacher guide gives reteach
moves for each lesson. Reading volume matters: the learner is expected
to read independently every day of the unit so the journal has real
material.
