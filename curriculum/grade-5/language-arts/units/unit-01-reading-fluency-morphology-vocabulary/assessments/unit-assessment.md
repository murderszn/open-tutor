# Unit 01 — Culminating Assessment

**Unit:** Reading Fluency, Morphology, and Precise Vocabulary (Grade 5
Language Arts)
**For the learner:** Work independently. Show word parts for every
word-analysis item. Use the strategies from the unit — clue, parts,
verify. (Scoring guidance is in the [answer key](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment),
kept separate from this page.)

## Part 1 — Word-part analysis (8 points, 1 per item)

Break each word into prefix/root/suffix pieces, name each piece's
meaning, and write the assembled meaning.

1. *irreversible*
2. *overestimate*
3. *carelessness*
4. *illegible*
5. *prediction*
6. *reconstruct*
7. *portable*
8. *misinformed*

## Part 2 — Vocabulary in context (6 points, 1 per item)

Read the original passage, then answer. For items 9–12, name the
context-clue type, the signal words, and the word's meaning. For items
13–14, follow the directions.

> **The Keepers of the Light** *(original passage, written for this
> assessment)*
>
> For over a hundred years, lighthouse keepers lived lonely, demanding
> lives. Each evening, the keeper would **ascend** the narrow tower
> stairs — all two hundred of them — to light the great lamp. The work
> was **perilous** in winter storms; waves crashed against the rocks
> below, and one slip on the icy steps could be fatal. Yet keepers
> rarely complained. Unlike the **fretful** sailors who paced the docks
> waiting for news, the keepers were calm and steady, keeping their
> careful routines through every gale. When the lamp was finally lit,
> its beam would **illuminate** the black water for miles, and ships
> steered clear of the reef. A keeper's logbook recorded each night's
> weather in neat handwriting — a **testament** to patience, duty, and
> light.

9. What does **ascend** mean? Name the clue type and signal words.
10. What does **perilous** mean? Name the clue type and signal words.
11. What does **fretful** mean? Name the clue type and signal words.
12. What does **illuminate** mean? Use word parts (*il-* + *lumin*)
    plus the context to explain it.

13. Look up **testament** in a dictionary. Copy the part of speech and
    the numbered sense that fits this sentence. Then explain in one
    sentence why the other senses do not fit.
14. The last sentence calls the logbook "a testament to patience,
    duty, and light." Rewrite the sentence replacing **testament**
    with a more precise everyday word — then explain whether your
    replacement is stronger or weaker than *testament*, and why.

## Part 3 — Fluency self-check (4 points)

Read "The Keepers of the Light" aloud **twice** (use the three-read
routine from Lesson 01; the third read is optional). Then complete this
checklist honestly — check each box you can defend with evidence.

- [ ] **Accuracy:** I read every word correctly, or I stopped and
      self-corrected when I didn't. (Evidence: which words did you
      fix? List up to 3.)
- [ ] **Rate:** I read at a talking speed — not racing, not dragging.
      (Evidence: did the adult or a recording agree? Note it.)
- [ ] **Expression:** My voice matched the meaning — I paused at the
      dashes and commas, and the storm sentences sounded different
      from the calm sentences. (Evidence: name one sentence you read
      with expression and what you did.)
- [ ] **Self-correction:** When the words stopped making sense, I went
      back and fixed them instead of skipping ahead. (Evidence: name
      one fix.)

Score: 1 point per box checked *with written evidence*.

## Part 4 — Precise-word writing (6 points)

15. (3 pts) Rewrite these three vague sentences with precise words.
    For each, name the shade or the swap you made and run the
    swap-and-check in writing:
    a. "The keeper **walked** up the stairs."
    b. "The storm was **big**."
    c. "She **said** the lamp was lit."

16. (3 pts) Combine these two sentences with a contrast signal word
    (*however, although, on the other hand*): "The work was lonely.
    The keepers were proud of it." Then write one more original
    sentence using an addition signal word (*moreover, in addition,
    furthermore*) about lighthouses.

**Total: 24 points.** Bands: 21–24 exemplary · 17–20 proficient ·
13–16 developing (reteach weakest part, then revise) · below 13 revisit
the unit's lessons with the teacher guide before retaking. This is a
curriculum check, not a standardized or validated test.
