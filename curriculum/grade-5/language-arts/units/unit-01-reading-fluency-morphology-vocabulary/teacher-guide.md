# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Reading Fluency, Morphology, and Precise Vocabulary

For the guiding adult. Read the [unit README](README.md) and the
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) before starting.
Sessions are ~40 minutes, four per week (track weeks 3–6). Review
practice the same day; keep all learner work, names, and scores in
private storage — never in this public repository.

## How to use this guide

Each lesson below lists: the misconception to watch for, what "good
enough" looks like, and the exact reteach move if the learner struggles.
The [answer key](answer-key.md) solves every item. Nothing here is shown
to the learner.

## Lesson 01 — fluency trio {#lesson-01}

**Watch for:** speed = fluency (racing while dropping words); the
opposite — word-by-word dragging praised as "being careful"; skipping
errors silently instead of self-correcting; monotone reading that
ignores punctuation.
**Good enough:** third read shows fewer errors or audibly smoother
phrasing than the first; the learner can name accuracy, rate, and
expression and point to one punctuation mark that changed their voice.
**Reteach:** drop to one paragraph; you read it first as the model,
then echo-read line by line (you read a line, the learner repeats it
with your phrasing). Mark pauses with slashes together before any
independent read. Do not time the learner until accuracy is stable.

## Lesson 02 — prefixes {#lesson-02}

**Watch for:** letter-matching without the meaning test (*un* in
*uncle*, *re* in *rest*); treating assimilated in-/im-/il-/ir- as four
unrelated prefixes; picking the wrong re- sense ("again" vs. "back") and
not letting the sentence decide.
**Good enough:** 8 of 10 words broken with prefix meaning named; the
meaning test applied correctly to at least one fake (e.g., *uncle*).
**Reteach:** teach 4 prefixes only (un-, re-, pre-, dis-) with physical
cards for a day; play the meaning-test game ("Does the base say a real
word? No? Then no prefix."). Add mis-, the assimilated set, over-,
under- only after those four are automatic.

## Lesson 03 — suffixes {#lesson-03}

**Watch for:** treating every ending as a suffix (*bus, this, plus*);
confusing -able/-ible spelling ("they sound the same, so either is
fine" — spelling still counts; teach dictionary verification);
forgetting the spelling moves (dropping the e, doubling, y→i); naming
the meaning but not the new part of speech.
**Good enough:** 6 of 8 words with part of speech and assembled meaning
correct; 2 of 3 spelling moves applied correctly.
**Reteach:** teach one suffix job at a time — start with -ful/-less as
an opposite pair on the same bases (*careful/careless, hopeful/
hopeless*). Drill the "the ___" noun test and the "how?" adverb test
before adding more suffixes. Use the word cards: sort into
noun/adjective/adverb piles physically.

## Lesson 04 — Greek and Latin roots {#lesson-04}

**Watch for:** letter-matching without the meaning test (*port* in
*important*, *form* in *formal*); freezing when a word has prefix +
root + suffix (too many pieces at once); treating base words as roots
and vice versa.
**Good enough:** 6 of 8 unpracticed words broken, read, and assembled
with sensible meanings; the 11 root meanings recalled with hooks.
**Reteach:** teach 4 roots only (*tele-, photo-, graph-, port-*) with
the keyring cards for a day; do the meaning test as a game on fakes
(*important, formal*). Build two-piece words first (root + one affix)
before attempting three- and four-piece words like *transportation*.

## Lesson 05 — context clues, dictionary, precise words {#lesson-05}

**Watch for:** naming a meaning without naming the clue type; calling
every comma phrase a definition; dictionary as first resort (skipping
clue → parts → verify); grabbing numbered sense #1; thesaurus swaps that
break the sentence ("slim chance" → "skinny chance").
**Good enough:** 6 of 8 clue items solved with type and signal words
named; 4 of 5 dictionary lookups land on the sentence-correct numbered
sense; 4 vague sentences upgraded with defensible swaps.
**Reteach:** enforce the order with the four-box notebook page (Clue →
Parts → Verify → Use) — no box may be skipped. Practice wrong-number
traps deliberately: give 3 sentences for one multiple-meaning word and
require the sense number plus the ruling-out reason each time. For
thesaurus work, require every swap to be read aloud in the full
sentence before it is accepted.

## Readiness and evidence signals (whole unit)

**Ready to move on from a lesson when:** the lesson's exit check passes
at its stated bar two sessions in a row (exit check + the next
practice session).
**Reteach the lesson (don't just reassign practice) when:** the exit
check fails AND the same error appears in the next session's work.
**Ready for the week-04 quiz when:** the fluency log shows improvement
across at least 3 entries and the mixed review (session 8) hits ≥80%.
**Ready for the unit assessment when:** the quiz hits ≥8/10 or the
missed items have been retaught and rechecked.

## Scoring the project rubric

Score with the [rubric](project.md#rubric) and the [answer-key scoring
guidance](answer-key.md#project-rubric). Notes:

- **Word collection:** spot-check 5 entries' source sentences against
  the learner's actual reading — the sentence must be real, not
  invented for the journal.
- **Word-part analysis:** a "correct" break means pieces in the right
  order with each piece's meaning named; the meaning test on at least
  one fake or tricky word (e.g., a word the learner first mis-split)
  earns full marks even if the first attempt was wrong — revision is
  the skill.
- **Verification:** the numbered sense must match the source sentence;
  "sense 1 because it's first" is a 2 at best on this criterion.
- **Upgrades:** "precise" means the new word carries more specific
  meaning in *that* sentence; a longer or fancier word that fits worse
  scores lower than a simple accurate one.

## Fluency observation guidance

Observe, don't interrogate. During reads, tally: errors (mark type:
skip/swap/misread), self-corrections (count them — they are successes),
and one expression note (a punctuation mark honored or missed). Give
feedback in the trio's order: accuracy first ("you fixed *shimmered* —
that's the self-correction working"), then rate, then expression. Never
report words-per-minute as the headline; if you time a read, report it
beside the error count so rate never outruns accuracy. The self-check
checklist in the unit assessment is scored on *evidence written*, not
on your judgment of the read — trust the learner's honest evidence and
discuss gaps.
