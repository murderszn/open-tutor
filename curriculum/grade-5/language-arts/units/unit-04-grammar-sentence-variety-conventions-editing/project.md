# Project — Editor's Portfolio: "The River Festival"

**Time:** two workshop sessions (sessions 11–12), plus revision in
session 14. **Standards:** W.5.5, L.5.1, L.5.2, L.5.3, SL.5.6.
**Materials:** this project; pencil (three colors if you can);
a dictionary or glossary.

## The job

You are the editor of the town's festival article. The draft below
was written quickly, and it has errors in sentences, verbs, commas,
titles, and spelling. Your portfolio has three parts:

1. **A marked draft** — the draft below with every error circled
   and fixed in the margin, using one color per pass (sentences,
   verbs, commas/titles) where you can.
2. **A clean copy** — the whole article rewritten with every error
   fixed and nothing else changed (do not add new facts).
3. **An editing log** — at least 8 entries. Each entry gives: the
   original words, the fix, and the rule in your own words (for
   example: "series comma — commas separate three or more items in
   a series").
4. **A formal paragraph** — rewrite the last paragraph (the
   informal invitation) in formal English for the school
   noticeboard, keeping every fact.

## The rough draft (original, written for this project)

> The River Festival happens every summer in our town. Last year,
> me and my family goes early in the morning. We buyed tickets at
> the gate, and we has already seen the boats lined up along the
> river. The boats were painted bright colors, and each one carried
> a crew of four rowers.
>
> We packed apples cheese crackers and juice for our picnic lunch.
> After lunch we watched the band it played for two hours without
> stopping. The band played our favorite songs, morning train and
> lantern dance, and the crowd sang along. Either we watched the
> parade nor the fireworks. We stayed for the parade. Because the
> rain stopped.
>
> Neither the rain nor the wind stoped the afternoon parade. The
> mayor welcomed everyone and the crowd cheered didn't they. Next
> friday we will have saw every lantern on the river before we go
> home. It was definately the best day of the summer. The festival
> is fun it is the best day of the year.
>
> Hey everyone! Wanna come next year? It's gonna be awesome, you
> gotta see the lanterns, trust me!

The town, festival, and people in this draft are invented for
teaching. The song titles are invented; no real work is quoted.

## Deliverables checklist

- [ ] Marked draft: every error circled, fix written above it,
  rule named in the margin.
- [ ] Clean copy: full article, errors fixed, no new facts added.
- [ ] Editing log: at least 8 entries (original → fix → rule in
  your own words).
- [ ] Formal paragraph: the invitation rewritten formally, same
  facts (festival, next year, lanterns worth seeing).
- [ ] Spelling pass: every unsure word checked in a reference; the
  adult initials the pass.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | 12–10 / 8 / 5 / 2 points (scaled per row) | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Clean copy — errors fixed (18 planted errors; see teacher guide) | 15–18 fixed: full marks; 11–14: most; 7–10: some; fewer: little | 12 |
| Editing log — 8+ entries with original, fix, and rule in own words | 8 complete entries: full; 6–7: most; 4–5: some; fewer: little | 10 |
| Formal paragraph — same facts, formal register throughout | Fully formal, all facts kept: full; mostly formal: most; mixed register: some; still informal: little | 8 |
| Final proofread — clean copy has no new errors; titles formatted; spelling checked | No new errors, titles correct, pass initialed: full; 1–2 slips: most; 3–4 slips: some; more: little | 10 |
| **Total** | | **40** |

**What "ready" looks like:** clean copy fixes at least 12 of the 18
planted errors, the log has 8 complete entries, and the formal
paragraph reads like a noticeboard invitation. Below that, reteach
with the lesson whose rule was missed most (see
[teacher-guide.md](teacher-guide.md)) and revise in session 14.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** the adult may read the draft aloud while the learner
  follows; the learner may dictate fixes and the adult scribes the
  clean copy. The log may be dictated too.
- **Alternate response mode:** instead of three pencil colors, the
  learner may label each fix S (sentence), V (verb), C (comma/title),
  or P (spelling) in the margin.
- **Extension:** write a two-sentence editor's note at the end of
  the portfolio explaining which pass caught the most errors and
  why — and which single rule the author of the draft most needs to
  practise.
