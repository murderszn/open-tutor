# Unit 04 — Grammar, Sentence Variety, Conventions, and Editing

**Grade:** 5 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #28](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/28), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Good writing is not just good ideas. It is sentences that fit together,
verbs that agree about *when* things happened, commas that tell the
reader where to pause, and titles punctuated so a reader knows what
kind of work is named. In this unit the learner becomes the editor of
one short piece — "The River Festival," an original draft about a town
celebration — and of many single sentences besides. The learner names
the job each joining word does, builds perfect verb tenses, fixes
comma and title errors, chooses formal or informal English for the
occasion, and finishes by producing a clean, revised festival article
with an editing log that explains every change by rule. Week 18 is the
track's midyear review: the learner retrieves grammar, vocabulary, and
reading tools from Units 01–04 before the formative quiz and the
culminating assessment.

## Essential question

How do grammar, punctuation, and word choice change what a reader
understands — and how does an editor check them all?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and Units 01–03:

- Read grade-level prose with accuracy, rate, and expression;
  self-correct with context (U01; RF.5.4)
- Use context clues, morphology, and reference materials to clarify
  unknown words (U01; L.5.4, L.5.6)
- Quote accurately from a text when explaining meaning and drawing
  inferences (U02, U03; RL.5.1, RI.5.1) — reused here when the learner
  quotes the festival draft to justify an editing change
- Summarize and paraphrase in one's own words with a source list
  (U03; RI.5.2, W.5.8) — reused in the project's editing log, which
  must state each rule in the learner's own words
- Form simple, compound, and complex sentences from earlier writing
  work; know subject, verb, and clause from grade-4 work (L.5.1 is
  deepened here, not introduced from nothing)

Lesson 01 re-teaches subject, predicate, and clause from scratch
rather than assuming they are secure. Nothing in this unit assumes
the learner has read the Unit 02 stories or the Unit 03 Willow Creek
passages; every sentence and draft this unit needs is printed inside
it.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. **Explain the function** of conjunctions, prepositions, and
   interjections in general and in particular sentences, and **use
   correlative conjunctions** (*either/or, neither/nor, both/and,
   not only/but also*) correctly (L.5.1.a, L.5.1.e).
2. **Form and use the perfect verb tenses** — present perfect
   (*have/has* + past participle), past perfect (*had* + past
   participle), future perfect (*will have* + past participle) — use
   verb tense to convey times, sequences, states, and conditions, and
   **recognize and correct inappropriate shifts** in verb tense
   (L.5.1.b, L.5.1.c, L.5.1.d).
3. **Expand, combine, and reduce sentences** for meaning,
   reader/listener interest, and style (L.5.3.a).
4. **Use punctuation to separate items in a series** (including the
   serial/Oxford comma); **use a comma** to separate an introductory
   element, to set off *yes* and *no*, to set off a tag question,
   and to indicate direct address; **use underlining, quotation
   marks, or italics to indicate titles of works**; and **spell
   grade-appropriate words correctly, consulting references as
   needed** (L.5.2.a, L.5.2.b, L.5.2.c, L.5.2.d, L.5.2.e).
5. **Compare and contrast varieties of English** (dialects,
   registers) used in stories, dramas, or poems; **adapt speech to
   context**, using formal English when appropriate; and **develop
   and strengthen writing by planning, revising, and editing** so the
   finished piece demonstrates command of Language standards 1–3 up
   to and including grade 5 (L.5.3.b, SL.5.6, W.5.5).

## Anchor texts (all original, authored for this unit)

- **"The River Festival" (rough draft)** — an original draft (~200
  words) printed in [project.md](project.md). It describes a town
  festival with boats, food stalls, and a lantern parade, and it
  contains planted errors in tense, commas, titles, and sentence
  variety for the learner to find and fix. It is invented for
  teaching; the town, festival, and people are not real.
- **"The River Festival" (edited model sentences)** — short original
  sentences drawn from the same festival topic appear in Lessons
  01–04 and in [practice.md](practice.md) so each new rule is
  practised on familiar material before new material is introduced.
- **"The Night Market"** — an original assessment passage (~300
  words) printed only in the culminating assessment. It is new to
  the learner at assessment time.
- **Editor's-desk illustration** — a generated illustration in
  [assets/editor-desk-illustration.png](assets/editor-desk-illustration.png),
  used in Lesson 05. It is an AI illustration, not a photograph,
  and it shows no real person's desk.

No copyrighted texts are reproduced. All example titles in this unit
(*The Lighthouse Cat*, *Rain on Tin Roofs*, *Morning Train*, *How
Bridges Stand*) are invented for teaching; any resemblance to a real

## 16-session pacing

Four sessions per week, ~40 minutes each. Each session names its
activity and success criterion; repeated labels are never reused
without a specific new task.

| Session | Week | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 | Lesson 01: Conjunctions, prepositions, interjections — jobs in sentences | Names the job of the underlined word in 8 of 10 sentences and explains it in a full sentence |
| 2 | 15 | Practice A: Joining-word sort, prepositional-phrase hunt, interjection punctuation | Sorts 12 words by job with ≤2 errors; finds 4 of 5 prepositional phrases; punctuates 4 of 5 interjections |
| 3 | 15 | Lesson 02: Correlative conjunctions; expanding, combining, reducing sentences | Combines 3 choppy pairs correctly; expands 2 thin sentences; reduces 1 wordy sentence without losing meaning |
| 4 | 15 | Practice B: Correlative-pair repair + sentence-combining workshop | Repairs 4 of 5 broken correlative pairs; combined sentences keep both ideas and correct punctuation |
| 5 | 16 | Lesson 03: Perfect verb tenses — present, past, future perfect | Forms all three perfect tenses for 4 verbs (including 2 irregular) and matches each to a timeline point |
| 6 | 16 | Practice C: Perfect-tense timeline + tense-shift repair | Labels 8 of 10 verb phrases by tense; repairs 4 of 5 shifted passages |
| 7 | 16 | Lesson 04: Commas — series, introductory elements, yes/no, tag questions, direct address; titles; spelling with references | Punctuates 8 of 10 comma sentences; formats 4 of 5 titles; corrects 4 of 5 spellings with a dictionary check |
| 8 | 16 | Practice D: Mixed comma-and-title editing pass on festival sentences | Finds and fixes ≥8 of 10 planted errors, naming the rule for each |
| 9 | 17 | Lesson 05: The editor's desk — full-draft editing, varieties of English, formal vs. informal | Matches 5 desk tools to editing jobs; rewrites 1 informal paragraph formally; names 2 register differences |
| 10 | 17 | Practice E: Register rewrite + dictionary spelling check | Formal rewrite keeps the meaning and changes the register; spelling check uses a reference for 3 words |
| 11 | 17 | Project workshop 1: Edit "The River Festival" draft + start the editing log (see [project.md](project.md)) | Draft marked with at least 8 found errors; log started with rule named for each |
| 12 | 17 | Project workshop 2: Clean copy + formal paragraph + finished log | Clean copy fixes every logged error; formal paragraph reads formally; log has 8 complete entries |
| 13 | 18 | Midyear retrieval: Units 01–04 relay — morphology, main ideas, structures, grammar | Relay score ≥8/10; retrieval questions answered from memory, then checked against the lessons |
| 14 | 18 | Review: cumulative editing clinic — re-edit a fresh flawed paragraph | Fixes ≥8 of 10 planted errors across all four convention areas (tense, commas, titles, sentence variety) |
| 15 | 18 | Formative quiz ([week-04_quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md)) + corrections discussion | Quiz ≥8/10; every miss re-explained in the learner's own words |

Weeks 1–2 of the track are diagnostic/placement and are not part of

## How lessons, practice, reading, investigation, and review fill the unit

Five explicit lessons (sessions 1, 3, 5, 7, 9) introduce each tool;
each is paired with a practice session that applies it to new
material (sessions 2, 4, 6, 8, 10). Reading runs through the unit as
rereading with a new question: the same festival sentences are first
read for joining words, then for tense, then for commas, then as a
whole draft to edit — so the learner sees that an editor rereads for
one job at a time. The investigation is the Editor's Portfolio
project (sessions 11–12, revised in session 14): it is the unit's
synthesis task, not an add-on. Midyear retrieval and a cumulative
editing clinic (sessions 13–14) precede the formative quiz (session
15); the culminating assessment (session 16) uses a new passage so
it measures transfer, not memory of the project draft. Feedback is
same-day on practice, and the teacher guide gives reteach moves for
each lesson.
