# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Grammar, Sentence Variety, Conventions, and Editing

For the guiding adult. This guide never goes to the learner with the
lessons; keep it separate from student pages. All question solutions
are in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md); this guide explains how to
teach, what to watch for, and what to do next when the learner
struggles.

## How to use this unit

- **Session length:** about 40 minutes, four sessions per week, as
  paced in the unit README. If a lesson runs long, split it at the
  guided-practice break and finish independent practice the next
  session — do not skip the exit check.
- **Feedback:** review practice the same day. A wrong answer that is
  corrected the same day, in the learner's own words, counts as
  learning; a wrong answer left uncorrected until the quiz does not.
- **What "proficient" means:** the success criteria in the unit
  README's pacing table. The formative quiz is formative: a score
  below 8/10 triggers reteaching (below), not a grade penalty.
- **Editing passes:** insist on the pass order in Lesson 05
  (sentences → verbs → commas/titles → spelling) until it is a
  habit. Learners who edit in one read miss roughly half the errors;
  learners who pass in order catch most of them.
- **Register sensitivity:** never let "correcting" a character's
  dialect become the lesson. The editing target is the learner's own
  formal writing. If the learner calls a dialect line "wrong," use
  the Lesson 05 reteach move before continuing.

## Per-lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — Conjunctions, prepositions, interjections

- **Likely misconception:** a comma before every *and*. **Reteach
  move:** "Can each side stand alone as a sentence?" If not, no
  comma. Practise with "peanut butter and jelly" vs. "We ate peanut
  butter, and we drank lemonade."
- **Likely misconception:** calling any long word group a
  prepositional phrase. **Reteach move:** a prepositional phrase
  must start with a preposition and end with a noun or pronoun.
- **Likely misconception:** comma splices "fixed" by deleting the
  comma (making a run-on). **Reteach move:** a splice needs a
  conjunction, a split, or a reordering — never just comma removal.

### Lesson 02 — Correlative conjunctions and sentence variety

- **Likely misconception:** *either/nor* and *neither/or* pairings.
  **Reteach move:** chant the pairs as fixed teams and cover one
  half for the learner to guess.
- **Likely misconception:** reducing by deleting an idea instead of
  empty words. **Reteach move:** list the ideas first, then check
  each one off in the reduced sentence.
- **Likely misconception:** combining with commas alone (a new
  splice). **Reteach move:** every combined sentence must pass the
  Lesson 01 test — clauses joined by comma + conjunction, or split.

### Lesson 03 — Perfect verb tenses and tense consistency

- **Likely misconception:** simple past used as the participle
  ("has went," "have ate"). **Reteach move:** keep the
  irregular-participle wall list (*eat/ate/eaten, write/wrote/
  written, go/went/gone, see/saw/seen, break/broke/broken,
  choose/chose/chosen*) and require a check against it.
- **Likely misconception:** "correcting" every tense change,
  including real time changes. **Reteach move:** the clock test,
  said aloud, before any fix: "Did the time actually change?"
- **Stretch note:** the *neither/nor* verb-agreement point in
  Lesson 02 (verb agrees with the nearer subject) is enrichment;
  do not hold back a learner who has the pair and parallelism right
  but misses the verb.

### Lesson 04 — Commas, titles, spelling

- **Likely misconception:** commas "wherever you pause." **Reteach
  move:** after placing a comma by ear, always name the rule; if no
  rule fits, remove the comma.
- **Likely misconception:** every title in quotation marks.
  **Reteach move:** "long gets slanted, short gets quoted" —
  books/movies/plays/albums italicized or underlined; poems, songs,
  articles, chapters in quotation marks.
- **Likely misconception:** capitalizing every word in a title.
  **Reteach move:** first word, last word, important words only.
- **Spelling habit:** the dictionary check is the skill, not the
  memorized list. Initial the learner's spelling pass in the
  project to make the habit visible.

### Lesson 05 — The editor's desk, varieties, formal/informal

- **Likely misconception:** dialect as error. **Reteach move:**
  "Does the character's speech follow a pattern? Would other
  speakers from that place say it the same way?" If yes, it is a
  variety, not a mistake.
- **Likely misconception:** formal English means longer words.
  **Reteach move:** compare the worked example's two versions —
  formal wins by completeness and exactness, not by word length.
- **Image note:** the editor's-desk illustration has no writing on
  it by design — the learner supplies the words (tool-to-job
  matches, sticky-note questions). If the learner asks what the
  notebook "says," answer: nothing yet; the editor has not written
  in it.

## Project scoring notes

The rough draft contains 18 planted errors (see
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md), Project). Count generously: a fix
that is grammatical and keeps the meaning counts even if it differs
from the key's wording. Do not count the learner's own new errors
against the "errors fixed" row — those belong in the proofread row.
The formal paragraph must keep all three facts (festival, next
year, lanterns worth seeing); a paragraph that drops the lanterns
loses the "facts kept" half of that row even if it reads formally.

## Midyear review (sessions 13–14)

Session 13 retrieves Units 01–04: morphology and context clues
(U01), quoting and theme (U02), main ideas, structures, and
synthesis (U03), and this unit's grammar. If the learner misses
more than two relay items from one earlier unit, re-teach that
unit's core lesson before the quiz rather than drilling this unit
further. Session 14's editing clinic uses a fresh flawed paragraph
(the adult may reuse Practice Set F with new errors, or write six
new sentences with one error per convention area plus two free
choices) — the point is transfer, not memorization of the project
draft's errors.

## Reteach paths

- Quiz misses concentrated in questions 1–4 → re-teach Lesson 01
  (joining words) and Lesson 02 (pairs) with new sentences.
- Misses in questions 5–8 → re-teach Lesson 04 (commas/titles) and
  the Lesson 03 participle list.
- Misses in questions 9–10 → re-teach Lesson 05 (register) and
  Lesson 02 (combining).
- Project log entries that name no rule → return the log with the
  rule column blank and have the learner fill it from the lessons,
  not from memory alone.
