# Practice — Unit 04: Grammar, Sentence Variety, Conventions, and Editing

Work with the adult. Do one set per practice session, in order.
Answers and reasoning are in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) — do not
open it until you have tried each set. Write in your own words
where a question asks for an explanation; the key accepts any
wording that states the same rule.

## Practice Set A — Joining words (Lesson 01)

1. Name the job of the underlined word in each sentence, and explain
   it in a full sentence:
   a. "We crossed the bridge **after** lunch."
   b. "We crossed the bridge, **but** the gate was closed."
   c. "**Wow!** The fireworks started early."
   d. "Maya **and** Jon carried the banner."
2. Sort these 12 words into conjunctions, prepositions, and
   interjections: *and, under, ouch, because, between, hooray, or,
   during, yet, beside, whew, so*.
   *(Note: because is a subordinating conjunction — it joins a
   dependent clause. Sorting it as a conjunction is correct here.)*
3. Find the prepositional phrase in each sentence and name its
   object:
   a. "Lanterns hung above the boats."
   b. "We waited near the stone wall."
   c. "Before sunset, the crowd gathered."
4. Punctuate each interjection two ways (mild with a comma, strong
   with an exclamation mark): "Ouch that hurt" / "Aha I found it".
5. Is each sentence correct, a comma splice, or a run-on? Fix the
   broken ones two ways (join correctly, or split):
   a. "The band played, the crowd danced."
   b. "The band played the crowd danced."
   c. "The band played, and the crowd danced."
6. Join each pair into one compound sentence with the conjunction
   given, using correct punctuation:
   a. "The rain stopped. The parade began." (*so*)
   b. "We wanted front seats. We arrived late." (*but*)
7. Explain why "Fish and chips were served" has no comma before
   *and*, but "Fish was served, and chips were served later" does.
8. Write two sentences: one in which *for* is a preposition ("a gift
   for Maya"), and one in which *for* is a conjunction ("We waited,
   for the show was late"). Explain the difference in one sentence.

## Practice Set B — Correlative pairs and sentence variety (Lesson 02)

1. Repair each broken pair. Name the broken rule (wrong partner or
   not parallel):
   a. "Either Maya nor Jon will steer."
   b. "Both the drums and playing loudly woke the baby."
   c. "Not only did it rain but also the wind blew."
   d. "Neither the cake nor the pie were eaten." *(Stretch: check
   the verb too.)*
2. Complete each sentence with a parallel second half:
   a. "Either we row ___."
   b. "Not only did the choir sing, but also ___."
   c. "Both the banners ___."
3. Expand this thin sentence three ways — add *when*, *where*, and
   *how*: "The boat moved."
4. Combine each choppy group into one sentence without losing an
   idea:
   a. "The gates opened. The crowd rushed in. The music started."
   b. "Maya sewed a flag. Jon painted a sign. They hung both by the
      gate."
5. Reduce each wordy sentence without losing meaning:
   a. "The festival, which is an event that happens once a year, is
      enjoyed by a lot of people who live in the town."
   b. "Due to the fact that it rained, the parade was delayed."
6. Rewrite this paragraph as three sentences using at least one
   correlative pair and one combined sentence. Keep every idea:
   "The market opens at nine. Bakers sell bread. Farmers sell
   cheese. Children play games. At noon, a band plays. Everyone
   dances."
7. Error analysis: what is wrong with "Both Maya and Jon likes
   lemonade"? Fix it and name every rule involved.
8. Write one sentence using *neither/nor* about festival food, and
   one using *not only/but also* about a parade. Check each pair
   and each parallel half before you finish.

## Practice Set C — Perfect tenses and tense shifts (Lesson 03)

1. Give the present perfect, past perfect, and future perfect of
   *to paint* for "she," and of *to write* for "they."
2. Name the tense of each underlined verb phrase:
   a. "She **has finished** the poster."
   b. "She **had finished** the poster before lunch."
   c. "She **will have finished** the poster before the show."
   d. "She **finished** the poster."
   e. "She **will finish** the poster."
3. Choose the correct participle and explain why:
   a. "They have (went / gone) to the festival twice."
   b. "He has (wrote / written) three songs."
   c. "We had (ate / eaten) before the gates opened."
   d. "She has (broke / broken) her own record."
4. Which event happened first in each sentence? Name the tense that
   proves it:
   a. "By the time we arrived, the band had played its first song."
   b. "We have visited the market, and we will return tomorrow."
5. Decide: error or not? Use the clock test in one sentence each:
   a. "He practises piano daily and won a prize last month."
   b. "She paints the fence yesterday and watches TV now."
   c. "They are rehearsing today and will perform Friday."
6. Fix the wrong shifts (leave correct shifts alone):
   a. "The crew decorated the hall. They hang lights, and they will
      open the doors at six."
   b. "Maya reads the notice and runs to tell Jon yesterday."
7. Write three sentences about one festival day: one present
   perfect, one past perfect (earlier event first), one future
   perfect. Label each tense in the margin.
8. Error analysis: explain what is wrong with "By the time the show
   started, we have bought tickets" — and rewrite it correctly.
   Then explain why "We have bought tickets, and the show starts at
   seven" is correct.

## Practice Set D — Commas, titles, spelling (Lesson 04)

1. Add series commas (use the serial comma) to each sentence:
   a. "We need red yellow and blue paint."
   b. "The stalls sold bread soup salad and pie."
2. Add commas for introductory elements:
   a. "After the storm the crew repaired the tents."
   b. "Finally the lanterns were lit."
3. Add commas for direct address, and say whether each name is
   spoken to or about:
   a. "Jon please hold this rope."
   b. "Jon held this rope all morning."
   c. "I believe Jon you are right."
4. Add commas for yes/no and tag questions:
   a. "Yes I will help."
   b. "The show is sold out isn't it"
   c. "No thank you I have already eaten."
5. Format these titles correctly (italics in print, underline in
   handwriting — use italics here) or in quotation marks, and say
   which kind of work each is:
   a. novel: the lighthouse cat
   b. poem: rain on tin roofs
   c. song: morning train
   d. article: how bridges stand
   e. movie: river of lights
6. Fix all comma, capitalization, and title errors:
   "in the morning maya please feed the dog cat and fish.
   we read the novel the lighthouse cat and sang the song morning
   train. Yes we loved them didn't we"
7. Circle the words you would check in a dictionary, then check
   them (the adult confirms with a reference): "The separate
   committee will accommodate forty visitors on Wednesday."
8. Explain, in your own words, why "Let's eat, Grandma!" and "Let's
   eat Grandma!" mean different things, and name the comma rule.

## Practice Set E — Register and reference checks (Lesson 05)

1. Rewrite each informal message formally for a school noticeboard.
   Keep every fact:
   a. "Hey guys! Wanna help paint banners Friday? It's gonna be fun!"
   b. "Plz bring snacks 2morrow — thx!"
2. Rewrite each formal message informally for a text to a friend.
   Keep every fact:
   a. "You are invited to the art show on Thursday at six o'clock."
   b. "Please bring your completed poster to the hall by Friday."
3. A story character says, "I ain't got no umbrella." The narrator
   writes, "He had no umbrella." Is the character's line an error?
   Explain with the words *dialect* and *register*.
4. Do a spelling pass: list every word in this sentence you would
   check in a dictionary, then check them: "The principal announced
   the necessary changes to the schedule." (All four words are
   spelled correctly here; the point is the checking habit.)
5. Name the editing pass that fixes each problem, and fix it:
   a. "apples bananas and grapes" (comma pass)
   b. "we goed to the fair" (verb pass)
   c. "Me and Sam runs fast" (sentence pass)
   d. "definately" (spelling pass)
6. A classmate "corrected" a character's line "Y'all come back now!"
   to "You all return at this time." What was lost? Was the original
   an error? Explain in two or three sentences.
7. Write a two-sentence note to a teacher asking to borrow a book.
   Then write the same request as a text to a friend. Underline one
   word or phrase that changed between versions and say why.

## Practice Set F — Cumulative editing (Lesson 05 applied task)

Edit this six-sentence paragraph in passes: sentences first, then
verbs, then commas/titles, then spelling. Use a different color for
each pass if you can. Mark every fix and name its rule in the
margin. Then write one sticky-note question for the author about
the sentence whose meaning is unclear.

> 1. me and lena walk to the river festival on saturday
> 2. We buyed our tickets at the gate and we has already seen the
>    boats
> 3. We packed apples cheese crackers and juice for the picnic
> 4. After lunch we watched the band it played our favorite song
>    morning train
> 5. The mayor welcomed everyone and the crowd cheered didn't they
> 6. By dusk, we will have saw them all and we will go home happy

Success check: your clean copy has no run-ons, no wrong verb forms,
commas in every series, a comma after the introductory element, a
comma before the tag question, the song title in quotation marks,
and every word spelled correctly. Bring the marked draft and your
sticky-note question to the next session.
