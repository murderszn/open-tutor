# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Informational Text Synthesis

For the guiding adult. This guide never goes to the learner with the
lessons; keep it separate from student pages. All question solutions
are in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md); this guide explains how to
teach, what to watch for, and what to do next when the learner
struggles.

## How to use this unit

- **Session length:** about 40 minutes, four sessions per week, as
  paced in the unit README. If a lesson runs long, split it at the
  guided-practice break and finish independent practice the next
  session — do not skip the exit check.
- **Reading routine:** read each new passage aloud first while the
  learner follows, then have the learner reread it for the lesson's
  question. Rereading with a new question is a taught skill here,
  not a sign of failure.
- **Feedback:** review practice the same day. A wrong answer that is
  corrected the same day, in the learner's own words, counts as
  learning; a wrong answer left uncorrected until the quiz does not.
- **What "proficient" means:** the success criteria in the unit
  README's pacing table. The formative quiz is formative: a score
  below 8/10 triggers reteaching (below), not a grade penalty.

## Per-lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — Main ideas, key details, summaries

- **Likely misconception:** the topic ("beavers") or a single vivid
  detail ("beavers have strong teeth") offered as a main idea.
  **Reteach move:** ask, "Can you prove that sentence with details
  from more than one paragraph?" If not, it is a detail or a topic
  label. Build the two-row table together before any summary is
  attempted.
- **Likely misconception:** summaries that copy the first paragraph
  and stop, or that add opinions ("beavers are wonderful").
  **Reteach move:** require both main ideas on the page before the
  summary is written, then check the summary sentence by sentence:
  "Is this in the passage? Is it a main idea or a small detail?"
- **Readiness signal:** the learner can state both main ideas and
  point to two proving details without rereading the whole passage.
- **Next step if not ready:** repeat the table-building with
  paragraph 3 alone (one main idea, two details) before returning
  to the whole passage.

### Lesson 02 — Chronology and cause/effect

- **Likely misconception:** any time word means chronology; any
  *because* means cause/effect — without checking the main job.
  **Reteach move:** always ask, "What is the author mainly doing
  here — listing steps in time, or explaining why something
  happened?" Then require the signal words as evidence, not as the
  whole answer.
- **Likely misconception:** reversed cause and effect (heron →
  pond). **Reteach move:** have the learner point to the sentence
  order in paragraph 3 and read the signal word aloud. The pond
  existed before the heron arrived; the heron cannot cause the pond.
- **Readiness signal:** the learner can build a 3-link chain and
  name one link that is both an effect and a cause.
- **Next step if not ready:** build chains with arrow cards on a
  table (cause card → effect card) before writing them.

### Lesson 03 — Comparison and problem/solution

- **Likely misconception:** a "comparison" that describes only one
  side, or a "solution" that is a wish ("the field should not
  flood"). **Reteach move:** require both columns of the chart for
  every row, and require every solution to be an action someone
  does, with a trade-off named.
- **Likely misconception:** carrying the fictional bird counts
  (12 vs. 4) into later work as real data. **Reteach move:** every
  time the counts appear, ask, "Real survey or practice example?"
  The project rubric requires the label if they are used at all.
- **Readiness signal:** the learner can complete a comparison chart
  from a new paragraph and name a problem, a solution, and its
  trade-off without prompting.
- **Next step if not ready:** sort sentence cards into "alike" and
  "different" piles before writing the chart.

### Lesson 04 — Two accounts; reasons matched to evidence

- **Likely misconception:** "one account is true and the other is
  lying." **Reteach move:** use the both-can-be-true test. The field
  flooded *and* the marsh came alive; the accounts differ because
  Tomas and Ana want different things from the same water.
- **Likely misconception:** treating an opinion as evidence ("Ana
  says the pond should stay, so ponds are good"). **Reteach move:**
  require a reason and an observation behind every opinion used as
  support. A recommendation alone proves nothing.
- **Readiness signal:** the learner can match 4 of 5 evidence items
  to their points and can attribute an unattributed sentence to
  Tomas or Ana with a reason.
- **Next step if not ready:** re-read the two accounts in two
  voices, underlining each recommendation sentence, before any
  matching is attempted.

### Lesson 05 — Synthesizing

- **Likely misconception:** paraphrase = changing one word.
  **Reteach move:** cover the original sentence and have the
  learner say the idea aloud in a new sentence, then write that
  sentence down. If the written sentence matches the original's
  shape, it is rewritten again.
- **Likely misconception:** synthesis = summarizing the longest
  passage plus one detail from another. **Reteach move:** require
  the source-note table first, with at least two sources in every
  synthesis paragraph, and check that each paragraph's ideas cannot
  all be traced to one source.
- **Readiness signal:** the learner can paraphrase a sentence
  (words and shape changed, meaning kept) and name its source
  without prompting.
- **Next step if not ready:** build the source-note table for one
  question together, row by row, before any drafting.

## Project scoring notes

Score with the rubric in [project.md](project.md). Common scoring
traps:

- **Do not reward length.** A long brief that copies a passage or
  hides the counter-point scores lower than a short brief that meets
  every rubric row.
- **Opposite answers earn equal points.** A brief arguing to remove
  the dam earns full points if its evidence, counter-point,
  paraphrase, and source list meet the rubric.
- **Quotations:** check exactness against the passage. A quotation
  with a changed word and no brackets is misquoted — deduct under
  "Paraphrase and quotation" and have the learner fix it in revision.
- **Oral summary:** the listener's retelling is the evidence. If
  the listener cannot retell the counter-point, the summary is
  incomplete even if the brief is strong.
- Keep scored rubrics in private storage, never in this repository.

## Assessment notes

- **Week-04 quiz:** formative. Below 8/10: reteach the missed skill
  with the lesson's reteach move, then re-ask one similar question
  the next day. Do not re-administer the same quiz as a grade.
- **Unit assessment:** Part A measures transfer to a new passage;
  Part B measures synthesis. A learner who scores well on Part A but
  weakly on Part B needs Lesson 05 reteaching, not more passage
  reading. A learner who scores weakly on Part A needs Lessons
  01–04 reteaching, not project revision.
- **Acceptable variation:** main-idea wording, summary wording,
  paraphrase wording, and synthesis judgments may vary. What must
  not vary: the facts cited, the structure named (with signal-word
  evidence), the evidence-to-point matches, and the fairness of the
  counter-point. The answer key marks where variation is acceptable.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** read passages aloud; allow dictated summaries,
  paraphrases, and briefs (the learner reads them back and corrects
  them); allow arrow-card and sentence-sort versions of charts and
  chains; allow oral exit checks.
- **Extension:** the extension tasks in each lesson; a third
  account in Lesson 04; a second synthesis question in Lesson 05;
  a brief that uses a Resource Pack source (adult-chosen, added to
  the source list with its check date).
- **Language access:** vocabulary is taught with examples first and
  definitions second in every lesson. Signal-word lists stay posted
  (or on a card) for the whole unit — they are tools, not test
  secrets, and the learner may use them during practice. During the
  assessments, signal-word lists are *not* provided; the learner
  must recognize the words in the text.

## Safety and content notes

- No investigation in this unit involves animals, water, tools, or
  fieldwork. All "observations" are readings of provided texts and
  one illustration. If the learner wants to visit a real pond, that
  is a family activity with adult supervision, not a unit
  requirement, and nothing from it is submitted as unit evidence.
- All Willow Creek people, places, and counts are fictional or
  practice material, as labeled in the lessons. Do not present them
  as real, and do not let the learner cite them outside the unit as
  facts about beavers.
- The beaver-pond illustration is an AI illustration, labeled as
  such wherever it appears. It is not a photograph and not evidence
  about a real pond.
