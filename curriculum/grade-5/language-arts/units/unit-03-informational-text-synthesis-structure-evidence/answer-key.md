# Answer Key — Unit 03: Informational Text Synthesis

For the guiding adult only. Do not give this file to the learner
before practice, quizzes, or assessments are complete. Wording may
vary where noted; facts, structure names (with signal-word evidence),
evidence-to-point matches, and counter-point fairness must not vary.

## Lesson 01 — Guided practice

1. Paragraph 3 main idea (acceptable): "The pond helps animals
   that need still water." (Also acceptable: "The dam's pond
   created a home for wildlife.")
2. Best dry-times detail, quoted exactly: "In the dry month of
   July, when a nearby stream shrank to a trickle, Willow Creek's
   pond still held water." (A shorter exact quotation — "Willow
   Creek's pond still held water" — is acceptable if the July
   context is in the learner's answer.)
3. Flaw: the summary has no main ideas at all — it states the topic
   ("beavers build dams") and stops. Missing: main idea 2 (the pond
   helps wildlife but floods a field) and the build stages of main
   idea 1. Fix by adding both main ideas with one detail each.
4. Picture/text match: dam, lodge, heron, pond, cattails, trees —
   any one named from both. Picture-only: the lodge is shown in
   detail but the passage never describes a lodge; the heron's
   reflection, the cattails, and the trickling water over the dam
   are also picture-only details (accept any one, verified against
   the text alternative).

## Lesson 01 — Independent practice

1. Topic: "beavers" / "Willow Creek beavers" / "beaver pond"
   (3 words or fewer).
2. Main idea 1: Beavers turn a fast stream into a pond by building
   a dam in stages. Main idea 2: The pond helps wildlife but also
   floods a farmer's low field. (Wording may vary; both ideas must
   be present.)
3. Key details for each main idea (two per main idea, at least one
   exact quotation per main idea): Main idea 1 — "First, the
   beavers gnawed down small trees"; "they dragged branches into
   the stream and piled them across it"; "they packed mud and
   stones into the gaps"; "the dam — held back the water, and a
   pond began to form." Main idea 2 — "Frogs laid eggs among the
   pond plants"; "A heron began to hunt along the bank"; "A low
   field beside the creek filled with water in spring"; the July
   still-water detail above.
4. Summary: must keep both main ideas, use mostly the learner's own
   words, and contain no opinion. Model in Lesson 01 ("Modeled
   example 3") is the reference.
5. Inference detail: "A low field beside the creek filled with
   water in spring, and the farmer … worried about his crop." The
   problem named is the water in the field, not beavers as animals.
   (Accept: "filled with water in spring" + the crop worry.)
6. Vocabulary: *ecosystem engineer* = a living thing whose building
   changes the land and water around it, creating homes or
   conditions for other living things. Dictionary check should
   confirm the "changes its environment by building" sense.

## Lesson 02 — Guided practice

1. Steps in order: (1) First — gnawed down small trees; (2) Then —
   dragged branches into the stream and piled them across it;
   (3) Next — packed mud and stones into the gaps; (4) Finally —
   the dam held back the water and a pond began to form.
2. Chain (at least 3 links): dam slowed the water → pond grew deep
   and calm → animals that need still water moved in → (in dry
   July) the pond still held water. Signal words: "Because",
   "As a result". The "deep and calm pond" link is both an effect
   (of the slowed water) and a cause (of animals moving in).
3. Chronology. Signal words: "First", "Then", "Finally" (any two).
   It lists steps in time; it does not explain why.
4. Error: the heron did not cause the pond. Paragraph 3 says the
   pond formed first ("Because the dam slowed the water, the pond
   grew deep and calm. As a result, animals … moved in"), and the
   heron is one of the animals that moved in afterward. Cause and
   effect are reversed in the classmate's sentence.

## Lesson 02 — Independent practice

1. Morning chronology: any 3 steps in an order that matters, with
   *first, then, finally* circled. Order must matter (for example,
   socks before shoes).
2. Chain: any 3-link pond chain with *because* and *as a result*,
   not copied word for word from paragraph 3.
3. Structure: cause/effect. Signal word: "As a result".
4. Structure: chronology. It lists Ana's actions in time order
   (walked → counted → wrote); it does not explain why counting
   followed walking as a cause. Accept: "sequence" for chronology.
5. Relationship: the heron depends on the pond the dam created —
   for example, "The dam created a calm pond, and the heron hunts
   there because the pond holds the animals it eats" (accept any
   sentence stating the pond came first and the heron uses it).
6. *Consequently* = as a result; therefore. Example sentence must
   use it to link a dam cause to a pond effect. Dictionary check:
   "as a result; therefore."

## Lesson 03 — Guided practice

1. Similarity not in the chart: both streams have minnows
   ("Similarly, both streams had minnows"). Difference not in the
   chart: Willow Creek has a heron that returns daily; Dry Fork
   does not ("only Willow Creek had a heron that returned every
   morning"). (Other acceptable pairs must be quoted from the
   passage.)
2. The counts are invented for teaching comparison. They must not
   be quoted as a real survey, because no real count was made; they
   may be used to practice reading a comparison and building a
   chart, labeled "practice example."
3. "Dry Fork's banks were quiet" is presented in the passage as an
   observation (what observers heard), not as a judgment that quiet
   is good or bad. Accept: fact-as-observation, with the reason that
   the passage reports it as something noticed, not evaluated.
4. Problem: the pond flooded the low field in spring. Solution: a
   pond leveler pipe that lets some water out slowly while keeping
   the pond deep enough for beavers. Trade-off: the field flooded
   less, but the pond sat lower and the farmer still watched the
   spring rain.

## Lesson 03 — Independent practice

1. Pond vs. puddle chart: each row must have both sides, for
   example — depth: pond deep, puddle shallow; duration: pond
   lasts, puddle dries fast; wildlife: pond supports frogs/herons,
   puddle supports little or none. Accept any accurate rows with
   both sides.
2. Problem: the town's well ran low every August. Solution:
   collected rainwater in tanks for the garden. Trade-off: the
   garden still needed town water in August, but much less.
3. Signal words: "Unlike", "Similarly", "However" (any two
   underlined; all three is full credit). Structure: comparison.
4. "The Pond Builders" paragraph 3: cause/effect ("Because",
   "As a result"). "Two Streams" paragraph 2: comparison
   ("In contrast", "while"). Both sentences required, each with a
   signal word.
5. *In contrast* = showing a difference; *similarly* = showing a
   likeness. The two sentences must use each word correctly about
   the two streams.
6. Relationship: water held in July is the cause; wildlife presence
   is the effect — for example, "Because Willow Creek's pond still
   held water in July, frogs and a heron could live there when Dry
   Fork could not support them."

## Lesson 04 — Guided practice

1. Agreement quotation pair (any matching pair): Tomas — "the pond
   rose and covered my low field for three days"; Ana — "the pond
   rose and spread into the marsh beside the creek." Agreement:
   the pond rose in spring and water spread beyond the creek.
2. Tomas wants: "The dam should be lowered so the field drains
   faster." Ana wants: "The dam should stay." (Quotations required.)
3. "Beaver ponds are wonderful" is an opinion (a judgment). It
   becomes a supported point only with a reason and evidence, for
   example: "because they hold water in dry summers (reason), as
   shown by Willow Creek's pond in July (evidence)."
4. Matches: (a) Point 1 (dry-summer water); (b) Point 2 (wildlife);
   (c) neither (construction detail, supports neither point).

## Lesson 04 — Independent practice

1. Agreement: the pond rose / water spread in spring (quotations
   as in Guided 1). Disagreement: lower the dam vs. keep the dam
   (quotations as in Guided 2).
2. Both accounts can be true at the same time: the field flooded
   and the marsh came alive. The disagreement is about what should
   happen next, which depends on what each person values — the
   field or the marsh wildlife.
3. Point 1 evidence: "the class measured bean plants every week
   and watched them climb" (shows students seeing plants grow).
   Irrelevant: "the garden is near the parking lot" (location does
   not show learning from growth).
4. "The marsh is alive again" is an opinion backed by observations:
   frogs singing every night and a heron hunting each morning.
   Accept: "opinion supported by evidence."
5. (a) Tomas — the young plants / hay crop are his concern;
   "stayed too long for the young plants" matches his field account.
   (b) Ana — "alive again" is her marsh judgment. (c) Tomas — "I
   lost part of my hay crop" is his loss.
6. Inference: Tomas says he is "not against the beavers" and then
   asks only that "the dam should be lowered" — he objects to the
   dam's height and its effect on drainage, not to beavers
   existing. Accept any reasoning that separates the height/drainage
   objection from a general objection to beavers.

## Lesson 05 — Guided practice

1. Paraphrase (acceptable): "A heron started fishing at the water's
   edge." / "Along the bank, a heron looked for food." Must change
   words and shape and keep the meaning (a heron hunting by the
   bank).
2. Repair: only the verb changed ("laid" → "placed"); the rest is
   copied. A true paraphrase changes words and shape, for example:
   "Frogs used the pond plants as a place to lay their eggs."
3. Illustration row: Source — beaver-pond illustration, Lesson 01.
   In my own words: it shows a beaver, a stick dam, a lodge, and a
   heron at a calm pond. Question it helps answer: "What does a
   beaver pond look like?" It cannot answer dated or causal
   questions (what happened in spring, why the pond formed).
4. Tomas's points (from listening): the spring flood covered his
   field for three days and hurt his hay crop; the dam should be
   lowered so the field drains faster. Evidence: the three days of
   standing water and the lost hay. (Wording may vary; both points
   and one evidence item required.)

## Lesson 05 — Independent practice

1. Paraphrase (acceptable): "While a nearby stream dried to almost
   nothing in July, Willow Creek's pond kept its water." Must keep
   the pond-vs.-stream comparison.
2. Paraphrase (acceptable): "Tomas suggests lowering the dam to let
   the field drain more quickly." Must remain a recommendation, not
   a fact ("the dam was lowered" is wrong).
3. Source-note row: Source — "Two Streams," Lesson 03. In my own
   words: Willow Creek's pond kept water in July while Dry Fork
   shrank. Exact phrase (any one): "while Dry Fork shrank to a
   trickle" / "In contrast, Dry Fork stays shallow and fast."
   Question: "How did the two streams differ in July?"
4. Synthesis: must name at least two sources, include one short
   quotation with its source named, and state both the wildlife
   benefit and the farmer's cost (or another fair counter-point).
   A synthesis that names only one side is incomplete.
5. Error analysis: (1) it presents an opinion ("good") as a
   consensus fact — Tomas does not agree; (2) it dismisses the
   counter-point instead of weighing it. Fair rewrite (acceptable):
   "People disagree about the dam because it helps pond wildlife
   but floods Tomas's field."
6. Ana's points (from listening): the spring flood spread into the
   marsh; by summer frogs and a heron returned; the dam should stay
   because the pond revived the marsh. Evidence for each: the
   spread into the marsh; frogs singing / heron hunting; the pond
   holding water in dry July. (Any two points with one evidence
   item each is full credit.)

## Week-04 quiz (10 points)

1. Topic: "beaver dam repair" / "dam repair" / "Willow Creek dam"
   (3 words or fewer; accept "autumn dam repair").
2. Main idea (acceptable): "Beavers repaired and strengthened the
   dam in autumn so the pond stayed deep through winter." (Also
   acceptable: "The autumn repairs kept the pond deep in winter.")
3. Chronology signal word: "First" / "Then" (also "Next" is not in
   this paragraph — do not accept it here). Cause/effect signal
   word: "Because" / "As a result". Last two sentences: mainly
   cause/effect.
4. Cause: the wall grew stronger (the dam was repaired). Effect:
   the pond stayed deep through the winter (and the beavers could
   swim under the ice to their food store — accept as a second
   effect).
5. Similarity: both flow through farm fields (quote: "both flow
   through farm fields"). Difference: Willow Creek's pond still
   held water in July, while Dry Fork shrank to a trickle (quotes
   required for both sides).
6. Comparison signal words: "alike", "However", "while",
   "Similarly" (any three; "but" also acceptable as a fourth).
7. They agree about the event: both describe water spreading in
   spring (Tomas's field, Ana's marsh). One-sentence explanation
   must state the shared event, not the disagreement.
8. (a) Fact. (b) Opinion.
9. Paraphrase (acceptable): "Frogs used the pond plants as a place
   to lay their eggs." / "Among the pond plants, frogs left their
   eggs." Must change words and shape.
10. No. Gnawing teeth explain how beavers build, not how ponds help
    towns in dry summers; the evidence does not match the point.
    (Accept: "No — it is about construction, not stored water.")

## Unit assessment (24 points)

**Part A**

1. (2) Topic: "garden ditches" / "school garden water" (3 words or
   fewer). Main ideas (both required): (a) the club dug stone-lined
   ditches in stages to catch rain and keep the beds damp; (b) the
   ditches helped the beds (soil kept, plants taller) but cost a
   lot of time, so members disagreed about doing more. Wording may
   vary; 1 point per main idea (topic is part of the 2 points —
   award 1 point for topic + one main idea, 2 for topic + both).
2. (2) One key detail per main idea, at least one quoted exactly.
   Examples: "the ditches caught it, and the beds below stayed
   damp for days"; "less soil washed away"; "the plants grew taller
   than the year before"; "they took every free afternoon in
   September."
3. (2) Paragraph 2: chronology. Signal words (any two): "First",
   "Then", "Next", "Finally".
4. (2) Expected: mainly cause/effect — "Because" and "As a
   result" carry the paragraph's explanation of what slowing the
   rain did; the "In contrast" sentence is a supporting
   comparison with the old garden, not the main structure. Accept
   a comparison-first answer only if it explains that the contrast
   organizes the paragraph and names the cause/effect sentences as
   supporting — otherwise award 1 point for naming both structures
   without a main-job argument.
5. (2) Chain (at least 3 links): ditches slowed the rain → less
   soil washed away → beds kept their soil → plants grew taller.
   (Also acceptable: ditches caught rain → beds stayed damp for
   days, as a parallel branch — award full points if the chain is
   accurate and has 3 links.)
6. (2) Agreement: the beds stayed damp longer than before
   (quote: "the beds stayed damp longer than before" / "the beds
   below stayed damp for days" — accept either with the agreement
   stated). Disagreement: Maya — "We should dig more of them
   before spring"; Jon — "We should ask the town for help next
   time" (or: the work took every free afternoon). Point of view,
   not facts: both agree on the result; they differ on what the
   cost means for what to do next.
7. (2) Summary: 3 sentences, both main ideas kept, small details
   left out. Model: "The garden club dug stone-lined ditches across
   the slope in stages to catch autumn rain. The ditches kept the
   beds damp, saved soil, and grew taller plants than the year
   before. But the work took every free afternoon in September, so
   Maya wanted more ditches while Jon wanted town help next time."
   Award 1 point if one main idea is missing, 0 if the summary
   copies sentences or adds opinions.

**Part B**

8. (3) Paraphrase (acceptable): "When rain moved more slowly
   through the ditches, the garden lost less soil." / "Slowing the
   rain down kept more soil in the beds." Must change words and
   shape and keep the cause/effect meaning. Deduct 1 point if the
   cause/effect link is dropped; deduct 2 if it is copy-close.
9. (3) Matches: (i) → (a) damp longer; (ii) → (b) kept soil;
   (iii) → neither (it states the original August problem, before
   the ditches — it does not show a benefit). 1 point per correct
   match; the explanation sentence is part of the third point —
   award it if at least one match is explained correctly.
10. (4) Rubric: 1 point for answering the question with a judgment;
    1 point for using at least two parts of the passage; 1 point
    for one short quotation with its paragraph named; 1 point for
    stating Jon's counter-point fairly (the time cost) and weighing
    it. A judgment agreeing with Maya, Jon, or neither earns full
    points if supported; an unsupported judgment ("it was worth it
    because gardens are nice") loses the judgment point.
