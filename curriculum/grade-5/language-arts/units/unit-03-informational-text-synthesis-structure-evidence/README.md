# Unit 03 — Informational Text Synthesis: Structure and Evidence

**Grade:** 5 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #28](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/28), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Informational reading is detective work. A text does not just hand you
its meaning — it organizes information in a pattern, supports points
with reasons and evidence, and sometimes tells only one side of a story.
In this unit the learner follows one place, Willow Creek, through three
original passages and one illustration: beavers build a dam, a pond
forms, two streams turn out very differently, and two neighbours disagree
about what the pond means. The learner names main ideas, maps text
structures, compares accounts, matches each reason to the evidence that
supports it, and finally synthesizes several sources into one short
evidence brief with a source list. No single passage is enough on its
own — that is the point.

## Essential question

How do I put information from several sources together — fairly, in my
own words, and with evidence — to answer a question?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2), Unit 01, Unit 02, and the
grade-4 language arts track:

- Read grade-level prose aloud with accuracy, rate, and expression;
  self-correct with context (U01; RF.5.4)
- Decode multisyllabic words with morphology; use context clues, then
  verify with a dictionary (U01; RF.5.3, L.5.4)
- Quote accurately from a text when explaining explicit meaning and
  drawing inferences; determine a theme and summarize (U02; RL.5.1,
  RL.5.2) — the same quoting and summarizing habits are reused here for
  informational text
- State a claim, support it with quoted evidence, and explain how the
  evidence proves the claim (U02 theme-analysis paragraph; W.5.9.a)
- Determine a main idea and explain how key details support it;
  describe an overall text structure; explain how an author uses reasons
  and evidence; integrate information from two texts (grade 4;
  RI.4.2, RI.4.5, RI.4.8, RI.4.9) — all four are deepened here to two or
  more main ideas, structure comparison across texts, reason-to-evidence
  matching, and synthesis across several sources
- Choose precise words, including words that signal contrast, addition,
  and other logical relationships (U01; L.5.6)

Lesson 01 re-teaches the topic-versus-main-idea distinction rather than
assuming it is secure, and Lesson 02 re-teaches signal words from
scratch. Nothing in this unit assumes the learner has read the Unit 02
stories; every passage this unit needs is printed inside it.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. **Quote accurately** from an informational text when explaining what
   the text says explicitly and when drawing inferences (RI.5.1).
2. Determine **two or more main ideas** of a text, explain how **key
   details** support them, and **summarize** the text (RI.5.2).
3. Explain **relationships or interactions** between individuals,
   events, ideas, or concepts in a historical, scientific, or technical
   text, based on specific information in the text (RI.5.3).
4. Determine the meaning of general academic and **domain-specific
   words** in context, and **compare and contrast the overall
   structure** (chronology, comparison, cause/effect, problem/solution)
   of events, ideas, concepts, or information in **two or more
   texts** (RI.5.4, RI.5.5).
5. Analyze **multiple accounts** of the same event or topic, noting
   important similarities and differences in the **point of view**
   they represent, and explain how an author uses **reasons and
   evidence** to support particular points, identifying **which
   reasons and evidence support which point(s)** (RI.5.6, RI.5.8).
6. Draw on information from **multiple print or digital sources**,
   demonstrating the ability to locate an answer or solve a problem
   efficiently; **integrate** information from several texts to write
   or speak knowledgeably; **summarize or paraphrase** information in
   notes and finished work and **provide a list of sources**; and
   **summarize a speaker's points** and how each claim is supported
   by reasons and evidence (RI.5.7, RI.5.9, W.5.8, SL.5.3).

## Anchor texts (all original, authored for this unit)

- **"The Pond Builders"** — an original informational passage (~360
  words) printed in Lesson 01. Beavers arrive at Willow Creek, build a
  dam in stages, and turn a fast stream into a pond. Used in Lessons
  01, 02, and 05 and in the project.
- **"Two Streams"** — an original comparison passage (~300 words)
  printed in Lesson 03. Willow Creek (with beavers) and Dry Fork
  (without beavers) are compared on depth, speed, and wildlife. All
  counts in it are a clearly labeled fictional practice dataset, not
  real field observations.
- **"Two Reports on Willow Creek"** — two original short accounts of
  the same spring flood (~180 words each) printed in Lesson 04: Tomas,
  a farmer whose low field flooded, and Ana, a pond watcher. Used in
  Lesson 04, Lesson 05, and the project.
- **"The Garden That Drinks Rain"** — an original assessment passage
  (~330 words) printed only in the culminating assessment. It is new
  to the learner at assessment time.
- **Beaver-pond illustration** — a generated illustration in
  [assets/beaver-pond-illustration.webp](assets/beaver-pond-illustration.webp),
  used in Lesson 01. It is an AI illustration, not a photograph and
  not evidence of a real pond.

No copyrighted texts are reproduced. General background about beavers
as ecosystem engineers is consistent with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife
Service and National Park Service pages listed in the Resource Pack;
the Willow Creek passages themselves are invented for teaching and
must not be cited as real places, people, or observations.

## 16-session pacing

Four sessions per week, ~40 minutes each. Each session names its
activity and success criterion; repeated labels are never reused
without a specific new task.

| Session | Week | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 11 | Lesson 01: Main ideas, key details, summaries — "The Pond Builders" + pond illustration | Two main ideas stated, each with two quoted or closely paraphrased details; one 3-sentence summary |
| 2 | 11 | Practice A: Main-idea/detail sort, topic-vs.-main-idea, summary check | Sorts 10 statements into topic / main idea / detail / opinion with ≤2 errors; summary keeps both main ideas |
| 3 | 11 | Lesson 02: Structures — chronology and cause/effect | Sequence map of the dam build in correct order; cause/effect chain with 3 links, each backed by a detail |
| 4 | 11 | Practice B: Structure sort — chronology vs. cause/effect paragraphs | Names the structure of 6 of 8 paragraphs and underlines the signal words that prove it |
| 5 | 12 | Lesson 03: Structures — comparison and problem/solution — "Two Streams" | Comparison chart with 2 similarities and 3 differences; problem/solution plan naming the problem, one solution, and its trade-off |
| 6 | 12 | Practice C: Comparison chart from a new paragraph + problem/solution rewrite | Chart complete from the paragraph alone; rewrite keeps the problem and adds a solution that fits it |
| 7 | 12 | Lesson 04: Two accounts, one event; matching reasons to points | Account chart: 2 agreements + 2 point-of-view differences; reason-to-evidence match 4 of 5 correct |
| 8 | 12 | Practice D: Fact vs. opinion; whose account is this? | Labels 8 of 10 statements fact or opinion with a reason; attributes 4 unattributed sentences to Tomas or Ana with evidence |
| 9 | 13 | Lesson 05: Synthesizing — paraphrase, quote, source notes | 3 paraphrases that keep the meaning and change the wording; source-note table complete for 3 sources |
| 10 | 13 | Practice E: Paraphrase repair + source-note building for the brief | Repairs 3 copy-close paraphrases; source notes cover all 3 project sources with a question each answers |
| 11 | 13 | Project workshop 1: Evidence table + brief plan (see [project.md](project.md)) | Evidence table with at least 4 rows from at least 3 sources, including one counter-point row |
| 12 | 13 | Project workshop 2: Draft the brief + rehearse the 1-minute oral summary | Full brief draft (2 paragraphs + conclusion + source list); oral summary rehearsed once with a partner |
| 13 | 14 | Re-reading and retrieval: all three passages + structure relay game | Structure relay: names the structure of 8 of 10 paragraph cards; retrieval questions answered from memory, then checked against the text |
| 14 | 14 | Review: evidence-matching game + brief revision clinic | Every brief revised for one paraphrase, one quotation, and one source-list entry; game score ≥8/10 |
| 15 | 14 | Formative quiz ([week-04_quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md)) + corrections discussion | Quiz ≥8/10; every miss re-explained in the learner's own words |
| 16 | 14 | Culminating unit assessment ([unit-assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md)) | Rubric-scored; review the answer key together afterward |

Weeks 1–2 of the track are diagnostic/placement and are not part of
this unit.

## How lessons, practice, reading, investigation, and review fill the unit

Five explicit lessons (sessions 1, 3, 5, 7, 9) introduce each tool;
each is paired with a practice session that applies it to new material
(sessions 2, 4, 6, 8, 10). Connected reading runs through the whole
unit: the same Willow Creek passages are reread for new purposes —
first for main ideas, then for structure, then for point of view, then
for synthesis — so the learner sees that rereading with a new question
finds new information. The investigation is the Evidence Brief project
(sessions 11–12, revised in session 14): it is the unit's synthesis
task, not an add-on. Retrieval and review (sessions 13–14) precede the
formative quiz (session 15); the culminating assessment (session 16)
uses a new passage so it measures transfer, not memory of the project
passages. Feedback is same-day on practice, and the teacher guide gives
reteach moves for each lesson.
