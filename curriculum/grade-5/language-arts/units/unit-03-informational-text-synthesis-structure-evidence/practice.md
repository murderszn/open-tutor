# Practice Sets — Unit 03: Informational Text Synthesis

Work with the adult the same day you finish each set. Use the
passages in Lessons 01, 03, and 04; do not look at the answer key
while you work. A task is complete when the adult can see the evidence
— a quotation, a signal word, or a source name — not just an answer.

## A. Main ideas, key details, summaries (after Lesson 01)

Use "The Pond Builders" (Lesson 01).

1. Write the topic of the passage in three words or fewer.
2. Write two main ideas of the whole passage, each as one sentence
   in your own words.
3. For main idea 1, list two key details. Quote at least one exactly.
4. For main idea 2, list two key details. Quote at least one exactly.
5. Write a 3-sentence summary. Then check it: does it keep both main
   ideas? Does it leave out small details? Circle any sentence that
   states your opinion rather than the author's point, and rewrite
   it as a fact from the passage.
6. Which detail best proves that the pond helped animals in dry
   times? Quote it exactly and name the main idea it proves.

## B. Chronology and cause/effect (after Lesson 02)

Use "The Pond Builders" and the paragraphs below (all original).

7. List the four dam-build steps in order, each with its signal word
   from paragraph 2.
8. Draw a cause/effect chain for paragraph 3 with at least three
   links. Label one link that is both an effect and a cause.
9. Read: "Before sunrise, Tomas checked the field. Next he saw the
   standing water. Then he called a neighbour." Name the structure
   and quote two signal words that prove it.
10. Read: "Because the pond leveler let water out slowly, the field
    drained faster. As a result, the farmer lost less hay."
    Name the structure, name the cause, and name the two effects.
11. Error analysis: a classmate wrote, "The heron built the pond."
    Explain in two sentences why that is wrong, using paragraph 3.

## C. Comparison and problem/solution (after Lesson 03)

Use "Two Streams" (Lesson 03).

12. Complete this comparison row with both sides and a quoted
    detail for each side: Feature: water in dry July.
13. Name one similarity between Willow Creek and Dry Fork that is
    not about water, with a quoted detail.
14. Read: "The town's well ran low every August. The town collected
    rainwater in tanks and used it for the garden. The garden still
    needed town water in August, but much less of it." Name the
    problem, the solution, and the trade-off.
15. Compare the overall structure of "The Pond Builders" and "Two
    Streams" in two sentences: which structure does each passage
    mainly use, and what signal words prove it?
16. The bird counts in "Two Streams" (12 kinds vs. 4 kinds) are a
    fictional practice dataset. In two sentences, explain what that
    means for a reader: may you quote the counts as real survey
    results? What may you use them for?

## D. Two accounts, facts, and opinions (after Lesson 04)

Use "Two Reports on Willow Creek" (Lesson 04).

17. Write one thing Tomas and Ana agree on and one thing they
    disagree on, each with a quotation.
18. Explain in two sentences why their disagreement is mainly about
    point of view rather than facts. Use the test: can both accounts
    be true at the same time?
19. Label each statement fact or opinion, and give a one-sentence
    reason: (a) "The pond rose in spring." (b) "The dam should
    stay." (c) "Frogs sang there every night by summer." (d) "A
    field that cannot drain cannot feed anyone."
20. Attribute each sentence to Tomas or Ana, with a reason: (a) "The
    water stayed too long for the young plants." (b) "The marsh is
    alive again." (c) "I lost part of my hay crop."
21. Match each piece of evidence to Point 1 ("Beaver ponds help
    towns in dry summers") or Point 2 ("Beaver ponds help
    wildlife") or "neither": (a) "Willow Creek's pond still held
    water in dry July." (b) "Frogs laid eggs among the pond
    plants." (c) "Beavers gnaw trees with strong teeth."

## E. Paraphrase, source notes, synthesis (after Lesson 05)

Use any unit passage. Name the source for every answer.

22. Paraphrase: "A heron began to hunt along the bank." Change the
    words and the sentence shape, and keep the meaning.
23. Repair this copy-close paraphrase of "The dam held back the
    water, and a pond began to form": "The dam held back the water,
    and a pond started to form." Explain what is still too close,
    then write a true paraphrase.
24. Build a source-note row for "Two Streams" that answers: "How did
    the two streams differ in July?" Include one exact phrase you
    might quote.
25. Write a 3-sentence synthesis answering: "Why do people disagree
    about the Willow Creek dam?" Use at least two sources and
    include one short quotation with its source named.
26. Error analysis: a classmate wrote, "Everyone agrees beaver dams
    are good." Explain two problems with that sentence as synthesis,
    using Tomas's account, and rewrite it fairly in one sentence.
