# Project — The Willow Creek Evidence Brief

**Task:** Answer this question in writing and in a short talk:
*Should Willow Creek keep its beaver dam?* Use evidence from at least
three sources, include the counter-point fairly, paraphrase most of
your evidence, quote short bits exactly when they prove a point, and
end with a source list.

**Time:** two project workshops (sessions 11–12) plus revision in
session 14. About 40 minutes per workshop.

## Deliverables (all four are required)

1. **Evidence table** — at least 4 rows from at least 3 sources,
   including at least 1 counter-point row (a cost or drawback of
   keeping the dam). Columns: Source | What it says, in my own
   words | Exact words I might quote | Question it helps answer.
2. **Evidence brief** — two paragraphs plus a concluding sentence:
   paragraph 1 states your answer and the strongest evidence for it;
   paragraph 2 states the counter-point fairly and explains why
   your answer still stands (or why it changes — changing your mind
   on the evidence is allowed and must be explained). At least one
   short quotation, with its source named in the same sentence or
   the one before it. No paragraph may copy a passage at length.
3. **Source list** — every source used, by title and lesson (for
   example: "'The Pond Builders,' Lesson 01"). The illustration
   counts as a source only if the brief actually uses something
   seen in it; name it as "Beaver-pond illustration, Lesson 01
   (AI illustration)."
4. **1-minute oral summary** — spoken from notes, not read from the
   brief: your answer, one piece of evidence, the counter-point,
   and your conclusion, in that order. A partner or the adult listens
   and retells your points (SL.5.3 check).

## Materials

Lessons 01, 03, and 04 passages; the Lesson 01 illustration (or its
text alternative); Lesson 05 source-note table; pencil or keyboard.
No outside sources are required. If the learner wants one, the adult
chooses it from the Resource Pack and it is added to the source list
with its title and the date checked.

## Steps

1. **Workshop 1 — evidence table.** Fill the table before drafting
   anything. Check: does every row name a source? Is there a
   counter-point row? Is the "in my own words" column really in your
   own words (changed words *and* shape)?
2. **Workshop 1 — plan.** Write one sentence for your answer, one
   sentence for your strongest evidence, and one sentence for the
   counter-point. Show the plan to the adult before drafting.
3. **Workshop 2 — draft.** Write the two paragraphs plus the
   concluding sentence. Keep quotations short (one phrase or one
   short sentence each). Add the source list.
4. **Workshop 2 — rehearse.** Give the 1-minute oral summary from
   notes. The listener retells your points; fix anything the
   listener could not retell.
5. **Session 14 — revise.** Fix one paraphrase, check one quotation
   for exactness, and check every source-list entry against the
   brief. Read the brief aloud once for sense.

## Rules that protect fairness

- The counter-point must be stated in words Tomas (or the
  counter-point source) would recognize — not as a weak, silly
  version of it.
- The fictional bird counts from "Two Streams" (12 kinds vs. 4
  kinds) may be used only if labeled "practice example" in the
  brief, or left out. They must never appear as real survey results.
- The illustration is an AI illustration, not a photograph and not
  proof about a real pond. It may support "what a beaver pond looks
  like," never "what happened at Willow Creek."
- A brief that copies a passage, hides the counter-point, or lists a
  source it never uses does not meet the rubric, even if it is long.

## Rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | Proficient (full points) | Developing (half points) | Beginning (0 points) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Answer and evidence (5) | Clear answer; at least 3 sources; evidence matched to the answer | Answer present but vague, or only 2 sources, or one piece of evidence mismatched | No clear answer, or evidence copied without matching |
| Counter-point (4) | Counter-point stated fairly, in the source's own terms, and answered or weighed | Counter-point mentioned but weakened or unanswered | Counter-point missing or mocked |
| Paraphrase and quotation (4) | Most evidence paraphrased (words + shape changed); quotations short, exact, and sourced | Some copy-close paraphrase, or a quotation without a source, or a quotation too long | Whole sentences copied, or quotations invented or misquoted |
| Organization (3) | Two paragraphs + conclusion in logical order; ideas linked (*however, similarly, as a result*) | Order confusing, or conclusion missing, or links missing | No paragraph structure |
| Source list and oral summary (4) | Source list complete and accurate; 1-minute summary covers answer, evidence, counter-point, conclusion | Source list incomplete, or summary misses one element | No source list, or no oral summary |

**Scoring:** 17–20 = Proficient; 12–16 = Developing (revise the
weakest row and resubmit); below 12 = rework with the adult, starting
from the evidence table. The rubric is scored by the adult, not
self-scored, and the scored rubric is kept in private storage, not in
this repository.

## Worked mini-example (not a model answer — a shape to follow)

> **Answer sentence:** Willow Creek should keep its dam, with a
> pond leveler to protect the low field.
>
> **Evidence sentence (paraphrased):** In dry July the pond still
> held water when a nearby stream had shrunk, which helped frogs and
> a heron.
>
> **Counter-point sentence (fair):** Tomas's field stayed under
> water for three spring days and lost part of its hay crop, so
> keeping the dam without any change would keep costing him.
>
> **Conclusion sentence:** With a leveler letting some water out
> slowly, the town can keep the pond's dry-summer help while
> flooding the field less.

A learner's brief may reach the opposite answer. It earns the same
points if the evidence, counter-point, paraphrase, and source list
meet the rubric.
