## lighthouse-storm-scene.png

- **File:** `lighthouse-storm-scene.png`
- **Prompt:** "A warm, friendly educational illustration for grade 5
  students: a white lighthouse with a red top on a rocky point at
  dusk, waves below, a young girl with curly dark hair and an older
  woman with grey hair in a shawl standing together at the
  lighthouse door holding a glowing lantern, storm clouds gathering
  but a warm light glowing in the lighthouse lamp room. No words,
  no letters, no labels, no people other than the girl and the
  woman."
- **Generation tool:** Muse built-in image-generation pipeline
  (media.generate_image), 2026-11-07.
- **Learning purpose:** Used in Lesson 05's "Observe and imagine"
  activity and application. The learner names what the picture
  shows, imagines what happened just before or just after it, and
  writes a scene from one character's point of view — practising
  orientation, dialogue, pacing, and sensory detail (W.5.3.a–e)
  from a picture prompt, following the ReadWriteThink
  image-to-narrative routine cited in the Resource Pack.
- **Factual review (2026-11-07, inspected before linking):** The
  image shows a white lighthouse with a red top on grey rocks above
  a dark sea, its lamp room glowing gold through storm clouds;
  waves breaking white on the rocks; a girl with curly dark hair
  in a blue jacket with a pink backpack; an older woman with grey
  hair, glasses, and a grey shawl; the two holding a glowing brass
  lantern between them and smiling at each other; the lighthouse
  door open with warm yellow light inside. No words, letters, or
  labels appear. No people other than the girl and the woman
  appear. The scene is plausible as an illustration; it is not a
  photograph, not a field observation, and not evidence about any
  real lighthouse, and the lesson says so. The lesson's text
  alternative describes only what is actually in the image: the
  lamp room is glowing in the picture, so the Lesson 05 activity
  asks what happened just before or just after it, not what the
  picture "proves" about the story.
- **Caption:** AI illustration of a lighthouse, a girl, and an
  older woman holding a lantern in a storm, generated for this
  unit. It is an illustration, not a photograph, and it shows no
  real place or people.
- **Alt text:** "A white lighthouse with a red top stands on grey
  rocks above a dark sea, its lamp room glowing gold through storm
  clouds. In front of it, a girl with curly dark hair in a blue
  jacket and a pink backpack and an older woman with grey hair and
  glasses in a grey shawl hold a glowing brass lantern between
  them, smiling at each other. The lighthouse door stands open
  with warm light inside, and waves break on the rocks around
  them."
- **Text-only alternative:** Printed in Lesson 05 directly under
  the image: a paragraph describing the lighthouse, lamp room,
  storm clouds, waves, rocks, the girl, the older woman, the
  lantern, and the open door, so a learner who cannot see the
  picture can answer the same "Observe and imagine" questions and
  write the same scene.
- **Known limitations:** Watercolour-style illustration; relative
  sizes are artistic, not to scale. The lighthouse is fictional —
  Gull Point does not exist — and the picture must not be used to
  teach lighthouse anatomy, real locations, or real storm safety.
  The girl and the woman resemble the story's Maya and Gran Rosa
  but are not portraits of real people.

## story-mountain-diagram.svg

- **File:** `story-mountain-diagram.svg`
- **Source:** Deterministic SVG authored for this unit (not
  generated), 2026-11-07.
- **Learning purpose:** Used in Lesson 01 as the teaching model for
  the story mountain: one rising-and-falling line with six labeled
  stages (exposition, inciting incident, rising action, climax,
  falling action, resolution). The learner maps "The Night the
  Light Went Out" onto it in Modeled example 1.
- **Factual review (2026-11-07):** The six labels match the stage
  names taught in Lesson 01 and in the track audit's U02 material.
  The shape is a teaching model, not a measurement: the horizontal
  axis is story order, not time, and the height is tension, not a
  quantity. No invented data, places, or people appear.
- **Alt text:** "Story mountain diagram: a mountain shape rising
  from left to right and falling again. Six labeled points mark the
  stages: Exposition at the bottom left, Inciting incident on the
  lower rising slope, Rising action on the rising slope, Climax at
  the peak, Falling action on the falling slope, and Resolution at
  the bottom right."
- **Text-only alternative:** Printed in Lesson 01 directly under
  the image, describing the shape and the six stages in order.
- **Known limitations:** A static diagram; it does not show that
  real stories may linger on a stage, repeat one, or reorder
  events with flashbacks — the lesson notes the mountain is the
  common shape, not a rule every story follows.
