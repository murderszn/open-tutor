# Resource Pack — Unit 05: Narrative Craft

**Goal (adult planning judgment):** Give the guiding adult free,
no-account videos and references that reinforce this unit's
objectives — story orientation and structure, dialogue punctuation
and character voice, pacing through scene and summary, sensory
detail and showing, and endings that follow — for a grade-5 learner
(ages 10–11). Videos supplement the lessons; they never replace
explicit teaching, practice, or the adult's check. The unit's own
original story and lessons carry all core learning; no external
source is required.

All external links below were opened or search-verified on
**2026-11-07** as described per item. Runtimes and captions were not
checked — preview before assigning. YouTube serves ads; use an
ad-blocked or supervised session with the learner. Nothing here is
claimed as state-adopted or accredited.

## Web references (6 verified by search or direct open on 2026-11-07)

1. [Character Clash: A Minilesson on Paragraphing and Dialogue — ReadWriteThink](https://www.readwritethink.org/classroom-resources/lesson-plans/character-clash-minilesson-paragraphing?tab=1)
   — after a brief review of paragraphing conventions in dialogue,
   learners highlight each character's speech in a different colour
   and correct "character clashes" where two speakers share one
   paragraph. Checked 2026-11-07: search result confirmed on-topic
   with matching description. Intended audience: adults/teachers
   (adapt the classroom routine for one learner). Free, no account.
   Adult background for Lesson 02; its character-clash idea is the
   name this unit uses for the two-speakers-one-paragraph error.
2. [A Picture's Worth a Thousand Words: From Image to Detailed Narrative — ReadWriteThink](https://www.readwritethink.org/classroom-resources/lesson-plans/picture-worth-thousand-words?tab=1)
   — learners look at an image that tells a story, brainstorm the
   events and characters it shows, then write from one character's
   point of view, telling what led up to the picture or what
   followed. Checked 2026-11-07: search result confirmed on-topic
   with matching description. Intended audience: adults/teachers
   (the plan is written for grades 6–8; adapt its image-prompt
   routine down for grade 5). Free, no account. Adult background
   for Lesson 05's illustration prompt; its point-of-view move is
   the shape of the Lesson 05 application.
3. [Descriptive Writing — Reading Rockets](https://www.ReadingRockets.org/strategies/descriptive_writing)
   — what descriptive writing is, why to teach it, and how: focus
   on the five senses, with a demonstration video of "show, not
   tell" using the senses on a hot day. Checked 2026-11-07: search
   result confirmed on-topic with matching description. Intended
   audience: adults/teachers. Free, no account. Adult background
   for Lesson 04; its five-senses framing matches this unit's
   sense chart.
4. [Using Quotation Marks — Purdue OWL (Purdue University)](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/punctuation/quotation_marks/)
   — the standard American rules for quotation marks: pairs, capital
   first letter of a complete-sentence quotation, no capital for a
   mid-sentence continuation, and commas and periods inside the
   closing quotation mark. Checked 2026-11-07: search result
   confirmed on-topic with matching rules. Intended audience:
   adults (a college-level reference; the adult adapts it, and the
   unit's Lesson 02 states the grade-5 rules). Free, no account.
   Adult reference for Lesson 02; its rules match this unit's six
   dialogue rules.
5. [Teaching Young Writers When to Show and Tell — Thoughtful Learning](https://k12.thoughtfullearning.com/blogpost/teaching-young-writers-when-show-and-tell)
   — telling conveys information quickly; showing brings
   experiences to life through sensory details; writers mix both,
   and the choice depends on purpose. Checked 2026-11-07: search
   result confirmed on-topic with matching description. Intended
   audience: adults/teachers. Free, no account. Adult background
   for Lessons 03–04; its "mixing showing and telling" section is
   the reason this unit teaches summaries that tell plainly
   alongside scenes that show.
6. [English Language Arts Standards — Common Core State Standards Initiative](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/)
   — official framework landing page for the ELA standards this
   unit references (W.5.3.a–e, W.5.4, W.5.5, W.5.10, L.5.2.b,
   L.5.2.d, L.5.3.a, RL.5.3). Checked 2026-11-07: page opened
   directly and confirmed live. Intended audience: adults. Free,
   no account. No state adoption or accreditation is claimed by
   this unit.
