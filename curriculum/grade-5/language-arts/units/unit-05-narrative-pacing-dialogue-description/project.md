# Project — A Story of Your Own

**Task:** Write, revise, and read aloud a complete original narrative
of your own — a real or imagined experience with a beginning, a
trouble, a turning point, and an ending that follows from what came
before. Use everything this unit taught: orientation, dialogue that
shows character, pacing, sensory detail, and an ending that passes
the three tests.

**Time:** two project workshops (sessions 11–12) plus revision in
session 14. About 40 minutes per workshop. The read-aloud happens in
session 14.

## Deliverables (all four are required)

1. **Story map** — the six story-mountain stages for your story, in
   one or two lines each: exposition (who, where, when), inciting
   incident (the one event that breaks the ordinary world), rising
   action (at least two obstacles), climax (the turning point),
   falling action (what happens right after), resolution (how things
   end and how the main character has changed).
2. **Narrative draft** — 400–600 words (about one and a half to
   three pages handwritten, or one to two pages typed), with:
   - an opening paragraph that orients the reader (who, where,
     when) before the trouble starts;
   - at least one dialogue scene of four or more lines, with a new
     paragraph for each speaker, correct punctuation, and one
     mid-sentence tag or action beat;
   - one stretched moment (a scene of at least five sentences at
     the climax or the hardest choice), and at least one passage
     of time summarized with a time transition;
   - at least one paragraph that shows feeling through two or more
     senses and one precise verb, without naming the feeling;
   - an ending of two or three sentences that passes the three
     ending tests;
   - a title, punctuated correctly (quotation marks for a story).
3. **Revision log** — one change for each of the five checklist
   items, written as: *what I changed — why I changed it.*
   Orientation, dialogue, pacing, sensory detail, ending: one entry
   each, five entries in total.
4. **Read-aloud** — read the finished story aloud to the guiding
   adult, at an understandable pace, in a clear voice. The adult
   listens for the orientation, the dialogue, the stretched moment,
   the sensory paragraph, and the ending, and tells you one strength
   and one next step afterward.

## Materials

Lessons 01–05 and the lighthouse story; the story-mountain diagram;
your story map; pencil or keyboard. No outside sources are required.
If you want a picture prompt, use the Lesson 05 illustration (or its
text alternative) — a scene from it may become your story, but the
story itself must be your own words.

## Steps

1. **Workshop 1 — story map.** Fill in all six stages before
   drafting anything. Check: is the inciting incident *one* event?
   Are there at least two obstacles in the rising action? Does the
   climax decide the outcome? Show the map to the adult before
   drafting.
2. **Workshop 1 — opening draft.** Write the opening paragraph and
   the first obstacle. Check: does the reader know who, where, and
   when before the trouble starts?
3. **Workshop 2 — middle draft.** Write the dialogue scene, the
   stretched moment, and the sensory paragraph. Check each line of
   dialogue against the six rules; check the stretched moment
   against the stretch-or-skip test.
4. **Workshop 2 — ending draft.** Write the ending, then test it:
   does it answer the story's question? Has the character changed?
   Does it use the story's own details? Fix it until it passes all
   three.
5. **Session 14 — revise.** Use the checklist to make one change
   for each item, and record each change in the revision log. Read
   the whole story aloud once for sense, and fix anything that
   sounds wrong to your ear. Title the story, punctuate the title,
   and read it aloud to the adult.

## Rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | Proficient (4 points) | Developing (2 points) | Beginning (0 points) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orientation and sequence (W.5.3.a, W.5.3.c) | Opening names who, where, and when before the trouble; events unfold naturally with transitions | Opening names two of who/where/when, or the sequence needs one repair | Opening gives no orientation, or events are out of order with no transitions |
| Dialogue and character (W.5.3.b; L.4.2.b) | Dialogue scene of 4+ lines, new paragraph per speaker, correct punctuation, and the speakers sound like different people | Dialogue present but with 2–3 punctuation errors, or both speakers sound alike | No dialogue scene, or punctuation errors on most lines |
| Pacing (W.5.3.b; L.5.3.a) | The hardest moment is stretched into a scene; unimportant time is summarized with a time transition | One of the two is done well; the other is missing or confused | Climax summarized in one sentence, or every moment stretched alike |
| Sensory detail and precise words (W.5.3.d) | At least one paragraph shows feeling through 2+ senses and a precise verb, without naming the feeling | Sensory detail present but thin (one sense), or the feeling is named as well as shown | No sensory paragraph, or the story only tells feelings |
| Ending, revision, and title (W.5.3.e, W.5.4, W.5.5, L.5.2.d) | Ending passes all three tests; revision log has one change per checklist item; title punctuated correctly | Ending passes two tests, or the log is missing one or two entries, or the title is mispunctuated | Ending fails two or more tests, or no revision log, or no title |

**How the score is used:** 16–20 points: ready to move on — the
story does all five jobs. 10–15 points: revise the lowest criterion
once more with the adult, then move on. Below 10 points: re-teach
the lowest criterion with its lesson, revise, and read aloud again.
The score is a teaching signal, not a grade to keep.
