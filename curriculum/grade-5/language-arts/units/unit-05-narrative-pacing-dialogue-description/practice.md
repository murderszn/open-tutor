# Practice Sets — Unit 05: Narrative Craft

Work with the adult the same day you finish each set. Use "The
Night the Light Went Out" (Lesson 01); do not look at the answer key
while you work. A task is complete when the adult can see the
evidence — a quotation, a rule, a sense, or a reason — not just an
answer.

## A. Orientation and story mountain (after Lesson 01)

Use the lighthouse story (Lesson 01).

1. Copy the story-mountain table from Lesson 01. For the **rising
   action** row, add one more obstacle from ¶5 or ¶6 that is not
   already in the table.
2. Which paragraph is the exposition? Write the three orientation
   facts it gives: who, where, and when.
3. These five events are out of order. Number them 1–5 in story
   order, then join them into one paragraph with transitions
   (*first, then, after that, at last*): (a) Gran throws the
   switch. (b) Maya counts the iron steps. (c) The beam stops at
   half past nine. (d) Maya writes in her notebook in the morning.
   (e) Maya arrives at the lighthouse for the weekend.
4. Name the inciting incident in one event, and name the climax in
   one event. Then write one sentence explaining how they are
   different jobs in the story.
5. True or false, with a reason: "The climax of the lighthouse
   story is when the light goes out."
6. Write an orientation of two sentences for a story of your own
   about a stormy night somewhere you know. Name who is there,
   where it is, and when it happens. Do not start the trouble yet.
7. Character comparison (RL.5.3): in ¶2, Gran Rosa lit the lamp
   at dusk, "the way she did every evening." In ¶3, Maya's stomach
   dropped. Quote one thing each of them *does* in those paragraphs,
   and write one sentence explaining what the two actions show
   about who is steady and who is frightened.
8. Join these two sentences with a time transition so the reader
   knows a night has passed: "Gran threw the switch. Maya stood on
   the rocks with her notebook." Choose from *at once, in the
   morning, an hour later, by Friday* — and explain why the others
   do not fit.

## B. Dialogue punctuation and character voice (after Lesson 02)

Use the lighthouse story (Lesson 01).

1. Correct the punctuation in each sentence. There is one error in
   each.
   a. "The stairs are dark now", said Gran.
   b. maya whispered, "the light went out."
   c. "Hand me the fuse" Gran said.
   d. "What if we can't fix it." she asked.
2. Rewrite this character clash as separate paragraphs, one for
   each speaker turn, with correct punctuation: *"Ready?" Maya
   asked. "Ready," Gran said. "Then throw the switch," Maya said.*
3. Write a mid-sentence-tag sentence of your own, using the
   lighthouse story: begin with *"The wind is shaking the tower,"*
   and split it with a tag.
4. Choose the tag that fits the feeling. Write the full sentence
   correctly punctuated.
   a. Maya, frightened, seeing the dark stairs: *"I don't want to
      go up there," ___ Maya.* (whispered / announced)
   b. Gran, delighted, hearing the boat's horn: *"There," ___
      Gran.* (muttered / laughed)
   c. Maya, curious, at the lamp room door: *"What is that big
      glass thing?" ___ Maya.* (asked / shouted)
   d. Gran, firm, giving an order: *"Keep your eyes on the step," 
      ___ Gran.* (said / sang)
5. Write a four-line dialogue exchange between Maya and Gran at the
   top of the stairs, just after the light comes back on. New
   paragraph for each speaker, one action beat instead of a tag in
   one line, and no line may use the word *said* more than once.
6. Find and fix the error: *"What if a boat is out there right
   now?" She asked.* Explain the rule in one sentence.
7. Rewrite this tag-first sentence as a tag-after sentence, keeping
   the punctuation correct: *Gran said, "Hand me the fuse."*
8. Label each line **dialogue** or **narration**. Then write one
   sentence explaining what the dialogue lines show about Maya
   that the narration lines do not.
   a. "What if we can't fix it?" she asked.
   b. Halfway up, Maya's legs began to shake.
   c. "Then we fix it faster," Gran said.
   d. Gran squeezed her shoulder.

## C. Pacing: scene and summary (after Lesson 03)

Use the lighthouse story (Lesson 01) and the fast/slow climb
(Lesson 03).

1. Label each passage **scene** or **summary**, and give one reason.
   a. "Gran and Maya climbed ninety-seven dark steps to the lamp
      room. Maya's legs shook, but she kept going."
   b. "Maya counted them under her breath — ten, twenty, thirty —
      and the lantern swung in her hand, throwing shadows that
      leapt ahead of her like startled cats."
   c. "In the morning, the storm had blown itself out. The sea lay
      flat and shining."
   d. "'Keep your eyes on the step in front of you,' Gran said.
      'Don't look at how far there is to go.'"
2. Stretch or skip? Write **stretch** or **skip** and one reason
   for each. (a) The boat circling the point in the dark.
   (b) Gran locking the lighthouse door in the morning. (c) Maya
   choosing which notebook to bring. (d) The moment Maya hands
   Gran the fuse. (e) Maya brushing her teeth before bed.
3. Write a fast (summary) version — three sentences or fewer — of
   this slow moment: Maya and Gran eat supper, wash the dishes,
   and Gran tells Maya about the first storm she ever kept the
   light through. Keep the facts; drop the detail.
4. Write a slow (scene) version — at least five sentences, with one
   line of dialogue and one sensory detail — of this fast moment:
   "Maya arrived at the lighthouse on Friday." Keep the facts; add
   the detail.
5. Combine these three short sentences into one flowing sentence
   that slows the pace: "The rain tapped the glass. The wind pushed
   at the windows. The sky turned the colour of wet slate."
6. Break this long sentence into two or three short ones to make
   the moment feel sudden: "When the beam stopped, the dark pressed
   in like a hand over a candle, Maya's stomach dropped, and she
   whispered Gran's name into the dark."
7. Choose the time transition that joins each pair honestly, and
   say why: (a) "Gran lit the lamp at dusk. ___ the beam stopped."
   (*at half past nine / the next morning*). (b) "The light flooded
   the room. ___ Maya stood on the rocks with her notebook."
   (*at once / in the morning*).
8. Explain in two sentences why the writer summarized the morning
   after (¶9) instead of stretching it into a scene. Use the
   stretch-or-skip rule from Lesson 03.

## D. Sensory detail and showing (after Lesson 04)

Use the lighthouse story (Lesson 01).

1. Write the five senses. For each one, write a detail from the
   lighthouse story that appeals to it. (For taste, invent a detail
   of your own — what would the salt air taste like? — and label it
   as your own detail, not the story's.)
2. Rewrite this telling paragraph as showing, in at least four
   sentences. Use at least two senses and one precise verb. Do not
   use the words *scared*, *dark*, or *brave*:
   "Maya was scared on the stairs. It was dark. Gran was brave.
   Maya kept going."
3. Upgrade these vague verbs to precise ones that fit the
   lighthouse story: (a) Maya *went* up the stairs. (b) The wind
   *made a sound* through the tower. (c) Gran *touched* Maya's
   shoulder. (d) The light *came back*. (e) The boat *made a
   noise*. (f) Maya *said* the words quietly.
4. Name the dominant sense in each passage, and give one reason.
   (a) "Maya took a deep breath, smelled oil and salt and rain,
   and climbed on." (b) "The wind moaned through the tower, and
   each gust made the iron sing." (c) "The stairs wound up and up,
   ninety-seven steps of cold iron."
5. Voice: write two sentences about the same staircase — one as
   Maya (eleven, excited, a little scared) would tell it, and one
   as Gran (calm, practical, used to the tower) would tell it.
   Then write one sentence explaining how the word choice shows
   who is speaking.
6. Find the vague word in each sentence and replace it with
   concrete words: (a) "It was a nice morning." (b) "The tower was
   big." (c) "Maya felt bad." (d) "The storm was bad."
7. Expand this flat sentence with two sensory details and one
   precise verb: "The boat turned toward the harbour."
8. Read ¶8 of the story. Copy the sentence that shows Gran is
   delighted *without* using the word *delighted* or *happy*.
   Then write one sentence explaining which sense or action does
   the showing.

## E. Endings and revision (after Lesson 05)

Use the lighthouse story (Lesson 01) and the illustration
(Lesson 05).

1. Three endings for the lighthouse story. For each, write
   **passes** or **fails**, and name the test it fails (or the
   strongest test it passes).
   a. "The next morning Maya decided to become a pirate and sailed
      away on a stolen boat. The end."
   b. "In the morning, the storm had blown itself out. Maya stood
      on the rocks with her notebook and wrote: *The light went
      out, and we climbed the dark stairs, and we fixed it.*"
   c. "And the moral of the story is that you should always be
      brave and never give up, no matter what. The end."
2. Revision clinic: revise this flawed paragraph. Fix at least
   four things (orientation, dialogue punctuation, telling vs.
   showing, pacing, ending):
   "Sam was nervous. He walked into the room. 'Is anyone here' he
   asked. 'Yes' a voice said. 'I am here' the voice said. Sam was
   relieved. The end."
3. Punctuate these titles correctly, and label each **short work**
   or **long work**: (a) the story *the night the light went out*;
   (b) the book *the lighthouse cat*; (c) the poem *song of the
   sea*; (d) the book *how bridges stand*.
4. Write an ending of two or three sentences for this unfinished
   story, then check it against the three tests and write one
   sentence for each test saying pass or fail: "Leo found his
   little sister's lost kite tangled in the big oak behind the
   school. He had promised to find it before dark. The branches
   were high, and the wind was rising."
5. List three revisions you would make to the Lesson 05 draft
   paragraph ("Maya and Gran went up the stairs…") *beyond* the
   ones shown in the lesson's revised version, and say why each
   one makes the story better.
6. Use the illustration: write three sentences that could happen
   just *after* the picture, with orientation (who, where, when)
   in the first sentence and one correctly punctuated line of
   dialogue.
7. Compare characters (RL.5.3): imagine Gran and Maya each telling
   the story of the climb the next morning. Write one sentence
   each would say about it, in their own voices — Gran's
   practical, Maya's wondering — and one sentence explaining how
   the two sentences show they are different people.
8. Punctuate this exchange from the illustration scene, with a new
   paragraph for each speaker: *Maya asked is the storm over Gran
   said not yet but the light is back on*.
