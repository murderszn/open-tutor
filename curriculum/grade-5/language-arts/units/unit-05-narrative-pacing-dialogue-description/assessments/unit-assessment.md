# Unit Assessment — Narrative Craft: Pacing, Dialogue, and Description

**Grade 5 Language Arts, Unit 05 (track weeks 19–22).** Read the
new passage first, then answer Part A. Part B is a writing task.
Do not look at the answer key while you work.

**Total: 24 points.**

## Part A — Reading the new passage (14 points)

### The passage — "The Map in the Attic" (original)

> **1.** On the first Saturday of summer, June found a brass key
> taped to the bottom of her grandfather's desk drawer. The key
> was small and cold, and a paper tag tied to it said, in her
> grandfather's neat handwriting, ATTIC.
>
> **2.** June climbed the pull-down stairs with the key in her
> pocket and her flashlight in her hand. The attic smelled of dust
> and old paper. In the far corner, under a canvas sheet, stood a
> wooden chest with a brass lock.
>
> **3.** The key turned with a click. Inside the chest, under a
> folded quilt, lay a map — hand-drawn, yellowed at the edges,
> showing the creek behind June's house, a big oak tree, and a red
> X beside a stone wall. At the bottom, in the same neat
> handwriting, it said: FOR THE ONE WHO LOOKS.
>
> **4.** "Grandpa?" June called down the stairs. "Did you draw this
> map?"
>
> **5.** "I drew it when I was your age," her grandfather called
> back. "I never found what the X marks. Maybe you will."
>
> **6.** June's heart beat faster. She folded the map carefully,
> put it in her pocket, and climbed down the stairs two at a time.
> The creek was behind the house. The oak was still there. And the
> stone wall — she had walked past it a hundred times without ever
> really looking at it.

1. (2 points) Orientation: name the who, where, and when of this
   passage, each in one short phrase, using only ¶1–2.
2. (2 points) These four events are out of order. Number them 1–4
   in story order: (a) June finds the map in the chest. (b) June
   finds the brass key in the desk drawer. (c) June climbs down
   the stairs with the map in her pocket. (d) June unlocks the
   chest in the attic.
3. (2 points) Fix the punctuation and capitalization in this
   sentence, which quotes June's words from ¶4: *"did you draw
   this map" june asked.* Explain in one sentence why there is no
   comma before the tag.
4. (2 points) Pacing: is ¶6 a scene or a summary? Give two pieces
   of evidence from the paragraph, and explain in one sentence why
   the writer chose that pace for this paragraph.
5. (2 points) Sensory detail: name the dominant sense in ¶2 and
   quote the detail that proves it. Then name one other sense the
   paragraph appeals to, with its detail.
6. (2 points) Character comparison (RL.5.3): in ¶4–5, June asks
   questions and her grandfather answers with a story about his
   own childhood. Quote one thing each of them says, and write one
   sentence explaining how their words show they are different
   kinds of people in this moment.
7. (2 points) Write a two-sentence ending for this passage that
   passes the three ending tests (answers the story's question,
   shows June changing, uses the story's own details). The story's
   question is: *will June look for what the X marks?*

## Part B — Writing a scene (10 points)

8. (10 points) Write a scene of at least six sentences that
   continues the story: June at the stone wall, with her
   grandfather. Your scene must include:
   - orientation (who, where, when) in the first two sentences
     (3 points);
   - one correctly punctuated dialogue exchange of at least two
     lines, with a new paragraph for each speaker (3 points);
   - one stretched moment (a scene of at least three sentences at
     the moment June looks at the wall closely) (2 points);
   - one sensory detail and an ending sentence that follows from
     the passage (2 points).
