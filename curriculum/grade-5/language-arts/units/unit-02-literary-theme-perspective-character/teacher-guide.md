# Unit 02 Teacher Guide

Adult-facing. Learner pages never link here except by the labeled
references in the lessons. Answer keys live in `answer-key.md`, not in
this file.

## Unit at a glance

Track weeks 7–10 (16 sessions). Anchor texts are original: "The Broken
Kite" (~700 words; Lessons 01, 02, 03, 05), "The Crash, Told by Priya"
(~200 words; Lesson 03), "The Quiet Audition" (~450 words; Lesson 04).
Objectives map to RL.5.1, RL.5.2, RL.5.3, RL.5.5, RL.5.6, RL.5.9, and
W.5.9.a. The generated illustration (`assets/kite-repair-scene.webp`) is
used in Lesson 03's perspective activity; a deterministic plot-diagram
SVG (`assets/story-mountain.svg`) supports Lesson 02's timeline task.

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — Theme (sessions 1–2)

- Read the story aloud first with expression; the learner follows, then
  re-reads. Pre-teach *hesitated* and *lashed* with the U01 dictionary
  routine — both carry plot weight.
- The three rules (sentence / general / provable) are the unit's
  backbone; post them where the learner can see them all unit.
- **Misconception watch:** plot restatement as theme ("Sam fixed his
  kite") → apply rule 2, the remove-the-names test. One-word "themes" →
  rule 1. Unearned morals ("obey adults") → rule 3, the ending test.
- The Theme Evidence Chart started here is reused in Lesson 05 — keep it.

### Lesson 02 — Character (sessions 4–5)

- Insist on the **detail with every trait**. If the learner says "Sam is
  brave," the next words out of your mouth are "show me where."
- The *careless* error-analysis item (practice B5 / lesson item 5) is
  diagnostic: learners who blame Sam for the crash are confusing *what
  happens to* a character with the character's traits. Redirect to the
  cause in the text ("a gust hit sideways") and the eleven days of
  careful work.
- **Shades of meaning:** use the U01 thesaurus routine for
  *embarrassed / ashamed / humiliated*. This is L.5.5.c in action.
- The character-change timeline feeds Lesson 05's paragraph planning.

### Lesson 03 — Point of view (sessions 6–7)

- Teach the camera metaphor concretely: cover the illustration and ask
  "what can this camera see?" for each narrator.
- **Misconception watch:** "first person = main character" (Priya is a
  witness-narrator); "one POV is truer" (each reveals and hides — the
  *probably* item in the quiz tests this).
- The POV comparison card is project planning material — keep it.
- Illustration note: the image is a fixed scene while the *telling*
  changes. If the learner describes the picture instead of the
  narrators' perspectives, redirect: "the picture doesn't change — what
  changes is who tells it."

### Lesson 04 — Comparing stories (sessions 9–10)

- Read "The Quiet Audition" aloud; have the learner mark Maya's feelings
  (nervous → frozen → trying → proud) before any comparison.
- **Misconception watch:** topic-level comparison ("both have kids") →
  push to the big idea; plot summary as comparison → require the "so
  what" sentence per point; "different = worse" → contrast is neutral.
- The guided-practice item 1 contains two false statements (both helpers
  are adults — Devon is a peer; both problems solved in one scene — Sam's
  spans the crash *and* the repair visit). Let the learner find them.
- The comparison chart is reused in assessment Task 4 — keep it.

### Lesson 05 — Theme-analysis paragraph (sessions 12–13)

- The model paragraph is for **study, not copying**. If the learner's
  draft echoes its sentences, require a re-draft from their own Theme
  Evidence Chart.
- The self-review checklist (☐ claim · ☐ two exact quotes · ☐
  explanation after each · ☐ two story parts · ☐ new-words closing) is
  the grading lens — have the learner check boxes *before* you score.
- **Summary vs. analysis:** the "this shows…" test. Any sentence that
  only retells plot gets converted.

## Reteach moves

- **Theme still = topic:** return to Lesson 01's worked example 2 (the
  wrong-theme tests). Have the learner test three candidate themes
  against the three rules out loud.
- **Traits without evidence:** back to the trait chart with a hard rule —
  no trait word may be written unless a detail is written first.
- **POV confusion:** use the Lesson 03 table (knows/hides) as a
  fill-in-the-blank for a new 3-sentence scene you dictate.
- **Dropped quotations:** the sentence-frame "The detail is ___. This
  shows ___ because ___." — oral rehearsal before writing.
- **Quiz below 8/10:** re-teach the missed objectives' lessons (not the
  whole unit), then re-take only the missed items orally.

## Unit assessment scoring (Tasks 1–4, each 1–4)

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 4 | Task 1: theme sentence + 2 exact quotes + explanation after each + details from 2+ story parts + new-words closing. Task 2: two evidenced traits + turning point + stated/inferred motive labeled correctly. Task 3: can/can't for both narrators + meaning-change explanation with a detail from each version. Task 4: chart complete + shared-theme paragraph showing *different* proof in each story. |
| 3 | Meets all requirements with one weak spot (e.g., one explanation is thin, one quote slightly off, motive label missing). |
| 2 | Claim present but evidence thin or misquoted; explanations are plot summary; comparison lists topics not themes. |
| 1 | Off-task, topic-word "theme," or no textual evidence. |

**Readiness signals:** the learner quotes accurately without prompting
(RL.5.1), catches their own dropped quotations during revision, and can
explain *why* an author might choose one POV over another (project
rubric's Strong column, row 4).
**Reteach triggers:** score ≤ 2 on Task 1 → redo Lesson 05 with the
sentence frames; score ≤ 2 on Task 3 → redo Lesson 03's knows/hides
table; score ≤ 2 on Task 4 → redo Lesson 04's Venn with the adult
modeling one cell aloud.

## Differentiation notes

- **Reading load:** all anchor texts may be read aloud by the adult; the
  learner's job is analysis, not decoding stamina (decoding was U01's
  job).
- **Writing load:** dictation is always allowed; the adult scribes while
  the learner composes. The thinking is the assessment, not the
  handwriting.
- **Extension paths:** the counter-theme paragraph (L5), the kite's-POV
  rewrite (L3), and the third-story fable comparison (L4) can become a
  portfolio trio for a learner who finishes early.
