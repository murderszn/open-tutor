# Unit 02 — Literary Theme, Perspective, and Character Development

**Grade:** 5 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #28](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/28), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Stories are built from three big questions: *What is this story really
about?* (theme), *Who is telling it, and what do they see?* (perspective),
and *How do the people in it change?* (character development). In this unit
the learner reads two original realistic stories — "The Broken Kite" and
"The Quiet Audition" — and learns to name a theme and prove it with details
from the text, to read characters from what they do and say, to notice how
a narrator's point of view shapes every scene, and to compare how two
stories can approach the same big idea in different ways. The unit ends
with a theme-analysis paragraph and a point-of-view rewrite project, both
built on quoted evidence from the stories.

## Essential question

How do theme, perspective, and character change work together to give a
story its meaning?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2), Unit 01, and the grade-4
language arts track:

- Read grade-level prose aloud with accuracy, rate, and expression;
  self-correct with context (U01; RF.5.4)
- Decode multisyllabic words with morphology; use context clues, then
  verify with a dictionary (U01; RF.5.3, L.5.4)
- Choose precise words, including shades of meaning among related words
  (U01; L.5.5.c, L.5.6)
- Ask and answer questions about stories; draw simple inferences from
  details (grade 4; RL.4.1)
- Know the five plot stages (exposition, rising action, climax, falling
  action, resolution) from the grade-4/5 legacy narrative-elements quiz —
  retaught in Lesson 01 rather than assumed secure

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Determine a **theme** of a story from details in the text and
   **summarize** how the theme develops, quoting accurately (RL.5.1,
   RL.5.2).
2. **Compare and contrast two or more characters** in a story, drawing on
   specific details about what they say, do, think, and how they change
   (RL.5.3).
3. Describe how a **narrator's point of view** (first person vs. third
   person) influences how events are described, citing what the narrator
   can and cannot know (RL.5.6).
4. Explain how a story's **parts** (scenes/chapters) fit together to build
   toward the theme (RL.5.5).
5. **Compare and contrast two stories** on their approaches to a similar
   theme, using evidence from both texts (RL.5.9).
6. Write a **theme-analysis paragraph** that states a claim, supports it
   with quoted evidence, and explains how the evidence proves the claim
   (W.5.9.a).

## Anchor texts (all original, authored for this unit)

- **"The Broken Kite"** — an original realistic short story (~700 words)
  used in Lessons 01, 02, 03, and 05. Sam's red kite crashes at the
  town festival; ashamed, he hides the wreckage until his neighbor
  Ms. Okafor offers help. Full text is printed in Lesson 01.
- **"The Quiet Audition"** — an original realistic short story (~450
  words) used in Lesson 04. Maya freezes at the school-play audition and
  must decide whether to try again. Full text is printed in Lesson 04.
- **"The Crash, Told by Priya"** — an original first-person retelling of
  the kite-crash scene (~200 words), used in Lesson 03 to contrast with
  the third-person telling.

No copyrighted texts are reproduced. Learners never need outside books
for the core lessons; the Resource Pack lists optional public-domain
read-alouds for extension.

## Pacing — 16 sessions across track weeks 7–10

Four sessions per week (~40–45 minutes each). A session is one lesson part,
a practice block, a reading/discussion block, or an assessment — never a
bare "practice" label.

| Week | Sessions | Focus and success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | 1–2 | Lesson 01 (theme vs. topic; theme statements; story read-aloud). Success: learner states "The Broken Kite"'s topic in one word and drafts a theme statement with one detail. |
| 7 | 3 | Guided + independent theme practice (practice.md §1). Success: two theme statements, each tied to a quoted detail. |
| 7 | 4 | Lesson 02 part 1 (character traits from indirect characterization). Success: trait chart for Sam with two text details per trait. |
| 8 | 5 | Lesson 02 part 2 (motives; how Sam changes). Success: learner explains one way Sam changes, with before/after details. |
| 8 | 6–7 | Lesson 03 (point of view; the generated scene illustration; Priya's retelling). Success: learner names the narrator of each version and tells what each version reveals and hides. |
| 8 | 8 | Formative quiz (`assessments/week-08_quiz.md`) + corrections discussion. Success: 8/10 with every miss re-explained in the learner's own words. |
| 9 | 9–10 | Lesson 04 ("The Quiet Audition"; comparing two stories on courage). Success: completed comparison chart with quoted evidence from both stories. |
| 9 | 11 | Project launch (`project.md`): plan the POV rewrite scene. Success: chosen scene, chosen new narrator, and a one-sentence theme claim. |
| 9 | 12 | Lesson 05 part 1 (theme-analysis paragraph: claim → evidence → explanation). Success: paragraph plan with claim and two quotations selected. |
| 10 | 13 | Lesson 05 part 2: draft the theme-analysis paragraph. Success: full draft with at least two quoted details. |
| 10 | 14–15 | Project work sessions: draft and revise the POV rewrite + theme paragraph. Success: both deliverables meet the rubric's "Proficient" column. |
| 10 | 16 | Culminating unit assessment (`assessments/unit-assessment.md`). Success: rubric-scored; review the answer key together afterward. |

Reading, discussion, and revision are inside the sessions above — six
lessons alone do not fill twenty sessions, so every session names its
activity and its success criterion.

## How this unit connects

- **Uses U01 daily:** precise-word choice (L.5.6) when naming traits
  ("stubborn" vs. "determined"); dictionary verification for new words
  like *perspective* and *motive*; morphology for *narrator/narration*.
- **Feeds U03:** quoting accurately (RL.5.1) and summarizing theme
  development become the evidence habits for informational-text
  synthesis; the claim–evidence–explanation paragraph is reused.
- **Feeds U05:** the POV rewrite project previews narrative craft
  (dialogue, pacing, description).
- **Standards crosswalk:** codes and descriptions below were verified
  against the Common Core ELA grade-5 reading-literature and writing
  standards (the track audit's verification method: two independent CCSS
  reproductions, since thecorestandards.org showed a bot challenge on
  2026-10-04). This unit does not claim state adoption or accreditation.
