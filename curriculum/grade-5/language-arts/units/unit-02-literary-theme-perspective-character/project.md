# Project — Two Voices, One Story

**Driving question:** How does changing the narrator change the story —
and what does the change reveal about the theme?

You will rewrite one scene from "The Broken Kite" from **another
character's point of view** (first person), then write a short theme
paragraph explaining how the new perspective changes what the reader
notices about the theme. Writers make POV choices on purpose; this
project puts you in the author's chair.

## Materials

- "The Broken Kite" (Lesson 01) and "The Crash, Told by Priya"
  (Lesson 03) as models
- The generated scene illustration (`assets/kite-repair-scene.webp`) —
  your scene may be the repair-table moment it depicts
- Notebook or a document; the POV comparison card from Lesson 03

## Steps

1. **Choose your scene** (one only): the festival crash, hiding the
   backpack behind the porch steps, or the repair-table scene.
2. **Choose your new narrator:** Priya, Ms. Okafor, or Sam's mother
   (who never appears on the page — you may invent her sparingly, but
   keep her consistent with the story). The narrator must be a character,
   telling in **first person ("I")**.
3. **List what your narrator knows and can't know** (3 items each). Your
   rewrite must respect the limits — no mind-reading other characters'
   private thoughts.
4. **Draft the rewrite** (150–250 words): the same events, new eyes.
   Include at least two concrete sensory details (what the narrator
   sees/hears/feels) and at least one feeling the narrator has *about*
   Sam or the event.
5. **Draft the theme paragraph** (5–8 sentences): state how the new
   point of view changes the reader's understanding of the theme
   "Asking for help takes courage." Quote **one** line from your rewrite
   and **one** line from the original story, and explain what each shows.
6. **Revise** with the adult using the rubric: check every "Proficient"
   box, then aim for one "Strong" box.

## Deliverables

1. The POV rewrite (150–250 words, first person, titled with scene and
   narrator, e.g. "The Repair Table, Told by Ms. Okafor").
2. The theme paragraph (5–8 sentences, two quotations with
   explanations).
3. The know/can't-know list from Step 3 (planning evidence).

## Rubric

| Criterion | Starting (1) | Developing (2) | Proficient (3) | Strong (4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **POV consistency** | Mixes "I" and "he/she"; narrator unclear | Mostly first person with slips | Sustained first person; narrator is clearly the chosen character | Proficient, plus the narrator's *voice* (word choice, what they notice) feels distinct from the original |
| **POV limits respected** | Narrator knows others' private thoughts | One or two limit breaks | No mind-reading; know/can't-know list is honored | Proficient, plus uses a limit *as a strength* (e.g., the narrator wonders about something, creating tension) |
| **Scene fidelity** | Events contradict the original | Key events present but jumbled | Same events in a sensible order; two+ sensory details | Proficient, plus a well-chosen invented detail that fits the story's world |
| **Theme paragraph** | Claim missing or is a topic word | Claim present; quotes dropped without explanation | Theme sentence + two quotes, each with an explanation tying it to the POV change | Proficient, plus explains *why the author* might have chosen the original POV over this one |
| **Conventions** | Frequent errors block meaning | Errors distract but meaning survives | Grade-level spelling, punctuation, and quotation marks | Proficient with varied sentence structure |

**Scoring:** 15–20 = ready to share; 10–14 = revise the lowest row with
the adult; below 10 = re-plan Steps 2–3 together, then re-draft.

## Safety and boundaries

All characters are fictional; no real people are depicted or described.
The illustration is an AI-generated scene from the fictional story,
labeled as such in `assets/README.md`. No personal information appears
in any deliverable.
