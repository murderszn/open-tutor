# Unit 02 Practice Sets

Short, purposeful practice for the week's lesson. Do the set that matches
the current lesson; answers and worked solutions are in `answer-key.md`
(adult-held). Default: 6–10 meaningful tasks per set.

---

## Set A — Theme vs. topic (Lesson 01)

**A1.** Write **T** (topic) or **Th** (theme) for each:
a. "friendship"
b. "True friends tell each other hard truths."
c. "a haunted amusement park"
d. "Running away never solves the real problem."

**A2.** Which one is a valid theme statement for "The Broken Kite"?
Explain your choice with the three rules from Lesson 01.
a. "Sam learns to fix kites."
b. "Asking for help takes courage."
c. "Kites are fun at festivals."

**A3.** A theme must be provable. For the theme "Hiding a problem never
fixes it," find **two** details from "The Broken Kite" that prove it and
**one** detail that would *not* count as proof. Explain each choice.

**A4.** Write a theme statement for "The Quiet Audition" (full sentence),
then name the single story detail that proves it best.

**A5. Explain-your-reasoning:** "A story's theme is always stated
directly by the narrator." Agree or disagree, using "The Broken Kite" —
where does the theme appear, in narrator statements or in events?

## Set B — Characters (Lesson 02)

**B1.** Match each clue to the trait it best supports. (Traits may be
used once.)
Clues: (i) "tied every knot twice" · (ii) "did not want to say the words
out loud" · (iii) "didn't laugh" · (iv) "let him do the last knot himself"
Traits: careful · proud · kind · respectful

**B2.** Is each characterization **direct** or **indirect**?
a. "Ms. Okafor was watering her roses next door."
b. "Asking felt worse than the crash."
c. "She cut a new stick, showed Sam how to lash it with tape."

**B3.** Sam's motive for hiding the kite: write it as "Sam hid the kite
because ___." Then write Ms. Okafor's motive for helping as "Ms. Okafor
helped because ___." One must be stated in the text; one must be a
supported inference — label which is which.

**B4.** Name Sam's **turning point** (the exact moment he changes course)
and explain in two sentences why the story's theme depends on that
moment.

**B5. Shades of meaning (U01 habit):** Would *embarrassed, ashamed,* or
*humiliated* best describe Sam right after the crash? Rank all three from
mildest to strongest and match each to a story detail.

## Set C — Point of view (Lesson 03)

**C1.** Identify the point of view of each sentence (first or third
person). Then name the narrator.
a. "He stuffed the torn kite into his backpack and walked home fast."
b. "I was the one who shouted 'Three… two… one!'"
c. "She cut a new stick, showed Sam how to lash it with tape."

**C2.** List **two** things Priya's retelling reveals that the
third-person story hides, and **two** things it hides that the
third-person story reveals.

**C3.** If "The Broken Kite" were retold in first person **by Ms.
Okafor**, what is one event she could describe that Sam never saw? What
is one thing she could *not* describe that the current narrator can?

**C4. Explain-your-reasoning:** "Third-person narrators know everything."
Use the narrator of "The Broken Kite" to agree, disagree, or qualify —
what does this narrator *not* tell us?

**D1.** Both stories are realistic fiction. Give **two** details — one
from each story — that prove the genre (things that could really happen,
no magic).

**D2.** Complete the table with one detail per cell:

**D3.** Write a shared-theme sentence that fits **both** stories. Then
write one sentence explaining how Sam's story and Maya's story prove it

## Set E — Theme-analysis writing (Lesson 05)

**E1.** Put these paragraph parts in order (1–4): explanation of quote 2
· claim · explanation of quote 1 · quote 2. Then add: where does quote 1
go?

**E2.** Fix this dropped quotation: *"Sam 'dragged it out and laid the
torn kite on her picnic table.'"* Add the two sentences this writer
should have written around it (one before, one after).

**E3.** Which quotation better supports the theme "Asking for help takes
courage"? Rank and justify:
a. "The kite leapt, wobbled, and climbed."
b. "Asking felt worse than the crash."

**E4.** Write a closing sentence for a paragraph whose claim is "In 'The
Quiet Audition,' courage means trying again after freezing." Restate the
theme in new words — do not repeat the claim word-for-word.
