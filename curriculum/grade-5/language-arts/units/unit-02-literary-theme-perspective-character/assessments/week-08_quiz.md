# Unit 02 Formative Quiz — Theme, Character, Point of View

**Track week 8** · 10 items · Adult-held answer key in `answer-key.md`
(§QZ). Do all work in your notebook. Quote exactly when asked — copy the
story's words word-for-word inside quotation marks.

**1.** Which one is a **theme statement**? Write the letter and explain
your choice in one sentence.
a. "The Broken Kite"
b. "Asking for help takes courage."
c. "kites"

**2.** A classmate says the theme of "The Broken Kite" is "Never fly kites
near trees." Give **two** reasons this cannot be the theme, using the
three rules from Lesson 01.

**3.** Name **two** character traits of Sam at the **beginning** of the
story. For each trait, quote or closely paraphrase **one** story detail
that proves it.

**4.** Is this characterization direct or indirect? Explain how you know:
*"Asking felt worse than the crash — like admitting he couldn't do it
alone."*

**5.** What is Sam's **motive** for finally bringing the kite to
Ms. Okafor? Answer in one sentence beginning "Sam brings the kite
because ___." Then label your answer **stated** (the text says it) or
**inferred** (you figured it out from clues).

**6.** "The Broken Kite" is told in third person. Write **two**
observations from the story that prove it, and name one thing this
narrator knows that Priya (first-person narrator of the retelling)
cannot know.

**7.** Priya writes, "he probably believes we were all laughing at him."
What does the word **probably** reveal about the limits of first-person
narration? Answer in 1–2 sentences.

**8.** Both "The Broken Kite" and "The Quiet Audition" are realistic
fiction about courage. Name **one** way the two stories show courage
**differently**, with one story detail from each.

**9.** Which quotation better supports the theme "Asking for help takes
courage"? Choose and justify in two sentences.
a. "The kite leapt, wobbled, and climbed."
b. "He dragged it out and laid the torn kite on her picnic table."

**10.** Put the parts of a theme-analysis paragraph in order (1–4):
___ explanation of the evidence · ___ claim (theme statement) ·
___ closing sentence in new words · ___ quoted evidence.
Then write one sentence explaining why the **explanation** must come
*after* the quotation, not before it.
