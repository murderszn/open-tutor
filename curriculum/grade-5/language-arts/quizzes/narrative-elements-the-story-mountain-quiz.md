## 📋 Quiz Questions

**1.** What are the five main stages of a narrative plot diagram (the Story Mountain)?

**2.** What happens during the **Exposition** of a story?

**3.** What is the **Inciting Incident**?

**4.** What occurs during the **Rising Action** of a plot?

**5.** Define the **Climax** of a narrative.

**6.** What is the difference between an **internal conflict** and an **external conflict**?

**7.** Give an example of a **character vs. nature** conflict.

**8.** What is the function of the **Resolution** at the end of a story?

**9.** What does the writing rule **"Show, Don't Tell"** mean?

**10.** Rewrite this 'telling' sentence into a vivid 'showing' sentence with sensory details: *"Marcus was terrified of the dark basement."*

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
1) Exposition, 2) Rising Action, 3) Climax, 4) Falling Action, 5) Resolution.

**Question 2:**  
The exposition introduces the protagonist, key characters, setting (time and place), and ordinary world before the main adventure begins.

**Question 3:**  
The spark or turning event that disrupts the character's ordinary life and sets the central conflict into motion.

**Question 4:**  
A series of obstacles, complications, and rising tensions that test the character as they attempt to solve their problem.

**Question 5:**  
The highest peak of emotional tension, turning point, or decisive confrontation where the outcome of the conflict is determined.

**Question 6:**  
An internal conflict happens inside a character's mind/heart (e.g., guilt, fear, difficult moral choice). An external conflict happens between a character and an outside force (e.g., another person, society, nature).

**Question 7:**  
Acceptable answers: A sailor caught in a violent hurricane; an explorer lost in a freezing snowstorm; a hiker facing a steep cliff.

**Question 8:**  
The resolution ties up loose narrative threads, reveals how the characters were changed by the journey, and establishes a new normal.

**Question 9:**  
Instead of simply stating an emotion or fact directly, the writer describes actions, physical reactions, sounds, and facial expressions so the reader experiences the moment.

**Question 10:**  
Acceptable model: *"Marcus froze at the top of the creaking wooden stairs, his knuckles white against the handrail, his breath shallow as cold shadows swallowed the basement floor below."*
