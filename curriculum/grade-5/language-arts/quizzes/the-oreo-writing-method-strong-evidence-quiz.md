**Topic:** The OREO Writing Method & Strong Evidence

## 📋 Quiz Questions

**1.** What do the letters in the **OREO** acronym stand for in opinion writing?

**2.** Why is it essential to provide concrete **Evidence/Examples (E)** rather than just repeating your opinion louder?

**3.** Which of the following serves as the strongest evidence to support the claim that reading daily improves vocabulary?
   A) I really like reading books in my room.
   B) A research study found that students who read 20 minutes a day encounter 1.8 million words per year.
   C) Books have a lot of words inside them.

**4.** Name three transition words or phrases used to introduce an example in an opinion essay.

**5.** What is the purpose of the final **O (Opinion Restated)** in the OREO framework?

**6.** How should the closing opinion sentence differ from the opening topic sentence?

**7.** True or False: Personal anecdotes (true personal stories) can serve as supporting examples in an opinion essay.

**8.** What is a **counter-argument** (counter-claim)?

**9.** Why does briefly addressing a counter-argument actually make your own opinion stronger?

**10.** Write a brief 3-sentence mini-paragraph using the O, R, and E steps on whether kids should learn to code.

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
**O** = Opinion, **R** = Reason, **E** = Example / Evidence, **O** = Opinion Restated.

**Question 2:**  
Evidence provides verifiable proof, facts, or realistic examples that convince skeptical readers, whereas repeating an opinion without proof is unpersuasive.

**Question 3:**  
B (It provides concrete, measured research data).

**Question 4:**  
Acceptable answers include: *For example, For instance, Specifically, In particular, To illustrate*.

**Question 5:**  
To remind the reader of your main claim and leave them with a strong, lasting impression after reading your evidence.

**Question 6:**  
It should restate the core belief using fresh, varied vocabulary and higher emotional impact rather than simply copying the opening sentence word-for-word.

**Question 7:**  
True (Personal anecdotes offer relatable, real-life proof when relevant to the prompt).

**Question 8:**  
A counter-argument is an opposing viewpoint or objection that someone who disagrees with you might raise.

**Question 9:**  
It shows that you have thoughtfully considered multiple perspectives and have a solid rebuttal, making your stance more mature and trustworthy.

**Question 10:**  
Acceptable model: *Learning to code is one of the most valuable skills a student can develop today (O). Coding teaches kids how to break down complex problems through step-by-step logic (R). For instance, when designing a simple Scratch game, students must debug code errors and test every variable until the system works smoothly (E).*

**Grading Scale:**
- 10/10: Exemplary Mastery (A+)
- 9/10: Proficient (A)
- 8/10: Solid Understanding (B)
- 7/10: Review Needed (C)
- Below 7: Needs 1-on-1 reteaching session with educator
