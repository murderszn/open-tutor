# Grade 5 Language Arts — Idioms, Adages & Cultural Sayings Quiz

**Topic:** Idioms, Adages & Cultural Sayings  

This quiz focuses on figurative expressions whose meanings cannot be understood simply by looking at the literal dictionary definitions of individual words.

## 📋 Quiz Questions

**1.** What is an **idiom**?

**2.** What is the difference between the **literal** meaning and the **figurative** meaning of a phrase?

**3.** What does the idiom *"spill the beans"* mean figuratively?

**4.** What does the idiom *"bite the bullet"* mean figuratively?

**5.** What does someone mean when they say a new gadget *"cost an arm and a leg"*?

**6.** What is an **adage** or **proverb**?

**7.** Explain the meaning of the famous proverb: *"A stitch in time saves nine."*

**8.** Explain the meaning of the proverb: *"Actions speak louder than words."*

**9.** Identify the idiom in this sentence: *"Learner was on cloud nine after his YouTube video crossed 1,000 views in its first weekend."*

**10.** Use the idiom *"hit the nail on the head"* correctly in an original sentence.

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
An idiom is a culturally established figure of speech whose figurative meaning is completely different from the literal words.

**Question 2:**  
The literal meaning is what the words actually say word-for-word. The figurative meaning is the symbolic or implied message behind the expression.

**Question 3:**  
To reveal a secret or disclose confidential information prematurely.

**Question 4:**  
To face a difficult, unpleasant, or painful situation with courage and endurance.

**Question 5:**  
It was extremely expensive.

**Question 6:**  
An adage or proverb is a short, well-known traditional saying that expresses a general truth, piece of wisdom, or moral rule for life.

**Question 7:**  
Fixing a small problem immediately prevents it from turning into a much bigger, more difficult catastrophe later.

**Question 8:**  
What a person actually does is far more meaningful and trustworthy than what they merely claim or promise to do.

**Question 9:**  
'On cloud nine' (meaning overjoyed or euphoric).

**Question 10:**  
Acceptable model: *"When Learner said our chess opening needed more control of the center squares, he hit the nail on the head."*
