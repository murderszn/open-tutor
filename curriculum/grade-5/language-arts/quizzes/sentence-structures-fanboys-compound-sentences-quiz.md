## 📋 Quiz Questions

**1.** What is an **independent clause**?

**2.** What does the acronym **FANBOYS** stand for?

**3.** What two things are needed to correctly join two independent clauses into a **compound sentence**?

**4.** What is a **comma splice** error?

**5.** Identify the error in this sentence and explain how to fix it: *"Learner edited his video, he uploaded it to YouTube."*

**6.** What is a **run-on sentence**?

**7.** Combine these two simple sentences into one compound sentence using the conjunction **but**: *"Learner studied the chessboard carefully. He could not find an escape from the pin."*

**8.** Combine these two simple sentences using **so**: *"The rain poured down in sheets. The referee postponed the championship game."*

**9.** Is the word 'because' a coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS) or a subordinating conjunction?

**10.** Write a correct compound sentence about school using the coordinating conjunction **and**.

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
An independent clause is a complete thought that contains a subject and a predicate (verb) and can stand alone as a sentence.

**Question 2:**  
**F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o.

**Question 3:**  
A comma AND a coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS).

**Question 4:**  
A comma splice occurs when two complete independent clauses are joined with only a comma and no coordinating conjunction.

**Question 5:**  
It is a comma splice. Fix it by adding 'and' after the comma: *"Learner edited his video, and he uploaded it to YouTube."* (or using a period or semicolon).

**Question 6:**  
A run-on occurs when two or more independent clauses are joined together without proper punctuation or conjunctions.

**Question 7:**  
*"Learner studied the chessboard carefully, but he could not find an escape from the pin."*

**Question 8:**  
*"The rain poured down in sheets, so the referee postponed the championship game."*

**Question 9:**  
Subordinating conjunction.

**Question 10:**  
Acceptable model: *"We completed our science experiment before lunch, and our educator checked our lab notes afterwards."*
