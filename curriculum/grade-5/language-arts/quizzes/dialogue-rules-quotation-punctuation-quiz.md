## 📋 Quiz Questions

**1.** What punctuation marks are used to show the exact words a character is speaking?

**2.** Where does the comma go when a dialogue tag follows the spoken words? (Inside or outside the quotation marks?)

**3.** Which of the following sentences is punctuated correctly?
   A) "Let's record the opening scene again", said Learner.
   B) "Let's record the opening scene again," said Learner.
   C) "Let's record the opening scene again" said Learner.

**4.** What is the golden rule about paragraphs when a new character starts speaking in a story?

**5.** What should be capitalized when writing dialogue that begins a sentence?

**6.** How do you punctuate dialogue when the speaker tag is in the middle of a single sentence? Example: *"We have three minutes" [tag] "before the camera battery dies."*

**7.** Why should authors avoid repeating the word "said" constantly in a dramatic scene?

**8.** Provide three expressive dialogue tags you could use instead of "said" when a character is angry or excited.

**9.** Correct the punctuation and capitalization errors in this sentence: *"are we almost there asked Learner as he adjusted his backpack"*

**10.** Write two lines of dialogue between two characters who just discovered a hidden treasure chest in an attic, following all formatting rules.

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
Quotation marks (" ").

**Question 2:**  
Inside the quotation marks (e.g., "Hurry up," shouted Marcus).

**Question 3:**  
B ("Let's record the opening scene again," said Learner.)

**Question 4:**  
Start a brand new indented paragraph every single time the speaker changes.

**Question 5:**  
The very first word spoken inside the quotation marks must always begin with a capital letter.

**Question 6:**  
Put a comma inside the first quote, then the lowercase tag, followed by a comma, then reopen quotes for the rest of the sentence (e.g., *"We have three minutes," whispered Maya, "before the camera battery dies."*).

**Question 7:**  
Overusing 'said' makes the writing monotonous and fails to convey the character's emotion, tone, volume, or urgency.

**Question 8:**  
Acceptable answers: *bellowed, barked, shouted, cheered, snapped, roared, demanded*.

**Question 9:**  
*"Are we almost there?" asked Learner as he adjusted his backpack.*

**Question 10:**  
Acceptable model:
*"Look under this dusty canvas!" gasped Learner, tugging at the frayed rope.*
*Learner leaned closer, his eyes wide. "It has a heavy brass padlock on it," he whispered.*
