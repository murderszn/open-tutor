# Grade 5 Language Arts — Figurative Language: Personification & Hyperbole Quiz

**Topic:** Figurative Language: Personification & Hyperbole  

This quiz assesses your understanding of personification (giving human traits to objects) and hyperbole (deliberate, wild exaggeration).

## 📋 Quiz Questions

**1.** What is **personification**?

**2.** Why do poets and authors use personification in their writing?

**3.** Identify what is being personified and what human action it is doing: *"The stubborn wind howled through the keyhole and rattled the locked door."*

**4.** Which of the following is an example of personification?
   A) The leaves fluttered down.
   B) The autumn leaves danced gracefully across the sidewalk.
   C) The leaves were green and brown.

**5.** What is **hyperbole**?

**6.** Is hyperbole meant to be taken literally by the reader? Explain.

**7.** Identify whether this statement is Hyperbole or Personification: *"I have told you to clean your studio workbench a million times!"*

**8.** Identify whether this statement is Hyperbole or Personification: *"The alarm clock screamed at 6:00 AM, demanding that we get out of bed."*

**9.** Write an original sentence using **personification** describing an old computer or camera.

**10.** Write an original sentence using **hyperbole** describing how hungry you were after a long soccer or basketball game.

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
Personification is giving human traits, emotions, behaviors, or intentions to non-human objects, animals, or natural elements.

**Question 2:**  
It makes inanimate objects feel dynamic and relatable, allowing the reader to form a vivid emotional connection with the scene.

**Question 3:**  
The wind is personified; it is described as 'stubborn' and as 'howling' and 'rattling' with human-like intent.

**Question 4:**  
B (Leaves cannot literally 'dance gracefully'—that is a human action).

**Question 5:**  
Hyperbole is an intentional and dramatic exaggeration used for emphasis, humor, or strong emotional impact.

**Question 6:**  
No. It is a figure of speech; if taken literally, it would be impossible or untrue, but readers understand it conveys intense emotion or scale.

**Question 7:**  
Hyperbole (exaggerating 'a million times').

**Question 8:**  
Personification (an alarm clock cannot literally 'scream' or 'demand').

**Question 9:**  
Acceptable model: *"The tired old laptop groaned and wheezed as it struggled to load the heavy video file."*

**Question 10:**  
Acceptable model: *"I was so starving after the fourth quarter that I could have eaten an entire banquet table including the wooden chairs!"*
