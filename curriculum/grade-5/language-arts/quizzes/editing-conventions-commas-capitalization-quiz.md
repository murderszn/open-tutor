## 📋 Quiz Questions

**1.** What is the rule for using commas when listing three or more items in a series?

**2.** What is the Oxford comma (serial comma)?

**3.** Which sentence uses commas in a series correctly?
   A) We packed apples cheese crackers and juice for the hike.
   B) We packed apples, cheese, crackers, and juice for the hike.
   C) We packed apples, cheese crackers, and juice, for the hike.

**4.** How should a comma be used with an **introductory phrase** at the start of a sentence? Give an example.

**5.** Explain the rule for using a comma in **direct address** (when speaking directly to someone by name).

**6.** Which sentence correctly uses direct address punctuation?
   A) Let's eat Grandma!
   B) Let's eat, Grandma!

**7.** Why does the comma in the famous example above change the entire meaning of the sentence?

**8.** What types of words must ALWAYS be capitalized? (Name at least four categories).

**9.** Find and correct the three capitalization errors in this sentence: *"last friday, uncle robert drove us across the golden gate bridge in san francisco."*

**10.** Fix all comma and capitalization errors in this sentence: *"in the morning educator asked Learner please feed the dog cat and fish."*

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
Separate each item in the list with a comma, placing commas after each item up until the conjunction.

**Question 2:**  
The Oxford comma is the optional but recommended final comma placed immediately before the coordinating conjunction in a series (e.g., apples, oranges[,] and bananas).

**Question 3:**  
B (We packed apples, cheese, crackers, and juice for the hike.)

**Question 4:**  
Place a comma after the introductory dependent clause or prepositional phrase before starting the main independent clause (e.g., *"After the final buzzer sounded, the team cheered."*).

**Question 5:**  
Set off the name of the person being spoken to with commas (e.g., *"Learner, grab the tripod,"* or *"Can you check this, educator?"*).

**Question 6:**  
B (Let's eat, Grandma!)

**Question 7:**  
Without the comma, 'Grandma' becomes the direct object of the verb 'eat' (implying cannibalism!). With the comma, the speaker is talking to Grandma.

**Question 8:**  
1) The first word of a sentence, 2) The pronoun 'I', 3) Proper nouns (specific names of people, places, holidays, days of the week, months), 4) Major words in titles.

**Question 9:**  
Capitalize **Last**, **Friday**, and **Uncle** (as part of a name). Corrected: *"Last Friday, Uncle Robert drove us across the Golden Gate Bridge in San Francisco."*

**Question 10:**  
*"In the morning, educator asked, 'Learner, please feed the dog, cat, and fish.'"*
