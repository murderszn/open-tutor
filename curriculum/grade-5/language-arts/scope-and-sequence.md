## 2. Prerequisites

Learners typically enter grade-5 language arts with the grade-4 language arts
track's end-of-year objectives (that track's audit is delivered as draft
PR #89; its units are not yet written):

- **Decode/fluency:** read unfamiliar multisyllabic words using letter-sound
  knowledge, syllabication patterns, and morphology (roots and affixes); read
  grade-level prose and poetry orally with accuracy, appropriate rate, and
  expression; self-correct with context (RF.4.3, RF.4.4).
- **Literature:** explain explicit meaning and draw inferences from details
  and examples; determine a theme and summarize; describe a character,
  setting, or event in depth; compare first- and third-person narration;
  compare similar themes across stories, myths, and traditional literature
  from different cultures (RL.4.1–4.7, RL.4.9).
- **Informational:** determine the main idea and explain how key details
  support it; explain what happened and why from specific information;
  describe overall text structures; explain how an author uses reasons and
  evidence; integrate information from two texts (RI.4.1–4.3, RI.4.5,
  RI.4.8–4.9).
- **Writing:** opinion pieces with grouped reasons and facts; narratives with
  dialogue, description, and closure; short research with notes and a source
  list; informative paragraphs with facts and quotations (W.4.1–4.3, W.4.7–4.8).
- **Language:** relative pronouns/adverbs, progressive tenses, modals,
  prepositional phrases, fragments/run-ons corrected, capitalization, commas
  and quotation marks for direct speech, spelling with references (L.4.1–4.2).
- **Speaking/listening:** prepared collaborative discussion with rules and
  roles, paraphrase across media, identify a speaker's reasons and evidence,
  organized presentations with audio/visual displays (SL.4.1–4.6).

## 3. Track objectives

Measurable, adult-assessed by end of year. Objectives mirror the track README.

1. Read unfamiliar multisyllabic words accurately using morphology (roots and
   affixes) and syllabication; read grade-level prose and poetry orally with
   accuracy, appropriate rate, and expression on successive readings; use
   context to confirm or self-correct word recognition and understanding
   (RF.5.3, RF.5.3.a, RF.5.4.a–c).
2. Clarify unknown and multiple-meaning words flexibly: context clues
   (cause/effect, comparisons, definitions), common grade-appropriate Greek
   and Latin affixes and roots (*photograph, photosynthesis*), and print and
   digital dictionaries, glossaries, and thesauruses; interpret similes and
   metaphors in context; explain common idioms, adages, and proverbs; use
   synonyms, antonyms, and homographs to understand words; acquire and use
   grade-appropriate academic and domain-specific words, including those that
   signal contrast, addition, and other logical relationships (*however,
   although, nevertheless, similarly, moreover, in addition*) (L.5.4.a–c,
   L.5.5.a–c, L.5.6).
3. In literature: quote accurately from a text when explaining explicit
   meaning and drawing inferences; determine a theme from details (including
   how characters respond to challenges and how a poem's speaker reflects on
   a topic) and summarize; compare and contrast two or more characters,
   settings, or events, drawing on specific details; explain how chapters,
   scenes, or stanzas fit together; describe how a narrator's or speaker's
   point of view influences events; analyze how visual and multimedia
   elements contribute to meaning, tone, or beauty; compare and contrast
   stories in the same genre; read literature at the high end of the
   grades 4–5 text complexity band independently and proficiently (RL.5.1–5.7,
   RL.5.9, RL.5.10).
4. In informational text: quote accurately when explaining explicit meaning
   and drawing inferences; determine two or more main ideas, explain how key
   details support them, and summarize; explain relationships or interactions
   between individuals, events, ideas, or concepts; compare and contrast the
   overall structure of two or more texts (chronology, comparison,
   cause/effect, problem/solution); analyze multiple accounts of the same
   event, noting similarities and differences in point of view; draw on
   multiple print or digital sources to locate answers or solve problems;
   explain how an author uses reasons and evidence, identifying which reasons
   and evidence support which point(s); integrate information from several
   texts to write or speak knowledgeably; read informational texts at the high
   end of the grades 4–5 complexity band independently and proficiently
   (RI.5.1–5.10).
5. Write narratives to develop real or imagined experiences: establish a
   situation, introduce a narrator and/or characters, organize a natural event
   sequence; use narrative techniques (dialogue, description, pacing) to
   develop experiences and show character responses; use transitional words,
   phrases, and clauses to manage sequence; use concrete words and sensory
   details; provide a conclusion that follows from the narrated experiences
   (W.5.3.a–e, W.5.4, W.5.5, W.5.10).
6. Write opinion pieces on topics or texts: introduce the topic, state an
   opinion, and create an organizational structure in which ideas are
   logically grouped; provide logically ordered reasons supported by facts
   and details; link opinion and reasons using words, phrases, and clauses
   (*consequently, specifically*); provide a concluding statement; draw
   evidence from literary or informational texts (W.5.1.a–d, W.5.4, W.5.5,
   W.5.9.a–b, W.5.10).
7. Conduct short research projects using several sources: recall or gather
   relevant information from print and digital sources; summarize or
   paraphrase in notes and finished work; provide a list of sources; write
   informative/explanatory texts that introduce a topic with a general
   observation and focus, group related information logically with formatting
   and multimedia, develop it with facts, definitions, concrete details,
   quotations, and examples, link ideas within and across categories
   (*in contrast, especially*), use precise domain-specific vocabulary, and
   provide a concluding section; type a minimum of two pages in a single
   sitting with some guidance (W.5.2.a–e, W.5.6, W.5.7, W.5.8, W.5.10).
8. Use grade-5 conventions and speaking skills: conjunctions, prepositions,
   and interjections; perfect verb tenses and correct verb-tense sequences;
   correlative conjunctions (*either/or, neither/nor*); punctuation to
   separate items in a series; commas for introductory elements, tag
   questions, and direct address; underlining, quotation marks, or italics
   for titles; grade-appropriate spelling; expand, combine, and reduce
   sentences for meaning and style; compare dialects and registers in
   stories, dramas, or poems; engage in prepared collaborative discussions,
   summarize texts and speakers' points with their supporting evidence, and
   report opinions with multimedia components in clear, appropriately formal
   presentations (L.5.1.a–e, L.5.2.a–e, L.5.3.a–b; SL.5.1–5.6).

## 4. Standards crosswalk

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for English Language
Arts**, grade 5 strands, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/).
On 2026-10-04 the official site was unreachable (bot-verification
challenge); every entry below was cross-checked against two independent
CCSS reproductions (the K–5 Common Core PDF hosted via schoolinsites and
the Georgia ELAGSE grade-5 standards page, which reproduces the CCSS text
verbatim). Descriptions match the official grade-5 standard text. No state
adoption or accreditation is claimed.

Two facts confirmed during verification: **RL.5.8 does not exist** in the
official framework (literature runs RL.5.1–5.7, 5.9, 5.10), and grade-5
Reading: Foundational Skills covers only RF.5.3 and RF.5.4 (RF.5.1–2 stop
at grade 1).

| L.5.2 | Demonstrate command of the conventions of standard English capitalization, punctuation, and spelling when writing. |

| L.5.2.b | Use a comma to separate an introductory element from the rest of the sentence. |
| L.5.2.c | Use a comma to set off the words *yes* and *no* (e.g., *Yes, thank you*), to set off a tag question from the rest of the sentence (e.g., *It's true, isn't it?*), and to indicate direct address (e.g., *Is that you, Steve?*). |
| L.5.2.d | Use underlining, quotation marks, or italics to indicate titles of works. |
| L.5.2.e | Spell grade-appropriate words correctly, consulting references as needed. |
| L.5.3 | Use knowledge of language and its conventions when writing, speaking, reading, or listening. |
| L.5.3.a | Expand, combine, and reduce sentences for meaning, reader/listener interest, and style. |

| L.5.4 | Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown and multiple-meaning words and phrases based on grade 5 reading and content, choosing flexibly from a range of strategies. |

## 5. 36-week sequence

Eight 4-week units plus four flexible weeks (diagnostic ×2, midyear review
embedded in Unit 04, final review ×2) = 36 weeks. Session model: **4 sessions
per week, 35–40 minutes each** (16 sessions per unit). Session types rotate
across explicit lesson, guided practice, reading or writing practice, and
review — named per unit below. Grade-5 learners do independent written
practice (8–12 tasks) that the adult reviews the same day; oral, pointing,
and drawing response modes remain available for checks.

### Weeks 1–2 — Diagnostic and routines (flexible)

- **Goal:** establish session routines (word-study warm-ups, read-aloud
  turn-taking, writing-notebook habits, discussion norms, exit-check rituals)
  and baseline each objective's entry point.
- Sessions: read a grade-level passage and note accuracy/rate/expression;
  decode unfamiliar multisyllabic words aloud; use context and a Greek/Latin
  root to explain three words; quote a text line to support an inference;
  find two main ideas of a short informational paragraph; write one opinion
  sentence with a reason; join a 5-minute discussion following rules.
- No new instruction; record observations against the track objectives.

### Unit 01 — Reading fluency, morphology, and precise vocabulary (Weeks 3–6)

- **Standards:** RF.5.3, RF.5.3.a, RF.5.4.a–c; L.5.4.a–c, L.5.6
- **Week 3 goal:** morphology — use letter-sound correspondences,
  syllabication patterns, and roots/affixes to read unfamiliar multisyllabic
  words accurately in and out of context (RF.5.3.a); grade-level text with
  purpose and understanding (RF.5.4.a).
- **Week 4 goal:** common Greek and Latin affixes and roots as clues —
  *photograph, photosynthesis* and grade-appropriate sets (L.5.4.b); read
  prose and poetry orally with accuracy, appropriate rate, and expression on
  successive readings (RF.5.4.b).
- **Week 5 goal:** context clues — cause/effect relationships and comparisons
  in text (L.5.4.a); print and digital dictionaries, glossaries, thesauruses
  (L.5.4.c); acquire academic words that signal contrast, addition, and
  logical relationships (L.5.6).
- **Week 6:** review week — self-correct word recognition with context
  (RF.5.4.c); fluency check; formative assessment.
- Sessions rotate: word-analysis lesson → morphology practice → read-aloud
  and fluency practice → review game.
- Reuses (revised; keys separated; taught lessons first):
  `quizzes/word-morphology-power-prefixes-quiz.md`,
  `quizzes/word-morphology-essential-suffixes-quiz.md`.

### Unit 02 — Literary theme, perspective, and character development (Weeks 7–10)

- **Standards:** RL.5.1–5.7, RL.5.9, RL.5.10; L.5.6; W.5.9.a
- **Week 7 goal:** quote accurately from a text when explaining explicit
  meaning and drawing inferences (RL.5.1); determine a theme from details —
  how characters respond to challenges — and summarize (RL.5.2).
- **Week 8 goal:** compare and contrast two or more characters, settings, or
  events, drawing on specific details (RL.5.3); how chapters, scenes, or
  stanzas fit together (RL.5.5).
- **Week 9 goal:** how a narrator's or speaker's point of view influences how
  events are described (RL.5.6); how visual and multimedia elements
  contribute to meaning, tone, or beauty (RL.5.7).
- **Week 10:** review week — compare and contrast stories in the same genre
  on their approaches to similar themes (RL.5.9); read at the high end of
  the grades 4–5 band independently (RL.5.10); formative check.
- Sessions rotate: modeled read-aloud lesson → evidence-and-theme practice →
  character/perspective analysis practice → review and discussion.
- Reuses (revised): `quizzes/narrative-elements-the-story-mountain-quiz.md`
  (plot-structure items only; extended-generation items reduced).

### Unit 03 — Informational text synthesis: structure and evidence (Weeks 11–14)

- **Standards:** RI.5.1–5.10; W.5.8; SL.5.3
- **Week 11 goal:** quote accurately for explicit meaning and inferences
  (RI.5.1); determine two or more main ideas, explain how key details support
  them, summarize (RI.5.2); relationships between individuals, events, ideas,
  or concepts (RI.5.3).
- **Week 12 goal:** academic and domain-specific words in context (RI.5.4);
  compare and contrast the overall structure of two or more texts —
  chronology, comparison, cause/effect, problem/solution (RI.5.5).
- **Week 13 goal:** multiple accounts of the same event — similarities and
  differences in point of view (RI.5.6); draw on multiple print or digital
  sources to locate answers efficiently (RI.5.7); explain how an author uses
  reasons and evidence, identifying which reasons and evidence support which
  point(s) (RI.5.8); integrate information from several texts (RI.5.9).
- **Week 14:** review week — summarize and paraphrase in notes and finished
  work; provide a list of sources (W.5.8); summarize a speaker's points and
  their supporting evidence (SL.5.3); formative check.
- Sessions rotate: informational read lesson → structure-and-source practice
  → evidence-and-synthesis practice → review.
- Reuses (revised; taught lessons first):
  `assignments/fact-opinion-topic-sentences.md`,
  `quizzes/main-idea-key-details-paraphrasing-quiz.md`,
  `quizzes/nonfiction-text-structures-quiz.md`,
  `templates/video-reviews.md`.

### Unit 04 — Grammar, sentence variety, conventions, and editing (Weeks 15–18)

- **Standards:** L.5.1.a–e; L.5.2.a–e; L.5.3.a–b; W.5.5; SL.5.6
- **Week 15 goal:** conjunctions, prepositions, and interjections — function
  in general and in particular sentences (L.5.1.a); correlative conjunctions
  — *either/or, neither/nor* (L.5.1.e); expand, combine, and reduce sentences
  for meaning and style (L.5.3.a).
- **Week 16 goal:** perfect verb tenses — *I had walked; I have walked; I
  will have walked* (L.5.1.b); verb tense for times, sequences, states, and
  conditions; correct inappropriate shifts in tense (L.5.1.c–d).
- **Week 17 goal:** punctuation to separate items in a series (L.5.2.a);
  commas for introductory elements, tag questions, and direct address
  (L.5.2.b–c); titles of works — underlining, quotation marks, or italics
  (L.5.2.d); grade-appropriate spelling with references (L.5.2.e); dialects
  and registers in stories, dramas, or poems (L.5.3.b); formal vs. informal
  English (SL.5.6).
- **Week 18:** midyear review (flexible) — cumulative reading, vocabulary,
  and conventions from Units 01–04; re-teach the highest-need objective;
  formative check.
- Sessions rotate: explicit grammar lesson → sentence-building practice →
  dictation and editing practice → review game.
- Reuses (revised):
  `quizzes/editing-conventions-commas-capitalization-quiz.md`,
  `quizzes/sentence-structures-fanboys-compound-sentences-quiz.md`.

### Unit 05 — Narrative craft: pacing, dialogue, and description (Weeks 19–22)

- **Standards:** W.5.3.a–e; W.5.4; W.5.5; W.5.10; L.5.2.b, L.5.2.d; L.5.3.a;
  RL.5.3
- **Week 19 goal:** establish a situation; introduce a narrator and/or
  characters; organize a natural event sequence (W.5.3.a); transitional
  words, phrases, and clauses to manage sequence (W.5.3.c).
- **Week 20 goal:** narrative techniques — dialogue, description, and pacing
  — to develop experiences and show character responses (W.5.3.b); dialogue
  punctuation conventions (L.5.2.b); compare characters' interactions as a
  craft model (RL.5.3).
- **Week 21 goal:** concrete words and phrases, sensory details (W.5.3.d); a
  conclusion that follows from the narrated experiences (W.5.3.e); expand and
  combine sentences for meaning and style (L.5.3.a); plan, revise, and edit
  with peer and adult guidance (W.5.5).
- **Week 22:** review week — clear, coherent writing appropriate to task,
  purpose, and audience (W.5.4); routine writing over short and extended
  time frames (W.5.10); formative check.
- Sessions rotate: writing-model lesson → guided drafting practice →
  dialogue-and-revision practice → share circle.
- Reuses (revised; taught lessons first):
  `assignments/dialogue-story-studio.md`,
  `quizzes/dialogue-rules-quotation-punctuation-quiz.md`,

### Unit 06 — Opinion writing: claims, reasons, and source evidence (Weeks 23–26)

- **Standards:** W.5.1.a–d; W.5.4; W.5.5; W.5.9.a–b; W.5.10; RL.5.1, RI.5.8;
  SL.5.1.a–d
- **Week 23 goal:** introduce the topic or text clearly, state an opinion,
  and create an organizational structure in which ideas are logically grouped
  (W.5.1.a).
- **Week 24 goal:** logically ordered reasons supported by facts and details
  (W.5.1.b); explain how an author uses reasons and evidence, matching each
  to its point (RI.5.8); draw evidence from texts (W.5.9.a–b; RL.5.1).
- **Week 25 goal:** link opinion and reasons — *consequently, specifically*
  (W.5.1.c); a concluding statement or section (W.5.1.d); develop and
  strengthen writing by planning, revising, and editing (W.5.5).
- **Week 26:** review week — test reasons in prepared collaborative
  discussions (SL.5.1.a–d); formative check.
- Sessions rotate: opinion-model lesson → reason-gathering practice →
  drafting practice → share-and-revise.
- Reuses (revised; taught lessons first):

### Unit 07 — Research: informational writing and citation foundations (Weeks 27–30)

- **Standards:** W.5.2.a–e; W.5.4; W.5.6; W.5.7; W.5.8; W.5.10; RI.5.7,
  RI.5.9; L.5.4.c; SL.5.2
- **Week 27 goal:** short research projects using several sources
  (W.5.7); recall or gather relevant information from print and digital
  sources; summarize or paraphrase in notes and finished work; list sources
  (W.5.8); consult dictionaries, glossaries, thesauruses for precise meaning

- **Week 28 goal:** introduce a topic with a general observation and focus;

- **Week 30:** review week — use technology to produce and publish;
  keyboard a minimum of two pages in a single sitting with some guidance

## 6. Retrieval and review cadence

Every unit's Week 4 re-teaches and re-checks that unit's objectives; each
unit opens with a retrieval warm-up from prior units (e.g., Unit 02 opens
with morphology review, Unit 04 with the quoting-text-evidence routine,
Unit 06 with sentence combining for opinion drafting, Unit 07 with
paraphrase review). Midyear (Week 18, inside Unit 04) and final (Weeks
35–36) weeks are full-track reviews. Formative checks are observed, oral,
drawn, or short written tasks (8–12 items) the adult reviews the same day;
each unit's teacher guide specifies what "ready to move on" looks like.

## 8. Safe materials

Household or dollar-store supplies: notebooks and pencils, word and affix
cards, syllable-sort trays, drawing paper and crayons, library books and
adult-made original passages, beginning dictionaries (print), pocket chart,
audio recorder (phone or simple device), timer. No sharp tools; adult
supervises small parts in shared settings. Read-aloud and research
selections previewed by the adult for age suitability and advertising-free
access; research sources are adult-selected print or digital pages, never
open web searching by the learner. Any quiz item whose factual claim is
uncertain (e.g., the showcase quiz's Amazon-oxygen figure) is verified or
replaced before use.

## 9. Accessibility supports

- Oral, pointing, and drawing response modes for all checks; adult scribes
  dictated writing when needed; independent written practice (8–12 tasks)
  reviewed the same day.
- Large-print, high-contrast word and affix cards; textured/tactile letters
  for low-vision learners.
- Short sessions with movement breaks; every lesson includes a seated-table
  and a floor-play variant.
- Language support: vocabulary taught with objects and pictures first, word
  second; visual word walls with root families; home-language labels welcomed
  alongside English.
- Every generated or drawn diagram ships with a text-only alternative; color
  is never the only cue.
- Hearing support: face the learner when modeling sounds; visual syllable
  and mouth-shape cues supplement auditory discrimination.
