## 🔑 Parent Answer Key (Educator)

> [!NOTE]
> *This section is for Educator (educator) to quickly check Learner’s work, celebrate victories, and review any tricky steps together.*

**Question 1:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** $V = l \times w \times h$ (or $V = B \times h$, where $B$ is base area).

**Question 2:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** $V = 6 \times 4 \times 5 = 120\text{ cm}^3$ (cubic centimeters).

**Question 3:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** $V = 35 \times 8 = 280\text{ cubic feet}$ ($\text{ft}^3$).

**Question 4:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** $V = 5 \times 5 \times 5 = 125\text{ in}^3$.

**Question 5:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** $V = 20 \times 10 \times 12 = 2{,}400\text{ in}^3$.

**Question 6:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** Base area $= 6 \times 4 = 24\text{ cm}^2$. Height $= 96 \div 24 = 4\text{ cm}$.

**Question 7:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** $7 \times 3 \times 2 = 42\text{ unit cubes}$.

**Question 8:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** Prism 1: $4 \times 3 \times 2 = 24$. Prism 2: $5 \times 3 \times 2 = 30$. Total Volume $= 24 + 30 = 54\text{ cubic units}$.

**Question 9:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** Box 1: $8 \times 5 \times 3 = 120\text{ cm}^3$. Box 2: $6 \times 6 \times 3 = 108\text{ cm}^3$. Box 1 holds more ($120 > 108$).

**Question 10:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** Cubic units (such as $\text{cm}^3$, $\text{in}^3$, $\text{m}^3$) because volume measures three-dimensional space.
