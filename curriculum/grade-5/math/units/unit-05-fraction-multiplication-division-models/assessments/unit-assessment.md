# Unit Assessment — Unit 05: Fraction Multiplication, Division, and Models

**Grade 5 Mathematics · Unit 05, Week 22 (Session S15) · 15 items ·
30 points (2 points each) · Objectives UO1–UO6**

Show your work for every item: the partition, the area model, the
conversion, or the multiplication check. A correct number with no
reasoning earns 1 point; a correct number with the reasoning shown
earns 2. Answers live only in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md).
This is a teacher-scored draft assessment, not a standardized or
validated test.

## Questions

**1.** (2 points) Find (2/3) × 9. Show the partition step (9 split
into 3 parts; take 2).

**2.** (2 points) Write a one-sentence story for (3/4) × 5, then find
the product.

**3.** (2 points) Find (3/5) × (4/7). Simplify completely.

**4.** (2 points) Draw or describe an area model for (1/2) × (2/3).
How many small rectangles tile the whole square, and how many are in
the overlap? Give the product.

**5.** (2 points) Find the area of a rectangle 5/8 meter long and 3/4
meter wide. Show the tiling count.

**6.** (2 points) Without multiplying, is (7/10) × 20 greater than,
less than, or equal to 20? Explain using the size of 7/10 next to 1.

**7.** (2 points) Without multiplying, is (6/5) × 9 greater than, less
than, or equal to 9? Explain, then compute to check.

**8.** (2 points) Find 1 3/4 × 4. Show the improper-fraction
conversion.

**9.** (2 points) Find 2 1/2 × 1 1/3. Simplify completely.

**10.** (2 points) One gift box needs 2 1/4 meters of ribbon. How
much ribbon do 3 gift boxes need? Show the multiplication.

**11.** (2 points) Find (1/4) ÷ 5. Check your answer with
multiplication.

**12.** (2 points) Find 6 ÷ (1/3). Check your answer with
multiplication.

**13.** (2 points) Write a one-sentence story for 3 ÷ (1/4), then
find the quotient. (Hint: the story must ask "how many quarter
servings fit in 3 wholes?")

**14.** (2 points) Compute both: 4 ÷ (1/2) and (1/2) ÷ 4. Explain in
one sentence why the answers are different.

**15.** (2 points) A pitcher holds 3 quarts of juice. (a) Each cup
holds 1/4 quart. How many full cups can you pour? (b) After serving,
1/2 quart remains. Five friends share it equally. How many quarts
does each friend get?
