# Project — Fraction Feast: Recipe Scaling Studio

**Grade 5 · Unit 05 project · Sessions S11–S12 (build) + S13 (present) ·
Objectives UO1–UO6**

This project builds on the legacy assignment
[Fraction Feast & Recipe Scaling Studio](../../assignments/fraction-operations-studio.md)
(keep decision in the track scope-and-sequence): its flatbread and
lemonade tasks are the project's core computations, extended here
with a scaling prediction, a serving plan, and an area-model garden
bed. Learners do the legacy tasks with the models and checks learned
in Lessons 01–05 — they do not copy the legacy answers.

## The job

A community group is hosting a Friday Family Game Night banquet. You
are the Head Chef. Your jobs:

1. **Scale the flatbread recipe** for 2 batches (doubling).
2. **Plan the lemonade pitcher**: how many celebration cups it pours,
   and how the leftover is shared.
3. **Lay out a garden bed** for the banquet salad: draw its area model
   and find its area.
4. **Present** your feast card and explain every computation with a
   model or a check.

## Materials

Measuring cups (1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 3/4), fraction strips, grid paper,
two crayons, a pitcher (or a drawing of one), the recipe card below.

## The recipes (given — do not change the amounts)

**Artisan Flatbread Pizza (one batch):** flour 2 1/2 cups · warm water
3/4 cup · olive oil 1/3 cup · shredded mozzarella 1 2/3 cups ·
grated parmesan 3/8 cup.

**Fresh Lemonade Pitcher:** 4 quarts of lemonade. Each celebration
cup holds exactly 1/4 quart.

## Steps

### Session S11 — Scale and serve (project session 1)

1. **Doubling the dough.** Multiply the flour (2 1/2 cups) by 2 and
   the warm water (3/4 cup) by 2. Show the improper-fraction
   conversion for the flour. Predict with scaling first: 2 batches
   is more than 1 batch, so each amount must grow.
2. **Cheese blend (optional stretch).** The mozzarella (1 2/3 cups)
   and parmesan (3/8 cup) are combined in one bowl — that addition
   is Unit 04 work; show it on the card as a "from Unit 04" line so
   the card is complete.
3. **Serving plan.** How many 1/4-quart celebration cups can you pour
   from the 4-quart pitcher? Write the division (4 ÷ 1/4) and the
   multiplication check. After serving, 1/3 quart remains: share it
   equally among three people and write each person's share
   ((1/3) ÷ 3) with its check.
4. **Garden bed.** The salad bed is 4/5 meter long and 2/3 meter
   wide. Draw its area model on grid paper (5 columns × 3 rows),
   shade the overlap, and find the area. Use the garden picture in
   Lesson 02 as the story prompt — the picture gives no numbers; the
   numbers above are the task.

### Session S12 — Feast card and rehearsal (project session 2)

5. **Feast card.** Make one card with: the doubled recipe amounts,
   the serving plan (cups poured + leftover shares), the garden-bed
   area with its model, and one scaling sentence ("multiplying by 2
   grows the recipe because 2 is greater than 1; taking 1/4-quart
   servings from 4 quarts gives more servings than quarts because
   each serving is smaller than 1 quart").
6. **Check every line** with the multiplication/division relationship
   (Lesson 05) or a scaling prediction (Lesson 03). Fix any line
   that fails its check.
7. **Rehearse** a two-minute presentation: one computation, its model,
   and its check — out loud, to the adult.

### Session S13 — Present

Present the feast card. Peers ask one question each; at least one
question must be a scaling question ("why is the product smaller
than the recipe amount?").

## Deliverables

1. **Scaled recipe card** — doubled flour and water with conversions
   shown; cheese blend line labeled "from Unit 04."
2. **Serving plan** — cups poured (with division + check) and leftover
   shares (with division + check).
3. **Garden-bed area model** — grid drawing with shaded overlap and
   area.
4. **Scaling sentence** — one sentence explaining a grow/shrink
   result with the "greater than 1 / less than 1" reason.
5. **Two-minute presentation** — one computation, its model, its check.

## Rubric

| Criteria | 3 — Feast ready | 2 — Almost there | 1 — Needs another pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scaling the recipe (UO4)** | Both doubled amounts correct with improper-fraction conversions shown; scaling prediction stated | One amount correct, or conversions missing | Amounts wrong or no conversion shown |
| **Serving plan (UO5–UO6)** | Both divisions correct (cups poured and leftover shares) with multiplication checks | One division correct, or checks missing | Divisions confused (wrong order or operation) |
| **Area model (UO2)** | Garden bed drawn as a 5 × 3 grid, overlap shaded, area 8/15 m² correct | Model drawn but area or overlap wrong | No model, or area found without a model |
| **Explanation (UO3)** | Scaling sentence correct with the greater-than-1 / less-than-1 reason; presentation explains one computation, model, and check | Sentence or presentation missing the reason | No explanation beyond the numbers |
| **Accuracy and care** | All fractions simplified; units (cups, quarts, m²) named on every answer | One unsimplified fraction or one missing unit | Several unsimplified answers or missing units |

**Scoring:** 13–15 points = feast ready; 9–12 = almost there, fix the
checked lines and re-present; below 9 = reteach with Lessons 01, 02,
and 05 before presenting.
