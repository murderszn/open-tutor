## Lesson 01 — A fraction times a whole number

**Readiness:** 1. 8 and 2. 2. 8/3 = 2 2/3. 3. 3 apples each; 12 ÷ 4 = 3.

**Guided:** G1. **5** (10 split into 2 parts is 5 each; take 1 part).
G2. **6** (15 split into 5 parts is 3 each; take 2 parts: 2 × 3 = 6).
G3. Sam is wrong: multiplying the denominator by 8 does not take
3/4 of 8 — it shrinks the fraction. Correct: (3 × 8) ÷ 4 = 24 ÷ 4 =
**6**. (Partition: 8 split into 4 parts is 2 each; take 3 parts:
3 × 2 = 6.)

**Independent:** I1. **6**. I2. **6**. I3. **10**. I4. **5**. I5. **12**;
acceptable story: "A baker has 15 cups of flour and uses 4/5 of it;
how much does she use?" I6. Acceptable: "You split 4 into 3 parts
and take only 2 of them, so you keep less than the whole 4. The
product 8/3 = 2 2/3 is less than 4."

**Application:** (2/3) × 5 = 10/3 = **3 1/3 cups**. Partition: 5 split
into 3 parts is 5/3 each; take 2 parts: 2 × (5/3) = 10/3.

**Exit:** 1. **6** (8 split into 4 parts is 2 each; take 3: 3 × 2 =
6). 2. Acceptable story: "Six friends share half of a pizza tray;
how much is that?" — (1/2) × 6 = **3**.

**Extension:** (2/3) × 4 = 8/3; half of 8/3 is (1/2) × (8/3) = 8/6 =
**4/3**.

## Lesson 02 — A fraction times a fraction: area models

**Readiness:** 1. 6 and 12. 2. **8/3 = 2 2/3**. 3. **6 square units**
(3 × 2 tiles).

**Guided:** G1. **1/12**: the whole square has 3 × 4 = 12 small
rectangles; the overlap is 1. G2. **2/5** (cancel the 3s:
(1/5) × (2/1) = 2/5; or 6/15 = 2/5). G3. Ana is wrong: the
denominators multiply — the whole square is split into 2 × 3 = 6
small rectangles, and the overlap is 1 × 2 = 2 of them. Correct:
(1/2) × (2/3) = 2/6 = **1/3**.

**Independent:** I1. **1/5** (2/10). I2. **3/10** (6/20). I3. **1/4**
(cancel first: (1/2) × (1/2) = 1/4; or 15/60 = 1/4). I4. **5/8
square meters**: tiling count 4 × 6 = 24 tiles, 3 × 5 = 15 covered,
15/24 = 5/8. I5. **4/9** (28/63; cancel the 7s: (4/1) × (1/9) =
4/9). I6. Acceptable: "You take a fraction of each factor — 2/3 of
3/4 keeps only part of 3/4, and 3/4 of 2/3 keeps only part of 2/3 —
so the product is smaller than both factors."

**Garden activity:** 5 × 3 = **15** small rectangles tile the bed;
4 × 2 = **8** are in the overlap; area = **8/15 square meters**.

**Application:** 3 1/2 = 7/2; (7/2) × 2 = 14/2 = **7 square feet**.

**Exit:** 1. **1/6** (2/12). 2. **3/10 square meters** (6/20).

**Extension:** (2/3) × (3/4) = 1/2; (1/2) × (1/2) = **1/4**. Both
ways agree because multiplication groups the same way: the three-way
model has 3 × 4 × 2 = 24 small pieces and the overlap is 2 × 3 × 1 =
6 of them, 6/24 = 1/4.

## Lesson 03 — Scaling: bigger, smaller, or the same?

**Readiness:** 1. 3/4 < 1; 5/4 > 1. 2. **6** and **10**. 3. Less than
0.4: 0.4 × 0.7 = **0.28**, and 0.7 < 1.

**Guided:** G1. **Less than 7** (4/5 < 1, so you keep only part of 7).
G2. **Greater than 6** (9/8 > 1, so you take more than all of 6).
G3. Leo is wrong: taking 1/2 of 8 keeps half of it. (1/2) × 8 = **4**,
which is less than 8.

**Independent:** I1. **Less; 4**. I2. **Greater; 15**. I3. **Equal; 9**.
I4. **Less; 14**. I5. **Greater; 5 1/2**. I6. Acceptable: "(2/3) × 18
takes 2 of the 3 parts of 18, so 12 — less than 18. (4/3) × 18 takes
4 of the 3 parts of 18, so 24 — more than one whole 18."

**Application:** 1. Maya makes **less** than 4 cups (2/5 < 1); Noah
makes **more** than 4 cups (7/5 > 1). 2. Maya: (2/5) × 4 = 8/5 =
**1 3/5 cups**. Noah: (7/5) × 4 = 28/5 = **5 3/5 cups**.

**Exit:** 1. **Less than 16** (3/4 < 1; product 12). 2. **Greater
than 16** (5/4 > 1; product 20).

**Extension:** (3/2) × (2/3) = 6/6 = **1**. Scaling words: 3/2 grows
a number by half, 2/3 shrinks it by a third, and the two undo each
other exactly — growing by 3/2 then keeping 2/3 of the result returns
the starting number.

## Lesson 04 — Mixed numbers and real-world problems

**Readiness:** 1. **5/2**; **4/3**. 2. **5**. 3. **3/2 = 1 1/2**.

**Guided:** G1. **6 3/4** (2 1/4 = 9/4; (9/4) × 3 = 27/4 = 6 3/4).
G2. **3 1/2** (1 2/5 = 7/5; 2 1/2 = 5/2; cancel the 5s:
(7/1) × (1/2) = 7/2 = 3 1/2). G3. Priya is wrong: 1 1/2 is one
number, 3/2 — multiplying the whole part only forgets the half.
Correct: (3/2) × 2 = **3**.

**Independent:** I1. **10** (3 1/3 = 10/3; (10/3) × 3 = 10). I2.
**7/10** (1 3/4 = 7/4; (7/4) × (2/5) = 14/20 = 7/10). I3. **4**
(2 2/3 = 8/3; 1 1/2 = 3/2; (8/3) × (3/2) = 24/6 = 4). I4. **9
meters** (2 1/4 × 4 = (9/4) × 4 = 9). I5. **7 square feet**
((7/2) × 2 = 7). I6. Acceptable: "1 1/2 = 3/2 and 2 1/2 = 5/2, so
the product is 15/4 = 3 3/4. It is greater than 2 1/2 because the
other factor, 1 1/2, is greater than 1."

**Application:** 1. (3/4) × 6 = 18/4 = **4 1/2 pounds**. 2. (2/3) ×
(4 1/2) = (2/3) × (9/2) = 18/6 = **3 pounds**.

**Exit:** 1. **6** ((3/2) × 4 = 12/2 = 6). 2. **1 5/6** (2 3/4 =
11/4; (11/4) × (2/3) = 22/12 = 11/6 = 1 5/6).

**Extension:** Flour: (5/2) × 3 = 15/2 = **7 1/2 cups**. Water:
(3/4) × 3 = 9/4 = **2 1/4 cups**.

## Lesson 05 — Dividing unit fractions

**Readiness:** 1. **1/3**; **4**. 2. **2**; 4 is being shared, 2 is
the number of groups. 3. 1 ÷ **3**.

**Guided:** G1. **1/6** (one half split into 3 equal parts; each part
is 1/6 of the whole). G2. **6** (each whole holds 2 halves; 3 wholes
hold 6). G3. Mia is wrong: 2 × (1/3) is a multiplication, not the
servings question. Correct: 2 ÷ (1/3) = **6** — each whole holds 3
thirds, so 2 wholes hold 6 thirds. Check: 6 × (1/3) = 2. ✓

**Independent:** I1. **1/8**. I2. **1/18**. I3. **15**. I4. **12
half-bar pieces** (6 ÷ (1/2) = 12). I5. **1/12 gallon**
((1/3) ÷ 4 = 1/12). I6. **20 patties** (5 ÷ (1/4) = 20).

**Application:** 1. 4 ÷ (1/4) = **16 cups**. 2. (1/3) ÷ 3 = **1/9
quart** per person. 3. Acceptable: "The first answer is bigger than
4 because each serving is smaller than 1 quart, so more servings fit
than quarts. The second answer is smaller than 1/3 because sharing
splits the leftover into smaller pieces."

**Exit:** 1. **1/8**. 2. **8**; check: 8 × (1/4) = 2. ✓

**Extension:** 3 ÷ (1/4) = **12** ("how many quarter servings fit
in 3 wholes?"); (1/4) ÷ 3 = **1/12** ("one quarter shared among 3
people?"). Acceptable stories for each.
