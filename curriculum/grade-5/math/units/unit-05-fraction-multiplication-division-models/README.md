# Unit 05 — Fraction Multiplication, Division, and Models

**Grade 5 Mathematics · Weeks 19–22 · 16 sessions (~40 minutes each, 4 per week)**

**Standards:** 5.NF.B.4.a–b, 5.NF.B.5.a–b, 5.NF.B.6, 5.NF.B.7.a–c (with
5.NF.A.1–A.2 fraction-addition foundations from Unit 04 and 5.NBT.B.7
decimal-scaling ideas from Unit 03)

## Overview

Unit 04 taught learners to add and subtract fractions by first making
the pieces the same size. This unit teaches the opposite surprise:
multiplication does *not* need a common denominator — it needs a
meaning. Multiplying a fraction by a whole number means taking a part
of a partition: (2/3) × 4 is two parts out of three equal parts of 4.
Multiplying two fractions means taking a fraction *of* a fraction, and
an area model shows why the answer is (ac)/(bd). Then comes the idea
that changes how learners judge answers: multiplying by a fraction
less than 1 makes a number *smaller* (scaling), and multiplying by a
fraction greater than 1 makes it bigger. The unit closes with the two
divisions this grade owns — a unit fraction shared among whole people,
and a whole number measured out in unit-fraction servings — where the
order of the numbers changes the question entirely. The Fraction Feast
project makes learners do all of it at once: scale a recipe, price
servings, and explain every step with a model.

## Essential question

*When I multiply or divide with fractions, what does the operation
mean — and can I tell whether my answer should be bigger or smaller
before I compute it?*

## Prerequisites (from Units 01–04 and Grade 4)

- Equivalent fractions and fraction bars (Unit 04): renaming with a
  common denominator; the whole must stay the same
- Multiplication of a fraction by a whole number (Grade 4):
  (2/3) × 4 as 2 groups of 4 thirds — extended here to the partition
  meaning (a/b) × q
- Decimal scaling from Unit 03: 0.4 × 0.7 = 0.28 is smaller than
  either factor — the same scaling idea, now with fractions
- Fraction as division (Unit 04, Lesson 05): a/b = a ÷ b, and the
  multiplication/division relationship used to check answers
- Multi-digit multiplication and division fluency (Unit 02)

The Week 19 opening session re-checks these; nothing new is assumed.

## Verified standards / source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**,
Grade 5, Number and Operations — Fractions, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/Math](https://thecorestandards.org/Math) (Grade 5
NF page; codes and descriptions re-verified 2026-11-06 against the
official page and the LibreTexts grade-5 standards overview).

- **5.NF.B.4** — "Apply and extend previous understandings of
  multiplication to multiply a fraction or whole number by a fraction."
- **5.NF.B.4.a** — "Interpret the product (a/b) × q as a parts of a
  partition of q into b equal parts; equivalently, as the result of a
  sequence of operations a × q ÷ b. *For example, use a visual fraction
  model to show (2/3) × 4 = 8/3, and create a story context for this
  equation. Do the same with (2/3) × (4/5) = 8/15. (In general,
  (a/b) × (c/d) = (ac)/(bd).)*"
- **5.NF.B.4.b** — "Find the area of a rectangle with fractional side
  lengths by tiling it with unit squares of the appropriate unit
  fraction side lengths, and show that the area is the same as would
  be found by multiplying the side lengths. Multiply fractional side
  lengths to find areas of rectangles, and represent fraction products
  as rectangular areas."
- **5.NF.B.5** — "Interpret multiplication as scaling (resizing), by:"
  **5.NF.B.5.a** — "Comparing the size of a product to the size of one
  factor on the basis of the size of the other factor, without
  performing the indicated multiplication." **5.NF.B.5.b** —
  "Explaining why multiplying a given number by a fraction greater
  than 1 results in a product greater than the given number
  (recognizing multiplication by whole numbers greater than 1 as a
  familiar case); explaining why multiplying a given number by a
  fraction less than 1 results in a product smaller than the given
  number; and relating the principle of fraction equivalence
  a/b = (n × a)/(n × b) to the effect of multiplying a/b by 1."
- **5.NF.B.6** — "Solve real world problems involving multiplication
  of fractions and mixed numbers, e.g., by using visual fraction
  models or equations to represent the problem."
- **5.NF.B.7** — "Apply and extend previous understandings of
  division to divide unit fractions by whole numbers and whole numbers
  by unit fractions." **5.NF.B.7.a** — "Interpret division of a unit
  fraction by a non-zero whole number, and compute such quotients.
  *For example, create a story context for (1/3) ÷ 4, and use a visual
  fraction model to show the quotient. Use the relationship between
  multiplication and division to explain that (1/3) ÷ 4 = 1/12 because
  (1/12) × 4 = 1/3.*" **5.NF.B.7.b** — "Interpret division of a whole
  number by a unit fraction, and compute such quotients. *For example,
  create a story context for 4 ÷ (1/5), and use a visual fraction model
  to show the quotient. Use the relationship between multiplication and
  division to explain that 4 ÷ (1/5) = 20 because 20 × (1/5) = 4.*"
  **5.NF.B.7.c** — "Solve real world problems involving division of
  unit fractions by non-zero whole numbers and division of whole
  numbers by unit fractions, e.g., by using visual fraction models and
  equations to represent the problem."

**Scope note (honest boundary):** this unit teaches only the two
divisions the grade-5 standard owns: unit fraction ÷ whole number and
whole number ÷ unit fraction. Dividing a fraction by a fraction (for
example, 1/2 ÷ 3/4) is **not** taught here — it belongs to grade 6.
The "flip the second fraction and multiply" rule in
`resources/math_fundamentals.md` (Fractions section) is a general
fraction-division shortcut, not the grade-5 method; this unit never
uses it, and the resource pack flags it as out of scope. Cancelling
(dividing out a common factor before multiplying) is taught only as a
simplification shortcut in Lesson 02, after the multiply-across rule
is understood.

## Differentiation and accessibility

- **Reading/language access:** every lesson states its goal in one sentence
  before any numbers appear; key words (*partition*, *scaling*,
  *serving*, *order matters*) are pre-taught with a picture or gesture.
- **Alternate response modes:** learners may answer orally, point to an
  area model or fraction strip, or dictate steps to the adult; written
  answers are never the only evidence.
- **Supports:** fraction strips, paper bars, hundredths grids (reused from
  Unit 03), and a scaling card (">1 grows · <1 shrinks · =1 stays") are
  available in every session; no timed speed tests anywhere in this unit.
- **Extensions:** three-factor products (Lessons 01–02), a scaling-proof
  task (Lesson 03), a tripled-recipe extension (Lesson 04), and a
  "which division is which?" extension (Lesson 05) are optional and
  never gate the required objectives.
