# Lesson 05 — Dividing Unit Fractions

**Grade 5 · Unit 05, Lesson 05 · ~40 minutes · Objectives UO5–UO6**

**Goal (in one sentence):** I can divide a unit fraction by a whole
number and a whole number by a unit fraction — and I can tell the two
apart, because the order changes the question.

**Materials:** fraction strips or paper bars, measuring cups and a
pitcher (or drawings of them).

**Readiness check (answer before the lesson):**

1. What is (1/12) × 4? What is 20 × (1/5)?
2. Find 4 ÷ 2. In the division, which number is being shared, and
   which number says how many groups?
3. Write 1/3 as a division: 1 ÷ __.

## Teaching

Division asks two different questions, and with fractions the order
of the numbers decides which question you are asking:

- **(1/3) ÷ 4 — "divvie up" (sharing):** one-third of something is
  shared equally among 4 people. Each person gets a *smaller* piece:
  1/3 cut into 4 equal parts is 1/12. The divisor (4) tells how many
  groups; the answer is smaller than the starting fraction.
- **4 ÷ (1/5) — "measure out" (how many fit?):** four wholes are
  measured out in one-fifth-size servings. Each whole holds 5 fifths,
  so 4 wholes hold 4 × 5 = 20 servings. The divisor (1/5) tells the
  size of each serving; the answer is *bigger* than the starting
  whole number.

Both answers are checked the same way — with multiplication, the
inverse operation:

> If (1/3) ÷ 4 = 1/12, then (1/12) × 4 must equal 1/3. It does. ✓
> If 4 ÷ (1/5) = 20, then 20 × (1/5) must equal 4. It does. ✓

**The rule to remember:** sharing a fraction makes smaller pieces;
measuring a whole in tiny servings makes many servings. And **order
matters**: 4 ÷ (1/2) = 8 asks "how many halves fit in 4 wholes?",
while (1/2) ÷ 4 = 1/8 asks "what is one half shared among 4?" — two
different questions, two different answers.

### Worked Example 1 — (1/3) ÷ 4 = 1/12 (sharing)

One-third quart of lemonade is left in a pitcher. Four friends share
it equally. How much does each friend get?

Split 1/3 into 4 equal parts. The whole quart was split into 3 parts
to make thirds, and each third is now split into 4 — so the whole is
split into 3 × 4 = 12 equal parts. Each friend gets **1/12 quart**.

Check: (1/12) × 4 = 4/12 = 1/3. ✓

### Worked Example 2 — 4 ÷ (1/5) = 20 (measuring out)

A pitcher holds 4 quarts of lemonade. Each cup holds 1/5 quart. How
many full cups can you pour?

Each quart holds 5 fifths, so 4 quarts hold 4 × 5 = **20 cups**.

Check: 20 × (1/5) = 20/5 = 4. ✓

### Worked Example 3 — Order matters: 4 ÷ (1/2) vs (1/2) ÷ 4

- 4 ÷ (1/2) = 8: "How many halves fit in 4 wholes?" Each whole has
  2 halves, so 4 wholes have 8 halves.
- (1/2) ÷ 4 = 1/8: "One half shared among 4 people?" Each person
  gets 1/8 of a whole.

Same numbers, opposite order, different questions — 8 vs 1/8. Always
ask first: *am I sharing, or am I measuring out servings?*

## Guided practice

**G1.** Find (1/2) ÷ 3. Draw the sharing: one half split into 3
equal parts. What fraction of the whole is each part?

**G2.** Find 3 ÷ (1/2). Ask: how many halves fit in 3 wholes?

**G3. Error analysis.** Mia says 2 ÷ (1/3) = 2/3 "because 2 × (1/3)
= 2/3." Is she right? Explain the mistake, and give the correct
answer with a servings reason.

## Application

A giant pitcher holds 4 quarts of fresh lemonade (the Fraction Feast
pitcher from the legacy studio).

1. Each celebration cup holds exactly 1/4 quart. How many full cups
   can you pour? Model and solve 4 ÷ (1/4).
2. After serving, 1/3 quart remains. Share it equally among three
   people. How many quarts does each person get? Model and solve
   (1/3) ÷ 3.
3. Explain in one sentence why the first answer is bigger than 4 and
   the second answer is smaller than 1/3.

## Exit check

1. Find (1/2) ÷ 4.
2. Find 2 ÷ (1/4). Check your answer with multiplication.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** act out each division with fraction strips before
  writing: sharing cuts a strip into more pieces; measuring counts
  how many small pieces fill the wholes.
- **Alternate response mode:** learners may draw the sharing or the
  servings and dictate the multiplication check to the adult.
- **Extension:** which is greater, 3 ÷ (1/4) or (1/4) ÷ 3? Compute
  both (12 vs 1/12) and write the two questions in words, then invent
  a story for each.

## Teacher note

- **Likely misconception:** multiplying instead of dividing (G3) —
  "2 × (1/3) = 2/3" answers a multiplication question, not "how many
  thirds fit in 2?" Fix: ask the servings question out loud first.
- **Likely misconception:** swapping dividend and divisor — the Q10
  pair (4 ÷ 1/2 vs 1/2 ÷ 4) is the cure; keep it visible all week.
- **Likely misconception:** using the "flip and multiply" shortcut.
  It is not the grade-5 method and is not taught in this unit; the
  multiplication *check* (quotient × divisor = dividend) is the
  verification tool instead.
