# Unit 01 — Culminating Assessment

**Standard focus:** 5.NBT.A.1, 5.NBT.A.2, 5.NBT.A.3.a–b, 5.NBT.A.4 · **All unit
objectives:** UO1–UO5 · **Session:** S16

**Instructions:** Answer all 18 questions plus the extended reasoning task.
Show your work: name place values, show digit shifts, and write one sentence of
reasoning wherever you explain. Take your time — accuracy over speed.

> **Note to the adult:** the answer key is kept separately in
> [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment) — never beside the
> learner's questions. This assessment is a teacher-made check, not a
> standardized or validated test.

## Questions

### 1 — Digit values (UO1)
In **88,000**, how does the value of the left 8 compare to the value of the
right 8? Use "10 times" or "1/10".

### 2 — Digit values in decimals (UO1)
In **2.22**, the 2 in the tenths place is worth how many times the 2 in the
hundredths place? Explain with the adjacent-place rule.

### 3 — Number names (UO2)
Write **0.73** in word form.

### 4 — Standard form (UO2)
Write "forty and five thousandths" in standard decimal form.

### 5 — Expanded form (UO2)
Write **6.019** in expanded form (fraction form or decimal form — label your
choice).

### 6 — Powers of ten (UO3)
Evaluate **45 × 10³**. Show the exponent-to-whole-number step.

### 7 — Powers of ten with decimals (UO3)
Evaluate **3.05 × 100**. Describe where each digit moved.

### 8 — Dividing by powers of ten (UO3)
Evaluate **7,600 ÷ 10²**. Check by multiplying back.

### 9 — Dividing decimals (UO3)
Evaluate **0.9 ÷ 10³**.

### 10 — Rounding to tenths (UO4)
Round **5.678** to the nearest tenth. Name the deciding digit.

### 11 — Rounding to hundredths (UO4)
Round **5.678** to the nearest hundredth. Name the deciding digit.

### 12 — Rounding with carrying (UO4)
Round **9.995** to the nearest tenth. Show the carrying step.

### 13 — Rounding to thousandths (UO4)
Round **4.4444** to the nearest thousandth. Explain why the fourth decimal
place does not change the answer.

### 14 — Comparing decimals (UO5)
Compare with <, >, or =: **0.345 ___ 0.354**. Name the deciding place.

### 15 — Ordering decimals (UO5)
Order from least to greatest: **0.9, 0.89, 0.899**. Justify each link.

### 16 — Error analysis (UO4)
Troy rounds 8.446 to the nearest tenth and gets 8.5. "The two 4s add to 8,"
he says, "so it rounds up." Explain his mistake and give the correct answer.

### 17 — Application (UO3)
A sticker pack holds 10 stickers and costs $2.40. The club orders 100 packs.
What is the total cost? Show the power-of-ten shift.

### 18 — True or false, with explanation (UO3)
"10⁵ = 50,000." True or false? Explain using the meaning of the exponent.

## Extended reasoning task (all objectives)

**Amara's claim.** Amara says: "Multiplying any decimal by 100 always gives a
whole number."

(a) Give a **counterexample**: one decimal that, when multiplied by 100, is
*not* a whole number. Show the multiplication and the digit shift.
(b) Explain **why** your counterexample works, using what happens to the
hundredths and thousandths digits when every digit shifts two places left.
(c) State **when** Amara's claim *is* true: for which decimals does ×100 give
a whole number? Explain.
