## Lesson 01

**Readiness:** 1. "two hundred forty-six thousand eight hundred ten."
2. 40,000 (ten-thousands place). 3. No — e.g., the leftmost 7 = 7,000 but the
rightmost 7 = 7 ones; same digit, different values.

**G1.** Leftmost 2 = 200,000 (hundred-thousands); middle 2 = 20,000
(ten-thousands). 200,000 = 10 × 20,000, so the leftmost 2 is worth **10 times**
the middle 2.

**G2.** Ones 3 = 3; tenths 3 = 3/10 = 0.3. 3 = 10 × 0.3, so the ones 3 is worth
**10 times** the tenths 3 (equivalently, the tenths 3 is worth 1/10 of the
ones 3).

**G3.** "…worth **10** times the same digit in the tens place. A digit in the
ones place is worth **1/10** of the same digit in the tens place."

**G4.** Lena is wrong. Left 6 = 600 (hundreds); right 6 = 6 (ones);
600 ÷ 6 = 100, so the left 6 is **100 times** the right 6. The 10× rule
connects *neighboring* places only — these 6s are two places apart, so it is
10 × 10 = 100.

**I1.** Leftmost 9 = 9,000 (thousands); next 9 = 900 (hundreds);
9,000 = 10 × 900 → **10 times**.

**I2.** Hundred-thousands 1 = 100,000; thousands 1 = 1,000;
100,000 ÷ 1,000 = 100 → **100 times** greater.

**I3.** Sample: **33,000** — left 3 = 30,000 (ten-thousands), right 3 = 3,000
(thousands); 30,000 = 10 × 3,000. They are one place apart (adjacent), so the
10× rule applies. (Any number with adjacent repeated digits and a correct
multiplication check is acceptable.)

**I4.** Tenths 7 = 0.7; hundredths 7 = 0.07; 0.7 = 10 × 0.07 → **10 times**.
Tenths and hundredths are adjacent places, so the rule holds across the
decimal point.

**I5.** **True.** Hundred-thousands 5 = 500,000; thousands 5 = 5,000;
500,000 ÷ 5,000 = 100. Two place-steps apart → 10 × 10 = 100.

**Application.** Sample: **466,400** — the 4 in the hundred-thousands place =
400,000; the 4 in the ten-thousands place = 40,000; 10 × 40,000 = 400,000, so
the left 4 is 10 times the right 4. (Any 6-digit number with a repeated digit,
both values, and a correct multiplication/division check is acceptable.)

**Exit.** The first 5 = 5 tenths = 0.5; the second 5 = 5 hundredths = 0.05;
0.5 = 10 × 0.05. The tenths digit is worth **10 times** the hundredths digit.

**Extension.** Adjacent pair, e.g., hundred-thousands 1 = 100,000 and
ten-thousands 1 = 10,000: 10 × 10,000 = 100,000 ✓. Leftmost 1 = 1,000,000,
rightmost 1 = 1; 1,000,000 ÷ 1 = 1,000,000 = 10 × 10 × 10 × 10 × 10 × 10 — six
10× steps, one per place-step between the seven digits.

## Lesson 03

**Readiness:** 1. 100; 1,000. 2. Any honest prediction. 3. 10 × 10 × 10.

**G1.** 7 × 1,000 = **7,000**. The 7 moved from the ones place to the
thousands place: 7 ones became 7 thousands (each ×10 shifts one place; three
tens multiplied = three shifts).

**G2.** 10² = 100. 2.5 × 100 = **250**: the 2 moved ones → hundreds, the 5
moved tenths → tens.

**G3.** 10³ = 1,000. 89 ÷ 1,000 = **0.089**: the 8 moved tens → hundredths,
the 9 moved ones → thousandths.

**G4.** Dividing by 100 must make each digit *smaller* — 46 is bigger than 4.6,
so the shift went the wrong way. Correct: shift two places right — 4 ones →
4 hundredths (0.04), 6 tenths → 6 thousandths (0.006): **0.046**.

**I1.** 10⁴ = 10,000; 12 × 10,000 = **120,000**.

**I2.** 10³ = 1,000; 0.35 × 1,000 = **350** (3 tenths → hundreds, 5 hundredths
→ tens).

**I3.** **10²** (100). 6.8 × 100 = 680: both digits shifted two places left
(6 ones → hundreds, 8 tenths → tens). Found by asking "how many places must
the digits move to turn 6.8 into 680?" — two, so the power is 10².

**I4.** 10³ = 1,000; 40,000 ÷ 1,000 = **40**. Check: 40 × 1,000 = 40,000 ✓.

**I5.** 10³ = 10 × 10 × 10. By Lesson 01, each ×10 shifts every digit one place
left; three ×10s shift three places. Saying "the point moves right three
places" is shorthand for the digits moving left three places.

**Application (legacy task).** 36 × 100 = **3,600** (3: tens → thousands;
6: ones → hundreds). 7 × 1,000 = **7,000** (7: ones → thousands).
4.2 × 100 = **420** (4: ones → hundreds; 2: tenths → tens). Sample
missing-power problem: 5.2 × ___ = 5,200 → **10³**; check: 5 ones → thousands,
2 tenths → hundreds; 5,200 ÷ 5.2 = 1,000 ✓. (Any solvable missing-power
problem with a correct power-of-ten answer is acceptable.)

**Exit.** 9.1 × 100 = **910**: 9 moved ones → hundreds; 1 moved tenths → tens.

**Extension.** (a) 250,000 = 25 × 10⁴ = 2.5 × 10⁵ (other valid forms accepted
with the exponent counting zeros correctly). (b) 3 × 100 + 4 × 10 + 5 × 1 =
**345** — yes, the same; 10⁰ = 1, from the pattern 10³ = 1,000, 10² = 100,
10¹ = 10, each step ÷10, so the next step is 10 ÷ 10 = 1. (c) 4.9 × 10³ =
4,900; 49 × 10² = 4,900; 490 × 10¹ = 4,900 — **all three are equal**.
