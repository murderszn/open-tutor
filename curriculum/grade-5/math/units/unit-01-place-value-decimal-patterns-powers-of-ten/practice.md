# Practice Bank — Unit 01

Supplemental practice and games keyed to the 16 sessions in
[README.md](README.md#pacing--16-sessions). All answers and solution notes are
in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md#practice-bank). Materials are safe household
items: paper, pencil, digit cards, a drawn place-value mat, coins or buttons as
counters. Fluency work is **strategy-based — no timed speed tests**.

## Game 1 — Place-value dictation

*(Sessions 04, 15)*

**Players:** 2 (learner + adult/partner). **Materials:** paper, pencil.

1. Player A reads a decimal aloud in **words** (e.g., "four and nineteen
   thousandths"); Player B writes the **numeral**.
2. Player B reads it back from the numeral; Player A confirms the words match.
3. Swap roles. Play 5 rounds each; a round scores when the numeral, the
   read-back, and an expanded-form check all agree.

**Success:** 4 of 5 rounds correct with matching expanded form.

**Starter word lists (mix and match):** "two and six tenths" · "nine
hundredths" · "five and forty-one thousandths" · "thirty and three hundredths"
· "eight hundred seven and two thousandths" · "one tenth" · "sixty and six
tenths" · "twelve thousandths".

## Game 2 — Powers-of-ten slider

*(Sessions 06, 14, 15)*

**Players:** 1–2. **Materials:** place-value mat, digit cards or paper strips.

1. Build a starting number on the mat (e.g., 3.6).
2. Draw a power-of-ten card: ×10, ×100, ×1,000, ÷10, ÷100, ÷1,000, ×10², ÷10³…
3. **Physically slide** each digit card the correct number of places
   (left for ×, right for ÷), then read and write the new number.
4. State the shift aloud: "3 ones became 3 hundreds; 6 tenths became 6 tens;
   3.6 × 100 = 360."

**Success:** 6 of 8 prompts correct, with the shift direction and the final
value both stated.

**Make the cards:** write each operation on a slip; include at least two
÷ cards and two exponent cards.

## Game 3 — Rounding relay

*(Sessions 10, 14, 15)*

**Players:** teams of 2 (turn-based relay, **not timed**). **Materials:**
number cards, a drawn number line per round, benchmark cards.

1. The adult draws a number card (e.g., 4.37) and names the rounding place
   ("nearest tenth").
2. Player 1 places the number on the number line between its two benchmarks
   and says which benchmark is nearer; Player 2 states the rounded value and
   names the deciding digit.
3. A round scores when the placement, the rounded value, and the deciding
   digit are all correct. Then the next card goes to the other team.

**Success:** 8 of 10 rounds fully correct as a team.

**Card set (24 cards, nearest tenth / hundredth / one mixed):** 6.784 · 14.296
· 3.141 · 9.95 · 0.847 · 45.5 · 12.3456 · 2.46 · 0.0998 · 7.62 · 5.678 · 23.45
· 0.502 · 8.446 · 1.05 · 99.99 · 3.279 · 0.615 · 27.454 · 4.37 · 10.499 ·
0.095 · 6.05 · 2.995.

## Session-keyed practice sets

*Use after the session's main activity, or as next-day retrieval. 3–4 short
tasks each.*

### S01 — Digit-value relationships

1. In 44,400, compare the two 4s: name each place, write each value, state the
   relationship.
2. In 0.77, how does the tenths 7 compare to the hundredths 7?
3. True or false: "In 303, the left 3 is 10 times the right 3." Explain.

### S02 — Ten-times detective (guided-practice follow-up)

1. Write three numbers where a repeated digit's left copy is exactly 10 times
   the right copy. Prove one with multiplication.
2. In 5,050, how many times greater is the 5 in the thousands place than the
   5 in the tens place? (Two place-steps — be careful.)
3. Error hunt: "In 2.2, the ones 2 is 1/10 of the tenths 2." Correct or not?
   Explain.

### S03 — Decimals to thousandths

1. Write 0.703 in words and in expanded form.
2. Write "nine and eighty-five hundredths" as a numeral.
3. Which digit is in the hundredths place in 16.042? What is its value?

### S04 — Dictation game day

Play Game 1 above. Bonus round: dictate "one thousand two hundred and four
thousandths" — what makes this one tricky?

### S05 — Powers of ten

1. 9 × 10³ = ?
2. 0.5 × 100 = ?
3. 6,400 ÷ 10² = ?
4. Explain: 1.25 × 10² = 125 — where did each digit go?

### S06 — Slider game day

Play Game 2 above. Challenge prompts: 0.08 × 10³ · 45.6 ÷ 10² · 7 × 10⁵ ·
0.004 × 10².

### S07 — Legacy powers-of-ten task

Complete [powers-of-ten-practice.md](../../assignments/powers-of-ten-practice.md)
(36 × 100; 7 × 1,000; 4.2 × 100; digit-value explanation; your own
missing-power problem). Then: write 8,500 as 85 × 10² and as 8.5 × 10³ —
which form matches "eighty-five hundreds"? Explain.

### S08 — Week-04 review

Redo, from memory, any two quiz items you missed; write a one-sentence rule
for each. Then: 2.2 × 10² = ? and 2.2 ÷ 10² = ? — explain both shifts.

### S09 — Rounding decimals

1. Round 8.446 to the nearest tenth.
2. Round 0.615 to the nearest hundredth.
3. Round 99.99 to the nearest one. What happens?

### S10 — Rounding relay day

Play Game 3 above. Captain's check: for 2.995 rounded to the nearest tenth,
name the benchmarks, the deciding digit, and the answer.

### S11 — Comparing and ordering decimals

1. Compare: 0.9 ___ 0.89.
2. Order least to greatest: 0.5, 0.55, 0.505.
3. True or false: "0.101 > 0.11 because 101 > 11." Explain.

### S12 — Sprint times + legacy review

Swim heat times: 28.4 s, 28.36 s, 28.5 s, 28.409 s. Rank 1st–4th with
place-by-place reasoning. Then rework two items from the legacy quiz
([decimal-place-value-comparing-to-thousandths-quiz.md](../../quizzes/decimal-place-value-comparing-to-thousandths-quiz.md))
with the key kept separate.

### S13 — Project launch (Powers-of-Ten City)

Draft 6 market prices as decimals to thousandths; write each price's number
name. Check: does any price need a trailing zero to show its place? Fix it.

### S14 — Project build + review games

Replay Game 2 or Game 3, choosing the strand your S08 feedback flagged.
Project check: scale one market object by ×10 and ÷10 — write both new
measurements with units.

### S15 — Mixed review circuit

Station 1 (dictation): partner dictates 3 decimals; write numerals +
expanded form. Station 2 (compare): order 0.66, 0.606, 0.6, 0.660. Station 3
(rounding): round 4.475 to the nearest tenth and hundredth. Station 4
(exponents): write 10⁴, 10⁶ as whole numbers; evaluate 3.3 × 10³.

### S16 — Assessment day

No new practice — complete the
[unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md) and present your
[Powers-of-Ten City](project.md) project.
