# Lesson 02 — Decimals to Thousandths

**My goal:** I can read, write, and expand decimals to thousandths using
base-ten numerals, number names, and expanded form.

**Time:** about 40 minutes (5 readiness · 12 teaching · 8 guided · 12
independent · 3 exit).

**Materials:** place-value mat, the place-value chart illustration below (or
its text alternative), pencil and paper.

**Standards:** 5.NBT.A.3.a · **Unit objectives:** UO2

## Readiness check (5 minutes)

1. Write the value of the underlined digit: 4.5**6** (tenths? hundredths?).
   Also write it as a decimal and as a fraction.
2. Say this number in words: 0.7.
3. True or false: 0.30 and 0.3 name the same amount. Explain in one sentence.

## Explicit teaching

### Reading decimals: "and" marks the decimal point

Read the whole-number part, say **"and"** for the decimal point, then read the
decimal digits as a whole number followed by the name of the **last** place:

- **2.4** → "two **and** four tenths"
- **0.458** → "four hundred fifty-eight thousandths" (no whole-number part,
  so no "and")
- **3.024** → "three **and** twenty-four thousandths"

Saying "and" anywhere else is the classic trap: "three hundred and
twenty-four thousandths" sounds like 300.024. Keep "and" for the point only.

### Zeros are placeholders, not silent

In **2.004**, the zeros hold the tenths and hundredths places so the 4 lands
in the thousandths place: "two and **four** thousandths." We do *not* say the
zeros ("two and zero tenths zero hundredths four thousandths" is never used).

### Worked example 1 — words and expanded form

Take **347.392**.

- **Number name:** "three hundred forty-seven and three hundred ninety-two
  thousandths." (The last decimal digit, 2, is in the thousandths place, so
  the decimal part is read as "three hundred ninety-two thousandths.")
- **Expanded form (fraction form):**
  3 × 100 + 4 × 10 + 7 × 1 + 3 × (1/10) + 9 × (1/100) + 2 × (1/1000).
- **Expanded form (decimal form):**
  300 + 40 + 7 + 0.3 + 0.09 + 0.002.

Check: 300 + 40 + 7 = 347; 0.3 + 0.09 + 0.002 = 0.392. Both forms describe the
same number — expanded form just writes each digit's value from Lesson 01 out
in the open.

### Worked example 2 — a decimal with zeros inside

Take **2.004**.

- **Number name:** "two and four thousandths." The 4 is the only nonzero
  decimal digit and it sits in the thousandths place.
- **Expanded form:** 2 × 1 + 4 × (1/1000), or 2 + 0.004.

Why is this right? 2.004 = 2 ones + 0 tenths + 0 hundredths + 4 thousandths.
The zeros contribute nothing, but they hold the places so the 4 stays in the
thousandths.

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** Write **0.458** in word form.
*Hint: no whole-number part, so no "and" — read the digits and name the last
place.*

**G2.** Write "three and twenty-four thousandths" in standard decimal form.
*Hint: "thousandths" tells you the last digit's place — how many decimal
places does that need?*

**G3.** In **7.892**, the digit 8 is underlined. What is its place value, and
what is its value?
*Hint: count places to the right of the decimal point: first is tenths, second
is…*

**G4 — Error analysis.** Darius writes 3.024 as "three and twenty-four
**hundredths**." Explain his mistake, then write the correct number name.
*Hint: which place does the 4 sit in? What number would "twenty-four
hundredths" actually be?*

## Independent practice

**I1.** Write **4.305** in expanded form — two ways: fraction form
(3 × (1/10) style) and decimal form (0.3 style).

**I2.** Write "six hundred two and seven tenths" in standard decimal form.

**I3.** Write **0.61** in words and in expanded form.

**I4 — Explain your reasoning.** Sam says 2.40 and 2.4 must be different
numbers "because one has more digits." Use place value to explain why
2.40 = 2.4. (What value does the extra 0 add?)

**I5.** Write **50.035** in words.

## Application task

**Price-tag studio.** A market stall sells three items: lemonade $12.75, a
bunch of carrots $4.05, and a single apple $0.99.
(a) Write each price in words.
(b) Write the apple's price ($0.99) in expanded form.
(c) The stall owner says "$4.05 is four and five tenths." Is that correct?
Explain with place value.

## Exit check

Write **0.126** in expanded form (fraction form or decimal form — your
choice). Label which form you used.

## Reading / language-access support

- Read number names aloud together before writing them; echo-reading helps
  lock in "and"-placement.
- Keep the "and = decimal point" rule posted; contrast with a non-example
  ("three hundred and twenty-four" is not said for 324).
- Home-language number names may be practiced alongside the English names.

## Alternate response mode

- Learners may **dictate** number names while the adult writes, then check the
  writing against the place-value mat.
- **Point-and-name:** point to each digit on the chart and say its place and
  value aloud instead of writing the expanded form.
- Build numbers with **digit cards** on the mat rather than writing numerals.

## Extension

Write the largest and smallest decimals to thousandths you can make using the
digits 3, 0, 7, 5 each exactly once (a decimal point may go anywhere, and you
may use a leading zero). Write both in words and in expanded form, and explain
why no larger or smaller arrangement is possible. Record your reasoning —
the answer key shows the full solution for the adult to compare against.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:**
- Saying "and" inside the whole-number part ("three hundred and forty-seven").
- Reading 3.024 as "twenty-four hundredths" (G4) — the trailing-place name
  always comes from the *last* digit's place.
- Treating 2.40 as larger than 2.4 (I4) — "longer decimal is bigger" thinking;
  counter with expanded form showing the 0 adds zero.
- Writing "sixty-one hundreths"-style spellings — separate spelling practice
  from math understanding when scoring.
