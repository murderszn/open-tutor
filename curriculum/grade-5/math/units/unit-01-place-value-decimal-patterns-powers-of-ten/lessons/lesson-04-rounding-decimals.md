# Lesson 04 — Rounding Decimals

**My goal:** I can round a decimal to any place — ones, tenths, hundredths, or
thousandths — and explain my decision using the digit to the right of the
rounding place.

**Time:** about 40 minutes (5 readiness · 12 teaching · 8 guided · 12
independent · 3 exit).

**Materials:** number-line strips (blank, labeled by the learner), pencil and
paper.

**Standards:** 5.NBT.A.4 · **Unit objectives:** UO4

## Readiness check (5 minutes)

1. Round 47 to the nearest ten.
2. On a number line from 6.7 to 6.8, where would 6.74 sit — closer to 6.7 or
   6.8? Mark it and explain your thinking with the distances.
3. Which digits make us "round up" when rounding whole numbers?

## Explicit teaching

### Rounding is "nearest benchmark"

To round a decimal, we pick which place we care about (the **rounding
place**), then ask: is this number closer to the benchmark below or the
benchmark above? The digit **immediately to the right** of the rounding place
decides:

- **0, 1, 2, 3, 4** → round **down** (stay at the lower benchmark).
- **5, 6, 7, 8, 9** → round **up** (move to the higher benchmark).

After deciding, all digits to the right of the rounding place become zero
(which we usually just drop for decimals).

### Worked example 1 — nearest tenth, with a number line

Round **6.784** to the nearest **tenth**.

1. The rounding place is the tenths: 6.**7**84. The benchmarks are 6.7 and 6.8.
2. Look right: the hundredths digit is **8** (≥ 5) → round **up** to 6.8.
3. Answer: **6.8**.

Number-line check: 6.784 sits between 6.7 and 6.8, and 6.784 − 6.7 = 0.084
while 6.8 − 6.784 = 0.016 — it is much closer to 6.8. ✓

### Worked example 2 — nearest hundredth, with carrying

Round **14.296** to the nearest **hundredth**.

1. The rounding place is the hundredths: 14.2**9**6. Benchmarks: 14.29, 14.30.
2. Look right: the thousandths digit is **6** (≥ 5) → round **up**.
3. 14.29 + 0.01 = **14.30**. (We keep the trailing zero to show the place we
   rounded to — 14.30 tells the reader "nearest hundredth," while 14.3 does
   not.)

### Benchmark strategy (no number line needed)

For **9.95** to the nearest tenth: benchmarks 9.9 and 10.0. The hundredths
digit is 5 → round up: 9.9 + 0.1 = **10.0**. Rounding up can carry into the
ones place — that is fine; the benchmark 10.0 is a real number.

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** Round **3.141** to the nearest hundredth.
*Hint: underline the rounding place first; then look one digit right.*

**G2.** Round **9.95** to the nearest tenth. Watch for carrying.
*Hint: benchmarks are 9.9 and 10.0.*

**G3.** Round **27.454** to the nearest tenth.
*Hint: the digit to the right of the tenths place is in the hundredths.*

**G4 — Error analysis.** Nina rounds 4.37 to the nearest tenth and gets 4.3.
"I looked at the 3," she says, "and 3 is small, so I rounded down." Explain
her mistake and give the correct answer.
*Hint: which digit is she supposed to look at — the one IN the rounding place
or the one to its RIGHT?*

## Independent practice

**I1.** Round **0.847** to the nearest hundredth.

**I2.** Round **45.5** to the nearest one.

**I3.** Round **12.3456** to the nearest thousandth.

**I4 — Explain your reasoning.** Both **2.46** and **2.451** round to 2.5 when
rounding to the nearest tenth. Explain why, using the digit to the right of
the tenths place in each case.

**I5.** Round **0.0998** to the nearest hundredth. (Careful — this one carries
twice.)

## Application task

**Rounding in the wild.** A gas station sign shows **$3.279 per gallon**.
(a) Round the price to the nearest cent (nearest hundredth) — what would you
actually pay per gallon? (b) A driver says "$3.279 is basically $3.27."
Do you agree? Explain with the thousandths digit. (c) When would rounding to
the nearest tenth ($3.3) be more useful than rounding to the nearest cent?

## Exit check

Round **7.62** to the nearest tenth. Write one sentence explaining which digit
decided the direction.

---

## Reading / language-access support

- "Round up / round down" and "benchmark" go on the word wall with a number-line
  sketch.
- Read each problem aloud together: "round 6.784 to the nearest tenth" —
  stress the *place* being rounded to.
- Home-language words for "nearest" and "about" may anchor the concept.

## Alternate response mode

- **Number-line pointing:** place a counter on a labeled line and point to the
  nearer benchmark instead of writing the rounded number.
- **Oral justification:** say the deciding digit and direction aloud; the adult
  scribes the final sentence.
- **Benchmark cards:** sort number cards into "closer to lower / closer to
  higher" piles before writing anything.

## Extension

**Rounding chains.** Start with 5.6789. Round it to the nearest thousandth,
then round *that result* to the nearest hundredth, then to the nearest tenth,
then to the nearest one. Do you get the same final answer as rounding 5.6789
directly to the nearest one? Explain why or why not. Then try 5.4499 and see
if the answer changes.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:**
- Deciding from the digit *in* the rounding place instead of the digit to its
  right (G4) — the most common error; require underlining the rounding place
  and circling the deciding digit.
- Dropping the trailing zero (14.3 instead of 14.30) — teach that the zero
  communicates precision/place.
- Thinking 9.95 → 10.0 is "wrong because 10.0 has fewer decimal places" —
  normalize carrying across the decimal point.
- Double-rounding chains (extension) — a good discussion: rounding in stages
  can drift; round from the original number.
