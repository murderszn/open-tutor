# Project — Powers-of-Ten City

**Driving question:** *How do place value and powers of ten run a city market?*

You are the city planner of **Powers-of-Ten City**, a market town where every
price is a decimal to thousandths and every building can be scaled up or down
by powers of ten. Design the market, price the goods, and prove — with digit
shifts — that your scaling is exact.

**Standards:** 5.NBT.A.1, 5.NBT.A.2, 5.NBT.A.3.a–b, 5.NBT.A.4
**Unit objectives:** UO1–UO5 · **Sessions:** S13 (launch), S14 (build), S16 (gallery walk)

## Materials (safe, household)

Paper, pencil, ruler, crayons or markers; optional: coins/buttons as goods,
a shoebox as a market stall, measuring tape. Adult supervises scissors.

## Steps

1. **Map the market (S13).** Draw your market with at least 4 stalls (e.g.,
   bakery, fruit stand, flower cart, tool shed). Label each stall.
2. **Price the goods (S13).** Create a price list of **at least 6 items**,
   every price a decimal to thousandths (e.g., $2.455 for a loaf, $0.075 for a
   single berry). Write each price's **number name** in words beside it.
3. **Scale the city (S14).** Choose one object (a stall front, a sign, a stack
   of crates). Measure it in centimeters, then compute its size at **×10**,
   **×100**, **÷10**, and **÷100** — showing each digit's shift on a
   place-value mat for at least one of the four scalings.
4. **Round for the sign painter (S14).** The sign painter only paints to the
   nearest tenth. Round each of your 6 prices to the nearest tenth and to the
   nearest hundredth; list both rounded price lists.
5. **Compare and rank (S14).** Order your 6 items from cheapest to most
   expensive using < between each pair, and write the place value that
   justifies each link.
6. **Present (S16).** Gallery walk: display your map, price list, scaling work,
   and ranked list. Explain one digit shift and one comparison to a visitor.

## Deliverables

1. Market map with 4+ labeled stalls.
2. Price list: 6+ decimal prices with number names in words.
3. Scaling sheet: one measured object, four scaled sizes, digit-shift work
   shown for at least one scaling.
4. Two rounded price lists (nearest tenth; nearest hundredth).
5. Cheapest→most-expensive comparison chain with justifying place values.
6. A 3–5 sentence reflection: which part of place value helped you most, and
   one thing you would fix.

## Rubric — observable criteria, 4 levels

| Criterion | Beginning (1) | Developing (2) | Proficient (3) | Exemplary (4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Decimal prices & number names** (UO2) | Fewer than 6 prices, or prices not written as decimals | 6+ prices, but 2+ number names wrong or missing | 6+ decimal prices, all number names correct | Proficient + explains how a zero placeholder changes a name (e.g., 2.405 vs 2.45) |
| **Powers-of-ten scaling** (UO3) | Scaled sizes incorrect or no shift work | Sizes correct but shift explanation missing or reversed | All four scaled sizes correct; one digit shift shown correctly on the mat | Proficient + writes at least one scaling with an exponent (e.g., ×10²) and verifies by multiplying back |
| **Rounding** (UO4) | Rounded lists missing or mostly wrong | One list correct | Both rounded lists correct | Proficient + notes a carrying case (e.g., 9.95 → 10.0) and explains it |
| **Comparing & ordering** (UO5) | Chain missing or unordered | Chain ordered but no justifying place values | Chain correct with a justifying place value at each link | Proficient + identifies one "trap" pair (e.g., 0.65 vs 0.605) and explains it |
| **Presentation & reflection** | No reflection or no gallery explanation | Reflection present but generic | Clear reflection naming a specific place-value idea; explains one shift and one comparison to a visitor | Proficient + proposes a concrete fix and how to test it |

**Scoring:** 5 criteria × 4 = 20 points. Adult records the level per criterion
with one observed evidence note each (see
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md#project-scoring-guidance) for scoring guidance
and sample evidence).

## Accessibility

- Build the market as a **physical model** (shoebox stalls, button goods)
  instead of drawing, if drawing is a barrier.
- Dictate number names and the reflection; the adult scribes.
- Prices may be read from pre-written digit cards placed on the mat.

## Boundaries

Anonymous, invented data only — no real store prices copied from receipts or
websites, no photos of real people or places, no personal information on the
map.
