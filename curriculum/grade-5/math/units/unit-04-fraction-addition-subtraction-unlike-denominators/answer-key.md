## Lesson 01 — Equivalent fractions and common denominators

**Readiness:** 1. 8 and 12. 2. 3/6. 3. Multiples of 3: 3, 6, 9, 12;
multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16.

**Guided:** G1. **9/12** (3 × 3 = 9, 4 × 3 = 12). G2. **4/10 and 5/10**
(LCD 10). G3. Sam is wrong: adding 1 to top and bottom is not
multiplying by the same number, and 2/6 = 1/3, not 1/4. Correct with
denominator 8: **2/8**.

**Independent:** I1. **10/12**. I2. **3/8 and 2/8** (LCD 8). I3.
**8/12 and 9/12** (LCD 12). I4. **7/10 and 5/10** (LCD 10). I5. **7/8
is larger:** 5/6 = 20/24, 7/8 = 21/24, and 21/24 > 20/24. I6. Acceptable:
"Cutting each third in half makes 6 equal pieces. Two thirds become
4 sixths, so 2/3 = 4/6. The pieces got smaller and more numerous, but
the total size did not change."

**Application:** renamed **5/6 and 4/6**; the first jug (5/6 litre)
holds more.

**Exit:** 1. **4/12**. 2. **3/12 and 8/12** (LCD 12).

## Lesson 02 — Adding fractions with unlike denominators

**Readiness:** 1. 2/4 and 1/4. 2. **3/4**. 3. 12.

**Guided:** G1. **3/6 = 1/2**. G2. **7/10**. G3. 3/7 cannot be right:
the sum must be larger than each addend, but 3/7 < 1/2. Correct:
4/10 + 5/10 = **9/10**.

**Independent:** I1. **5/6**. I2. **11/12**. I3. **7/10**. I4.
**7/8**. I5. **11/12**. I6. 3/6 + 2/6 = **5/6 km**.

**Application:** 8/12 + 3/12 = **11/12 cup** (less than 1 cup, so no
mixed number is needed).

**Exit:** 1. **11/12**. 2. **7/8**.

**Extension (three fractions):** 6/12 + 4/12 + 3/12 = **13/12 = 1 1/12**.

## Lesson 03 — Subtracting fractions with unlike denominators

**Readiness:** 1. 7/8 and 2/8. 2. **5/8**. 3. 6.

**Guided:** G1. **5/12** (9/12 − 4/12). G2. **7/12** (10/12 − 3/12).
G3. Subtracting denominators invents a new piece size; denominators
name the size and must match first. Correct: 3/6 − 2/6 = **1/6**.

**Independent:** I1. **1/2**. I2. **3/8**. I3. **1/5** (2/10). I4.
**7/12**. I5. **11/24**. I6. **3/10 litre** left; check:
3/10 + 1/2 = 3/10 + 5/10 = 8/10 = 4/5.

**Application:** 5/6 − 2/6 = 3/6 = **1/2 m** left; check:
1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

**Exit:** 1. **1/4**. 2. **5/8**.

**Extension:** 10/12 − 3/12 − 4/12 = 3/12 = **1/4**.

## Lesson 04 — Mixed numbers, word problems, and benchmarks

**Readiness:** 1. 1/4 and 2/4. 2. **3 3/4**. 3. **1/2** (2/3 is 1/6
away from 1/2 and 1/3 away from 1).

**Guided:** G1. **3 5/6** (1 4/6 + 2 1/6). G2. 4 1/3 = 4 2/6, regroup
to 3 8/6; 3 8/6 − 1 5/6 = 2 3/6 = **2 1/2**. G3. Estimate about 1
(2/5 is close to 1/2, 1/2 is 1/2); exact 4/10 + 5/10 = **9/10**, which
supports the estimate.

**Baking-scene activity:** 1. No: the total must be more than 3/4,
but 4/7 < 3/4 (16/28 < 21/28). 2. 9/12 + 4/12 = 13/12 = **1 1/12 cups**.

**Independent:** I1. **3 3/4**. I2. **4 1/2**. I3. **2 3/4** (regroup:
4 5/4 − 2 2/4). I4. **3 1/8**. I5. **4 1/4 km**. I6. No: because
1/4 > 1/6 and 5/6 + 1/6 = 1, the sum must be more than 1, but 11/12
< 1. Exact: 10/12 + 3/12 = 13/12 = **1 1/12**.

**Application:** 2 2/4 + 1 3/4 = 3 5/4 = **4 1/4 m**. Estimate: about
2 1/2 + about 2 = about 4 1/2; 4 1/4 is close — reasonable.

**Exit:** 1. **3 1/2**. 2. **1 3/4** (regroup: 2 5/4 − 1 2/4).

**Extension:** 2 8/12 + 1 9/12 = 3 17/12 = **4 5/12 cups**.

## Lesson 05 — A fraction is division

**Readiness:** 1. **3/4** of a bar each. 2. 12; **3**. 3. Between
**3 and 4**.

**Guided:** G1. **5/4 = 1 1/4**, between **1 and 2**. G2. 50 ÷ 9 =
**50/9 = 5 5/9 pounds** each, between **5 and 6**. G3. The mistake
swaps the roles: 1 is shared, 2 is the number of sharers, so
1 ÷ 2 = **1/2**, not 2/1.

**Independent:** I1. **3/5**, between **0 and 1**. I2. **11/4 =
2 3/4**, between **2 and 3**. I3. **4/3 = 1 1/3**, between **1 and
2**. I4. **10/8 = 5/4 = 1 1/4**, between **1 and 2**. I5. 5 ÷ 3 =
**5/3 = 1 2/3 bars** each; check: 1 2/3 × 3 = 5. I6. **7 ÷ 3 is
larger:** the same 7 shared among fewer people gives larger shares;
7/3 > 7/4.

**Application:** 6 ÷ 4 = **6/4 = 3/2 = 1 1/2 litres** per jug; check:
1 1/2 × 4 = 6.

**Exit:** 1. **8/5 = 1 3/5**, between **1 and 2**. 2. **2/3**,
between **0 and 1**.

**Extension:** 23 ÷ 5 = **23/5 = 4 3/5**, between 4 and 5; with 6
sharers each share shrinks, because the same amount is split more ways.
