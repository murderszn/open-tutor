# Lesson 05 — A Fraction Is Division

**Grade 5 · Unit 04, Lesson 05 · ~40 minutes · Objective UO5**

**Goal (in one sentence):** I can interpret a fraction as division
(a/b = a ÷ b) and say between which two whole numbers a sharing answer
lies.

**Materials:** paper bars or strips, counters for sharing.

**Readiness check:**

1. Share 3 whole bars equally among 4 people. How much does each person
   get, in words?
2. What is 3 × 4? What is 3/4 × 4?
3. Between which two whole numbers does 7/2 lie?

## Teaching

So far, a fraction has meant "parts of one whole." It has a second
meaning that is exactly the same idea, seen from the sharing side:
**a/b = a ÷ b**. The fraction bar *is* a division sign.

> 3/4 means "3 divided by 4": take 3 wholes, share them equally among
> 4 people, and each person gets 3/4 of a whole. Check: 3/4 × 4 = 3.
> The sharing gives back the 3 wholes we started with.

This meaning answers a new kind of question: whole-number division
problems whose answers are not whole numbers. If 7 sandwiches are
shared equally by 2 people, each person gets 7 ÷ 2 = 7/2 = 3 1/2
sandwiches. The answer lies **between 3 and 4**, because 3 wholes are
not quite enough (3 × 2 = 6, one short) and 4 wholes are too many
(4 × 2 = 8, one too many).

**How to find "between which two whole numbers":** divide, keep the
whole-number part, and the answer lies between that whole number and
the next one up. For 7/2: the whole-number part is 3, so the answer
lies between 3 and 4. For a fraction smaller than 1, like 3/5, the
answer lies between 0 and 1.

**Same whole, same rule:** as in Lessons 02–04, every share must come
from same-size wholes, or the shares are not equal.

### Worked Example 1 — 3 ÷ 4

3 ÷ 4 = 3/4. Each of 4 people gets 3/4 of a whole. Check:
3/4 × 4 = 12/4 = 3. The 3 wholes are fully shared. 3/4 is less than 1,
so the answer lies between 0 and 1.

### Worked Example 2 — 7 ÷ 2

7 ÷ 2 = 7/2. Write it as a mixed number: 7/2 = 6/2 + 1/2 = 3 1/2.
Each person gets 3 1/2 sandwiches. Check: 3 1/2 × 2 = 7. The
whole-number part is 3, so the answer lies between 3 and 4.

## Guided practice

**G1.** 5 ÷ 4. Write the fraction, the mixed number, and the two whole
numbers the answer lies between.

**G2. Story (from the standard).** 9 people share a 50-pound sack of
rice equally by weight. How many pounds does each person get? Between
what two whole numbers does the answer lie?

**G3. Error analysis.** A learner writes 1 ÷ 2 = 2/1. Explain the
mistake (which number is shared, and among how many?) and give the
correct fraction.

## Independent practice

**I1.** 3 ÷ 5, with the two whole numbers the answer lies between.

**I2.** 11 ÷ 4, as a mixed number, with the two whole numbers the
answer lies between.

**I3.** 4 ÷ 3, as a mixed number, with the two whole numbers the
answer lies between.

**I4.** 10 ÷ 8, simplified, with the two whole numbers the answer lies
between.

**I5. Story.** 5 granola bars are shared equally among 3 friends. How
much does each friend get? Show the division, the fraction, and a check
by multiplying back.

**I6. Reasoning.** Which is larger, 7 ÷ 3 or 7 ÷ 4? Explain without
computing a decimal, using the sharing meaning.

## Application

6 litres of juice are poured equally into 4 jugs. How many litres are
in each jug? Write the division, the fraction, the mixed number, and a
multiplication check.

## Exit check

1. 8 ÷ 5, as a mixed number, with the two whole numbers the answer lies
   between.
2. 2 ÷ 3, with the two whole numbers the answer lies between.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** act out the sharing with paper bars first — cut each
  whole into the right number of pieces and deal the pieces out round
  by round.
- **Alternate response mode:** learners may show the sharing with
  counters and dictate the division sentence.
- **Extension:** 23 ÷ 5 = 23/5 = 4 3/5, between 4 and 5. Then ask: if
  one more person joins (23 ÷ 6), does each share grow or shrink, and
  why?

## Teacher note

- **Likely misconception:** swapping numerator and denominator
  (1 ÷ 2 = 2/1). Fix: always say "the first number is shared, the
  second number is how many people share it."
- **Likely misconception:** thinking a division answer must be a whole
  number, or rounding 7/2 to 3 or 4. The fraction *is* the exact
  answer; "between 3 and 4" describes where it sits.
