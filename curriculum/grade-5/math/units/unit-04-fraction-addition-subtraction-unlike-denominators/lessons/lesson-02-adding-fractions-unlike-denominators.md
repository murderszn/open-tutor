# Lesson 02 — Adding Fractions with Unlike Denominators

**Grade 5 · Unit 04, Lesson 02 · ~40 minutes · Objective UO2**

**Goal (in one sentence):** I can add fractions with unlike denominators
by renaming them with a common denominator first.

**Materials:** fraction strips or bars, paper for bar drawings.

**Readiness check:**

1. Rename 1/2 and 1/4 with denominator 4.
2. What is 2/4 + 1/4?
3. Find the least common multiple of 3 and 4.

## Teaching

Adding fractions means combining same-size pieces. If the pieces are
different sizes, we first cut them — on paper, by renaming — into
same-size pieces. The steps never change:

1. Find a common denominator (the least one keeps numbers small).
2. Rename each fraction as an equivalent fraction with that denominator.
3. Add the numerators. The denominator stays the same.
4. Simplify, and write an improper fraction as a mixed number, when the
   problem asks for it.

**Never add denominators.** Denominators name the size of the piece;
adding them would invent a new piece size that neither fraction used.

### Worked Example 1 — 1/2 + 1/4

Halves and fourths: the least common denominator is 4, because 4 is a
multiple of 2 and of 4.

- 1/2 = 2/4. 1/4 stays 1/4.
- 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4.

Draw it: shade half of a bar, then shade one more fourth of the same
bar. Three of the four equal parts are shaded.

### Worked Example 2 — 2/3 + 5/4

From Lesson 01: 2/3 = 8/12 and 5/4 = 15/12.

- 8/12 + 15/12 = 23/12.
- 23/12 = 1 11/12, because 12/12 = 1 whole and 11 twelfths remain.

Reasonableness check: 2/3 is a bit less than 1, and 5/4 is a bit more
than 1, so the sum should be a bit less than 2. 1 11/12 is a bit less
than 2. The answer is plausible.

## Guided practice

**G1.** 1/3 + 1/6. (Rename 1/3 with denominator 6.)

**G2.** 3/5 + 1/10. (Rename 3/5 with denominator 10.)

**G3. Error analysis.** A learner writes 2/5 + 1/2 = 3/7. Show why 3/7
cannot be right (hint: compare 3/7 with 1/2 — see 5.NF.A.2), then find
the correct sum.

## Independent practice

**I1.** 1/2 + 1/3.

**I2.** 3/4 + 1/6.

**I3.** 2/5 + 3/10.

**I4.** 5/8 + 1/4.

**I5.** 2/3 + 1/4.

**I6. Story.** Sam walks 1/2 km to the library and then 1/3 km to the
park. How far does he walk in all? Give the renamed fractions and the
sum.

## Application

A recipe needs 2/3 cup of oats and 1/4 cup of raisins. How many cups of
dry ingredients is that in all? Show the common denominator and the
renamed fractions, and write the answer as a single fraction and as a
mixed number if it is more than 1 cup.

## Exit check

1. 1/4 + 2/3.
2. 3/8 + 1/2.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** always draw the two bars and shade the renamed fractions
  before adding numerators.
- **Alternate response mode:** learners may build the sum with fraction
  strips and dictate the written steps.
- **Extension:** add three fractions: 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/4. (Common
  denominator 12; answer 13/12 = 1 1/12.)

## Teacher note

- **Likely misconception:** adding numerators *and* denominators
  (2/5 + 1/2 = 3/7). Fix with the benchmark test: the sum must be bigger
  than each addend, and 3/7 is smaller than 1/2.
- **Likely misconception:** forgetting to rename one fraction. Require
  both renamed fractions to be written before any addition.
