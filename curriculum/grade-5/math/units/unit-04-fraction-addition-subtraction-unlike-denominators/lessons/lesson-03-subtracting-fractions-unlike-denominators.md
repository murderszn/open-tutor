# Lesson 03 — Subtracting Fractions with Unlike Denominators

**Grade 5 · Unit 04, Lesson 03 · ~40 minutes · Objective UO3**

**Goal (in one sentence):** I can subtract fractions with unlike
denominators by renaming them with a common denominator first.

**Materials:** fraction strips or bars, paper for bar drawings.

**Readiness check:**

1. Rename 7/8 and 1/4 with denominator 8.
2. What is 7/8 − 2/8?
3. Find the least common multiple of 6 and 3.

## Teaching

Subtraction follows the same renaming rule as addition, with one extra
care point: **order matters.** The larger fraction comes first, and we
subtract the smaller from it. The steps:

1. Find a common denominator.
2. Rename each fraction with that denominator.
3. Subtract the numerators. The denominator stays the same.
4. Simplify when possible.

Renaming does not change either size, so the difference is the same
difference we started with — now measured in same-size pieces.

### Worked Example 1 — 7/8 − 1/4

Denominators 8 and 4: the least common denominator is 8.

- 1/4 = 2/8. 7/8 stays 7/8.
- 7/8 − 2/8 = 5/8.

Draw it: shade seven eighths of a bar, then cross out two eighths.
Five eighths remain.

### Worked Example 2 — 5/6 − 1/3

Denominators 6 and 3: the least common denominator is 6.

- 1/3 = 2/6. 5/6 stays 5/6.
- 5/6 − 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2.

Check by adding back: 1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6. The subtraction is
correct, because the answer plus the amount taken away gives the
starting amount.

## Guided practice

**G1.** 3/4 − 1/3. (Least common denominator 12.)

**G2.** 5/6 − 1/4. (Least common denominator 12.)

**G3. Error analysis.** A learner writes 1/2 − 1/3 = 0/1, "because 1 − 1
= 0 and 2 − 3 = −1, so 0/1." Show why subtracting denominators makes
no sense, then find the correct difference.

## Independent practice

**I1.** 2/3 − 1/6.

**I2.** 5/8 − 1/4.

**I3.** 7/10 − 1/2.

**I4.** 3/4 − 1/6.

**I5.** 5/6 − 3/8.

**I6. Story.** A jug holds 4/5 litre of water. Maya pours out 1/2 litre.
How much is left? Show the renamed fractions and check by adding back.

## Application

A ribbon is 5/6 m long. Priya cuts off 1/3 m for a gift. How much ribbon
is left? Rename, subtract, simplify, and check your answer by adding
the piece cut off back to the piece left.

## Exit check

1. 3/4 − 1/2.
2. 7/8 − 1/4.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** shade the starting fraction on a bar and cross out the
  renamed second fraction; count what remains before writing numbers.
- **Alternate response mode:** learners may act out the subtraction with
  fraction strips and dictate the steps.
- **Extension:** 5/6 − 1/4 − 1/3. (Common denominator 12:
  10/12 − 3/12 − 4/12 = 3/12 = 1/4.)

## Teacher note

- **Likely misconception:** subtracting denominators, or subtracting
  the smaller numerator from the larger in each column regardless of
  order. Fix: bars first, and always check by adding back.
- **Likely misconception:** leaving 3/6 unsimplified (Worked Example 2).
  Simplifying is part of a finished answer in this unit unless the
  problem asks for a specific denominator.
