# Lesson 04 — Mixed Numbers, Word Problems, and Benchmarks

**Grade 5 · Unit 04, Lesson 04 · ~40 minutes · Objectives UO3–UO4**

**Goal (in one sentence):** I can add and subtract mixed numbers with
unlike denominators, regrouping when I need to, and I can use benchmark
fractions to estimate and check word-problem answers.

**Materials:** fraction strips, the baking-scene picture below.

**Readiness check:**

1. Rename 1/4 and 1/2 with denominator 4.
2. What is 2 1/4 + 1 2/4?
3. Is 2/3 closer to 1/2 or to 1?

## Teaching

A **mixed number** has a whole-number part and a fraction part:
2 1/4 means 2 wholes and 1 fourth. When the denominators differ, rename
the fraction parts first — exactly as in Lessons 02 and 03 — then work
with the whole parts and the fraction parts separately.

**Adding mixed numbers:** add the whole numbers, add the renamed
fractions. If the fraction sum is 1 or more, carry the whole: for
example, 3 7/6 = 4 1/6.

**Subtracting mixed numbers:** if the top fraction part is smaller than
the bottom fraction part, **regroup** one whole from the top whole
number into fraction pieces before subtracting. Example: 6 1/4 becomes
5 5/4, because 1 whole = 4/4, and 4/4 + 1/4 = 5/4.

**Benchmarks** are friendly fractions — 0, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1 — used to
estimate before computing. An estimate does not replace the exact
answer; it tells you whether the exact answer is in the right
neighbourhood. The standard's own example: 2/5 + 1/2 cannot be 3/7,
because 3/7 is less than 1/2 while the sum must be more than 1/2.

### Worked Example 1 — Adding mixed numbers: 2 1/4 + 1 1/2

Rename 1/2 as 2/4. Then:

- Whole parts: 2 + 1 = 3.
- Fraction parts: 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4.
- Total: 3 3/4.

Estimate first: 2 1/4 is about 2, and 1 1/2 is about 1 1/2, so the sum
should be about 3 1/2. 3 3/4 is close — reasonable.

### Worked Example 2 — Subtracting with regrouping: 6 1/4 − 2 3/4

The fraction parts already share denominator 4, but 1/4 is smaller than
3/4, so regroup one whole from 6:

- 6 1/4 = 5 + 1 + 1/4 = 5 5/4.
- Whole parts: 5 − 2 = 3.
- Fraction parts: 5/4 − 3/4 = 2/4 = 1/2.
- Total: 3 1/2.

Check by adding back: 3 1/2 + 2 3/4 = 3 2/4 + 2 3/4 = 5 5/4 = 6 1/4.
Correct.

## Guided practice

**G1.** 1 2/3 + 2 1/6.

**G2.** 4 1/3 − 1 5/6. (Rename, then regroup.)

**G3. Benchmark check.** Estimate 2/5 + 1/2 with benchmarks (2/5 is
close to 1/2), then find the exact sum and say whether the estimate
supports it.

## Independent practice

**I1.** 2 1/2 + 1 1/4.

**I2.** 3 1/3 + 1 1/6.

**I3.** 5 1/4 − 2 1/2.

**I4.** 4 3/8 − 1 1/4.

**I5. Story.** A trail is 2 3/4 km long. After a detour it is extended
by 1 1/2 km. How long is the trail now?

**I6. Reasonableness.** A learner says 5/6 + 1/4 = 11/12. Use this
benchmark reasoning: 1/4 is more than 1/6, and 5/6 + 1/6 = 1, so the
sum must be more than 1. Is 11/12 more than 1? Then check by computing
exactly.

## Application

Two garden beds run along a path. One is 2 1/2 m long and the other is
1 3/4 m long. What is their combined length? Write the renamed
fractions, the sum, and a benchmark estimate that supports your answer.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** keep a regrouping card on the desk: "1 whole = 4/4 =
  6/6 = 8/8 = 12/12 — trade a whole for pieces of the size you need."
- **Alternate response mode:** learners may explain the regrouping
  aloud with strips ("I trade one whole for four fourths") and dictate
  the written steps.
- **Extension:** a recipe calls for 2 2/3 cups of flour and 1 3/4 cups
  of sugar. How many cups in all? (Common denominator 12:
  2 8/12 + 1 9/12 = 3 17/12 = 4 5/12 cups.)

## Teacher note

- **Likely misconception:** subtracting mixed numbers column by column
  without regrouping (6 1/4 − 2 3/4 = 4 −2/4). Fix: regroup first,
  always, when the top fraction part is smaller.
- **Likely misconception:** treating a benchmark estimate as the answer.
  Grade the estimate and the exact answer separately.
