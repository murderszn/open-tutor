# Lesson 05 — Choosing Operations and Checking Reasonableness

**My goal:** I can choose the right decimal operation from a story,
estimate before I compute, and spot an unreasonable answer.

**Time:** about 40 minutes (5 readiness · 12 teaching · 8 guided ·
12 independent · 3 exit).

**Materials:** estimation number lines (0–50), pencil and paper.

**Standards:** 5.NBT.B.7 · **Unit objectives:** UO4, UO5

## Readiness check (5 minutes)

1. Round 12.48 and 9.96 to the nearest whole number.
2. A bag holds 6 apples at $3.25 each. Should you add, subtract, multiply,
   or divide to find the total? Why?
3. $19.50 is shared equally among 6 friends. Which operation finds one
   share? Why?

## Explicit teaching

### Estimate first — always

Estimation is not a chore you do after computing. It is a prediction you
make *before* computing, so that a wild answer cannot sneak past you.
Round each number to a friendly whole number, compute with those, and
write the estimate next to your exact answer. If the two disagree wildly,
recompute — do not just copy the estimate.

### Worked example 1 — estimate, then compute

**12.48 + 9.96**

- Estimate: 12 + 10 = 22.
- Exact: 12.48 + 9.96. Hundredths: 8 + 6 = 14 → write 4, carry 1 tenth.
  Tenths: 4 + 9 + 1 = 14 → write 4, carry 1 one. Ones: 2 + 9 + 1 = 12.
  **Answer: 22.44.**
- Verdict: 22.44 is near 22. Reasonable.

If a learner got 2.244 or 224.4, the estimate of 22 would expose it
instantly: the decimal point is in the wrong place.

### Worked example 2 — the story chooses the operation

**Story A:** "Each of 6 friends pays $3.25 for a ticket." The word *each*
with a repeated amount means multiplication: 6 × $3.25 = **$19.50**.

**Story B:** "$19.50 is shared equally among 6 friends." The words
*shared equally* mean division: $19.50 ÷ 6 = **$3.25**.

Same numbers, opposite operations — and each answer checks the other:
$3.25 × 6 = $19.50, and $19.50 ÷ 6 = $3.25. When two stories are inverses,
use that: compute one way, check the other way.

### Signal words — useful, never sufficient

*Total, altogether, combined* often mean add. *Left, change, difference*
often mean subtract. *Each, per, groups of* often mean multiply.
*Shared equally, split, per person* often mean divide. But signal words
can mislead ("how many *more* apples at $2.50 each?" needs both
operations, in order). Always ask: "What is happening in this story?"
before "Which word do I see?"

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** Estimate, then compute exactly: 7.89 × 3. (Hint: estimate with
8 × 3.)

**G2.** Estimate, then compute exactly: 48.36 ÷ 6. (Hint: estimate with
48 ÷ 6.)

**G3. Error analysis.** A learner computed 4.2 × 5 = 2.10. Use estimation
to show why 2.10 cannot be right, then give the correct answer.

## Independent practice

**I1.** Estimate, then compute exactly: 15.75 − 6.28.

**I2.** Estimate, then compute exactly: 0.62 × 8.

**I3.** Estimate, then compute exactly: 9.6 ÷ 8.

**I4.** Four concert tickets cost $6.50 each. Write the operation, the
computation, and the total.

**I5. Write your own.** Write a one-sentence story problem for
2.4 ÷ 3 = 0.8 in which the answer means something real (money, length,
or weight). Then solve it and check by multiplying back.

## Application

A shopkeeper claims: "3 notebooks at $4.25 each cost $12.75, and with a
$1.50 discount the total is $11.25." Check both claims. Is the shopkeeper
right? Show the estimate and the exact computation for each step.

## Exit check

1. Estimate 23.9 + 11.2, then compute it exactly. Is your exact answer
   reasonable?
2. A learner claims 3.4 × 7 = 238. Use estimation to explain why this
   cannot be right, and give the correct answer.

## Supports, alternate modes, extensions

- **Support:** keep an estimation strip (0, 5, 10, 20, 50) on the desk;
  place each estimate on it before computing.
- **Alternate mode:** state the estimate and the operation orally; the
  adult records them before the written computation begins.
- **Extension:** find a real receipt or price list at home (with an
  adult). Estimate its total, compute it exactly, and report whether the
  printed total was reasonable.

## Teacher note

under time pressure. Grade the estimate separately from the exact answer
for the first two weeks of this unit — an estimate is evidence of thinking,
