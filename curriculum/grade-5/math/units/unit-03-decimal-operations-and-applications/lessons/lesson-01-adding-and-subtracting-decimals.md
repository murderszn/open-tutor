# Lesson 01 — Adding and Subtracting Decimals

**My goal:** I can add and subtract decimals to hundredths by lining up
place values and explaining regrouping.

**Time:** about 40 minutes (5 readiness · 12 teaching · 8 guided ·
12 independent · 3 exit).

**Materials:** place-value mat (ones, tenths, hundredths), hundredths grids
or play money, pencil and paper.

**Standards:** 5.NBT.B.7 · **Unit objectives:** UO1

## Readiness check (5 minutes)

1. Write 4.57 in expanded form (ones, tenths, hundredths).
2. What is 7 tenths + 6 tenths? Give the answer as tenths and as a decimal.
3. You have $5.00 and spend $2.25. About how much is left? (Estimate only.)

## Explicit teaching

### The one rule: line up place value, not the right edge

When you add whole numbers, you line up the ones. With decimals you do the
same thing — only now the landmark is the **decimal point**. Tenths add to
tenths, hundredths add to hundredths, ones add to ones. If you line up the
right edge instead, you will add tenths to hundredths, and the answer will
be wrong even though every digit was added "correctly."

A helpful habit: **fill empty places with zero.** Writing 4.5 as 4.50 does
not change its value (Unit 01), and it makes the columns line up.

### Worked example 1 — addition with regrouping

**4.57 + 2.36**

Write the numbers with decimal points aligned, then work column by column
from the right:

- Hundredths: 7 + 6 = 13 hundredths = 1 tenth + 3 hundredths. Write 3,
  carry 1 tenth.
- Tenths: 5 + 3 + the carried 1 = 9 tenths. Write 9.
- Ones: 4 + 2 = 6. Write 6.

**Answer: 6.93.** Check by estimating first: 4.57 ≈ 5 and 2.36 ≈ 2, so the
answer should be near 7. 6.93 is near 7 — reasonable.

Another way to see it: 457 hundredths + 236 hundredths = 693 hundredths =
6.93. Adding decimals *is* adding hundredths, once the places line up.

### Worked example 2 — subtraction with regrouping

**9.42 − 3.78**

- Hundredths: 2 − 8 cannot be done without borrowing. Borrow 1 tenth (10
  hundredths): 12 − 8 = 4 hundredths. The tenths column drops from 4 to 3.
- Tenths: 3 − 7 cannot be done. Borrow 1 one (10 tenths): 13 − 7 = 6 tenths.
  The ones column drops from 9 to 8.
- Ones: 8 − 3 = 5.

**Answer: 5.64.** Check: 5.64 + 3.78 = 9.42, back where we started. Addition
undoes subtraction — that check always works.

### Why the right edge fails

Take 4.5 + 2.36. Lined up by place value (4.50 + 2.36) the answer is 6.86.
A learner who lines up the right edge adds the 5 (tenths) to the 6
(hundredths) and gets a wrong answer. The digits did not change — only
their *places* were misunderstood. Place value is the whole lesson.

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** 3.48 + 5.27. (Hint: hundredths first: 8 + 7 = 15 hundredths.)

**G2.** 7.05 − 2.49. (Hint: the hundredths column needs a borrow from the
tenths — but the tenths digit is 0. Borrow from the ones first: 7.05
becomes 6 ones, 9 tenths, 15 hundredths. Work carefully.)

**G3. Error analysis.** Sam computed 8.3 − 2.47 = 6.17. He subtracted the
smaller digit from the larger digit in every column, without regrouping.
Where did that go wrong, and what is the correct answer? (Hint: check by
adding your answer to 2.47 — you should get back to 8.3.)

## Independent practice

**I1.** 7.83 + 4.29

**I2.** 6.04 − 3.58

**I3.** 9.15 + 0.87

**I4.** 5.20 − 1.75

**I5.** Maya had $12.50. She spent $7.85 on a book. How much money does she
have left? Show the subtraction lined up by place value.

**I6. Explain.** Why does writing 3.4 as 3.40 help you add 3.4 + 2.75?
Answer in two sentences, using the words *tenths* and *hundredths*.

## Application

You are checking a receipt: a sandwich costs $4.75 and a drink costs
$2.50. The receipt total says $7.25. Is the receipt right? Add to check,
and write one sentence explaining your verdict.

## Exit check

1. 4.68 + 2.37 = ?
2. 8.12 − 3.49 = ?

## Supports, alternate modes, extensions

- **Support:** use a hundredths grid to shade 4.68 and 2.37, then combine;
  count the shaded hundredths.
- **Alternate mode:** dictate the column steps aloud while the adult writes
  the digits; pointing to the mat counts as answering.
- **Extension:** 12.486 + 3.7 — thousandths appear. Does the same rule
  still work? (Yes: line up the decimal points and fill with zeros.)

## Teacher note

The persistent misconception is right-edge alignment, and it survives
because it *looks* tidy. Every time it appears, return to the mat: put a
tenths card over a hundredths card and ask, "Are these the same size?"
