## Lesson 01 — Adding and subtracting decimals

**Readiness:** 1. 4 + 0.5 + 0.07. 2. 13 tenths = 1.3. 3. About $2.75
($5.00 − $2.25 = $2.75 exactly; an estimate near $3 is acceptable).

**Guided:** G1. 3.48 + 5.27 = **8.75** (hundredths 8 + 7 = 15 → 5, carry 1;
tenths 4 + 2 + 1 = 7; ones 3 + 5 = 8). G2. 7.05 − 2.49 = **4.56**
(7.05 → 6 ones, 9 tenths, 15 hundredths; 15 − 9 = 6 hundredths;
9 − 4 = 5 tenths; 6 − 2 = 4). G3. Sam's method subtracts the smaller
digit in each column: hundredths 7 − 0 = 7, tenths 4 − 3 = 1, ones 8 − 2
= 6, giving 6.17. Correct: 8.3 − 2.47 = **5.83** (8.30 − 2.47; check
5.83 + 2.47 = 8.30).

**Independent:** I1. **12.12**. I2. **2.46**. I3. **10.02**. I4. **3.45**.
I5. $12.50 − $7.85 = **$4.65**. I6. Acceptable: "3.4 means 3 ones and 4
tenths. Writing 3.40 adds a hundredths place (0 hundredths) so the
tenths and hundredths columns line up with 2.75, and the value does not
change."

**Application:** $4.75 + $2.50 = $7.25. The receipt is right.

**Exit:** 1. **7.05**. 2. **4.63**.

## Lesson 02 — Multiplying decimals

**Readiness:** 1. 40 hundredths in 0.4; 70 hundredths in 0.7. 2. 28 and
2,800 — each factor grew by 10, so the product grew by 100. 3. 4 columns.

**Guided:** G1. **0.45** (5 × 9 = 45 hundredths). G2. **8.00** (32 × 25 =
800 hundredths; $8.00 = $8). G3. Priya forgot that tenths × tenths =
hundredths: 0.3 × 0.4 = **0.12**, not 1.2.

**Independent:** I1. **0.42**. I2. **0.72**. I3. **7.2**. I4. **2.70**.
I5. **1.80**. I6. 6 × $1.25 = **$7.50**.

**Application:** 0.6 × 0.5 = **0.30 kg**. The grid has 6 columns and 5
rows; the overlap is 30 of 100 squares. The answer is smaller than 0.6
because 0.5 of something is half of it.

**Exit:** 1. **0.63**. 2. **6.45**.

**Guided:** G1. **0.8**. G2. **0.9**. G3. **0.40** (2 ones = 20 tenths;

**Independent:** I1. **1.2**. I2. **0.9**. I3. **2.1**. I4. **0.5**.

**Exit:** 1. **2.3**. 2. **0.3**.

## Lesson 04 — Money and measurement applications

**Readiness:** 1. "Per" means "for each one." 2. Multiply to find the
total, then subtract from $10 for the change. 3. About $7.50.

**Guided:** G1. 2 × $1.75 = **$3.50**. G2. 4 × $0.60 = **$2.40**.
G3. $2.50 + $1.75 = $4.25; $5.00 − $4.25 = **$0.75 change**.

**Independent:** I1. 3 × $0.60 = **$1.80**. I2. 2 × $2.50 + $1.75 =
$5.00 + $1.75 = **$6.75**. I3. 2 × $2.50 + 2 × $1.75 + 3 × $0.60 =
$5.00 + $3.50 + $1.80 = $10.30. No — she is **$0.30 over** her $10.
I4. 4.75 + 2.5 = 7.25; 7.25 − 1.25 = **6.00 m**.

**Application:** One person: $2.50 + $1.75 + 2 × $0.60 = $5.45.
Expression: 4 × ($2.50 + $1.75 + 2 × $0.60) = 4 × $5.45 = **$21.80**.

**Exit:** 1. $2.50 + 2 × $0.60 = $2.50 + $1.20 = **$3.70**. 2. $5.00 −
$3.70 = **$1.30 change**.

**Extension:** 3 × $0.60 = $1.80, so $1.50 for 3 is cheaper by $0.30.
A "deal" is not cheaper when the per-item price is higher than the
regular price — accept any worked example.

## Lesson 05 — Choosing operations and checking reasonableness

**Readiness:** 1. 12 and 10. 2. Multiply (repeated equal amounts).
3. Divide (sharing equally).

**Guided:** G1. Estimate 8 × 3 = 24; exact 7.89 × 3 = **23.67**.
G2. Estimate 48 ÷ 6 = 8; exact 48.36 ÷ 6 = **8.06**. G3. Estimate
4 × 5 = 20, so 2.10 is ten times too small: the point was misplaced.
Correct: **21.0** (21).

**Independent:** I1. Estimate 16 − 6 = 10 (or 15.75 ≈ 16, 6.28 ≈ 6);

0.6 × 8 ≈ 4.8; exact 0.62 × 8 = **4.96**. I3. Estimate 10 ÷ 8 ≈ 1.25;
exact 9.6 ÷ 8 = **1.2**. I4. Multiply: 4 × $6.50 = **$26.00**.
I5. Accept any sensible story with the correct check, e.g. "$2.40
shared among 3 friends is $0.80 each; check $0.80 × 3 = $2.40."

**Application:** 3 × $4.25 = $12.75 — right. $12.75 − $1.50 = $11.25 —
right. The shopkeeper is correct on both claims.

**Exit:** 1. Estimate 24 + 11 = 35; exact 23.9 + 11.2 = **35.1** —
reasonable. 2. Estimate 3 × 7 = 21, so 238 is ten times too big;
