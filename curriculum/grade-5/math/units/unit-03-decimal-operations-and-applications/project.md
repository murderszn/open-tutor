# Project — The Decimal Market Stall

**Sessions 11–12, presented in Session 13 · Pairs or small teams ·
Objectives UO1–UO5**

## The job

Your team runs a market stall for a class picnic. You have a **budget of
$50.00** — you may not spend more than that, and you must use all four
operations (add, subtract, multiply, divide) somewhere in your plan.

## Price list (fictional practice prices)

| Item | Price |
|---|---|
| Apples | $2.50 each |
| Oranges | $1.75 each |
| Lemons | $0.60 each |
| Bananas | $0.85 each |
| Grapes | $3.20 per bag |
| Juice boxes | $2.45 per pack |

## Deliverables

1. **Stall board:** a poster or page showing your stall name, your
   shopping list (item, quantity, line cost), and your total.
2. **Cost sheet:** every computation shown step by step — totals by
   multiplication, the grand total by addition, the change from $50.00
   by subtraction, and a shared cost by division (see Task 3).
3. **Reasonableness check:** one estimate for your grand total, written
   *before* the exact computation, and one sentence saying whether the
   exact total is reasonable.
4. **Presentation (Session 13):** each team presents one computation and
   answers one peer "does that make sense?" question.

## Tasks

**Task 1 — Plan the shop.** Choose quantities so your grand total is
between **$45.00 and $50.00** (inclusive). Show each line cost
(quantity × price) and add the lines for the grand total.

**Task 2 — Bulk check.** Somewhere in your plan, buy at least 6 of one
fruit. Show that multiplication separately and explain, in one sentence,
why multiplying was the right operation.

**Task 3 — Share the cost.** Five friends split the grand total equally.
Compute each friend's share. If your total does not divide evenly by 5,
say so honestly: give the share to the nearest cent and state the
leftover cents — do not hide a remainder. (A team that chooses a total
ending in 0 or 5 will divide exactly; that is good planning, not cheating.)

**Task 4 — Change.** From $50.00, how much change is left after your
shop? Show the subtraction.

## Worked sample (not a plan to copy)

A team buys 6 apples ($15.00), 4 oranges ($7.00), 8 lemons ($4.80),
3 bags of grapes ($9.60), and 4 juice packs ($9.80).

- Grand total: $15.00 + $7.00 + $4.80 + $9.60 + $9.80 = **$46.20** (inside
  the $45.00–$50.00 window).
- Change: $50.00 − $46.20 = **$3.80**.
- Shared by 5: $46.20 ÷ 5 = **$9.24 each** (5 × $9.24 = $46.20 — exact).

Your plan must be your own: different quantities, your own total, your
own checks. Copying this sample's list earns no credit for Task 1.

## Rubric (12 points)

| Criterion | 3 points | 2 points | 1 point | 0 points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Accuracy (UO1–UO3)** | All line costs, total, change, and share correct | One computation error | Two errors | Three or more errors |
| **Operation choice (UO4)** | Every operation used where its story requires it, explained | One operation used without explanation | An operation chosen wrongly | Operations missing |
| **Reasonableness (UO5)** | Estimate made before computing; verdict argued from it | Estimate present but after the fact, or verdict missing | Estimate is a wild guess with no comparison | No estimate |
| **Presentation** | Stall board and cost sheet complete, readable, and presented | One deliverable incomplete | Board or sheet hard to follow | Deliverable missing |

**Ready:** 10–12 points, all four operations shown, total in window.
**Almost:** 7–9 points — fix the flagged computation and re-present it.
**Reteach:** 0–6 points — revisit the flagged lesson(s) with the adult
before the unit assessment.

## Extension — Beat the budget (optional)

Can you feed the same picnic for under $40.00 by changing quantities
only (same prices)? Show the new total and the saving versus your first
plan. This does not replace Tasks 1–4.
