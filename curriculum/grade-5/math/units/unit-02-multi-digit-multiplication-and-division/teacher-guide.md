## Session notes and misconception alerts

- **S01 (Estimation):** The point is *range checking*, not a second exact
  answer. Watch for learners who "adjust" their estimate to match the computed
  answer (Lesson 01, G3) — that habit destroys the check. Insist: estimate is
  written *before* computing, in ink.
- **S03 (Standard algorithm):** The dropped tens-row 0 is the classic error.
  Grid paper is the cheapest fix. When a product is off by ~10×, ask: "What
  did your estimate say?" before re-teaching the algorithm — let the estimate
  do the diagnosing (UO5 in action).
- **S05 (Partial quotients):** Accept any valid chunk path (see answer key,
  Lesson 03 I3). The non-negotiable rules: remainder < divisor, and the final
  multiplication check. If a learner takes 12 tiny chunks, nudge toward
  ×100/×10 chunks rather than correcting the method.
- **S07 (Two-digit divisors + image):** Do *not* let learners count buttons in
  the illustration — the art is a story scene, and counts can't be read from
  it. The numbers live in the written story (see the text-only alternative in
  Lesson 04). The three judge's questions (full / leftover / in-all) are the
  heart of UO3; drill them until they're automatic.
- **S09 (Parentheses):** The A-vs-B contrast (3 × (126 + 57) vs. (3 × 126) +
  57) is the whole lesson in one pair. Learners who "evaluate left to right
  through parentheses" (Noah's error) need the physical-card version from the
  supports section.
- **S11 (Legacy division task):** the legacy
  [multi-digit-division-practice.md](../../assignments/multi-digit-division-practice.md)
  is a blank prompt — the numbers (936 ÷ 24 = 39; 1,344 ÷ 32 = 42;
  775 ÷ 18 = 43 R1) and their checks live in this unit's answer key.
- **S12–S13 (Project):** Part B step 6 needs only ~40 small objects to model
  *one* box of 18 plus the leftover pattern — learners sometimes think they
  must model all 775. Clarify early.

## Reteach moves (by objective)

- **UO1 weak:** back to the area model — rebuild one product (e.g., 23 × 45)
  as four partial products, then map each to a row of the standard algorithm.
  The [area-model SVG](assets/area-model-23-x-45.svg) is the bridge.
- **UO2 weak:** shrink the divisor back to one digit and the dividend to three
  digits; rebuild with base-ten blocks on a place-value mat; then reintroduce
  the second divisor digit as "just bigger chunks."
- **UO3 weak:** run Remainder Court (practice.md, Game 4) with real objects
  until the three questions separate cleanly. Never accept "43 R1" as a final
  answer to a story question.
- **UO4 weak:** expression cards — numbers, operators, and ( ) [ ] { } on
  separate cards; the learner physically builds the story's expression before
  writing anything.
- **UO5 weak:** make the estimate *scored* for a week: one point for an honest
  pre-computation estimate on every problem, win or lose on the exact answer.

## Readiness criteria for Unit 03 (Decimal operations and applications)

The learner is ready when **all** of these are true:

1. Multiplies two two-digit numbers and three-digit × two-digit numbers by the
   standard algorithm with correct place value (quiz Q3–Q4 / assessment Q3–Q4).
2. Divides up-to-four-digit dividends by one- and two-digit divisors with a
   valid strategy and a multiplication check (assessment Q6–Q8).
3. Interprets a remainder in context — full groups, leftover, groups-needed —
   without prompting (assessment Q9–Q10; project Part B).
4. Writes and evaluates a parenthesized multistep expression (assessment
   Q11–Q14).

If criterion 3 or 4 is shaky, Unit 03's decimal word problems will compound
the confusion — use the Week-10 fix-it hour (S15) on those rows first.

## Accessibility and differentiation notes

- Grid paper, place-value mats, and base-ten blocks are first-class tools here,
  not crutches — offer them to everyone.
- Oral/narrated computation ("6 times 8 is 48, write 8, carry 4") is a valid
  response mode for any lesson; the adult scribes.
- Learners with slower fact recall: the friendly-chunk call-out drills in
  practice.md build the exact facts partial quotients needs.
- **No timed tests.** Fluency here means "correct method, reasonable pace" —
  speed pressure produces the dropped-zero and dropped-row errors this unit
  exists to prevent.

## Answer-key and assessment use

- `answer-key.md` holds every solution, worked independently. Grade with it
  open; give partial credit for correct method with a computation slip.
- Week-08 quiz priority signals: Q7 (two-digit division + check) and Q10
  (remainder trio). Misses there → reteach Lessons 03–04 before Week 9.
- Unit assessment: 14 of 17 at full credit with work shown = ready for
  Unit 03. The extended item (Q17) is graded holistically — expression
  structure, evaluation, and the doubling argument each count.
- **These are teacher-made checks, not standardized or validated assessments.**
