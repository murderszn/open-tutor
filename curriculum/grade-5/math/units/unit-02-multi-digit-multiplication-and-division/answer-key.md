## Lesson 02 — Multi-Digit Multiplication and the Standard Algorithm

**Readiness:** 1. 7 × 253 = 1,771 (7×3=21, write 1 carry 2; 7×5=35+2=37, write
7 carry 3; 7×2=14+3=17). 2. 46 × 32 ≈ 50 × 30 = 1,500. 3. 20×40, 20×5, 3×40,
3×5 → 800, 100, 120, 15.

**Guided practice**
- **G1.** 57 × 18: ones row 8 × 57 = 456 (8×7=56, write 6 carry 5; 8×5=40+5=45);
  tens row 10 × 57 = 570; total **1,026**. Partial products: 50×10=500,
  50×8=400, 7×10=70, 7×8=56 → 500+400+70+56 = 1,026. ✓
- **G2.** Estimate: 40 × 25 = 1,000. 38 × 25: ones row 5 × 38 = 190; tens row
  20 × 38 = 760; total **950**. ✓
- **G3.** Recompute: 3 × 1 = 3; 3 × 3 = 9; 3 × 2 = 6; 3 × 4 = 12 → **12,693**.
  The written 12,963 swapped the middle digits — the carried 1 from 3×3=9… wait:
  4,231 × 3: 3×1=3; 3×3=9; 3×2=6; 3×4=12 → 12,693. The error "12,963" transposes
  the 6 and 9 in the middle places (likely a copying slip, not a carry error).

**Independent practice**
- **I1.** (a) 46 × 32: 2 × 46 = 92; 30 × 46 = 1,380; total **1,472**.
  Estimate 50 × 30 = 1,500 ✓. (b) 64 × 29: 9 × 64 = 576; 20 × 64 = 1,280;
  total **1,856**. Estimate 60 × 30 = 1,800 ✓.
- **I2.** 317 × 8: 8 × 7 = 56 (write 6, carry 5); 8 × 1 = 8 + 5 = 13 (write 3,
  carry 1); 8 × 3 = 24 + 1 = 25 → **2,536**.
- **I3.** Estimate 300 × 40 = 12,000. 287 × 42: 2 × 287 = 574; 40 × 287 =
  11,480; total **12,054**. Close to estimate. ✓
- **I4.** Accept: "The tens row multiplies by the *tens* digit — by 10s, 20s,
  etc. — and any multiple of 10 ends in 0, so the row always ends in 0; the 0
  holds the ones place so every other digit sits in its true place value."

**Applied task.** 25 boxes × $38: estimate 25 × 40 = 1,000; exact **$950**
(5 × 38 = 190; 20 × 38 = 760; 190 + 760 = 950). Trustworthy because the
estimate brackets it and the partial products agree.

**Exit check.** 3,742 × 6: 6 × 2 = 12 (write 2, carry 1); 6 × 4 = 24 + 1 = 25
(write 5, carry 2); 6 × 7 = 42 + 2 = 44 (write 4, carry 4); 6 × 3 = 18 + 4 = 22

## Lesson 03 — Division with Partial Quotients

**Readiness:** 1. 84 ÷ 4 = 21 (e.g., 4 × 20 = 80 → 4; 4 × 1 = 4 → 0).
2. 7,200; 150. 3. 13 × 4 + 2 = 52 + 2 = **54**.

**Guided practice**
- **G1.** 856 ÷ 4: 4 × 200 = 800 → 56; 4 × 10 = 40 → 16; 4 × 4 = 16 → 0.
  Quotient **214**. Check: 214 × 4 = 856. ✓
- **G2.** 1,215 ÷ 9: 9 × 100 = 900 → 315; 9 × 30 = 270 → 45; 9 × 5 = 45 → 0.
  Quotient **135**. Check: 135 × 9 = 1,215. ✓
- **G3.** 2,508 ÷ 3: 3 × 800 = 2,400 → 108; 3 × 30 = 90 → 18; 3 × 6 = 18 → 0.
  Quotient **836**. Check: 836 × 3 = 2,508. ✓

**Independent practice**
- **I1.** (a) 4,806 ÷ 9: 9 × 500 = 4,500 → 306; 9 × 30 = 270 → 36; 9 × 4 = 36
  → 0. Quotient **534**. Check: 534 × 9 = 4,806. ✓
  (b) 9,216 ÷ 8: 8 × 1,000 = 8,000 → 1,216; 8 × 100 = 800 → 416; 8 × 50 = 400
  → 16; 8 × 2 = 16 → 0. Quotient **1,152**. Check: 1,152 × 8 = 9,216. ✓
- **I2.** 743 ÷ 5: 5 × 100 = 500 → 243; 5 × 40 = 200 → 43; 5 × 8 = 40 → 3.
  **148 R3**. Check: 148 × 5 + 3 = 740 + 3 = 743. ✓
- **I3.** Both correct. Partial quotients only require that the chunks add to
  the dividend and each chunk be a whole-number multiple of the divisor; any
  valid chunk path reaches the same quotient (here 300+100+16 = 416 and
  400+10+6 = 416).
- **I4.** 2,508 ÷ 3 = 836, not 86 — the "86" answer dropped the hundreds
  place: the first chunk 3 × 800 contributes 800 to the quotient, and 86 is
  missing that place value.

**Applied task.** 1,152 ÷ 4: 4 × 200 = 800 → 352; 4 × 80 = 320 → 32;
4 × 8 = 32 → 0. **288 books per shelf**. Check: 288 × 4 = 1,152 proves each
shelf holds the same count with nothing left over.

**Exit check.** 2,916 ÷ 7: 7 × 400 = 2,800 → 116; 7 × 10 = 70 → 46;
7 × 6 = 42 → 4. **416 R4**. Check: 416 × 7 + 4 = 2,912 + 4 = 2,916. ✓

## Lesson 05 — Parentheses in Multistep Problems

**Readiness:** 1. 3 × 183 = **549** (add first). 2. 378 + 57 = **435** — no,
different, because the parentheses group different operations first.
3. Accept: "parentheses say 'do me first.'"

**Guided practice**
- **G1.** 2 × (5 + 5) = 2 × 10 = **20** — matches "2 packs, each with 5 red and
  5 blue." (2 × 5) + 5 = 10 + 5 = **15** — "2 packs of 5 red, plus 5 loose blue."
- **G2.** 100 − (25 + 25) = 100 − 50 = **50**. (100 − 25) + 25 = 75 + 25 =
  **100**.
- **G3.** 1,000 − (83 × 12) = 1,000 − 996 = **4**.
- **G4.** { [ (4 + 4) × 2 ] − 6 } ÷ 2: (4+4)=8; [8×2]=16; {16−6}=10; 10÷2 =
  **5**.

**Independent practice**
- **I1.** (a) 4 × (36 + 24) = 4 × 60 = **240** — e.g., "4 bags each holding 36
  apples and 24 oranges." (b) (4 × 36) + 24 = 144 + 24 = **168** — e.g.,
  "4 bags of 36 apples, plus 24 loose oranges." Answers differ because the
  parentheses group the addition before the multiplication in (a) but not
  in (b).
- **I2.** (14 × 126) + 70 = 1,764 + 70 = **$1,834**.
- **I3.** (3 × 45) − 20 = 135 − 20 = **$115**.
- **I4.** Noah's error: he distributed the subtraction through the
  parentheses as if they weren't there. Correct: 20 − (8 + 4) = 20 − 12 =
  **8**. The parentheses force 8 + 4 first.

**Applied task.** (25 × 38) + 70 = 950 + 70 = **$1,020**. Without the
parentheses, "25 × 38 + 70" is still 1,020 by convention — but the written
expression no longer *shows* that the 25 boxes are one grouped line item; a
reader could mis-attach the +70 to the wrong part (e.g., reading it as
25 × (38 + 70) = 2,700). Parentheses make the intended grouping unambiguous.

**Exit check.** 50 − (3 × (4 + 2)): (4+2)=6; 3×6=18; 50−18 = **32**.
