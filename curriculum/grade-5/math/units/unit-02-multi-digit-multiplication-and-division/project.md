# Project — Packing & Pricing Co.

**An investigation in four parts.** You are the operations manager of a small
pretend workshop that makes and ships button craft kits. Your job: price a
customer order, pack the kits, check every number, and present the plan to the
"board" (your adult). All numbers come from this unit's lessons — your work is
to put them together the way a real workshop would.

**Materials:** paper and pencil, the
[packing-workshop illustration](assets/packing-workshop-illustration.png), grid
paper, small objects for packing (buttons, beans, or paper clips — about 40 is
enough to model the method) and a few small boxes or cups.

**Time:** Sessions 12–13 (launch + build) and Session 15 (gallery).

## The situation

A customer orders three product lines for a craft fair:

- 14 lamps at $126 each
- 18 microphones at $57 each
- 25 storage boxes at $38 each

Shipping is a flat $70 added after the items are totaled. The workshop also has
775 loose buttons to pack into kit boxes that hold 18 buttons each.

## Part A — Price the order (UO1, UO4)

1. **Estimate first.** Round each line and estimate the grand total *before*
   computing. Write your estimate and say whether you rounded up, down, or
   mixed — and what that tells you about the true total.
2. Write the **whole order as one numerical expression** with parentheses,
   like a real order sheet. Then evaluate it step by step.
3. Compare: is your exact total reasonably close to your estimate? If not,
   find the slipped digit before moving on.

## Part B — Pack the buttons (UO2, UO3)

4. Divide 775 ÷ 18 by partial quotients (or an area model). Check by
   multiplication.
5. Look at the [packing-workshop illustration](assets/packing-workshop-illustration.png).
   The open crate that is only partly full is your remainder. Answer the three
   judge's questions from Game 4 (Remainder Court): how many **full** boxes?
   How many buttons **left over**? How many boxes are needed to pack
   **everything**?
6. **Model it:** use your small objects and boxes to pack *one* box of 18 and
   show what the leftover pile represents. (You don't need 775 objects — pack
   one box, then explain the pattern.)

*Text-only alternative for the illustration question: the story numbers are
775 buttons, 18 per box. 775 ÷ 18 = 43 R1: 43 full boxes, 1 leftover, 44 boxes
to pack everything. The illustration's partly-full crate stands for the
remainder; exact button counts cannot be read from the art.*

## Part C — Check everything (UO5)

7. For each line total in Part A, write the inverse check (e.g., total ÷
   quantity = price — or recompute with partial products).
8. Write the buttons remainder as one expression: `775 − (___ × ___) = ___`
   (see Lesson 05, Worked Example 3).
9. **Error audit:** trade papers with your adult (or re-read your own work an
   hour later). Find one place a slipped digit *could* have hidden, and explain
   how your estimate or check would have caught it.

## Part D — Present (all objectives)

10. Make a one-page **order sheet**: the expression, each line total, the
    grand total, the packing plan (full boxes / leftover / boxes needed), and
    your checks — all with units ($, boxes, buttons).
11. Present it to the board. Your adult scores it with the rubric below.

## Deliverables

1. Order sheet (one page) with the expression, line totals, grand total, and units.
2. Packing plan: full boxes, leftover, boxes needed — with the division work shown.
3. The remainder expression and all inverse/estimate checks.
4. Written explanations for steps 3, 6, and 9 (a few sentences each).

## Rubric

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Solid | 2 — Developing | 1 — Starting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Accuracy** (UO1, UO2) | All line totals, the grand total (3,810), and the division (43 R1) correct | One small computation slip, caught or minor | Two slips; division or multiplication method shaky | Major errors; methods not applied |
| **Checking** (UO5) | Estimates precede every computation; every quotient checked by multiplication; one caught-or-prevented slip explained | Estimates and checks present for most items | Checks attempted but incomplete or after-the-fact | No estimates or checks |
| **Expression writing** (UO4) | Order written as one correct parenthesized expression and evaluated stepwise | Expression correct with a small evaluation slip | Expression written but parentheses misplaced | No valid expression |
| **Remainder reasoning** (UO3) | All three packing questions answered (43 / 1 / 44) with the remainder expression and the illustration interpreted | Three questions answered; explanation thin | Only "43 R1" stated without interpretation | Remainder ignored or misread |

**Scoring:** 14–16 = ready for Unit 03 with confidence; 10–13 = review the
flagged row's sessions before Unit 03; below 10 = re-teach the flagged
objectives (see teacher guide) before moving on.

## Safety and access notes

- Small objects are a choking hazard for younger siblings — keep the modeling
  tray out of their reach and put objects away when done.
- If fine-motor work is hard, the adult packs the model box while the learner
  directs each step aloud.
- All numbers in this project are fictional practice data for a pretend
  workshop; no real orders, prices, or customers are involved.
