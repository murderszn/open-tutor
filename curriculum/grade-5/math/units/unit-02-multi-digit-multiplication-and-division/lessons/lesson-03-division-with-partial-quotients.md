# Lesson 03 — Division with Partial Quotients

**My goal:** I can divide numbers with up to four-digit dividends by one-digit
divisors using partial quotients, and I can check every quotient by
multiplication.

**Time:** about 40 minutes (5 readiness · 12 teaching · 8 guided · 12
independent · 3 exit).

**Materials:** paper and pencil, grid paper (helpful). Multiplication facts to
10 × 10 should be quick-recall by now — use the [practice.md](../practice.md)
fact drills if not.

**Standards:** 5.NBT.B.6 · **Unit objectives:** UO2, UO5

## Readiness check (5 minutes)

1. Divide 84 ÷ 4 by any method you know. Show your thinking.
2. What is 6 × 1,200? What is 5 × 30? (No long work — use place value.)
3. If quotient × divisor + remainder = dividend, what does 13 × 4 + 2 equal?

## Retrieval warm-up (3 minutes, from Unit 01)

- 7,200 ÷ 10 = 720 · 456 ÷ 100 = 4.56 — digit shifts work both directions.
- Quick oral: 24 × 40 = 960; 32 × 40 = 1,280. (You'll need these as
  "friendly" partial quotients in Lesson 04.)

## Explicit teaching

### Partial quotients: subtract friendly chunks

**Partial quotients** means this: instead of dividing digit-by-digit, you
subtract easy multiples of the divisor — big friendly chunks — until nothing
(or only a remainder) is left. Add the chunks: that's your quotient.

Friendly chunks are multiples you know cold: ×100, ×50, ×10, ×5, ×2, ×40…

**The remainder rule:** when you finish, the leftover must be **smaller than
the divisor**. If it isn't, you could subtract one more chunk — your quotient
is too small.

### Worked example 1 — no remainder

**7,248 ÷ 6.**

| Step | Chunk | Remaining |
|---|---|---|
| 6 × 1,000 = 6,000 | 1,000 | 7,248 − 6,000 = 1,248 |
| 6 × 200 = 1,200 | 200 | 1,248 − 1,200 = 48 |
| 6 × 8 = 48 | 8 | 48 − 48 = 0 |

Quotient: 1,000 + 200 + 8 = **1,208**.
Check: 1,208 × 6 = 7,248. ✓ (Inverse operation — multiplication undoes division.)

### Worked example 2 — four chunks

**5,670 ÷ 5.**

| Step | Chunk | Remaining |
|---|---|---|
| 5 × 1,000 = 5,000 | 1,000 | 5,670 − 5,000 = 670 |
| 5 × 100 = 500 | 100 | 670 − 500 = 170 |
| 5 × 30 = 150 | 30 | 170 − 150 = 20 |
| 5 × 4 = 20 | 4 | 20 − 20 = 0 |

Quotient: 1,000 + 100 + 30 + 4 = **1,134**.
Check: 1,134 × 5 = 5,670. ✓

Notice: your chunks don't have to match anyone else's. 5 × 1,000 + 5 × 100 +
5 × 34 also works (670 − 170 = 500… try it). Any correct path to 0 is fine.

### Worked example 3 — with a remainder

**1,000 ÷ 7.**

| Step | Chunk | Remaining |
|---|---|---|
| 7 × 100 = 700 | 100 | 1,000 − 700 = 300 |
| 7 × 40 = 280 | 40 | 300 − 280 = 20 |
| 7 × 2 = 14 | 2 | 20 − 14 = 6 |

Leftover 6 < 7, so we're done. Quotient: 100 + 40 + 2 = **142 R6**.
Check: 142 × 7 = 994; 994 + 6 = 1,000. ✓

### Worked example 4 — error analysis

Sam's work for 936 ÷ 3:

> 3 × 300 = 900 → 36 left; 3 × 6 = 18 → 18 left; 3 × 6 = 18 → 0.
> Quotient: 312, remainder 18.

The error: after the first two chunks the "remaining" is **18 — bigger than
the divisor 3**. Sam stopped too early. One more chunk (3 × 6 = 18) takes it
to 0, and the real quotient is 300 + 6 + 6 = **312** with remainder **0**.
Same digits, totally different meaning — **the remainder must be smaller than
the divisor**, or the job isn't finished.

## Guided practice (with hints)

**G1.** Divide **856 ÷ 4** by partial quotients. Check by multiplication.
*Hint: start with 4 × 200 = 800.*

**G2.** Divide **1,215 ÷ 9**. Check by multiplication.
*Hint: 9 × 100 = 900. What's left?*

**G3.** Divide **2,508 ÷ 3**. Check by multiplication.
*Hint: 3 × 800 = 2,400.*

## Independent practice

**I1.** Divide by partial quotients; check each by multiplication:
(a) 4,806 ÷ 9 (b) 9,216 ÷ 8.
**I2.** Divide 743 ÷ 5. Write the full check (quotient × divisor + remainder).
**I3.** **Explain your reasoning:** Two learners both get 416 R4 for 2,916 ÷ 7.
One used chunks 400 + 10 + 6; the other used 300 + 100 + 16. Are both correct?
Why is that allowed?
**I4.** Find and fix: "2,508 ÷ 3 = 86." Show the missing place-value piece.

## Applied task

A school library has 1,152 new books and 4 shelves to fill evenly. How many
books per shelf? Show partial quotients and write one sentence explaining why
your check (quotient × 4) proves the shelves are even.

## Exit check

Divide **2,916 ÷ 7** by partial quotients. Write the quotient, the remainder,
and the multiplication check.

## Supports, alternate response modes, extensions

- **Reading/language access:** "partial quotient" = *one chunk of the answer*.
  Keep a three-column table template (chunk × divisor / chunk / remaining) on
  hand.
- **Alternate response mode:** build the subtraction with base-ten blocks or
  labeled paper strips instead of writing every row; dictate chunks aloud.
- **Extension:** 13,608 ÷ 12 = 1,134 — estimate first (about 1,100), then take
  bigger chunks (12 × 1,000 = 12,000). What changes when the divisor has two
  digits? (Preview of Lesson 04.)

## Teacher note

- **Likely misconceptions:** stopping while remainder ≥ divisor (Sam's error);
  losing track of subtractions (grid paper helps); choosing chunks so small the
  table takes forever (nudge toward ×100/×10 chunks); forgetting the final
  check-by-multiplication.
- **Answer-key reference:** all items solved in `answer-key.md` (Lesson 03).
