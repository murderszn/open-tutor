# Practice Bank — Unit 02: Multi-Digit Multiplication and Division

Practice and game bank keyed to the unit's 16 sessions. Materials are ordinary
household items; nothing is purchased. Every game is strategy-based — **no
timed speed tests**.

## Game 1 — Estimate Duel (Sessions 02, 06, 14)

Two players, one paper each. The adult reads a problem (e.g., 48 × 63,
2,905 ÷ 29, 287 × 42). Each player writes an estimate *and* whether they
rounded up, down, or mixed. Then both compute exactly. Scoring: 1 point for
the closer estimate, 1 point for the correct exact answer, 1 bonus point for
naming the direction of the estimate's bias. First to 7 wins. (UO1, UO2, UO5)

## Game 2 — Partial-Product Match (Sessions 04, 14)

Write each factor of a multiplication on cards, split by place value
(23 → "20" and "3"; 45 → "40" and "5"). Deal the cards face down; players flip
two and write the partial product (e.g., 20 × 40 = 800). First to collect all
four partial products of a problem and add them correctly takes the round.
(UO1, UO5)

## Game 3 — Chunk Race (Sessions 06, 14)

For a division (e.g., 7,248 ÷ 6, 936 ÷ 24), players alternate subtracting
friendly chunks on a shared table. A chunk must be a multiple the player can
justify ("6 × 200 = 1,200 — easy"). Smallest number of chunks to reach a valid
remainder wins the round. (UO2, UO5)

## Game 4 — Remainder Court (Sessions 11, 14)

The adult reads a remainder story; the learner plays judge and must deliver
all three verdicts: (1) full groups, (2) leftover, (3) groups needed to hold
everything. Sample docket: 1,000 eggs / cartons of 12 → 83, 4, 84; 775 buttons
/ boxes of 18 → 43, 1, 44; 2,916 students / buses of 72 → 40, 36, 41.
(UO2, UO3)

*2,916 ÷ 72: 72 × 40 = 2,880 → 36 left; 36 < 72, so 40 R36. Buses needed: 41.*

## Game 5 — Parentheses Switch (Sessions 10, 14)

The adult writes an ungrouped string like `3 × 126 + 57`. Players take turns
placing one pair of parentheses to make the expression match a given story
("3 bundles of 126 pencils and 57 erasers" → `3 × (126 + 57)` = 549;
"3 bundles of pencils plus 57 loose erasers" → `(3 × 126) + 57` = 435).
Evaluate after each placement. (UO4)

## Session-keyed practice sets

### Set A — Estimation (Sessions 01–02)

1. Estimate, then compute: 46 × 32. (Estimate ≈ 1,500; exact 1,472.)
2. Estimate, then compute: 64 × 29. (Estimate ≈ 1,800; exact 1,856.)
3. Estimate 5,670 ÷ 5 with compatible numbers, then divide. (≈ 1,100; exact 1,134.)
4. **Error hunt:** "Tariq estimates 14 × 126 ≈ 10 × 100 = 1,000 and concludes
   the true answer is about 1,000." What two things is he wrong about? (A weak
   estimate is still information about *direction*; and 14 rounds toward 15, not
   10 — a better estimate is 15 × 130 = 1,950.)

### Set B — Standard algorithm (Sessions 03–04)

1. 18 × 57 = 1,026 (show both rows; check with partial products).
2. 25 × 38 = 950 (estimate first).
3. 4,231 × 3 = 12,693 (watch the carries).
4. **Error hunt:** "Lena's work: 29 × 41 = 1,160." Find the missing tens-row
   zero. (29 × 40 = 1,160 is the tens row alone; the ones row 29 × 1 = 29 is
   missing → 1,189.)

### Set C — Partial quotients, one-digit divisors (Sessions 05–06)

1. 856 ÷ 4 = 214. Check: 214 × 4 = 856.
2. 1,215 ÷ 9 = 135. Check: 135 × 9 = 1,215.
3. 2,508 ÷ 3 = 836. Check: 836 × 3 = 2,508.
4. **Error hunt:** "Priya: 743 ÷ 5 = 148 R8." (Remainder 8 ≥ divisor 5 —
   one more chunk: 5 × 1 = 5 → 3 left. Answer: 148 R3.)

### Set D — Two-digit divisors and remainders (Sessions 07, 11)

1. 1,152 ÷ 24 = 48. Check: 48 × 24 = 1,152.
2. 2,860 ÷ 26 = 110. Check: 110 × 26 = 2,860.
3. 1,960 ÷ 28 = 70. Check: 70 × 28 = 1,960.
4. 3,108 ÷ 29 = 107 R5. Check: 107 × 29 + 5 = 3,108.
5. **Story trio** (775 buttons, boxes of 18): full boxes = 43; leftover = 1;
   boxes to hold all = 44.

### Set E — Parentheses and expressions (Sessions 09–10)

1. Evaluate (14 × 126) + (18 × 57) + (25 × 38) + 70 = 3,810.
2. Evaluate 100 − [ (12 + 8) × 2 ] = 60.
3. Write the eggs remainder as one expression: 1,000 − (83 × 12) = 4.
4. **Error hunt:** "Theo writes (20 − 8) + 4 = 16 for the story 'spend 8 of 20,
   then get 4 back.'" (Wrong grouping: the story is 20 − (8 − 4) = 16 —
   same number by luck, wrong expression. Try 'spend 8 of 20, then spend 4
   more': 20 − (8 + 4) = 8 vs. his 16. The expression must match the story.)

## Fact-fluency drills (any session)

- 10 × 10 multiplication facts, mixed order, oral — 2 minutes.
- "Friendly chunk" call-outs: adult says "24 × ?", learner answers ×10, ×20,
  ×30, ×40 (240, 480, 720, 960); same for 32 and 18.
- Division-fact reversals: 7 × 8 = 56 → 56 ÷ 7 = 8, 56 ÷ 8 = 7.

- S03: 34 × 100, 5 × 1,000; "a partial product is one piece of the whole."

- S07: 1,280 ÷ 10, 45.6 × 10; remainder rule aloud.
