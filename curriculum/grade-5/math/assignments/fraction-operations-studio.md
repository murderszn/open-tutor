## 🎯 Mission Brief
A community group is hosting a giant Friday Family Game Night banquet! You are the Head Chef in charge of modifying the family's secret Artisan Flatbread Pizza and Fresh Lemonade recipes. To feed everyone, you need to scale up ingredients, add fractions with different denominators, and split batches evenly.

---

## 📜 Recipe 1: Artisan Flatbread Pizza

A single batch recipe calls for:
- Flour: $2\frac{1}{2}$ cups
- Warm Water: $\frac{3}{4}$ cup
- Olive Oil: $\frac{1}{3}$ cup
- Shredded Mozzarella: $1\frac{2}{3}$ cups
- Grated Parmesan: $\frac{3}{8}$ cup

### Cooking Challenges:

#### 1. Finding Common Denominators for Liquids
To mix the liquids, you combine the Warm Water ($\frac{3}{4}$ cup) and Olive Oil ($\frac{1}{3}$ cup).
- What is the least common denominator (LCD) between 4 and 3?
- Rewrite both fractions with this common denominator.
- Add them together to find the total liquid volume. Express as an improper fraction and as a mixed number.

#### 2. Cheese Blend Total
You combine the Shredded Mozzarella ($1\frac{2}{3}$ cups) and Grated Parmesan ($\frac{3}{8}$ cup) in a big bowl.
- What is the common denominator between 3 and 8?
- Convert both fractions and add them. How much total cheese do you have in all?

#### 3. Doubling the Recipe
You need to make **2 full batches** of flatbread dough!
- Multiply the flour amount ($2\frac{1}{2}$ cups) by $2$. How many total cups of flour do you need?
- Multiply the warm water amount ($\frac{3}{4}$ cup) by $2$. How many total cups of water?

## 🍋 Recipe 2: Fresh Lemonade Pitcher Division

You brewed a giant pitcher with **4 quarts** of sweet fresh lemonade.

#### 4. Serving Portions (Whole Number Divided by Unit Fraction)
Each small celebration cup holds exactly **$\frac{1}{4}$ quart** of lemonade.
- How many full cups can you pour from the 4-quart pitcher?
- Model and solve: $4 \div \frac{1}{4} = \text{?}$

#### 5. Sharing the Leftover Batch (Unit Fraction Divided by Whole Number)
At the end of dinner, there is only **$\frac{1}{3}$ of a dessert pan** left.
- You want to share this leftover dessert equally among yourself, Learner, and Learner (3 kids).
- What fraction of the original pan does each equal portion represent? Show the division that supports your answer.
- Model and solve: $\frac{1}{3} \div 3 = \text{?}$
