## 📜 Historical Mission

Imagine stepping into a time machine set for the year **1750**. The thirteen British colonies along the Atlantic coast are bustling with activity, but life looks completely different depending on whether you step out in Boston, Philadelphia, or Williamsburg!

The thirteen colonies were split into **three distinct regions**:
1. **New England Colonies** (Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Connecticut, Rhode Island)
2. **Middle Colonies** (New York, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Delaware)
3. **Southern Colonies** (Maryland, Virginia, North Carolina, South Carolina, Georgia)

Your job is to compare life across these three regions and apply for a colonial trade or job!

Fill in the facts for each colonial region:

| Region | Climate & Soil | Major Jobs & Economy | Daily Life / Community Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **New England** | Cold, long winters; thin, rocky soil | Shipbuilding, fishing/whaling, timber, trading | Puritan religion, church meetings, grammar schools |
| **Middle Colonies** ("The Breadbasket") | Moderate climate; rich, fertile soil | Growing grains (wheat, rye, barley), ironworks, bustling merchants | Diverse cultures (Quakers, Dutch, German), trade ports |
| **Southern Colonies** | Warm, humid climate; long growing season, fertile flatlands | Cash crops: tobacco, rice, indigo | Large plantations and small farms; heavy reliance on enslaved Black labor |

## 🔨 Part 2: Choose Your Colonial Trade (Job Application)

Back in colonial times, boys and girls learned skilled trades by becoming **apprentices** around age 10–12 to master a craft. Choose **ONE** colonial job from the list below:

- **Blacksmith**: Forging horseshoes, iron tools, nails, and cooking pots over fiery hot coals.
- **Cooper**: Handcrafting wooden barrels and casks to store flour, cider, and gunpowder.
- **Printer**: Setting lead type by hand, inking presses, and printing newspapers, pamphlets, and books (like Benjamin Franklin!).
- **Shipwright**: Constructing massive wooden sailing vessels along harbor shipyards.
- **Silversmith**: Hammering and carving beautiful silver teapots, spoons, and buckles (like Paul Revere!).

### Answer These Questions About Your Job:
1. **Which job did you choose and in which colonial town or region would you set up your shop?**
2. **What tools would you use every single day?**
3. **What is the most difficult or dangerous part of your trade?**
4. **Why is your trade essential to the survival of your community?**

## 🔍 Part 3: Uncovering the Full Story (Enslaved People in the Colonies)

Colonial history isn't just about shopkeepers and farmers. Enslaved African men, women, and children were brought to America against their will and forced to clear forests, plant crops, build houses, cook, and blacksmith without pay or freedom.

Answer in 3–4 thoughtful sentences:
*Why was the economy of the Southern colonies so dependent on the skilled, hard labor of enslaved African people? How did their farming knowledge (especially regarding rice and tobacco) help the colonies survive?*
