### Questions

**1.** Did Indigenous peoples live in North America before European explorers arrived? Approximately how many distinct nations and languages existed across the continent?

**2.** How did Indigenous nations in the **Great Plains** (such as the Lakota, Cheyenne, and Comanche) use the American bison (buffalo) for their survival?

**3.** What type of permanent homes did the **Iroquois (Haudenosaunee)** people of the Eastern Woodlands build, where multiple related families lived together?

**4.** What was the **Iroquois Confederacy (The Great Law of Peace)**, and how did it unite five (and later six) distinct nations in peace and democratic council?

**5.** In the **Pacific Northwest**, how did Native nations (such as the Haida, Tlingit, and Chinook) take advantage of the rich ocean and river environment?

**6.** What are **totem poles**, and what do the carved animal figures and symbols represent to Pacific Northwest families?

**7.** What agricultural planting method known as the **"Three Sisters"** was developed by Indigenous farming communities, and which three crops grow harmoniously together?

**8.** How did Southwestern tribes like the Hopi and Navajo weave blankets and craft pottery, and what materials did they use to build their homes?

**9.** How did Indigenous peoples view their relationship with nature and the land compared to European ideas of private land ownership?

**10.** Why is it important to understand that Indigenous nations are not just people of the ancient past, but sovereign nations with vibrant cultures living across America today?

<summary><strong>Parent Answer Key (Click to Expand)</strong></summary>

1. **Yes, for thousands of years;** there were hundreds of diverse nations (over 500) speaking hundreds of distinct languages.
2. **They used every part of the bison:** meat for food, thick hides for clothing and teepee shelter, bones for tools and weapons, and sinew for thread and bowstrings. Nothing was wasted.
3. **Longhouses** (sturdy wooden framework covered with bark, often 100+ feet long).
4. **An alliance of Indigenous nations** (Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, Seneca, and later Tuscarora) governed by a constitution of peace and a representative grand council, which influenced early American democratic thinkers.
5. **They were expert fishermen and navigators who harvested abundant salmon, halibut, seals, and whales**, and built large cedar dugout canoes.
6. **Tall wooden cedar poles carved with crests and animals** that record family histories, spiritual legends, clan lineage, and notable ancestors.
7. **Corn (maize), beans, and squash.** Corn provides a tall stalk for beans to climb; beans fix nitrogen in the soil; broad squash leaves shade the ground to prevent weeds and retain moisture.
8. **They wove sheep wool, shaped clay pottery, and built multi-story adobe homes** made of clay, water, and straw baked in the sun.
9. **They viewed the land and water as sacred gifts to be respected and cared for as stewards**, rather than individual property that could be bought, sold, or fenced off.
10. **Because hundreds of federally recognized tribal nations continue to govern themselves, preserve their traditions and languages, and contribute powerfully to modern American life.**
