### Questions

**1.** Name the **five Southern Colonies** established along the southern Atlantic coast.

**2.** What is a **cash crop**, and what were the three primary cash crops grown in the Southern Colonies?

**3.** What is the difference between an **indentured servant** and an **enslaved person**?

**4.** Which colony was founded by Lord Baltimore in 1634 as a refuge for persecuted English Catholics, and passed the historic Maryland Toleration Act of 1649?

**5.** Which colony was the last of the thirteen colonies to be founded, created in 1732 by James Oglethorpe as a place for English debtors to start a new life and as a military buffer against Spanish Florida?

**6.** What was the **plantation system**, and why did Southern plantation owners build their estates along tidal rivers?

**7.** How did enslaved Africans from West Africa bring essential knowledge and skills that made **rice cultivation** wildly successful in coastal South Carolina and Georgia?

**8.** What was the **Middle Passage**, and what were the terrifying conditions endured by captive Africans chained inside slave ships crossing the Atlantic?

**9.** How did enslaved families resist the cruelty of slavery and maintain their humanity, culture, and dignity (such as through song, spirituals, and storytelling)?

**10.** Why did the Southern Colonies have far fewer large towns and cities compared to the New England and Middle colonies?

<summary><strong>Parent Answer Key (Click to Expand)</strong></summary>

1. **Maryland, Virginia, North Carolina, South Carolina, and Georgia.**
2. **A crop grown specifically to be sold for profit rather than for personal consumption; tobacco, rice, and indigo.**
3. **An indentured servant signed a legal contract to work without pay for a set number of years (usually 4–7) in exchange for passage to America, gaining freedom after their term; an enslaved person was held in permanent, hereditary bondage for life with no legal rights.**
4. **Maryland.**
5. **Georgia.**
6. **Large agricultural estates focused on mass-producing cash crops;** plantations were built along deep tidal rivers so ocean-going merchant ships could dock directly at plantation wharves to load crops.
7. **Many West Africans came from societies with centuries of sophisticated expertise in growing wet rice in tidal floodplains**, engineering the complex dikes, irrigation gates, and planting techniques that planters lacked.
8. **The forced, horrific transatlantic voyage of enslaved Africans to the Americas;** captives were packed shoulder-to-shoulder in dark, suffocating ship holds with little food or water, filthy air, and rampant disease.
9. **By singing spirituals with secret messages of hope and escape, sharing African folk tales (like Anansi the spider), practicing faith, creating close-knit surrogate families, and secretly preserving African rhythms and foods.**
10. **Because the Southern economy was based on self-contained, isolated rural plantations and farms**, each with its own river access, reducing the need for centralized market cities.
