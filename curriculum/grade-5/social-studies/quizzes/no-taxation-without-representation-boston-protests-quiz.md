### Questions

**1.** What famous protest slogan did American colonists shout to protest laws passed by the British Parliament, and what did it mean?

**2.** What was the **Stamp Act of 1765**, and what kinds of everyday paper items were taxed with an official embossed royal stamp?

**3.** What secret colonial patriotic organization, founded by Samuel Adams in Boston, organized rallies, hung effigies of tax collectors, and led boycotts?

**4.** What is a **boycott**, and how did colonial merchants and the "Daughters of Liberty" effectively use boycotts against British goods?

**5.** What deadly confrontation took place on icy King Street in Boston on **March 5, 1770**, when nervous British soldiers fired into an angry crowd of colonists throwing snowballs, chunks of ice, and oyster shells?

**6.** Who was **Crispus Attucks**, and why is he revered in American history as the very first martyr of the American Revolution?

**7.** What famous Boston silversmith created a dramatic, widely printed engraving of the Boston Massacre that was used as anti-British propaganda throughout the colonies?

**8.** What happened during the **Boston Tea Party** on the cold night of December 16, 1773?

**9.** How did King George III and the British Parliament punish Boston for the dumped tea through the harsh laws known in the colonies as the **"Intolerable Acts" (Coercive Acts)**?

**10.** In response to the Intolerable Acts, representatives from twelve colonies met in Philadelphia in September 1774. What was the name of this historic meeting?

<summary><strong>Parent Answer Key (Click to Expand)</strong></summary>

1. **"No taxation without representation!"** It meant that because colonists had no elected representatives in the British Parliament in London, Parliament had no legal right to levy taxes on them.
2. **A British tax requiring colonists to pay for a royal stamp on all printed paper goods**, including legal documents, newspapers, playing cards, calendars, and pamphlets.
3. **The Sons of Liberty.**
4. **Refusing to buy, sell, or use goods from a particular source.** Colonists refused to purchase British cloth, tea, and tools, while women made their own homespun fabric and herbal tea, hurting British merchants financially until Parliament repealed the taxes.
5. **The Boston Massacre.**
6. **A brave sailor of African and Wampanoag descent** who stood at the front of the crowd demanding freedom; he was shot twice in the chest, becoming the first person to die for American liberty.
7. **Paul Revere.**
8. **Members of the Sons of Liberty disguised themselves as Mohawk Indians, boarded three British tea ships in Boston Harbor, and dumped 342 chests of expensive British East India Company tea into the ocean.**
9. **They closed Boston Harbor to all shipping, abolished Massachusetts' town meetings and self-government, appointed British General Thomas Gage as military governor, and forced colonists to quarter British soldiers in their homes.**
10. **The First Continental Congress.**
