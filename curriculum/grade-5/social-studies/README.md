# Grade 5 Social Studies

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are delivered;
the eight units are planned, not yet written. See [scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained for
one grade-5 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 5 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-5 social studies course organized as an
American story in chronological order: North American geography and Indigenous
histories told with sources; colonial societies, labor, trade, and diverse
perspectives; the Revolution's causes, events, and competing viewpoints;
founding documents, the Constitution, and citizenship; the early republic —
expansion, displacement, and resistance; nineteenth-century change, abolition,
and reform; the Civil War, Reconstruction, and rights foundations; and a
historical-inquiry, U.S.-geography, and civic capstone.

Sessions are about 40 minutes, four per week: a concept-launch session, a
skills-practice session, an investigation or application session, and a
review/check session each week. Work is map- and evidence-based: learners read
and construct maps, sequence events across centuries, compare historical
sources and perspectives, weigh economic choices, and build evidence-backed
claims. Grade-5 learners do independent written practice (10–14 tasks) that the
adult reviews the same day.

Difficult history (displacement, enslavement, conquest, war) is named honestly
and plainly at an age-appropriate level with adult guidance — never glossed,
never graphic — and every encounter is examined from at least two perspectives.
Indigenous nations are taught as living, sovereign communities with homelands,
not as a past-tense topic.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. **Do geographic inquiry at North American scale:** construct maps and
   graphic representations of familiar and unfamiliar places; use maps,
   satellite images, photographs, and other representations to explain how
   location connects to environmental characteristics; use maps of different
   scales to describe cultural and environmental characteristics; explain why
   environmental characteristics vary among regions. (C3 D2.Geo.1–3, 10.3-5)
2. **Explain Indigenous histories and living nations:** explain how
   environment shaped culture areas and ways of life; explain how culture
   influences how people modify and adapt to environments; describe how places
   change over time; name nations as living, sovereign communities with
   homelands. (C3 D2.Geo.2, 4–6, 8.3-5; D2.His.2, 4, 5.3-5)
3. **Explain colonial societies with diverse perspectives:** compare the three
   colonial regions' environment, settlement, and daily life; explain labor
   systems — including enslaved labor, named plainly — and trade; explain why
   people in the same period held different perspectives. (C3 D2.His.2, 4, 5,
   14.3-5; D2.Eco.3, 4.3-5; D2.Geo.8, 11.3-5)
4. **Explain the Revolution's causes, events, and competing viewpoints:**
   build and use chronological sequences; compare sources about the same
   event; infer a source's audience and purpose from the source itself;
   explain probable causes and effects; use evidence to develop a claim about
   the past. (C3 D2.His.1, 10, 11, 14, 16.3-5; D3.1–3.4)
5. **Understand founding documents, the Constitution, and citizenship:**
   examine the origins and purposes of rules, laws, and key U.S.
   constitutional provisions; distinguish the responsibilities and powers of
   government officials; explain how groups make rules that create
   responsibilities and protect freedoms, and how people change rules and
   laws. (C3 D2.Civ.1–5, 12.3-5; D2.His.3.3-5)
6. **Explain the early republic — expansion, displacement, and resistance:**
   explain how the movement of people, goods, and ideas reshapes places;
   explain perspectives of displaced nations and of resisters; explain
   probable causes and effects of expansion. (C3 D2.Geo.7, 11.3-5; D2.His.4,
   5, 14.3-5)
7. **Explain nineteenth-century change — abolition and reform:** generate
   questions about individuals and groups who shaped change; explain how
   policies and movements change society and how people change rules and
   laws; illustrate historical means of changing society. (C3 D2.His.3,
   14.3-5; D2.Civ.12–14.3-5)
8. **Explain the Civil War, Reconstruction, and rights foundations:** build
   chronological sequences of the war and its aftermath; explain causes and
   effects; use evidence to develop claims about rights; explain how rules
   and laws change society. (C3 D2.His.1, 14, 16.3-5; D2.Civ.12.3-5)
9. **Inquire and argue from evidence:** identify disciplinary concepts behind
   compelling and supporting questions; determine which sources will help;
   gather information from multiple sources using origin, structure, and
   context; distinguish fact from opinion to judge credibility; identify
   evidence from multiple sources; develop claims and construct arguments and
   explanations with reasons, sequence, examples, and details; present
   summaries. (C3 D1.2–1.5, D3.1–3.4, D4.1–4.3.3-5)
10. **Take informed action on a civic or community question:** draw on
    disciplinary concepts to explain challenges people have faced; explain
    strategies people use to address problems and predict possible results;
    use deliberative and democratic procedures to decide on and act on a
    classroom, school, or community civic problem. (C3 D4.6–4.8.3-5)

Standards source: the College, Career, and Civic Life (C3) Framework for Social
Studies State Standards (National Council for the Social Studies), grades 3–5
band. The four Dimensions and D2 sub-strands were re-confirmed on the NCSS C3
landing page 2026-10-04; codes and descriptions were verified against the
framework's 3–5 indicator tables (merged grade-3 audit, 2026-10-03). **No state
adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification is claimed.** The 3–5 band
spans three grades; this track teaches each indicator at the grade-5
upper-band level, with source criticism, evidence-based claims, and informed
action taught more independently than in grade 4. Indicators the framework
defers to later bands (D2.His.7–8, 9–12; D2.His.15, 6–8) are not taught here.

## Existing materials and their status

The legacy library under this folder (1 subject README, 4 assignments, 14
quizzes, 1 template — 20 Markdown files, matching the issue baseline) was
re-audited 2026-10-04 — see the scope-and-sequence for keep/revise decisions
per file. Coverage is strongest in U01–U03 (geography, colonial, Revolution);
**U04, U05, U06, and U07 have no legacy files at all** and will be built new.
Every quiz carries an embedded "Parent Answer Key" beside the student
questions; the unit sections will separate keys into teacher guides before any
assignment. Two reference problems were found across the quizzes: every
"Reference" link points to the Library of Congress American Revolution
collection even on geography and Indigenous-nation quizzes, and every
"Explainer video" is a templated YouTube search link for a nonexistent video
title — unit builds replace both with topic-matched, verified references.
No copyrighted text was reproduced in any track file.

## Accessibility and safety

- Maps and diagrams always pair color with symbols and labels (never
  color-only); every image carries alt text and a text-only description.
- Map and timeline work offers oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative
  options; the adult scribes written explanations when writing is not the
  skill being measured.
- Original passages are written at grade-5 level; vocabulary is pre-taught;
  key terms appear with kid-friendly definitions in each lesson.
- Videos (in later unit Resource Packs) require captions or transcripts; the
  adult previews for ads, age suitability, and accuracy.
- Investigations are observation-, map-, and source-based; any community
  contact is adult-supervised and stays private — no learner names, photos,
  addresses, or schedules enter this repository. Capstone presentations are
  shared only with the guiding adult's approval, never published from this
  track.
- Displacement, enslavement, conquest, and war content is adult-led with
  pre-selected, age-appropriate sources presenting multiple perspectives;
  nothing graphic is assigned. Indigenous nations are named as living
  communities with homelands throughout.
