# Teacher Guide — Unit 04

This guide is for the guiding adult only. It is not a learner file. All
solutions live in the [answer key](answer-key.md).

## Session-by-session notes

### Week 15 — The Declaration's ideas

- **S1–S2 (Lesson 01).** Read the one-paragraph paraphrase aloud first,
  then the exact short phrases. Do not assign the full Declaration text
  to the learner; it is adult reference (Resource Pack, Reference 1).
  Spend most of S2 on A3 and A6 — the "who was not included" correction
  is the week's hardest idea and the unit's moral center. If the learner
  resists the idea that a beloved document excluded people, return to
  Phillis Wheatley (A5): a real person, not an abstraction.
- **S3 (claims task).** The learner starts the evidence chart (A8).
  Accept any honest for/against pair from Lesson 01; the chart is
  revisited in Lessons 02–04 and graded only in the assessment, where
  any defensible position earns full marks. Keep the chart visible all
  unit.
- **S4 (review + weekly check).** Use Practice Bank 1 (P1–P6). Review
  same day; re-teach "equal in rights, not equal in everything" if P1
  or P5 is weak.

### Week 16 — The Constitution's structure

- **S1–S2 (Lesson 02).** Teach the Articles' failure as a testable
  chain (no tax → no money → no army/debts paid), not as a list to
  memorize. For B6, say the Three-Fifths sentence out loud yourself
  before asking the learner to write it: "It counted enslaved people as
  three-fifths of a person for representation, and it freed nobody."
  Learners who soften this into "it was about counting people" need
  the follow-up: counting them gave slaveholding states more seats in
  Congress.
- **S3 (Lesson 05 + project launch).** Run the picture investigation
  before the preamble drafting — the "who is missing" discussion (E3)
  makes the class constitution's fairness check (project rubric) feel
  necessary rather than abstract. End S3 with the E7 preamble sentence
  written down.
- **S4 (review + weekly check).** Use Practice Bank 2 (P7–P12). The
  branches-and-checks diagram should be on the table for every check
  this week; remove it in Week 18 to test recall.

### Week 17 — The Bill of Rights

- **S1–S2 (Lesson 03).** Do not teach all ten amendments as equal
  weight: the 1st, 4th, 5th, and 6th carry the scenarios; the 2nd, 3rd,
  7th, 9th, and 10th are taught for recognition. For the 2nd Amendment,
  use the unit's neutral plain language and do not extend into current
  policy debate — the unit's job is what the amendment says, not what
  today's laws should be. C8(ii) needs the adult's framing: opinion vs.
  disruption, per the lesson's teacher note.
- **S3 (deliberation + project).** Draft the class bill of rights
  (project step 3). Enforce the fairness check strictly: any proposed
  right that singles out a person or a small group is revised or
  dropped, with the reason stated aloud. This is the unit's
  minority-rights lesson in practice.
- **S4 (review + weekly check).** Use Practice Bank 3 (P13–P18).

### Week 18 — Participation and changing laws

- **S1–S2 (Lesson 04).** D6 is the week's key item; if the learner says
  "the majority wins, end of story," replay the lunchroom example from
  the assessment (Part 5) and ask who the rule hurts. D5's policy
  proposal should name a real problem the learner cares about; a made-up
  problem produces a made-up policy.
- **S3 (quiz + project completion).** Give the Week-18 quiz first, then
  the ratification vote — the vote's excitement makes a poor setting
  for a quiz taken after it. Record the vote count honestly, including
  "no" votes; a failed ratification followed by revision is a better
  lesson than a forced unanimous vote.
- **S4 (unit review + assessment).** Review the evidence chart before
  the assessment. Part 4 accepts any position (agree, disagree, partly
  agree) that is supported; do not steer the learner toward a preferred
  verdict.

## Misconception notes (all lessons)

1. "Equal" means "the same in every way" → correct to "equal in
   rights" (Lesson 01).
2. The Declaration ended slavery or gave women the vote → correct
   with dates: 13th Amendment 1865; 19th Amendment 1920 (Lessons 01,
   03).
3. The President makes the laws → correct to "Congress makes them;
   the President carries them out and can veto" (Lesson 02).
4. The Three-Fifths Compromise freed people or measured human worth →
   correct to "a political counting rule that increased slaveholding
   states' power" (Lesson 02).
5. The Bill of Rights "gives" rights → correct to "it lists protections
   for rights the Declaration says people already have" (Lesson 03).
6. Democracy means the majority always gets its way → correct with
   minority rights and compromise (Lesson 04).
7. An old-looking picture is evidence → correct to "style is not a
   date stamp; only sources from the time prove what happened"
   (Lesson 05).

## Reteaching guidance

- **Branches confused (B2, P7 weak):** Re-teach with verbs only —
  *passes* (legislative), *signs/enforces* (executive), *rules*
  (judicial) — using three fresh news-style sentences a day until the
  sort is automatic.
- **Amendment path confused (C6, Q8 weak):** Use the two-fraction chant
  — "two-thirds propose, three-fourths ratify" — and the project
  amendment process as the concrete model.
- **Claim-with-evidence weak (A8, assessment Part 4):** Return to the
  Unit 03 routine: claim → evidence 1 + reason → evidence 2 + reason.
  Accept oral claims first, then require the written version.
- **Deliberation as "arguing to win" (D4, D6 weak):** Re-run the
  hallway-rule worked example (Lesson 04) with roles reversed: the
  learner must argue the side they disagree with for two minutes.

## Sensitive-content guidance

- Slavery, exclusion, and the Constitution's compromises are taught
  plainly and without graphic detail. If the learner asks why the
  founders allowed slavery, answer honestly: many founders profited
  from it, and the compromises were the price of getting slaveholding
  states to join the Union. Do not excuse it as "just how things
  were" — the unit's own evidence (Wheatley, the Declaration's
  language) shows people at the time knew better.
- The Declaration's "merciless Indian Savages" phrase is quoted in the
  adult reference (National Archives transcript) and named in Lesson
  01 as wrong and hurtful. Do not assign that passage as learner
  reading.
- The 2nd Amendment is taught in neutral plain language. Current gun
  policy debates are out of scope for this unit; redirect questions
  about today's laws to the adult's judgment.
- All outside sources are adult-previewed. Nothing in this unit is
  published or shared outside the class without the guiding adult's
  review (see the track's privacy rules).

## Readiness evidence

The learner is ready for Unit 05 (early republic) when they can, without
the diagram: name the three branches and one check each; state the
amendment path's two fractions; explain one Bill of Rights protection
with a scenario; and write a claim supported by two pieces of evidence.
If two or more of these are missing, use the midyear review week
(Week 19) to re-teach Lessons 02–03 before Unit 05 begins.
