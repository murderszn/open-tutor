# Project — Our Classroom Constitution

**Launch:** Week 16, S3 (preamble, from Lesson 05, task E7).
**Drafting:** Week 17, S3 (articles and bill of rights).
**Ratification:** Week 18, S3 (vote and reflection).
**Deliverables:** (1) a written class constitution; (2) a ratification
vote record; (3) a one-paragraph reflection. The adult reviews the
draft before ratification and keeps the final document private to the
class.

## The task

Your class will write its own constitution — a real plan for how your
class governs itself for the rest of the term. It must have four parts:

1. **Preamble** — one or two sentences beginning "We the students
   of…" that state at least two purposes (modeled on the Constitution's
   Preamble: for example, "to learn in peace" and "to treat everyone
   fairly").
2. **Articles** — three short articles naming three class jobs and who
   does them, modeled on the three branches: one group that proposes
   rules, one person or group that carries them out day to day, and one
   group that settles disagreements about what a rule means. (For a
   single learner working with an adult, the three jobs are roles the
   learner and adult share and rotate.)
3. **Bill of Rights** — five classroom rights (for example: the right to
   speak respectfully without being laughed at; the right to a fair
   hearing before a consequence; the right to ask for help). No right
   may single out, exclude, or embarrass any person.
4. **Amendment process** — a written rule for changing this
   constitution later: who may propose a change, what vote is needed to
   propose it, and what vote is needed to ratify it (the class version
   of Lesson 03's two-step path).

**Ratification:** The constitution takes effect only if at least
three-fourths of the class votes for it — the same fraction the states
needed for a real amendment. Record the vote count honestly, including
"no" votes. If ratification fails, the class deliberates once more,
amends the draft, and votes again.

## Steps

1. **Preamble (Week 16, S3).** Use Lesson 05's E7 sentence as the
   starting draft. Deliberate: does it state two real purposes? Revise
   until the class accepts it.
2. **Articles (Week 17, S3, first half).** In small groups, draft one
   article each: who proposes rules, who carries them out, who settles
   disputes. Present each article with one reason for its wording.
3. **Bill of Rights (Week 17, S3, second half).** Brainstorm ten
   candidate rights, deliberate, and keep five. Check each kept right:
   is it fair to everyone, including the smallest minority in the room?
4. **Amendment process (Week 17, S3, close).** Agree the two-step rule
   and write it down. Test it: propose a silly amendment and walk it
   through both steps to prove the process works.
5. **Ratification and reflection (Week 18, S3).** Vote, record the
   count, and each learner writes one paragraph: one rule they are
   proud of, and one compromise they made (something they gave up so
   the class could agree).

## Roles (rotate where possible)

- **Facilitator:** runs the deliberation, makes sure every view is
  heard, states the decision and its reason.
- **Recorder:** writes the agreed wording exactly; reads it back for
  approval.
- **Presenter:** presents one article or right to the class with its
  reason.

## Materials

Paper or a shared document, the Lesson 02 branches-and-checks diagram,
the Lesson 03 plain-language amendment table, and the learner's
evidence chart.

## Rubric (20 points)

| Criterion (5 points each) | 5 — Strong | 3 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preamble and purposes | Two clear purposes, "We the students…" opening, language the class actually uses | One purpose, or purposes unclear | No preamble or purposes missing |
| Articles (three jobs) | Three distinct jobs named (propose, carry out, settle disputes), with who does each | Two jobs named, or roles confused | One job or roles missing |
| Bill of rights | Five rights, all fair to everyone, none singling out any person | Three or four rights, or one unfair right | Fewer than three rights, or rights that exclude |
| Amendment process, ratification, reflection | Two-step process written and tested; honest vote count recorded; reflection names a proud rule and a compromise | Process or vote record incomplete; reflection names only one element | No process, no vote record, or no reflection |

**Scoring note for the adult:** A constitution that passes with a
recorded vote but an unfair right loses points on the bill-of-rights
criterion even though it was "ratified" — ratification does not make an
unfair rule fair. That is the unit's minority-rights lesson in action.
