# Practice Banks — Unit 04

Use these banks for S2/S3 sessions and the S4 weekly checks. The adult
reviews written work the same day. Solutions are in the
[answer key](answer-key.md) — never beside the learner's items.

## Bank 1 — Declaration ideas (Week 15)

**P1.** Define "unalienable right" in your own words, and name one right
the Declaration lists.

**P2.** A town council makes a rule without ever asking the town's
people, and there is no way to object. Does that rule have the consent
of the governed? Explain in one sentence.

**P3.** True or false, with one reason: "Most of the Declaration of
Independence is a list of complaints against King George III."

**P4.** Who was the principal author of the Declaration, and in what
year was it approved?

**P5.** The Declaration says "all men are created equal." Name one group
that was not treated as equal in 1776, and one fact showing it.

**P6.** Open response (two or three sentences): Give one promise from
the Declaration and one way practice in 1776 fell short of it. Explain
why the gap matters for understanding American history.

## Bank 2 — Branches, checks, federalism (Week 16)

**P7.** Which branch is acting? (a) The Senate confirms a new Supreme
Court justice. (b) The President orders a department to enforce a new
law. (c) A federal court rules that a state law violates the
Constitution.

**P8.** The President vetoes a bill. What vote in Congress can still
make it law? Name the fraction and the houses.

**P9.** The Great Compromise created two houses of Congress. Name both
houses and state how seats are divided in each.

**P10.** Which level of government — federal, state, or shared — runs
public schools? Which level prints money? Which level collects taxes?

**P11.** How long is one term for: (a) a House member, (b) a senator,
(c) a president? How many terms may a president serve at most?

**P12.** The President nominates a federal judge. Which body confirms
the nomination, and which branch does that body belong to?

## Bank 3 — Rights scenarios (Week 17)

**P13.** How many freedoms does the First Amendment protect? Name
three of them.

**P14.** Match: (a) 4th Amendment, (b) 6th Amendment, (c) 8th Amendment —
with: (i) no cruel and unusual punishments; (ii) no unreasonable
searches; a warrant needs probable cause; (iii) speedy public trial,
impartial jury, and a lawyer.

**P15.** In what year were the first ten amendments ratified? What are
they called together?

**P16.** How many amendments does the Constitution have in total?
Which amendment number, ratified in 1992, began as one of the twelve
proposals of 1789?

**P17.** Scenario: A mayor orders the town newspaper to stop printing
criticism of the town council. Which amendment is at stake, and is the
mayor's order consistent with it? Give one reason.

**P18.** Scenario: A person accused of theft is tried without ever being
allowed a lawyer. Which amendment is at stake, and what does it require?

## Bank 4 — Participation and deliberation (Week 18)

**P19.** Match each decision to its best procedure — vote, consensus,
or leader decides: (a) choosing the class party theme; (b) setting the
school's fire-escape routes; (c) three friends writing rules for a club
everyone must accept.

**P20.** Put these deliberation steps in order: decide and state the
reason; hear all views; state the problem; review after trying the
rule; weigh reasons.

**P21.** Name one real school problem and propose one policy for it,
with one step for making it happen. (Short version of Lesson 04, task
D5.)

**P22.** A student listens quietly while a classmate explains a view
they disagree with, then answers with a reason instead of an insult.
Which civic virtue is the student practicing? Name one more civic
virtue and give an example of it.

**P23.** Name two lawful ways ordinary people can help change a law.

**P24.** A class votes 24–1 to exclude one student from every class
game. The majority says "we voted, so it's fair." Is it? Explain in two
sentences, using majority rule and minority rights.
