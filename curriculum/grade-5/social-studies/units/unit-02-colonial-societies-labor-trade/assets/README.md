### `colonial-port-scene.png`

- **Used in:** [Lesson 05 — Colonial Port Towns: Reading the Trade Map](../lessons/lesson-05-port-town-trade-investigation.md)
  (image observation activity, Week 8 S3).
- **Generation prompt (verbatim):** "AI illustration of a busy colonial
  American harbor around 1750: tall-masted wooden sailing ships tied at
  wooden docks, workers at a distance loading barrels and crates, simple
  wooden warehouses and small town buildings along the shore, calm water,
  daytime. No readable text, no signage, no labels, no close-up faces.
  Painterly, educational illustration style."
- **Tool:** media.generate_image (built-in image pipeline).
- **Date generated:** 2026-10-18.
- **Learning purpose:** Harbor-scene observation for the Lesson 05
  activity — learners list 3 observable details, infer what each suggests
  about colonial trade, and state what the picture cannot tell them.
- **Factual review:** Inspected 2026-10-18. Generic reconstruction: four
  to five tall-masted wooden sailing ships at long wooden docks; small
  distant figures loading barrels and crates; simple wooden warehouses
  with smoking chimneys; small houses and trees on the hillside; calm
  reflective water. No readable text, signage, labels, flags with text,
  borders, or close-up faces. No claim about any specific real port; no
  historical evidence depicted.
- **Caption (as used in the lesson):** "AI illustration of a busy colonial
  American harbor around 1750 — tall-masted sailing ships at wooden docks,
  workers loading barrels and crates, warehouses and a small town on
  shore. Generated 2026-10-18 for this lesson's observation activity; a
  reconstruction, not a primary source."
- **Alt text:** "AI illustration of a busy colonial American harbor around
  1750: tall-masted wooden sailing ships tied at wooden docks, workers at
  a distance loading barrels and crates, simple wooden warehouses and
  small town buildings along the shore, calm water, daytime. No readable
  text, no close-up faces."
- **Text-only alternative:** provided in the lesson (enumerates the same
  features); carries identical instructional content for learners who
  cannot use the image.
- **Limitations:** AI illustration, not a photograph and not a primary
  source; learners must not cite it as historical evidence. It shows a
  scene, not a system — it cannot show who the workers were, where ships
  were bound, or whether labor was free or forced.

### `triangular-trade-map.svg`

- **Used in:** [Lesson 05](../lessons/lesson-05-port-town-trade-investigation.md)
  (trace-the-triangle task, Week 8 S3).
- **Source:** deterministic SVG authored for this unit (not generated);
  supplements the required generated illustration where precision matters.
- **Date authored:** 2026-10-18.
- **Learning purpose:** schematic flow diagram of the simplified
  triangular-trade pattern — New England goods to West Africa; forced
  voyage of enslaved people to the Caribbean and colonies; colonial goods
  to New England and Britain.
- **Factual review:** Flow schematic with labeled boxes and arrows; no
  geographic boundaries drawn, so no invented borders. Labels name goods
  and the forced nature of the Middle Passage leg; caption states the
  pattern is simplified and not every voyage followed it.
- **Caption (as used in the lesson):** "Simplified triangular-trade flow,
  1600s–1700s (deterministic diagram). Historians use 'triangular trade'
  for a common route pattern — not every voyage sailed the full triangle."
- **Alt text:** carried in the SVG's `<title>`/`<desc>` and the lesson's
  image description.
- **Limitations:** a schematic, not a map to scale; omits the many
  voyages that did not follow the triangle.
