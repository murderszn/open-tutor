# Unit 02 — Colonial Societies: Labor, Trade, and Diverse Perspectives

**Grade 5 Social Studies · Weeks 7–10 of 36 · 16 sessions (~40 minutes each)**

**Status:** validated draft — delivered 2026-10-18; not yet educator-reviewed or merged.

This unit continues the story where Unit 01 left off: Europeans established
colonies along North America's Atlantic coast, and those colonies became
thirteen distinct societies shaped by their environments, their economies,
and the people — willing and unwilling — who did the work. Learners compare
the three colonial regions, trace how goods moved from field and forest to
market, distinguish indentured servitude from enslavement honestly and
plainly, and practice seeing one event through more than one person's eyes.

**Essential question:** How did colonial Americans earn a living, who did
the work, and why did different people see the same events so differently?

**Sensitive-content note (for the guiding adult):** Colonial economic life
included enslaved labor. Introduce this honestly and plainly at an
age-appropriate level — people were forced to work without freedom or pay —
pre-select all sources, and keep the focus on systems (labor, crops, trade),
on diverse perspectives, and on the people who resisted. No graphic detail.
See the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) for session-level guidance.

See the track [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) (U02, Weeks
7–10) and the [subject README](../../README.md).

## Lessons

1. [Lesson 01 — Three Colonial Regions](lessons/lesson-01-three-colonial-regions.md)
   — New England, Middle, and Southern colonies; environment → settlement →
   daily life; the roots of colonial self-rule (Week 7, S1–S2).
2. [Lesson 02 — Colonial Economic Life: Resources, Specialization, and Trade](lessons/lesson-02-colonial-economic-life.md)
   — human, physical, and natural resources; why regions specialized; trade
   within and beyond the colonies; money and profit (Week 8, S1–S2).
3. [Lesson 03 — Labor: Who Worked, and Who Chose](lessons/lesson-03-labor-systems.md)
   — family, hired, indentured, and enslaved labor; the plain legal and human
   difference between servitude and enslavement; West African expertise; the
   Middle Passage; resistance (Week 9, S1–S2).
4. [Lesson 04 — Two Viewpoints, One Event](lessons/lesson-04-perspectives.md)
   — identifying a perspective and its reasons; modeling two accounts of one
   event; whose voice is missing (Week 10, S1–S2).
5. [Lesson 05 — Colonial Port Towns: Reading the Trade Map](lessons/lesson-05-port-town-trade-investigation.md)
   — observation of the generated harbor illustration; the simplified
   triangular-trade flow; tracing one good; who benefited and who was harmed
   (Week 8, S3).

## Prerequisites (from Unit 01 and grade-4 end-of-year objectives)

- Read maps with title, key, compass rose, scale bar, and symbols; sort
  states into regions and justify with evidence.
- Explain how environment shaped ways of life; compare life in a historical
  period to life today.
- Sequence events across centuries; compare accounts of the same event;
  explain probable causes and effects.
- Weigh benefits and costs of a choice; identify resources used to produce
  goods and services; explain specialization and trade (grade-4 level).

Unit 02 re-teaches specialization and trade at the colonial scale rather
than assuming they are secure, and introduces perspective-taking as a
modeled routine before learners attempt it independently.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of this unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Compare the three colonial regions** — name the colonies in each,
   explain how environment shaped settlement and daily life in each, and
   compare colonial daily life to life today. (C3 D2.Geo.8, D2.His.2.3-5)
2. **Explain colonial economic life** — identify the natural resources,
   human capital, and physical capital behind colonial goods; explain why
   regions and countries specialized and traded; describe how money made
   exchange easier and how profit influenced sellers. (C3 D2.Eco.3, 4, 5,
   7.3-5; D2.Geo.11.3-5)
3. **Distinguish labor systems honestly** — explain family, hired,
   indentured, and enslaved labor; state plainly the legal and human
   difference between indentured servitude and enslavement; and explain who
   did which work and why. (C3 D2.His.2, 14.3-5; D2.Eco.3.3-5)
4. **Take multiple perspectives** — explain why individuals and groups in
   the same period saw the same event differently, name each perspective
   and its reasons, and connect perspectives to their historical context.
   (C3 D2.His.4, 5.3-5; D2.Civ.10.3-5)
5. **Ask supporting questions and choose sources** — pose supporting
   questions about colonial life that are open to interpretation and name
   the kinds of sources that would help answer them. (C3 D1.3, D1.5.3-5)

## Vocabulary

| Term | Kid-friendly definition |
|---|---|
| **colony** | A settlement in a new land that is ruled by a distant country. |
| **region** | An area grouped together because places in it share characteristics like landforms, climate, or culture. |
| **cash crop** | A crop grown mainly to be sold for money, not eaten by the farmer's family. |
| **plantation** | A very large farm growing one cash crop, worked by many laborers. |
| **indentured servant** | A worker who signed a contract to work for a set number of years (often for the cost of the ocean voyage), then became free. |
| **enslaved person** | A person forced to work without pay or freedom; in the colonies, enslavement was lifelong and passed to children. |
| **Middle Passage** | The forced voyage across the Atlantic that carried enslaved Africans to the Americas. |
| **specialize** | To focus on producing what you can make best or most easily, then trade for the rest. |
| **trade** | Exchanging goods or services — giving one thing to get another. |
| **barter** | Trading goods or services directly, without money. |
| **profit** | The money a seller keeps after paying all the costs of producing and selling something. |
| **port** | A harbor town where ships load and unload goods. |
| **mercantilism** | Britain's policy that colonies should supply raw materials and buy British-made goods — the "rules of the game" for colonial trade. |
| **perspective** | A point of view — how a person sees an event, shaped by who they are and what they want. |
| **primary source** | Something created at the time being studied — a letter, law, diary, tool, or building from that period. |
| **charter** | A written grant from a king giving a company or group permission to start a colony. |
| **assembly** | A group of elected representatives who make laws — like the House of Burgesses. |
| **tolerance** | Allowing people with different beliefs to live and worship freely. |
