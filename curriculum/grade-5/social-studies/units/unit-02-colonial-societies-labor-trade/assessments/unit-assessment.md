# U02 Unit Assessment — Colonial Societies: Labor, Trade, and Diverse Perspectives

**Unit:** [U02 — Colonial Societies: Labor, Trade, and Diverse Perspectives](../README.md)
**When:** Week 10 S4 · **Time:** ~40 minutes · **Format:** four sections.

Answer in your notebook or on separate paper. Write in complete sentences
where asked. Solutions and scoring guidance are in the [answer
key](../answer-key.md), not in this file.

---

## Section A — Regions and settlement (D2.Geo.8.3-5)

**A1.** (4 points) Name the three colonial regions. For each region, name
two of its colonies.

**A2.** (4 points) Choose **one** region. Explain how its environment
shaped both how people settled (towns close together? plantations spread
out? port cities?) and how they earned a living. Use at least two
environmental facts.

**A3.** (2 points) Name one way colonists practiced ruling themselves
before independence, and write one sentence on why it matters for later
American history.

---

## Section B — Then and now; labor systems (D2.His.2, 14.3-5)

**B1.** (4 points) Describe the four kinds of colonial labor in one
sentence each: family labor, hired/skilled labor, indentured servitude,
enslaved labor.

**B2.** (3 points) State plainly the legal and human difference between
indentured servitude and enslavement. Then explain: why is the question
"who chose?" the most important question to ask about each kind of labor?

**B3.** (3 points) Give one cause and one effect for the growth of
enslaved labor in the Southern colonies. (Cause and effect must connect
logically.)

**B4.** (2 points) Compare one colonial child's day to your day: name two
differences and one similarity.

---

## Section C — Economic life (D2.Eco.3, 4, 7.3-5; D2.Geo.11.3-5)

**C1.** (3 points) A barrel of flour: name one natural resource, one piece
of human capital, and one piece of physical capital behind it.

**C2.** (3 points) Explain why the Middle colonies specialized in wheat
rather than trying to produce everything themselves. Use the words
**specialize** and **trade** correctly.

**C3.** (4 points) Trace a hogshead of Virginia tobacco from field to
London buyer in **four** steps. At one step, name who did the work and
their labor kind; in a final sentence, name who profited.

**C4.** (2 points) In one sentence each: how does money make exchange
easier than barter, and how did profits influence what sellers chose to
sell?

---

## Section D — Perspectives (D2.His.4, 5.3-5; D1.3.3-5)

Read these two short accounts of **one event** — market day in
Philadelphia, 1750. (Both are original teaching material written for this
assessment; they are not historical documents.)

> **Account 1 — a German shopkeeper:** "Market day is the best day of the
> week. Farmers bring wheat, butter, and eggs; I sell cloth and tools. My
> neighbors speak three languages, and we all go home with what we need."

> **Account 2 — an enslaved porter:** "On market day I carry crates from
> the wagons to the stalls, from sunup until the bell rings. The market
> is full of goods I will never buy."

**D1.** (4 points) For **each** account, answer two of the historian's
four questions: who is speaking (and their place in the story), and what
do they want (what is at stake for them)?

**D2.** (3 points) Explain why these two accounts of the same market day
differ. Connect each perspective to its historical context in one sentence
per account.

**D3.** (2 points) Name one voice that is missing from these two accounts
— someone else who was at the market — and write one sentence on what
they might have seen differently.

**D4.** (2 points) Write one supporting question about colonial market
days that is open to interpretation (not yes/no), and name one kind of
source that would help answer it.
