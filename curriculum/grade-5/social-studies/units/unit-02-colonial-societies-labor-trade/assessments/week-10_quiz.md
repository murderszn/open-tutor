# Week-10 Formative Quiz — Colonial Societies

**Unit:** [U02 — Colonial Societies: Labor, Trade, and Diverse Perspectives](../README.md)
**When:** Week 10 S3 · **Time:** ~20 minutes · **Format:** 10 items, mixed.

Write your answers in your notebook. This quiz checks what is secure
before the unit assessment — the adult reviews it with you the same day.
Solutions are in the [answer key](../answer-key.md), not in this file.

**1.** Name the three colonial regions and write two colonies in each.
(3 points — one per region; colonies must be correct.)

**2.** Match each region to its economy (write the letter):

- a. New England
- b. Middle colonies
- c. Southern colonies

- __ "Breadbasket" — wheat, flour, mills, diverse peoples, busy ports
- __ Plantations — tobacco, rice, indigo; tidal rivers
- __ Fishing, whaling, shipbuilding, timber; towns with meeting houses

**3.** Complete the chain: *environment → available resource → work.*
"Middle colonies → ______ → ______." (One resource, one kind of work.)

**4.** Name the three kinds of resources behind colonial goods, and give
one colonial example of each.

**5.** In two sentences, explain why specialization leads to trade.

**6.** State plainly the difference between indentured servitude and
enslavement. (Two sentences maximum.)

**7.** Give one cause and one effect for the growth of enslaved labor in
the Southern colonies.

**8.** A London merchant and an enslaved field worker describe the same
tobacco shipment. In one sentence, explain why their accounts differ.

**9.** Name the historian's four questions used in Lesson 04.

**10.** Look at the triangular-trade diagram from Lesson 05. Trace **one**
good around the triangle in three steps, then write one sentence naming
who benefited and one sentence naming who was harmed.

**Scoring guide (for the adult):** 24 points total (Q1: 3, Q2: 3, Q3: 2,
Q4: 3, Q5: 2, Q6: 2, Q7: 2, Q8: 1, Q9: 2, Q10: 4). 20+ secure → proceed
to the unit assessment. 14–19 → reteach the missed objective with the
lesson noted in the teacher guide, then reassess those items orally.
Below 14 → revisit Lessons 01–04 one at a time before the assessment.
