# Project — The Journey of a Good

**Unit:** [U02 — Colonial Societies: Labor, Trade, and Diverse Perspectives](README.md)
**Launched:** Week 9 S3 · **Completed:** Week 10 S3 · **Presented:** Week 10 S3/S4

## The task

Choose **one colonial good** — tobacco, wheat/flour, dried fish, or lumber
— and trace its journey from where it was produced to where it was sold.
Your project has three parts. Work may be written, drawn and labeled, or a
mix; the adult scribes dictated captions when the learner uses the drawn
option.

### Part 1 — The trade-chain diagram (the route)

Draw and label a chain with **at least 5 steps** from production to sale.
Example for tobacco: *Virginia field → drying barn → packed hogshead →
wagon and river to port → ship to London → sold to merchant.* For each
step, label: **where** it happens, **what** is done, and **which resources**
(natural, human capital, physical capital) are used.

### Part 2 — Who worked, who profited (the people)

For **at least 3 steps** of your chain, name who did the work and which
labor kind they belong to (family, hired/skilled, indentured, enslaved —
Lesson 03). Then write a short paragraph answering: **who profited from
this good's journey, and who was harmed or left out?** Name the gap
plainly, as Lesson 02's tobacco example does.

### Part 3 — Two voices (the perspectives)

Choose **one step** of your chain. Write **two short accounts** (3–5
sentences each, clearly labeled as your own writing) of that step from two
different people's perspectives — for example, a planter and an enslaved
worker at harvest, or a ship's captain and a dock worker at loading. Then
add the historian's four questions answered about your own accounts,
including **whose voice is still missing** (Lesson 04).

## Deliverables

1. Trade-chain diagram (≥5 labeled steps with resources).
2. "Who worked, who profited" — labor kinds for ≥3 steps + the
   profit/harm paragraph.
3. Two-voice page — two labeled accounts + four-questions paragraph.
4. A one-paragraph **supporting question** you still have about your good
   (open to interpretation — D1.3.3-5) and the **kinds of sources** that
   would help answer it (D1.5.3-5).

## Rubric — 20 points

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Solid | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Chain accuracy** (4) | ≥5 steps, correct order, each step labeled with place, action, and resources | ≥5 steps in order; most steps labeled | 3–4 steps or order errors | 1–2 steps or major errors |
| **Resources** (3) | All three resource kinds correctly identified across the chain | Two kinds correctly identified | One kind, or frequent mislabels | Resources missing |
| **Labor honesty** (4) | ≥3 steps name correct labor kinds; profit/harm paragraph names who profited and who was harmed plainly | ≥3 steps named; paragraph names profit but softens harm | 1–2 steps named; paragraph vague | Labor kinds missing or paragraph missing |
| **Perspectives** (4) | Two accounts clearly differ by speaker's place and stake; four questions answered including a missing voice | Two accounts differ; four questions answered | Accounts sound alike; questions partial | One account or questions missing |
| **Sources question** (2) | Supporting question is open to interpretation; source kinds fit the question | Question + source kinds present, one weaker | Question is yes/no or sources don't fit | Missing |
| **Communication** (3) | Labels, sentences, and diagram are clear; a reader can follow the whole journey | Mostly clear; small gaps | Hard to follow in places | Unclear |

**Scoring:** 18–20 exemplary · 14–17 proficient · 10–13 developing ·
below 10 — revisit with the adult (reteach the weakest criterion, revise,
resubmit).

## Milestones (adult checks)

- **Week 9 S3 (launch):** good chosen; first 3 chain steps drafted; labor
  kinds identified for 1 step.
- **Week 10 S3 (work session):** diagram complete; profit/harm paragraph
  drafted; two-voice page drafted.
- **Week 10 S3/S4:** final assembly; learner presents the chain aloud
  (2 minutes) and answers one adult question about who profited.

## What "done" looks like (exemplar summary)

A proficient tobacco project shows: field → barn → hogshead → river/port
→ ship → London sale (6 steps, resources labeled); field work = enslaved
labor, barrel-making = hired cooper, sailing = hired sailors; paragraph
names planter/shipowner/merchant profit and enslaved workers' unpaid labor
plainly; two voices (planter at harvest vs. enslaved worker at harvest)
differ in what each saw and wanted; four questions answered with a missing
voice (the ship's captain); a supporting question like "Why did tobacco
prices change what planters planted?" with source kinds (price lists,
planters' letters, shipping records — adult-selected).
