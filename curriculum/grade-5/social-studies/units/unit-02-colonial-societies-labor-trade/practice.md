# U02 Practice Banks

**Unit:** [U02 — Colonial Societies: Labor, Trade, and Diverse Perspectives](README.md)

These sets supply the S2 guided/independent practice and the S3
investigation tasks named in the lesson plans. The adult reviews written
work the same day. Solutions for every item are in the [answer
key](answer-key.md); nothing here ships with answers.

---

## Set A — Regions (Week 7; Lessons 01 + S3/S4)

**A1.** Sort the 13 colonies into the three regions (4 New England, 4
Middle, 5 Southern). Check against a map, then cover the map and sort
again from memory.

**A2.** Match each region to its description (write the letter):

- a. Thin rocky soil, short growing season, forests and harbors; fishing, shipbuilding, trade
- b. Warm climate, long growing season, tidal rivers; plantations growing tobacco, rice, indigo
- c. Fertile soil, moderate climate, broad rivers; wheat and flour, diverse peoples, busy ports

**A3.** For each region, complete the chain: *environment → available
resource → work people did.* (Example: New England → tall straight trees
→ shipbuilding.)

**A4.** True or false — fix each false statement in one sentence:

1. All thirteen colonies had the same economy.
2. The Middle colonies were called the "breadbasket colonies" because of their wheat.
3. Southern plantations grew mostly food for the planter's own family.
4. New England towns often made local decisions in town meetings.

**A5.** Order these events on a timeline and label each with its year:
Jamestown founded; Plymouth/Mayflower Compact; House of Burgesses;
Rhode Island founded; Pennsylvania founded; Georgia founded.
(Years: 1607, 1619, 1620, 1636, 1681, 1732.)

**A6.** A family wants to start a fishing business; another wants to grow
rice to sell. Which region fits each family? Give one environmental reason
per answer.

**A7.** Name one way colonists practiced ruling themselves *before*
independence, and write one sentence on why it matters for later units.

**A8. Then/now task (Week 7 S3).** Choose a colonial child in one region.
Write two short paragraphs: (1) their day, using at least three facts
from Lesson 01 (chores, food, travel, town or plantation life); (2) your
day. Then name two differences and one similarity. (D2.His.2.3-5)

**A9. Weekly check (Week 7 S4).** From memory: (1) name the three regions
and two colonies in each; (2) one sentence: why did New England specialize
in shipbuilding rather than cash crops? (3) one sentence: name a colonial
self-rule practice and why it matters later.

**A10.** Challenge: explain in a paragraph why historians group the
colonies into regions at all — what does the grouping help us see that a
list of thirteen separate colonies would hide?

## Set B — Economy (Week 8; Lessons 02 + 05, S3/S4)

**B1.** For each good — a ship's mast, a barrel of flour, a bundle of
indigo — name one natural resource, one piece of human capital, and one
piece of physical capital behind it.

**B2.** In two sentences, explain why the Middle colonies and New England
traded with each other instead of each producing everything. Use the word
**specialize**.

**B3.** A shopkeeper chooses between British cloth (costs more, sells for
more) and colonial cloth (costs less, sells for less). Show her profit
thinking in three steps: costs → price → where is the profit?

**B4.** Complete the tobacco production chain with the missing steps:
field → ______ → packed hogshead → ______ → ship to London → sold.
(Use: drying barn; wagon/river to port.)

**B5.** Benefit/cost table: a Pennsylvania family considers growing only
wheat and buying everything else. List two benefits and two costs of
specializing.

**B6.** In one sentence each: (1) how does money make exchange easier than
barter? (2) what is profit? (3) how did profits influence what planters
chose to grow?

**B7.** Cause and effect: Britain's Navigation Acts said most colonial
goods had to travel on British ships through British ports. Name one
probable effect on colonial merchants and one on British tax collectors.
(D2.His.14.3-5)

**B8. Plan-a-trade task (Week 8 S2/S3).** You are a Philadelphia merchant
in 1750 with a warehouse of flour and a small ship. Write a one-page
plan: (1) where you will sail and why; (2) what you carry there and bring
back; (3) one British trade rule affecting your plan; (4) where you expect
the most profit and what could go wrong.

**B9. Weekly check (Week 8 S4).** From memory: (1) the three resource
kinds + one colonial example of each; (2) one sentence: why does
specialization lead to trade? (3) one British trade rule and one way it
shaped colonial production; (4) trace rum OR sugar around the triangular
trade in 3 steps.

**B10.** Challenge: a New England merchant writes, "The Navigation Acts
protect our ships." A Virginia planter writes, "The Navigation Acts rob
us of better prices." Explain why both can be sincere — whose
perspective is each, and what is at stake for each? (Preview of Lesson 04.)

## Set C — Labor (Week 9; Lesson 03, S3/S4)

**C1.** Sort into the four labor kinds — family, hired/skilled, indentured,
enslaved: (a) a blacksmith's apprentice; (b) a woman spinning for her
family; (c) a man working off his ocean passage; (d) a girl born on a rice
plantation to an enslaved mother; (e) a sailor paid wages per voyage.

**C2.** In two sentences, state the plain legal and human difference
between indentured servitude and enslavement. (Use *temporary/contract*
and *lifelong/hereditary/forced*.)

**C3.** Complete the cause-and-effect chain for the growth of enslaved
labor: labor-hungry cash crops → ______ → laws making enslavement lifelong
and hereditary → ______.
(Use: too few willing workers; or: 1619 arrival shows forced labor
possible / West African rice expertise sought → plantation economy built
on enslaved labor. Accept either well-reasoned chain; see answer key.)

**C4.** In plain language (2–3 sentences), what was the Middle Passage?

**C5.** Name two forms of resistance by enslaved people. For each, write
one sentence on what it shows about the people who resisted.

**C6.** "Who chose?" table: for an indentured servant and an enslaved
person, answer: who chose it? for how long? could they leave? were they
paid?

**C7.** Why did South Carolina rice planters specifically seek enslaved
people from West Africa's Rice Coast? Answer with a cause → effect chain
using the term **human capital**.

**C8. Kept assignment with taught model (Week 9 S3).** Complete Part 3 of
`curriculum/grade-5/social-studies/assignments/thirteen-colonies-life.md` ("Daily Life in the 13 Colonies")
using Lesson 03's taught model: West African expertise rows (modeled),
then yourself — one form of resistance + what it shows; a then/now
comparison of an enslaved child's day vs. a free colonial child's day
(two differences, one similarity); one supporting question about colonial
labor open to interpretation.

**C9. Weekly check (Week 9 S4).** From memory: (1) the four labor kinds;
(2) the plain servitude-vs-enslavement difference in two sentences;
(3) one cause and one effect for the growth of enslaved labor; (4) one
form of resistance and what it shows.

## Set D — Perspectives (Week 10; Lesson 04, S3/S4)

**D1.** Re-read Lesson 04's two tobacco-shipment accounts. For each,
answer the four historian's questions aloud or in writing: who is
speaking? what did they see? what do they want? whose voice is missing?

**D2. Modeled notice (adult reads aloud):**

> "RUN AWAY from the subscriber, an indentured servant named Thomas
> Webb, about 20 years of age. Whoever returns him shall have forty
> shillings reward. — R. Carter, Williamsburg."
> *(Original teaching material written for this lesson, modeled on the
> form of colonial newspaper notices.)*

Name two perspectives on this notice — the master who placed it and the
servant who ran — and give one reason for each perspective.

**D3.** "Whose voice is missing?" List two people present at a colonial
market day whose accounts we rarely have, and for each, one sentence on
what they might have seen differently.

**D4.** Two views of a town meeting vote to build a new meeting house:
a farmer who wants it ("our town needs a gathering place") and a poor
widow who opposes the tax ("I cannot pay more"). Apply the four
historian's questions to each view in a short paragraph.

**D5.** Match each speaker to the likeliest perspective and reason:

- Speakers: (a) a British tax collector; (b) a colonial smuggler; (c) an
  enslaved dock worker.
- Event: a ship unloads French sugar at night, avoiding British taxes.
- For each speaker, write one sentence: how do they see this event, and
  why? (Preview of Unit 03's smuggling debates.)

**D6.** Explain in 2–3 sentences why the Powhatan view of Jamestown's
founding differed from the English settler view. Use the word **context**.

**D7. Two-voice account (Week 10 S2/S3).** Choose one colonial event
(tobacco shipment sailing, town meeting vote, Philadelphia market day,
founding of a plantation). Write two short accounts (4–6 sentences each)
from two different perspectives, clearly labeled as your own writing.
Add a final paragraph answering the four historian's questions about
your accounts, including whose voice is still missing.

**D8. Weekly check (Week 10 S4, before the assessment).** From memory:
(1) the historian's four questions; (2) one sentence: why did the
merchant and the enslaved worker describe the same shipment differently?
(3) one sentence: what does "whose voice is missing?" add?

**D9.** Challenge: "Being fair to the past means giving every perspective
equal weight." Agree or disagree in a paragraph, using one example from
this unit. (See teacher guide: fairness = hearing all, weighing with
evidence.)
