# Project — One Nation, One Environment

**Unit:** [U01 — North American Geography, Indigenous Histories, and Sources](README.md)
**Launched:** Week 5 S3 · **Due:** Week 6 S4 (with the unit assessment)

**Driving question:** How did one Indigenous nation's environment shape its
way of life — and how does that nation live today?

## Overview

You are a geographer-historian. Choose one Indigenous nation from your
adult's pre-approved list. Investigate how its environment shaped how its
people got food, built shelter, and traveled — and how people adapted to
and modified that environment. Then show what you found in three
deliverables, and connect the nation's past to its living present.

**Adult setup (before launch):** The adult pre-selects a list of 4–6
nations (at least one from four different culture areas) and, for each,
2–3 approved sources — Indigenous-authored, tribal, or museum sources
preferred (see the [Resource Pack](resource-pack.md)). The learner chooses
from this list only. Suggested list: Haudenosaunee (Eastern Woodlands),
Diné (Southwest), Lakota (Great Plains), Haida (Northwest Coast), Hopi
(Southwest), Cherokee (Southeast).

## Materials

- Project folder (paper or digital)
- Culture-area profile sheet (below — copy or print)
- Blank outline map of North America (for the environment map)
- Colored pencils, ruler
- Approved sources (adult-provided)
- Vocabulary list from the [unit README](README.md)

## Steps

1. **Choose** (Week 5 S3, with the adult): pick one nation from the
   approved list. Write why you chose it (2 sentences).
2. **Locate** (Week 5 S3): find your nation's homeland on the environment
   map. Mark it with a symbol and add the symbol to your map key.
3. **Gather** (Week 5 S3–S4): read or listen to your approved sources with
   the adult. Fill in the profile sheet: environment, food, shelter,
   travel, one adaptation, one modification, one present-day connection.
   For each fact, note which source it came from (source-kind + title).
4. **Map** (Week 5 S4 / Week 6 S3): draw your **environment map** — the
   nation's homeland with at least 3 labeled environmental features
   (mountains, rivers, desert, forest, coastline…) and a title, key,
   compass rose, and scale bar. Add one sentence on the map explaining how
   one feature shaped daily life.
5. **Claim** (Week 6 S3): write your **evidence-backed claim** — 4–5
   sentences answering the driving question for your nation, with at least
   3 pieces of evidence from at least 2 different sources. End with one
   sentence connecting the nation's past to its present.
6. **Check** (Week 6 S3): trade folders with the adult. Use the rubric
   below as a checklist. Fix anything missing before the due date.
7. **Share** (Week 6 S4): present your map and claim to your adult (2–3
   minutes, oral with the map as your visual). The adult scores with the
   rubric.

## Deliverables

### 1. Culture-area profile sheet

| Section | What to write |
|---|---|
| Nation & culture area | Nation name (use the nation's preferred name), culture area |
| Homeland & environment | Where the homeland is (region + landmarks); 3 environmental features |
| Food | How people got food — at least 2 ways, linked to the environment |
| Shelter | What homes were like and why they fit the environment |
| Travel | How people moved and what they used |
| Adapt | One way people adapted *how they lived* to the environment |
| Modify | One way people changed the *environment itself* |
| Present-day connection | How this nation lives today — government, language, art, or land (at least 2 facts) |
| My sources | Each source's kind, title/maker, and what it told you |

### 2. Environment map

Hand-drawn or digital map of North America showing the nation's homeland,
with: title, key/legend, compass rose, scale bar, homeland marked with a
keyed symbol, at least 3 labeled environmental features, and one
annotated sentence linking a feature to daily life.

### 3. Evidence-backed claim

4–5 sentences answering the driving question, with ≥3 evidence pieces from
≥2 sources, ending in a present-day connection sentence. Example structure
(yours must be about *your* nation): "The [environment] of the [homeland]
shaped how the [nation] lived. [Evidence 1]. [Evidence 2]. The people also
changed the land by [modification]. Today, the [nation] [present-day fact],
showing the nation continues."

## Rubric — 20 points

Score each criterion 0–4 using the descriptors. 18–20 = Exceeding, 14–17 =
Meeting, 10–13 = Approaching, below 10 = re-teach and revise with the adult.

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Solid | 2 — Partial | 1 — Beginning | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Environment accuracy** (4 pts) | Homeland correctly located; 3+ environmental features labeled correctly on the map and sheet | Homeland located; 2 features correct; minor label error | Homeland approximately located; 1 feature correct | Homeland misplaced or features missing | Not attempted |
| **Environment → way of life** (4 pts) | Food, shelter, AND travel each explicitly linked to an environmental feature with reasoning | Two of the three linked with reasoning; third stated without link | Links stated but reasoning missing or incorrect | Only lists facts, no links | Not attempted |
| **Adapt vs. modify** (4 pts) | One clear adaptation AND one clear modification, each correctly distinguished and explained | Both present; one slightly confused | Only one present, or both confused | Terms used without understanding | Not attempted |
| **Map construction** (4 pts) | Title, key, compass rose, scale bar all present and correct; homeland symbol keyed; annotation sentence links a feature to daily life | All 4 tools present; annotation present but vague | 3 tools present; annotation missing | 2 or fewer tools; no annotation | Not attempted |
| **Evidence & present-day connection** (4 pts) | Claim has 3+ evidence pieces from 2+ named sources; ends with a specific present-day connection; "some historians argue" framing used if a debated claim appears | 3 evidence pieces from 2 sources; present-day connection general | 2 evidence pieces or 1 source; present-day connection missing | Claim is opinion without evidence | Not attempted |

**Rubric exemplar (for the adult):** A "Strong" profile of the Haida might
read: environment — rainy forested islands and coastline of the Pacific
Northwest, salmon-rich seas; food — salmon, halibut, and sea mammals caught
from cedar dugout canoes; shelter — large cedar plank houses for extended
families; travel — ocean-going canoes along the coast; adapt — waterproof
cedar clothing and rain-shedding house design for the wet climate; modify —
managed clam gardens and cleared village sites ( shoreline shaping);
present-day — the Haida Nation governs Haida Gwaii today, carvers raise new
totem poles, and the Haida language is taught to children. Sources: NMAI
exhibit page + Ketchikan Totem Heritage Center page (named). The claim
cites the canoe, the plank house, and the salmon runs as evidence.

**Retake/revision:** Any criterion scored 0–1 may be revised once with
adult guidance; the adult re-scores only the revised criterion.
