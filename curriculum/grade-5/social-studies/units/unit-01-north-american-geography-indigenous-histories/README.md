# Unit 01 — North American Geography, Indigenous Histories, and Sources

**Grade 5 Social Studies · Weeks 3–6 of 36 · 16 sessions (~40 minutes each)**

**Status:** validated draft — delivered 2026-10-11; not yet educator-reviewed or merged.

This unit launches the grade-5 year with geography: how geographers read maps,
how North America's regions and environments differ, how environment shaped
the ways of life of Indigenous nations, and how we learn about the past from
sources. Indigenous nations are taught as living, sovereign communities with
homelands — not as a past-tense topic. Every culture-area study includes a
present-day connection.

**Essential question:** How did North America's land shape the lives of the
people who have lived here, and how do we know?

See the track [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) (U01, Weeks
3–6) and the [subject README](../../README.md).

## Lessons

1. [Lesson 01 — How Geographers Read Maps](lessons/lesson-01-map-tools.md) —
   title, key/legend, compass rose, scale bar, grid, symbols; making evidence
   claims from maps.
2. [Lesson 02 — North American Regions](lessons/lesson-02-regions.md) —
   landforms, climate, and water; why environmental characteristics vary;
   sorting states into regions with evidence (4-region model reconciled with
   `us_states.csv`).
3. [Lesson 03 — Culture Areas: Environment Shapes Ways of Life](lessons/lesson-03-culture-areas.md) —
   the 8 culture areas; how environment shaped food, shelter, and travel;
   Pacific Northwest coastal-environment image activity.
4. [Lesson 04 — Living Nations](lessons/lesson-04-living-nations.md) — nations
   as living sovereign communities with homelands; present-day connections;
   the Haudenosaunee evidence note; Diné naming.
5. [Lesson 05 — Working with Sources](lessons/lesson-05-sources.md) — source
   kinds (oral accounts, artifacts, maps, photographs); what each can and
   cannot tell us; inferring audience and purpose from within the source;
   fact vs. opinion.

## Prerequisites (grade-4 social studies end-of-year objectives)

- Geographic tools — construct and read maps with title, symbols, key,
  compass rose, and labels; name cardinal directions; use maps of different
  scales; describe U.S. regions and their environmental characteristics.
- Indigenous nations — explain how geography shaped nations' ways of life;
  take multiple perspectives on encounters.
- History with timelines and evidence — sequence events across centuries;
  compare past and present; identify kinds of historical sources; compare
  accounts of the same event.
- Evidence and communication — with adult support, gather information from
  multiple sources; distinguish fact from opinion; build arguments with
  claims, reasons, and sequenced explanations.

Unit 01 re-teaches map tools (scale, grid, region boundaries) rather than
assuming they are secure. The diagnostic weeks (Weeks 1–2) verify these
entry skills.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of this unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Read and construct maps** using title, key/legend, compass rose, scale
   bar, grid, and symbols, and state an evidence claim that cites a specific
   map feature. (C3 D2.Geo.1, 2, 3.3-5)
2. **Explain how landforms, climate, and water create North America's
   regions**, why environmental characteristics vary among them, and sort
   U.S. states into the unit's 4-region model, justifying each placement
   with evidence from `resources/us_states.csv` and a map. (C3 D2.Geo.2, 3,
   10.3-5)
3. **Explain how environment shaped ways of life** in the 8 culture areas
   (Arctic, Northwest Coast, Plateau, Great Plains, Eastern Woodlands,
   Southeast, Southwest, California) — food, shelter, travel — and explain
   how people adapted to and modified their environments. (C3 D2.Geo.4, 5, 6,
   8.3-5)
4. **Name Indigenous nations as living, sovereign communities with
   homelands**, explain how their cultures and environments changed over
   time, compare life in historical periods to life today, and explain why
   people in the same period held different perspectives. (C3 D2.His.2, 4,
   5.3-5)
5. **Identify source kinds** (oral accounts, artifacts, maps, photographs),
   explain what each can and cannot tell us, infer a source's intended
   audience and purpose from clues within the source, distinguish fact from
   opinion, and use evidence from the `us_states.csv` dataset to support a
   claim about population distribution. (C3 D2.His.9–11.3-5; D3.1–3.4;
   D2.Geo.1, 6.3-5)

## Vocabulary

| Term | Kid-friendly definition |
|---|---|
| **compass rose** | A map symbol showing direction — north, south, east, and west. |
| **scale bar** | A marked line on a map showing what a distance on the map equals in real miles or kilometers. |
| **legend / key** | The box on a map that explains what each symbol and color means. |
| **grid** | Numbered rows and lettered columns across a map that help you find places, like a game board. |
| **region** | An area grouped together because places in it share characteristics like landforms, climate, or culture. |
| **landform** | A natural shape of Earth's surface — mountains, plains, valleys, deserts, coasts. |
| **climate** | The usual weather pattern of a place over many years (not today's weather). |
| **culture area** | A region where Indigenous nations shared similar ways of life because they lived in similar environments. |
| **nation** | A group of people with a shared identity, history, and (for tribal nations) the right to govern themselves. |
| **sovereign** | Having the right to make your own laws and govern your own community. |
| **homeland** | The land where a nation has lived for generations — its original home territory. |
| **adapt** | To change how you live so you can do well in your environment. |
| **modify** | To change the environment itself — for example, by building irrigation or farming. |
| **oral account** | History or knowledge passed down by spoken storytelling, often by elders. |
| **artifact** | An object people made or used in the past that we can study today. |
| **audience** | The people a source was made for — who was supposed to see, hear, or read it. |
| **purpose** | The reason a source was made — to teach, to convince, to record, to remember, to sell. |

## 16-session pacing

Four weeks × four ~40-minute sessions. **S1** concept launch (explicit
explanation + modeled examples), **S2** skills practice (guided then
independent — 10–14 written tasks, reviewed by the adult the same day),
**S3** investigation/application, **S4** review + check.

| Session | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| **Week 3 — Map tools** (Lesson 01) | | |
| S1 | Launch: how geographers read maps — title, key, compass rose, scale bar, grid, symbols; 2 worked map-evidence claims. Readiness check. | Learner names all 6 map tools and reads one correctly on a North America map. |
| S2 | Practice bank: map-reading set (guided 4, independent 10–14); adult reviews same day. | ≥80% of independent tasks correct with a cited map feature per claim. |
| S3 | Investigation: find two cities on a grid map using the scale bar; justify with map evidence (oral or drawn allowed). | Learner locates both cities and states the distance using the scale bar. |
| S4 | Review + week check: map-tool recap, misconceptions, short check. | Learner makes one evidence claim citing a map feature. |
| **Week 4 — Regions** (Lesson 02) | | |
| S1 | Launch: North American regions — landforms, climate, water; why environments vary; the 4-region model (reconciled with `us_states.csv`). | Learner explains why two regions differ environmentally, using a landform + climate reason. |
| S2 | Practice: sort states into regions from the CSV `subregion` column; justify with map evidence (guided 4, independent 10–14). | All sorted states placed correctly with one evidence reason each. |
| S3 | Region profile task: environment, landmarks, one fact per region; the kept `fifty-states-and-capitals-challenge.md` begins. | Profile sheet complete for all 4 regions; challenge log started. |
| S4 | Review + check: region-model reconciliation (4 vs. 5 models); formative quiz administered. | Learner explains which region model the unit uses and why. |
| **Week 5 — Culture areas + living nations** (Lessons 03–04; project launch) | | |
| S1 | Launch: the 8 culture areas with environment maps; worked examples of environment → way of life. | Learner matches 2 culture areas to environments and explains the link. |
| S2 | Practice: match environmental features to ways of life; AI-illustration observation activity (Lesson 03). | Learner lists 3 features from the image and explains a use for each. |
| S3 | Living nations (Lesson 04): nations as sovereign communities with homelands; Haudenosaunee evidence note; Diné naming. Project launched — adult pre-selects nation list. | Learner states one present-day connection for a nation and begins the profile sheet. |
| S4 | Review + check: culture areas and living nations; project work time. | Learner explains one environment→way-of-life link and names one living nation. |
| **Week 6 — Sources + data** (Lesson 05; formative quiz; assessment) | | |
| S1 | Launch: source kinds — oral accounts, artifacts, maps, photographs; what each can and cannot tell us; audience/purpose clues; fact vs. opinion. | Learner names all 4 source kinds and one limit of each. |

## Sensitive-content and framing notes

- Indigenous nations are named as living, sovereign communities with
  homelands. The adult selects sources with Indigenous authorship or
  museum/tribal sources where available; every culture-area study includes a
  present-day connection.
- Displacement and conquest are named honestly and plainly at an
  age-appropriate level — never glossed, never graphic. This unit's honest
  naming focuses on forced removal from homelands and the breaking of
  treaties; battle detail is not assigned.
- The Haudenosaunee-influence claim is phrased as **"some historians
  argue"**, with the evidence presented (see Lesson 04): it is a debated
  claim, and learners see both the evidence for it and the disagreement.
- Language: use **nations/peoples** (not "tribes" as a generic); use
  **Diné** as the preferred name for the people commonly called Navajo
  (first use: "Diné (Navajo)").

## Region model (reconciliation)

This unit uses the **4-region Census-style model** from
`resources/us_states.csv`'s `subregion` column — `United States Northeast`,
`United States Midwest`, `United States South`, `United States West` — as
its single official model. The legacy
`fifty-states-and-capitals-challenge.md` assignment uses a 5-region model
(Northeast / Southeast / Midwest / Southwest / West & Pacific). When that
assignment is used (Week 4 S3), the teacher guide maps the models: Northeast
→ Northeast; Midwest → Midwest; Southeast → South; Southwest → South (Texas,
Oklahoma) or West (Arizona, New Mexico); West & Pacific → West. The future
revision of the fifty-states assignment will adopt the 4-region model.

Dataset tasks name the exact CSV columns used: `name_common`, `usps`,
`capital`, `subregion`, `population_approx`, `area_sq_mi`,
`population_density_per_sq_mi`. Populations and areas are rounded
approximations (see the CSV header note in the teacher guide).
