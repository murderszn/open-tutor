# U01 Unit Assessment

**Unit:** [U01 — North American Geography, Indigenous Histories, and Sources](../README.md)
**Administered:** Week 6 S4 · **Time:** ~40 minutes (adult may extend)
**No answer key is included in this file** — solutions are in the [answer
key](../answer-key.md).

**Materials:** blank outline map of North America; `resources/us_states.csv`
(Texas and Rhode Island rows); the two sources in Section C (printed below);
calculator allowed for Section D division.

## Section A — Map reading and construction (D2.Geo.1, 2, 3.3-5)

**Task:** On the blank outline map, construct a map of North America that a
geographer could actually use. Your map must include: a title, a key/legend
with at least 4 symbols, a compass rose, a scale bar, and a grid. Then mark
and label: (1) the Mississippi River, (2) the Rocky Mountains, (3) two state
capitals of your choice (mark capitals with the key's capital symbol), and
(4) the approximate homeland area of one Indigenous nation you studied
(shade it and add it to your key as "homeland area — approximate").

Then answer:

A1. Write the title of your map. What question does your map answer?
A2. Using your scale bar, estimate the distance between your two capitals.
    Show your work.
A3. Write one evidence claim about a place on your map, citing one specific
    map feature you drew.

*(12 points: 6 for map tools present and correct, 2 for the four marked
features, 4 for A1–A3 with evidence.)*

## Section B — Short responses: environment shapes life (D2.Geo.4, 8.3-5; D2.His.2.3-5)

Answer each in 2–4 sentences. Cite lesson facts as evidence.

B1. Choose one culture area. Explain how its environment shaped how people
    got food AND how they built shelter. (Name the area and the nation or
    peoples you mean.)
B2. Give one example of people **adapting to** their environment and one
    example of people **modifying** their environment, from two different
    culture areas. Explain the difference between adapting and modifying.
B3. Compare life for a child in one Indigenous nation 200 years ago with
    life for a child in that nation today: name one big change and one
    thing that continued. (Use "nations/peoples" language; name the nation.)
B4. "Some historians argue that the Haudenosaunee Confederacy influenced
    early American democratic thinkers." What does the word "argue" signal
    in that sentence? Name one piece of evidence used by historians who
    make this claim.

*(16 points: 4 per item — 2 for content accuracy, 2 for evidence/reasoning.)*

## Section C — Source comparison: audience and purpose (D2.His.9, 10, 11.3-5)

Read both sources. **Both passages below are original, composed for this
lesson** — they are modeled examples, not historical texts and not
quotations from any real person.

**Source 1 — Oral-account excerpt (modeled example, original):**

> *"My grandmother taught me what her grandmother taught her: you plant the
> corn first, and when it stands as tall as your hand, you plant the beans
> at its feet so they can climb. The squash goes last, its big leaves
> shading the ground so the earth stays cool and damp. We plant them
> together because they are sisters — they take care of each other, the way
> our families do." — modeled telling in the voice of an Eastern Woodlands
> elder, composed for this lesson*

**Source 2 — Artifact description (modeled example, original):**

> *A clay cooking pot, about as tall as a school ruler, found at a village
> site in the desert Southwest. The outside is blackened on one side, as if
> it sat in cooking fires for many years. Three small cracks were mended
> long ago with a sticky plant sap. Nothing is written on it; the pot
> itself is the whole message.*

Answer:

C1. For each source, name its **kind** (oral account / artifact / map /
    photograph).
C2. For Source 1: who is the intended **audience**, and what is the
    **purpose**? Point to two clues *inside* the passage.
C3. For Source 2: list two things the pot **can** tell us and two things it
    **cannot** tell us. (Stick to what the object itself proves.)
C4. A student says, "Source 1 proves exactly how farming worked 800 years
    ago." Do you agree? Explain using what you know about the strengths
    and limits of oral accounts — and name one other source kind that
    could check (confirm or challenge) the account.

*(16 points: C1: 2; C2: 5 — audience 1, purpose 1, two clues 2, reasoning 1;
C3: 4; C4: 5 — position 1, strengths/limits 2, other source kind 2.)*

*Write your answers on a separate sheet — do not write in this file.*

## Section D — Dataset task: comparing two states (D2.Geo.1, 6.3-5; D3.4.3-5)

Use `resources/us_states.csv`. The columns you need: `name_common`,
`capital`, `subregion`, `population_approx`, `area_sq_mi`,
`population_density_per_sq_mi`. Remember: populations and areas are rounded
approximations — write "about" or "approximately" in your claim.

**Texas row:** population_approx 29,145,000 · area_sq_mi 268,596 ·
population_density_per_sq_mi 108.5 · capital Austin · subregion United
States South.
**Rhode Island row:** population_approx 1,097,000 · area_sq_mi 1,545 ·
population_density_per_sq_mi 710.0 · capital Providence · subregion United
States Northeast.

D1. Copy the two rows' figures into a small table (state, capital,
    subregion, population, area, density).
D2. **Check the math:** compute each state's density yourself with
    `population_approx ÷ area_sq_mi`, rounded to one decimal. Show the
    division. Does your result match the CSV's figure?
D3. About how many Rhode Islands (by area) would fit inside Texas? Show
    the division and round to a whole number.
D4. **Write an evidence-backed claim** (3–4 sentences): which state has
    more people per square mile, and what does that suggest about how
    people are settled in each state? Use your computed numbers as
    evidence, and connect the pattern to each state's environment or
    cities.

*(16 points: D1: 3; D2: 5 — two divisions with work 4, match verdict 1;
D3: 3; D4: 5 — claim 2, numbers as evidence 2, environment/settlement
link 1.)*
