# U01 Teacher Guide

**Unit:** [U01 — North American Geography, Indigenous Histories, and Sources](README.md)
**For the guiding adult.** Learner-facing materials never include answer
keys — all solutions live in the [answer key](answer-key.md).

## How to use this guide

Each week below gives session-by-session notes: what to emphasize, what to
watch for, and what to do when the learner struggles. The unit's session
model: **S1** concept launch, **S2** skills practice (10–14 written tasks,
reviewed the same day), **S3** investigation/application, **S4** review +
check. Sessions are ~40 minutes. Pacing is a plan, not a law — if S2
reveals a gap, spend S3 re-teaching rather than pushing forward.

## Week 3 — Map tools (Lesson 01)

**S1 — Launch.** Read the lesson's big idea and both worked examples aloud
with the printed North America map in front of you. The single habit to
build: *every claim points at a map feature.* When the learner says
"Mexico is south of the U.S.," ask "which tool proves it?" until the
answer is automatic. Do the readiness check first; if cardinal directions
are shaky, spend 10 minutes on compass-rose drills before the worked
examples.

**S2 — Practice.** [Practice bank 1](practice.md): guided 4 together, then
the 12 independent tasks. Review the same day — circle any claim missing
its cited tool and hand it back for one fix. Common stuck points: scale-bar
multiplication (let them use a calculator for the arithmetic; the skill is
setting up segments × miles) and HOMES recall (it's fine to keep the
acronym visible all week).

**S3 — Investigation.** The applied task (two capitals, grid + scale bar).
Accept oral or drawn responses — the assessed skill is map reading, not
writing. If the learner finishes early, start the extension (satellite vs.
political map comparison).

**S4 — Review + check.** Quick oral quiz: six tools in 30 seconds, one
evidence claim. Record which tools are still wobbly — Lesson 02's region
work leans on the compass rose, key, and scale bar.

**Reteach triggers:** If the learner can't name all six tools by S4, begin
Week 4 S1 with a 10-minute map-tool recap before the regions launch.

## Week 4 — Regions (Lesson 02)

**S1 — Launch.** Teach the three forces (landforms, climate, water) with
the relief map, then the 4-region model. The worked examples matter more
than the table: walk through the Southwest-vs.-Northwest reasoning aloud,
then have the learner try a new pair (Northeast vs. Southeast) with the
same three-force structure. Emphasize: regions are *groupings geographers
choose* — that's why two models can both be reasonable.

**S2 — Practice.** [Practice bank 2](practice.md). Keep the CSV open to the
`subregion` column; insist on "CSV says ___; the map shows ___" for every
sort. Watch for movie-based sorting ("cowboys = Southwest") — redirect to
the column every time.

**S3 — Region profile + fifty-states challenge.** The profile sheet is the
core deliverable; the kept `fifty-states-and-capitals-challenge.md` begins
here as the ongoing capitals thread (it completes in U08). **Deliver the
region-model reconciliation explicitly:** show the assignment's 5-region
list next to our 4-region table and map the split (Texas/Oklahoma →
South; Arizona/New Mexico → West). Write the mapping on the challenge log
so the learner doesn't "unlearn" it later. Flag for the future: the
fifty-states assignment is queued for revision to the 4-region model.

**S4 — Review + check.** Region-model recap; the learner should be able to
say which model the unit uses and why (because the dataset uses it).
Administer nothing new — the formative quiz waits until Week 6 S3, after
all lessons are taught.

**Reteach triggers:** If sorting is still guesswork at S4, the issue is
usually the three forces, not the states — re-teach with one region at a
time (cover the others) until the learner can explain *why* the Midwest is
flat and fertile.

## Week 5 — Culture areas + living nations (Lessons 03–04); project launch

**S1 — Culture areas launch (Lesson 03).** Eight areas is a lot — teach
them as four pairs of contrasts (Arctic/Southwest = opposite harshness;
Northwest Coast/California = ocean-rich; Plains/Eastern Woodlands =
grassland/forest; Plateau/Southeast = river/warmth). Both worked examples
aloud. Keep the environment map visible all week.

**S2 — Practice + image activity.** [Practice bank 3](practice.md) guided
set, then the AI-illustration observation activity in Lesson 03. Read the
caption's "AI illustration" label aloud and discuss what it is and isn't:
an imagining of an environment, not a photograph, not evidence about
people. The text-only alternative carries identical content — use it
whenever the image isn't available. Then the independent 12 tasks.

**S3 — Living nations (Lesson 04) + project launch.** This session's tone
matters: present tense, living communities. Pre-select the nation list and
approved sources *before* the session (Indigenous-authored, tribal, or
museum preferred). Teach the Haudenosaunee evidence note exactly as
written — the "some historians argue" sentence is the skill, not just the
facts. Launch the [project](project.md): learner chooses a nation, locates
the homeland, begins the profile sheet.

**S4 — Review + project work.** Culture-area and living-nations check
(exit checks from both lessons). Project work time with the adult nearby
for source questions.

**Reteach triggers:** If the learner slips into past tense for living
nations, correct in the moment every time for a week — the habit is the
learning. If "adapt vs. modify" is confused, re-teach with the
Arctic/Southwest worked example (Lesson 03).

## Week 6 — Sources + data (Lesson 05); quiz; assessment

**S1 — Source kinds launch.** The four kinds with can/can't. Both worked
examples aloud — the museum-label one is the lesson's core skill (clues
from *inside* the source). Emphasize: the adult pre-selects all sources;
the learner never browses alone for nation content.

**S2 — Practice.** [Practice bank 4](practice.md). The fact/opinion work is
usually quick; spend the time on audience/purpose inference — learners
default to "its purpose is to teach history because it's about history."
Insist on pointing to words.

**S3 — Dataset task + formative quiz + project catch-up.** Do the dataset
task first (Lesson 05's Texas/Rhode Island comparison) — calculator
allowed; the assessed skill is setting up the comparison and writing the
claim. Then the [formative quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md) (20 points).
Use remaining time for project revisions against the rubric checklist.

**S4 — Unit review + assessment + project due.** 10-minute spiral review
(one map tool, one culture area, one source kind), then the [unit
assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md) (~40 min; extend if needed),
then the 2–3 minute project share. Score with the answer key and the
project rubric the same day if possible.

## Region-model reconciliation note (for the adult)

This unit's official model is the CSV's 4 Census-style regions. The legacy
fifty-states assignment's 5-region model is **not wrong** — it is a
different, widely used grouping. The mapping:

| 5-region model | → Our 4-region model |
|---|---|
| Northeast | United States Northeast |
| Southeast | United States South |
| Midwest | United States Midwest |
| Southwest (Texas, Oklahoma) | United States South |
| Southwest (Arizona, New Mexico) | United States West |
| West & Pacific | United States West |

When the learner meets the 5-region model in videos or the assignment,
treat it as a translation exercise, not a contradiction. The fifty-states
assignment is flagged for future revision to the 4-region model.

## Sensitive-content guidance

- **Framing (all weeks):** Indigenous nations are living, sovereign
  communities with homelands. Use present tense for living nations; use
  "nations/peoples"; use **Diné** (first use: "Diné (Navajo)").
- **Difficult history:** forced removal from homelands, broken treaties,
  and boarding schools are named honestly and plainly at an
  age-appropriate level — never glossed, never graphic. This unit keeps
  the focus on geography, living nations, and sources; displacement detail
  belongs to U05, where it is taught with adult-selected sources.
- **The debated claim:** the Haudenosaunee-influence note is taught exactly
  as "some historians argue," with evidence on both sides. Do not let it
  harden into fact in retellings.
- **Sources:** the adult pre-selects every nation-specific source.
  Indigenous-authored, tribal, or museum sources preferred. The learner
  does not browse the open web for nation content.
- If the learner asks for more detail than is age-appropriate, acknowledge
  honestly ("Yes, that happened, and it was wrong") and return to the
  lesson.

## Accessibility supports (built into every lesson)

- Maps/diagrams pair color with symbols and labels; the generated image
  ships with alt text, a caption, and a text-only alternative.
- Oral, pointing, drawing, and manipulative options for map tasks; the
  adult scribes written explanations when writing is not the assessed
  skill.
- Original passages are grade-5 level; vocabulary is pre-taught in each
  lesson.
- Videos require captions/transcripts; the adult previews for ads, age
  suitability, and accuracy.
- Calculator allowed for dataset division; the assessed skill is the
  comparison setup and the evidence claim.

## Readiness and retake criteria

- **Proceed to U02 when:** the unit assessment scores ≥45/60 (Meeting) and
  the project scores ≥14/20, with no criterion at 0.
- **Revise-and-retry:** assessment 36–44 → the learner revises the two
  weakest sections with the answer key's reasoning as the model (not
  copying), then re-takes those sections once.
- **Re-teach:** assessment below 36, or project below 10 → re-teach the
  flagged lessons' worked examples before U02 begins; U02's colonial
  regions build directly on U01's region and source skills.
- **Quiz (Week 6 S3):** 16+/20 = on track; 12–15 = review the missed
  lesson before S4; below 12 = re-teach before the assessment.
