# Practice Banks — Unit 05

**How to use:** Each set supports one lesson's S2/S3 sessions and weekly
checks. The adult reviews completed work the same day. Solutions are in the
[answer key](answer-key.md) — never beside a student task.

## Set A — Maps and new states (Lesson 01)

1. How many states were there in 1800, and how many in 1850? How many new
   states joined in between?
2. Name the year, price, and size of the Louisiana Purchase.
3. Order these: California becomes a state (1850) · Louisiana Purchase
   (1803) · Ohio becomes a state (1803) · Missouri becomes a state (1821).
4. Which came first: the Louisiana Purchase or the Lewis and Clark
   expedition? By how many years did the first begin before the second?
5. Name two states admitted in the 1840s and one state admitted in the
   1810s.
6. True or false, with a correction if false: "The Louisiana Purchase
   bought empty land that no one lived on."
7. Why did settlers follow rivers? Give two reasons.
8. **Explain your reasoning:** A learner says, "The map doubled in 1803,
   so the country doubled in size the same day." What is right and what is
   wrong about that sentence? (Two sentences.)

## Set B — Treaties and removal (Lesson 02)

1. What is a treaty, in your own words?
2. Name the year of the Indian Removal Act and what it allowed.
3. What did the Supreme Court decide in Worcester v. Georgia (1832)?
4. Who signed the Treaty of New Echota, and why was it not a fair agreement
   for the Cherokee Nation?
5. How many Cherokee people were forced west in 1838–1839, and about how
   many died?
6. Name two nations besides the Cherokee that were forced west in the
   removal era.
7. True or false, with a correction if false: "The Supreme Court's 1832
   ruling stopped the Cherokee removal."
8. **Explain your reasoning:** A learner says, "The Cherokee lost their
   land because they did not fight back." Correct this in two sentences,
   naming two ways they resisted.

## Set C — Two perspectives (Lesson 03)

Use Accounts A and B from Lesson 03.

1. List two facts both accounts agree on.
2. Quote one opinion from Account A and one from Account B.
3. Who is Account A's audience? Give the clue inside the text.
4. What is Account B's purpose? Give one clue.
5. For the question "Did the Cherokee Nation agree to removal?", which
   account is more useful? Why?
6. For the question "How did Georgia supporters justify removal?", which
   account is more useful? Why?
7. True or false, with a correction if false: "Accounts A and B are real
   documents written in 1838."
8. **Explain your reasoning:** Why is a persuasive source not useless?
   Give one example from this lesson.

## Set D — Trade and changing places (Lesson 04)

1. Name the year the Erie Canal opened and the two cities it connected.
2. How long was the Erie Canal, in miles?
3. Before the canal, the Buffalo-to-New York trip took about three weeks.
   By canal boat it took about eight days. About how many days did the
   canal save?
4. What is a steamboat, and why did it change river trade? (Hint: think
   about going upstream.)
5. Name one group the Erie Canal helped and one group it harmed, each with
   a reason.
6. What is interdependence? Use the cotton shirt example in your answer.
7. True or false, with a correction if false: "The river-port illustration
   proves what a real 1840s port looked like."
8. **Explain your reasoning:** A learner says, "Trade made everyone richer
   in the 1840s." Rewrite this as a fair sentence that names who gained
   and who was harmed.

## Set E — Causes, effects, and claims (Lesson 05)

1. Build a three-box chain: cotton gin (1793) → ______ → cotton farming
   spreads west. Fill the middle box and defend it with one reason.
2. Write a probable-cause claim for the growth of New York City after 1825,
   using the pattern: "Because ______, New York City ______."
3. What is the difference between a fact and a claim? Give one example of
   each from this unit.
4. A claim needs at least two pieces of evidence from ______ sources. Why
   does that rule make a claim stronger?
5. Match each cause to its probable effect: (a) Erie Canal opens → (b)
   Louisiana Purchase → (c) cotton gin → to: (i) cotton farming spreads
   west, (ii) the United States doubles in size on paper, (iii) shipping
   between Buffalo and New York gets faster and cheaper.
6. True or false, with a correction if false: "The cotton gin caused the
   Trail of Tears all by itself."
7. Draft a claim about your mini-exhibit place in one sentence, then name
   the two pieces of evidence you will use.
8. **Explain your reasoning:** A learner says, "My exhibit is finished
   because I proved my claim." What is missing from a good exhibit besides
   proof? (Hint: what a historian admits they do not know.)
