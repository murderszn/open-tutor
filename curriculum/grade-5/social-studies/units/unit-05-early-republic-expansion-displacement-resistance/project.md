# Project — One Place, Two Stories: A Mini-Exhibit

**Launched:** Week 22, S3. **Completed:** Week 23, S3. **Points:** 20.

## The task

Choose **one place** from the list below and build a small exhibit that
teaches a visitor how expansion changed that place — for at least two
different groups of people.

- A Cherokee town in Georgia (for example, New Echota) in the 1830s
- A new Ohio farm town in the 1810s
- A canal town in New York (for example, Rochester) in the 1820s–1830s
- A Mississippi cotton port (for example, Natchez) in the 1830s–1840s

Your exhibit has four parts:

1. **A claim (one sentence).** An arguable sentence about how expansion
   changed your place — for example, "The canal made Rochester grow, but
   the growth came at the cost of Haudenosaunee land."
2. **Two pieces of evidence (with reasons).** Each piece must come from a
   different source — a map, a lesson fact, a Resource Pack reference, the
   river-port illustration, or a practice-bank item. Under each piece,
   write one sentence explaining how it supports your claim.
3. **A panel.** A drawn or written display: a simple map or picture of the
   place, your claim at the top, and your evidence underneath. Label
   everything with words so the panel can be read without the picture.
   (Oral option: present the exhibit aloud in about two minutes, with the
   panel as a prop.)
4. **A question you still have (one sentence).** Something your exhibit
   does not answer — for example, "What did the Cherokee families who hid
   in the mountains live on that first winter?" Historians name what they
   do not know.

**Rules:** Do not invent speeches, dialogue, or numbers. Do not present
the AI river-port illustration as a photograph or as evidence from the
1840s — if you use it, label it "AI illustration." Keep the exhibit about
real people and real places; do not re-enact suffering or draw scenes of
violence. The guiding adult reviews the exhibit before it is shown to
anyone else.

## Materials

Paper or card, pencils/pens, the timeline diagram
(`assets/early-republic-timeline.svg`), the river-port illustration
(`assets/river-port-1840s.png`), the practice banks, and the Resource Pack.
Adult-selected sources only.

## Steps

1. **Week 22, S3 — Choose and gather.** Pick your place. Write a first
   draft of your claim. Find two pieces of evidence from different sources
   and write a reason for each. Sketch the panel.
2. **Week 23, S1–S2 — Build and check.** Finish the panel. Read your claim
   and evidence aloud to the adult: can a visitor follow your argument?
   Add the question you still have. Check every fact against the answer
   key or a Resource Pack reference.
3. **Week 23, S3 — Present.** Present the exhibit in about two minutes:
   claim first, evidence second, question last. The adult scores it with
   the rubric below.

## Rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Good | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claim (4) | Arguable, clear, and about how expansion changed the place | Arguable and clear, but vague about change | A fact, not a claim, or unclear | No claim |
| Evidence 1 (4) | Accurate fact from a named source, with a reason linking it to the claim | Accurate fact with a reason; source named loosely | Fact present but inaccurate, unsourced, or unlinked to the claim | Missing |
| Evidence 2 (4) | Accurate fact from a *different* source than Evidence 1, with a reason linking it to the claim | Accurate fact from a different source with a reason; source named loosely | Same source as Evidence 1, or unlinked to the claim | Missing |
| Two stories (4) | Names at least two groups with different experiences, fairly, each with a reason | Names two groups fairly, but one reason is thin | Names only one group's experience | One-sided or missing |
| Panel and question (4) | Panel is labeled and readable without the picture; the open question is real and specific | Panel readable; question present but vague | Panel hard to read or unlabeled; no question | No panel or no question |

**Scoring note:** A claim that is fair to two groups and supported by two
different sources scores higher than a longer exhibit with one source.
Honest uncertainty in the final question earns full marks for that row.

## Exemplar (for the adult — not a learner model to copy)

*Place:* Rochester, New York. *Claim:* "The Erie Canal made Rochester
grow from a small settlement into a busy town, and that growth pushed
Haudenosaunee people off more of their land." *Evidence 1:* Rochester sits
on the canal route between Buffalo and Albany (map — the timeline diagram
shows the canal, 1817–1825). *Reason:* a town on a cheap, fast route
attracts traders and settlers. *Evidence 2:* canal boats cut the
Buffalo–New York trip from about three weeks to about eight days (Lesson
04). *Reason:* faster, cheaper shipping is why traders chose the canal
route. *Question:* "How much Haudenosaunee land around Rochester was lost
in the 1820s, and where did those families go?" The adult may read this
exemplar aloud after the learner has drafted their own exhibit — not
before.
