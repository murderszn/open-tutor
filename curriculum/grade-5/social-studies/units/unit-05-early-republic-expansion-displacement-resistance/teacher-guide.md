## Misconceptions and reteaching

- **"The Louisiana Purchase bought empty land."** Reteach: it bought
  France's claim to land where many Indigenous nations already lived. Use
  the timeline SVG and the Lesson 01 worked examples.
- **"Lewis and Clark discovered the West."** Reteach: they mapped routes
  through lands Indigenous peoples already knew; "discovery" describes
  what eastern Americans learned.
- **"The Cherokee agreed to move."** Reteach with the Treaty of New
  Echota: about 500 people signed without the nation's approval, against
  the chief and council's written protest.
- **"The Supreme Court protected the Cherokee."** Reteach: the Court ruled
  for them in 1832, but the ruling was not enforced — a decision only
  works if it is carried out.
- **"The Trail of Tears was a migration."** Reteach: it was a forced
  removal by soldiers; "migration" hides the force.
- **"Canals and steamboats helped everyone."** Reteach with Lesson 04's
  two-sided test: name one group helped and one harmed, each with a
  reason.
- **"The picture proves what a port looked like."** Reteach: the
  river-port illustration is a modern AI reconstruction; it teaches how a
  port worked, not a real place.
- **"One cause explains everything."** Reteach: big changes have several
  causes; always build at least two chains.

## Sensitive-content guidance

- Forced removal: keep descriptions at the level "soldiers forced families
  from their homes; about 4,000 died from hunger, exposure, and disease."
  No graphic detail, no re-enactment of suffering, no role-play as
  soldiers or as removed families.
- Enslavement: name it plainly ("people forced to work without freedom or
  pay"), focus on systems and resistance, and never present enslaved
  people's choices as a puzzle for the learner to solve for fun.
- Indigenous peoples: nations are living communities with homelands. Use
  present-tense language where it fits (the Cherokee Nation exists today;
  the Eastern Band lives in North Carolina today). The Trail of Tears is
  taught as the trail where they cried, not as a journey.
- Settler perspectives: teach them fairly, with reasons. Fairness to a
  side is not agreement with it, and the adult should say that
  distinction aloud.
- Numbers: always present the death toll as an estimate ("about 4,000 is
  the most-cited estimate; scholars' counts run higher and lower") and
  the removal total as "more than 16,000 Cherokee" and "about 60,000
  people from the five southeastern nations."

## Readiness and next steps

- **Ready for Unit 06** if the learner can: place ten new states in order,
  explain the removal chain (Act → court → disputed treaty → forced
  march), compare two accounts with audience/purpose, explain who gained
  and who was harmed when trade expanded, and write a claim with two
  pieces of evidence from different sources.
- **Reteach first** if the learner cannot yet sequence the 1803–1839
  timeline or confuses a fact with a claim: repeat Weeks 20 and 23 S1–S2
  with the practice banks before starting Unit 06's reform work.
- **Extension ready** if the assessment claim anticipates a critic: move
  to Unit 06's "whose strategy was this, and who did it aim to persuade?"
  question, which rewards exactly that skill.
