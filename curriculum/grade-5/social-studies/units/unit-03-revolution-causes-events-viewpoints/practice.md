# Practice Banks — Unit 03

**How to use:** Each set supports one lesson's S2/S3 sessions and weekly
checks. The adult reviews completed work the same day. Solutions are in the
[answer key](answer-key.md) — never beside a student task.

## Set A — Timeline and cause/effect (Lesson 01)

1. Order these: (a) First Continental Congress, (b) Stamp Act, (c) Boston
   Tea Party, (d) Proclamation of 1763, (e) French and Indian War ends.
2. Which two events in Question 1 happened in the same decade, and which
   happened latest?
3. Write a probable-cause claim for the Townshend Acts (1767) using the
   pattern: "Because ______, Parliament ______."
4. A chain reads: war debt → Stamp Act → colonial protest → repeal.
   Which link is a *response* rather than a cause? Explain.
5. Give one reason the Proclamation of 1763 angered colonists and one
   reason Britain said it was necessary.
6. True or false, with a correction if false: "The Declaration of
   Independence was signed before the fighting at Lexington and Concord."
7. Place Saratoga (1777) and Yorktown (1781) on the timeline strip. How
   many years apart are they?
8. **Explain your reasoning:** Could the Boston Tea Party have caused the
   Stamp Act? Why or why not? (Two sentences.)

## Set B — Two claims and Boston protests (Lesson 02)

1. State the colonial claim about taxation in one sentence.
2. State the British claim about taxation in one sentence.
3. Name the three Tea Party ships and the number of chests destroyed.
4. Why was the Tea Act's cheaper tea still protested? (Principle, not price.)
5. A merchant's British cloth is boycotted. Name one incentive for the
   merchant to support the boycott and one to oppose it.
6. How many colonists were killed in the Boston Massacre, and who was one
   of them?
7. What is propaganda, and why is Revere's engraving an example?
8. **Explain your reasoning:** A learner says, "The British were just
   greedy." Rewrite this as a fair British claim, then say what evidence
   that side would cite.

## Set C — Sources (Lesson 03)

Use Accounts A and B from Lesson 03.

1. List two facts both accounts agree on.
2. Quote one opinion from Account A and one from Account B.
3. Who is Account B's audience? Give the clue inside the text.
4. What is Account A's purpose? Give one clue.
5. For the question "What did British officials want done after the Tea
   Party?", which account is more useful? Why?
6. For the question "How did Patriots want the Tea Party remembered?",
   which account is more useful? Why?
7. True or false, with a correction if false: "Accounts A and B are real
   documents written in 1773."
8. **Explain your reasoning:** Why is a persuasive source not useless?
   Give one example from this lesson.

## Set D — Turning points (Lesson 04)

1. Match: (a) Saratoga → (b) Yorktown → (c) Valley Forge → to: end of major
   fighting / French alliance / winter camp and training.
2. Why did Saratoga change France's choice? (Two sentences.)
3. Name two Black patriots and one contribution each.
4. Name one woman from this unit and her contribution, noting honestly
   whether her story is a verified biography or a traditional story.
5. What was decided at the Treaty of Paris (1783) about independence and
   boundaries?
6. True or false, with a correction if false: "Valley Forge was a battle
   Washington won."
7. Why might an enslaved person in 1778 have chosen the British side?
   (Two sentences.)
8. **Explain your reasoning:** Improve this claim: "France helped." Use
   one Yorktown fact.

## Set E — Weekly checks (mixed, all lessons)

1. Sequence: Stamp Act, Boston Massacre, Tea Party, Lexington, Declaration,
   Saratoga, Yorktown. (Seven events, in order.)
2. Give the colonial claim and the British claim in one sentence each.
3. Two accounts disagree about the Tea Party. Name one thing they agree
   on and one judgment they differ on.
4. What can the harbor illustration prove, and what can it not prove?
   (One sentence each.)
5. Write a claim about the past supported by two pieces of evidence from
   different lessons. Label each piece with its lesson.
