# Answer Key — Unit 03 (Teacher Only)

**Do not give this file to the learner.** Every answer below was solved
independently and reconciled with its lesson, practice, quiz, assessment,
or project task. Acceptable variation is noted where an open response
admits more than one good answer.

## Lesson 01 — Guided

1. Order: French and Indian War ends (1763) → Proclamation of 1763 →
   Stamp Act (1765) → Boston Tea Party (1773). The war's end and the
   Proclamation share 1763; order still matters because the Proclamation
   responded to the war's outcome (new territory, new costs).
2. Townshend Acts (1767): after the Stamp Act was repealed (1766),
   Parliament still needed revenue and still claimed the right to tax, so
   it taxed imports (glass, paint, paper, tea) instead of taxing paper
   directly inside the colonies.
3. No — a good claim needs the cause before the effect and a reason
   linking them. Both the Proclamation and the Stamp Act were effects of
   the war (debt, new territory); neither caused the other.
4. Example chain: war debt → Stamp Act → colonial protest. Reason: a
   government in debt looks for revenue, and direct taxation of paper was
   the revenue device Parliament chose.

## Lesson 01 — Independent

1. Timeline order (from the SVG): French and Indian War 1754–63,
   Proclamation 1763, Stamp Act 1765, Boston Massacre 1770, Boston Tea
   Party 1773, Lexington & Concord 1775, Declaration 1776, Saratoga 1777,
   Yorktown 1781, Treaty of Paris 1783. Any three circled with a reasoned
   sentence are acceptable.
2. Model claim: "The First Continental Congress probably met because
   Parliament's punishment of Boston (the Coercive Acts, 1774) alarmed
   other colonies, which wanted a coordinated response; even colonies
   that had dumped no tea feared the same punishment." Accept any version
   with a prior event + a reason.
3. Errors: (a) order is wrong — the Stamp Act (1765) came before the Tea
   Party (1773); (b) a later event cannot cause an earlier one. Fixed
   order: Stamp Act 1765 → … → Tea Party 1773.
4. Historians say "probable cause" because evidence rarely proves a single
   cause; several causes usually act together, and the link is an
   inference from order + reason, not a laboratory proof.

## Lesson 02 — Guided

1. Colonial claim: no representatives in Parliament, so no consent to
   taxes; their own assemblies should tax them. British claim: Parliament
   represents the whole empire and must pay for defense/government. (Any
   fair version accepted; the adult checks recognition, not wording.)
2. Revere's engraving emphasizes soldiers firing in a neat line at
   colonists; a historian needs another source to check the crowd's size,
   the soldiers' orders, and who provoked whom.
3. Merchant incentives: supporting the boycott keeps colonial customers
   and avoids being targeted; opposing it protects British supply lines
   and profits from British goods. (Any plausible pair accepted.)
4. The Intolerable Acts alarmed other colonies because (a) they punished
   a whole city for a group's act, and (b) they changed Massachusetts
   government and sent soldiers — a precedent other colonies feared for
   themselves.

## Lesson 02 — Independent

1. Two paragraphs as in the worked examples; each ends with the side's
   evidence (assemblies for colonists; war costs/empire-wide rule for
   Britain).
2. "Greed" is weak because it gives no reason Parliament itself would
   recognize and no checkable evidence. Replacement: the British claim
   from the lesson (empire-wide representation; paying for defense).
3. Three one-sentence voices, labeled as imagining (for example, a Son of
   Liberty celebrating a protest of principle; a Loyalist merchant fearing
   lost business; an enslaved dockworker noting that liberty talk did not
   include him). Any three distinct viewpoints, labeled as imagining,
   accepted.
4. The actors were colonists in costume, not Mohawk people. It matters
   because repeating the disguise without comment turns a real nation's
   identity into a costume and misstates who acted.

## Lesson 03 — Guided

1. Fact in A: "they boarded the ships in the harbor" / "342 chests."
   Opinion in A: "brave townsmen" / "blow for liberty." Fact in B:
   "boarded three merchant vessels" / "destroyed their cargoes." Opinion
   in B: "lawless mob" / "outrage."
2. Account B's audience: a superior official. Clue: "I request
   instructions and additional protection."
3. Judgment difference: A judges the actors brave and principled; B
   judges them lawless and criminal. (Any one judgment difference accepted.)
4. For "Why did the Tea Party happen?", neither account alone is enough:
   A gives the Patriot motive, B gives the British frame, but the Tea Act
   and the tax principle (Lesson 02) are needed to explain the cause.

## Lesson 03 — Independent

1. Agrees (at least two): ships boarded at night in Boston Harbor; tea
   cargoes destroyed. A only: 342 chests; no person harmed; protest of
   principle. B only: officers could not prevent it; great loss to the
   East India Company; request for protection.
2. A's purpose: to celebrate/rally (clue: "Let all lovers of freedom
   rejoice"). B's purpose: to report and request help (clue: "I request
   instructions").
3. Error: liking a side is not a source test. Rewrite with the five
   questions: maker, audience, purpose, agreement/difference, usefulness
   for a stated question.
4. For "How many chests?", Account A helps (342) because it gives a
   number; it should still be checked against a third source because its
   purpose is persuasion, not accounting.

## Lesson 04 — Guided

1. Trenton: a small victory that restored morale and re-enlistment.
   Saratoga: a major victory that won the French alliance. Yorktown: the
   surrender that ended major fighting.
2. Yorktown ended major fighting because Cornwallis's whole army
   surrendered with no escape by sea (French ships) or land (siege);
   Saratoga changed the war's scope but fighting continued for four more
   years.
3. Any three: for example, Peter Salem (Bunker Hill, Saratoga); Phillis
   Wheatley (poems arguing for liberty); Indigenous nations (own strategic
   choices, land losses). One sentence each, accepted if accurate.
4. "The war was long" is vague: it gives no dates, no comparison, no
   checkable claim. Improvement: add a span (1775–1783, eight years) and a
   comparison or consequence.

## Lesson 04 — Independent

1. Either choice accepted if defended with two pieces of evidence
   (Saratoga: alliance + wider war; Yorktown: surrender + end of major
   fighting). The adult marks the reasoning, not the choice.
2. Errors: Valley Forge was not a battle, and Washington won no battle
   there. It was the winter camp of 1777–78, marked by cold, hunger, and
   von Steuben's training.
3. An enslaved person might have chosen the British side because the
   British promised liberty to enslaved people who joined them, while
   Patriot liberty-talk had not freed them — a rational choice for
   personal freedom, not disloyalty to an abstract cause.
4. "France helped a lot" is vague and uncheckable. Strong version: "French
   ships at Yorktown were necessary for Cornwallis's surrender, because
   they blocked his escape by sea" (or any specific, evidence-linked
   version).

## Lesson 05 — Guided and Independent

- Guided 1: any five visible items (three ships, wharf, crates, rowboats,
  town houses, church steeples, calm water).
- Guided 2: any three not shown (named ships, disguised people, the date,
  the number 342, any named person).
- Guided 3: the scene belongs with the Boston Tea Party (1773) on the
  timeline; the written facts needed are the ships' names and the 342
  chests, which come from records, not the picture.
- Guided 4: supported — "Boston was a harbor town with large ships."
  Not supported — "The ships shown are the Tea Party ships." Difference:
  the first describes what is visible; the second names what the picture
  does not label.
- Independent 1: table with at least 5 "I can see" rows and 4 "I cannot
  tell" rows, as above.
- Independent 2: counting painted crates cannot give 342, because a
  painter chooses how many crates to paint; the number comes from written
  records.
- Independent 3 (text-only): three ships; a town of small houses and
  church steeples behind the harbor. A text description substitutes fairly
  for counting/identifying visible objects, but fails for arrangement,
  color, and mood, which only the picture carries.
- Exit check: the picture is a modern (2026) AI illustration, not a
  document or photograph from 1773.

## Unit assessment (50 points)

**Task A (1 point each, in order):** French and Indian War ends (1),
Proclamation of 1763 (2), Stamp Act (3), Boston Massacre (4), Boston Tea
Party (5), Lexington and Concord (6), Declaration of Independence (7),
Saratoga (8), Yorktown (9), Treaty of Paris (10).

**Task B:** 1. War debt after 1763 → Parliament sought revenue and passed
the Stamp Act (1765) as a direct internal tax. 2. The Coercive Acts'
punishment of Boston (1774) → other colonies, fearing the same treatment,
sent delegates to coordinate a response. Full points require prior event
+ linking reason; half points for an event without a reason.

**Task C:** Colonial claim (no representation, no consent; assemblies
should tax) and British claim (empire-wide Parliament; pay for defense),
each fairly stated with its reason. Half points if one side is a cartoon.

**Task D:** 1. Agree: at dawn at Lexington, British troops ordered an
armed colonial body to disperse, firing followed, and the British
continued toward Concord. 2. Differ in judgment: C presents militiamen
standing for rights; D presents rebels refusing a lawful order and firing
first. 3. Usefulness: each account is useful for its own side's view and
for what its maker wanted reported, but neither alone settles who fired
first — both are interested parties. A third source (another witness,
a later inquiry, or physical evidence) would be needed; accept any
reasoned answer that names the bias and the need for corroboration.

**Task E:** Any specific, evidence-linked claim (for example, "French
help was the most important reason the Patriots won, because the alliance
after Saratoga brought ships, and those ships blocked Cornwallis at
Yorktown") with two labeled pieces of evidence from different lessons,
reasons linking each, and a critic's sentence (for example, "A critic
could say American endurance and British overreach mattered as much").
Mark reasoning and evidence use, not the choice of "most important."
