# Formative Quiz — Unit 01, Week 4 (S18)

**Grade 11 · Language Arts · ~25 minutes · 20 points**
Covers objectives 1–5. This quiz is formative: misses are re-taught in
S19's review time before the unit assessment. Key in `answer-key.md` §Q.

**1.** (2 pts) List the six literary periods from Lesson 01 in chronological
order with their approximate date ranges.

**2.** (2 pts) In one to two sentences, state what the Romanticism /
Transcendentalism period believed literature should do.

**3.** (2 pts) Name the four moves of the close-reading routine in order,
and write one sentence on what the *question* move produces.

**4.** (2 pts) State one theme of *The Great Gatsby* as a **full
sentence** (not a topic word). Then quote one brief passage (under 25
words) that supports it.

**5.** (2 pts) Explain in two to three sentences the difference between an
objective summary and an analysis. Give a one-sentence example of each
about *Gatsby*.

**6.** (2 pts) Douglass writes, "This Fourth of July is yours, not mine."
In two to three sentences, explain what work the word "yours" is doing in
that sentence.

**7.** (2 pts) Name one similarity and one difference in how Douglass's
speech and Whitman's "Song of Myself" treat American freedom. Support
with one quotation (either text).

**8.** (2 pts) What is an *extended metaphor*? Identify the extended
metaphor in Dickinson's "'Hope' is the thing with feathers —" and name
the two things being compared.

**9.** (2 pts) A learner writes: "Whitman's grass means everyone is equal."
In two to three sentences, explain why this is a topic-level observation
rather than a theme-level claim, and rewrite it as a theme-level claim
about section 6.

**10.** (2 pts) In Whitman's section 6, the grass grows "among black folks
as among white." In two to three sentences, what claim about American
identity does this image make, and why does it matter that Whitman wrote
it in 1855?
