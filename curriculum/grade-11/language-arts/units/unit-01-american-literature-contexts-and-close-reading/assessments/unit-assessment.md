# Unit Assessment — Unit 01 (S19)

**Grade 11 · Language Arts · one 50-minute session · 60 points**
Drafting (S13) and revision (S15) precede this session; S19 is final
write-up plus Part B. Key and scoring guidance in `answer-key.md` §UA.
Rubric for the essay is in `teacher-guide.md`.

## Part A — Close-reading essay (40 pts)

Choose **one** of the three passages below. Write a 500–700-word analysis
of how the passage develops a theme (for the Dickinson poem, the poem's
central idea). Your essay must:

- State the theme as a full, arguable sentence (your thesis).
- Quote the passage at least **three** times (brief quotations, correctly
  punctuated and cited).
- Explain what each quotation proves — never leave a quote to speak for
  itself.
- Name **one place where the passage leaves something uncertain**, and
  explain why that uncertainty matters to your reading (RL.11-12.1).

**Passage 1 — Fitzgerald, *The Great Gatsby*, Ch. 9:**
"Gatsby believed in the green light, the orgiastic future that year by
year recedes before us. It eluded us then, but that's no matter — tomorrow
we will run faster, stretch out our arms further... And one fine morning —
[...] So we beat on, boats against the current, borne back ceaselessly
into the past."

**Passage 2 — Whitman, "Song of Myself" (1855), §6:**
"Or I guess the grass is itself a child, the produced babe of the
vegetation. Or I guess it is a uniform hieroglyphic, And it means,
Sprouting alike in broad zones and narrow zones, Growing among black
folks as among white..."

**Passage 3 — Dickinson, "'Hope' is the thing with feathers —," stanza 2:**
"And sweetest — in the Gale — is heard — And sore must be the storm —
That could abash the little Bird That kept so many warm —"

## Part B — Short answers (20 pts, 2–3 sentences each unless noted)

**1.** (3 pts) Name all six periods in order with date ranges, and write
the one-sentence belief for any two.

**2.** (2 pts) Write a two-sentence objective summary of the opening of
Douglass's speech (the "why am I called upon to speak here today?"
passage). Mark any word you'd need to remove if it were secretly a
judgment.

**3.** (3 pts) State two themes of *Gatsby* as full sentences and explain
in three to four sentences how they interact in the novel's ending.

**4.** (3 pts) Compare how Douglass and Dickinson treat freedom: one
similarity and one difference, each supported by a quotation.

**5.** (2 pts) Define *extended metaphor* in your own words and explain
what Dickinson's bird comparison lets the poem say that a plain statement
could not.

**6.** (2 pts) In the "Harbor at Dawn" applied task, you compared a text
with its illustration. In two to three sentences, explain one thing an
illustration can show a reader that text alone cannot — and one thing text
can do that an illustration cannot.

**7.** (2 pts) During the seminar, a classmate claims "Whitman and
Douglass basically agree about America." Write the probing question you
would ask, and name the quotation you would offer in response.

**8.** (3 pts) Reflection: which of the unit's five objectives is
strongest for you now, and which needs more work? For each, cite one
specific piece of your own work from the unit as evidence.
