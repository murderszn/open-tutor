# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: American Literature Contexts, Themes, and Close Reading

**Grade 11 · Language Arts · for the guiding adult · companion to the
unit README's 20-session pacing**

This unit establishes the two habits the entire year depends on: placing
texts in their historical period, and proving claims from the words on the
page. Everything later (founding rhetoric, criticism, research) assumes
the close-reading routine is automatic by week 5. If the routine isn't
solid, slow down here — U02 will punish gaps.

## Session-by-session notes

- **S1–S2 (Lesson 01):** Build the period timeline *together* — don't
  hand it over finished. The act of writing the one-sentence beliefs is
  the learning. For the Douglass excerpt, model annotation aloud on the
  first paragraph ("I'm circling 'pardon me' because..."), then hand the
  pen over. Collect the error-analysis task (bad summary) and discuss it
  before independent work — it prevents a week of opinion-summaries.
- **S3 (Workshop A):** Check quotation accuracy character-by-character.
  Learners retyping from memory corrupt quotes; this is the session to
  break the habit. The claim–evidence–explanation format introduced here
  is used all year — name it and keep the name.
- **S4–S5 (Lesson 02):** The full-sentence theme test ("can you disagree
  with it?") is the lesson's engine. If the learner's theme is really a
  topic, don't accept it and move on — the whole unit's writing depends
  on this distinction. *Gatsby* reading: about 2–3 chapters per week;
  check progress at S4 and S6, not just at the end.
- **S6 (Workshop B):** The theme-tracking chart's last column ("serves
  both?") is the highest-value work in the unit. If entries are thin,
  model one: the green light's diminishment (Ch. 5) serves the
  Dream-theme *and* the illusion-theme — possessing the illusion kills it.
- **S7–S8 (Lesson 03):** Comparison paragraphs fail most often as
  "stapled descriptions." Require the "while" sentence: "Douglass does
  ___, *while* Whitman does ___." The error-analysis task (agreement
  fallacy) should be discussed, not just assigned.
- **S9 (Workshop C):** One comparative paragraph, done well, beats three
  rushed ones. Spend the session on quotation punctuation and the
  explanation sentence after each quote.
- **S10–S11 (Lesson 04):** The modeled close reading is meant to be
  *watched*, not just read — do it live, thinking aloud, including one
  moment where you change your mind about a line. Learners need to see
  revision as part of reading. Collect the seminar prep sheet at S11;
  skim for empty evidence notes and send learners back before S12.
- **S12 (Seminar):** Your job is traffic control and one or two probes,
  not content delivery. Track who speaks with evidence; ensure everyone
  speaks at least twice. End with 10 minutes of synthesis: "What did we
  figure out together?"
- **S13/S15 (Drafting/Revision):** S13 produces a complete draft (not a
  plan). S15 is revision against margin notes — require visible changes,
  not just proofreading. The essay rubric below is shared with the
  learner at S13.
- **S14 (Applied task):** The "Harbor at Dawn" activity is transfer:
  the routine applied to an unfamiliar text. Score it on use of evidence
  from *both* text and image, not on which side of question 4 the learner
  takes.
- **S16 (Project workshop):** Approve the three texts and the theme
  sentence before any drafting. The most common failure is a
  theme-sentence that collapses into a topic — narrow with
  when/where/for whom.
- **S17 (Review):** Retrieval practice: periods from memory, both *Gatsby*
  themes from memory, one comparative claim aloud. Re-teach whatever is
  shaky — this is cheaper than re-teaching it in U02.
- **S18 (Quiz):** 25 minutes, then immediate scoring together. Every miss
  becomes a 2-minute re-teach on the spot or a flagged item for S19's
  opening review.
- **S19 (Assessment):** Essay final write-up (draft exists from S13/S15)
  plus Part B. Collect the anthology project with it.
- **S20 (Reflection/conference):** Read all four reading-log entries
  together. Ask: what kind of reader are you now that you weren't in
  week 2? Record notes for the U02 starting point.

## Essay rubric (Part A, 40 pts)

| Criterion (pts) | Exemplary | Proficient | Developing | Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thesis as arguable theme sentence (8) | 8: precise, arguable, governs the essay | 6: arguable but broad | 4: topic phrase, not a sentence | 2: missing or plot summary |
| Quoted evidence, 3+ passages (12) | 12: accurate, well-chosen, correctly punctuated | 9: accurate, mostly well-chosen | 6: fewer than 3, or inaccuracies | 3: quotes absent or decorative |
| Explanation of evidence (12) | 12: every quote explained; reasoning explicit | 9: mostly explained | 6: quotes left to speak for themselves | 3: no explanation |
| Uncertainty addressed (8) | 8: names a real ambiguity and explains its significance | 6: names an ambiguity | 4: gestures at uncertainty vaguely | 2: not addressed |

## Common misconceptions and re-teaching moves

| Misconception | Re-teach |
|---|---|
| Theme = topic ("The theme is wealth") | The disagreement test: "Can a reasonable reader disagree? Then it's a theme." |
| Summary smuggles judgment ("tragically," "deserved") | "Which words in the passage say that?" — the Lesson 01 error task, repeated as needed |
| Comparison = two descriptions | Require the "while" sentence; ban "also" as a transition for one session |
| A quote proves itself | "The quote is the evidence; where's the argument?" — require the explanation sentence every time |
| Period labels are boxes writers lived in | Revisit the timeline note: labels are readers' tools; overlap (Harlem Renaissance/Modernism) is the proof |
| Uncertainty = weakness in the text | Model: the strongest readings *find* the uncertain spots (Whitman's "you," the green light's "might have been") |

## Differentiation

- **Reading load:** The novel may be paired with the free LibriVox
  audiobook (listen-while-reading) for any chapters where pace slips;
  the analysis work stays the same.
- **Writing load:** Comparative paragraphs and the essay may be dictated
  first, then transcribed and revised — the thinking is assessed, not the
  typing.
- **Extension:** The Lesson 02 extension (first page vs. last page),
  Lesson 03 extension (adding "I Hear America Singing"), and Lesson 04
  extension (gull's-eye vignette) are ready-made for learners ahead of
  pace. The project rubric's "Exceeds" band gives them somewhere to aim.

## Reteaching triggers

- If the S3 workshop shows quotation errors in more than half the
  claim–evidence sets, add a 15-minute quotation-accuracy drill at S4's
  start before Lesson 02.
- If the S9 comparative paragraph has no real contrast sentence, do not
  proceed to Lesson 04's seminar prep until one contrast sentence exists
  in writing — the seminar will fail without it.
- If the S18 quiz shows objective 1 (periods) below 50%, open S19 with a
  10-minute timeline reconstruction from memory before the assessment.
