# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Literary Criticism and Analytical Writing

**For the guiding adult only.** Do not place this file beside student
questions; the answer key is a separate file. This guide covers pacing,
misconceptions, differentiation, and reteaching for each session.

## Misconceptions and corrections

| Misconception | Correction |
|---|---|
| A lens is a label for a text ("a formalist story") | Ask: "What did *you* do with the lens?" A lens is a method a reader chooses; the same text takes every lens. |
| Reader-response means "anything goes" | The check is always a quotation. A response without a quoted line is a report, not criticism. (Lesson 04, Independent practice 5.) |
| Inventing history when the historical lens has no evidence | Praise "there is no evidence" as a complete answer. The Lesson 01 invented passage exists for exactly this. |
| Naming a repeated word and stopping | A pattern needs the *difference* — what changes each time it returns. Ask: "What is different the second time?" |
| "Unity" means everything agrees | The *Gatsby* ending shows unity can mean a tension held steadily, not solved. |
| Biography as a skeleton key ("the story means X because her life was Y") | Require a quotation for every biographical claim; the story is not a diary (Lesson 03, limit 1). |
| The author's stated purpose is the final word | Gilman's 1913 account is evidence about intention; the text's form and effect are separate evidence (Lesson 03, limit 2). |
| Presentism — judging 1887 medicine by today's standards without naming the standard | Name the standard being used, then judge. The historical lens forbids pretending the judgment is the writer's. |
| The counter-reading as a straw reader to defeat | The test: would the rival reader recognize their own position in the summary? If not, rewrite it. |
| Quotation as analysis ("this shows…") | A paragraph that is mostly quotation has not explained anything. Require the explanation sentence after every quotation. |
| A conclusion as a new argument | The Mitchell example (Lesson 05, Independent practice 5) is the classic case: the conclusion must follow from the evidence. |

## Differentiation

- **Supports:** the lens table as a bookmark for the whole unit; the
  pattern–tension–unity table before any formalist paragraph; the
  essay-plan table before any drafting; read-alouds of the short texts;
  dictation or recorded responses where writing is the barrier rather
  than the thinking; extended time on the essay (the S15 studio may
  run long — protect it, and move the S16 project workshop rather than
  cutting drafting time).
- **Extensions:** a second counter-reading from a third lens (Lesson 05);
  the Dickinson formalist paragraph (Lesson 02); the *Gatsby* final-line
  reader-response paragraph (Lesson 04); the publication-history research
  check (Lesson 03); comparative criticism of two published readings of
  "The Yellow Wallpaper" (adult-selected, previewed) for learners ahead
  of pace.
- **Reading load:** no new long text. The re-reading is the work; check
  that the learner arrives with the passage annotated *for the lens in
  use*, not merely re-skimmed. If the learner has not re-read, spend the
  first 10 minutes of the session reading together rather than starting
  the lesson behind.

## Reteaching

- **Lens table not retained (S17 review):** re-teach Lesson 01 with the
  image task only — match objects to lenses, then re-apply to "The Tide
  Clock." Do not re-teach all five lessons.
- **Formalist paragraph is description, not analysis:** return to
  practice.md §B and require the table (pattern | tension | unity, with
  a quotation in each row) before any sentences.
- **Context paragraph invents or over-claims:** return to Lesson 03's
  three rules and the three limits; have the learner underline the
  sentence that states the limit — if there is no such sentence, the
  paragraph is unfinished.
- **Two theses use the same evidence:** return to Lesson 04's worked
  examples and require one quotation per thesis that the other thesis
  does not use.
- **Essay has no counter-reading or a straw one:** re-teach Lesson 05's
  counter-reading section with the Worked Example 1 table; the learner
  must be able to state the rival position in one sentence the rival
  would accept.
- **Essay conclusion introduces a new claim:** have the learner delete
  the conclusion and write a new one using only sentences already
  proved in the body.

## Readiness signals

- **Ready for U06** (argument, evidence synthesis, public reasoning)
  when the learner can: state all three lenses with question, evidence,
  and blind spot without notes; write a formalist paragraph with two
  quotations; state one historical limit in writing; and produce an
  essay with a fair counter-reading. U06 builds directly on the
  counter-reading skill — a learner who cannot yet state a rival fairly
  should re-do Lesson 05's counter-reading exercise before U06's
  counterargument work.
- **Not yet ready** if the essay's quotations are dropped in cold or
  the counter-reading is a straw reader: use the S20 reflection session
  for a targeted re-teach rather than carrying the gap into U06.

## S19 notes (after scoring)

Record per objective: which objective(s) each miss maps to (the quiz
header and the assessment's Part 2 key give the mapping). Note the next
teaching step in the margin of the learner's copy: a miss on lens
identification → re-teach the lens table; on formalist reading → re-do
practice.md §B; on context → re-teach the three rules; on two-lens
comparison → re-do the Lesson 04 two-thesis exercise; on the
counter-reading → re-teach Lesson 05 before U06.
