## C. Context check — which claims need a date? (S9)

Sort each claim into **needs a date** (a historical/biographical claim:
it is only evidence if it can be dated and sourced) or **needs no date**
(a textual claim: the passage itself is the evidence). Write one line
explaining why.

1. "The wallpaper's pattern defeats the eye that follows it."
2. "Douglass delivered his speech to an abolitionist audience."
3. "The narrator is forbidden to write."
4. "Mitchell told Gilman never to touch a pen again."
5. "'The Yellow Wallpaper' was published in 1892."
6. "The narrator's brother is also a physician."
7. "Gilman wrote the story to save people from being driven crazy."
8. "The novel's final sentence opposes forward and backward motion."

## D. Thesis workshop (S13–S14, with Lesson 05)

thesis.

2. "Gilman's story shows that doctors in the 1890s were wrong."
3. "Hughes's poem is powerful because it is emotional."
4. "'The Yellow Wallpaper' is a feminist story."

## E. Quotation integration and error analysis (S15, drafting studio)

Each item below shows a draft sentence using a quotation. For each:
(a) name the problem (cold quotation? quotation without explanation?
quotation doing the arguing?), and (b) rewrite the sentence so the
quotation does all three jobs (introduce, quote, explain).

1. "John is practical in the extreme." This is important.
2. Gilman shows the wallpaper is bad. "I never saw a worse paper in my
   life." The narrator hates it.
3. "So we beat on, boats against the current, borne back ceaselessly
   into the past." This proves the American Dream is dead.
4. Hughes writes, "I, too, am America." This is a good ending.
