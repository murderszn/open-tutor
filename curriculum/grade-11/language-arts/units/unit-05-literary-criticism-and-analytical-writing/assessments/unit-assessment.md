# Unit Assessment — Unit 05: Literary Criticism and Analytical Writing

**Session S19 · essay drafted in S15, submitted here with short answers · the essay is the culminating assessment**

The adult scores the essay with the rubric below and the short answers
with the key in `answer-key.md`. This assessment is not standardized or
validated; it measures the unit's stated objectives for this learner.

## Part 1 — Analytical essay (900–1,200 words; 60 points)

Write an analytical essay on **one** anchor text from this unit —
"The Yellow Wallpaper," the closing pages of *The Great Gatsby*,
Douglass's "What to the Slave Is the Fourth of July?" (excerpt), or
Hughes's "I, Too" — through **one** critical lens (formalist,
historical/biographical, reader-response, or feminist). Your essay must:

1. State a precise, lens-based analytical thesis (lens, claim, how,
   so-what) in the introduction.
2. Develop the thesis in at least three evidence blocks, each opening
   with a claim sentence, integrating at least one exact quotation
   (introduce–quote–explain), and closing with an explanation of what
   the quotation shows.
3. Develop a counter-reading fairly: state a rival interpretation of
   the same text, give its best evidence, and answer it by concession
   or refutation.
4. Conclude from the evidence — no new claims in the conclusion.
5. Use the conventions taught in Unit 04 (deliberate syntax, precise
   word choice, standard citation of quotations).

**Essay rubric (60 points):**

| Criterion | Points | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis | 10 | Names the lens; arguable, specific, falsifiable; contains claim, mechanism, and so-what |
| Evidence blocks | 18 | Three or more blocks; each opens with a claim, integrates an exact quotation, and explains what it shows in the lens's terms |
| Counter-reading | 12 | Rival interpretation stated accurately with its best evidence; answered by concession or refutation the reader can follow |
| Conclusion | 6 | Follows from the evidence; restates the thesis in light of the argument; introduces nothing new |
| Organization and style | 8 | Clear paragraphing; deliberate syntax and precise vocabulary (Unit 04 tools); formal tone |
| Accuracy and citation | 6 | Every quotation exact and traceable to its edition; dated facts sourced; no invented history, no anachronism |

**Bands:** 54–60 excellent; 45–53 good; 36–44 developing (revise one
criterion with the adult and resubmit); below 36 beginning (re-teach
with the teacher guide and rebuild one evidence block).

## Part 2 — Short answers (4 questions, 10 points each; 40 points)

Answer in the notebook, 3–5 sentences each, quoting the text where
asked.

1. **(Objective 1)** State the central question, the evidence that
   counts, and one blind spot of the formalist lens *and* of the
   reader-response lens. (Two lenses, three items each.)
2. **(Objective 2)** Choose one passage from *The Great Gatsby* or
   "The Yellow Wallpaper" and trace a pattern through it: name the
   pattern, the tension it creates, and whether the passage resolves,
   holds, or deepens the tension — quoting twice.
3. **(Objective 3)** State one dated historical/biographical fact about
   Gilman or Douglass (with its date and source) and explain what it
   explains about the text — and one thing it cannot decide. (Your
   answer must name the limit explicitly.)
4. **(Objective 4)** Hughes's "I, Too" ends "I, too, am America." Write
   one reader-response sentence and one feminist sentence about that
   line, each with a different piece of evidence, and one sentence
   naming what each reading leaves open.

**Total: 100 points.** The adult records the result per objective and
notes the next teaching step in the teacher guide's S19 notes: a miss
on Part 2, question 1 → re-teach the lens table; on question 2 → re-do
the formalist drill (practice.md §B); on question 3 → re-teach the
three rules of context (Lesson 03); on question 4 → re-do the
two-thesis exercise (Lesson 04); a weak counter-reading in the essay →
re-teach Lesson 05's counter-reading section before U06, where
counterargument returns as a public-reasoning skill.
