# Formative Quiz — Unit 05: Literary Criticism and Analytical Writing

**Session S18 · ~30 minutes · open notebook (lens table allowed) · scored by the adult; misses feed S19 preparation, not punishment**

Answer in the notebook. Short answers: 2–4 sentences each unless noted.
Quote the text where the question asks for a quotation.

## Part A — Lenses (4 questions, 4 points each)

1. Name the three lenses taught in Lessons 01–03 and, for each, state
   its central question in one sentence.
2. A critic writes: "The story's power comes from the way its images
   repeat and reverse, not from anything about the author's life."
   Which lens is this critic using? Name one thing this lens cannot see
   about the story.
3. A critic writes: "Gilman sent a copy of the story to Dr. Mitchell,
   which tells us what she hoped the story would do." Which lens is this
   critic using? Name one thing this lens cannot decide about the story.
4. A critic writes: "Two readers in different decades will read this
   ending differently, because each fills the gaps the text leaves."
   Which lens is this critic using? Name the two things reader-response
   critics agree on (Lesson 01).

## Part B — Formalist reading (2 questions, 6 points each)

5. The final sentence of *The Great Gatsby* is: "So we beat on, boats
   against the current, borne back ceaselessly into the past." Name the
   pattern, the tension, and the unity of this sentence, each in one
   sentence, quoting at least one word from the sentence.
6. Gilman writes of the wallpaper: "It is dull enough to confuse the eye
   in following, pronounced enough to constantly irritate, and provoke
   study." Explain in 3–4 sentences what tension this sentence sets up
   and how the pattern of *pursuit-and-collapse* continues it, quoting
   one more phrase from the story.

## Part C — Historical reading (2 questions, 5 points each)

7. State two dated facts about Gilman and the rest cure (with dates),
   and in two sentences explain what they explain about "The Yellow
   Wallpaper" — and one thing they cannot decide about it.
8. Douglass delivered "What to the Slave Is the Fourth of July?" on
   July 5, 1852, the day after Independence Day, to the Rochester
   Ladies' Anti-Slavery Society. In 3–4 sentences, explain how that
   occasion shapes the speech's opening move, quoting the speech's
   central question.

## Part D — Two lenses, one text (1 question, 8 points)

9. Write two thesis sentences about Hughes's "I, Too" — one
   reader-response, one feminist (or historical/biographical) — each
   naming its lens. Under each thesis, give one quotation that counts as
   evidence for *that* thesis. Then write one sentence naming what each
   thesis leaves open.

## Part E — Thesis repair (1 question, 8 points)

10. A classmate's thesis reads: "The wallpaper is a symbol of the
    narrator's mind." (a) Name two things missing from this thesis
    (use the four-part analytical thesis frame from Lesson 05).
    (b) Rewrite it as a four-part analytical thesis through a lens of
    your choice. (c) Give one quotation that would count as evidence
    for your rewritten thesis, with one sentence explaining what it
    shows.
