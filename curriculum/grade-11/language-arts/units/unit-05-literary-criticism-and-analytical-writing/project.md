# Project — Two Lenses, One Text

**Unit 05 · introduced S16, presented S20 · ~3 sessions of work plus presentation**

## Task

Choose **one** anchor text from this unit — "The Yellow Wallpaper,"
the closing pages of *The Great Gatsby*, Douglass's "What to the Slave
Is the Fourth of July?" (excerpt), or Hughes's "I, Too" — and read it
through **two different critical lenses**. Produce a portfolio with
three parts:

1. **Lens A analysis** (400–600 words): a formalist, historical/
   biographical, reader-response, or feminist reading of the text, with
   a named thesis and at least three integrated quotations.
2. **Lens B analysis** (400–600 words): the same text through a
   *different* lens, with a named thesis and at least three integrated
   quotations. At least one of the two theses must differ from the
   thesis in your unit-assessment essay.
3. **Comparison and reflection** (300–500 words): what changed when the
   lens changed — thesis, evidence, conclusion — and what each lens
   could not see; which lens you would keep if you could keep only one
   for this text, and why; and one question about the text that neither
   lens answers, with the kind of evidence that question would need.

## Materials

Notebook, the text, the Lesson 01 lens table, the unit's lessons and
practice, and the Resource Pack. No new research is required; the
historical lens, if chosen, uses the dated facts already verified in
Lesson 03 and the Resource Pack.

## Steps

1. **Choose and re-read (S16).** Pick the text and the two lenses.
   Re-read the text once per lens, annotating only for that lens.
   Get the adult's approval of the pairing: the two lenses must be
   genuinely different (formalist + historical, reader-response +
   feminist, and so on — not two names for the same question).
2. **Draft Lens A (S16–S17).** Write the thesis first, then the three
   evidence blocks, then the conclusion. Check every quotation against
   the text.
3. **Draft Lens B (S17–S19).** Same structure. Do not reuse Lens A's
   quotations as your main evidence unless you explain why the same
   passage counts differently under the new lens — that explanation is
   itself good analysis.
4. **Write the comparison (S19–S20).** Name exactly what moved when the
   lens changed. Name each lens's blind spot for this text. Answer the
   "keep only one" question with a reason tied to the text, not to
   preference.
5. **Present (S20).** A 5-minute presentation of the comparison to the
   adult (or a small audience the adult arranges): the two theses, one
   quotation each, and the question neither lens answers. The adult
   asks at least two evidence-based questions; the learner answers with
   quotations.

## Deliverables

- The portfolio (Parts 1–3), 1,100–1,700 words total, with quotations
  checked against the texts and a short source note naming the edition
  used for each quotation.
- The presentation (S20), with notes the learner prepared in advance.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Excellent (4) | Good (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lens A analysis | Thesis names the lens, is arguable and specific; three quotations integrated (introduce–quote–explain); conclusion follows from evidence | Thesis names the lens and is arguable; quotations mostly integrated; conclusion mostly follows | Thesis vague or lens unnamed; quotations dropped in or unexplained; conclusion restates the thesis | No clear thesis; quotations missing or misquoted; no conclusion |
| Lens B analysis | As above, with a genuinely different lens; at least one thesis differs from the assessment essay's | As above; lens different; thesis differs from the assessment essay's | Lens barely differs from Lens A, or thesis repeats the assessment essay | Same lens twice, or no second analysis |
| Comparison and reflection | Names precisely what changed (thesis, evidence, conclusion); names each lens's blind spot; "keep one" choice reasoned from the text; unanswered question stated with the evidence it would need | Names what changed and one blind spot; choice reasoned; question stated | Lists differences without saying what changed; blind spots missing; choice is preference only | No real comparison; no blind spots; no question |
| Evidence and accuracy | Every quotation exact and sourced; no invented history; no anachronism; counter-reading or rival noted where relevant | Quotations exact; one minor sourcing slip, corrected on review | A misquotation or an unsourced historical claim | Invented facts, or quotations used as decoration |
| Presentation | Clear 5-minute comparison; answers questions with quotations | Clear; answers questions, sometimes without quotation | Reads the portfolio aloud; cannot answer questions | No presentation, or presentation without the text |

**Scoring:** 18–20 = excellent; 14–17 = good; 10–13 = developing
(revise one criterion with the adult and resubmit); below 10 = beginning
(re-teach with the teacher guide and rebuild one analysis).

## Notes for the adult

- The pairing matters more than the text: formalist + historical is
  the clearest contrast for *Gatsby*; reader-response + feminist is
  the richest for Gilman; historical + reader-response is the most
  demanding for Douglass. Steer the learner toward a pairing whose
  contrast they can actually show.
- The comparison (Part 3) is where the unit's essential question is
  answered; if time is short, protect Part 3, not the word counts.
- A portfolio that quotes the same passage in both analyses *without*
  explaining why it counts differently has not yet done the comparison
  — send it back with that one question.
