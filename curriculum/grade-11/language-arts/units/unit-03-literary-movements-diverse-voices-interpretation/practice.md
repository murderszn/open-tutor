# Practice — Unit 03: Literary Movements, Diverse Voices, and Interpretation

Work in order. Each set is used in the session shown in the unit
README's pacing table. Do not look at the answer key until you
have finished a set; the key explains the reasoning, not just the
answer.

## §A — Movement sorting (Workshop A, S3)

Sort each original passage into one movement. For each, write the
four-part classification: label, trait, quotation, what the trait
does. Passages are original, written for this unit.

**A1.** "The tide came in the way it always had, and the
fisherman mended his nets the way his father had, and nobody in
the village called this fate — they called it Tuesday."

**A2.** "She did not so much enter the room as the room entered
her: the wallpaper, the clock, the smell of other people's
cooking, all arriving at once and none of them in order."

**A3.** "He believed the soul could be weighed the way a
merchant weighs grain, and spent his life building finer scales,
never noticing that the thing he weighed grew lighter every
time he touched it."

**A4.** "The contract was read aloud, the witnesses signed, the
clerk filed it under the correct letter, and the marriage
continued exactly as the contract required."

**A5.** "We sang because the work was hard, and the song made
the work ours: a verse for lifting, a chorus for carrying, and
a last low note for going home."

**A6.** "The city gave him a job counting other men's money, a
room with a window facing a wall, and a cough he had inherited
along with his father's name."

## §B — Ambiguity audit (Workshop B, S6)

For "Young Goodman Brown," complete a three-column table:

| Proved (with quotation) | Suggested (with quotation) | Left open |
|---|---|---|

Rules: nothing goes in the "Proved" column without a quotation
that proves it; nothing goes in "Left open" that the story
actually settles. Include at least four rows. Then answer:

**B1.** What does your audit show about the difference between
what happens in the forest and what happens to Brown afterward?
**B2.** Which column was hardest to fill, and why?

## §C — Whose perspective centers? (Workshop C, S9)

Complete a perspective map for each anchor text. Columns: *What
the narrator/heroine knows* | *What the reader knows that she
does not (or not yet)* | *What nobody in the story knows*.
Include at least two items per column, each with a quotation or
page reference.

**C1.** Gilman, "The Yellow Wallpaper."
**C2.** Chopin, *The Awakening* (excerpts assigned).
**C3.** In two sentences per text, state what changes when the
perspective moves: if Gilman's story were told by John, and
Chopin's novel by Edna's husband, what would each story lose?

## §D — Theme comparison (S11)

**D1.** Gilman and Chopin both write about confinement. Write
one paragraph comparing them, using the comparison sentence from
Lesson 04: "Both texts treat the theme of _____, but where Text A
uses _____ to _____, Text B uses _____ to _____." Quote at
least one line from each text.

**D2.** Hughes and Hawthorne both write about a person's
standing before a larger power (history, guilt, society). Write
one paragraph comparing them the same way. What does the
comparison show about the difference between a movement that
trusts the soul and a movement that centers a people's voice?

**D3.** Write an objective summary (5–7 sentences, no judgment
words) of one anchor text, then a second paragraph of analysis
that *does* judge it. Label the two paragraphs clearly. Why does
keeping them separate matter?

## §E — Version comparison (S14)

**E1.** Using the four questions from Lesson 05 (emphasis,
omission, addition, judgment), write a full comparison of the
Lesson 05 illustration against "The Yellow Wallpaper": one
paragraph per question, at least one story quotation per
paragraph.

**E2.** Choose one line from any anchor text. Describe two
different performed readings of it (tone, pace, pause) and
evaluate each in two sentences: what does each reading bring
out, and what does each flatten?

**E3.** A classmate says the illustration "proves" that John
watched his wife write. In three sentences, explain the error:
an illustration interprets; it does not prove. What kind of
evidence *would* prove a claim about the story, and where would
you find it?

## §F — Vocabulary in context

Use each word correctly in a sentence about a text from this
unit: *movement, symbol, ambiguity, unreliable narrator, point
of view, dramatic irony, understatement, vernacular,
fragmentation, interpretation*. Then circle the three words you
are least sure of and write a question about each for the adult.
