# Culminating Assessment — Unit 03: Literary Movements, Diverse Voices, and Interpretation

**Week 13, session S19 · 50 points · about 90 minutes.**
Answer in your notebook or on paper. You may use your movement
field guide and the anchor texts, but not the lessons, practice
sets, or answer key. The adult scores with `answer-key.md`.

## Part A — Movement classification (12 pts)

Classify each original passage below with the four-part format:
label, trait, quotation, what the trait does. (6 pts each.)

**A1.** "She kept the shop the way the sea keeps a shore: not by
holding on, but by returning every morning to what the night had
rearranged, and calling the rearrangement her own."

**A2.** "The engine failed on a grade too steep for its load, the
company sent a form instead of a man, and the family learned
that hunger, like weather, does not negotiate."

## Part B — Point of view (12 pts)

**B1. (6 pts)** Read this original passage, written for this
assessment in the manner of a 19th-century journal entry:

> "My husband says I imagine the draft. He says it with such
> kindness that I find myself apologizing for feeling cold. I
> have taken to writing down the temperature each morning, in
> a hand so small that no one but I could read it."

Analyze the passage's point of view: what is stated, what is
meant, and what the first-person voice reveals and conceals.
Use the terms *understatement* and *irony* correctly, and quote
the passage at least twice.

**B2. (6 pts)** Is the narrator of B1 reliable? Answer in a
paragraph: define what you mean by reliable, give two pieces of
evidence, and name one thing the reader cannot determine from
this passage alone.

## Part C — Interpretation across versions (14 pts)

**C1. (7 pts)** Using the four questions from Lesson 05
(emphasis, omission, addition, judgment), evaluate the Lesson 05
illustration as an interpretation of "The Yellow Wallpaper."
Write one paragraph per question, with at least one story
quotation in each paragraph. Your judgment paragraph must state
a verdict (clearer, muddier, or different) and defend it.

**C2. (7 pts)** Describe, in words, your own staged reading of
one passage from any anchor text in this unit: the passage, the
speaker's position, one sound, and one lighting choice. Then
justify each choice from the text in one sentence each. Your
staging must be your own, not a copy of a worked example.

## Part D — Theme comparison essay (12 pts)

**D. (12 pts)** Write an essay of 400–600 words comparing how
two texts from this unit treat a shared theme (confinement and
autonomy, identity and voice, or freedom and belonging — your
choice). Your essay must:

- state the shared theme and the two texts in the first
  paragraph;
- quote each text at least twice;
- use the comparison sentence from Lesson 04 ("Both texts treat
  the theme of _____, but where Text A uses _____ to _____,
  Text B uses _____ to _____") at least once;
- write one objective summary paragraph (no judgment words)
  before your analysis paragraphs;
- end with a sentence stating what the comparison shows that
  either text alone does not.

**Scoring reminder.** An essay that argues a reading the adult
disagrees with, but supports it with quotations and reasoning,
scores higher than an essay that repeats the adult's reading
without evidence. Misquotations cost accuracy points; check
every quotation against its edition before submitting.
