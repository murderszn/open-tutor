# Resource Pack — Unit 03: Literary Movements, Diverse Voices, and Interpretation

**Goal:** Give a grade-11 learner (and guiding adult) free,
no-account routes to every core objective: mapping five literary
movements, analyzing symbol and point of view in Hawthorne,
Gilman, and Chopin, reading Hughes and the Harlem Renaissance,
and evaluating interpretations across versions. **Age fit:**
high-school grade 11; the adult previews every link for
advertising, comments, and suitability before the learner opens
it. Checked 2026-10-27 unless noted. "Verified" = the source
page's text and publisher were confirmed on that date; the adult
opens each page before assigning and confirms quotations against
the edition used. "Search link" = a clearly labeled
preferred-channel search the adult runs and previews (no invented
titles, runtimes, or captions).

   <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=literary+movements+romanticism+realism+naturalism+modernism+explained>
   (Use for: Lesson 01; prefer videos that define movements by

   date-memorization videos.)

   symbol, ambiguity, and the forest journey:
   <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=young+goodman+brown+hawthorne+symbol+ambiguity+analysis>

   night's reality open rather than settling it.)
3. **Search link (YouTube):** "The Yellow Wallpaper" analysis —
   point of view, the rest cure, and the wallpaper:
   <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=yellow+wallpaper+gilman+point+of+view+rest+cure+analysis>
   (Use for: Lesson 03; prefer videos that discuss understatement
   and the narrator's reliability carefully, without diagnosing
   the narrator.)
4. **Search link (YouTube):** Langston Hughes and the Harlem
   Renaissance — "The Negro Speaks of Rivers" and "I, Too":
   <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=langston+hughes+harlem+renaissance+negro+speaks+of+rivers+i+too+analysis>

   project, not as a Modernist subset.)

## Web references

1. **Project Gutenberg — *Mosses from an Old Manse and Other
   Stories*, by Nathaniel Hawthorne (EBook #512).** Checked
   2026-10-27. The collection contents include "Young Goodman
   Brown" (first published 1835):
   <https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/512>.
   (Use for: Lesson 02 reading; the unit's Hawthorne quotations
   were checked against this text.)
2. **Project Gutenberg — *The Yellow Wallpaper*, by Charlotte
   Perkins Gilman (EBook #1952).** Checked 2026-10-27. First
   published 1892:
   <https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1952>.
   (Use for: Lesson 03 reading; the unit's Gilman quotations
   were checked against this text.)
3. **Project Gutenberg — *The Awakening and Selected Short
   Stories*, by Kate Chopin (EBook #160).** Checked 2026-10-27.
   *The Awakening* first published 1899; the volume also contains
   "Désirée's Baby" and other stories:
   <https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/160>.
   (Use for: Lesson 03 reading; the unit's Chopin quotations
   were checked against this text.)
4. **Poetry Foundation — Langston Hughes (poet page and poems).**
   Checked 2026-10-27. The Foundation's event and poet materials
   confirm "The Negro Speaks of Rivers" was published in June
   1921 and Hughes's central place in the Harlem Renaissance;
   use the site's search for the two poems and preview the
   pages before assigning. Start at the Poetry Foundation's
   Hughes materials via its poet search (run and preview):
   <https://www.google.com/search?q=site+poetryfoundation.org+Langston+Hughes+poems>.
   (Use for: Lesson 04 reading; confirm every quotation against
   the page used.)
5. **Academy of American Poets (poets.org) — Langston Hughes.**
   Checked 2026-10-27. Biographical and poem materials for
   Hughes, including Harlem Renaissance context; run and preview
   before assigning:
   <https://www.google.com/search?q=site+poets.org+Langston+Hughes>.
   (Use for: Lesson 04 background and alternate poem copies;
   cross-check quotations against reference 4.)
6. **Toronto Metropolitan University Pressbooks — *Poetry and
   Poetics*, Langston Hughes chapter.** Checked 2026-10-27.
   An open educational edition that reproduces early Hughes
   poems with source notes stating that "The Negro Speaks of
   Rivers," "I, Too," and other early poems are in the public
   domain as retrieved from Poetry Foundation:
   <https://pressbooks.library.torontomu.ca/poetryandpoetics/chapter/langston-hughes/>.
   (Use for: Lesson 04 reading when a stable open text is
   needed; the adult confirms the edition before quoting.)
7. **Library of Congress — classroom materials and American
   literature collections.** Checked 2026-10-27. Authoritative
   background on American literary periods and primary-source
   sets; run and preview before assigning:
   <https://www.google.com/search?q=library+of+congress+american+literature+harlem+renaissance+classroom+materials>.
   (Use for: Lessons 01 and 04 extension and adult background;
   verify the edition before quoting from it.)
