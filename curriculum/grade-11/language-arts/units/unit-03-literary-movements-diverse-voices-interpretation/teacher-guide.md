# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Literary Movements, Diverse Voices, and Interpretation

This guide is for the guiding adult only. Learner pages never
contain answers; keep this file and `answer-key.md` separate
from the learner's materials.

## Pacing and session notes

The unit runs 20 sessions of about 50 minutes (see the unit
README's pacing table). The five lessons take 8–10 sessions;
workshops, the seminar, drafting, project work, review, quiz,
assessment, and presentations fill the rest. Do not compress
the unit into the lessons alone: the workshops are where
classification, auditing, and comparison become habits.

- **S1–S3 (Lesson 01 + Workshop A).** Check the movement field
  guide before Lesson 02. Learners who cannot yet write a
  four-part classification need S3 repeated, not skipped.
- **S4–S6 (Lesson 02 + Workshop B).** The ambiguity audit is
  the unit's hardest thinking. Expect most learners to put
  too much in "Proved" on the first try; the rule (no quotation,
  no proof) does the teaching.
- **S7–S9 (Lesson 03 + Workshop C).** Preview Gilman's story
  yourself first (see the sensitive-content note in the unit
  README). The point-of-view rewrite (guided practice 2) is the
  best diagnostic in the unit: a learner who can write John's
  version understands centering.
- **S10–S12 (Lesson 04 + seminar).** The seminar question —
  "Do movements help us read, or get in the way?" — has no
  correct answer. Score participation on evidence use (SL.11-12.1),
  not on the position taken.
- **S13–S14 (Lesson 05).** Keep the illustration's caption
  visible during discussion: it is an AI-generated original,
  not historical evidence. Correct any learner who cites the
  image as what "really" happened in the story.
- **S15–S20.** Drafting studio, project workshop, review, quiz,
  assessment, presentations. The quiz (S18) is formative: use
  its misses to plan S19 preparation, not to punish.

## Misconceptions and reteaching

| Misconception | What to say / do |
|---|---|
| Date = movement ("1835, so Romantic") | Ask for a trait and a quotation. If neither exists, the classification fails however correct the date. Re-teach with Lesson 01's worked examples. |
| Subject = movement ("a forest, so Romantic") | Ask what the passage *does* with the forest. Re-teach with the two-walks example (Lesson 01). |
| Ambiguity = confusion / author failure | Re-teach with the ribbon: a symbol that means two things at once is a choice, and the story's ending depends on it. Use Workshop B. |
| Unreliable = lying / worthless | Distinguish: an unreliable narrator's perceptions are compromised, not necessarily false. Gilman's narrator perceives real things (dismissal, confinement) that the doctors miss. |
| Irony = "saying the opposite" always | Teach the family: verbal irony, dramatic irony, situational irony, understatement. Gilman's "of course" is understatement, not opposite-saying. |
| Harlem Renaissance = Modernism subset | Teach the distinct project: centrality of Black voice, audience, and argument — in dialogue with Modernism, not inside it. Use Lesson 04's comparison drill. |
| Version change = unfaithfulness | Ask the four questions. A change that serves a true tension in the source is interpretation; a change that invents without support is the problem. |
| Illustration = evidence about the story | Restate the caption every time the image is used. The image is an interpretation to evaluate, never a source to quote. |

## Differentiation

- **Reading access:** LibriVox or adult-read audio for
  "Young Goodman Brown" and "The Yellow Wallpaper"; chunked
  reading with guided annotation for *The Awakening* excerpts;
  large-print or text-to-speech rendering of public-domain texts.
- **Writing access:** Speech-to-text for drafting; graphic
  organizers (fingerprint table, three-column audit, perspective
  map) for every essay; sentence starters from the lessons'
  model answers for learners who stall at the first sentence.
- **Discussion access:** Written discussion-board alternatives
  to the live seminar; extended time on the quiz and assessment;
  the text-only alternative for the illustration study.
- **Extensions:** A fourth anthology headnote; a comparison with
  a writer outside the unit (Hurston, Cullen, Toomer); a
  four-question evaluation of an adult-selected recorded reading
  (Lesson 05 extension).

## Readiness, reteaching, and follow-up

- **Ready for Unit 04** if the learner can: fingerprint all five
  movements without notes; write a four-part classification;
  distinguish stated from meant in at least one passage; and
  evaluate a version with the four questions. The S17 review
  checks exactly these.
- **Reteach** movement classification with Lesson 01's guided
  practice if the quiz's question 3 or assessment Part A is
  weak; reteach point of view with Lesson 03's two-column habit
  if quiz question 4 or assessment Part B is weak; reteach
  version evaluation with Lesson 05's four questions if quiz
  question 9 or assessment Part C is weak.
- **Follow-up for Unit 04** (language, rhetoric, syntax, stylistic
  analysis): this unit's vocabulary work (word choice, syntax for
  effect in Hughes and Gilman) is the bridge — note which
  learners already comment on syntax, and which only on content.

## Sensitive content and source notes

- Gilman and Chopin: see the unit README's notes. Keep
  discussion literary and historical, without graphic detail.
  Pause and substitute practice passages if the learner is
  distressed; no assignment requires dwelling on a distressing
  scene.
- Quotations: check every learner quotation against the
  editions in the unit README (Gutenberg #512, #1952, #160;
  Hughes via the Resource Pack's linked editions). Hughes
  quotations in the lessons are brief lines from the 1921/1926
  poems; the adult confirms them against the linked edition
  before the assessment.
- RL.11-12.7's Shakespeare/American-dramatist clause is met in
  Unit 08, not here; do not tell the learner this unit alone
  satisfies every clause of the standard.
