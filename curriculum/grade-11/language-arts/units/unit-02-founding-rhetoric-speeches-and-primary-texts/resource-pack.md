# Resource Pack — Unit 02: Founding Rhetoric: Speeches and Primary Texts

**Goal:** Give a grade-11 learner (and guiding adult) free, no-account
routes to every core objective: the rhetoric toolkit, the Declaration's
structure, Madison's faction argument, Lincoln's Second Inaugural, and
the Bill of Rights as rhetoric. **Age fit:** high-school grade 11; the
adult previews every link for advertising, comments, and suitability
before the learner opens it. Checked 2026-10-21 unless noted.
"Verified" = the source page's text and publisher were confirmed on
that date; the adult opens each page before assigning and confirms
quotations against the edition used. "Search link" = a clearly labeled
preferred-channel search the adult runs and previews (no invented
titles, runtimes, or captions).

## Focused queries

1. `Declaration of Independence transcription National Archives four parts preamble grievances`
2. `Federalist No. 10 Madison faction extended republic summary analysis`
3. `Lincoln Second Inaugural Address malice toward none charity for all analysis`
4. `Bill of Rights transcription National Archives first ten amendments ratified 1791`
5. `ethos pathos logos parallelism antithesis rhetorical analysis high school`

## Web references

1. **National Archives — Declaration of Independence: A Transcription.**
   Checked 2026-10-21. Transcription of the Stone Engraving; original
   spelling and punctuation:
   <https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/declaration-transcript>.
   (Use for: all Declaration reading; the unit's quotations were
   checked against this text.)
2. **National Archives — The Bill of Rights: A Transcription.**
   Checked 2026-10-21. First ten amendments in original form, ratified
   December 15, 1791:
   <https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/bill-of-rights-transcript>.
   (Use for: Lesson 05 reading; the unit's Amendment I and IV
   quotations were checked against this text.)
3. **National Park Service — Lincoln's Second Inaugural Address.**
   Checked 2026-10-21. Full text with the March 4, 1865 occasion and
   the note that the speech is engraved at the Lincoln Memorial:
   <https://www.nps.gov/linc/learn/historyculture/lincoln-second-inaugural.htm>.
   (Use for: Lesson 04 reading; the unit's Lincoln quotations were
   checked against this text.)
4. **National Constitution Center — Second Inaugural Address (1865).**
   Checked 2026-10-21. Full text with historical summary of the 1864
   election and Reconstruction context:
   <https://constitutioncenter.org/the-constitution/historic-document-library/detail/abraham-lincoln-second-inaugural-address-1865>.
   (Use for: Lesson 04 background; cross-check quotations against
   reference 3.)
5. **Bill of Rights Institute — Handout A: Federalist No. 10, by James
   Madison (1787).** Checked 2026-10-21. Full handout text including
   the faction definition and the air-and-fire passage:
   <https://billofrightsinstitute.org/activities/handout-a-federalist-no-10-by-james-madison-1787-civil-discourse/>.
   (Use for: Lesson 03 reading; the unit's Madison quotations match
   this text.)
6. **Teaching American History — Declaration of Independence.**
   Checked 2026-10-21. Full text sourced to the National Archives:
   <https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/declaration-of-independence-2/>.
   (Use for: Lesson 02 alternate reading copy; cross-check against
   reference 1.)
7. **Search link (web):** Library of Congress — Federalist Papers
   collection and founding-documents classroom materials. Run and
   preview before assigning:
   <https://www.google.com/search?q=library+of+congress+federalist+papers+madison+no+10>.
   (Use for: Lesson 03 extension and adult background; verify the
   edition before quoting from it.)

Every core objective has a free, no-account route: objective 1 via
videos 1–4 (adult-previewed) and the lessons' own worked examples;
objectives 2–5 via references 1–6, all free with no account required.
No core objective depends on a video: every video supplements the
lessons' explicit teaching and the primary texts themselves.
