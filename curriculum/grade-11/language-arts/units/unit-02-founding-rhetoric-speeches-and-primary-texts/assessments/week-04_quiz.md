# Formative Quiz — Unit 02: Founding Rhetoric (Week 4)

**Grade 11 · Language Arts · Session S18 · ~30 minutes · closed notes
except the anchor texts**
Answer in the notebook. Each question names its objective. The key is in
`../answer-key.md` (§Q) — do not open it until the adult scores the quiz.

1. **(Objective 1)** Define *ethos, pathos,* and *logos* in one line
   each, and give one example of each from any founding text studied
   in this unit, quoted briefly.
2. **(Objective 1)** Name the device in each phrase and say in one
   sentence what it does: (a) "Enemies in War, in Peace Friends";
   (b) "He has refused his Assent… He has forbidden his Governors…
   He has dissolved Representative Houses…".
3. **(Objective 2)** List the Declaration's four parts in order, with
   one quoted phrase for each part.
4. **(Objective 2)** In one paragraph (4–5 sentences), explain what
   "consent of the governed" claims — and name one thing the phrase
   leaves undefined.
5. **(Objective 3)** Quote or closely paraphrase Madison's definition
   of faction, keeping both the majority/minority point and the
   "adverse to" test. Then name, in one sentence each, the two cures
   Madison rejects.
6. **(Objective 3)** Why does Madison prefer controlling faction's
   effects to removing its causes? Answer in 3–4 sentences using the
   air-and-fire analogy in your own words (do not quote it at length).
7. **(Objective 4)** Quote Lincoln's closing clause ("With malice
   toward none; with charity for all") and explain in 3–4 sentences how
   it follows from his reasoning about slavery as the war's cause.
8. **(Objective 4)** True or false, with a one-sentence reason:
   "Lincoln's speech blames both sides equally for slavery." (Use the
   cause/responsibility distinction.)
9. **(Objective 5)** Paraphrase Amendment I in two sentences without
   reversing its negative phrasing, and name its audience in one
   sentence.
10. **(Objective 5)** Name one mechanism difference between the
    Declaration and the Bill of Rights as ways of limiting power
    (principle vs. prohibition, or another accurate difference), and
    support it with one quotation from each text.

**Scoring:** 10 questions × 3 points = 30. Full credit needs the
quotation or paraphrase *and* the explanation. A correct label without
a quotation earns at most 1 point. Misses feed S19 preparation: the
adult re-teaches any objective missed by the learner before the unit
assessment.
