# Project — A Public Address for Today

**Unit 02 · project workshop S16; final submission with the unit
assessment (S19) · Standards: W.11-12.1, W.11-12.9.b, SL.11-12.1,
SL.11-12.3, RI.11-12.6**

## The task

You are a speaker addressing a real audience about a real civic
question — and you must argue the way the founding texts argue: with a
stated claim, delineated reasoning, fair treatment of the other side,
and a closing that commits the audience to something specific.

Choose, with the adult's approval, one civic question that matters to
your community and can be argued without partisan position-taking as
an assignment (examples: library hours, a park curfew, a school
recycling program, a town meeting rule, a local volunteer drive). The
question must be one where reasonable people disagree, so that a
counterclaim is real, not invented.

## Requirements

**1. A rhetorical map (one page, submitted first, S16).** Before any
drafting, submit:

- The question, in one sentence.
- Your claim, in one sentence.
- Your three premises, each in one sentence, with one piece of evidence
  or reasoning noted for each.
- The strongest counterclaim, stated fairly in two sentences (the
  adult checks: would a holder of that view recognize it?).
- Your concession: one sentence granting what is true in the
  counterclaim, and one sentence answering what remains.
- The device plan: which two devices from Lesson 01 you will use, and
  where.

**2. The address (600–800 words, written).** A speech text, not an
essay: it must be speakable aloud in about 5 minutes, address its
audience directly at least twice, and include:

- An opening that states the occasion and the claim (Lesson 01's
  preamble move).
- A reasoning section that delineates premises in order (Lesson 03's
  premise-map move).
- A counterclaim paragraph that develops the other side fairly before
  answering it (Lesson 02's concession move).
- A closing, modeled on Lincoln's close rather than copied from it:
  an antithesis or parallelism that turns the argument into a program
  of action (Lesson 04's close move).
- At least three accurately quoted or clearly paraphrased pieces of
  evidence, cited in the Resource Pack's format (no invented
  statistics; if a number is used, its source is named in a footnote).

**3. A delivery (spoken, about 5 minutes).** Deliver the address to the
adult (live or recorded). The adult scores delivery with the teacher
guide's speaking checklist: audible and paced, claim stated early,
evidence cited aloud, counterclaim treated fairly in tone, closing
delivered as a program rather than trailed off.

**4. A reflection (200–300 words).** Which founding text most shaped
your address, and how — quote one line from that text and one line from
your own address, and explain the borrowing (structure, device, or
reasoning move). Name one decision you reversed while drafting and why.

## Materials

Notebook, the unit's anchor texts, the rhetorical map template above,
and the Resource Pack's evidence-finding references for any factual
claims. No invented quotations, statistics, or endorsements: every
factual claim in the address carries a source the adult has checked.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Full credit (4) | Partial (2–3) | Missing/weak (0–1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claim and premises | Claim precise; 3 premises delineated in order, each supported | Claim present but vague, or a premise unsupported | No clear claim, or premises asserted without support |
| Evidence | 3+ cited pieces, accurate, relevant, no invented facts | 1–2 pieces, or a citation missing/unclear | No evidence, or invented statistics/quotations |
| Counterclaim | Strongest counterclaim stated fairly, conceded in part, answered | Counterclaim weak (straw man) or answered without concession | No counterclaim, or opponent caricatured |
| Devices and style | 2 devices used deliberately and named in the map; formal, speakable register | Devices present but decorative, or register uneven | No identifiable devices, or informal/unprepared register |
| Closing | Antithesis or parallelism turns argument into a specific program of action | Closing summarizes without committing audience to action | No closing, or closing contradicts the argument |
| Delivery | Audible, paced, claim early, evidence cited aloud, fair tone on counterclaim | Uneven pace/volume, or counterclaim delivered mockingly | Inaudible, unread, or not delivered |
| Reflection | Names a founding text, quotes both texts, explains the borrowing, names a reversed decision | One element missing (quotation, explanation, or reversal) | No reflection, or borrowing unexplained |

Score: 24–28 = strong; 17–23 = proficient with revision notes; below 17
= revise and resubmit the weakest two criteria. The adult records which
criteria were revised; the grade is never recorded in this repository.
