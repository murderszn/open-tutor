# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Language, Rhetoric, Syntax, and Stylistic Analysis

**Grade 11 · Language Arts · for the guiding adult · companion to the
unit README's 20-session pacing**

This unit is the year's precision instrument: Units 01–03 taught the
learner what texts argue and mean; this unit teaches how sentences and
words argue. Everything later (criticism in U05, public reasoning in
U06, research in U07) assumes the learner can name a stylistic means
and state its effect. If the name → effect habit is shaky at S17, slow
down — U05's criticism essays will punish gaps.

## Session-by-session notes

- **S1–S2 (Lesson 01):** Teach main-clause position before any
  labeling, or learners will sort by length instead of structure.
  The sentence-strip task (S2) works best with real paper strips;
  the illustration is the model, not a substitute for handling them.
- **S3 (Workshop A):** The combining items are the lesson. Check the
  effect sentence on every item, not just the rewritten sentence — a
  correct periodic sentence with no effect statement is half an
  answer.
- **S4–S5 (Lesson 02):** Do the *stubborn / resolute / obstinate* set
  aloud as a class before the pairs work; learners who have never
  felt connotation shift will not find it alone. The "tabled" drill
  is the unit's best ambiguity lesson — do not rush it.
- **S6 (Workshop B):** Sorting is not the point — the one-line reasons
  are. A sorted list without reasons earns no credit in the key.
- **S7–S8 (Lesson 03):** Teach the three steps (name → mapping →
  judgment) explicitly and require step 3 on every item. The applied
  task's partner check is the real teaching: a figure that is misread
  by a partner needs revision, not defense.
- **S9 (Workshop C):** The tone revisions are harder than they look.
  Accept any revision whose changed words genuinely move the tone;
  reject revisions that change facts instead of diction.
- **S10–S11 (Lesson 04):** Run one dispute together through all four
  routine steps before releasing pairs. The routine is the content —
  learners who skip to a verdict have missed the lesson even when the
  verdict is right.
- **S12 (Seminar):** Question: "Is singular *they* correct in formal
  prose?" Your job is to keep every claim tied to a reference. Taste
  claims ("it sounds wrong") are ruled out of order; guide citations
  are the only admissible evidence.
- **S13–S14 (Lesson 05):** Approve thesis paragraphs before S15
  drafting begins. A vague thesis approved now becomes a vague essay
  later; send weak theses back with the thesis test, not with a
  rewrite of your own.
- **S15 (Drafting studio):** Require the counter-reading paragraph in
  the draft. Learners omit it when unsupervised, and an essay without
  it cannot score above proficient.
- **S16 (Project workshop):** Approve passage choices and revision
  targets. Veto passages longer than 300 words and paragraphs with
  nothing to revise — both waste the workshop.
- **S17 (Review):** Retrieval without notes: all unit terms defined,
  one structure and one word choice applied to an unseen sentence.
  Re-teach from the missed items only; do not re-teach the whole unit.
- **S18 (Quiz):** Scored; misses feed S19 preparation, not punishment.
  If more than half the class misses any one item, re-teach that item
  in S19's first 10 minutes.
- **S19 (Assessment):** Part A first (30 minutes, timed), then Part B
  begun in session and finished as homework if needed. Collect Part A
  before Part B starts so the essay cannot borrow from it.
- **S20 (Reflection + presentations):** Read the strongest project
  aloud (with permission). Preview U05's criticism lenses as "the same
  analysis, one level up: from sentences to whole readings."

## Misconceptions and reteaching

- **Length = structure.** "Long = periodic" is the unit's most common
  error. Reteach: find the main clause; if it comes first, the
  sentence is loose however long it is. (Lesson 01 teacher note.)
- **Labeling = analysis.** "This is a metaphor" is not analysis.
  Reteach: name → mapping → judgment, and require the judgment
  sentence every time. (Lesson 03 teacher note.)
- **Connotation is in the word alone.** Reteach with *cheap* at a
  discount store vs. *cheap* applied to a gift: context and audience
  decide. (Lesson 02 teacher note.)
- **"Correct" is absolute.** Reteach with the four-step routine:
  every usage answer names a guide and an audience. (Lesson 04
  teacher note.)
- **A device list is an essay.** Reteach: the thesis is an argument
  about what devices do *together*; every body paragraph ends with a
  link-back sentence. (Lesson 05 teacher note.)

## Differentiation

- **Learners who need more support:** allow the applied tasks to be
  completed orally or by dictation; let the sentence-strip task use
  real paper strips; permit the thesis paragraph to be built as a
  labeled diagram (thesis box → quotation boxes → counter-reading
  box) before it is written as prose.
- **Learners who need extension:** the extension in each lesson
  (find a sentence in another subject's text; trace a word's history;
  analyze a figure in a song or advertisement; compare two
  dictionaries' usage notes; write the full essay early) — assign one
  per lesson rather than all at once, and use the best extensions as
  S20 readings.
- **Language access:** keep the connotation chart (Lesson 02) visible
  all unit; read every practice passage aloud twice; underline main
  clauses before labeling.

## Reteaching paths

- **Objective 1 weak (syntax):** return to Lesson 01 with five new
  sentences; drill main-clause position only, then re-test with
  practice §A items 1 and 5.
- **Objective 2 weak (deliberate variation):** return to Workshop A
  combining items; require the effect sentence for every rewrite.
- **Objective 3 weak (word choice):** return to Lesson 02's
  connotation chart; re-do practice §B items 1–2 with new pairs.
- **Objective 4 weak (usage):** return to Lesson 04's four-step
  routine; run one new dispute aloud together before independent
  work.
- **Objective 5 weak (essay):** return to Lesson 05's thesis test;
  approve a new thesis paragraph before any drafting; require the
  link-back sentence in every body paragraph.

## Answer-key use

The answer key gives worked solutions for every lesson item, practice
item, quiz question, assessment task, and project rubric application.
For open responses it gives sample reasoning and acceptable variation —
not a single "right answer." Accept any response that names the
required structure or term, quotes or describes the evidence, and
states a defensible effect; reject responses that label without
explaining, or explain without evidence.
