# Unit Assessment — Unit 04 (S19)

**Two parts · ~50 minutes · adult-scored from `../answer-key.md`**
This assessment is not standardized or validated; it checks this
unit's objectives as taught. Part A checks short analysis; Part B is
the culminating essay.

## Part A — Short analysis (30 minutes)

Answer all four items in the notebook.

1. Label each sentence's structure and state its effect in one
   sentence each:
   - (a) "Before dawn, before the first bus, before the city woke,
     the bakers had finished."
   - (b) "The bakers had finished before dawn, before the first bus,
     and before the city woke."
   - (c) "Bread. Coffee. News. The morning began."
2. For the pair *thrifty* / *stingy*: state the shared denotation,
   then explain the connotative difference and what each word would
   do to a reader's attitude toward the person described.
3. Read this original sentence (written for this unit): "The
   committee praised the plan as '**bold**,' then funded it with
   money it did not have." Does the word *bold* build or undercut the
   committee's praise, once the second clause is read? Explain in
   2–3 sentences, naming the tone the sentence creates.
4. Resolve this dispute with the four-step routine (all four steps,
   briefly): in a formal school essay, is it acceptable to write
   "There are **less** problems than last year"? Name the reference
   you would consult and give your decision with its trade-off.

## Part B — Stylistic analysis essay (the rest of the session, finished as homework if needed)

Read this unseen original passage (written for this unit) twice before
writing:

> "The hearing lasted nine minutes. Nine minutes, after nine months
> of letters, petitions, and waiting rooms. The chair read the
> decision in a voice like a weather report — flat, practiced,
> already moving on to the next item. The families in the back row
> did not protest. They did not applaud. They stood, gathered their
> coats, and left through the side door, the way people leave a room
> where someone has just died. The report called the outcome 'a
> resolution.' The families called it nothing at all. They had run out
> of words long before they ran out of patience."

Write an essay (about 3–4 pages) that:

1. States a precise thesis about how the passage's style creates
   meaning (effect + means + location).
2. Develops the thesis with at least three evidence chains (quote →
   effect → link-back), covering at least two different stylistic
   means (syntax, word choice, figure, or tone).
3. States and answers one counter-reading fairly.
4. Concludes with a judgment the evidence earned — what the stylistic
   reading reveals that a theme-only reading would miss.

**Scoring (adult).** Thesis precision (0–4); evidence chains, each
complete and linked (0–4 per chain, best three counted, max 12);
counter-reading fairness and answer (0–4); conclusion earned from
evidence (0–4); conventions and quotation accuracy (0–4). Total /28:
24–28 exemplary, 18–23 proficient, 12–17 developing, below 12 revise
and resubmit with re-teaching. Sample reasoning and acceptable
variation are in the answer key.
