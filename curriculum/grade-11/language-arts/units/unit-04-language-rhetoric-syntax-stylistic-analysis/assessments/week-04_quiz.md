# Formative Quiz — Unit 04 (S18)

**10 questions · ~25 minutes · adult-scored from `../answer-key.md`**
This quiz checks recognition and short analysis. It does not replace
the unit assessment essay. Misses feed S19 preparation.

## Questions

1. Label the structure of this sentence and state its effect in one
   sentence: "When the votes were counted, when the hall had emptied,
   and when the rain began, the chair announced the result."
2. Label the structure of this sentence and state its effect in one
   sentence: "The chair announced the result, after the votes were
   counted, after the hall had emptied, and after the rain began."
3. What is the difference between denotation and connotation? Give
   one original example pair (two words, same denotation, different
   connotation) and state the difference.
4. "The committee tabled the motion." State both readings of
   *tabled* and name the single piece of information that would
   settle which reading is correct.
5. Name the figure in this sentence and judge whether it builds or
   undercuts the claim, with a reason: "The new stadium is a **crown
   jewel** for the city, funded by a tax nobody voted for."
6. A writer calls a policy "a **gamble**" while arguing the policy is
   wise. Does the figure build or undercut the writer's claim?
   Explain in one sentence.
7. Resolve this dispute with the four-step routine in brief (name the
   reference type you would consult and give your decision): "Each
   student should bring **their** book" in a formal school essay
   under a traditional guide.
8. Is a split infinitive ("to boldly go") always an error? Answer yes
   or no, then give the one-sentence historical reason from Lesson 04.
9. Rewrite this weak thesis so it passes the thesis test (effect +
   means + location): "The passage uses imagery to create a mood."
10. In the anchor passage "A Letter from Mill Town," the council calls
    the closure "a transition" and the town calls it "an ending."
    In 2–3 sentences, explain what the connotative difference between
    the two words does to the passage's argument.
