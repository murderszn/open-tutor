# Project — Style Audit and Revision Portfolio

**Unit 04 · Workshop S16, presented S20 · adult-assessed**

## The task

Build a two-part portfolio that proves you can do what this unit
teaches — analyze style in someone else's prose, and control style in
your own.

### Part 1 — Style audit (about 2 pages)

Choose one passage of 150–300 words from a public-domain text (the
adult approves the choice; suggestions: a speech from the National
Archives founding-documents collection, a Douglass address, or a
passage from a novel on Project Gutenberg). Write an audit with:

1. A thesis sentence making a precise style claim (effect + means +
   location), passing the Lesson 05 thesis test.
2. Three evidence chains (one syntax, one word choice, one figure or
   tone), each following quote → effect → link-back.
3. One counter-reading, stated fairly and answered.
4. A conclusion that states what the stylistic reading reveals that a
   theme-only reading would miss.

### Part 2 — Revision portfolio (about 2 pages)

Take one paragraph of your own writing from earlier this year (Unit
02 or Unit 03 work is ideal — the adult helps you choose a paragraph
with room to improve). Submit:

1. The original paragraph, unchanged.
2. A revised paragraph in which you make **at least three deliberate
   stylistic changes**: at least one syntactic change (restructure,
   combine, or break a sentence) and at least one word-choice change
   (connotation or figure). Mark every change (underline or bold).
3. A change log: for each change, one row stating the original, the
   revision, the technique (named), and the intended effect.
4. A reflection paragraph (5–7 sentences): which change most improved
   the paragraph, which change was hardest to control, and what you
   would still revise.

## Deliverables

- Part 1 audit (2 pages, quoted passage attached or cited by source).
- Part 2 original paragraph, revised paragraph with marked changes,
  change log, and reflection.
- Both parts in final, proofread form; quotations checked against the
  source.

## Rubric

Scored by the adult. Each criterion is scored 0–4; 14–16 = exemplary,
10–13 = proficient, 6–9 = developing, below 6 = revise and resubmit.

| Criterion | 4 — Exemplary | 3 — Proficient | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Style claim** | Precise thesis: effect + means + location; genuinely arguable | Precise thesis with one element slightly vague | Thesis names a theme, not a style mechanism, or is too broad | No thesis, or a topic restated as a claim |
| **Evidence chains** | 3 chains, each quote → effect → link-back, all supporting the thesis | 3 chains, most with all three steps | 1–2 chains, or chains missing the link-back | Quotations listed without effect statements |
| **Counter-reading** | Fairly stated, specific, and answered with evidence | Fairly stated and answered, briefly | Straw man, or stated but unanswered | Missing |
| **Revision control** | 3+ marked changes, both types present, change log complete, effects match the revisions | 3 changes, both types present, log mostly complete | Fewer than 3 changes, or one type missing, or log incomplete | Changes unmarked, or log missing, or revisions worsen the paragraph |
| **Reflection** | Names the most-improved change, the hardest change, and a next revision, each with a reason | All three elements present, one thin | One or two elements missing | Summary of the task, not a reflection |

**Readiness note.** A portfolio scoring below 6 is not a failure — it
is a signal to re-teach: return to the thesis test (Lesson 05) and the
quote → effect → link-back rhythm with a single passage before
resubmitting. A portfolio scoring 14+ may be read aloud in S20 with
the learner's permission.
