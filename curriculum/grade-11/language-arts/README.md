# Grade 11 Language Arts

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are
delivered; the eight units and the review package are planned, not yet written.
See [scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained
for one grade-11 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials,
and supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades
in private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 11 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-11 language arts course following the
**American literature, rhetoric, and research** pathway: reading foundational
American works from the eighteenth through early twentieth centuries alongside
later diverse voices; studying how rhetoric works in founding documents,
speeches, and public advocacy; analyzing language, syntax, and style; writing
literary criticism and evidence-based argument; conducting an independent
research project with ethical source use; and closing with a poetry and drama
seminar plus a capstone portfolio.

Sessions run about 50 minutes, five per week: explicit lessons with modeled
analysis, close-reading workshops on original or public-domain texts (a guiding
adult selects any licensed editions through a library), writing studios with
same-day adult-reviewed drafting and revision, discussion seminars the learner
increasingly leads, and review. Anchor texts are public-domain American works —
the Declaration of Independence, Federalist No. 10, the Bill of Rights,
Frederick Douglass's "What to the Slave Is the Fourth of July?", Lincoln's
Second Inaugural Address, Whitman, Dickinson, Poe, Hawthorne, Twain, Gilman,
Chopin, Fitzgerald's *The Great Gatsby*, and early Langston Hughes poems —
with adult-selected licensed editions only where a later work is essential
(e.g., a play by an American dramatist for the drama seminar).

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Read and analyze American literary texts — including eighteenth-,
   nineteenth-, and early-twentieth-century foundational works — in the grades
   11–CCR text complexity band, citing strong and thorough textual evidence
   for explicit and inferential claims, including where a text leaves matters
   uncertain.
2. Determine two or more themes or central ideas in a text, analyze how they
   develop and interact to produce a complex account, and write an objective
   summary.
3. Analyze an author's choices — setting, plot order, character development,
   structure, and point of view (including satire, sarcasm, irony, and
   understatement) — and explain their contribution to meaning and aesthetic
   impact.
4. Analyze word choice, including figurative and connotative meanings, and
   syntax for their impact on meaning and tone; interpret figures of speech in
   context; and vary syntax deliberately for effect in the learner's own
   writing.
5. Delineate and evaluate reasoning in seminal U.S. texts — including
   constitutional principles and legal reasoning — and in works of public
   advocacy; analyze seventeenth- through nineteenth-century foundational
   U.S. documents for themes, purposes, and rhetorical features.
6. Determine an author's point of view or purpose in highly rhetorical texts,
   analyzing how style and content create power, persuasiveness, or beauty;
   and evaluate whether an exposition's or argument's structure makes its
   points clear, convincing, and engaging.
7. Integrate and evaluate multiple sources presented in different media and
   formats to address a question or solve a problem; evaluate a speaker's
   point of view, reasoning, evidence, and use of rhetoric.
8. Write arguments about substantive topics or texts using valid reasoning
   and relevant, sufficient evidence: precise claims distinguished from
   counterclaims, fair development of both sides, cohesion, formal style and
   tone, and a conclusion that follows from the argument.
9. Write informative and explanatory texts that examine complex ideas clearly
   and accurately through effective selection, organization, and analysis of
   content, using precise and domain-specific vocabulary.
10. Conduct short and sustained research projects driven by self-generated
    questions: use advanced searches, gather from multiple authoritative
    sources, assess each source's strengths and limitations, synthesize
    findings, integrate information selectively without plagiarism, and follow
    a standard citation format.
11. Draw evidence from literary and informational texts to support analysis,
    reflection, and research; write routinely across extended and short time
    frames for a range of tasks, purposes, and audiences.
12. Initiate and participate effectively in collaborative discussions and
    seminars — prepared, evidence-based, and civil — and present findings with
    a clear perspective and strategic use of media, adapting to formal
    contexts; demonstrate command of standard English grammar, usage,
    mechanics, and college-ready vocabulary.

## Standards basis

Objectives are crosswalked to the Common Core State Standards for English
Language Arts, grades 11–12 band (Reading: Literature, Reading: Informational
Text, Writing, Speaking & Listening, Language). Standard codes and descriptions
were verified against the official standards pages at
<https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/> on 2026-10-07; the
scope-and-sequence paraphrases the expectations and cites exact codes. This is
a reference alignment for planning — it is not a claim of state adoption,
accreditation, or standards certification, and no state or district
requirements were specified.

## Planned units

- **U01** — American literature contexts, themes, and close reading
- **U02** — Founding rhetoric: speeches and primary texts
- **U03** — Literary movements, diverse voices, and interpretation
- **U04** — Language, rhetoric, syntax, and stylistic analysis
- **U05** — Literary criticism and analytical writing
- **U06** — Argument, evidence, synthesis, and public reasoning
- **U07** — Independent research, documentation, and ethical source use
- **U08** — Poetry, drama, seminar, and capstone portfolio
- **R00** — Diagnostic, midyear and final review, cumulative assessment, and
  keys (delivered last)

Units are planned in prerequisite order: close reading and historical context
before founding rhetoric; movements and interpretation before criticism;
analytical writing before public argument; argument before independent
research; everything before the capstone seminar and portfolio.

## For the guiding adult

- **Pacing:** eight four-week units (20 sessions each) plus four flexible
  weeks — diagnostic placement, catch-up, midyear review, final review — for
  about 36 weeks. The scope-and-sequence shows weekly goals and how lessons,
  reading, writing studios, seminars, and review fill each unit.
- **Entry skills:** the learner should arrive able to cite textual evidence,
  analyze theme and structure, and write organized argument and exposition
  (the grade-10 track's end-of-year objectives); the diagnostic week verifies
  these and the track re-teaches insecure skills in use. This track's new
  content is independence at the high end of the complexity band, foundational
  American works, seminal-document reasoning, advanced rhetoric and syntax,
  sustained research ethics, and seminar leadership.
- **Texts:** prefer public-domain editions (Project Gutenberg, Library of
  Congress, Founders Online). Any licensed work comes through the learner's
  library; never reproduce copyrighted texts in the repo.
- **Research supervision:** preview every source the learner will use;
  supervise all internet searching; keep the learner's name and identifying
  details out of research products and correspondence.
- **Supports:** audiobook alternatives (e.g., LibriVox) for long texts,
  chunked reading with guided annotation, speech-to-text for drafting,
  graphic organizers for argument and research, extended time, and written
  discussion alternatives to live seminars. Extension: additional primary
  sources, comparative criticism, and publication-style revision.
- **Assessment:** each unit has a formative quiz and a culminating
  assessment; separate teacher guides and answer keys ship with each unit.
  The R00 package adds the diagnostic, midyear/final reviews, and cumulative
  assessment.
