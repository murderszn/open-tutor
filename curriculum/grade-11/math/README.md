# Grade 11 Mathematics

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are
delivered; the eight units are planned, not yet written. See
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained
for one grade-11 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials,
and supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades
in private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 11 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-11 math course following the **Algebra II**
pathway: function transformations, composition, and inverses; polynomial
functions, zeros, and the complex number system; rational expressions and
functions with asymptotes; radical functions and equations with extraneous-solution
analysis; exponential and logarithmic functions and models; trigonometric
functions and periodic models built on the unit circle; sequences, series, and
financial models; and probability, statistical inference, and modeling.
Sessions are about 50 minutes, five per week, blending paper-and-pencil
symbolic work, Desmos graphing and table technology, real-dataset modeling
(exponential growth, periodic phenomena, survey data), structured written
justification of every solution step, and independent practice that the adult
reviews the same day.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Predict and verify the effect on a graph of replacing f(x) by
   f(x) + k, kf(x), f(kx), and f(x + k) for positive and negative k;
   recognize even and odd functions from graphs and expressions.
2. Compose functions from context and decompose composite functions; solve
   f(x) = c and write the inverse of a simple invertible function; interpret
   an inverse's meaning in a real situation.
3. Factor polynomials of degree 3 and 4; apply the Remainder and Factor
   Theorems; find all zeros including complex zeros written as a ± bi; graph
   polynomial functions showing zeros, end behavior, and turning-point
   counts.
4. Add, subtract, multiply, and divide rational expressions; rewrite a(x)/b(x)
   as q(x) + r(x)/b(x); graph rational functions showing zeros, vertical,
   horizontal, and slant asymptotes, and end behavior.
5. Solve rational and radical equations in one variable; detect and explain
   extraneous solutions; graph square-root and cube-root functions with
   transformations and state their domains and ranges.
6. Convert between exponential and logarithmic forms; apply logarithm
   properties; solve exponential equations of the form ab^(ct) = d and
   logarithmic equations; fit exponential models to data and interpret rate
   parameters in context.
7. Convert between degrees and radians; use the unit circle to evaluate sine,
   cosine, and tangent for standard and non-standard angles; prove and apply
   the Pythagorean identity.
8. Choose sine or cosine models for periodic phenomena with specified
   amplitude, period or frequency, and midline; solve applied periodic
   problems and interpret each parameter in context.
9. Write arithmetic and geometric sequences recursively and explicitly;
   translate between forms; derive and use the finite geometric series sum;
   evaluate savings, loan, and payment models with compound interest.
10. Distinguish sample surveys, experiments, and observational studies; use
    randomization and simulation to judge whether a model fits observed
    results, estimate a margin of error, and decide whether a treatment
    difference is significant.
11. Summarize single-variable data with plots matched to the question and
    center/spread measures matched to shape; fit normal models where
    appropriate; fit linear, quadratic, and exponential functions to bivariate
    data and interpret slope, intercept, and the correlation coefficient.
12. Compute conditional probabilities and test independence with two-way
    frequency tables; apply the addition and multiplication rules; use
    permutations and combinations in counting-based probability.
13. Build, compare, and select function models (polynomial, rational,
    radical, exponential, logarithmic, trigonometric) for a context; justify
    the choice with rate-of-change and residual reasoning in writing.
14. Use units to understand and guide multi-step solutions; define
    appropriate quantities for descriptive modeling; report results with
    accuracy appropriate to measurement limits.

## For the guiding adult

Grade-11 math turns grade-10's geometric and coordinate fluency — and
grade-9's symbolic fluency — into function-family reasoning: comparing
polynomial, rational, radical, exponential, logarithmic, and trigonometric
models, choosing among them with evidence, and justifying every solution step
in writing. Learners enter expected to solve linear and quadratic equations,
factor, use function notation and the quadratic formula, graph on the
coordinate plane, work with rational exponents and radicals, solve right
triangles with trigonometric ratios, and write a clear worked solution with a
justification at each step (the grade-9 track's objectives in unmerged draft
PR #106 and the grade-10 track's in unmerged draft PR #114).

This track introduces genuinely new territory: complex numbers, rational
functions and asymptotes, logarithm properties, the unit circle and radian
measure, statistical inference with simulation, and conditional probability.
Complex-number work stays algebraic (adding, subtracting, multiplying,
a ± bi solutions); the advanced (+) standards — conjugates and quotients,
the Binomial Theorem, inverse trigonometric functions, matrix methods — are
scheduled only as optional enrichment. Financial modeling (U07) uses
educational examples only, never investment advice; the adult verifies any
rates or dates before use.
