# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Exponential and Logarithmic Functions and Models

**For the guiding adult.** This guide is kept separate from student
materials. It states what "ready to move on" looks like, the
misconceptions to watch for, and what to re-teach when evidence
shows a gap. Full worked answers are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## Session-by-session guidance

- **S1–S2 (Lesson 01, Set A):** insist on the sentence "base 0.92
  means 92% remaining, 8% lost" before any evaluation. If a learner
  draws a decay graph crossing `y = 0`, stop and re-teach the
  asymptote: `b^x > 0` always, so the graph can approach but never
  reach or cross it.
- **S3–S4 (Lesson 02, Set B):** require both rows — differences
  *and* ratios — under every table before a family is named. The
  lily-pond task (Lesson 01) is the Session 4 discussion anchor:
  "half the time" is not "half the coverage" for exponential growth.
- **S5–S6 (Lesson 03, Set C):** the conversion sentence ("base to
  what power equals the argument?") must be written out for the
  first ten logarithms, not skipped once the learner "gets it."
  The reflection table (Lesson 03) should be drawn on paper, not
  just read — the domain/range swap is what makes Lesson 06's
  asymptote work intelligible.
- **S7–S8 (Lesson 04, Set D):** the sum-property error is the unit's
  most durable misconception. Session 8's error-analysis task is
  mandatory, not optional: the learner must *produce* a numerical
  counterexample, not just nod at one.
- **S9–S10 (Lesson 05, Set E):** the first line of every logarithmic
  equation solution must be "Domain: ___". A solution checked only
  in the combined equation is unfinished. Keep logarithms exact
  until the final step; mid-solution rounding is the most common
  cause of failed checks.
- **S11–S12 (Lesson 06, Set F):** for every log graph, the order is
  domain, asymptote, intercept — in that order, every time. For
  modeling, refuse any prediction reported without a limit sentence.
- **S13 (Reading):** the learner restates Paul's Online Notes'
  exponential and logarithm sections in their own words and applies
  the inverse relationship to one new function. Copying the
  reference verbatim does not meet the session's success criterion.
- **S14–S15 (Project):** approve a dataset's family only from its
  signature table. Reject any real account balance, prescription,
  or personal measurement substituted for the fictional data, and
  reject any memo that presents a prediction as certain.
- **S16–S20 (Workshop, quiz, assessment, gallery, flex):** see the
  reteach triggers below and the scoring guides in the quiz and
  assessment files.

## Readiness criteria (what "secure" means)

The learner is ready for Unit 06 when they can, without prompting:

1. Read `a`, `b`, and the percent rate off `a·b^x`, and sketch the
   graph with intercept, asymptote, and end behavior.
2. Classify a table by differences, second differences, and ratios,
   and explain why exponential growth eventually outruns linear and
   quadratic growth.
3. Convert between exponential and logarithmic forms in both
   directions, and evaluate base-2, base-10, and base-`e` logarithms
   exactly where possible.
4. Expand and condense with the three properties, and refuse the
   sum property with a counterexample.
5. Solve `ab^(ct) = d` exactly (as a logarithm) and approximately,
   and solve logarithmic equations with domain-first checking that
   rejects extraneous solutions by name.
6. Fit an exponential model, report residuals, and state a limit on
   any prediction outside the observed range.

## Reteach triggers

- **Base read as percent:** return to Lesson 01's "remaining vs.
  lost" language; drill `1.12 → +12%`, `0.86 → −14%`, `2 → +100%`.
- **Logarithm treated as division:** return to Lesson 03's
  definition sentence; evaluate five logarithms by "base to what
  power?" before any property work.
- **Sum-property error persists:** do not re-explain — require the
  learner to generate three numerical counterexamples of their own.
- **Extraneous solutions kept:** require the domain line and the
  original-equation check as separately scored steps for five
  consecutive equations.
- **Model reported without limits:** return the project memo
  ungraded until every prediction carries a limit sentence; the
  habit is the objective, not the polish.
- **Average rate of change without dividing by interval length:**
  re-teach it as "slope of the secant line" with a graph, not as a
  difference.

## What to do with the midyear position

This unit follows the midyear review (Unit 04, Week 18). If the
midyear check showed weak factoring or radical-domain habits, those
resurface here as exponent-property errors and domain errors; repair
them inside this unit's sessions rather than pausing the unit —
Lesson 03's domain work and Lesson 05's checking habit are the
repair vehicles.
