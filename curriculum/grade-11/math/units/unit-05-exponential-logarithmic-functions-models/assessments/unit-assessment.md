# Culminating Assessment — Unit 05: Exponential and Logarithmic Functions and Models

**For the learner.** 14 items, about 50 minutes, calculator allowed
for approximations only. Show every step: a bare answer earns partial
credit at most, because justification is part of every objective.
Full solutions and scoring guidance are in
[../answer-key.md](../answer-key.md), for the guiding adult only.

## Part A — Functions and graphs (Lessons 01, 03, 06)

1. A rumor spreads so that the number of people who have heard it
   follows `R(t) = 50 · 2^t`, where `t` is days. State the initial
   value, the percent growth rate, the horizontal asymptote, and the
   range. Evaluate `R(4)`.
2. Sketch `y = 3 · (1/2)^x`. Label the y-intercept and the
   horizontal asymptote, and state the end behavior as `x → ∞`
   and as `x → −∞`.
3. For `f(x) = log_2(x − 2) − 1`, state the domain, the vertical
   asymptote, and the x-intercept. (Show the intercept computation.)
4. Explain, using the reflection idea from Lesson 03, why the domain
   of `y = log_2(x)` is `x > 0` while the range of `y = 2^x` is
   `y > 0`. (Two to three sentences.)

## Part B — Logarithms and properties (Lessons 03–04)

5. Evaluate without a calculator: `log_7(49)`, `log_10(0.001)`,
   `log_5(1)`, `log_2(√8)`.
6. Expand `log(4x³ / y)` as far as possible, stating the domain.
7. Condense `log_2(x) + 2·log_2(y) − log_2(z)` to a single logarithm.
8. Use change of base to evaluate `log_3(150)` to two decimal
   places, and verify by raising 3 to your answer.

## Part C — Equations (Lesson 05)

9. Solve `80 · (1.05)^t = 200`. Give the exact answer and an
   approximation to one decimal place.
10. Solve `log_4(x) + log_4(x − 6) = 2`. State the domain first,
    check every candidate, and name any extraneous solution.
11. Solve `500·e^(0.04t) = 1500`. Give the exact answer and an
    approximation to two decimal places.

## Part D — Comparing and modeling (Lessons 02, 06)

12. Classify each table and give its model:
    a. `x = 0, 1, 2, 3` → `y = 10, 14, 18, 22`.
    b. `x = 0, 1, 2, 3` → `y = 10, 30, 90, 270`.
    c. `x = 0, 1, 2, 3` → `y = 10, 11, 14, 19`.
13. A town's population (fictional practice data) is 8,000 in year 0
    and grows 2.5% per year. Write the model, predict the population
    in year 12 to the nearest whole person, and write one limit
    sentence for that prediction.
14. **Justification task.** Two learners argue about a dataset with
    outputs 100, 150, 225, 338 at `t = 0, 1, 2, 3`. One says "linear,
    because it goes up by about 50, then 75, then 113 — steady
    growth." The other says "exponential, factor about 1.5." Settle
    the argument: compute the differences and the ratios, name the
    correct family, write the model, compute the residual at `t = 3`
    for that model, and state one limit on predicting `t = 8` from
    four points.

## Scoring guide (for the guiding adult)

Total 100 points: Part A 24 (items 1–4: 6 each), Part B 28 (items 5–8:
7 each), Part C 24 (items 9–11: 8 each), Part D 24 (items 12–14: 6, 6,
12). A solution that omits a domain statement, a check, or a limit
sentence where the item asks for one loses at least 2 points on that
item, even if the number is right — those habits are the objectives.
80+ with no part below 60% of its points: secure for Unit 06.
Otherwise, reteach by part using the teacher guide's triggers before
Unit 06's periodic modeling, where inverse reasoning returns.
