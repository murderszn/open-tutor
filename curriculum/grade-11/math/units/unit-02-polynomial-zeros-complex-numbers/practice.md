# Practice Sets — Unit 02

Complete one set per practice session (S2, S4, S7, S12, S14). Show
every step; the adult scores same-day against `answer-key.md`.
"Verify" items are finished only when the check is written out.

## Practice Set A — Complex arithmetic (Session S2, after Lesson 01)

1. Write each in standard `a + bi` form: (a) `√(−121)`; (b) `4 −
   √(−36)`; (c) `√(−7)`.
2. Add or subtract: (a) `(7 − 3i) + (−2 + 8i)`; (b) `(5 + i) − (9 −
   4i)`; (c) `(−6 − 2i) − (−6 − 2i)`.
3. Multiply and write in standard form: (a) `(2 + 5i)(3 − i)`; (b)
   `4i(3 + 2i)`; (c) `(1 − 6i)²`.
4. Simplify: (a) `i⁴¹`; (b) `i⁹⁸`; (c) `i²⁰²⁶`.
5. Find the mistake: `(3 − 2i)(1 + 4i) = 3 + 12i − 2i − 8i² = 3 + 10i
   − 8i²`. Finish correctly and name the skipped step.
6. Compute `(a + bi) + (a − bi)` and `(a + bi) − (a − bi)` in terms of
   `a` and `b`. What do the results tell you about conjugates?
7. **Explain:** Why must square roots of negative numbers be converted
   to `bi` form *before* multiplying them? Give the `√(−4)·√(−9)`
   trap as your example.

## Practice Set B — Quadratics and conjugates (Session S4, after Lesson 02)

1. Solve by completing the square: `x² + 10x + 41 = 0`. Verify one
   solution by substitution.
2. Use the quadratic formula: `2x² − 6x + 9 = 0`. Write the answer in
   lowest `a ± bi` form.
3. For each, predict the solution type from the discriminant, then
   solve: (a) `x² + 4x + 13 = 0`; (b) `5x² − 20x + 20 = 0`; (c) `x² −
   2x + 10 = 0`.
4. Solve completely over the complex numbers: (a) `x³ − 9x = 0`; (b)
   `x⁴ + 4x² = 0`.
5. Write the conjugate of each and multiply the pair: (a) `5 + 12i`;
   (b) `−3 − 4i`; (c) `7i`.
6. (Enrichment) Divide and write in standard form: (a) `(4 + 2i) /
   (3 − i)`; (b) `10 / (2 + 3i)`.
7. (Enrichment) Factor `4x² + 25` completely over the complex numbers
   and multiply back to check.
8. **Explain:** A parabola `y = x² + 2x + 5` never touches the x-axis,
   yet its equation "has solutions." Resolve the apparent
   contradiction in three sentences.

## Practice Set C — Polynomial structure (Session S7, after Lesson 03)

1. Factor by grouping: (a) `x³ + 4x² + 3x + 12`; (b) `x³ − 2x² − 9x +
   18`.
2. Use special forms: (a) factor `x³ + 125`; (b) factor `64x³ − 27`;
   (c) write `x⁴ − 625` as a difference of squares twice.
3. Use the Remainder Theorem to find each remainder without dividing:
   (a) `p(x) = x³ − 5x² + 2x + 8` divided by `(x − 3)`; (b) `p(x) =
   2x³ + x² − 7x − 6` divided by `(x + 2)`.
4. Use the Factor Theorem to test and then factor completely: (a) is
   `(x − 4)` a factor of `x³ − 7x² + 14x − 8`? Factor it fully. (b)
   Factor `x³ + 3x² − 4` completely.
5. Factor `2x⁴ − 8x² + 8` in two stages; name each structure you used.
6. (Enrichment) Expand `(x + 2y)³` with the Binomial Theorem and check
   by direct multiplication.
7. **Explain:** Why does `p(a) = 0` guarantee that `(x − a)` is a
   factor? Retell the "why it works" paragraph from Lesson 03 in your
   own words.

## Practice Set D — Zeros and graphs (Session S12, after Lesson 04)

1. Find all zeros of `f(x) = x³ + 2x² − 5x − 6`. (Test integer
   candidates.)
2. Find all zeros of `g(x) = x⁴ − 2x³ − 7x² + 8x + 12`. (Hint: `x = 3`
   and `x = −1` are zeros; divide them out.)
3. A degree-4 polynomial has zeros `1 + i` and `−2`. List all four
   zeros and write one possible factored form with real coefficients.
4. For `h(x) = −x³ + 4x² − 4x`: find all zeros, state end behavior,
   give the turning-point bound, and sketch a rough graph.
5. For `k(x) = (x − 2)³(x + 1)`: list the zeros with multiplicity,
   state end behavior, and describe what the graph does at each zero
   (crosses or touches).
6. A cubic's graph crosses the x-axis exactly once. What are the
   possible zero configurations? (Consider multiplicities and complex
   pairs.)
7. **Explain:** Two students sketch `y = x³ − x`. One draws 2 turning
   points, the other draws 4. Settle the dispute using the
   turning-point bound and the derivative-free reasoning from Lesson
   04.

## Practice Set E — Intersections (Session S14, after Lesson 05)

1. Solve exactly and give the intersection points: (a) `x² + 3 = 4x −
   1`; (b) `x³ − x = 2x² − 2x`.
2. Show that `y = −x² + 1` and `y = x² − 3` intersect, find the
   points exactly, and verify one point in both functions.
3. Show that `x² − 6x + 13 = 2x − 5` has no real solution. What does
   this mean for the two graphs?
4. Approximate all real solutions of `x³ = 5x − 2` with Desmos and
   verify each by substitution (report with `≈`).
5. Two gyms charge `G₁(m) = 30 + 15m` and `G₂(m) = 45m − 0.5m²`
   dollars for `m` months. Find when the total costs are equal,
   state the units, and say which gym is cheaper for a 12-month
   membership.
6. **Explain:** Why is "bring everything to one side and factor"
   safer than "divide both sides by `x`"? Use `x³ = 5x²` as your
   example.
