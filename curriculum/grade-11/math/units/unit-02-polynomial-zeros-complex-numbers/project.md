# Project — The Polynomial Design Studio

**Unit 02 · studios in Week 8 (S9), Week 9 (S15); gallery in Week 10 (S17)**

## Purpose

Design a polynomial model for a real situation — from its zeros up —
then prove the model behaves the way you claim. The project uses
everything the unit taught: complex numbers (Lessons 01–02),
factoring and the Factor Theorem (Lesson 03), zeros and graphs
(Lesson 04), and equation-solving with unit checks (Lesson 05).
Nothing is graded on artistry; it is graded on the mathematics being
correct, complete, and explained.

The project stays in the learner's notebook and is shown to the
guiding adult at the gallery. No learner work, names, or photos go
into any public repository — see the privacy boundaries in the track
README.

## Materials

Notebook, pencil, ruler, calculator. Desmos (free, no account) for
verification. The illustration
`assets/conjugate-mirror-landscape.png` as the design brief's visual
anchor.

## The brief

A client wants a polynomial model `P(x)` for a real quantity that
varies with `x`. Your model must satisfy **all** of these:

1. **Degree 3 or 4**, with real coefficients.
2. **A signature conjugate pair**: at least one pair of complex
   conjugate zeros `a ± bi` (inspired by the mirrored lights in
   `assets/conjugate-mirror-landscape.png` — the pair your graph
   "hides").
3. **At least one real zero** inside a realistic domain for the
   situation.
4. **End behavior that matches the story** (e.g., a profit model that
   eventually declines must go down on the right).
5. **Every quantity named with units**; the domain trimmed to what
   the situation allows.

Situation sparks (not to copy): an open-top box's volume as the
corner-cut size varies; a product's profit as its price varies (rises,
peaks, falls); water depth in a tidal basin over hours; the height of
a thrown ball with air resistance approximated; a bridge arch's height
versus distance along the span.

## Part 1 — Model plan (studio: Session 9)

Deliver in the notebook:

1. **The story** (one paragraph): what varies, what is modeled, why
   a polynomial is a reasonable shape for it.
2. **Quantity definitions**: name `x` and `P(x)` with units; state the
   realistic domain and why values outside it are rejected.
3. **The zero specification**: list every zero — real zeros with
   their story meaning, and the signature conjugate pair. Justify
   each choice in one sentence.
4. **The model**: write `P(x)` in factored form from the zeros, then
   expand to standard form. **Multiply back** to verify the expansion
   matches the factored form.
5. **The Factor Theorem check**: evaluate `P` at one real zero by
   substitution and confirm you get 0.

## Part 2 — Graph and technical memo (studio: Session 15)

6. **The rough graph**: sketch `P(x)` by hand using the Lesson 04
   recipe — zeros marked, end behavior correct, turning-point count
   within the bound, y-intercept labeled.
7. **Technology verification**: graph the same polynomial in Desmos.
   Record (in the notebook, in words) whether Desmos agrees with
   your zeros, end behavior, and turning points; note any
   discrepancy and resolve it.
8. **The technical memo** (about one page, addressed to the client):
   - What each real zero means in the story, with units.
   - Why the conjugate pair never appears on the graph — and why it
     still counts toward the degree.
   - What the end behavior says about the situation, and where the
     model stops being trustworthy.
   - One limitation of the model and one question you would ask the
     client before using it for a real decision.

## Gallery (Session 17)

Present the memo and the graph to the guiding adult. The adult gives
a written critique using the rubric; the learner addresses it or logs
why not, in the notebook.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mathematical correctness** | Factored and standard forms match (multiply-back shown); all zeros correct including the conjugate pair; Factor Theorem check written out | Forms match; zeros correct; minor arithmetic slip caught by the learner's own check | Forms inconsistent or a zero missing/miscomputed; checks absent | Model does not satisfy the brief's degree/zero requirements |
| **Zeros ↔ graph** | Sketch follows the recipe exactly; end behavior and turning-point bound both correct and labeled; Desmos comparison resolved | Sketch correct; one recipe element unlabeled; Desmos comparison present | Sketch contradicts the zeros or end behavior; no technology check | No coherent graph, or graph unrelated to the stated zeros |
| **Modeling and units** | Every quantity named with units; domain trimmed with reasons; memo interprets each zero in story language; limitation is specific | Units present; domain stated; memo interprets most zeros; limitation generic | Units missing in places; domain untrimmed; memo restates math without story meaning | No units; no domain; memo missing |
| **Communication** | Memo is clear, organized, and honest about the model's limits; gallery critique addressed in writing | Memo clear; critique acknowledged | Memo hard to follow or missing sections | Memo missing or copied from the sparks |

**Scoring:** 14–16 = exemplary; 11–13 = proficient; 8–10 =
developing; below 8 = revise and resubmit. The adult records the
score privately; it never enters the repository.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** the adult may approve a degree-3 model with zeros the
  learner chooses from a shortlist (e.g., `2, −1, 1 ± i`); the memo
  may be dictated.
- **Extension:** add a second model for the same situation with a
  different signature pair and compare their graphs; or prove that
  the product `(x − (a + bi))(x − (a − bi))` always has real
  coefficients, and explain why the brief's real-coefficient
  requirement forces conjugate pairs.
