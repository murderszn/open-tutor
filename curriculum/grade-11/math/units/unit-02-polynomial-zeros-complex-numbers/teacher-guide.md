# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Polynomial Functions, Zeros, and Complex Numbers

**For the guiding adult.** Student-facing material never contains
answers; all solutions are in `answer-key.md`. This guide covers
pacing judgment, misconception watch-lists, scoring, reteach
triggers, and next steps.

## Unit arc

Weeks 7–8 build the number system and the factoring toolkit; Weeks
9–10 convert zeros into graphs and put intersections to work. The
through-line to stress aloud: *every lesson answers "what are the
zeros?"* — first for quadratics (Lessons 01–02), then for higher
degree (Lesson 03), then "what do the zeros tell you?" (Lesson 04),
then "where do two functions agree?" (Lesson 05).

## Pacing judgment

- **S1–S5 (complex numbers):** Do not rush Lesson 01. If Practice Set
  A shows the `i²` replacement being skipped, spend S5 re-working
  Lesson 01's examples rather than previewing Lesson 02. Complex
  arithmetic is the foundation everything else stands on.
- **S6–S10 (structure):** The S8 reading session is enrichment
  (Binomial Theorem, identities). A learner who is still shaky on
  grouping should use S8 as a second factoring workshop instead —
  say so explicitly; the enrichment is optional.
- **S11–S15 (graphs):** Require the Lesson 04 sketching recipe in
  order (zeros → end behavior → turning-point bound → intercept).
  Learners who "just draw" produce wrong graphs confidently; the
  recipe is the discipline.
- **S16–S20:** The S16 synthesis workshop should include at least one
  Unit-01 connection (e.g., "how does `f(x) + k` move a polynomial's
  zeros?") to keep transformations alive.

## Misconception watch-list (by lesson)

- **L01:** leaving `i²` in the final answer; treating `i` as a
  variable to solve for; alarm at real products of complex numbers.
- **L02:** dividing only the real part in the quadratic formula;
  "negative discriminant = no solution"; conjugate as "the opposite".
- **L03:** stopping after one factoring round; sign errors in
  grouping; testing `x + 2` with `p(2)` instead of `p(−2)`.
- **L04:** "complex zeros don't count"; multiplicity confusion at
  sketch time; more turning points than `n − 1`.
- **L05:** dividing by `x` and losing `x = 0`; reporting `≈` answers
  with `=`; stopping at the x-value without finding the point.

Each lesson's Teacher note has the full list with fixes.

## Scoring

- **Practice sets:** completion + same-day scoring; the point is
  error detection, not ranking. Require every "verify" step to be
  written, not claimed.
- **Week-10 quiz (formative):** score 0–2 per item (2 = correct with
  reasoning shown; 1 = correct answer, missing check; 0 =
  incorrect). Use the pattern of 0s and 1s to plan the S18 reteach —
  do not average it into any grade.
- **Unit assessment:** score per the answer key's partial-credit
  notes. Part 4's written reasoning (item 11) is scored 0–4 on the
  rubric in the answer key: names three unit ideas in order (2),
  connects them correctly (1), clear sentences (1).
- **Project:** use the rubric in `project.md` (14–16 exemplary).
  Score privately; nothing enters the repository.

## Reteach triggers

- Practice Set A below ~70% → re-teach Lesson 01 before Lesson 02
  (see README).
- Factoring stalls in S6–S7 → review grade-9 quadratic factoring
  (prerequisite) before S11.
- Quiz item 4 or 5 missed → the zeros→graph link is insecure;
  re-work Lesson 04 Examples 1–2 in S18 before the assessment.
- Quiz item 6's approximation missing its substitution check →
  re-teach "an approximation is finished when it's checked" (Lesson
  05).

## Readiness and next steps

The unit is ready to move on when the learner can: compute with `i`
fluently; factor a cubic by grouping or theorem-testing; list all
zeros of a degree-3/4 polynomial; sketch from zeros with correct end
behavior; and solve `f(x) = g(x)` both ways. If the assessment shows
insecure complex arithmetic but solid graphing, carry a 10-minute
complex-arithmetic warm-up into Unit 03's first two weeks rather than
holding the unit open.

**Next unit (U03):** rational expressions and functions — the
factoring toolkit transfers directly to simplifying rational
expressions and finding asymptotes. Preview in S20: "what happens to
`p(x)/q(x)` near a zero of `q`?"
