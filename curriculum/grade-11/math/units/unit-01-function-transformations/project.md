# Project — The Two-Stage Machine Studio

**Unit 01 · studios in Week 4 (S9), Week 5 (S15); gallery in Week 6 (S17)**

## Purpose

Design a working two-stage function machine — a real situation modeled
as a composition — then transform it, reverse it, and defend every
choice in writing. The project uses everything the unit taught:
function notation and domain (Lesson 01), transformations (Lessons
02–03), composition (Lesson 04), and inverses (Lesson 05). Nothing
about the project is graded on artistry; it is graded on the
mathematics being correct, complete, and explained.

The project stays in the learner's notebook and is shown to the
guiding adult at the gallery. No learner work, names, or photos go
into any public repository — see the privacy boundaries in the track
README.

## Materials

Notebook, pencil, ruler, calculator, graph paper (optional), and the
illustration `assets/two-stage-function-machine.png` as a planning
reference. Desmos (free, no account) for verification graphs.

## Part 1 — Machine plan (studio: Session 9)

Invent a two-stage process from everyday life — two real steps where
the output of the first feeds the second. Examples to spark (not to
copy): farm stand → jam kitchen (pounds of berries → jars → revenue);
print queue → binding (pages → booklets → shipping weight); workout
(distance run → calories → recovery drink mix).

For **your** machine, deliver in the notebook:

1. **The story** (one paragraph): what the two stages are, what goes
   in, what comes out, and why the order cannot be swapped.
2. **The model**: name the stage functions `g` (first) and `f`
   (second) with formulas, realistic domains, and units on every
   quantity. Write the composite `(f∘g)(x)` in simplified form.
3. **The machine diagram**: redraw the two-stage flow from
   `assets/two-stage-function-machine.png` in your own hand, labeling
   *your* stages, *your* input/output quantities with units, and one
   worked input→output trace with numbers.
4. **The unit check**: show that the units cancel correctly through
   the composition (N-Q.1), and verify the composite two ways — once
   by direct substitution, once stage-by-stage — for one realistic
   input value.

## Part 2 — Transform it, reverse it (studio: Session 15)

5. **Transform**: apply a horizontal shift and a vertical stretch to
   your composite (your choice of direction and factor). Write the
   new formula, describe each transformation in words, and interpret
   what the change *means* for your story. (Example: "shifting the
   input left by 2 means the process starts 2 units earlier.")
6. **Reverse**: choose the stage function that is invertible (if
   neither is, say so honestly and restrict a domain until one is).
   Write its inverse with swap-and-solve, verify with both
   compositions, and interpret the inverse in your story's language,
   with domain and units.
7. **Decompose**: give a *different* valid decomposition of your
   composite (different from the `f`, `g` of Part 1) and prove it
   chains correctly. Explain in two sentences what the alternative
   stages would mean in the story.

## Part 3 — Gallery and defense (Session 17)

Mount the diagram and the seven deliverables as a one-spread gallery
in the notebook. Then write a **defense paragraph** (8–12 sentences)
answering:

- Where in your machine does order matter most, and what goes wrong
  if the stages swap?
- Which transformation in Part 2 was hardest to interpret in the
  story, and what did you decide it meant?
- What is one input your model must refuse (outside the realistic
  domain), and what would the formula wrongly predict if you fed it
  in anyway?

Present the spread to the guiding adult, who critiques using the
rubric below. Address each critique in writing or log why you
disagree, with mathematical backing.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | 10 — Proficient | 6 — Developing | 2 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Correct models** (10) | Stage formulas, composite, domains, and units all correct; two-way verification agrees | One formula or domain error; verification attempted | Formulas missing or do not chain; no verification |
| **Transformations** (8) | Shift + stretch written, described in order, and interpreted in the story | Transformations written but order or story meaning unclear | Transformation missing or purely decorative |
| **Inverse** (8) | Inverse found by swap-and-solve, both compositions verified, interpreted with domain and units | Inverse found but verification incomplete or interpretation missing units | No inverse, or `1/f(x)` confusion uncorrected |
| **Reasoning & defense** (8) | Defense answers all three questions with specific mathematics; critique addressed | Defense generic or one question unanswered | No defense or no engagement with critique |
| **Communication** (6) | Notation consistent; diagram labeled with quantities and units; one worked trace shown | Notation mostly consistent; labels incomplete | Work unreadable or unlabeled |

**Scoring note:** 34–40 = mastery (ready for Unit 02); 26–33 =
proficient with one revision task assigned by the adult; below 26 =
revisit Lessons 04–05 before the unit assessment. The adult records
the score and the revision plan privately — never in this repository.
