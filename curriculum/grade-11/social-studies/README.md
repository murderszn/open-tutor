# Grade 11 Social Studies

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are
delivered; the eight units are planned, not yet written. See
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained
for one grade-11 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 11 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-11 **U.S. government and civics** course,
built on the C3 Framework's inquiry arc and taught **nonpartisan** throughout:
foundations of constitutional government and political ideas; the
Constitution, federalism, and institutional powers; civil liberties, civil
rights, and equal protection; elections, representation, participation, and
political parties; Congress, the presidency, the courts, and policy making;
state, local, and tribal governments and civic institutions; media, public
opinion, comparative government, and civic evidence; and a public-policy
inquiry and nonpartisan civic capstone.

Each of the eight units runs four weeks at about five 50-minute sessions per
week: a concept launch, a close-reading and skills session, an investigation
or application session (data tasks, simulations, moot courts, deliberations),
an evidence-based writing or structured-deliberation session, and a
review-and-check session. Learners work with public-domain founding and legal
texts (Declaration of Independence, Constitution, Federalist/Anti-Federalist
writings, Supreme Court opinions, executive orders, treaties), official
datasets, structured deliberations, and policy analysis. Two diagnostic weeks
open the year, with midyear and final review weeks.

This is a proposed pathway, not a universal requirement: high-school
social-studies course order varies by system. A guiding adult checks placement
before starting, previews every primary source and topic for mature themes —
racial discrimination, state violence, and contested elections are handled
with age-appropriate, adult-supervised inquiry (see the audit's source and
safety notes) — and keeps all contested questions multi-perspective and
evidence-based.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. **Explain founding political ideas and evaluate the constitutional
   founding:** social-contract, natural-rights, and limited-government ideas;
   the Declaration and the Articles' failure; the Federalist–Anti-Federalist
   debate. (C3 D2.Civ.4.9-12; D2.Civ.8.9-12)
2. **Analyze federalism and the separation of powers as contested systems:**
   enumerated, reserved, and concurrent powers; checks and balances and
   judicial review; how federal–state relations and institutional powers
   changed over time. (D2.Civ.1.9-12; D2.Civ.4.9-12)
3. **Distinguish civil liberties from civil rights and trace the
   equal-protection struggle:** the Bill of Rights and incorporation; how
   people used and challenged law — litigation, legislation, movements — to
   expand liberty and equality. (D2.Civ.12.9-12; D2.Civ.14.9-12)
4. **Explain elections, parties, and participation:** the electoral system
   and the Electoral College debate; party functions, realignment, and
   interest groups; citizens' roles and participation patterns across
   democratic theories. (D2.Civ.1.9-12; D2.Civ.2.9-12)
5. **Analyze how Congress, the presidency, and the courts make and check
   policy:** the legislative process with its frictions; formal and informal
   presidential powers; judicial philosophies and the courts' role.
   (D2.Civ.4.9-12; D2.Civ.11.9-12)
6. **Compare state, local, and tribal governments and evaluate civic
   institutions:** state and local structures and direct-democracy tools;
   tribal sovereignty; how citizens and institutions address problems at each
   level. (D2.Civ.1.9-12; D2.Civ.5.9-12)
7. **Evaluate media and public-opinion evidence and compare political
   systems:** polls and news coverage assessed for framing, selection, and
   omission; democratic and nondemocratic systems analyzed across contexts.
   (D2.Civ.8.9-12; D2.Civ.9.9-12; D3.1–D3.4)
8. **Evaluate public policies by intended and unintended outcomes:** a
   policy's aims, implementation, costs and benefits, and consequences for
   different groups. (D2.Civ.13.9-12; D2.Eco.7.9-12; D2.Eco.8.9-12)
9. **Frame civic questions and work with sources across viewpoints:**
   compelling and supporting questions, source planning, and evidence
   evaluation with attention to limitations. (D1.1–D1.5.9-12; D3.1–D3.4.9-12)
10. **Communicate conclusions and assess options for informed civic action:**
    evidence-based argument, presentation to a real audience, and evaluation
    of action options within lawful, adult-supervised channels.
    (D4.1–D4.4.9-12; D4.6.9-12; D4.7.9-12)

Standards references are to the College, Career, and Civic Life (C3) Framework
for Social Studies State Standards (National Council for the Social Studies),
grades 9–12 band. Codes were verified during the audit (see §4 of the
scope-and-sequence); descriptions there are brief paraphrases — check the
authoritative framework for exact wording. **No state adoption, accreditation,
or alignment certification is claimed.**

The audit (2026-10-08, against `main`) found **no existing files** in this
track's folder — it is planned from scratch. Reusable material inventoried

  `resources/black_excellence_figures.md` (adult pre-selects figures for
  U03), `resources/supply_and_demand_economics.md` (government-role and
  policy background for U05/U08 — re-leveled for grade 11).

  timeline and analysis tasks (note: `fact` strings are templated
  boilerplate; officeholder data may be dated — re-verify at authoring);
  `resources/us_states.csv` for U06 state/local comparison tasks (note:
  figures are approximations).
- **Gap to fill:** a verified adult-side tribal-government reference
  (sovereignty, treaties, contemporary tribal governance) — authored during
  the U06 unit section.
- **Gaps to build:** all lessons, teacher guides, separate answer keys,
  quizzes, assessments, diagnostics, resource packs, generated images, and
  the R00 review package — authored in U01–U08 and R00.
