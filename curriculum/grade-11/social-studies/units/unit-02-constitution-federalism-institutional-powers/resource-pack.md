# Resource Pack — Unit 02: The Constitution, Federalism, and Institutional Powers

**Goal:** Give a grade-11 learner (ages ~16–17) direct access to the
constitutional text, the founding-era and court sources, and reputable
explainers behind this unit's lessons, with free no-account routes for
every core objective.
**Checked:** 2026-10-21. Links marked *(opened)* were opened and verified
on that date; links marked *(search link)* are clearly labeled
preferred-channel searches, not verified items; links marked
*(recommended starting point; not opened this run)* are listed for the
adult to verify before assigning.

## Focused queries

Use these verbatim in a search engine when you need more than the pack
lists:

1. `site:archives.gov founding-docs constitution-transcript Article I Section 8`
2. `McCulloch v Maryland 1819 necessary and proper "power to tax involves the power to destroy"`
3. `Gibbons v Ogden 1824 commerce navigation federal license monopoly`
4. `Marbury v Madison 1803 judicial review "province and duty" original jurisdiction`
5. `site:constitution.congress.gov Article V amendment proposal ratification three-fourths`
6. `United States v Lopez 1995 commerce clause school zones limit`

## Web references

1. *(opened)* **National Archives — Constitution transcription (with
   amendments):** https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-transcript
   — the authoritative public-domain text used in every lesson
   (Article I, §8; Article II; Article III; Article V; Article VI;
   Tenth Amendment).
2. *(opened)* **Congress.gov, Constitution Annotated — Article V:**
   https://constitution.congress.gov/constitution/article-5/ — Library
   of Congress clause-by-clause Article V text and essays, used in
   Lesson 05 for proposal/ratification paths and ratification
   deadlines. Free, no account.
3. *(search-result verified 2026-10-21)* **Cornell LII — Overview of
   Article V:**
   https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution-conan/article-5/overview-of-article-v
   — explains both proposal methods (including that the convention
   method has never been used) and that Congress has proposed
   33 amendments, 27 ratified. Free, no account.
4. *(search link)* **Oyez — *Marbury v. Madison* (1803), *McCulloch v.
   Maryland* (1819), *Gibbons v. Ogden* (1824)** — search
   `site:oyez.org Marbury Madison` (and likewise for McCulloch and
   Gibbons). Free, no account; plain-language case facts, holdings,
   and opinion summaries for Lessons 02–03. The adult confirms the
   case page before assigning.
5. *(search link)* **Oyez — *United States v. Lopez* (1995), *NFIB v.
   Sebelius* (2012), *Printz v. United States* (1997)** — search
   `site:oyez.org Lopez 1995 commerce` (and likewise for NFIB and
   Printz). Free, no account; holdings and reasoning for Lesson 04's
   limits on national power.
6. **National Constitution Center — Interactive Constitution:**
   https://constitutioncenter.org/the-constitution — nonpartisan,
   scholar-written clause-by-clause explainers; useful for Lessons
   01–05 extension work. Free, no account. *(Recommended starting
   point; not opened this run — verify before assigning.)*
7. **UCF Pressbooks, *American Government 2e* — Federalist Papers #10
   and #51:**
   https://pressbooks.online.ucf.edu/pos2041bw/chapter/federalist-papers-10-and-51/
   — open-textbook full text of Federalist No. 51 (Madison's "double
   security" and "ambition must be made to counteract ambition"), used
   in Lesson 01. Free, no account. *(Listed in the Unit 01 pack as
   search-result verified 2026-10-14; re-verify before assigning.)*

## Internal references (repository)

- `resources/government_basics.md` — adult-side reference for
  three-branches/federalism vocabulary (elementary framing; the adult
  re-levels for grade 11; never assigned as lesson text).
- `resources/united_states_understanding_and_principles.md` — adult-side
  reference on consent of the governed and the Articles' failure
  (background for Lesson 01's "why divide power" discussion).
- `resources/us_presidents.csv` — *not* used in U02: its `fact` strings
  are templated boilerplate and officeholder data may be dated (see the
  track audit's cautions). U02's data tasks use constitutional
  arithmetic and dated official sources instead, so no stale figure is
  baked into a lesson.
- `resources/us_states.csv` — candidate for the project's state-level
  research *only* with the audit's cautions (figures are approximations;
  verify and date before learner-facing use). The project requires
  official `.gov` sources and access dates, not this dataset alone.
