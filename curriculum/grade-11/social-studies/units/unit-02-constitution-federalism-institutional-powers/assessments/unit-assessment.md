# U02 Unit Assessment — The Constitution, Federalism, and Institutional Powers

**When:** W10S5. **Time:** 60 minutes. **Format:** closed-notes
except the Lesson 04 text-only alternative and the Article V table you
completed in Lesson 05 (blank table provided below for Task 5 — you
fill it in). 9 tasks, 50 points. Scoring guidance is in
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) — never beside these questions.

## Task 1 — Powers-sort (6 pts)

Classify each power as enumerated, reserved, concurrent, or denied
(denied: name the level denied), with a clause or amendment citation:

1. Regulating commerce among the states
2. Establishing a state university system
3. Taxing personal income
4. A state coining money
5. Punishing piracy on the high seas
6. Suspending habeas corpus in peacetime (national government)

## Task 2 — McCulloch application (6 pts)

Congress, citing its power to regulate commerce, creates a federal
agency to inspect all meatpacking plants that ship meat across state
lines. A plant owner sues, arguing that no clause says "inspect
meatpacking plants."

1. (3 pts) Apply Marshall's three gates to this agency. Does it pass
   each gate? Explain.
2. (3 pts) Now change one fact: the agency inspects *only* plants that
   sell meat within a single state and never ship across a state line.
   Does your answer change? Explain which gate changes and why.

## Task 3 — Marbury reasoning (6 pts)

A federal statute gives the Supreme Court original jurisdiction over
all cases involving federal taxes. A taxpayer sues directly in the
Supreme Court under that statute.

1. (3 pts) Walk through *Marbury*'s three steps as applied to this
   case: right, remedy, conflict.
2. (3 pts) What should the Court do, and what principle from *Marbury*
   justifies that result even though the taxpayer may have a genuine
   complaint?

## Task 4 — Image analysis (5 pts)

Use the Lesson 04 illustration (or its text-only alternative):

1. (2 pts) Match each of the four buildings to its level of government

2. (3 pts) Choose one service from the scene that needs two levels
   working together. Explain what each level contributes and where a
   conflict between them could arise.

## Task 6 — Federalism in practice (7 pts)

Congress offers states education funds on one condition: states must
adopt a federal testing regime. A state that refuses loses *all* of its
existing federal education funds — not just the new money.

1. (3 pts) Is the offer best analyzed as a categorical grant condition,
   a block grant, a mandate, or preemption? Justify your classification.
2. (4 pts) Apply *NFIB*'s coercion reasoning and *Printz*'s
   anti-commandeering rule: does either doctrine limit this offer, and
   if so, how? (Address each doctrine separately.)

## Task 7 — Lopez application (5 pts)

Congress passes a federal law banning bullying in all public schools,
citing the commerce power: bullied students learn less, earn less, and
therefore affect the national economy.

1. (3 pts) Apply *Lopez*: what is the strongest argument that this law
   exceeds the commerce power?
2. (2 pts) What is Congress's best reply, and why does *Lopez* still
   leave that reply weak?

## Task 8 — Informal change (4 pts)

Name three forms of informal constitutional change from Lesson 05 and
give one accurate example of each. For one of your examples, explain
in two sentences why it counts as *change* even though no constitutional
word changed.

## Task 9 — Argument essay (5 pts)

**Prompt:** "Is the Constitution hard or easy to change — and should
it be?" Write 250–350 words defending one position with at least two
pieces of evidence from this unit (one formal, one informal), and
include a one-paragraph counterargument to your own position. Your
essay is scored on evidence use, reasoning, and counterargument
quality — not on which position you choose.
