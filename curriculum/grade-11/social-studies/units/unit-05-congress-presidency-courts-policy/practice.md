# Unit 05 — Practice Sets

Complete these after the lessons they follow. All items are solved
in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md). Keep answers separate from the
questions while working.

## Set A — Lessons 01–02 (Congress and the bill's path)

A1. A bill passes the House 300–135. How many more votes would it
need to override a presidential veto in the House? Show the
arithmetic.

A2. A Senate bill has 59 supporters and 41 opponents. Its supporters
move for cloture. What happens, and how many votes short are they?

A3. List the five stages of the legislative path in order, and name
one friction point at each of stages 2, 3, and 5.

A4. A bill is referred to the House Committee on Energy and Commerce.
The chair never schedules a hearing. What has happened to the bill,
and is there a vote that defeated it?

A5. Explain in three sentences why the Senate's six-year staggered
terms make it harder for a single election to change the Senate's
majority than a single election changes the House's majority.

A6. **Error analysis:** "The House passed the bill 250–185, so it is
now a law." Identify the error and name the next three things that
must happen before it can become law.

A7. A state has 9 House seats. After the census it has 8. What
happens to its number of senators? Explain in one sentence.

A8. A conference committee produces a single text, but the Senate
rejects it 48–52. What is the status of the bill?

## Set B — Lessons 03–04 (Presidency and bureaucracy)

B1. A president issues an executive order directing the Department of
Defense to change a procurement policy. A president issues an
executive order directing a private company to change its hiring
policy. Which order is within the president's authority, and why?

B2. Name the three sources of authority EO 9981 cites, and state in
one sentence what the order actually created.

B3. An agency publishes a proposed rule and, two weeks later, a final
rule — with no comment period and no response to comments. Name the
two APA violations a challenger would allege.

B4. A statute says an agency shall set standards "as necessary to
protect workers." In 2023 a court defers to the agency's reasonable
reading. In 2025 the same question returns. What does the court do
differently after *Loper Bright*, and why?

B5. Explain in three sentences the difference between a treaty and an
executive agreement, and say which one needs the Senate.

B6. **Error analysis:** "The EPA sets air-quality standards because
Congress gave up its power to the agency." Identify the error and
correct it with the Clean Air Act chain.

B7. A citizen finds a proposed rule she opposes. List the four steps
she takes to participate, in order, and name the journal where the
proposal appears.

B8. Place each action in Jackson's zones: (a) the president acts
where Congress has authorized the action; (b) the president acts
where Congress has forbidden it; (c) the president acts where
Congress has said nothing.

## Set C — Lessons 05–06 (Courts and budgets)

C1. A case is decided by a district court, appealed to a court of
appeals, and the losing party petitions the Supreme Court. Four
justices vote to grant review; five vote to deny. Is the case heard?
Explain the Rule of Four in one sentence.

C2. An originalist and a living-constitutionalist judge disagree
about whether the Fourth Amendment covers a new surveillance
technology. State each judge's likely starting point in one sentence
each.

C3. Using the FY2024 figures (receipts $4.919T, outlays $6.752T,
deficit $1.833T): (a) compute the deficit as a percentage of
receipts; (b) explain what the percentage means in one sentence.

C4. A program is authorized but never appropriated. A second program
is appropriated without authorization. Which program can spend money,
and why? (Assume no permanent-law exception applies.)

C5. **Error analysis:** "The Supreme Court declined to hear the
case, so it ruled for the lower court's winner." Identify the error
and correct it.

C6. Explain the difference between mandatory and discretionary
spending, and name one example of each from the FY2024 budget
discussion.

C7. A president's budget proposes $500 billion for a program.
Congress appropriates $420 billion. How much does the agency receive,
and whose decision controls?

C8. **Explain your reasoning:** A learner argues that the courts are
the most powerful branch because they can strike down anything.
Give the strongest counter-argument from this unit in three
sentences — using *Youngstown*, *Loper Bright*, or the budget
process as evidence.
