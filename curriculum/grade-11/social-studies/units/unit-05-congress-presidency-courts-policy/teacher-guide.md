# Unit 05 — Teacher Guide

**For the guiding adult.** This unit is a validated draft, not
educator-reviewed. Preview every source, date every current-data
claim, and keep the unit nonpartisan: institutions and processes are
analyzed without endorsing parties, candidates, or causes. Every
answer is in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md); solve items yourself
before the learner attempts them.

## Pacing at a glance

Five ~50-minute sessions per week, Weeks 20–23 (see the unit README
for the full 20-session table). Lessons 01–06 are the S1/S2 launches
and close reads; S3 is investigation or simulation; S4 is
evidence-based writing or deliberation; S5 is review and the unit
check. If a session runs long, cut the extension, never the exit
check — the exit checks are the adult's record of standing.

## Per-lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — Congress structure

- **Watch for:** the "Congress is one body that votes once" error.
  Insist on chamber + stage every time ("the House passed it" is not
  "Congress passed it").
- **The hearing image:** the illustration is AI-generated and
  labeled as such. Use it to teach the room's layout, not as evidence
  about any real hearing. The text-only alternative carries the same
  content for learners who cannot see the image.
- **Reteach:** if the learner cannot name both chambers' sizes and
  terms from memory, rebuild the comparison table together (size,
  term, leadership, debate control) before Lesson 02.

### Lesson 02 — How a bill becomes law

- **Watch for:** "passed the House = became law" and "the
  filibuster is in the Constitution." Both are corrected in the
  lesson's error-analysis item; revisit them in the Week 20, S5
  review.
- **Arithmetic:** the override threshold (290 House, 67 Senate) is
  the unit's most-tested computation. Have the learner derive it
  from 435 and 100 rather than memorizing it: 435 × 2/3 = 290;
  100 × 2/3 = 66.67, rounded up to 67 because a fractional vote
  cannot be cast.
- **The fictional bill:** the School Lunch Nutrition Standards Act is
  invented for this unit. Say so explicitly — learners must not cite
  it as a real law.

### Lesson 03 — Presidency and EO 9981

- **Sensitive content:** EO 9981 concerns racial segregation in the
  armed forces. Preview the material, frame discussion around
  evidence and institutional reasoning, and offer a reflection
  alternative for any learner who finds the material distressing.
- **Watch for:** "an executive order is a law by another name."
  Drill the limits: binds the executive branch, cannot appropriate
  money, cannot contradict a statute, revocable by the next
  president.
- **Close read:** the EO 9981 excerpt is public domain. Read the
  full order (National Constitution Center link in the Resource
  Pack) before assigning; the lesson quotes the operative sentence
  and the authority clause only.

### Lesson 04 — Bureaucracy and rulemaking

- **Watch for:** "agencies make laws on their own authority." Drill
  the chain: statute first, rule second, court review third.
- ***Loper Bright* neutrality:** present both positions with their
  evidence (supporters: judges, not agencies, decide what the law
  means; critics: technical questions move from expert agencies to
  generalist courts). Do not let the lesson become a verdict on the
  decision.
- **Federal Register:** the site is the official journal. On
  2026-11-11 the crawler hit its automated-access screen; open it in
  a browser and complete its access check. If it is unreachable on
  the day, use the printed rule summary in the lesson and the CRS
  report instead — do not invent a rule text.

### Lesson 05 — Courts and judicial philosophies

- **Watch for:** "the Supreme Court hears every appeal" (correct
  with the Rule of Four and the grant rate) and treating the two
  philosophies as team jerseys (insist the learner argue both sides
  of *Youngstown*).
- **Moot court (Week 22, S3):** roles are two advocates per side,
  three justices, one clerk. Eight minutes per side; justices may
  interrupt with questions. The debrief must name one good argument
  each side made and the zone the justices chose. If only one learner
  is present, the adult plays the opposing advocate and the learner
  argues both sides in turn.
- ***Youngstown* facts:** check the opinion text before assigning;
  the lesson's summary is accurate (6–3, Black majority, Jackson
  concurrence, three zones) but the adult should read the primary
  source.

### Lesson 06 — Budget and who makes policy

- **Watch for:** deficit/debt confusion ("flow vs. stock") and
  treating the president's budget proposal as the budget. Both are
  corrected in the lesson.
- **Figures:** all figures are FY2024, dated, from the CBO. Re-check
  before assigning; if newer figures exist, redo Worked example 1
  with the fresh numbers — that is a feature, not a disruption.
- **Essential-question essay:** grade it on evidence and the stated
  limitation, not on which institution the learner ranks first. A
  verdict for any institution can earn full marks if the evidence is
  accurate and the limitation is genuine.

## Misconception map

| Misconception | Where it appears | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Congress votes once | Lesson 01, exit check | Chamber + stage, every time |
| Committees are decorative | Lesson 01 | Most bills die in committee; the chair's schedule is the first veto point |
| Passed the House = law | Lesson 02 | Five stages; both chambers + president |
| Filibuster is constitutional | Lesson 02 | Senate Rule XXII; ends debate, needs 60 for cloture |
| Executive orders are laws | Lesson 03 | Bind the executive branch only; revocable; cannot appropriate or contradict a statute |
| EO 9981 ended segregation in a day | Lesson 03 | Policy declaration + committee; integration took years |
| Agencies make law alone | Lesson 04 | Statute → rule → court review; no statute, no power |
| *Loper Bright* ended agency expertise | Lesson 04 | Expertise still persuasive; only its binding force ended |
| Supreme Court hears every appeal | Lesson 05 | Rule of Four; ~60–80 grants from thousands of petitions |
| Deficit = debt | Lesson 06 | Flow vs. stock; FY2024 deficit $1.833T, debt above $35T |
| President's budget = the budget | Lesson 06 | Proposal is a request; appropriations are the law |

## Differentiation

- **Reading access:** all legal texts are excerpted with
  plain-language bridges; full documents are optional extensions;
  read aloud or use text-to-speech.
- **Processing:** graphic organizers for every argument task;
  sentence starters for claims and counterclaims; the bill-path
  diagram and its text-only version; the hearing illustration and
  its text-only role table.
- **Output:** oral or scribed responses accepted for any written task
  with adult documentation; extended time built into S5 sessions.
- **Extension:** each lesson carries an extension task (current
  committee chairs, a real law traced on Congress.gov, a second
  executive order, a Federal Register comment draft, the *Youngstown*
  opinions, current-year CBO figures).

## Project facilitation

- Approve the topic before step 1 (fictional or local-administrative;
  nonpartisan; no current partisan controversy).
- Play the committee chair in the markup: propose one friendly and
  one hostile amendment, and insist the learner record a reason for
  each decision.
- Play the president in step 4 and give a written reason for the
  chosen action.
- Score with the project rubric; record the score and the one
  criterion to strengthen.

## Readiness and reteaching

- **Ready for Unit 06** if the learner scores 30+ on the unit
  assessment, can trace a bill through all five stages with the
  override arithmetic, and can place an action in Jackson's zones.
- **Reteach** with the S5 review sessions and the practice sets:
  Set A for the legislative path, Set B for presidency/bureaucracy,
  Set C for courts/budgets. A learner below 22 on the assessment
  retakes it after reteaching the weakest part.
- **Carry forward:** the essential-question verdict, the project bill,
  and the moot-court brief feed directly into Unit 06 (state/local/
  tribal governments) and Unit 08 (the policy capstone).
