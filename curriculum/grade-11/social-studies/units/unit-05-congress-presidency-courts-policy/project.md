# Unit 05 — Project: Policy-Making Simulation

## One bill from idea to implementation

**Time:** six sessions across Weeks 21–23 (S5 of Week 21, S3 of Week
22, S3–S4 of Week 23, plus homework between sessions) · **Adult
role:** the adult approves the topic, plays the committee chair in
the markup, and reviews every source before it is used.

## The task

Take one policy idea through the whole system this unit describes.
The learner works as the bill's sponsor, then as the agency that
implements it, then as the lawyer who must defend it in court.

**Topic rules (nonpartisan by design):** choose a fictional, local,
or administrative topic the adult approves — for example, school-bus
safety standards, a town's flood-warning system, public-library
hours, or a state park's trail maintenance. Do not choose a current
partisan controversy, a candidate, or a pending real bill. The topic
must be one where a federal statute, an agency rule, and a budget
line can all plausibly exist.

## Steps

1. **Draft the bill (Week 21, S5).** Write a one-page bill: title,
   findings (three facts the policy responds to, each dated and
   sourced), operative sections (what the law requires, who must do
   it, and by when), and an authorization figure. State the
   constitutional basis (Article I power) in one paragraph.
2. **Committee markup (Week 22, S3, with the moot-court session).**
   The adult, as chair, holds a hearing: the learner testifies as
   sponsor and answers questions. The adult proposes two amendments
   — one friendly, one hostile. The learner accepts, rejects, or
   revises each, with a reason recorded for each decision.
3. **Floor and conference (homework, Week 22).** Write a half-page
   floor record: the House vote total, the Senate's different version
   (change one provision), and the conference text that reconciles
   them. Show the arithmetic for each vote and name the friction the
   bill met (or avoided).
4. **Presidential action (Week 23, S3).** The adult plays the
   president and chooses: sign, veto, or pocket veto — with a reason
   in writing. If vetoed, the learner computes whether an override is
   possible from the recorded vote totals and writes the override
   argument or the concession.
5. **Agency rulemaking (Week 23, S3–S4).** Assume the bill became law.
   The learner, now as the implementing agency, drafts a proposed
   rule in the Federal Register style: the statute it implements, the
   rule's operative requirement, a 30-day comment period, and two
   expected comment themes (one supportive, one opposed) with the
   agency's draft response to each.
6. **Court challenge (Week 23, S4).** A challenger sues, saying the
   agency exceeded the statute. The learner writes a one-page legal
   memo arguing both sides — the agency's case and the challenger's
   case — and predicts the outcome under *Loper Bright*, naming the
   statute's best meaning as the learner reads it.

## Deliverables

- The one-page bill with constitutional-basis paragraph.
- A markup record (two amendments, decisions, reasons).
- A half-page floor and conference record with vote arithmetic.
- The presidential-action note and, if vetoed, the override computation.
- The proposed rule with two comment themes and draft responses.
- The one-page two-sided legal memo with a *Loper Bright* prediction.
- A 300-word reflection: which institution had the most power over
  your bill, and what surprised you about where it stalled or moved?

## Rubric

| Criterion | Excellent (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Constitutional basis | Names the correct Article I power and explains why it fits, with a citation | Names the power with a brief reason | Names a power but the fit is unclear | No constitutional basis stated |
| Legislative path | All five stages traced with correct vote arithmetic and a real friction named | Five stages traced; one arithmetic or friction error | Stages missing or out of order | Path not traced |
| Markup reasoning | Both amendments decided with reasons tied to the bill's purpose | Both decided with reasons | Decisions without reasons | No markup record |
| Rulemaking | Proposed rule cites the statute, states a requirement, and answers both comment themes | Rule cites the statute and answers one theme | Rule missing a citation or a response | No proposed rule |
| Legal memo | Both sides argued with the statute's text; *Loper Bright* applied correctly | Both sides argued; *Loper Bright* named but loosely applied | One side only, or the holding misstated | No memo |
| Reflection | Names the most powerful institution with evidence and one genuine surprise | Names an institution with evidence | Names an institution without evidence | No reflection |
| Accuracy and sourcing | Every figure dated and sourced; no invented law, case, or figure | Figures dated; one sourcing gap | Undated figures or one invented claim | Multiple invented claims |

**Scoring:** 24–28 = excellent; 18–23 = proficient; 12–17 =
developing; below 12 = revise with the adult and resubmit one
criterion. The adult records the score and the one criterion to
strengthen next.

## Safety and boundaries

All topics are fictional or local-administrative, adult-approved, and
nonpartisan. No contact with real officials, agencies, or
organizations. No real rule is filed and no real comment is
submitted — the Federal Register work is a classroom simulation on
paper. Sources are official or reputable only; every figure is dated.
