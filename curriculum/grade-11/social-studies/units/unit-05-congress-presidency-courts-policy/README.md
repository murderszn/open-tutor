# Unit 05 — Congress, Presidency, Courts, and Policy Making

**Grade:** 11 · **Subject:** Social Studies (U.S. government and civics)
**Track weeks:** 20–23 · **Backlog:** [issue #53](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/53), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Units 01–04 asked where government authority comes from, how the
Constitution organizes it, which rights limit it, and who gets to choose
the people who exercise it. This unit asks the next question: once
elected, how do Congress, the president, and the courts actually make
policy — and who really decides? The answer runs through committee
rooms and floor rules, executive orders and the bureaucracy, courtrooms
and judicial philosophies, and the federal budget. The unit is
nonpartisan throughout: institutions and processes are analyzed without
endorsing parties, candidates, or causes, and every contested question
is argued from at least two sides with evidence.

## Essential question

Who really makes policy — Congress, the president, the courts, or the
bureaucracy — and what keeps each of them in check?

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–04 and the track diagnostic weeks (weeks 1–2):

- Explain federalism and the separation of powers, and trace one power
  through the checks that restrain it; explain judicial review from
  *Marbury v. Madison* (1803) and the Supremacy Clause.
- Explain the amendment process, the Electoral College, parties, and
  interest groups; read a data table and compute a percentage.
- Classify a source by kind and infer its maker and audience; write a
  claim supported by at least two pieces of evidence, with a
  counterclaim; argue a contested question from both sides.
- Read founding-era and legal English with vocabulary support (the
  adult pre-teaches terms before every primary-source reading; see the
  track [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md), §2).

No cross-grade learner links: these are described skills, not references
to other grades' lessons.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Explain** Congress's design — bicameralism, two-year House and
   six-year Senate terms, leadership, and the committee system — and
   how each chamber's rules shape what becomes law.
   (D2.Civ.1.9-12; D2.Civ.11.9-12)
2. **Trace** a bill's path through introduction, committee, floor,
   conference, and presidential action; name the friction points
   (filibuster and cloture, holds, veto, pocket veto) and compute a
   veto-override threshold. (D2.Civ.11.9-12; D2.Civ.4.9-12)
3. **Compare** the presidency's formal powers with its informal powers,
   and **evaluate** one executive order — Executive Order 9981 (1948)
   — for its legal basis, its reach, and its limits.
   (D2.Civ.4.9-12; D2.Civ.1.9-12)
4. **Explain** the federal bureaucracy's structure and the
   Administrative Procedure Act's notice-and-comment rulemaking
   process, and **analyze** how courts review agency action after
   *Whitman v. American Trucking Ass'ns* (2001) and *Loper Bright
   Enterprises v. Raimondo* (2024). (D2.Civ.6.9-12; D2.Civ.4.9-12)
5. **Explain** the federal court structure, how cases reach the Supreme
   Court, and two judicial philosophies, and **argue** a moot-court
   case — *Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer* (1952) — from both
   sides using Justice Jackson's three-zone framework.
   (D2.Civ.4.9-12; D2.Civ.9.9-12)
6. **Analyze** the federal budget process — authorization,
   appropriation, deficit, and debt — with dated fiscal-year 2024
   figures, and **evaluate** who really makes policy, with evidence
   for each institution. (D2.Civ.13.9-12; D2.Eco.7.9-12; D2.Eco.8.9-12)

In every unit the learner also develops compelling questions
(D1.1–D1.5.9-12), evaluates sources and their limits (D3.1–D3.4.9-12),
and communicates evidence-based conclusions (D4.1–D4.4.9-12).

**Currency note:** election results, officeholders, court composition,
and budget figures change. Every current-data claim in this unit is
dated: the budget figures are fiscal year 2024 (Congressional Budget
Office, Monthly Budget Review: September 2024, checked 2026-11-11);
the *Loper Bright* holding is dated June 28, 2024; court and agency
examples are framed around institutional roles and rules, not named
officeholders, wherever possible. Re-check every figure before
assigning.

**Text rights:** all learner-facing founding and legal texts are public
domain (Constitution and amendment texts, Executive Order 9981,
Supreme Court opinions, the Administrative Procedure Act, the Federal
Register). Modern commentary and news coverage are linked or briefly
quoted, never reproduced. The generated hearing illustration is labeled
as an AI illustration, never presented as a photograph or a primary
source.

## 20-session pacing (Weeks 20–23)

Five ~50-minute sessions per week. Lessons are the S1 launches and S2
close-reading sessions; S3 is investigation or simulation, S4 is
evidence-based writing or deliberation, S5 is review and the unit check.
Practice, project work, and the assessments fill the remaining sessions
as shown — six lessons alone do not fill twenty sessions.

## Materials

Printed or digital Constitution and Declaration texts (public domain);
the generated committee-hearing illustration and the bill-path diagram
in this unit's `assets/`; access to Congress.gov, the Federal Register,
and the Congressional Budget Office site for S3 investigations; a
notebook or digital document for the inquiry journal. No specialized
purchases; household and library materials suffice. Founding-era and
legal texts are excerpted to the essential passage with vocabulary
glossaries; the adult may read aloud or use text-to-speech.

## Accessibility supports

- **Multiple representations:** the bill-path diagram ships as an SVG
  with a text-only step list; the hearing illustration ships with alt
  text, a caption, and a text-only role table; every data task ships
  with a data table.
- **Reading:** legal opinions and executive-order text are excerpted
  with plain-language bridges; full documents are optional extensions.
- **Processing and output:** graphic organizers for every argument
  task; sentence starters for claims and counterclaims; structured
  deliberation and moot-court protocols with roles; oral or scribed
  responses accepted for any written task with adult documentation.
- **Sensitive content:** Executive Order 9981 concerns racial
  segregation in the armed forces; the adult previews the material,
  frames discussion around evidence and institutional reasoning, and
  offers a reflection alternative. *Youngstown* concerns a wartime
  labor dispute; no graphic content.
