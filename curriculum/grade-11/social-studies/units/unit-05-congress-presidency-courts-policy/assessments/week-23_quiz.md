# Week-23 Quiz — Congress, Presidency, Courts, and Policy Making

**Formative quiz** · Track week 23, session 5 · ~20 minutes ·
Closed book except the FY2024 figures table in Lesson 06. All items
are solved in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md). Do not write answers
on this page.

## Part A — Recall (1 point each)

1. The House has ___ seats with ___-year terms; the Senate has ___
   seats with ___-year terms.
2. Name the Senate rule that requires 60 votes to end debate.
3. A president vetoes a bill. How many votes in each chamber are
   needed to override? (Give both numbers.)
4. Name the journal in which an agency publishes a proposed rule.
5. What did *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo* (2024) do to
   *Chevron* deference?
6. How many justices must vote to grant certiorari?

## Part B — Application (2 points each)

7. A bill passes the House 290–145. The president vetoes it. The
   Senate votes 66–34 to override. Does the override succeed?
   Show the arithmetic.
8. An agency proposes a rule, takes public comments for 45 days,
   and publishes a final rule that answers the major comments. A
   challenger sues, saying the agency had no statutory authority at
   all. Which question will the court decide first — the procedure or
   the authority — and why?
9. Place this action in Jackson's zones: the president orders an
   agency to do something Congress has expressly forbidden by
   statute. Name the zone and state what the action needs to survive.

## Part C — Short response (3 points each)

10. In three sentences, explain the difference between the deficit
    and the national debt, using the FY2024 deficit figure
    ($1.833 trillion).
11. A learner says, "The president makes policy because the president
    runs the bureaucracy." Give the strongest one-paragraph reply
    from Congress's side, using one fact from this unit.

**Total: 6 + 6 + 6 = 18 points.** 16–18 = ready for the unit
assessment; 13–15 = review the missed lesson before the assessment;
below 13 = re-teach with the adult using the teacher guide's
reteaching notes.
