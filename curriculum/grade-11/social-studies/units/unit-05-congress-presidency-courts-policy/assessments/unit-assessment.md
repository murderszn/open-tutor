# Unit 05 — Culminating Assessment

**Track weeks 20–23** · ~50 minutes · Closed book except the FY2024
figures table in Lesson 06 and the bill-path diagram. All items are
solved in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md). Do not write answers on
this page. This assessment is not standardized or validated; it
measures this unit's objectives only.

## Part A — The legislative path (10 points)

1. (4 points) Trace the fictional School Lunch Nutrition Standards
   Act through all five stages of the legislative path, naming one
   friction point it could meet at each of stages 2, 3, and 5.
2. (3 points) A bill passes the House 301–134 and the Senate 62–38.
   The president vetoes it. Can Congress override? Show the
   arithmetic for both chambers and give the verdict.
3. (3 points) Explain in three sentences why a bill can have majority
   support in the Senate and still never reach a vote.

## Part B — Presidency and bureaucracy (10 points)

4. (4 points) Executive Order 9981 (1948): (a) quote or paraphrase
   its key sentence; (b) name the three sources of authority its
   opening cites; (c) state one thing it could do that Congress could
   not do as fast, and one limit on it.
5. (3 points) An agency proposes a rule on school-bus safety. List
   the notice-and-comment steps in order, and name the journal where
   the proposal and the final rule appear.
6. (3 points) In 2023 a court deferred to an agency's reasonable
   reading of an ambiguous statute. In 2025 the same question
   returns. Explain what changed after *Loper Bright* (2024) and
   what the court must do now.

## Part C — Courts and judicial philosophies (10 points)

7. (4 points) State Jackson's three zones from *Youngstown* (1952)
   in one sentence each, and place the steel seizure in the correct
   zone with one reason from the facts.
8. (3 points) A case reaches the Supreme Court by petition for
   certiorari. Explain the Rule of Four and why "declined to hear"
   is not a ruling.
9. (3 points) An originalist judge and a living-constitutionalist
   judge disagree about a new technology the framers never saw.
   State each judge's starting point in one sentence each, without
   saying which is right.

## Part D — Budget and the essential question (10 points)

10. (4 points) Using the FY2024 figures (receipts $4.919 trillion,
    outlays $6.752 trillion, deficit $1.833 trillion, net interest
    $0.950 trillion): (a) compute the deficit as a percentage of
    outlays; (b) compute net interest as a percentage of outlays;
    (c) state in one sentence what the interest figure means for
    the budget.
11. (6 points) **Essential-question essay (one page):** "Who really
    makes policy — Congress, the president, the bureaucracy, or the
    courts?" Argue a verdict with one paragraph of best evidence for
    each of two institutions, one paragraph for the institution you
    rank first, and a final paragraph stating one limitation of your
    verdict. Use at least two dated facts from this unit.

**Total: 40 points.** 36–40 = excellent; 30–35 = proficient; 22–29 =
developing (re-teach the weakest part with the adult); below 22 =
revise and retake after reteaching. The adult records the score and
the one objective to strengthen before Unit 06.
