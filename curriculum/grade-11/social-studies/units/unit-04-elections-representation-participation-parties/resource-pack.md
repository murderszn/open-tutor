# Resource Pack — Unit 04: Elections, Representation, Participation, and Political Parties

**Goal:** Give a grade-11 learner (ages ~16–17) direct access to the
constitutional text, the official election and campaign-finance
sources, and reputable explainers behind this unit's lessons, with
free no-account routes for every core objective.
**Checked:** 2026-11-03. Links marked *(search-result verified)* were
confirmed in search results on that date; links marked *(search
link)* are clearly labeled preferred-channel searches, not verified
items; links marked *(listed in an earlier pack; re-verify)* were
verified in an earlier unit's pack and are listed here for the adult
to re-confirm before assigning.

## Web references

1. *(listed in the Unit 02 pack as opened 2026-10-21; re-verify)*
   **National Archives — Constitution transcription (with
   amendments):** https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-transcript
   — the authoritative public-domain text used in every lesson
   (Article I, §§2, 4; Article II, §1; Amendments 12, 15, 19, 23, 24,
   26).
2. *(search-result verified 2026-11-03)* **National Archives —
   "Choosing a President: How the Electoral College Works":**
   https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/2012/fall/electoral-college.html
   — NARA's own explainer: 538 electors, 270 to win, December elector
   meetings, January congressional count, House contingent election
   (1800, 1824). Free, no account.
3. *(search-result verified 2026-11-03)* **Brennan Center for Justice
   — "The Electoral College, Explained":**
   https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/electoral-college-explained
   — plain-language explainer of allocation, winner-take-all, and the
   Maine/Nebraska method. Free, no account.
4. *(search-result verified 2026-11-03)* **National Constitution
   Center — "On this day, Bush v. Gore settles 2000 presidential
   race":**
   https://constitutioncenter.org/blog/on-this-day-bush-v-gore-anniversary
   — dated account of the 2000 Florida recount, the 537-vote certified
   margin, and the December 12, 2000 decision. Free, no account.
5. *(search-result verified 2026-11-03)* **Federal Election
   Commission — "Registering as a Super PAC":**
   https://www.fec.gov/help-candidates-and-committees/filing-pac-reports/registering-super-pac/
   — the FEC's own definition: independent expenditure-only committees
   may receive unlimited contributions but may not contribute to or
   coordinate with candidates. Free, no account.
6. *(search-result verified 2026-11-03)* **U.S. Census Bureau —
   2022 Congressional Election Voting Report release:**
   https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USCENSUS/bulletins/397ff84
   — 52.2% turnout in 2022, 69.1% registration, 2018 comparison (53.4%).
   The full report's tables carry the age-breakdown figures used in
   Lesson 05. Free, no account.
7. *(search-result verified 2026-11-03)* **Vote.gov — about the
   official voting portal:**
   https://vote.gov/about-us — Vote.gov directs voters to their state
   election websites; it does not itself register voters. Managed by
   the U.S. Election Assistance Commission. Free, no account. For
   registration steps, see also
   https://www.usa.gov/register-to-vote (search-result verified
   2026-11-03).
8. *(search link)* **Oyez — *Bush v. Gore* (2000), *Citizens United v.
   FEC* (2010), *Shelby County v. Holder* (2013), *Rucho v. Common
   Cause* (2019), *Baker v. Carr* (1962), *Reynolds v. Sims* (1964),
   *Shaw v. Reno* (1993)** — search `site:oyez.org Bush Gore`
   (and likewise for each case). Free, no account; plain-language
   case facts, holdings, and opinion summaries for Lessons 01, 02,
   and 04. The adult confirms each case page before assigning.
