# Project — The Participation Audit and Mock Election

**Due:** Week 18, Session 4 (defense and deliberation).
**Format:** Written brief (3–4 pages) or 10-minute presentation, plus
a ballot, rules sheet, and tally sheet. Work in a group where possible;
an individual learner plays all roles and records each decision.
**Nonpartisan rule:** The mock election votes on a **policy
question**, never on real candidates or parties. No learner is asked
to disclose how they would vote in a real election, and no real
learner data is published — all community figures are aggregate,
public, and dated, or explicitly fictional.

## The task

Your group is a nonpartisan civic audit team. You will (1) profile
participation in a real local community, (2) run a mock election on a
policy question for your class or group, and (3) write a brief
arguing what the audit and the mock election together suggest about
representation — with one lawful participation proposal.

## Part 1 — Participation audit (Weeks 16–17)

Choose one real local community (your town, county, or school
district — adult-approved). Using adult-verified, dated public
sources only (state/local election office reports, Census Bureau
data, local government sites — never social-media claims), record:

- The community's most recent general-election turnout, with its
  denominator stated (registered voters? citizen voting-age
  population?) and the source and access date.
- Registration rate, if published, with the same sourcing.
- Which offices were on the ballot, and whether the election was a
  presidential-year, midterm, or local-only election.
- One participation gap you can document (by age, by neighborhood,
  or by election type — only where the source publishes it in
  aggregate form).
- One sentence on what the source does *not* show (for example: it
  does not show *why* people stayed home).

If a figure cannot be verified from a public source, write "not
found in a verified public source" — do not estimate, and do not
substitute a national figure for a local one.

## Part 2 — Mock election (Week 18, Session 3)

1. **Choose a policy question** (adult-approved, genuinely contested,
   and nonpartisan in wording — for example: "Should the school
   district extend library evening hours?" or "Should the town fund
   a new bike lane on Main Street?"). Write the question neutrally:
   no loaded adjectives, no embedded argument.
2. **Write the rules sheet:** who may vote (the class/group), how
   ballots are secret, how the tally is checked (two counters, one
   observer), and how ties are handled (state the tie rule before
   voting).
3. **Run the vote:** secret ballots, one vote per voter, counted in
   public with the tally sheet. Record total votes, votes for each
   option, and turnout as a percentage of eligible group members.
4. **Debrief immediately:** Did anyone feel unable to vote freely?
   Did the question's wording favor one side? Did turnout differ
   from what you expected, and why? Record the answers honestly —
   a debrief that reports problems scores higher than one that
   claims perfection.

## Part 3 — Brief (due Week 18, Session 4)

Your brief (or presentation) must contain:

1. **The audit** (Part 1 findings, with sources and dates).
2. **The mock election** (question, rules, tally, turnout, debrief).
3. **The argument** (2 pages or equivalent): what do the audit and
   the mock election together suggest about representation in your
   community? Use at least two lessons' evidence (for example,
   turnout gaps from Lesson 05 and party/group mobilization from
   Lessons 03–04) and at least one counterargument you answer.
4. **One participation proposal:** a lawful, nonpartisan action that
   could raise informed participation on your policy question (for
   example, a registration drive, a public information session, or a
   youth advisory seat with a defined role). State which participation
   explanation it targets (resources, mobilization, rules, or
   attitudes), how you would measure success, and what could go wrong.
5. **Evidence log:** every factual claim tied to a source with an
   access date; every fictional or illustrative figure labeled
   fictional.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Audit accuracy | Every figure sourced, dated, denominator stated; gaps and limits named | Most figures sourced and dated; denominator usually stated | Some figures sourced; denominators or dates missing | Figures unsourced, estimated, or national figures substituted for local ones |
| Mock election integrity | Neutral question, secret ballots, two-counter tally, honest debrief including problems | Neutral question, secret ballots, tally recorded, brief debrief | Question slightly loaded or tally unchecked; debrief thin | Vote on candidates, non-secret ballots, or no tally/debrief |
| Argument quality | Uses ≥2 lessons' evidence, answers a counterargument, distinguishes fact from judgment | Uses 2 lessons' evidence; counterargument mentioned | One lesson's evidence; no counterargument | Assertions without evidence |
| Proposal | Lawful, nonpartisan, targets one explanation, measurable, risks named | Lawful and nonpartisan, targets one explanation, measurable | Vague or unmeasurable; explanation unclear | Partisan, unlawful, or missing |
| Evidence log | Every claim sourced with access date; fictional figures labeled | Most claims sourced; fictional figures labeled | Some sourcing; some unlabeled figures | No log, or invented "sources" |

**Scoring:** 17–20 = exemplary; 13–16 = proficient; 9–12 = developing;
5–8 = beginning. The teacher guide gives reteach moves for each
criterion below proficient.
