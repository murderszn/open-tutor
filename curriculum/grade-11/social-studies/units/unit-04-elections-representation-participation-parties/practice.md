# Practice — Unit 04: Elections, Representation, Participation, and Political Parties

Use with the lessons; answers are in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md)
only — never beside the questions. Work in order; later tasks assume
earlier ones. Figures marked "fictional" are practice data, not real
election results.

## Task 1 — Election-sequence sort (Lesson 01)

Sort these eight steps into nomination, general election, or
post-election count, in order: (a) electors meet in state capitals;
(b) a party holds a closed primary; (c) Congress counts electoral
votes; (d) voters cast ballots on the first Tuesday after the first
Monday in November; (e) a national convention nominates a candidate;
(f) a state certifies its slate of electors; (g) a caucus chooses
delegates; (h) a candidate wins 270 electoral votes.

## Task 2 — Elector arithmetic (Lesson 01)

Compute the electors for: (a) a state with 5 House seats; (b) a state
with 17 House seats; (c) a state with 1 House seat; (d) the District
of Columbia. Then: (e) under the district method, a 9-elector state
whose winner takes 5 of its 7 districts — how many electoral votes
does each candidate get?

## Task 3 — Apportionment check (Lesson 02)

A state has 2,400,000 people and 6 House seats. (a) Ideal district
population? (b) A proposed district has 460,000 people — how many
percent over the ideal, and is it defensible for a congressional
district? (c) A second proposed district has 400,000 people exactly —
what does that prove, and what does it not prove?

## Task 4 — Map the gerrymander (Lesson 02)

Fictional state, 4 districts × 100 voters, Party A 220 (55%), Party B
180 (45%). Map X: D1 A 80–B 20; D2 A 55–B 45; D3 A 55–B 45; D4 A 30–B 70.
(a) Seats for each party? (b) Which technique — packing, cracking,
or both — does Map X use, and where? (c) Draw or describe one
alternative map giving a 2–2 split, with totals shown.

## Task 5 — Party functions in the wild (Lesson 03)

For each headline (all fictional), name the party function: (a) "Party
convention adopts platform calling for expanded job training"; (b)
"Majority party names committee chairs, sets session calendar"; (c)
"Party registers 30,000 voters ahead of deadline"; (d) "Voters oust
governing party after budget crisis." One headline may illustrate two
functions — say so when it does.

## Task 6 — 1912 vs. 1992 (Lesson 03)

Complete the table and answer: (a) Roosevelt 1912 — popular vote,
electoral votes, states won; (b) Perot 1992 — popular vote, electoral
votes, states won; (c) in two sentences, why did the larger national
share in one case buy fewer electoral votes than the smaller share in
the other? (d) What would Perot have needed to win electoral votes?

## Task 7 — Classify the spending (Lesson 04)

Classify each as direct contribution, independent expenditure, or
coordinated spending (in-kind contribution), and state the rule:
(a) $3,500 from an individual to a candidate (2025–2026 cycle);
(b) a Super PAC ad designed with no campaign contact; (c) a union's
traditional PAC gives $5,000 to a candidate; (d) a corporation funds
an ad after sharing strategy with the campaign it supports; (e) a
nonprofit runs ads without disclosing donors — what is this called,
and what do its critics and defenders each argue?

## Task 8 — Media effects spotting (Lesson 04)

Name the effect — agenda-setting, framing, or horse-race coverage —
for each: (a) a week of lead stories on one factory closing; (b) two
outlets describe the same bill as "relief" and "giveaway"; (c) nightly
poll averages with no policy comparison; (d) a group stages a rally
timed for the evening news. For (d), name two effects it exploits.

## Task 9 — Turnout data task (Lesson 05)

Using Lesson 05's table: (a) rank 2010, 2014, 2018, 2020, 2022 by
turnout; (b) compute the 2020→2022 drop in points; (c) compute the
2018→2022 change in early/mail share; (d) a news story says "2022
turnout collapsed." Using two numbers, show why that wording is
misleading; (e) if 18–29-year-olds are 19.6% of the citizen population
but 11.7% of voters, how many points underrepresented are they?

## Task 10 — Theories applied (Lesson 05)

A school-board election draws 12% turnout, but a parents' association
with 300 members packs every board meeting. (a) Which theory is most
reassured, and why? (b) Which theory is most troubled, and why?
(c) Which of the four participation explanations best explains the
gap between the election and the meetings? Defend your choice in two
sentences.
