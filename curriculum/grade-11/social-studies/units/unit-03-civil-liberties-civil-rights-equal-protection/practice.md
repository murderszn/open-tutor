# Practice — Unit 03: Civil Liberties, Civil Rights, and Equal Protection

Complete the tasks for the session the adult assigns. Show reasoning,
not just answers: every ruling task names the test or clause applied.
All answers are in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) — kept separate from
these questions.

## Task 1 — Liberties/rights sort (W11S2)

Sort each item as LIBERTY or RIGHT and give one sentence of reasoning:

1. A state bans a newspaper from publishing the governor's tax returns.
2. A county assigns Black voters to a polling place 30 miles away while
   white voters vote at the town hall.
3. Congress requires a permit for any religious gathering of more than
   50 people, but no permit for any other gathering.
4. A state funds schools in one county at half the rate of a
   neighboring county with identical tax revenue, where the counties
   differ racially.
5. A city arrests a speaker for criticizing the mayor at a council
   meeting.
6. A state refuses to issue marriage licenses to interracial couples
   (*Loving v. Virginia*, 1967, fact pattern).

## Task 3 — Speech-scenario rulings (W12S2)

Rule on each scenario with the governing test (*Brandenburg* or
*Tinker* or an unprotected category). State the test, apply both
relevant parts, and give the ruling:

1. A student silently wears a button supporting a controversial cause;
   classmates debate it loudly but classes continue normally.
2. A speaker at a rally points at a nearby building and shouts, "Burn
   that building now — follow me!" The crowd surges toward it.
3. A student newspaper (school-sponsored, faculty-advised) is blocked
   from publishing an editorial criticizing school lunch contracts.
4. A leaflet falsely claims a local business owner committed a crime;
   the leaflet's author knew the claim was false.
5. A student posts a video from home on Saturday mocking the principal;
   on Monday, no disruption occurs at school.
6. A protester stands outside a clinic screaming threats at individual
   patients by name, inches from their faces.

## Task 4 — Scrutiny sorting (W13S2)

Assign each law to strict, intermediate, or rational-basis review and
justify the assignment in one sentence. Predict the likely outcome
where the lesson gives you enough to do so:

1. A state bans 18-year-olds from buying alcohol but allows 21-year-olds.
2. A city requires all police officers to be at least 5'8" tall.
3. A state awards state contracts preferentially to businesses owned by
   members of one racial group, to remedy past discrimination.
4. A school district provides free bus transport to public-school
   students but not to private-school students.
5. A state bans marriage between people of the same sex (pre-2015 fact
   pattern; decide under the tier framework only, then note in one
   sentence what *Obergefell v. Hodges* (2015) actually held).
6. A state charges higher university tuition to students whose parents
   live out of state.

## Task 5 — Campaign-strategy audit (W13S4 / project preparation)

Pick one campaign from Lesson 05 (Montgomery boycott, sit-ins,
Freedom Rides, Birmingham, Selma). Write a half-page audit:

1. What was the injustice, in the campaign's own terms?
2. Which strategy led — litigation, direct action, or legislation?
3. What did courts, Congress, or the executive actually do, and when?
4. What remained unchanged a decade later?

## Task 6 — Turnout arithmetic on a fictional dataset (W14S3)

**This dataset is fictional practice data, invented for arithmetic
only. Never cite it as real turnout.** Columns: `group`, `turnout_pct`
(share of the citizen voting-age population that reported voting, in
percent), `election_type` (presidential or midterm).

| group | turnout_pct | election_type |
|---|---|---|
| Group A | 68 | presidential |
| Group B | 52 | presidential |
| Group A | 48 | midterm |
| Group B | 36 | midterm |

1. Compute the Group A–Group B gap in percentage points for the
   presidential year. Show the subtraction.
2. Compute the same gap for the midterm year.
3. Compute each group's drop from presidential to midterm year, in
   percentage points.
4. State two things this fictional table cannot tell you about real
   elections (see Lesson 06's data limits: cause, and who is counted).
5. Now repeat question 1's computation with the real CPS table the
   adult obtained for Lesson 06, and record the table title, year, and
   access date beside your answer.

## Task 7 — Shelby County: two-sided brief (W14S4 preparation)

Write two paragraphs of equal length and equal care:

1. The majority's case in *Shelby County* (current data, equal state
   sovereignty, Congress's power to write a new formula).

   argument).

evidence you find most persuasive and the strongest objection to your
