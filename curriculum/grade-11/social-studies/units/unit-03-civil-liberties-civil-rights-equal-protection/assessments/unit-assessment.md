# Unit Assessment — Unit 03: Civil Liberties, Civil Rights, and Equal Protection

**Time:** ~60 minutes. **Total:** 50 points. Take in W14S5, after all
six lessons and the project defense. Answers and scoring guidance are
in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) — kept separate from these tasks.

## Part A — Short answers (20 points, 4 points each)

1. Distinguish civil liberties from civil rights, using one example of
   each that is *not* used in Lesson 01's worked examples.
2. Explain selective incorporation in three sentences: the mechanism
   (which clause), why it is called "selective," and one provision
   never formally incorporated by the Supreme Court.
3. A state law bans protests within 100 feet of a hospital entrance,
   applying to all protesters regardless of message. Analyze it under
   the time/place/manner framework: content-based or content-neutral,
   and what must the state show?
4. State *Plessy*'s rule and *Brown*'s holding, and explain in one
   sentence why identical funding would not have saved the segregated
   schools under *Brown*.
5. State what *Shelby County v. Holder* (2013) decided, what it
   expressly left intact, and what enforcement path remains for voting
   changes in formerly covered jurisdictions.

## Part B — Scenario rulings (15 points, 5 points each)

Rule on each scenario, naming the governing test or clause and
applying it. A ruling without the test earns at most 2 points.

6. A public university expels a student for a social-media post
   praising a violent extremist group, posted from home, with no
   evidence of any planned action. Apply *Brandenburg*.
7. A state offers tax exemptions to all nonprofits, then revokes the
   exemption of a religious charity that publicly criticizes state
   policy. Analyze under the religion clauses.
8. A state law sets different ages for men (21) and women (18) to
   purchase the same product. Assign the tier of scrutiny, state the
   test, and predict the outcome, citing *Craig v. Boren* (1976).

## Part C — Evidence-based argument (15 points)

9. **Prompt:** "Rights expand only when courts, movements, and
   legislatures act together; no single strategy has ever been enough."
   Defend or challenge this claim using **two** campaigns or statutes
   from Lessons 04–06 (for example, *Brown* and the Civil Rights Act
   of 1964, or Selma and the Voting Rights Act). Your answer must:
   - State a clear thesis (3 points).
   - Present evidence for each campaign, with dates (6 points).
   - State the strongest objection to your thesis and answer it with
     evidence (4 points).
   - Conclude by naming one thing the combined strategies left undone
     (2 points).

**Scoring:** 45–50 exemplary; 38–44 proficient; 30–37 developing;
below 30 revise with the adult (re-teach: incorporation mechanism,
*Brandenburg*/*Tinker* tests, tiers of scrutiny, and the
*Shelby County* holding — see [teacher-guide.md](../teacher-guide.md)).
