# Project — Two Roads to Rights

**Type:** evidence-based brief plus presentation/defense. **Time:**
launched W13S3; research W14S2; defense W14S4. **Deliverable:** a
written brief (1,200–1,800 words) or a 10-minute presentation (slides
or poster) with a one-page evidence log. **Adult supervision:**
required — the adult approves the campaign pair and every source
before drafting.

## The task

Rights have expanded by more than one road. Choose **one litigation-led
campaign** and **one legislation/movement-led campaign** from the
pairs below (or propose a pair the adult approves), and answer the
unit's essential question for both: *when one person's liberty
collides with another person's equality, who decides — and by what
rule?*

Suggested pairs (pick one pair):

- **A.** *Brown* litigation ladder (1938–1954) × Civil Rights Act of
  1964 (legislation).
- **B.** NAACP school-desegregation litigation × Selma and the Voting
  Rights Act of 1965 (movement + legislation).
- **C.** *Tinker* student-speech litigation (1969) × a local
  free-speech controversy the adult selects and dates (movement /
  public deliberation).

## Steps

1. **Compelling question (W13S3).** Write one compelling question
   covering both campaigns (for example: "Which road — courts or
   Congress — changed daily life faster, and for whom?") plus two
   supporting questions, one per campaign.
2. **Source plan (W13S3).** For each campaign, list at least three
   sources: one primary source (amendment text, opinion excerpt,
   statute section, or movement document — linked, never copied beyond
   brief quotation), one reputable secondary source, and one source
   that disagrees with or complicates your position. Record access
   dates for every source.
3. **Evidence log (W14S2).** For each campaign, log at least four
   evidence items: the injustice as contemporaries described it, the
   strategy that led, the institution that acted (court, Congress,
   executive, movement), and the outcome with a date. Flag any figure
   (turnout, registration, damages) with its source and date; undated
   figures are returned.
4. **Draft (W14S2).** Structure the brief: introduction (compelling
   question); Campaign 1 (strategy, evidence, outcome, limit);
   Campaign 2 (same structure); comparison (which road changed what,
   for whom, how fast, and what each left undone); conclusion (your
   answer to the compelling question, with the strongest objection
   stated and answered).
5. **Defense (W14S4).** Present to the adult (and, if available, a
   small audience). Defend one claim under questioning, including a
   question from the opposing side of your verdict.

## Materials

- This unit's lessons and [resource-pack.md](resource-pack.md)
- Notebook or document for the evidence log; presentation materials
  of the learner's choice (slides or poster)
- Adult-approved primary sources (National Archives transcriptions,
  Congress.gov Constitution Annotated, govinfo statute texts)

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | Full marks | Partial marks | No / weak marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compelling + supporting questions (6 pts) | One compelling question covers both campaigns; two focused supporting questions | Question covers one campaign only, or supporting questions are vague | No question, or questions unrelated to the campaigns |
| Evidence quality and dating (10 pts) | ≥4 logged items per campaign; primary + secondary + complicating source per campaign; every figure sourced and dated | Evidence present but thin (2–3 items), or some figures undated | Evidence missing, unsourced, or invented |
| Strategy analysis (8 pts) | Each campaign's leading strategy named with its limit; the combination thesis (what each road could not do alone) applied | Strategies named but limits missing, or combination thesis asserted without evidence | Strategies confused or absent |
| Comparison and verdict (8 pts) | Comparison addresses speed, reach, and who benefited; verdict answers the compelling question with evidence | Comparison partial (one dimension only); verdict asserted without evidence | No comparison, or verdict contradicts the evidence shown |
| Objection handled fairly (4 pts) | Strongest objection stated in its best form and answered with evidence | Objection stated weakly or answered with assertion | No objection, or caricature of the other side |
| Presentation / defense (4 pts) | Clear, organized, within time; defends one claim under questioning | Understandable but disorganized or over time; defense evasive | Incoherent, or defense refuses the question |

**Scoring:** 36–40 exemplary; 30–35 proficient; 24–29 developing;
below 24 revise with the adult (re-teach: evidence dating and the
strategy/limit distinction — see [teacher-guide.md](teacher-guide.md)).

## Nonpartisanship and safety notes

- Present contested verdicts (for example, on *Shelby County* or on
  race-conscious remedies) with both sides sourced; the adult checks
  that no side is caricatured.
- Movement documents still under copyright are linked or briefly
  quoted, never reproduced in full.
- Sensitive material (discrimination, violence against demonstrators)
  is handled textually and with adult preview; the learner may choose
  the reflection alternative for any distressing source.
