# Project — The Founding Legitimacy Report

**Driving question:** *From which theory did the American founders claim
their government's legitimacy — and how well does the founding evidence
support that claim?*

**Format (learner's choice):** a 3–5 page written report **or** a 10-minute
oral presentation with a one-page evidence handout. The adult approves the
format at launch.

**Timeline:** launched W3S4 · research in investigation sessions
(W3S4, W4S4, W5S3) · draft W6S3 · defense/delivery W6S4.

## Requirements

Your report must:

1. **State a thesis** answering the driving question in 1–2 sentences
   (e.g., "The founders claimed social-contract legitimacy, and the
   Declaration and ratification debates support the claim — but the
   exclusion of enslaved people and the Articles' collapse show the claim
   was incompletely realized").
2. **Use at least three primary-source excerpts** (each under 75 words,
   quoted or closely paraphrased with citation), drawn from at least two
   of: the Declaration of Independence, the Articles of Confederation, the
   Constitution/Bill of Rights, Federalist No. 10, Brutus No. 1. All are
   public domain and linked in the [resource pack](resource-pack.md).
3. **Include one visual**: either the trace-the-power diagram (Lesson 05)
   or an annotated version of the two-ledgers table (Lesson 06), with a
   2–3 sentence caption explaining what it shows.
4. **Address a counterclaim**: present the strongest evidence *against*
   your thesis (at least two pieces), then explain why your thesis still
   holds — or revise the thesis honestly.
5. **Conclude with a judgment**: in one paragraph, say what the founding's
   legitimacy story means for how Americans today should evaluate their
   government's claims. Nonpartisan framing required (institutions and
   principles, not parties or candidates).

## Milestones

| Session | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| W3S4 | Compelling question + source plan: which three excerpts, and what each is expected to prove |
| W4S4 | Evidence log: excerpts collected, each with a 2-sentence "what it proves" note |
| W5S3 | Visual draft + counterclaim paragraph draft |
| W6S3 | Full draft; adult feedback on thesis arguability and evidence fit |

## Rubric

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Thesis** | Arguable, precise, answers the driving question; governs the whole report | Answers the question; arguable | Vague or merely descriptive ("the founding was important") | Missing or incoherent |
| **Evidence** | 3+ excerpts, well-chosen, each explicitly tied to the thesis with citations | 3 excerpts, tied to thesis, citations present | Fewer than 3, or excerpts decorate rather than prove | No primary-source evidence |
| **Source reasoning** | For each excerpt: maker, audience, purpose noted; limits acknowledged | Maker/purpose noted for most excerpts | Excerpts treated as neutral facts | No source analysis |
| **Counterclaim** | Strongest opposing evidence presented fairly (2+ pieces) and answered or thesis revised | Counterclaim present and addressed | Counterclaim straw-manned or dismissed | No counterclaim |
| **Visual** | Diagram/table accurate, captioned, referenced in the argument | Accurate and captioned | Present but inaccurate or unreferenced | Missing |
| **Conclusion** | Judgment connects founding evidence to present-day evaluation of legitimacy claims; nonpartisan | Connects past to present; nonpartisan | Generic ("we should learn from history") | Missing or partisan |
| **Mechanics** | Clear organization; quotations under 75 words and cited; public-domain sources only | Minor issues | Disorganized or citation gaps | Incoherent or uses copyrighted text |

**Scoring:** 24–28 = exemplary; 18–23 = proficient (meets the unit's
expectations); 12–17 = developing (revise one criterion with the adult);
below 12 = re-teach and resubmit. The adult records the score and the
revision plan privately.

## Adult directions

- Preview all sources the learner selects; confirm excerpts stay under 75
  words and come from the public-domain set.
- Keep the framing nonpartisan: redirect any present-day example toward
  institutions and principles.
- The defense Q&A (W6S4) should probe the counterclaim most: "What is the
  strongest evidence against your thesis, and why doesn't it change your
  mind?" A learner who revises the thesis under questioning has done
  *better*, not worse — score accordingly.
