# Unit Assessment — Unit 04: Momentum, Collisions, and Impulse

**Unit 04 · Session S18 (Parts A–C) and Session S19 (Part D, midyear
checkpoint) · ~50 minutes per sitting**
**Covers unit objectives 1–6; Part D is cumulative over Units 01–04.**
Right is positive. Show working for every numerical answer. Open
responses are scored on reasoning, not on matching a single sentence:
the answer key gives acceptable reasoning and common errors.

## Part A — Momentum and impulse (objectives 1–2)

A1. A 0.25 kg ball moves right at 12 m/s, hits a wall, and rebounds
left at 9.0 m/s. The contact lasts 0.035 s. (a) Find the ball's
change in momentum. (b) Find the average force the wall exerted on
the ball, with sign and magnitude.

A2. A force–time graph for a 1.0 kg object at rest is a rectangle of
20 N for 0.30 s, followed immediately by a triangle rising from
20 N to 0 over 0.20 s. (a) Find the total impulse. (b) Find the
object's final speed. (Hint: split the area into a rectangle and a
triangle; the triangle's base is 0.20 s and its height is 20 N.)

## Part B — Collisions (objectives 3–4)

B1. A 3.0 kg cart moves right at 4.0 m/s and a 2.0 kg cart moves left
at 2.0 m/s. They stick together. (a) Find the common velocity.
(b) Find the kinetic energy before and after. (c) Find the energy
lost and name where it went.

B2. A 1.0 kg cart moves right at 6.0 m/s toward a 1.0 kg cart at
rest, and they collide elastically. (a) State the velocities after
the collision, using the equal-mass exchange rule. (b) Verify both
conservation laws numerically. (c) In one sentence, say why this
collision is classified elastic.

B3. **Data table (fictional practice data, not learner
observations).** Two carts collide and stick together. Three trials
are given:

| Trial | Cart 1 before | Cart 2 before | Joined carts after |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2.0 kg at +3.0 m/s | 1.0 kg at 0 m/s | 3.0 kg at +2.0 m/s |
| 2 | 2.0 kg at +2.0 m/s | 2.0 kg at −1.0 m/s | 4.0 kg at +0.50 m/s |
| 3 | 1.0 kg at +4.0 m/s | 3.0 kg at −2.0 m/s | 4.0 kg at −0.50 m/s |

For each trial, compute total momentum before and after and state
whether conservation holds within rounding. (Trial 2's after
velocity is exact: total before is `4.0 − 2.0 = 2.0`, total mass
4.0 kg, so `2.0 / 4.0 = 0.50 m/s`.)

## Part C — Safety design and tool choice (objectives 5–6, project)

C1. A 70 kg occupant travels at 14 m/s and stops. (a) Find the
momentum change. (b) Find the average force for a 0.030 s stop and
for a 0.30 s stop. (c) A manufacturer claims a new restraint "cuts
the momentum change in half." Explain why that claim cannot be
true for a full stop, and restate what a real restraint can do.

C2. A 2.0 kg cart moves at 5.0 m/s and sticks to a 3.0 kg cart at
rest. The joined carts then slide to rest under a 5.0 N friction
force. (a) Find the common velocity after the collision. (b) Find
the sliding distance. (c) Name the tool used in each part.

C3. **Project reflection (open response).** Describe your
protective device's best result and its control result. Did the
device protect the payload better than the control? Cite one
number from your test log (a damage score, or a computed force)
and one limit of your evidence. (If you used the simulation
alternative, cite one simulated before/after total instead.)

## Part D — Midyear checkpoint, Units 01–04 (Session S19)

D1. **Motion (U01).** A ball is launched horizontally at 8.0 m/s
from a height of 5.0 m. (a) Find the time to fall (`g = 9.80 m/s²`,
vertical motion only). (b) Find the horizontal range.

D2. **Forces (U02).** A 4.0 kg block rests on a frictionless incline
at 30° to the horizontal. Find the component of its weight acting
down the slope and its acceleration down the slope.

D3. **Energy (U03).** A 0.50 kg ball is dropped from 2.0 m. (a) Find
its speed at impact. (b) Find its momentum at impact.

D4. **Momentum synthesis (U04).** A 0.50 kg ball moving right at
6.0 m/s collides with a 0.50 kg ball at rest and they stick
together. Find the common velocity, the kinetic energy before and
after, and the energy lost. Then state, in one sentence, which
quantity was conserved and which was not.
