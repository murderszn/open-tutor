# Unit 04 — Momentum, Collisions, and Impulse

**Grade 11 · Science · Physics pathway · Weeks 15–18 of the course year**
(draft, delivered 2026-11-03 for issue #51; not educator-reviewed)

## Overview

Units 01–03 taught the learner to measure motion, draw the forces on an
object, and account for energy. This unit answers a different question:
**when two things hit, what is transferred, what is conserved, and what
can an engineer change about the outcome?** The answer is built in four
moves:

1. **Momentum** — mass times velocity, a vector: `p = mv`, in
   kg·m/s. It measures how hard a moving object is to stop.
2. **Impulse** — a force acting over a time changes momentum:
   `J = FΔt = Δp`. The same momentum change can come from a huge
   force acting briefly or a small force acting longer.
3. **Conservation** — in a system with no net external force, total
   momentum before a collision equals total momentum after it, whether
   the collision is bouncy or sticky. Kinetic energy is conserved only
   in the elastic case.
4. **Safety engineering** — because `F_avg = Δp / Δt`, extending the
   collision time lowers the average force for the same momentum change.
   That single idea explains crumple zones, airbags, helmets, and the
   unit's protective-device project.

The essential question running through all four weeks is: **if I cannot
change how much momentum must be lost in a crash, what can I change
about how it is lost — and how do I prove my design works?**

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–03 and the Weeks 1–2 diagnostic (re-taught in context
where insecure):

- **From Unit 01:** vector direction and sign conventions; reading
  velocity–time graphs; significant figures; `g = 9.80 m/s²`.
- **From Unit 02:** Newton's second and third laws; free-body diagrams;
  net force as the cause of acceleration.
- **From Unit 03:** kinetic energy `KE = ½mv²`; work–energy reasoning;
  energy lost to deformation, heat, and sound.
- **From grade-10 math:** rearranging formulas; solving a two-unknown
  problem from two equations (used only in the Lesson 04 extension).

Session S1 re-checks the sign convention (right = positive) before any
collision arithmetic begins. All collisions in this unit are
one-dimensional, between macroscopic objects at ordinary speeds — the
assessment boundary carried by HS-PS2-2.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Calculate momentum `p = mv` for a single object, with correct sign
   for direction and correct units (kg·m/s), and compare the momenta of
   two objects to decide which is harder to stop. (track objective 6)
2. Calculate impulse from a constant force, from a force–time graph
   area, and from a velocity change, and use `J = Δp` to find an
   unknown force, time, mass, or velocity change. (track objective 6)
3. Apply conservation of momentum to one-dimensional two-body
   collisions and explosions: write the before/after momentum equation,
   solve for an unknown velocity (including recoil and
   stick-together cases), and state the system and the
   no-net-external-force condition the result depends on.
   (track objective 6)
4. Distinguish elastic, inelastic, and perfectly inelastic collisions
   by testing both momentum and kinetic energy before and after;
   compute kinetic energy lost in an inelastic collision and name
   where that energy goes. (track objective 6)
5. Evaluate a collision-safety design quantitatively: compute the
   average force from `Δp` and `Δt`, explain why extending collision
   time reduces peak force without changing the momentum change, and
   judge a design against measured damage or stopping-time criteria.
   (track objective 6)
6. Plan and carry out a safe bench investigation: write a testable
   question, identify variables, record data in tables with units,
   compare measured outcomes with a prediction, evaluate error, and
   communicate an evidence-based conclusion — with the adult approving
   the procedure first. (track objective 12)

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: the Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS),
high school (9–12) band. Codes and descriptions were re-verified
2026-11-03 against the NSTA NGSS display for the HS-PS2 disciplinary
core ideas and the California Department of Education's NGSS record
for HS-PS2-3; descriptions here are short paraphrases, not
reproductions of the standards text. **This crosswalk is a planning
reference, not a claim of state adoption, accreditation, or
certification of alignment.**

- **HS-PS2-2** — use mathematical representations to support the
  claim that the total momentum of a system of objects is conserved
  when there is no net force on the system. This unit is its main
  home: Lessons 03–04 build it from two-body collisions, and the
  project and assessments test it. Its clarification statement
  emphasizes quantitative conservation and its qualitative meaning;
  its assessment boundary limits assessment to systems of two
  macroscopic bodies moving in one dimension — every collision
  problem here respects that boundary.
- **HS-PS2-3** — apply scientific and engineering ideas to design,
  evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a
  macroscopic object during a collision. Lesson 05 and the project
  are its home: the learner designs a protective device, tests it
  against a damage criterion, and refines it. Its clarification
  statement names football helmets and parachutes as example devices
  and names determining protection success and modifying the design
  as the evaluation/refinement moves; its assessment boundary
  limits assessment to qualitative evaluations and/or algebraic
  manipulations — the project rubric follows exactly that.
- Science and engineering practices used in this unit: developing
  and using models (momentum/impulse models, Lessons 01–04); planning
  and carrying out investigations (project, S15–S16); analyzing and
  interpreting data (Lessons 02–04, project); using mathematics and
  computational thinking (every lesson); constructing explanations
  from evidence (Lessons 03–06, project); designing solutions
  (Lesson 05, project).
- `g = 9.80 m/s²` throughout, as in Units 01–03. All practice numbers
  are stated practice values, not measured material constants, unless
  an investigation measures an effective value and says so. The
  generated illustration is an AI illustration, not a photograph of a
  real experiment and not measured data.
