# Project — Protective-Device Investigation

**Unit 04 · Sessions S15–S16 (build and test; write-up) · HS-PS2-3**
**Adult approval required before any drop.** Simulation alternative
below; no human test subjects, ever.

## Question

How can a small, light device extend the stopping time of a falling
payload enough to protect it — and how do you prove, from measured
results rather than from a claim, that your design worked?

## Materials (bench version)

- Payload: one raw egg in a sealed zip bag (contains mess if it
  breaks), **or** the allergy-safe alternative: a 0.20 kg modelling
  clay ball with a thin cracker pressed on top as a break indicator.
  The adult chooses; if an egg is used, check for egg allergy first
  and wash hands and surfaces afterwards.
- Device materials from home (choose freely within the constraints):
  cotton wool, bubble wrap, paper, card, string, rubber bands, sponge,
  straws. No glass, no sharp metal, no mains-powered tools.
- A 1.5 m drop height measured with a tape measure (adult holds the
  device at shoulder height over the drop zone; nobody stands on
  furniture to reach the height).
- Tape measure, kitchen scale, notebook, phone camera for slow-motion
  timing if available (optional, adult-operated).
- Eye protection for everyone near the drop zone if a device could
  shed fragments.

## Constraints

- Device plus payload: total mass no more than 0.50 kg; no dimension
  longer than 30 cm.
- The device must be reusable for at least a second drop after a
  minor repair, or the report must say it is single-use and why.
- The drop zone is clear of people, pets, and breakables; the adult
  gives a countdown and checks the zone before every drop.

## Procedure

1. **Predict (before building).** Compute the impact speed from
   1.5 m (`v = √(2gh)`), the payload momentum at impact (payload
   mass 0.20 kg), and the average force for a hard 0.010 s stop.
   Write the prediction down; it is the comparison for your test.
2. **Design.** Sketch the device, label the cushioning parts, and
   write one sentence per part saying how it extends stopping time
   or spreads force. State how you will judge success: the damage
   scale in step 4.
3. **Build and weigh.** Build within the constraints; record total
   mass and largest dimension.
4. **Test (three drops).** Drop from 1.5 m onto a hard floor, same
   orientation each time if the design has an orientation. After
   each drop record: survived / cracked / broken (egg) or intact /
   cracked / shattered (cracker indicator), plus any device damage.
   Damage scale: 0 = undamaged, 1 = minor crack or dent, 2 = broken.
5. **Refine and retest.** Change exactly one feature (more
   cushioning, a different orientation, a stiffer frame), state why
   the change should help in terms of stopping time, and repeat
   three drops.
6. **Control (required).** Drop the bare payload (in its bag, or the
   clay ball with cracker and no device) once from the same height
   and record the damage score. A design claim means nothing without
   this comparison.

## Deliverables

1. Design sketch with labels and a one-sentence purpose per part.
2. Prediction calculation (impact speed, momentum, hard-stop
   average force).
3. Test log: a table with units for every drop (design version,
   drop number, damage score, device damage, notes).
4. Refinement note: what changed, why, and what the retest showed.
5. Conclusion (one paragraph): did the device protect the payload
   better than the control, what evidence shows it, and what would
   you change next? Include the honest limits: a damage score is not
   a force measurement, and three drops are a small sample.

## Simulation / observation alternative

If materials, space, or supervision are inadequate, do not improvise
a bench test. Instead, use PhET "Collision Lab" (adult previews):
set two balls with known masses and velocities, record total
momentum and kinetic energy before and after at elasticity 0% and
100%, and write a design report explaining how a device that
extends collision time would change the force in a real version of
each simulated collision, using `F_avg = Δp / Δt` with two stated
stopping times. The same rubric applies, with "simulated trials"
replacing drop scores.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (4) | Competent (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Design reasoning | Every part's purpose is tied to stopping time or force spreading, with a stated trade-off | Most parts explained in impulse terms | Parts listed but reasoning is generic ("it is soft") | No reasoning, or reasoning contradicts `F = Δp/Δt` |
| Prediction math | Impact speed, momentum, and hard-stop force all computed correctly with units and signs | Two of three correct, or a small rounding slip | One correct, or units missing throughout | No usable prediction before testing |
| Test honesty | All drops logged including failures; control drop present; orientation and height stated | Drops logged with one gap, control present | Selective logging, or control missing | Results claimed without a log |
| Refinement | One feature changed for a stated impulse reason and retested; result compared fairly | A change made and retested with partial reasoning | Change made without a reason or without retest | No refinement attempted |
| Communication | Conclusion answers the question with evidence, names limits, and states a next change | Conclusion supported, limits partly named | Conclusion restates results without a claim | No conclusion, or a claim the data contradict |

Score: 17–20 strong; 13–16 competent; below 13: reteach Lesson 05
and repeat the refinement step before the unit assessment.

## Safety and adult notes

- The adult previews the procedure, approves the drop height and
  zone, and stops the test if a device sheds sharp fragments.
- Never drop a device toward a person, and never test a design by
  hitting or catching with the body.
- Wash hands and surfaces after egg tests. If the learner has an egg
  allergy, use the clay-and-cracker alternative only.
- A broken device is data, not failure: record it, explain it in
  impulse terms (the stop was too short, or the force concentrated
  on one point), and refine.
