# Practice Sets — Unit 01

Student practice for Sessions S2, S4, S7, S12, S14. Work in the
notebook; the adult scores same-day. All solutions are in
`answer-key.md` (adults only).

**How to use these sets:** each set targets its lesson, but every set
ends with two *retrieval* items from earlier lessons — spaced review is
part of the plan, not extra credit. Carry units through every step;
state significant figures on final answers.

---

## Practice Set A — SI units, dimensions, significant figures (S2)

A1. A metal's density is given as 2.70 g/cm³. Convert it to kg/m³
using dimensional analysis. Show the canceling units.

A2. A light-year is approximately 9.46 × 10¹² km. Express it in meters
with correct significant figures.

A3. a) Multiply: 4.20 m × 3.1 m. b) Add: 15.5 cm + 2.25 cm. Report
each with correct significant figures and name the rule you used.

A4. A learner proposes the formula `s = vt + ½at²` for displacement.
Check it dimensionally: show that every term has units of meters,
given v in m/s, t in s, a in m/s². (The ½ is exact.)

A5. (Retrieval) A ruler is marked in millimeters. You read a length as
12.3 cm. a) State the reading uncertainty. b) Convert 12.3 cm to
meters.

A6. (Retrieval) Two timers report 2.5 s and 2.50 s for the same event.
Which reading is more precise, and what does the extra digit claim?

## Practice Set B — accuracy, precision, uncertainty (S4)

B1. Seven mass readings (g): 102.1, 101.8, 102.4, 102.0, 101.9,
102.3, 102.1. Compute the mean, the range, and the ± report.

B2. A standard 10.00 mL volume is measured four times by each of two
pipettes. Pipette P: 9.98, 10.01, 9.99, 10.02 mL. Pipette Q: 10.45,
10.43, 10.47, 10.44 mL. For each pipette, classify the results as
accurate, precise, both, or neither, using the mean and range. Which
pipette has the systematic error?

B3. A thermometer is marked every 0.5 °C and reads 23.0 °C.
a) State the single-reading uncertainty.
b) Five readings: 23.0, 23.5, 23.0, 22.5, 23.0 °C. Give the ± report.

B4. A digital timer displays to 0.01 s. Five trials (s): 3.21, 3.24,
3.19, 3.23, 3.20. Report as value ± uncertainty, and say whether the
instrument limit or the trial scatter dominates.

B5. (Retrieval) Convert 45.0 min to seconds. Express the answer in
scientific notation with correct significant figures.

B6. (Retrieval) Compute 2.40 m × 1.5 m with correct significant
figures, and explain in one sentence why the answer has the precision
it does.

## Practice Set C — vectors (S7)

C1. Resolve a 50.0 m displacement at 60.0° above the +x axis into
components. Verify by recombining.

C2. Walk 8.0 m east, then 6.0 m north. Find the resultant displacement
(magnitude and direction) by tip-to-tail reasoning and by components.

C3. Vectors **A** = (10.0 m, 4.0 m) and **B** = (3.0 m, 9.0 m): compute **A** − **B** by
components, then its magnitude and direction. (This is Lesson 03's
3.6 restated — solve it without looking back, then check.)

C4. Two forces act on a bolt: 12.0 N due east and 5.00 N due north.
Find the resultant force (magnitude and direction). A washer needs at
least 12.5 N to seat — does this resultant suffice? (Three
significant figures.)

C5. (Retrieval) Convert 90.0 km/h to m/s with the factor-label setup.

C6. (Retrieval) A 3.00 kg block sits on a table. Is its mass a scalar
or a vector? What about its weight — compute it (g = 9.80 m/s²) and
state the direction that makes it a vector.

## Practice Set D — kinematics graphs and constant acceleration (S12)

D1. A velocity–time graph: 0 → 30.0 m/s linearly over 5.00 s, then a
constant 30.0 m/s for 10.0 s, then 30.0 → 0 m/s linearly over 4.00 s.
a) Describe the motion in words first.
b) Compute the total displacement from the areas.
c) Find the acceleration in each phase.

D2. An object starts from rest and accelerates at a constant
3.00 m/s² for 4.00 s.
a) Use the a–t area to find the change in velocity.
b) Find the displacement two ways (two different kinematic
equations) and confirm they agree.

D3. A sprinter accelerates from rest to 11.0 m/s over a 60.0 m dash
start (assume constant acceleration).
a) Find the acceleration.
b) Find the time for this phase.
c) Check: does Δx = ½at² return 60.0 m?

D4. An elevator starts from rest and accelerates upward at 2.00 m/s²
for 3.00 s. Find its velocity and displacement at t = 3.00 s. State
your sign convention.

D5. (Retrieval) Resolve 15.0 m/s at 25.0° above the horizontal.
(cos 25.0° = 0.90631, sin 25.0° = 0.42262.)

D6. (Retrieval) Three length readings: 12.1 cm, 12.4 cm, 12.2 cm.
Report as value ± uncertainty.

## Practice Set E — projectile motion (S14)

E1. A ball is launched horizontally from 2.00 m height at 8.00 m/s.
Compute the flight time and range (three significant figures).

E2. A ball is launched from ground level at 20.0 m/s, 50.0° above the
horizontal. Compute v_x, v_y₀, total flight time, maximum height, and
range.

E3. From level ground at fixed speed, launches at 30° and 60° land at
the same range. Explain why using the range formula
R = v₀²·sin(2θ)/9.80 — no full trajectory computation needed.

E4. A rock is thrown horizontally at 12.0 m/s from a 45.0 m cliff
(up = +).
a) Flight time and range.
b) The vertical velocity just before impact.
c) The impact speed (magnitude of the velocity vector).

E5. (Retrieval) A car accelerates from 12.0 m/s to 28.0 m/s over
160 m. Find the acceleration — and name the equation you chose and
why.

E6. (Retrieval) A displacement vector has components (9.0 m, 12.0 m).
Find its magnitude and direction.
