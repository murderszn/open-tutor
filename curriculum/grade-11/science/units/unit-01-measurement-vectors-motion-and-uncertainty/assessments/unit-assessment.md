# Unit Assessment — Unit 01: Measurement, Vectors, Motion, Uncertainty

**Session S19 · ~50 minutes · summative for objectives 1, 2, 3, 12**
Calculator allowed. Show all reasoning: equation choice, substitution
with units, and significant figures on every final answer.
Use g = 9.80 m/s². The investigation portfolio (project.md) is scored
separately by its rubric.

**UA1 (computation — objective 1).** A steel sample's density is
listed as 7.85 g/cm³. Convert it to kg/m³ by dimensional analysis,
showing the canceling units. Report with correct significant figures.

**UA2 (computation — objective 1).** A metal block has mass 52.4 g
and volume 6.5 cm³. Compute its density with correct significant
figures, and explain in one sentence which measurement limits the
result.

**UA3 (error analysis — objective 1).** The accepted density of
aluminum is 2.70 g/cm³. Four lab groups report (g/cm³):
- Group A: 2.69, 2.71, 2.70
- Group B: 2.95, 2.97, 2.94
- Group C: 2.40, 2.95, 2.70
a) Classify each group as accurate, precise, both, or neither.
b) For Group A, compute the mean and report it as value ± uncertainty
using the half-range method.
c) Group B's instructor suspects a miscalibrated balance. Is that
suspicion consistent with the data pattern? Explain in one or two
sentences.

**UA4 (computation — objective 2).** Three displacement vectors:
**A** = 20.0 m at 0° (along +x), **B** = 15.0 m at 90° (along +y),
**C** = 10.0 m at 180° (along −x). Add them by components and find
the resultant's magnitude and direction with a direction reference.

**UA5 (computation — objective 2).** A hiker walks 3.00 km due north,
then 4.00 km due east, then 2.00 km due south.
a) Find the resultant displacement (magnitude and direction).
b) What is the hiker's total distance walked? Why is it different
from the magnitude in (a)?

**UA6 (graph interpretation — objective 3).** A position–time table
for a cart: t (s): 0, 2.0, 4.0, 6.0, 8.0; x (m): 0, 6.0, 12.0, 12.0,
4.0.
a) Describe the motion in each interval in words.
b) Compute the velocity in each interval from the slopes.
c) Find the total displacement and the total distance traveled.

**UA7 (computation — objective 3).** A car traveling at 30.0 m/s
brakes with constant acceleration −5.00 m/s² until it stops.
a) How long does it take to stop?
b) How far does it travel while braking? Solve with a kinematic
equation and check with the average-velocity form.

**UA8 (computation — objective 3).** A soccer ball is kicked from
ground level at 16.0 m/s, 35.0° above the horizontal. Compute:
a) the initial velocity components,
b) the total flight time,
c) the maximum height,
d) the range.
(Three significant figures throughout.)

**UA9 (investigation reasoning — objective 12).** You want to test
whether launch angle affects the range of a projectile fired at a
fixed speed from ground level.
a) Write a testable question.
b) Identify the independent, dependent, and two controlled variables.
c) Sketch the data table you would use (column headings with units).
d) Name one specific source of error and state which direction it
would push your range measurements.

**UA10 (explain your reasoning — objective 3).** A classmate says:
"The horizontal and vertical motions of a projectile take turns —
first it flies forward, then it falls. That's why the path is curved."
Refute this in 3–4 sentences using the independence of motion and
one piece of evidence from the Launch Lab pattern (predicted vs.
measured range, or the mass check).
