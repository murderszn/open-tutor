# Unit 01 — Measurement, Vectors, Motion, and Uncertainty

**Grade 11 · Science · Physics pathway · Weeks 3–6 of the course year**
(draft, delivered 2026-10-14 for issue #51; not educator-reviewed)

## Overview

Physics is a science of *measured* motion, and every honest measurement
carries three things: a number, a unit, and a statement of what is not
known. This unit builds that habit in four moves:

1. **Measure honestly** — SI base units and prefixes, dimensional
   analysis with canceling units, significant-figure rules, and the
   accuracy–precision distinction with uncertainty estimated from
   repeated trials.
2. **Describe direction** — scalars vs. vectors; graphical tip-to-tail
   addition; resolving vectors into components with right-triangle
   trigonometry; resultant magnitude and direction.
3. **Describe motion** — reading position–time, velocity–time, and
   acceleration–time graphs (what slopes and areas mean); the
   constant-acceleration kinematic equations with `g = 9.80 m/s²`.
4. **Predict motion** — independence of horizontal and vertical motion;
   predicting a projectile's range, maximum height, and flight time,
   then testing the prediction in the Launch Lab bench investigation.

The essential question running through all four weeks is: **how do we
measure motion honestly — with the right units, the right math, and an
honest statement of what we do not know?**

## Prerequisites

From the Weeks 1–2 diagnostic (re-taught in context where insecure):

- **From grade-10 science:** measuring length, mass, and time with the
  correct instrument; recording to instrument precision; significant
  figures; accuracy vs. precision; multi-step unit conversions.
- **From grade-10 math:** right-triangle trigonometry (`sin`, `cos`,
  `tan`); coordinate graphing and reading slopes; proportional
  reasoning; rearranging formulas; multi-step numerical problems with
  units.
- **From grade 8:** forces as pushes and pulls; energy as the ability
  to do work (background only).

Session S5 re-checks right-triangle trigonometry before vectors begin;
anyone missing it gets targeted warm-ups there.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Convert between SI units using dimensional analysis with canceling
   units; record measurements to the instrument's precision; apply
   significant-figure rules for measured vs. exact numbers; and check a
   formula's dimensional consistency. (track objective 1)
2. Distinguish accuracy from precision; estimate the uncertainty of a
   repeated measurement from its range (about half the range); and
   report results as *value ± uncertainty* with matching decimal
   places. (track objective 1)
3. Add and subtract vectors graphically (tip-to-tail) and by components
   using right-triangle trigonometry; compute a resultant's magnitude
   and direction; and express the result with a unit and a direction
   reference. (track objective 2)
4. Interpret x–t, v–t, and a–t graphs (slope and area meanings); solve
   constant-acceleration problems carrying units and significant
   figures; and predict a projectile's range, maximum height, and
   flight time from its launch conditions, including the independence
   of horizontal and vertical motion. (track objective 3)
5. Plan and carry out a safe bench investigation: write a testable
   question, identify variables, record data in tables with units,
   compare predicted and measured values, evaluate sources of error
   and uncertainty, and communicate an evidence-based conclusion —
   with the adult approving the procedure first. (track objective 12)
