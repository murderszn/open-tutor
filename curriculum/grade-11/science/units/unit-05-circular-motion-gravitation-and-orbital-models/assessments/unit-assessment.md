# Unit Assessment — Unit 05: Circular Motion, Gravitation, and Orbital Models

**Unit 05 · Session S19 · one sitting · 40 points**
**Covers:** unit objectives 1–6 (Lessons 01–06 and the Orbit
Investigation). Calculator allowed. Show the equation you use
before substituting numbers. Open-response answers are scored
against the sample reasoning in `answer-key.md` (adults only).
This assessment is not standardized or validated.

## Part A — Short problems (20 points, 5 each)

**A1.** A carousel of radius 4.0 m makes one full turn in 8.0 s.
Find the speed and the centripetal acceleration of a rider at
the edge, and state the direction of the acceleration.

**A2.** An 800 kg car rounds a flat curve of radius 50 m at
20 m/s. What friction force is needed to hold the curve, and in
which direction does it act? If the tires can provide at most
5,000 N, does the car hold the curve?

**A3.** An 80.0 kg astronaut stands on Mars
(`g_mars = 3.71 m/s²`). What is their weight there, and what is
their mass there? Explain in one sentence why the two numbers
behave differently.

**A4.** Two charges, `+2.0 µC` and `−5.0 µC`, sit 0.25 m apart.
Compute the electrostatic force between them and state whether
it is attractive or repulsive.

## Part B — Data analysis: Kepler's third law (10 points)

The table gives real reference data for three planets
(from `resources/solar_system_planets.csv`).

| Planet | Distance from Sun (million km) | Orbital period (days) |
|---|---|---|
| Mercury | 57.9 | 88 |
| Earth | 149.6 | 365 |
| Jupiter | 778.6 | 4,333 |

**B1. (6 pts)** For each planet, compute the orbital speed in
km/s (`v = 2π r / T`) and the ratio `T² / r³` in SI units
(convert million km to meters and days to seconds first).
Present your work in a table with units.

**B2. (2 pts)** Compare your three `T² / r³` values with each
other and with the predicted constant
`4π² / (G M_sun) = 2.97 × 10⁻¹⁹ s²/m³`. What do you conclude?

**B3. (2 pts)** Mercury moves at about 48 km/s and Jupiter at
about 13 km/s. Explain this pattern with `v = √(G M / r)` in
two sentences.

## Part C — Explanation and orbital models (10 points)

**C1. (5 pts)** A learner says, "Astronauts on the ISS float
because there is no gravity in space, and the station stays up
because centrifugal force balances gravity." Correct both
claims. Your answer must use: the value of `g` at 400 km
(about 8.7 m/s²), the free-fall argument, and the correct
account of what provides the centripetal force.

**C2. (5 pts)** A geostationary satellite orbits at
`r = 4.22 × 10⁷ m` with a period of one day. (a) Explain why
its orbit must be above the equator. (b) Name two things the
two-body circular-orbit model leaves out, and for each one name
a real situation where the omission would matter.
