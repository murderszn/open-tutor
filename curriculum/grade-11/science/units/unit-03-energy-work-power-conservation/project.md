# Project — Energy-Conversion Device Build

**Sessions S16–S17 (Week 14) · HS-PS3-3 · track objective 12**
Adult approves the procedure before any build or trial begins.

## The challenge

Design, build, and refine a small device that converts one form
of energy into another **within these constraints**:

1. The device must be built from household or low-cost
   materials (cardboard, rubber bands, wooden skewers, bottle
   caps or CDs for wheels, string, tape, cups, water — no
   purchased kits, no mains electricity, no motors).
2. It must convert **stored energy into motion energy**:
   choose either (a) a **rubber-band car** (elastic potential
   energy → kinetic energy) or (b) a **waterwheel** (falling
   water's gravitational potential energy → rotational kinetic
   energy, driving a small lift or a paddle count).
3. It must be testable: one measurable input (stretch distance
   or water height/volume) and one measurable output (distance
   traveled, time, height lifted, or rotations).
4. It must be safe: eye protection during rubber-band trials
   (a snapped band stings), water trials outdoors or over a
   tray, adult present for all trials.

## Part 1 — Plan and first trials (S16)

1. **State the conversion** in one sentence: input form →
   output form, naming the device.
2. **Predict before building.** For a rubber-band car: state
   the spring's effective stiffness only if you can measure it
   (hang known masses, measure stretch — otherwise predict
   qualitatively: "more winds → more stored energy → farther
   travel"). For a waterwheel: compute the input energy from
   the water's mass and drop height (`PE = mgh`; 1 litre of
   water = 1.0 kg).
3. **Build, then run three identical trials.** Record every
   trial in a data table with units: input setting, output
   measurement, and anything that went wrong (wheel slip, band
   snag, spill).
4. **Compute the energy account for the best trial:**
   input energy (measured or honestly estimated — say which),
   output kinetic energy or lifted potential energy, and the
   difference as thermal/sound/wasted energy. State the
   efficiency as useful ÷ input.

## Part 2 — Refine and report (S17)

5. **Refine once, from evidence.** Pick the single change
   your data most supports (more winds, better axle alignment,
   larger wheel, straighter track, steadier pour) and run
   three more trials. State whether the refinement helped, with
   numbers.
6. **Write the report** (one page, handwritten or typed):
   conversion sentence, labeled sketch or photo-free diagram
   (draw it — do not photograph people), data tables with
   units, energy account with efficiency, refinement evidence,
   and a conclusion that answers: "Did the device convert
   energy as predicted, and where did the missing energy go?"
   Name one source of error that is *not* "human error."

## Simulation alternative

If materials or supervision are inadequate, replace the bench
build with a PhET "Energy Skate Park" investigation (adult
previews the sim first): set friction to zero, then to a
visible value, and record KE, PE, and thermal energy from the
bar graph at three track positions for each setting. The same
deliverables apply: data table, energy account, efficiency
comparison between the frictionless and friction runs, and a
conclusion about where the missing energy went. The
investigation demand is the same; only the apparatus changes.

## Deliverables

- One-sentence conversion statement.
- Data tables with units (three trials before, three after
  refinement).
- Energy account with efficiency, honestly labeled as
  measured or estimated.
- One-page report with sketch, refinement evidence, and
  conclusion.
- Adult sign-off that the procedure was approved before trials.

## Rubric (16 points)

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Solid | 2 — Partial | 1 — Weak | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Design within constraints (4)** | Device converts stored energy to motion using allowed materials; constraints named and met | Conversion correct; one minor constraint stretched | Conversion attempted but device barely functions | Device built but conversion unclear | No device |
| **Measurements (4)** | Three trials per phase, tables with units, failures recorded | Three trials, units mostly present | Fewer trials or missing units | One trial only, or no table | No data |
| **Energy account (4)** | Input, output, and thermal/wasted energy computed; efficiency = useful ÷ input; estimates labeled | Account complete; one arithmetic slip | Account attempted; efficiency inverted or missing | Numbers listed without accounting | No account |
| **Refinement & communication (4)** | One evidence-based refinement tested with data; conclusion answers the question and names a real error source | Refinement tested; conclusion mostly complete | Refinement claimed without data, or conclusion vague | Report incomplete | No report |

**Safety gate (pass/fail, separate from points):** eye
protection worn for rubber-band trials (or water contained for
waterwheel trials) and adult approval obtained before trials.
A failed gate means trials stop until fixed — it is not a
point deduction.

**Scoring guide:** 14–16: exceeds; 11–13: meets; 8–10:
approaching; below 8: revise with the adult (re-run one phase
with support). The rubric is an adult-assessed guide, not a
standardized score.
