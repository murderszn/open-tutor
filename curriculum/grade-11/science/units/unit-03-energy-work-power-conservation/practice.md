# Practice — Unit 03: Energy, Work, Power, and Conservation

Work every item with units and, where the item asks for a
change, with a sign. Check against `answer-key.md` only after
completing a full set. `g = 9.80 m/s²` throughout.

## Practice Set A — Work (Lesson 01)

1. A force of 75 N pushes a box 6.0 m in the direction of the
   push. Find the work done.
2. A force of 90 N acts at 60° to the displacement while an
   object moves 8.0 m. Find the work done. (`cos 60° = 0.500`.)
3. A learner holds a 200 N box motionless overhead for 30 s.
   How much physics work is done on the box? Explain in one
   sentence.
4. A 3.0 kg object falls 4.0 m straight down. Gravity pulls
   with force `mg`. How much work does gravity do? Positive
   or negative?
5. A crate is pushed 5.0 m by a 120 N horizontal force while
   friction of 50 N opposes the motion. Find the work done by
   the push, by friction, and the net work.
6. A rope pulls a sled with 60 N at 30° above the horizontal
   while the sled moves 10 m horizontally. Find the work done
   by the rope. (`cos 30° = 0.866`.)

## Practice Set B — Kinetic energy and the work–energy theorem (Lesson 02)

1. A 6.0 kg object moves at 5.0 m/s. Find its kinetic energy.
2. A net work of 200 J is done on a 4.0 kg cart starting from
   rest. Find its final speed.
3. A 1500 kg car travels at 12 m/s. (a) Find its kinetic
   energy. (b) If it brakes with a constant 6000 N force, how
   far does it travel before stopping?
4. A 2.0 kg object speeds up from 4.0 m/s to 10 m/s. Find its
   change in kinetic energy.
5. **Error analysis.** A learner writes: "The ball's speed
   doubled, so its kinetic energy doubled." Correct the error
   and give the right factor with a number example.
6. A 1.0 kg object moves at 6.0 m/s and then experiences a net
   work of −10 J. Find its final kinetic energy and final
   speed.

## Practice Set C — Gravitational potential energy (Lesson 03)

1. An 8.0 kg box is lifted 3.0 m. Find its gravitational
   potential energy gain.
2. A 0.50 kg book sits on a shelf 1.5 m above a desk (datum at
   the desk). Find its potential energy.
3. A crane lifts a 200 kg load 15 m. Find the potential energy
   gain.
4. A ball is carried up two different staircases to the same
   landing 4.0 m up. Is its potential energy gain the same by
   both routes? Why?
5. A 2.0 kg object sits 3.0 m below a chosen datum. Find its
   potential energy relative to that datum, and the change in
   potential energy in rising to the datum.
6. A 50 kg hiker climbs 120 m up a hill. Find the potential
   energy gain.

## Practice Set D — Elastic potential energy and conservation (Lesson 04)

1. A spring with `k = 300 N/m` is compressed by 0.10 m. Find
   the stored elastic potential energy.
2. A 2.0 kg ball is dropped from rest from 3.0 m (air
   resistance ignored). Find its speed just before hitting
   the ground.
3. A pendulum bob is released from 0.50 m above its lowest
   point. Find its speed at the lowest point.
4. **Bar-chart reading.** A system's total mechanical energy
   is 200 J. At one instant its PE bar reads 120 J. What does
   its KE bar read? If the system is a 2.0 kg ball, how high
   above the datum is it at that instant?
5. A spring (`k = 100 N/m`) compressed by 0.20 m launches a
   0.20 kg ball horizontally (ignore gravity during the short
   launch). Find the launch speed.
6. **Error analysis.** A learner claims: "Doubling the drop
   height doubles the impact speed." Correct the claim with
   the right factor.

## Practice Set E — Nonconservative forces and efficiency (Lesson 05)

1. A slide system starts with 600 J of potential energy at the
   top; the rider reaches the bottom with 450 J of kinetic
   energy. How much energy became thermal energy, and what is
   the slide's efficiency as a PE → KE converter?
2. A motor takes in 4000 J of electrical energy and delivers
   3000 J of useful mechanical energy. Find its efficiency
   and the wasted energy.
3. A 5.0 kg block slides down a rough ramp from a height of
   2.0 m and reaches the bottom at 5.0 m/s. Find the initial
   PE, the final KE, and the energy converted to thermal
   energy.
4. Two engines: engine A takes in 2000 J and delivers 1500 J;
   engine B takes in 3000 J and delivers 1800 J. Which is more
   efficient? Which delivers more useful energy?
5. A system's mechanical energy decreases by 80 J while
   sliding. What is `W_nc`, and how much thermal energy is
   gained?
6. A 1500 kg car brakes from 20 m/s to rest. How much kinetic
   energy becomes thermal energy in the brakes, tires, and
   road?

## Practice Set F — Power and energy budgets (Lesson 06)

1. A machine does 600 J of work in 12 s. Find its average
   power.
2. A motor lifts a 30 kg mass 6.0 m in 3.0 s. Find the work
   done and the average power.
3. An elevator motor lifts a 1000 kg car at a constant
   2.0 m/s. Find the power it must deliver (the lifting force
   equals the weight at constant velocity).
4. **Budget table.** A closed cart system's total energy is
   900 J. At one instant its KE is 500 J and its GPE is
   100 J. Find its thermal energy at that instant.
5. A 70 kg sprinter climbs 10 m of stairs in 20 s. Find the
   work done against gravity and the average power.
6. Two motors do the same 1000 J of work: one in 5.0 s, the
   other in 10 s. Find each motor's average power. Which is
   more powerful, and does it do more work?
