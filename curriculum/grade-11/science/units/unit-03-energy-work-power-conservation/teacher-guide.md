# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Energy, Work, Power, and Conservation

**Adults only.** This guide contains teaching notes, not
student answers. All solutions live in `answer-key.md`. Do not
link this page from student-facing pages.

## How to teach this unit

The unit's argument has three beats. First, work is defined
narrowly (Lesson 01) — learners who carry the everyday
"effort" meaning will over-assign work to held loads and
perpendicular forces, so spend the full guided practice on
signs before moving on. Second, energy accounting replaces
force tracking (Lessons 02–04) — the payoff moment is Lesson
04's image activity, where the skater's speed comes from one
subtraction instead of a kinematic chain; let the learner feel
that shortcut. Third, the account is made honest (Lessons
05–06) — friction's missing energy is found, not mourned, and
the spreadsheet budget turns the account into a model the
learner can reuse in the project.

Pacing risks: Lesson 06's spreadsheet setup can eat a whole
session if the tool is unfamiliar — do the paper table first
(S13), then transfer to the spreadsheet in S14. The project
needs its full two sessions; do not compress S16–S17 into one.

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — Work

- **Trap:** "I carried the box across the room, so I did work
  on it." The vertical holding force is perpendicular to the
  horizontal displacement; the horizontal work is done by the
  small forward force overcoming friction, not by the hold.
  Separate the two directions explicitly.
- **Check:** if a learner writes work without a sign, send
  them back — unsigned work is the root of most later errors.
- **Reteach trigger:** exit check Q2 wrong → re-do Worked
  example 3 before Lesson 02.

### Lesson 02 — Kinetic energy and the work–energy theorem

- **Trap:** KE proportional to speed. The guided practice's
  error-analysis item (item 5) is the cure; insist on a number
  example, not just "it's squared."
- **Tool choice:** drill the question "does the problem ask
  for a speed and give forces and distances?" — if yes, the
  theorem is the fast path. If it asks for a time, the theorem
  cannot answer; say so out loud.
- **Reteach trigger:** stopping-distance scaling wrong →
  re-work Worked example 3 at both speeds.

### Lesson 03 — Gravitational potential energy

- **Trap:** PE as an absolute property. Require "datum: ___"
  written at the top of every PE problem for the first two
  weeks. Negative PE below the datum is correct, not an error.
- **Check:** any answer to a *change* question that depends on
  the datum is wrong — teach the learner to test their own
  answer by re-choosing the datum.

### Lesson 04 — Elastic potential energy and conservation

- **Image activity:** the generated illustration is a scene,
  not a data source. If the learner tries to estimate the ramp
  height from the picture, redirect to the problem text (3.0 m).
  The text-only alternative carries the same information.
- **Trap:** speed proportional to height. The image activity's
  part (c) exists to break this: half the height, equal energy
  split, but speed 5.4 m/s vs. 7.7 m/s at the bottom.
- **Bar chart:** the SVG values are computed (98 J total);
  have the learner re-derive one bar before trusting the chart.

### Lesson 05 — Nonconservative forces and efficiency

- **Language:** insist on "converted to thermal energy," never
  "lost" without a destination. "Lost" is allowed only in the
  full phrase "lost from the mechanical account."
- **Trap:** efficiency inverted (input ÷ useful). The
  definition "useful ÷ input, always ≤ 100%" is the check.
- **Extension honesty:** real-world efficiencies vary; the
  home-audit rankings are estimates, not measurements — say so.

### Lesson 06 — Power and budgets

- **Trap:** power vs. energy ("more powerful" for "more
  energy"). The independent-practice item 4 (100 W × 60 s vs.
  1000 W × 1 s) is the cleanest correction.
- **Budget discipline:** an unbalanced row is a finding — a
  missing component or a bad measurement. Do not let the
  learner force a balance by adjusting the total.
- **HS-PS3-1 boundary:** keep budgets to two or three
  components, as the standard's assessment boundary requires.

## Project notes (S16–S17)

- **Safety gate is not optional:** eye protection for
  rubber-band trials; water over a tray or outdoors; adult
  approval before trials. A failed gate stops trials.
- **Honest estimates:** if a learner cannot measure a spring
  constant, the input energy is an *estimate* — the rubric
  rewards labeling it as such, not inventing precision.
- **Refinement:** "more winds" is the most common choice and
  is fine if the data supports it; "better axle alignment" is
  often the real winner. Either is acceptable with numbers.
- **Simulation alternative:** the PhET route must still
  produce a data table, an energy account, and an efficiency
  comparison — not just "I played with the sim."

## Differentiation

- **Support:** allow oral answers for the applied tasks;
  provide the three-case work summary (Lesson 01) and the
  bar-chart template (Lesson 04) as permanent references;
  extend time on the project build.
- **Extension:** the force–distance area idea (Lesson 06
  extension) leads naturally into variable-force work; the
  Unit 08 enrichment picks it up. Strong learners may also
  derive `v = √(2gh)` from conservation in general form.
- **Reading:** key terms are defined in place in every
  lesson; no outside reading is required for any core task.

## Assessment guidance

- **Quiz (S18):** 20 points, 10 items × 2. On-track score:
  16+/20. Missed clusters: work sign → Lesson 01; KE square →
  Lesson 02; datum → Lesson 03; conservation setup → Lesson 04;
  efficiency → Lesson 05; power form choice → Lesson 06.
  Re-teach the cluster in S18's second half, before S19.
- **Unit assessment (S19):** 8 tasks covering objectives 1–6.
  Task 8 (open response) is graded on reasoning quality, not
  on matching a single sentence — accept any answer that uses
  energy accounting correctly and names thermal energy as the
  destination of the missing energy.
- **Readiness for Unit 04:** objectives 1–5 secure; objective
  6 (project) complete with an honest energy account. A learner
  who cannot balance a budget row needs Lesson 06 retaught
  before Unit 04's momentum budgets.
