# Unit Assessment — Unit 02: Forces, Free-Body Diagrams, and Dynamics

**Session S19 · ~50 minutes · completed in one sitting**
Calculator allowed. Show reasoning, not just answers. Draw a
free-body diagram (or list its forces) before any `F = ma`
division. Carry units; report three significant figures.

**Task 1 (first law, objective 1).** A spacecraft coasts in deep
space, engines off, at constant velocity. (a) What is the net
force on it? (b) A learner says, "It will gradually slow down
because no force keeps it going." Explain why that prediction
is wrong, using inertia.

**Task 2 (third law, objective 2).** A runner's foot pushes
backward on the track with 400 N during a sprint start.
(a) Name the third-law pair (both forces, both objects,
directions). (b) Explain which member of the pair accelerates
the runner, and why the pair does not cancel.

**Task 3 (free-body diagram, objective 3).** A 3.00 kg block is
pulled up a frictionless 25.0° incline by a rope parallel to
the slope, moving at constant velocity. (a) Draw the diagram.
(b) State the normal force. (c) State the rope tension.
(sin 25.0° = 0.423, cos 25.0° = 0.906.)

**Task 4 (second law, objective 4).** A 8.00 kg box is pushed
with 70.0 N on a floor where kinetic friction is 22.0 N.
(a) Find the net force. (b) Find the acceleration. (c) The push
stops; only friction remains. Find the new acceleration
(magnitude and direction relative to motion).

**Task 5 (friction, objective 4).** A 15.0 kg crate on a flat
floor has `μ_s = 0.400` and `μ_k = 0.300`. (a) Find the maximum
static friction. (b) A 40.0 N pull does not move it. What is
the static friction force? (c) A 70.0 N pull sets it sliding.
What is the kinetic friction force, and what is the
acceleration under the 70.0 N pull?

**Task 6 (incline, objective 4).** A 6.00 kg block slides down a
35.0° incline with `μ_k = 0.150`. (a) Compute the normal force
and the downhill weight component. (b) Compute kinetic friction
and the acceleration. (c) Check your acceleration against the
frictionless value `g sin θ` and state whether your answer is
sensibly below it. (sin 35.0° = 0.574, cos 35.0° = 0.819.)

**Task 7 (connected system, objective 4).** A 3.00 kg block on a
frictionless table is tied over a pulley to a hanging 2.00 kg
mass. (a) Find the acceleration by the system method.
(b) Find the tension two ways and confirm they agree.
(c) State one ideal-rope/ideal-pulley assumption used, and one
way a real system would depart from it.

**Task 8 (incline + hanging mass, objective 4).** A 5.00 kg block
on a frictionless 30.0° incline is tied over a pulley to a
hanging 4.00 kg mass that pulls it up the slope. (a) Compute the
downhill weight component of the incline block and the hanging
weight. (b) Find the system's acceleration and direction.
(c) Find the tension.

**Task 9 (error analysis, objectives 3–4).** A learner's
solution for a 2.00 kg block on a flat floor reads: "Weight =
2.00 kg. Normal force = 2.00 kg. Push = 30 N, friction =
`0.200 × 2.00 = 0.400 N`. Acceleration = `30 / 2.00 =
15.0 m/s²`." Identify every error (units, normal-force value,
friction formula, net force), and write the corrected solution
for `μ_k = 0.200` and a 30.0 N push.

**Task 10 (data, objective 4 / HS-PS2-1).** A group measures a
cart's acceleration for different hanging masses (same cart,
nearly frictionless track). Their table shows that doubling the
net pulling force doubles the acceleration, and that
`F / a = 2.50 kg` in every row. (a) What is the cart's mass,
and how do you know? (b) If the group had instead doubled the
cart's mass at fixed force, what would happen to the
acceleration, and why? (c) Name one way their "nearly
frictionless" track could bias the `F / a` values, and the
direction of the bias.

**Task 11 (investigation reasoning, objective 5).** In the Ramp
Investigation, a learner's measured accelerations fall below
`g sin θ` by about 30% at every angle, and the implied
`μ_k` values come out 0.061, 0.092, and 0.118 at 10°, 15°, and
20°. (a) Does a single constant friction coefficient explain
these data well? Justify with the numbers. (b) Name one
systematic timing error that could contribute, and explain its
direction. (c) What is the single most informative repeat
measurement to run next, and why?

**Task 12 (synthesis, objectives 1–4).** A 1,200 kg car brakes
on a flat road. The braking force (friction at the tires) is
7,200 N, and no other horizontal force matters. (a) Draw the
diagram (vertical forces included). (b) Find the deceleration.
(c) If the car was traveling at 20.0 m/s, how far does it travel
while stopping? (Use `v² = v_0² + 2aΔx`.) (d) Explain, using
Newton's first law, why the passengers continue forward as the
car slows, and what stops them.

---
*Scoring (adult): Tasks 1–12, 5 points each = 60 points.
48+ = on track; 36–47 = targeted reteach on missed objectives;
below 36 = re-teach the unit's weak lessons before Unit 03.
Open-response tasks are scored on reasoning and correct
force identification, not on a single phrasing.*
