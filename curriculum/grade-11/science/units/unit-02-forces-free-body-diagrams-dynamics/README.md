# Unit 02 — Forces, Free-Body Diagrams, and Dynamics

**Grade 11 · Science · Physics pathway · Weeks 7–10 of the course year**
(draft, delivered 2026-10-21 for issue #51; not educator-reviewed)

## Overview

Unit 01 taught the learner to measure motion honestly. This unit
answers the next question: **what changes motion, and by how much?**
The answer is built in four moves:

1. **Inertia and equilibrium** — Newton's first law: an object keeps
   its velocity unless a net force acts. Motion does not need a
   sustaining force; *change* of motion does.
2. **Action and reaction** — Newton's third law: forces always come
   in pairs on two different objects. A pair never cancels on one
   object, which is why the horse-and-cart "paradox" is not a paradox.
3. **Free-body diagrams** — the unit's central skill: isolate one
   object, draw every force on it (and nothing else), and read the
   net force off the diagram before writing any equation.
4. **Dynamics** — Newton's second law, `F_net = ma`, applied with
   friction, on inclines, and in connected (pulley) systems, and
   tested in the Ramp Investigation against the prediction
   `a = g sin θ` for a frictionless slope.

The essential question running through all four weeks is: **if I can
draw every force on an object, can I predict exactly how its motion
will change — and can I test that prediction?**

## Prerequisites

From Unit 01 and the Weeks 1–2 diagnostic (re-taught in context
where insecure):

- **From Unit 01:** vector components with sine and cosine;
  resultant magnitude and direction; significant figures and the
  `value ± uncertainty` report; `g = 9.80 m/s²`.
- **From grade-10 math:** right-triangle trigonometry; rearranging
  formulas; solving two simultaneous equations (needed in Lesson 06).
- **From grade 8:** forces as pushes and pulls (background only).

Session S9 re-checks vector components before inclines begin;
anyone missing them gets targeted warm-ups there.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. State Newton's first law in their own words; explain inertia as
   resistance to change in motion that grows with mass; and decide,
   for a described situation, whether the net force is zero (constant
   velocity) or nonzero (acceleration), including the case of an
   object at rest that is *about* to move. (track objective 4)
2. Identify Newton's third-law pairs correctly: name both forces,
   both objects, and both directions; and explain why a pair does
   not cancel, why weight and normal force on one object are
   generally *not* a third-law pair, and why motion is decided by
   the net force on one object at a time. (track objective 4)
3. Draw a complete free-body diagram for an object at rest, in
   constant-velocity motion, accelerating on a flat surface, on an
   incline, or in a pulley system: every force on that object, with
   correct direction, a label, and — where numbers are given — a
   magnitude; no forces the object exerts on something else, no
   velocity or acceleration arrows mixed in as forces. (track
   objective 4)
4. Apply `F_net = ma` component by component to solve for net
   force, acceleration, friction (`f_k = μ_k N`, static friction as
   a self-adjusting force up to `μ_s N`), tension, and normal force
   — on flat surfaces, on inclines (`N = mg cos θ`,
   downhill component `mg sin θ`), and in connected systems —
   carrying units and significant figures, and checking each answer
   against the diagram and against common sense. (track objective 4)
5. Plan and carry out a safe bench investigation: write a testable
   question, identify variables, record data in tables with units,
   compare measured accelerations with the `a = g sin θ`
   prediction, evaluate friction and timing error as explanations
   for the difference, and communicate an evidence-based
   conclusion — with the adult approving the procedure first.
   (track objective 12)

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: the Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS),
high school (9–12) band. Codes and descriptions were re-verified
2026-10-21 against the California Department of Education's NGSS
content-standards record for HS-PS2-1 (which restates the Achieve
performance expectation); descriptions here are short paraphrases,
not reproductions of the standards text. **This crosswalk is a
planning reference, not a claim of state adoption, accreditation,
or certification of alignment.**

- **HS-PS2-1** — analyze data to support the claim that Newton's
  second law describes the mathematical relationship among the net
  force on a macroscopic object, its mass, and its acceleration.
  This unit is its main home: Lesson 04 builds the relationship
  from data, Lessons 05–06 apply it, and the Ramp Investigation
  (project) tests a prediction derived from it. Note the
  assessment boundary carried in the standard: assessment is
  limited to one-dimensional motion of macroscopic objects at
  non-relativistic speeds — every dynamics problem here is
  one-dimensional along the direction of motion (a flat surface,
  a slope, or a rope), even when the diagram is drawn in two
  dimensions.
- Science and engineering practices used in this unit: developing
  and using models (free-body diagrams, Lessons 03–06); planning
  and carrying out investigations (project, S16–S17); analyzing
  and interpreting data (Lesson 04, project); using mathematics
  and computational thinking (every lesson); constructing
  explanations from evidence (Lessons 01–02, project).
- `g = 9.80 m/s²` throughout (three significant figures), as in
  Unit 01. Friction coefficients in this unit are given practice
  values, not measured material constants, unless the investigation
  measures an effective value and says so.
