# Practice — Unit 02: Forces, Free-Body Diagrams, and Dynamics

Calculator allowed. Show the free-body diagram (or its force
list) before any `F = ma` division. Carry units; report three
significant figures unless the data gives fewer.

## Set A — First law and third law (Lessons 01–02)

A1. A 1,000 kg car moves at constant 15.0 m/s on a flat road.
(a) What is the net force on the car? (b) The engine's forward
force is 1,200 N. What total backward force (friction plus air
resistance) acts? Explain why the engine can push hard while
the net force is zero.
A2. An astronaut pushes off the wall of a space station and
drifts away. Name the third-law pair, and explain why the
astronaut moves but the station (very massive) barely does.
A3. A book rests on a table. A learner lists the third-law pair
of the book's weight as "the table pushing up on the book."
Correct the learner: name the true pair and its objects.
A4. A golf ball is struck and flies through the air (ignore air
resistance). List the forces on the ball during flight. Is the
club's force still acting? Explain.
A5. Two people on ice skates push off each other. Person P
(60.0 kg) and person Q (90.0 kg) push with 150 N. (a) What force
does each person feel? (b) Who changes velocity more, and why?
(Qualitative — the calculation is Lesson 04's.)
A6. A truck (8,000 kg) and a car (1,200 kg) collide head-on.
During contact each exerts 20,000 N on the other. (a) State the
pair. (b) Explain why the car is damaged far more severely
even though the forces are equal. (Use inertia and Lesson 04
reasoning.)

## Set B — Free-body diagrams (Lesson 03)

For each item, draw the diagram (box, arrows, labels) and state
the net force on each axis where numbers allow.

B1. A 3.00 kg picture hangs at rest from two symmetric ropes,
each making 45.0° with the ceiling. (Do not solve for the
tensions yet — just draw the diagram and name the forces.)
B2. A 20.0 kg crate is pulled to the right at constant velocity
by a 90.0 N horizontal force. State the friction force and the
normal force.
B3. A 1.50 kg ball is in free fall (air resistance ignored).
Draw the diagram and state the net force.
B4. A person of mass 70.0 kg stands in an elevator accelerating
downward at 2.00 m/s². Draw the diagram. Is the normal force
greater than, less than, or equal to the person's weight?
B5. A block slides down a rough incline, slowing as it goes.
Draw the diagram and state the friction direction and the
direction of the net force along the slope.
B6. A satellite orbits Earth at constant speed. A learner draws
a "forward force" keeping it moving, plus gravity toward Earth.
Correct the diagram: which arrow is real, which is invented,
and what does the real net force do to the velocity?

## Set C — Second law and friction (Lesson 04)

C1. A 6.00 kg box experiences a net force of 18.0 N. Find its
acceleration. What net force would double that acceleration?
C2. A 12.0 kg crate on a floor with `μ_k = 0.200` is pushed with
50.0 N. (a) Find the normal force. (b) Find kinetic friction.
(c) Find the acceleration.
C3. A block on a flat table has `μ_s = 0.500` and weighs
40.0 N. (a) Find the maximum static friction. (b) A 12.0 N
horizontal pull does not move it. What is the static friction
force now? (c) An 18.0 N pull still does not move it. What is
the static friction force now?
C4. A 0.200 kg tennis ball is slowed by air resistance and
rolling friction totaling 0.800 N after a serve. Find its
acceleration (magnitude and direction relative to motion).
C5. A learner solves "a 5.00 kg mass, net force 25.0 N" as
`a = 25.0 × 5.00 = 125 m/s²`. Correct the calculation and the
reasoning error in one sentence each.
C6. A 50.0 kg student stands on a bathroom scale in an elevator
accelerating upward at 1.50 m/s². What does the scale read
(the normal force, in newtons)? Explain why the reading is not
the student's weight.

## Set D — Inclines (Lesson 05)

Use `g = 9.80 m/s²`. sin/cos values are given where needed.

D1. A 2.00 kg block on a frictionless 40.0° incline. Compute the
normal force, the downhill force, and the acceleration.
(sin 40.0° = 0.643, cos 40.0° = 0.940.)
D2. The same slope as D1, now with `μ_k = 0.250` and the block
sliding downhill. Compute kinetic friction and the
acceleration.
D3. A block is at rest on a 28.0° incline, held by static
friction. Its mass is 5.00 kg. What static friction force acts?
(sin 28.0° = 0.469, cos 28.0° = 0.883.) Is `μ_s` needed? Why not?
D4. A 3.00 kg block slides down a frictionless incline and
reaches the bottom at 6.00 m/s after starting from rest. It
traveled 4.00 m along the slope. (a) Find its acceleration from
kinematics (`v² = v_0² + 2aΔx`). (b) Use `a = g sin θ` to find
the slope angle. (sin⁻¹(0.459) ≈ 27.3°.)
D5. Two blocks, 1.00 kg and 4.00 kg, are released together on
the same frictionless incline. Which reaches the bottom first?
Justify with the acceleration formula, not with intuition.
D6. A delivery crate (25.0 kg) on a loading ramp at 15.0° is
held by a rope parallel to the ramp. With negligible friction,
what rope tension holds it at rest? (sin 15.0° = 0.259,
cos 15.0° = 0.966.)

## Set E — Connected systems (Lesson 06)

E1. Atwood machine: 6.00 kg and 2.00 kg. Find the acceleration
and the tension (two ways).
E2. A 5.00 kg block on a frictionless table is tied to a hanging
3.00 kg mass. Find the acceleration and the tension (two ways).
E3. Two blocks in contact on a frictionless table (4.00 kg
front, 1.00 kg behind) are pushed from behind with 30.0 N.
Find the shared acceleration and the contact force.
E4. A 2.00 kg block on a frictionless 45.0° incline is tied over
a pulley to a hanging 2.00 kg mass pulling it up the slope.
Which way does the system accelerate, and at what rate? Find
the tension. (sin 45.0° = cos 45.0° = 0.707.)
E5. A learner claims an Atwood machine with equal masses
(3.00 kg each) has tension `T = 0` "because nothing moves."
Correct the claim: find the tension, and explain what zero
acceleration does and does not imply about tension.
E6. A 8.00 kg block on a table with `μ_k = 0.100` is tied to a
hanging 4.00 kg mass. Find the acceleration and the tension.
(First find the table block's normal force and kinetic friction.)

## Set F — Cumulative retrieval (Lessons 01–06)

F1. State Newton's three laws in your own words, one sentence
each, and give one everyday example of each that does not
appear in the lessons.
F2. A 4.00 kg crate is pushed with 30.0 N on a floor with
`μ_k = 0.200`. (a) Draw the diagram. (b) Find the acceleration.
F3. A book on a table in an elevator accelerating upward: a
learner says the normal force equals the weight "because the
book is not moving relative to the elevator." Is that
reasoning sound? Explain using net force.
F4. A 7.00 kg block slides down a 30.0° incline with
`μ_k = 0.100`. Compute the acceleration. Then compute the
frictionless acceleration for comparison, and state in one
sentence what friction cost.
F5. An Atwood machine's masses are 4.00 kg and 4.00 kg, but one
side's rope passes over a pulley with noticeable friction, so
the system accelerates slowly in one direction. Which
ideal-pulley assumption is violated, and what does that do to
the two-way tension check?
F6. (Explain your reasoning.) A rocket in deep space fires its
engine and accelerates. A learner says, "There is nothing to
push against in space, so the rocket cannot accelerate."
Refute the learner using third-law pairs, and name the object
that receives the "action" force.
