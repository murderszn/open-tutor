# Unit 05 — Parent–Offspring Similarities and Behavior

**Grade 1 · Science · Weeks 19–22 (4 weeks, 16 sessions, 20–25 minutes each)**

Part of [Grade 1 Science](../../README.md) ·
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) ·
Issue [#11](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/11). Draft curriculum —
not educator-approved, not aligned/certified by any state.

## Big idea

Babies look like their parents — but not exactly. A puppy has four legs
and fur like its mother, yet it is smaller and its spots may sit in
different places. A bean seedling has a stem and leaves like a grown bean
plant, yet it is tiny and its first leaves are round, not bean-shaped.
And parents do things that help their babies stay alive: a mother bird
brings food to chicks that cannot fly yet, a mother duck leads her
ducklings to water, a penguin parent keeps an egg warm. In this unit the
learner matches babies to parents, names what is alike and what is
different, grows a bean seedling to watch a plant baby grow, finds
behavior patterns in books and pictures, and builds a family album of
living things.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of this unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Compare a young animal with its adult and name at least one trait
   that is alike and one trait that is different** — for example, a
   puppy and its mother both have fur and four legs, but the puppy is
   smaller (Lessons 01, 05; 1-LS3-1, LS3.A, LS3.B).
2. **Compare a young plant with its adult and name at least one part
   that is alike and one way it is different** — for example, a bean
   seedling and a grown bean plant both have a stem and leaves, but the
   seedling is much shorter and has fewer leaves (Lessons 02, 05;
   1-LS3-1, LS3.A, LS3.B).
3. **Name patterns in parent behavior that help offspring survive** —
   feeding, keeping warm, sheltering, protecting, and guiding babies to
   food, water, or safety — and say what each behavior helps the baby do
   (Lesson 03; 1-LS1-2, LS1.B).
4. **Use a text or picture/video read and shown by the adult to find a
   behavior pattern and state it as evidence** — "The book shows the
   mother bird feeding the chicks, so feeding is one way parents help
   babies survive" (Lesson 04; 1-LS1-2).
5. **Explain a baby–adult match with evidence, in their own words** —
   "I matched this baby to this parent because both have a flat beak
   and webbed feet, but the baby is smaller and yellower" — and state
   the unit rule: like, but not exactly like (Lessons 01, 05,
   [project](project.md); 1-LS3-1).
6. **Follow the unit's living-thing safety rules in every investigation:**
   handle plants and animals gently, never taste seeds, soil, or plant
   parts, wash hands after soil or plant work, observe wild animals
   from a distance or in pictures only, and use the observation-only
   alternative whenever touching would be unsafe (all lessons; track
   objective 10).

Oral, pointing, drawing, or adult-scribed responses count as evidence
throughout. The adult reads all prompts aloud; learner independence grows
only as far as writing stamina allows. Short independent recording tasks
are 4–6 items (draw + label, adult-scribed), reviewed by the adult the
same day.

## Prerequisites

- From Unit 01: asking questions that can be explored by looking or
  testing, observing with more than one sense, recording in the science
  journal, and the four science safety rules (adult supervision; nothing
  near the mouth; gentle handling; the safe-alternative question).
- From Unit 04 (draft PR #288, named here in prose only): naming
  external parts of plants (roots, stem, leaves, flower) and animals
  (legs/feet, ears, eyes, beak, fins, tail) and saying what each part
  does. If Unit 04 was not yet worked through, the adult re-teaches the
  part names in Lesson 01's first ten minutes with the picture cards
  before starting the matching game.
- No reading is required. The learner does not need the words
  "inheritance," "gene," or "metamorphosis," and the unit never teaches
  them: this grade band does not assess animals that change form
  completely (such as caterpillar to butterfly) or hybrids, and it never
  explains traits by internal parts the learner cannot see.

## Standards and source notes

- **1-LS1-2** — *Read texts and use media to determine patterns in
  behavior of parents and offspring that help offspring survive.*
  Lessons 03–04 and the project. The framework's clarification examples
  are the direct model for the behavior patterns taught here: signals
  offspring make (such as crying, cheeping, and other vocalizations)
  and parent responses (such as feeding, comforting, and protecting the
  offspring). Description re-verified 2026-11-04 against the California
  Department of Education NGSS standards display for 1-LS1-2; matches
  the track audit crosswalk (checked 2026-10-02).
- **LS1.B: Growth and Development of Organisms** — *Adult plants and
  animals can have young. In many kinds of animals, parents and the
  offspring themselves engage in behaviors that help the offspring to
  survive.* This is the content behind Objectives 3–4.
- **1-LS3-1** — *Make observations to construct an evidence-based
  account that young plants and animals are like, but not exactly like,
  their parents.* Lessons 01–02, 05, and the project. The framework's
  clarification examples are the direct model: leaves from the same
  kind of plant are the same shape but can differ in size, and a
  particular breed of dog looks like its parents but is not exactly the
  same. Description re-verified 2026-11-04 against the California
  Department of Education NGSS standards display for 1-LS3-1; matches
  the track audit crosswalk (checked 2026-10-02).
- **LS3.A: Inheritance of Traits** — *Young animals are very much, but
  not exactly, like their parents. Plants also are very much, but not
  exactly, like their parents.*
- **LS3.B: Variation of Traits** — *Individuals of the same kind of
  plant or animal are recognizable as similar but can also vary in many
  ways.* This is why two puppies from the same mother can have
  different spot patterns and still both be puppies.
- **Assessment boundary respected:** 1-LS3-1 does not include
  inheritance or animals that undergo metamorphosis or hybrids. No
  lesson, picture, quiz, or assessment item in this unit uses a
  caterpillar/butterfly, tadpole/frog, or hybrid example.
- **Bean seedling growth** (root first, then shoot, then first leaves;
  about 5–10 days to sprout in a warm, damp bag) follows the
  ScienceWorld "Bean Garden" activity and the Scholastic windowsill
  germination guide (see [resource-pack](resource-pack.md)). The unit
  keeps the observation at the outside level: what can be seen and
  measured, not seed anatomy vocabulary.
- **Parent-care examples** (birds feeding chicks in a nest; penguin
  parents taking turns keeping eggs warm and bringing food; a mother
  duck leading ducklings) follow the National Geographic Kids animal
  pages cited in the [resource-pack](resource-pack.md). All wild-animal
  examples are met in pictures or text, never by handling a wild animal.
- No claim of state adoption, accreditation, or complete alignment is made.

## Vocabulary

Adult introduces each word in context during the lesson; learners are not
expected to spell them.

- **parent** — a mother or father animal or plant that has young
- **offspring** — the young of a living thing; a baby animal or a young plant
- **baby** — a very young animal
- **seedling** — a very young plant that has just grown from a seed
- **adult** — a grown plant or animal
- **trait** — something you can see about a living thing, such as its color, shape, size, or number of legs
- **alike / like** — the same in some way
- **different** — not the same in some way
- **exactly** — in every way, with no difference
- **pattern** — something that happens again and again in the same way
- **survive** — to stay alive: to get food, water, air, and safety
- **feed** — to give food to a baby
- **shelter** — a safe place to live, such as a nest, a burrow, or a den
- **protect** — to keep a baby safe from danger
- **guide** — to lead or show a baby where to go or what to do
- **nest** — a home a bird builds for its eggs and chicks
- **observe** — to look carefully and notice what happens
- **evidence** — what you saw or read that backs up what you say
- **match** — to pair a baby with its parent because they go together
- **gentle** — touching softly and carefully, so nothing is hurt

## Materials (whole unit)

Science journal, pencils, crayons; picture cards (adult-made or printed
from the text alternatives in this unit — never a learner's photo):
dog mother + puppy, cat mother + kitten, duck mother + ducklings, hen +
chicks, cow + calf, sunflower adult + seedling, bean plant adult +
seedling; dry bean seeds (adult-handled — choking hazard, never near
the mouth), clear zip bag or clear cup, damp paper towel, water, a
sunny windowsill, a ruler or paper strip marked in "finger-widths";
the generated illustration
[`assets/parent-offspring-families.png`](assets/parent-offspring-families.png);
one adult-read picture book or printed page about animal parents (see
[resource-pack](resource-pack.md)); album paper or a folded-paper
booklet for the project. All investigations are adult-supervised; see
[teacher-guide](teacher-guide.md) for safety notes. Never taste seeds,
soil, or plant parts; wash hands after soil or plant work; wild animals
are observed in pictures only.

## 16-session pacing

Sessions are 20–25 minutes. Each names its activity and success criterion.

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lesson 01** — Animal babies: like, but not exactly like | Matches 3 baby–adult pairs and names one alike trait and one different trait for each. |
| 2 | Practice — Baby–adult matching game (animals) | Matches 6 animal pairs and gives a reason for at least 4 matches. |
| 3 | Observation — Bean-in-a-bag setup | Plants 2–3 bean seeds in a damp bag, predicts "root first, then shoot," and records the prediction by drawing. |
| 4 | Review — Like / different chart + Unit 04 retrieval | Sorts 8 trait cards into "alike" and "different" and names one plant part and one animal part from Unit 04. |
| 5 | **Lesson 02** — Plant babies grow up | Compares a seedling picture with an adult plant picture and names one alike part and one difference for each of 2 plants. |
| 6 | Observation — Seedling check + picture investigation | Measures/draws the bean seedling (or the picture seedling if the bag has not sprouted) and answers 5 questions from the generated illustration or its text alternative. |
| 7 | Practice — Whose baby is it? (mixed sort) | Sorts 8 mixed baby cards to the right adult (animal or plant) and explains 2 matches with evidence. |
| 8 | Review — Journal check + seedling measure 2 | Records a second seedling measurement and states whether it grew taller, with the numbers/drawings as evidence. |
| 9 | **Lesson 03** — Parents help babies survive | Names 4 parent behaviors (feed, warm, shelter, protect, guide) and says what each helps the baby do. |
| 10 | Practice — Behavior pattern sort | Sorts 6 behavior cards into feed / shelter-or-warm / protect / guide and gives the "helps the baby…" reason for each. |
| 11 | **Lesson 04** — Books and pictures as evidence | Finds 2 behavior patterns in an adult-read text or picture and states each as evidence ("The book shows… so…"). |
| 12 | Project — Family album, Part 1 | Chooses 4 album pairs and draws the first 2 pairs with alike/different labels. |
| 13 | **Lesson 05** — Mystery baby: use the evidence | Matches 2 mystery babies to parents using traits alone and explains each match with at least one alike trait. |
| 14 | Project — Family album, Part 2 | Finishes all 4 album pages, adds one "parent helps" note per page, and shares one page aloud (or by pointing). |
| 15 | Review — Matching fair + formative quiz | Re-matches the fair cards and completes the [formative quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md). |
| 16 | Assessment — [Unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md) + re-teach planning | Completes all 4 performance tasks; adult records readiness per objective. |

Weeks 19–20 introduce and practice alike/different comparisons for
animals, then plants; Week 21 is behavior patterns and evidence from
texts and media, plus the album build; Week 22 is the review week: the
mystery-baby reasoning, the finished album, re-teaching, the formative
check, and the culminating assessment. If the learner is not yet secure
on an objective after Session 16, use the diagnostic/catch-up guidance
in the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) before starting Unit 06 — the
track's flexible weeks exist for exactly this.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, drawing, or gesture responses; the adult
scribes. Each lesson includes a seated-table and a floor-play variant, a
language-access support, an alternate response mode, and an extension.
Trait words are never taught by color alone: every comparison also names
size, shape, number of parts, or where a part is (on the head, at the back,
underground). Bean-bag observations have a picture alternative for any
learner who cannot handle seeds, and every picture question has a
text-only alternative.
