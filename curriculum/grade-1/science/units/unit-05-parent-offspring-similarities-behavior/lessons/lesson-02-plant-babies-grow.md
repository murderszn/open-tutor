# Lesson 02 — Plant Babies Grow Up

**Student goal:** I can compare a young plant with a grown plant and say
one part that is alike and one way it is different.
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Unit:** [U05](../README.md) · **Session 5 of 16**

## Materials

- Picture cards: adult bean plant + bean seedling, adult sunflower +
  sunflower seedling (adult-made drawings or printed pictures)
- The bean-in-a-bag from Session 3, for a live check
- Science journal, crayons, ruler or finger-width paper strip
- The "alike / different" chart from Lesson 01

**Safety note for the adult:** seeds are adult-handled — dry beans are a
choking hazard and are never near the learner's mouth. Hands are washed
after touching soil, seeds, or the damp bag. If the bag has not sprouted
yet, use the picture seedling; never rush a plant by pulling it open.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Point to the bean bag. What did we predict would happen first — a root
or a shoot?" (Root first, then shoot.) "What do you see today?" If the
learner can name the prediction or describe one change in the bag,
begin. If the bag shows nothing yet, say so honestly: "Nothing has
changed yet. Plants take their time. Today we compare pictures while we
wait."

## Teaching

A plant has young too. A **seedling** is a very young plant that has
just grown from a seed. A seedling is not a different kind of plant —
it is the same kind of plant, young. The rule from Lesson 01 works for
plants as well:

> **Young plants are like their parents — but not exactly like them.**

A bean seedling is like a grown bean plant: it has a stem and leaves.
A bean seedling is not exactly like a grown bean plant: it is much
shorter, it has fewer leaves, and its first leaves are small and round,
not shaped like the grown plant's leaves. A sunflower seedling is like
a grown sunflower: it has a green stem and green leaves. A sunflower
seedling is not exactly like a grown sunflower: it is tiny, and it has
no big yellow flower yet — the flower comes later, when the plant grows
up.

Plants grow in a pattern we can watch: a seed takes in water, a root
grows down first, a shoot grows up, and leaves open. The seedling
already has the plant's parts in small form; growing up mostly means
getting bigger, growing more leaves, and, for a sunflower, growing a
flower.

## Worked example 1 — the bean pair (modeled)

The adult puts the adult bean plant card and the bean seedling card side
by side.

> "Look at the grown bean plant. It has a long stem and many leaves.
> Now look at the seedling. What is alike? The seedling also has a stem
> and leaves — the same parts, just small. That is our evidence that
> they are the same kind of plant.
>
> What is different? The seedling is very short — about as tall as your
> finger. The grown plant is taller than your arm. And count the
> leaves: the seedling has two small round leaves; the grown plant has
> many bigger leaves. Same parts, different size, different number of
> leaves. Like — but not exactly like."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** we matched by parts (stem, leaves), not by
height. Height is a difference, because babies are always smaller — for
plants as well as animals.

## Worked example 2 — the sunflower pair, predicted first (modeled)

Before showing the seedling card, the adult asks: "A grown sunflower
has a tall stem, big leaves, and a yellow flower. What do you think its
seedling will have?"

> "A stem and leaves — because young plants are like their parents.
> Let's check." The adult shows the sunflower seedling card. "Yes! A
> short stem and two small leaves. Now, what is different? The seedling
> has no flower yet, and it is tiny next to the grown sunflower. The
> flower comes later. Like — but not exactly like."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** "no flower yet" is a difference in time,
not a different kind of plant. Young does not mean unrelated.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds. Offer choices; never demand a spoken answer.

1. Bean round: "Point to the stem on the seedling. Point to the stem on
   the grown plant." *(Both have a stem.)* "Which plant is taller?"
   *(The grown plant.)*
2. Leaf-count round: "How many leaves does the seedling have?"
   *(Two.)* "Does the grown plant have more or fewer?" *(More.)*
3. Error-analysis round: the adult says, very seriously: "The seedling
   cannot be a bean plant, because it has no beans on it." Ask: "Is
   that right? Or is it a young bean plant that has not grown beans
   yet?" *(Expected: it is a young bean plant; beans come later, like
   the sunflower's flower.)*
4. Bag round: look at the bean bag together. "What do you see — a root,
   a shoot, leaves, or nothing yet?" *(Whatever is true that day.)*
   "What do you predict for next time?" *(The next step in the
   pattern: root, then shoot, then leaves.)*
5. Chart round: together, put one alike part ("both have leaves") in
   the "Alike" column and one difference ("seedling is shorter") in the
   "Different" column for the sunflower pair.
6. If the learner is verbal: "Finish the sentence: a young plant is
   like its parent plant, but ___." *(Not exactly like it.)*

## Independent practice

The learner draws the sunflower pair in the journal: a tall sunflower
with a yellow flower and a tiny seedling with two round leaves. The
adult labels each drawing from dictation ("grown sunflower"; "young
sunflower"). Under each, the learner points to or dictates one alike
part and one difference, and the adult writes them in the two chart
columns. The adult sits nearby and observes: does the seedling drawing
show a stem and leaves, but no flower? When finished, the adult asks,
"Is the seedling the same kind of plant as the grown sunflower?" and
scribes the answer for private storage.

*Alternate response mode:* the learner sorts the four plant cards
(adult + seedling for each kind) into pairs on the table instead of
drawing, and points to the alike part on each card.

## Application

"Tonight, look at any plant at home — a houseplant, or one the adult
shows you in a picture. Is it grown, or young? How do you know?" (In a
one-on-one setting, the learner reports back at the next session.) The
learner practices the rule outside the lesson.

## Exit check

The adult shows a new pair the learner has not compared yet (a second
bean-plant picture set, or the bean bag next to the adult bean plant
card) and asks: "Do these belong together? What part is alike? What is
different?" The learner should name at least one alike part and one
difference (words, pointing, or gesture). The adult records: secure /
developing / not yet. A second probe: "Will the seedling ever have a
flower?" (For a sunflower: yes, when it grows up. For a bean: it will
grow beans later — accept any "when it grows up" answer.)

## Supports

- **Language access:** teach "seedling" with the live bag first, word
  second. Point to the tiny plant and say "seedling" while the learner
  looks at it. Accept home-language words alongside English.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing to parts, sorting cards,
  drawing, measuring with fingers ("this tall" vs. "this tall").
- **Floor-play variant:** lay the plant cards on the floor in a row
  from smallest to tallest; the learner walks the growth order.
- **Seated-table variant:** do the whole lesson at the table with the
  cards, bag, and journal.

## Extension

"If we plant a sunflower seed and a bean seed side by side, will their
seedlings look alike? Why not?" (Both will be small and green, but
their leaf shapes and growth will differ — they are different kinds of
plants. Accept any sensible answer; the unit returns to kind-matching
in Lesson 05.)

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) "A seedling is a different plant" — the
shared parts (stem, leaves) are the correction; (2) "No flower means
not a sunflower" — flowers and beans come with growing up, not at
birth; (3) "Bigger means a different kind" — size is a difference in
age, not in kind.
