# Teacher Guide — Unit 03

Session-by-session guidance for the guiding adult. Student materials never
contain answers; this guide and [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) are the
only places solutions live.

## Session map and emphasis

| Sessions | Focus | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Darkness rule + light sources | "Things disappear in the dark" — re-run the two-object round. |
| 5–8 | Four material words + fair beam test | Translucent vs. opaque confusion — return to the wall result, not the look of the material. |
| 9–10 | Shadow needs, shape, size | "Shadows stay when light goes off" — run the lights-off proof. |
| 11–12 | Code agreement + build | Receiver watching the sender instead of the wall — re-aim attention to the wall. |
| 13–14 | Fair comparison + judgment | "Winner takes all" overclaim — force the condition into every judgment sentence. |
| 15–16 | Story review, quiz, assessment | Rushing the quiz — it is oral/observed; let the learner point and draw. |

## Misconceptions (all lessons)

1. **"Things disappear in the dark."** Objects stay; light changes.
   Re-test: lid closed, then opened — the object was there all along.
2. **"Our eyes make light."** Light comes from sources. Test: close your
   eyes in a lit room — you see nothing, because light is blocked from
   reaching your eyes, not because your eyes stopped making it.
3. **"Translucent means no light gets through."** The dim glow on the
   wall proves some light passes. Opaque = no light + dark shadow.
4. **"A shadow is a dark thing."** A shadow is blocked light. Lights off
   → shadow gone. It is not paint and it does not stay.
5. **"Bigger object = bigger shadow, always."** At this grade, hold object
   size constant and vary distance only. Distance is the tested cause.
6. **"Any flash is a message."** Only agreed signals count. The code list
   decides, not the sender's claim.
7. **"The best messenger wins everywhere."** Every judgment needs its
   condition: "better in noise," "better when the receiver looks away."

## Re-teaching

- **Objective 1 weak (darkness rule):** repeat the dark-box detective
  session with a new object; add the "tonight at home" lamp observation.
- **Objective 2 weak (material words):** re-run beam tests with only two
  materials at a time (transparent vs. opaque first), then add translucent
  and reflective. Keep the wall-result language ("bright spot / dim glow /
  dark shadow / bounced light") before the formal words.
- **Objective 3 weak (shadows):** re-run the near/far round with the
  learner moving the puppet; measure in hand-widths and record both
  numbers side by side.
- **Objective 4 weak (messenger):** shorten the code to two signals
  (1 flash = "yes", 2 flashes = "come here") and re-test across a short
  distance first.
- **Objective 5 weak (comparison):** re-run ONE condition (noise) for both
  devices and fill just that table row together; add conditions one at a
  time.

## Readiness criteria

- **Ready for Unit 04:** unit assessment 6–8 with no zero, safety check
  passed, and the learner can state the darkness rule and the shadow
  distance rule without prompting.
- **Ready with a note:** assessment 4–5 — record which objective to
  revisit in Unit 04's retrieval warm-ups (Unit 04 opens with
  observations; fold the weak rule into its first two sessions).
- **Not yet:** assessment 0–3 — use a flexible week (diagnostic/catch-up
  or the midyear review week, since Unit 04 follows the midyear review)
  to re-teach the weakest objectives; do not start Unit 04 cold.

## Safety notes (every session)

- Never look directly at the sun — this unit never asks for it. All sun
  work is replaced by flashlight work.
- Flashlight beams point at objects, walls, and screens — never at eyes
  or faces. The receiver watches the wall.
- The mirror is fixed to a stand; the learner never waves it.
- Batteries are adult-handled. Small parts (paper clips, craft sticks)
  stay away from the mouth.
- String from the Unit 02 telephone is adult-handled, never around a neck.
- If the learner is sensitive to darkness, the dark box is used with the
  lid ajar and room lights dimmed, not off; the learner may hold the
  flashlight throughout.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** fewer signals (2-code list), fewer materials per test
  (2 at a time), pointing/sorting instead of speaking, the text
  alternative for the illustration, seated-table variants throughout.
- **Extension:** the mirror-bounce bonus in the project; the combined
  light-and-sound messenger sketch in Lesson 05; the morning-vs-noon
  shadow question in Lesson 03 (previews Unit 06).
- **Language access:** every new word is taught by experience first
  (show the bright spot, then say "transparent"); home-language words
  are accepted alongside English; message cards carry pictures.
