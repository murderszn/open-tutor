# Answer Key — Unit 03

Teacher-only. Every practice, quiz, assessment, and project item is solved
here. Accept any response that carries the same meaning in the learner's
own words, drawings, or gestures; the adult scribes.

## Lesson 01

- **Guided practice:** (1) Ball: no, then yes; what changed was the light.
  (2) Two objects: zero seen with light off, two with light on; nothing
  was added — the light let the learner see what was already there.
  (3) Error-analysis: no — the toys do not hide and return; they stay,
  and only the light changes. (4) Flashlight makes its own light; the
  ball needs light shone on it. (5) Light source: lamp, window sun,
  screen; non-source: table, book, toy. (6) "We see something only when
  light shines on it."
- **Exit check:** spoon, lid closed: cannot see it, because no light
  shines on it. Light on: can see it, because light shines on it. Sun:
  yes, a light source.

## Lesson 02

- **Guided practice:** (1) Wax paper: dim glow → translucent. (2) Mirror:
  light bounces → reflective. (3) Error-analysis: no — "cannot see
  clearly through it" describes translucent, not opaque; opaque means no
  light gets through and a dark shadow appears. (4) Picture: clear
  plastic → bright spot; wax paper → dim glow; cardboard → dark shadow.
  (5) Window → transparent; towel → opaque; paper → translucent or
  opaque depending on thickness (the wall test decides — accept either
  with a test); spoon → reflective. (6) "Opaque means light cannot get
  through; it is blocked and makes a shadow."
- **Exit check:** clear plastic → transparent, bright spot; wax paper →
  translucent, dim glow; cardboard → opaque, dark shadow; mirror →
  reflective, bounced light. At least 3 of 4 panels correct. Second
  probe: any opaque item (cardboard, towel, book).

## Lesson 03

- **Guided practice:** (1) Animal shadow has the animal's shape; made by
  flashlight light + the puppet blocking it. (2) Close to light → bigger;
  close to wall → smaller. (3) Error-analysis: no — turn the light off
  and the shadow vanishes; a shadow is blocked light, not paint.
  (4) Very close to the light → big shadow. (5) The puppet closer to the
  light has the bigger shadow. (6) "A shadow needs light and an object
  blocking the light."
- **Exit check:** size from the distance rule (close to light = big;
  close to wall = small); two needs = light source + blocking object.
  Second probe: the puppet's own shape.

## Lesson 04

- **Guided practice:** (1) 1 flash = yes; 2 = come here; 3 = lunchtime
  (or the learner's own third message). (2) Learner-sent "yes" read
  correctly by the adult from the wall. (3) Error-analysis: no — 2
  flashes mean "come here" in the agreed code; "lunchtime" is 3 flashes;
  the code list decides. (4) From the far side: yes, the message gets
  through; a whisper would not. (5) Any sensible fourth message, added
  to the code list. (6) "A code works because we agree what each signal
  means before we send."
- **Exit check:** at least 2 of 3 flashed codes read correctly; the plan
  sketch shows sender, beam, wall, receiver (all four parts for full
  credit). Second probe: so sender and receiver agree.

## Lesson 05

- **Guided practice:** (1) Bright room: sound clearly through; light
  through but harder to see on the wall. (2) Receiver facing away: light
  missed; sound heard — sound wins. (3) Error-analysis: no — light won
  in noise only; it struggled when the receiver was not looking and in
  bright light. "Best" needs a condition. (4) Noisy playground: light
  (if the receiver watches the wall); dark bedroom, receiver facing
  away: sound. (5) Improvement: add a sound signal too, or a bigger
  wall — any sensible improvement. (6) "The light messenger is better
  when it is noisy and the receiver is watching the wall."
- **Exit check:** noisy room → light messenger (noise does not block
  light); receiver facing away → sound messenger (sound does not need
  looking). "Best everywhere": no — it depends on the condition.

## Practice sessions

- **Session A — Dark-box detective:** (1) Each prediction checked against
  the peep: light off = cannot see; light on = can see. (2) Two objects:
  zero, then two. (3) Role swap: adult sees nothing with lid closed,
  names the object with light on. (4) Drawings: dark half empty, lit half
  showing the object. (5) Error hunt: wrong — the ball did not disappear
  and return; opening the lid proves it was there all along.
  (6) Accept: "We see it only when light shines on it."
- **Light-source hunt:** (1) Sources: sun, lamp, flashlight, screen,
  candle (adult-only); non-sources: table, book, toy, mirror (reflects
  only). (2) Cards: makes its own light — sun, lamp, flashlight;
  needs light — table, book, mirror, toy, window (sunlight passes
  through it; the window does not make light). (3) Window: not a source.
  (4) Moon: at grade 1 accept "the moon shines at night"; adult note —
  moonlight is reflected sunlight; not tested. (5) Drawings: one source,
  one non-source, labeled.
- **Light or no light game:** (1) Can see: lamp-on room, sunny window,
  starry sky (stars are light sources; accept "can see the stars"),
  flashlight scene. Cannot see: dark box (lid closed), cave without a
  light, closed closet, book in a dark drawer. (2) Fixes: open the door,
  turn on the lamp, use a flashlight, open the drawer. (3) Any testable
  "I notice / I wonder" question about light. (4) Speed round: "light!"
  / "no light!" matching the sort above.
- **Session B — Beam tests:** (1) Same: flashlight, distance, wall; only
  the material changes. (2) Clear plastic → bright spot, transparent;
  wax paper → dim glow, translucent; cardboard → dark shadow, opaque;
  mirror → bounced light, reflective. (3) Order: clear plastic (most),
  wax paper, cardboard (least); mirror apart — it bounces light.
  (4) Mystery: paper → translucent (thin) or opaque (thick) — wall test
  decides; plastic bag → translucent; towel → opaque. (5) Journal: four
  wall results matched to materials.
- **Session C — Picture investigation:** (1) Light comes from the
  flashlight at left; the beam goes right toward the panels. (2) Clear
  plastic → bright spot; wax paper → dim glow; cardboard → dark shadow.
  (3) Transparent / translucent / opaque, decided by the wall results.
  (4) The mirror bounces a small spot onto the table. (5) Real tests are
  one material at a time; the picture lines them up to compare results.
  (6) Redrawn three panels with matching wall results.
- **Material sort game:** (1) Transparent: plastic wrap, window glass;
  translucent: wax paper, paper (thin); opaque: cardboard, towel;
  reflective: mirror, spoon. Spoon may sit in both opaque and
  reflective. (2) Any defense citing the correct wall result.
  (3) Journal check: results matched to materials; one error fixed
  together if present. (4) Fast sort: count correct out of 8; second
  round aims to beat the first honestly.
- **Session D — Shadow hunt:** (1) Four shadows, each with its light
  source and blocking object named. (2) Near-light shadow measures more
  hand-widths than near-wall shadow (exact numbers vary — the comparison
  is the answer, not the count). (3) Halfway shadow falls between the
  two measurements. (4) Yes — each shadow shows its puppet's shape.
  (5) Lights off → shadow gone; a shadow is blocked light, not paint.
- **Shadow-puppet storytelling:** (1) Three puppets ordered beginning /
  middle / end. (2) Performed with shadows as actors. (3) Big shadow =
  puppet close to the light; small shadow = puppet close to the wall.
  (4) Retell names light + puppets blocking it as the shadow makers.
  (5) Quiz follows (see below).

## Formative quiz

- **Part A:** (1) Cannot see (no light on it); can see (light shines on
  it). (2) Clear plastic → transparent, bright spot; wax paper →
  translucent, dim glow; cardboard → opaque, dark shadow; mirror →
  reflective, bounced light. (3) Star-shaped shadow; made by light +
  the puppet blocking it. (4) 2 flashes = "come here"; known from the
  agreed code list.
- **Part B:** (5) Both halves drawn: dark/empty and lit/object visible.
  (6) Four wall results matched to material words. (7) Close-to-light
  shadow drawn bigger than close-to-wall shadow. (8) Messenger drawing
  shows sender, beam, wall with flashes, receiver.

## Unit assessment

- **Task 1 (Secure):** both "why" answers use the light rule; light source
  correct (lamp, window sun, screen); non-source correct (table, book,
  toy). Developing: one "why" or one sort item wrong. Not yet: neither
  "why" uses the rule.
- **Task 2 (Secure):** at least 4 of 5 words correct with matching wall
  results (paper = translucent or opaque by test); both shadow moves
  correct with the distance reason. Developing: 3 words, or one move
  wrong. Not yet: fewer than 3 words.
- **Task 3 (Secure):** both sent messages read correctly, the received
  "yes" read correctly, sketch matches the build. Developing: one message
  misread or partial sketch match. Not yet: messages do not get through.
- **Task 4 (Secure):** noisy room → light (noise does not block light);
  facing away → sound (sound needs no looking); "best everywhere" → no,
  because the winner depends on the condition. Developing: one winner or
  reason missing. Not yet: claims one device wins everywhere.
- **Safety check:** must pass (beams on walls, no sun-looking, mirror
  safe, string safe) before the unit is marked complete.
