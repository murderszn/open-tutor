## Text-only alternative

For learners who cannot use the picture. It carries every panel and wall
result the activities ask about.

> A flashlight on the left shines a straight beam of light to the right.
> First the beam meets a clear plastic sheet. Behind the plastic, on the
> wall, there is a BRIGHT round spot of light. The plastic is
> TRANSPARENT: almost all the light goes through.
>
> Next the beam meets a sheet of wax paper. Behind the wax paper, on the
> wall, there is a DIM, soft glow — not bright, not dark. The wax paper
> is TRANSLUCENT: some light goes through, but blurry.
>
> Last the beam meets a piece of brown cardboard. Behind the cardboard,
> on the wall, there is a DARK shadow shaped like the cardboard. The
> cardboard is OPAQUE: no light goes through; it blocks the light.
>
> On the table at the lower right lies a small round hand mirror. It
> bounces a small spot of light onto the table. The mirror is
> REFLECTIVE: it bounces light back instead of letting it through.
>
> Remember: in the real investigation we test one material at a time. This
> picture lines them up side by side only so we can compare their results.
