# Unit 03 — Light, Visibility, Shadows, and Materials

**Grade 1 · Science · Weeks 11–14 (4 weeks, 16 sessions, 20–25 minutes each)**

Part of [Grade 1 Science](../../README.md) · [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) ·
Issue [#11](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/11). Draft curriculum —
not educator-approved, not aligned/certified by any state.

## Big idea

We can only see something when light reaches it and then reaches our eyes.
In a dark box, toys are invisible until a flashlight shines on them. Some
materials let almost all the light through, some let only a little through,
some block it and make a shadow, and some bounce it back. Once we know how
light behaves, we can send messages with it — a flash of light can mean
"yes," two flashes can mean "come here," and a code of flashes can carry a
message across the room without a single spoken word.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of this unit the learner will be able to:

1. Show, with a dark-box observation, that **objects in darkness can be
   seen only when illuminated**; name light sources (sun, lamp, flashlight)
   and tell them apart from objects that only reflect light (Lessons 01–02;
   1-PS4-2).
2. Plan and carry out a fair beam test and **sort materials as transparent,
   translucent, opaque, or reflective**, describing the light or shadow
   each one makes (Lessons 02–03; 1-PS4-3).
3. Describe shadows accurately: a shadow **needs a light source and an
   object**, shows the object's shape, and **grows or shrinks when the
   object moves toward or away from the light** (Lesson 03; 1-PS4-3).
4. Design (sketch first), build, and test a simple **device that uses light
   to communicate over a distance** — a flashlight code that sends a real
   message across the room (Lessons 04–05, [project](project.md); 1-PS4-4,
   K-2-ETS1-2).
5. **Compare two messenger designs** (light code vs. the Unit 02 sound
   device) using test results, and say which works better, where, and why
   (Lesson 05, project; 1-PS4-4, K-2-ETS1-3).
6. Follow the unit's light-safety rules in every investigation: **never
   look directly at the sun**, point flashlight beams at objects and walls
   — never at eyes — and use the indoor flashlight alternative for all
   sun work (all lessons; track objective 10).

Oral, pointing, drawing, or adult-scribed responses count as evidence
throughout. The adult reads all prompts aloud; learner independence grows
only as far as writing stamina allows. Short independent recording tasks
are 4–6 items (draw + label), reviewed by the adult the same day.

## Prerequisites

- From Unit 01: asking testable questions, observing with more than one
  sense, recording in the science journal, and the four science safety
  rules (adult supervision; nothing near the mouth; gentle handling; the
  safe-alternative question).
- From Unit 02: the design cycle — plan (sketch), build, test, compare —
  and the cup-string telephone or tap-code device, reused in Lesson 05 for
  the light-vs-sound comparison. If Unit 02's device was not kept, the
  adult rebuilds a cup-string telephone in 5 minutes before Lesson 05.
- No reading is required. The learner does not need to know that light
  "travels" or how eyes work inside; the unit stays at the observable
  level (light on an object → we can see it).

## Vocabulary

Adult introduces each word in context during the lesson; learners are not
expected to spell them.

- **light** — what lets us see things
- **dark / darkness** — no light reaching our eyes from that place
- **illuminate** — to shine light on something so it can be seen
- **light source** — something that makes its own light (sun, lamp, flashlight)
- **reflect** — to bounce light back (a mirror reflects; a white wall reflects)
- **beam** — a straight stream of light, like from a flashlight
- **transparent** — lets almost all light through; you can see clearly through it (clear plastic)
- **translucent** — lets some light through, but blurry; you cannot see clearly through it (wax paper)
- **opaque** — lets no light through; it blocks light and makes a shadow (cardboard)
- **shadow** — the dark shape where an object blocks light
- **signal** — a flash (or sound) that means something
- **code** — an agreed set of signals that stand for messages
- **message** — words or a signal you send to someone
- **communicate** — to send a message so someone else gets it
- **device** — a tool you build to solve a problem
- **plan / sketch** — to decide and draw what you will build before you build it
- **test** — to try something and watch what happens
- **compare** — to tell how two things are the same and different
- **fair test** — a test where only one thing changes at a time

## Materials (whole unit)

Science journal, pencils, crayons; a shoebox with a lid and a small
flashlight-sized peep hole (the **dark box**, built by the adult); 2
flashlights with fresh batteries (adult handles batteries); 4–6 small
familiar objects for the dark box (toy animal, crayon, ball, spoon); a
sheet of clear plastic or cling wrap stretched over a cardboard frame; a
sheet of wax paper in a frame; a piece of cardboard in a frame; a small
hand mirror (adult holds it or tapes it to a stand — never a loose
hand-mirror waved near faces); a white wall or large white paper screen;
paper shapes on craft sticks for shadow puppets (star, tree, animal);
masking tape; a ruler or paper strip for measuring shadow size in
"hand-widths" (no formal units at this grade); the generated illustration
[`assets/light-beam-materials.png`](assets/light-beam-materials.png). All
investigations are adult-supervised; see [teacher-guide](teacher-guide.md)
for safety notes. No sun-looking at any point: outdoor shadow work uses
the learner's own shadow on the ground with the adult, or is replaced by
the indoor flashlight version.

## 16-session pacing

Sessions are 20–25 minutes. Each names its activity and success criterion.

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lesson 01** — Seeing in the dark | Says what is in the dark box before and after the light; states "we see it only when light shines on it." |
| 2 | Practice — Dark-box detective | Predicts, then checks, 4 objects in the dark box; records light-on/light-off with drawings. |
| 3 | Observation — Light-source hunt | Sorts 6 room/home items into "makes its own light" vs. "needs light shone on it." |
| 4 | Review — Light or no light game + Unit 01 retrieval | Sorts 8 picture cards by "can we see it in the dark?"; asks one "I notice / I wonder" question. |
| 5 | **Lesson 02** — What materials do to light | Names all four material words and matches each to its beam result. |
| 6 | Practice — Beam tests, one material at a time | Tests 4 materials fairly (same flashlight, same distance); records bright spot / dim glow / dark shadow / bounced light. |
| 7 | Practice — Picture investigation with the generated illustration | Answers 4 questions from the image (or its text alternative); sorts the three panels correctly. |
| 8 | Review — Material sort game + journal check | Sorts 8 new items by material word without help; journal reviewed same day. |
| 9 | **Lesson 03** — Shadows: shape and size | Explains a shadow needs light + an object; shows a shadow grows when the object moves toward the light. |
| 10 | Practice — Shadow hunt and shadow-size test | Finds 4 shadows; measures one shadow near vs. far in hand-widths and records the change. |
| 11 | **Lesson 04** — Sending messages with light | Agrees a 3-signal flashlight code with the adult and sketches the messenger plan. |
| 12 | Project — Build the flashlight messenger, Part 1 | Builds the device from the sketch; first test across the room succeeds or fails honestly. |
| 13 | **Lesson 05** — Light messenger vs. sound messenger | Runs the same message through both devices; records which got through and where each fails. |
| 14 | Project — Test two designs and compare, Part 2 | Completes the comparison table; states which messenger works better in light, in noise, and in the dark, with a reason. |
| 15 | Review — Shadow-puppet storytelling + formative quiz | Tells a 3-part shadow story (beginning/middle/end) and completes the [formative quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md). |
| 16 | Assessment — [Unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md) + re-teach planning | Completes all 4 performance tasks; adult records readiness per objective. |

Weeks 11–13 introduce and practice; Week 14 (track week 14) is the review
week: the messenger comparison, the shadow-puppet story, re-teaching, the
formative check, and the culminating assessment. If the learner is not yet
secure on an objective after Session 16, use the diagnostic/catch-up
guidance in the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) before starting Unit 04
— the track's flexible weeks exist for exactly this.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, drawing, or gesture responses; the adult
scribes. Each lesson includes a seated-table and a floor-play variant, a
language-access support, an alternate response mode, and an extension.
Material words are never taught by color alone: every material is also
named by what it does to light (bright spot / dim glow / dark shadow /
bounced light), so a learner who cannot distinguish colors can still sort
by result. The generated illustration ships with a text-only alternative
carrying every panel and wall result the activities ask about. For a
learner sensitive to darkness, the dark box is used with the lid ajar and
the room lights dimmed rather than off, and the learner may hold the
flashlight themselves throughout.
