# Unit 02 — Sound, Vibration, and Communication

**Grade 1 · Science · Weeks 7–10 (4 weeks, 16 sessions, 20–25 minutes each)**

Part of [Grade 1 Science](../../README.md) · [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) ·
Issue [#11](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/11). Draft curriculum —
not educator-approved, not aligned/certified by any state.

## Big idea

Every sound starts with something **shaking back and forth** — vibrating.
When a rubber band twangs, when a drum booms, when a voice calls across the
room, something is moving very fast, and that movement can make other things
move too. Once we know how sound works, we can build simple devices that
carry a message farther than our voices can reach.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of this unit the learner will be able to:

1. Notice and describe everyday sounds with **loud/soft** and **high/low**
   language, and sort sounds by what made them (Lessons 01–02).
2. Plan and carry out a simple investigation that shows a **vibrating
   material makes sound** — observing the shake and hearing the sound
   together, e.g., a twanged rubber band or a struck drum (Lesson 02;
   1-PS4-1).
3. Observe and describe that **sound can make materials move** — rice hops
   on a struck drum; a louder sound moves it more (Lesson 03; 1-PS4-1).
4. Design, build, and test a simple **device that uses sound to communicate
   over a distance**; compare two designs and explain which works better
   and why (Lessons 04–05, [project](project.md); 1-PS4-4, K-2-ETS1-2,
   K-2-ETS1-3).
5. Follow the unit's sound-safety rules in every investigation: protect
   ears from loud sounds, keep small materials away from the mouth, and
   never put string or cords around the neck (all lessons; track
   objective 10).

Oral, pointing, drawing, or adult-scribed responses count as evidence
throughout. The adult reads all prompts aloud; learner independence
grows only as far as writing stamina allows.

## Vocabulary

Adult introduces each word in context during the lesson; learners are not
expected to spell them.

- **sound** — what your ears notice
- **vibrate** — to shake back and forth very fast
- **vibration** — the fast shaking that makes a sound
- **loud** — a big, strong sound (covers your ears sound)
- **soft / quiet** — a small, gentle sound (whisper sound)
- **high** — a sound like a bird's tweet
- **low** — a sound like a big drum's boom
- **message** — words or a signal you send to someone
- **communicate** — to send a message so someone else gets it
- **device** — a tool you build to solve a problem
- **plan** — to decide what you will do before you do it
- **test** — to try something and watch what happens
- **compare** — to tell how two things are the same and different
- **sketch** — a quick drawing of your idea
- **signal** — a sound that means something (a clap code, a bell ring)
- **protect** — to keep safe (cover ears near loud sounds)

## Materials (whole unit)

Science journal, pencils, crayons; a shoebox or oatmeal container; rubber
bands in 2–3 sizes; a drum (toy drum, or an upside-down pot struck with a
wooden spoon — adult supervises striking); **dry rice in a sealed small
container opened only by the adult** (choking hazard — rice stays on the
drum head, never near the mouth); 2–4 paper cups; about 3 meters of string
or yarn (adult handles; never around the neck); paper clips; a bell or
jingle bells; one sealed shaker (plastic container with dry beans, taped
shut by the adult); printed [cup-string telephone image](assets/cup-string-telephone.png)
or a screen to view it. All investigations are adult-supervised; see
[teacher-guide](teacher-guide.md) for safety notes.

## 16-session pacing

Sessions are 20–25 minutes. Each names its activity and success criterion.

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lesson 01** — Sounds are all around us | Describes 4+ sounds using loud/soft or high/low. |
| 2 | Practice — sound walk + sound sort | Records 6 sounds from the room/home; sorts 4 by what made them. |
| 3 | **Lesson 02** — Vibrating things make sound | Sees the rubber band shake AND hears the twang; states "shaking makes the sound." |
| 4 | Practice — rubber-band box tests | Tests 3 band sizes; records which make higher/lower sounds. |
| 5 | **Lesson 03** — Sound can make things move | Observes rice hop when the drum is struck; louder hit → more hopping. |
| 6 | Practice — rice-on-drum trials | Runs 3 soft + 3 loud strikes; compares with tallies. |
| 7 | **Lesson 04** — Carrying messages with sound | Uses the telephone image to point to what carries the sound; sends one message through a cup-and-string telephone. |
| 8 | Practice — telephone message relay | Sends and receives 3 messages over ~2 meters; string stays tight. |
| 9 | **Lesson 05** — Design a better sound messenger | Defines the problem ("my voice can't reach across the room") and sketches one device idea. |
| 10 | Practice — build and first test | Builds from the sketch; tests whether a whispered message arrives. |
| 11 | **Project** — build design A, test, record | Design A tested at 2 distances; results recorded as yes/no + drawing. |
| 12 | **Project** — build design B, compare, share | Compares A vs B; names which worked better and why. |
| 13 | Review — vibration detectives (retrieval) | Re-runs the Lesson 02 test from memory; restates 1-PS4-1 in own words. |
| 14 | Review — redesign relay + safety check | Improves one design; restates the 3 sound-safety rules. |
| 15 | Formative check — [week-04 quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md) | Adult notes which objectives need reteaching. |
| 16 | Culminating — [unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md) + sharing circle | Assessment evidence recorded; learner shares one design. |

Sessions 13–16 revisit earlier learning (retrieval practice): Session 13
reuses Unit 01's "I notice / I wonder" routine and the Lesson 02 shake
test; Session 14 reuses the Lesson 05 sketch-compare routine; the
assessment asks the learner to apply all five objectives to one new
sound device.

## Safety and boundaries

Every investigation is adult-supervised. The unit teaches: ears are
protected from loud sounds (cover ears or move away — whisper test);
small materials (rice, beads, beans) stay on the table or drum head and
never go near the mouth; string is handled by the adult and **never**
wrapped around any neck; the adult opens the rice container and reseals
it. Observation-only alternatives are given for every hazard. No learner
data, names, or photos are recorded in this repository.
