# Lesson 01 — Sounds Are All Around Us

**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** journal, crayons, a bell (or jingle
bells), a sealed shaker the adult prepared (plastic container with dry
beans, taped shut), a book to tap

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will become a sound detective —
you will find sounds around you and describe each one as loud or soft and
high or low.

**Readiness check:** "Close your eyes for ten seconds. Hold up one finger
for every sound you hear." Learner holds up 1–3 fingers — ready.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

A **sound** is what your ears notice. Sounds are everywhere, even when we
are not listening for them.

**Worked example 1 — loud/soft, high/low:**

> Adult (rings the bell once, gently): "I heard a sound! Let me describe
> it like a scientist. It was **soft** — a gentle sound, like a whisper.
> And it was **high** — like a bird's tweet. Now listen again." *(Rings the
> bell hard.)* "Now it was **loud** — a big strong sound. Same bell, but
> loud or soft changed. The high part stayed."

**Worked example 2 — sorting by what made it:**

> Adult (taps the book, shakes the shaker, claps hands — one at a time):
> "Book tap: soft and low. Shaker: soft and... a little high, a rattle.
> Clap: loud! Now I sort them: the *book tap* and the *clap* both came
> from my **hands** hitting something; the *shaker* sound came from beans
> **moving inside**. Sorting by *what made the sound* is how scientists
> start."

Key idea to say plainly: **Every sound can be described — loud or soft,
high or low — and every sound was made by something.**

## Guided practice — sound hunt

The adult leads a slow "sound walk" around one room (or the learner sits
still and listens while the adult makes 5–6 everyday sounds: closing a
book, pouring water, jingling keys, tearing paper, footsteps).

For each sound, the adult asks: "Was it loud or soft? High or low? What
made it?" The adult scribes the learner's answers as a two-column journal
table: **Sound** | **What made it**.

## Independent practice

Learner makes **four sounds of their own** (stomp, whisper, finger snap,
tap the table) and tells the adult for each: loud/soft, high/low. The
adult scribes; the learner draws a star next to the loudest one.

## Application

"Your grown-up is in another room and you want them to come. Which would
work better — a whisper or a loud clap? Why?" (Learner should choose the
loud clap and say the whisper would not be heard far away. This previews
Lesson 04's communication problem.)

## Exit check

Learner names **two sounds** from the walk and describes each with
loud/soft and high/low. Oral or pointing to the journal drawings is fine.

## Supports

- **Language access:** pair each word with a gesture — arms wide for loud,
  finger to lips for soft, hand high in the air for high, hand low for low.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner points to four picture cards
  (loud/soft/high/low) the adult draws.
- **Hearing differences:** the lesson works through felt vibration too —
  the learner can rest a hand on the table while the adult taps it and
  describe what the hand *feels*.

## Extension

"Secret sound": the learner makes a sound behind the adult's back; the
adult guesses what made it from loud/soft + high/low clues. Then they
swap roles — the learner guesses.

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- *Loud means high.* Young learners often call a loud sound "high."
  Separate the two with the bell: ring it gently (soft + high), then
  strike the book (loud + low). "Loud is about *size* of the sound; high
  is about *which* sound."
- *Silence means no sounds exist.* During the eyes-closed check, point out
  the hum of the fridge or a clock tick — quiet sounds count.
- *Describing is optional.* Insist on the words: scientists describe
  before they explain.
