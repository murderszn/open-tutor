# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Sound, Vibration, and Communication

Adult-facing. Learner pages never link here with answers; the unit README
links it as "teaching notes." Misconception notes also appear inside each
lesson; this guide adds pacing, readiness, and reteaching.

## Pacing notes

- **Sessions 1–6** are the science core (1-PS4-1): noticing sounds, then
  the two-way vibration rule. Do not rush Lesson 02 — the "shake makes
  sound" rule is the unit's load-bearing idea.
- **Sessions 7–10** shift to engineering (1-PS4-4): the telephone is the
  worked example; Lesson 05 hands design to the learner.
- **Sessions 11–12** are the project; **Session 14** is a deliberate
  redesign slot, not filler — engineers improve after testing.
- If a session runs short, the practice.md set for that lesson is the
  overflow; if it runs long, move the practice set to the next day's
  warm-up.

## What "ready to move on" looks like

- **After Lesson 02:** the learner can twang the band and say "it shakes,
  so it makes sound" without the adult supplying the words.
- **After Lesson 03:** the learner predicts rice behavior before a strike
  and reads the result as evidence ("loud → hops").
- **After Lesson 04:** the learner can assemble the telephone, check
  string tightness, and send one message.
- **After Lesson 05:** the sketch exists *before* the build, and the
  learner can name the problem in one sentence.
- **End of unit:** see the unit assessment scoring; the non-negotiable is
  the vibration rule + safe device use.

## Common misconceptions (unit-wide)

- *Loud = high.* Drill the bell (soft+high) vs. book (loud+low) contrast
  until the two dimensions separate.
- *Sound is stored inside objects.* The still-drum strike (Lesson 02) and
  the saggy-string test (Lesson 04) are the counters — run them whenever
  the idea resurfaces.
- *The device is magic / works like a real phone.* Keep explanations at
  "shaking travels along the string"; real-phone electronics are
  explicitly out of scope (assessment boundary).
- *A failed test means I failed.* Reframe every miss as data: "What did
  the test teach us? Change one thing." Session 14 exists to practice
  this.
- *Bigger/louder is always better.* Louder moves more, but louder also
  needs ear care — the safety objective is the counterweight.

## Reteaching moves

| Weak objective | Reteach (15 min) |
|---|---|
| O1 (describe sounds) | Replay Lesson 01's mystery sounds with the gesture pairs; add 4 new sounds |
| O2 (vibration makes sound) | Throat-hum with fingers on the voice box — the learner *feels* their own vibration; then re-twang the band |
| O3 (sound moves things) | Re-run the rice tally with the learner calling "soft/loud"; graph the two columns |
| O4 (device + compare) | Rebuild the telephone together with the learner directing; re-run the 3-message relay |
| O5 (safety) | "Spot the unsafe plan" card game from the quiz, 6 new scenarios |

## Project

Full rubric and steps: [project.md](project.md). Two teaching notes:

1. **Contrast the designs.** If both designs are telephones, require
   design B to use a different sound-maker (bell, shaker, tap code) — the
   K-2-ETS1-3 comparison needs genuinely different strengths.
2. **Honor honest limits.** "Neither reached 5 meters, but the bell won at
   3 meters" is a strong result. Engineers report what the test showed,
   including limits.

**Differentiation:** a learner who finishes early runs the Session 14
improvement test at *two* distances and adds the data to the compare
chart. A learner who struggles gets the adult as "hands" while they stay
the "director" — the design remains theirs.

## Safety script (say at the start of Sessions 3, 5, 7, 9, 11)

"Three sound-safety rules: **one** — we protect our ears; loud sounds stay
away from ears. **Two** — small things stay on the table or the drum, never
near mouths. **Three** — string stays in hands, never around necks."
The adult opens and reseals the rice container; the learner never handles
loose rice.

## Accessibility

- **Hearing differences:** every sound activity has a felt/seen channel —
  throat buzz, drum-skin touch, rice hop, wiggle-line drawings. Assess
  through vibration observation, not hearing acuity.
- **Fine motor:** the adult ties knots and threads cups; the learner
  directs placement.
- **Attention:** each session has one physical "do" moment in the first 5
  minutes (twang, strike, ring) — lead with it.
