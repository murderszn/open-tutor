# Practice Sets — Unit 02

Extra 4–6-task oral/manipulative/drawing sets for each lesson. The adult
reads every prompt aloud and scribes; the learner answers orally, by
pointing, by drawing, or with manipulatives. Answer key:
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md#practice).

## Lesson 01 — sounds all around us

1. The adult makes 4 mystery sounds behind a screen (keys jingle, water
   pour, book close, zipper). Learner names loud/soft + high/low for
   each.
2. "Sound sort": 6 picture cards the adult draws (dog barking, whisper,
   drum, bird, car horn, rain) — learner sorts into **loud** vs **soft**
   piles.
3. Learner closes eyes; adult taps the table near, then far. "Which tap
   was louder? Why?" (Closer = louder.)
4. "High/low parade": learner makes a high sound (squeak) and a low sound
   (stomp) and labels each.
5. Error-analysis: the adult says, "A whisper is a high sound." Learner
   agrees or corrects — and explains. (Whisper is *soft*; it can be high
   *or* low. The correction is the win.)

## Lesson 02 — vibrating things make sound

1. Learner hums with fingers on throat: "Do you feel the buzz? Buzz =
   vibrating. No buzz, no hum — try it."
2. The adult plucks the rubber band while the learner watches through a
   cardboard tube (focuses the eyes on the blur). "What do you see?"
3. "Vibrate or not?" — 4 items: a ringing bell (yes), a sleeping cat
   (no), a bouncing ball while bouncing (yes), a book on a shelf (no).
   Learner sorts and says the rule for each yes.
4. Learner draws 3 vibrating sound-makers with wiggle lines and tells the
   adult one sentence for each.
5. The adult holds the drum skin still and strikes: "Why is the sound so
   small now?" (The shaking was stopped.)

## Lesson 03 — sound can make things move

1. Learner predicts, then the adult tests: "Soft strike — will the rice
   tremble or hop?" (3 trials; learner tallies.)
2. "Loud strike — tremble or hop?" (3 trials; tally; compare with set 1.)
3. Learner places a hand lightly on the drum skin during a strike: "What
   does the shaking feel like — a tickle or a thump?" (Soft = tickle,
   loud = thump.)
4. Paper-strip test: learner hums softly, then loudly, at a dangling
   paper strip. "Which hum moved the paper more?"
5. Explain-your-reasoning: "The rice hopped on the loud strike. What does
   that prove about the loud sound?" (It moves things more — its shaking
   is stronger.)

## Lesson 04 — carrying messages with sound

1. Learner assembles the telephone from a parts pile with the adult
   narrating each step ("thread, knot, pull tight").
2. "Saggy vs. tight": learner sends the same message twice — once with a
   saggy string, once tight. "Which arrived? Why?"
3. The learner is the sender for 2 messages and the receiver for 2
   (adult scribes ✓/✗).
4. "Broken telephone" diagnosis: the adult secretly leaves the string
   saggy; the learner must find and fix the problem before resending.
5. From the generated image: "Point to three things: the cup that talks,
   the cup that listens, and the part that carries the message." Then the
   learner covers the image and names them from memory.

## Lesson 05 — design a better sound messenger

1. Learner restates the engineering order from the picture strip
   (problem → sketch → build → test → compare → improve) by pointing.
2. "Problem match": 3 problems (across the room / across the playground /
   secret yes-no) — learner picks which device fits each (bell, shout
   through cupped hands, tap code) and says why.
3. Learner sketches a device for a *new* problem: "Tell the class it is
   story time from the hallway." Adult asks: "How does its shape help?"
4. Build-and-test with loose parts; adult scribes yes/not-yet.
5. Error-analysis: "My design failed, so I am a bad engineer." Learner
   corrects the adult: "No — a failed test is data. Change one thing and
   test again."
