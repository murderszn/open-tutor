# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Questions, Observations, and Safe Investigations

For the guiding adult. Learner-facing material is in the lessons; this
guide holds the teaching judgment: pacing, misconceptions, supports, and
what to do when evidence says reteach.

## Unit at a glance

- **Sessions:** 16 × 20–25 min (see [README](README.md) pacing table).
- **Throughline:** every session rehearses observe → record → share, so
  the habits compound. Lessons 01–05 each introduce one practice;
  Sessions 11–16 apply all five.
- **Readiness signals:** learner can sit for a 20-minute guided activity,
  answer "what do you notice?" with at least one observation, and follow
  a two-step direction. If not yet, extend the diagnostic play routines
  (scope-and-sequence Weeks 1–2) before starting Session 1.
- **Reteach signals:** formative quiz (Session 15) below 12/16, or the
  project rubric showing "Starting" on the same criterion twice → loop
  back to that lesson + its [practice](practice.md) set before the unit
  assessment. Do not advance a learner who cannot yet name the four
  safety rules — Unit 02's sound investigations assume them.

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

### Lesson 01 — Testable questions

- **Core misconception:** "not testable" sounds like "bad question."
  Say explicitly, more than once: wonder-list questions are welcome; they
  just need tools we don't have yet. Never let sorting feel like grading
  the learner's curiosity.
- **Watch for:** learners who classify by *interest* ("I like this
  question, so it's testable"). Always follow the sort with "how would
  you explore it?" — the method is the test of the sort.
- **Pacing:** the two-column journal page becomes a unit-long tool;
  keep it visible for Sessions 2–14.

### Lesson 02 — Senses

- **Core misconception:** opinions ("it's pretty") offered as
  observations. Redirect with "what did your eyes notice that makes you
  say that?" until descriptions are sense-based.
- **Taste rule:** state it every session this unit, not just once. The
  rule is absolute during science: nothing in the mouth unless the adult
  names a safe food.
- **Sensory sensitivity:** touch/smell opt-outs are legitimate; record
  them here and plan look-based questions for that learner's project.
- **Wafting:** demonstrate smelling from a short distance; never let the
  learner put their nose into a material.

### Lesson 03 — Tools

- **Core misconception:** "the tool does the observing." Keep the
  learner as the scientist: "the lens helped *your eyes* see."
- **Ruler pitfall:** learners read the farthest visible number instead
  of lining up the end. Re-teach the lineup every time until it's
  automatic; exact units don't matter, consistent method does.
- **Flashlight note:** it reveals what's there — it doesn't create
  color. Unit 03 will build on this; don't over-explain now.

### Lesson 04 — Recording

- **Core misconception:** drawings must be pretty. Praise quick,
  informative sketches; a labeled blob beats a beautiful irrelevant
  picture.
- **The image:** it is AI-generated practice art (see
  [assets/README.md](assets/README.md)), not a photograph of a real
  experiment. Say so if the learner asks "is this real?"
- **Tally habit:** insist on recording *during* observing, not after —
  "observe → record immediately" is the habit being built.

### Lesson 05 — Safety

- **Core misconception:** rules are only for "dangerous" science. Every
  investigation follows all four rules; habits form now or not at all.
- **The safe-alternative question** ("what can I observe instead?") is
  the unit's most transferable skill — use it in every later unit when a
  plan needs adjusting.
- **Never** demonstrate an unsafe behavior "to show why." Describe,
  don't demo.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** all responses may be oral/pointing/drawn/adult-scribed;
  gesture pairs for vocabulary (see lessons); shorter sessions (15 min)
  with movement breaks are fine — split any session across two days.
- **Extension:** prediction-before-testing (Lesson 01), second-look
  observations (Lesson 02), tool-sorting judgments (Lesson 03),
  dual-record comparisons (Lesson 04), safety-checker role play
  (Lesson 05).
- **Language access:** vocabulary is introduced in context with gestures;
  no spelling or reading is required of the learner anywhere in this unit.

## Materials prep (one-time)

Journal, pencils/crayons, hand lens, ruler, flashlight, apple, 5–8
pebbles or buttons, paper cup of water, small potted seedling or leafy
plant, one opaque bag + mystery object, printed or on-screen observation
image. Total cost is near zero; everything is household or dollar-store.

## Assessment use

- The [week-04 quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md) is formative: use it to
  decide what to reteach, not to label the learner.
- The [unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md) is
  curriculum-embedded, not standardized or validated. Score with the
  rubric in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md#unit-assessment); keep results
  private.
- **Advancement rule:** a learner at "approaching" on the assessment
  re-attempts only the weak tasks after targeted reteaching; a learner
  "starting" overall revisits the lessons. Unit 02 assumes the safety
  rules and the observe→record habit are secure.

## Answer-key discipline

Every learner-facing question has a worked solution in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md). If a learner gives a scientifically
equivalent alternative, accept it and note it for the next revision of
this unit.
