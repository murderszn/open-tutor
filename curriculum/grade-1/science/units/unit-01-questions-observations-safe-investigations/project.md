# Project — My First Investigation

**Sessions 11–12 (plus sharing in Session 16)** · Adult-supervised throughout.

## The mission

The learner becomes a real scientist: pick **one object**, ask **one
testable question** about it, plan **safe** observations, record findings
over three days, and share what was discovered.

This project practices K-2-ETS1-1 at a first-grade level: the learner asks
questions and gathers information to define a simple problem they can
explore.

## Materials

Science journal, pencil/crayons, the learner's chosen object (apple,
pebble collection, seedling, or cup of water), hand lens, ruler,
flashlight — only the tools the plan names.

## Steps

### Session 11 — Plan (20–25 min)

1. **Choose.** The learner picks one object to investigate.
2. **Ask.** With the adult's help, the learner writes (adult scribes) one
   **testable** question about it — a question they can explore by
   looking, touching, or testing. Examples:
   - "How many days until the seedling grows a new leaf?" (observe daily)
   - "Which of my three pebbles is the longest?" (measure with ruler)
   - "What happens to the water's surface when I tap the cup?" (tap, watch, listen)
3. **Safety-check the plan.** Together, check the plan against the four
   safety rules. Write (adult scribes) one sentence: "My plan is safe
   because ___."
4. **Choose tools.** The learner names which tools the investigation
   needs and why ("the ruler, to measure the pebbles").
5. **Predict.** The learner says what they *think* will happen — a
   prediction — and the adult scribes it. Predictions may be wrong; that
   is fine and worth saying.

### Between sessions — Observe (3 days, 5 minutes each)

Each day, the learner observes the object and records **at least two**
observations (words, drawings, or tallies) on a dated journal page. The
adult supervises and scribes on request. If the object is the seedling,
observations must be gentle (look, soft touch of a leaf edge only).

### Session 12 — Conclude and share (20–25 min)

1. **Review the records.** Look back at all three days of journal pages.
2. **Answer the question.** The learner states what they found out, in
   their own words (oral, drawn, or scribed). Compare the finding to the
   prediction: "I predicted ___. I observed ___."
3. **Share.** In the Session 16 sharing circle (or with family), the
   learner shows one journal page and tells the group: my question, what
   I did, what I found.

## Deliverables

1. A written (adult-scribed) investigation plan: question, safety check,
   tools, prediction.
2. Three dated journal pages with at least two recorded observations each.
3. A conclusion: the question answered in the learner's own words.
4. A sharing-circle telling (oral/drawn).

## Rubric

| Criterion | 3 — Strong | 2 — Developing | 1 — Starting |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Question** (Obj. 1, 5) | Question is testable; learner can say how to explore it (look/touch/test) | Question is testable with adult reshaping | Question is a wonder-list question; needs adult to reframe |
| **Observing** (Obj. 2) | Used 2+ senses or a tool on purpose; 4+ distinct observations recorded | Used 1 sense or tool; 2–3 observations | Observations vague ("nice," "cool") or fewer than 2 |
| **Recording** (Obj. 3) | 3 dated pages; 2+ records per day; mix of words/drawings/tallies | 2 pages or 1 record per day | 1 page or records missing |
| **Safety** (Obj. 4) | Plan names a safety rule it follows; all sessions supervised; living things handled gently | Safety checked with heavy adult prompting | Unsafe step attempted or rule ignored |
| **Communicating** (Obj. 3) | Tells question, method, and finding clearly; journal page supports the telling | Tells 2 of the 3 parts | Cannot retell without the adult reading the journal aloud |

**Scoring:** 13–15 = exceeds; 10–12 = meets; 7–9 = approaching (reteach
the weak criterion with [practice.md](practice.md) sets, then re-observe
one day); below 7 = revisit Lessons 01–04 before continuing to Unit 02.

## Accessibility and alternatives

- Non-writing learners: every written element may be dictated, drawn, or
  pointed; the adult scribes and dates.
- If no seedling or living thing is available, the water-cup or pebble
  investigations are complete substitutes.
- If the learner is uncomfortable touching, observation may be sight-only;
  note it in the teacher guide and choose a look-based question.
