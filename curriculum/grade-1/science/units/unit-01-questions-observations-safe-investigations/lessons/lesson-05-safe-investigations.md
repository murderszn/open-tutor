# Lesson 05 — Safe Investigations

**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** journal, crayons, 6 scenario
cards (adult reads aloud; printable cards optional)

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will learn the four safety
rules scientists follow — and how to fix an unsafe plan by choosing a
safe alternative.

**Readiness check:** "Yesterday we used our senses to observe. Which
sense did we say is only for food, and only when a grown-up says so?"
(Taste.) "Today we learn the rules that keep every investigation safe."

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

Scientists explore — but they explore **safely**. Four rules, always:

1. **A grown-up stays with you.** Science is a together activity. Never
   start an investigation alone.
2. **Science things stay out of your mouth.** We observe with eyes, ears,
   hands, and nose — not by tasting, unless the grown-up names a safe food.
3. **Living things are handled gently.** Plants and animals can be hurt;
   we touch softly, and we never squeeze, pull, or poke.
4. **Never look at the sun.** The sun is too bright for eyes. To study
   sunlight, we observe shadows or bright spots it makes — never the sun
   itself.

The adult posts the four rules where the learner can see them (a wall
chart or the journal's inside cover) and reads them together twice.

**Worked example 1 — fixing an unsafe plan:**

> Adult: "Here is a plan: *'I will stare at the sun to observe what it
> looks like.'* Let's check the rules. Rule 4 says: never look at the
> sun. This plan breaks Rule 4 — it is **unsafe**. Now the scientist's
> question: **'What can I observe instead?'** A safe alternative: I can
> observe the sun's *shadow* — I stand with my back to the sun and watch
> my shadow on the ground. I still explore sunlight, but safely."

**Worked example 2 — the mouth rule:**

> Adult: "New plan: *'I will taste the pebbles to see if any are salty.'*
> Check the rules — Rule 2: science things stay out of my mouth. Unsafe!
> Safe alternative: I can **rub** two pebbles and compare their color,
> or **tap** them and compare their sounds. I changed the plan, kept the
> question."

Key idea to say plainly: **When a plan is unsafe, scientists don't quit —
they ask, "What can I observe instead?"**

## Guided practice — safe or unsafe?

The adult reads each scenario; the learner says **safe** or **unsafe**,
names the rule (with prompting), and gives a safe alternative for the
unsafe ones:

1. "Maya smells the flower from a little way away, without touching it." (safe — gentle, wafting)
2. "Leo puts the pebble in his mouth to test if it's smooth." (unsafe — Rule 2; alternative: rub it with a finger)
3. "Ana looks straight at the sun to see its shape." (unsafe — Rule 4; alternative: observe her shadow)
4. "Sam squeezes the seedling's stem hard to test if it's strong." (unsafe — Rule 3; alternative: look closely, touch a leaf softly)
5. "Jon observes the flashlight beam with a grown-up beside him." (safe — Rule 1 followed)
6. "Priya does an experiment alone while the grown-up is in another room." (unsafe — Rule 1; alternative: wait for the grown-up)

## Independent practice

The learner draws **one safety rule** in the journal (e.g., a hand
covering a mouth with a red X, a gentle hand near a plant — the adult
labels the drawing with the rule number). Under it, the adult scribes the
learner's one-sentence telling of the rule.

## Application

Adult presents a new plan: *"I want to find out what the inside of the
seedling's pot soil smells like, so I will stick my whole nose in the
soil."* The learner must: name the broken rule (Rule 3 — gentle with
living things; also reasonable to cite careful smelling), and propose a
safe alternative (waft the air above the soil toward the nose from a
short distance, without touching the plant).

## Exit check

Learner recites the four rules with adult prompts ("Rule 1 starts with
'grown-up'…"). Full wording is not required — the meaning is: grown-up
present, nothing in mouth, gentle with living things, never look at sun.

## Supports

- **Language access:** teach each rule as a gesture + short phrase
  ("grown-up with me," "not in mouth," "gentle hands," "eyes away from
  sun"). The learner may answer the sort with gestures.
- **Alternate response mode:** green/red cards for safe/unsafe; the adult
  scribes the alternative.
- **Anxiety:** frame rules as *scientist tools*, not punishments —
  "rules are how scientists take care of themselves and the living things."

## Extension

The learner becomes the "safety checker" for a stuffed-animal scientist:
the adult proposes three sloppy plans, and the learner catches each broken
rule and prescribes the fix, in role.

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- *Rules are only for "dangerous" science.* Correct: every investigation,
  even looking at pebbles, follows all four rules. Habits form now.
- *Gentle means not touching at all.* Clarify: gentle touch is good
  science; rough handling is not. Demonstrate the difference on the
  learner's own hand.
- *The safe alternative is "do nothing."* Push back: the alternative
  still answers a question — it just uses a safer method.
