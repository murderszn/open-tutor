# Lesson 03 — Parts Help Living Things Meet Their Needs

**Student goal:** I can match a plant or animal part to the need it
meets — food, water, moving, or staying safe — and explain why.
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Unit:** [U04](../README.md) · **Session 9 of 16**

## Materials

- Six part-to-need cards the adult makes before the lesson (picture or
  word on each card): roots / webbed feet / long beak / rabbit ears /
  turtle shell / leaves — plus four need cards: FOOD, WATER, MOVING,
  STAYING SAFE
- The potted plant and one toy animal from earlier lessons
- Science journal, crayons

**Safety note for the adult:** no new materials, no tasting, no live
animals. Cards only. If the learner is tired of cards, the same game
runs with toys and pointing.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Quick game: I name a part, you name its job. Roots?" (Drink water and
hold steady.) "Webbed feet?" (Swim.) "Leaves?" (Catch sunlight, make
food.) If the learner answers two of three, begin. If not, re-run one
pointing round from Lesson 01 or 02 first — do not start a new idea on
a shaky old one.

## Teaching

A **need** is something a living thing must have to stay alive: **food,
water, air, and safety** (a safe place, and a way to escape danger).
Every part we learned is a tool for meeting a need. The scientist's
question is never just "what part is this?" — it is "**what need does
this part meet, and how do I know?**"

Four matches to learn by heart, because each one shows a different kind
of need:

1. **Roots → WATER (and staying put).** Roots drink water from soil,
   and they anchor the plant so wind cannot blow it away. Evidence: our
   celery test — colored water climbed the stem, so water must have
   entered through the cut end the way roots take water in.
2. **Leaves → FOOD (making food).** Leaves catch sunlight and help the
   plant make its own food. Evidence: plants kept in light grow green
   and strong; a plant in a dark cupboard goes pale and weak. (The adult
   states this as a known observation, not as an experiment the learner
   must run on a living plant.)
3. **Webbed feet → MOVING (in water).** A duck's feet are paddles. In
   water they push and steer; on land the duck waddles slowly. Evidence:
   our beak-tools test showed the same rule — the tool must fit the
   place.
4. **Rabbit ears → STAYING SAFE (hearing danger).** Long ears catch
   sounds from far away, so the rabbit hears a fox before it sees one
   and can run. Evidence: animals with big ears turn their ears toward
   a sound — the learner can watch a pet's ears move, or the adult can
   describe it.

Two more matches, for the game: a **turtle shell → STAYING SAFE**
(protection, like a helmet), and a **long thin beak → FOOD** (reaching
nectar deep in a flower, where a short beak cannot reach).

The reasoning pattern, said aloud every time: "**Part** — **need** —
**how I know.**" Example: "Webbed feet — moving in water — because
paddles push water, and ducks swim fast but waddle slowly on land."

## Worked example 1 — roots and water, step by step (modeled)

The adult holds the potted plant and the celery result from Session 4
(or describes it: the celery leaves turned a little colored).

> "Need: WATER. The plant must drink, or it wilts and dies. Part:
> ROOTS. Roots live in soil, where the water is. How do I know? Our
> celery test: we cut the stem and stood it in colored water. The next
> day, color had climbed into the stem and the leaves. Water travels
> up from the bottom. In a whole plant, roots are the bottom — they are
> the part that takes the water in."
>
> "Say it with me: roots — water — because water comes in from the
> bottom and climbs up."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** evidence beats guessing. We do not say
"roots drink" because it sounds nice; we say it because we **saw**
color climb from the water into the plant.

## Worked example 2 — a wrong match, and how to catch it (modeled)

The adult lays out the cards: LEAVES next to STAYING SAFE.

> "Someone matched leaves to staying safe. Hmm. Do leaves protect the
> plant from danger? Not really — leaves are soft, animals eat them,
> and they do not hide the plant. What do leaves do? They catch sunlight
> and help make food. So leaves match FOOD, not STAYING SAFE. The shell
> matches STAYING SAFE — a shell is hard, and it covers the animal."
>
> "The test for every match is: **does this part really do that job?**
> If the answer is 'not really,' the match is wrong, and we try another
> need card."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** a match is a claim, and claims get tested.
"Not really" is a complete and scientific answer — it is how we find
the right match.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds. Offer choices; never demand a spoken answer.

1. Match round: the adult lays out three part cards (webbed feet, long
   beak, turtle shell) and three need cards (MOVING, FOOD, STAYING
   SAFE). The learner matches them and says one reason for each.
   (Webbed feet → MOVING, paddles push water; long beak → FOOD, reaches
   nectar; turtle shell → STAYING SAFE, hard cover protects.)
2. Reason round: the adult gives a match and asks "how do you know?"
   — "Roots match WATER. How do you know?" (Celery test: color climbed
   up from the water.) "Rabbit ears match STAYING SAFE. How do you
   know?" (Ears hear danger early; the rabbit can run.)
3. Swap round: the adult deliberately swaps two matches (leaves →
   STAYING SAFE, shell → FOOD) and asks, "Are these right?" (No —
   leaves make food; a shell does not find food, it protects.)
4. New-animal round: "A cat has sharp claws. Which need — FOOD, MOVING,
   or STAYING SAFE?" (Accept FOOD — claws grasp prey — or STAYING SAFE
   — claws defend. The adult accepts either **with a reason**, and says
   so: some parts meet more than one need, and the reason decides.)
5. Plant round: "A flower makes seeds. Which need is that — FOOD,
   WATER, or making new plants?" (Making new plants — growing and
   surviving as a kind. Accept "growing" as the learner's wording.)
6. If the learner is verbal: "Finish the sentence: a part meets a need
   when it helps a living thing ___." (Stay alive and grow: get food
   and water, move, or stay safe.)

## Independent practice

The learner draws two matches in the journal: one plant part and one
animal part, each drawn with its need written (by the adult, from
dictation) beside it. The adult observes: is the part real, is the need
real, and does the pairing make sense? When finished, the adult asks,
"How do you know that match is right?" Full solutions are in the
[answer key](../answer-key.md).

## Application

"Winter is coming to the pond. The duck needs to swim to food, and the
fish needs to steer away from danger. Which parts do they use — and
what would happen if they swapped parts?" The learner answers: the duck
uses webbed feet, the fish uses fins and tail; swapped, the duck could
not paddle well and the fish could not steer in water, because each
tool fits its place. (Accept any answer that keeps place-fit: the tool
must fit the water and the animal.)

## Exit check

The adult lays out four part cards and four need cards, mixed. The
learner matches at least three correctly with a reason, by speaking,
pointing, or drawing lines. Three out of four with reasons counts as
secure for today.

## Supports, alternate response, extension

- **Language access:** need cards carry a picture as well as the word
  (cup of water, food, arrow for moving, shield for safety); the adult
  reads every card aloud.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing to the match, drawing a line,
  or acting the job all count; no writing is required.
- **Seated-table variant:** cards on the table; the learner sorts by
  sliding cards together.
- **Floor-play variant:** need words are taped to four floor zones;
  the learner carries each part card to its zone.
- **Extension:** "A cactus has spines instead of big leaves. Which two
  needs might spines meet?" (STAYING SAFE — spines keep animals from
  eating it — and saving WATER, since small leaves lose less water.
  Accept either with a reason; the adult explains both.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: one part = one need, always. Re-teach that some
parts do two jobs (roots drink **and** anchor; ears hear **and** warn)
and that a match is only right if the reason holds. Also watch for
"leaves protect the plant" — leaves are food-makers, not armor. See the
[teacher guide](../teacher-guide.md). Answer-key reference:
[answer key, Lesson 03](../answer-key.md#lesson-03).
