# Lesson 01 — Plant Parts You Can See

**Student goal:** I can name the outside parts of a plant — roots, stem,
leaves, flower — and say what each part does.
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Unit:** [U04](../README.md) · **Session 1 of 16**

## Materials

- One potted plant with a visible stem and leaves (bean seedling,
  sunflower seedling, or a houseplant)
- The generated illustration
  [`assets/plant-animal-parts.png`](../assets/plant-animal-parts.png)
  (printed or on a screen) plus its text alternative in
  [`assets/README.md`](../assets/README.md#text-only-alternative)
- The labeled diagram
  [`assets/plant-animal-parts-diagram.svg`](../assets/plant-animal-parts-diagram.svg)
- Science journal, crayons

**Safety note for the adult:** handle the plant yourself if it must come
out of its pot; the learner looks and points, then the plant goes straight
back into soil and is watered. Nobody tastes any part of any plant. Wash
hands after touching soil. If no plant may be disturbed, use the picture
and the diagram only — the learning is identical.

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Yesterday in our garden walk (or: look at this plant), what did you
notice growing outside? A tall green thing? Something colorful?" Point to
the plant. "This is one whole plant. Today we take it apart — with our
eyes, not our hands — into four parts. Can you point to the green flat
things?" (Leaves.) If the learner can point to any one part, begin.

## Teaching

A plant is a living thing, and like you, it has outside parts you can
see and touch. Each part has a job — something it does to help the plant
live and grow. Here are the four parts:

1. **Roots** — the part that grows **underground**, in the soil. Roots
   have two jobs: they **drink water** (and food from the soil) for the
   plant, and they **hold the plant steady**, like an anchor, so wind
   does not blow it away.
2. **Stem** — the stalk that grows **up from the roots**. The stem has
   two jobs: it **holds the plant up** so leaves and flowers can reach
   sunlight, and it **carries water** from the roots up to the rest of
   the plant, like a straw or a lift.
3. **Leaves** — the flat green parts that grow **off the stem**. Leaves
   **catch sunlight** and help the plant **make its own food**. A plant
   with no leaves cannot make enough food.
4. **Flower** — the colorful part at the top (or on a branch). The
   flower's job is to **make seeds**. Seeds can grow into new plants.
   (Some plants also make a **fruit** — the part that holds seeds, like
   an apple or a tomato. A seed is a tiny packet that can grow into a
   new plant.)

Say it as a chain, touching each part on the real plant or the picture
as you go: "Roots drink and hold on. Stem lifts and carries. Leaves catch
sunlight and make food. Flower makes seeds." A plant needs **all** its
parts working together.

## Worked example 1 — naming parts on the real plant (modeled)

The adult holds the potted plant where the learner can see it, and
points slowly, top to bottom, then bottom to top.

> "Start at the top. This colorful part is the **flower**. Its job is
> to make seeds. Come down the tall green stalk — that is the **stem**.
> It holds the plant up and carries water up from below. These flat
> green things are **leaves**. They catch sunlight and help make food.
> And under the soil, where we cannot see without digging, are the
> **roots**. They drink water and hold the plant steady."
>
> "Now you try, pointing: flower… stem… leaves… roots. Say the job
> with each one: flower makes seeds; stem lifts and carries; leaves
> catch sunlight; roots drink and hold on."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** we name parts by **where they are** as
well as what they look like — flower on top, stem in the middle,
leaves off the stem, roots underneath. Place + job together is how we
remember.

## Worked example 2 — the picture plant, and a wrong-guess check (modeled)

Show the generated illustration, plant side only (cover the duck with a
hand or a sheet of paper if the learner is distracted by it).

> "Find the plant in this picture. Where are its roots? They are the
> white stringy parts **in the brown soil** — you can see them because
> the picture cuts the soil open like a cake. Where is the stem? The
> tall green stalk. Leaves? The two big green flats. Flower? The big
> yellow star at the top."
>
> "Now a trick question. Someone says: 'The roots catch sunlight,
> because they are the biggest part in the picture.' Is that right?
> No! Roots live **underground**, where there is no sunlight to catch.
> The **leaves** catch sunlight. Size in a picture does not decide the
> job — **place and what we observe** decide the job."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** a picture can show roots only by cutting
the soil open. In real life roots are hidden — that is why we must
remember their place (underground) and their job (drink and hold on),
not just what they look like.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds. Offer choices; never demand a spoken answer.

1. Pointing round: "Point to the stem. Point to a leaf. Point to the
   flower. Point to where the roots are hiding." (Any order; the adult
   names each part as the learner points.)
2. Job round: the adult points to a part and asks, "What is this part's
   job?" (Roots → drink water and hold steady; stem → hold up and
   carry water; leaves → catch sunlight and help make food; flower →
   make seeds.)
3. Missing-part round: "If a plant had no roots, what would go wrong?"
   (It could not drink water, and wind could blow it over.) "If a plant
   had no leaves?" (It could not catch sunlight to make food.)
4. Picture round: show the illustration again. "Find one part and tell
   me its job." Repeat until the learner has named all four parts at
   least once. (Learners who cannot use the picture use the text
   alternative — same questions, same answers.)
5. Error-analysis round: the adult says, very seriously: "The stem
   drinks water straight from the soil." Ask: "Is that right?"
   (Expected: not quite. The **roots** drink from the soil; the stem
   **carries** the water up. Both are needed, but the jobs are
   different.)
6. If the learner is verbal: "Finish the sentence: leaves catch ___."
   (Sunlight, to help make food.)

## Independent practice

The learner draws the plant from the picture (or from the real plant) in
the journal: soil line, roots below it, stem, two leaves, flower. The
adult labels each part from dictation. The adult sits nearby and
observes: are all four parts present, and is each part in the right
place (roots below the soil line, flower at the top)? When finished, the
adult asks, "Which part drinks water?" (Roots.) Full solutions are in
the [answer key](../answer-key.md).

## Application

"Your plant is thirsty. Where does the water go first — on the leaves,
or into the soil by the roots?" The learner answers and explains:
water goes into the soil, because roots drink. The adult pours a little
water into the pot together with the learner, and the learner watches
the soil darken. (If the plant must not be watered, the learner points
to the soil on the picture and says where the water would go.)

## Exit check

The adult points to each part on the picture in mixed order. The learner
names it and says its job, by speaking, pointing to a word card, or
gesturing (for example, a drinking mime for roots). Three out of four
parts with correct jobs counts as secure for today; the fourth is
re-taught in Practice Session A.

## Supports, alternate response, extension

- **Language access:** the adult says the part word, the learner repeats
  or points; a home-language word for each part is welcome alongside the
  English word.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing to the part, drawing it, or
  acting its job (arms up like a stem, hands drinking like roots) all
  count; no writing is required.
- **Seated-table variant:** work with the potted plant on the table and
  the picture flat in front of the learner.
- **Floor-play variant:** build a giant plant on the floor with the
  learner's body as the stem, arms as leaves, and yarn as roots under a
  blanket "soil line."
- **Extension:** "Some plants have a fruit. Find an apple or a tomato in
  the kitchen — which plant part is it, and what is inside it?" (A fruit;
  seeds inside.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: "the flower is the plant" or "roots eat soil."
Re-teach with place + job: the flower is one part; roots drink water
from soil, they do not eat the soil itself. See the
[teacher guide](../teacher-guide.md) for reteaching steps. Answer-key
reference: [answer key, Lesson 01](../answer-key.md#lesson-01).
