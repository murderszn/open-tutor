# Answer Key — Unit 04

**Unit:** [U04](README.md) · For the guiding adult only. Keep this file
separate from student materials. Every answer below was solved
independently from the lesson, practice, quiz, and assessment questions
and reconciled with them. Where observation varies (celery color, tool
tests, design tests), the key gives the expected pattern and what counts
as acceptable, not a single required result.

## Lesson 01

- **Guided practice:** (1) pointing — stem, leaf, flower, roots below
  the soil; (2) jobs — roots drink water and hold steady; stem holds up
  and carries water; leaves catch sunlight and help make food; flower
  makes seeds; (3) no roots → cannot drink, wind can blow it over; no
  leaves → cannot catch sunlight to make food; (4) picture round — all
  four parts named with jobs; (5) error analysis — "stem drinks from
  soil" is not quite right: roots drink, stem carries; (6) leaves catch
  sunlight.
- **Independent practice:** drawing shows soil line, roots below it,
  stem, two leaves, flower on top. "Which part drinks water?" — roots.
- **Application:** water goes into the soil by the roots, because roots
  drink.
- **Exit check:** 3 of 4 parts with correct jobs = secure for today.

## Lesson 02

- **Guided practice:** (1) any two visible parts with correct jobs;
  (2) visible ears — rabbit, cat, dog; duck ears hidden under feathers
  but the duck can still hear; (3) moving — duck paddles/swims, fish
  wiggles fins and tail, rabbit hops, bird flaps wings; (4) picture —
  beak (flat yellow-orange), webbed feet (orange, flat), tail fin on
  the fish; (5) error analysis — "fish use fins to walk on land" is
  wrong: fins swim and steer in water; (6) webbed feet help a duck swim.
- **Independent practice:** drawing of a duck or fish with three real
  outside parts and correct jobs. "Which part helps your animal move?"
  — duck: webbed feet (or wings); fish: fins and tail.
- **Application:** duck needs webbed feet (pond), rabbit needs hopping
  legs (grass); swapped tools do not fit the place.
- **Exit check:** 4 correct part-and-job pairs across two animals =
  secure for today.

## Lesson 03

- **Guided practice:** (1) webbed feet → MOVING (paddles push water);
  long beak → FOOD (reaches nectar); turtle shell → STAYING SAFE (hard
  cover protects); (2) roots → WATER — celery test, color climbed up;
  rabbit ears → STAYING SAFE — ears hear danger early, rabbit can run;
  (3) swap — leaves → STAYING SAFE is wrong (leaves make food); shell →
  FOOD is wrong (shell protects); (4) cat claws — FOOD (grasp prey) or
  STAYING SAFE (defend), either with a reason; (5) flower makes seeds
  — making new plants / growing; (6) a part meets a need when it helps
  a living thing stay alive and grow.
- **Independent practice:** one plant part and one animal part, each
  with a sensible need (for example, roots → WATER; webbed feet →
  MOVING).
- **Application:** duck uses webbed feet, fish uses fins and tail;
  swapped, neither moves well, because each tool fits its place.
- **Exit check:** at least 3 of 4 matches with reasons = secure.

## Lesson 04

- **Guided practice:** (1) problem sentence — any sentence naming the
  chosen problem (A, B, or C); (2) nature part — A: bird beak, pinches
  and grips; B: duck beak, scoops and drains; C: roots, spread and hold
  steady; (3) sketch — learner points to the copied shape in their
  drawing; (4) label — "copies a ___" beside the circled shape;
  (5) error analysis — plain cup is not ready for Problem A: it shows
  no beak shape; (6) sketch before building so the plan (and the copied
  shape) is not forgotten.
- **Independent practice:** sketch shows a tool for the chosen problem
  with the copied shape visible and labeled. "What will you build
  first?" — the part carrying the copied shape.
- **Application:** yes, a smaller tool still works if the copied shape
  stays the same — shape, not size, does the job.
- **Exit check:** all three questions answered (problem, copied part,
  where the shape is in the drawing) = secure.

## Lesson 05

- **Guided practice:** (1) one change only for version 2 (any single
  change counts); (2) same setup for both versions — same bowl/table/
  beans/leaves, same three tries; (3) test results — recorded as
  observed; no winner is fixed in advance; (4) comparison sentence —
  "Version ___ works better, because ___," with the winner matching
  the recorded win count; (5) error analysis — "version 2 won once so
  it is best" is wrong: count all three tries, the version with more
  wins is better; (6) a fair test changes one thing at a time.
- **Independent practice:** comparison table honestly completed, star
  beside the version with more wins. "What would you change to make
  the loser better?" — any change tied to the observed failure (for
  example, wider arms, more holes, wider base).
- **Application:** liking is not testing — test both designs the same
  way, count wins, let results decide.
- **Exit check:** comparison sentence stated and the one changed thing
  named = secure.

## Practice sessions

- **A — Plant-part hunt:** stem, leaf, flower (where present) named on
  two different plants; roots live below the soil; a trunk is a stem;
  grass without flowers is still a plant with roots, stems, and leaves.
- **B — Celery setup:** prediction recorded exactly as said (any
  prediction counts, including "nothing will happen"); fair-test reason
  — one stalk and one glass so only time changes; the cut end does the
  roots' job of taking water in.
- **C — Celery check + sort:** color climbed up from the water (if
  faint, "a little color climbed up" counts); roots take water in and
  water travels up; sort at least 7 of 8 cards; apple → FRUIT pile
  (holds seeds; flower-pile placement accepted with the reason "flowers
  make fruits and seeds"); a white celery does not disprove roots
  drinking — one test can fail for many reasons; re-test or wait longer.
- **D — Picture investigation:** plant parts top-to-bottom — flower,
  leaves, stem, roots (bottom-to-top reverses the order); duck parts —
  any three of beak, wing, tail, webbed feet, eyes; fish parts — any two
  of fins, tail, eye; roots help the plant drink; webbed feet or
  fins/tail help an animal swim (animal must be named); duck ears
  hidden does not mean the duck cannot hear.
- **E — Beak tools test:** tweezers win for single beans; spoon wins
  for scooping; tweezers-like beak — small seed-eating bird; spoon-like
  beak — duck; tweezers would not help a duck scoop, because they pick
  one thing at a time.
- **F — Animal-part sort:** at least 7 of 8 cards sorted correctly;
  fin → fish, fins swim and steer; wings also cover and warm the duck,
  not only fly; hardest part — any honest answer, noted for reteaching.
- **G — Matching game:** roots → WATER; leaves → FOOD; webbed feet →
  MOVING; long beak → FOOD; rabbit ears → STAYING SAFE; turtle shell →
  STAYING SAFE; at least 5 of 6 with reasons = success; claws — FOOD or
  STAYING SAFE with a reason; flower — making seeds / new plants
  (accept "growing"); one part can match two needs — roots (WATER and
  staying put), ears (hearing and STAYING SAFE); the deliberate swap is
  found and fixed with a reason.
- **H — Midyear revisit:** (1) any "I notice / I wonder" question;
  (2) sound — vibrating materials; (3) we see something only when light
  shines on it; one light source (sun, lamp, flashlight) and one
  reflector (mirror, white wall, book); (4) one plant part and one
  animal part with jobs; (5) nature-tool used parts most (accept design
  cycle, with a reason); at least 4 of 5 = success, then the quiz.

## Formative quiz

- **Part 1:** (1) roots — drink water and hold steady; (2) stem — holds
  up and carries water; (3) leaves — catch sunlight, help make food;
  (4) flower — makes seeds; (5) duck swimming part — webbed feet, like
  paddles; (6) fish swimming part — fins and tail, swim and steer.
  Scoring: 5–6 secure; 3–4 nearly secure; 0–2 re-teach Lessons 01–02.
- **Part 2:** (1) plant drawing — roots below soil line, stem, leaves,
  flower on top; (2) webbed foot drawing + "helps a duck swim" (or
  "works like a paddle"); (3) seed-pickup tool drawing with the
  beak-copy circled; (4) one plant need (water, sunlight, soil) and one
  animal need (food, water, safety, shelter). Scoring: 3–4 secure;
  2 nearly secure; 0–1 re-teach.

## Unit assessment

- **Task 1:** four plant parts — roots, stem, leaves, flower — with jobs
  as in Lesson 01. Safety: never taste a plant part; wash hands after
  touching soil.
- **Task 2:** two parts per animal with jobs (for example, duck: beak
  scoops food, webbed feet swim; fish: fins swim and steer, tail steers
  and pushes). Safety: watch a wild animal from a distance, do not
  touch it.
- **Task 3:** matches as in Practice G: roots → WATER; leaves → FOOD;
  webbed feet → MOVING; long beak → FOOD; rabbit ears → STAYING SAFE;
  turtle shell → STAYING SAFE. Accept a defensible alternative match
  when the learner gives a correct reason (for example, rabbit ears →
  hearing as part of STAYING SAFE is the intended match; claws-style
  double matches do not apply here).
- **Task 4:** scored from the learner's own project — problem sentence,
  copied part and its job, winner by win count with a reason, and a
  next change tied to the test results. There is no required winning
  version; score the process and the reasoning.
- **Overall scoring:** use the per-objective table in the
  [unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md). 5–6 Secure = ready
  for Unit 05; 3–4 Secure = ready with targeted re-teaching; fewer than
  3 Secure = re-teach Lessons 01–03 and the design cycle, then re-assess
  only the "not yet" objectives.

## Project rubric scoring notes

Score from observation and the journal, not from a required design.
Secure in "Fair test and comparison" requires three tries per version,
one changed thing, and a winner chosen by counting wins. Secure in
"Explanation" requires the learner's own words linking the copied
shape's job to the human problem — repeating the adult's sentence
verbatim is Developing, not Secure. See the rubric in
[project.md](project.md).
