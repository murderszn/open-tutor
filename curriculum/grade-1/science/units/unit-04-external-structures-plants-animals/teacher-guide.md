# Teacher Guide — Unit 04

**Unit:** [U04](README.md) · For the guiding adult only. Student
materials never link here for answers; this guide and the
[answer key](answer-key.md) are kept separate from student questions.

## How this unit runs

Sixteen sessions of 20–25 minutes, four per week across Weeks 15–18.
Week 15 is plant parts; Week 16 is animal parts; Week 17 is the
nature-tool design, build, test, and compare; Week 18 is the track's
midyear review week (Units 01–04 revisit, formative quiz, culminating
assessment). Every session is adult-led and adult-scribed as needed.
Oral, pointing, drawing, and gesture responses are full evidence.

## Session-by-session guidance

| Session | Adult moves | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Lesson 01 | Plant on the table, picture beside it. Teach place + job together; never name a part without its job. | "Flower is the plant" — re-teach: flower is one of four parts. |
| 2 — Practice A | Outdoor or indoor plant hunt. Let the learner lead the pointing; correct gently by re-pointing. | Tree trunk confusion — a trunk IS a stem; say so proudly, it is a good catch. |
| 3 — Practice B | Adult cuts celery and handles food coloring. Write the prediction exactly as said. | Predictions of "nothing will happen" are fine — they make tomorrow's observation a real surprise. |
| 4 — Practice C | Start by looking at the celery before any sorting. If color is faint, say so honestly and look closely with the hand lens. | "Roots eat soil" — re-teach: roots drink water from soil. |
| 5 — Lesson 02 | Toys first, picture second. Teach the hidden-ears point explicitly — it prevents the "missing means absent" error later. | "Every animal has the same parts" — re-teach with fish vs. duck. |
| 6 — Practice D | Picture or text alternative, learner's choice. Ask each question once, then let the learner re-find the part. | Do not ask for duck ears in the picture — they are not visible. Ask about hidden ears by talking, not by picture-finding. |
| 7 — Practice E | Tweezers are adult-supervised; beans and pom-poms are not food and never go near mouths. | Tool-swap confusion — repeat: tweezers = single pickups, spoon = scooping. |
| 8 — Practice F | Sort game, then journal check as a conversation, not a grade. Praise one clear drawing; pick one to improve together. | Rushing the journal check — it is where the learner learns to judge their own recording. |
| 9 — Lesson 03 | Cards on the table, needs as pictures + words. Insist on the reason every time: "part — need — how I know." | One-part-one-need rigidity — name two-job parts (roots, ears) out loud. |
| 10 — Practice G | Let the learner run the game; the adult only plays the deliberate swap in step 5. | Claw and flower challenge cards — accept any defensible reason; say so. |
| 11 — Lesson 04 | Sketch day only. Guard the boundary: no building today, even if the learner begs. The wait is the lesson. | "Copy the looks" — re-teach: copy the working shape. |
| 12 — Project build | Build from the sketch. If the build drifts, note it on the sketch — honest revision, not cheating. | Frustration with tape and cardboard — help with holding, not with designing; the design stays the learner's. |
| 13 — Lesson 05 | Three tries per version, same setup. Record failures exactly. Count wins together. | Unfair tests — reset to one change and re-run, cheerfully. |
| 14 — Project improve | Improve or keep-with-reason. One re-test of the changed design is enough. | "My first design was best because I made it" — return to the win count. |
| 15 — Practice H + quiz | Midyear revisit as a game, then the quiz. Anything missed goes on the Session 16 warm-up list. | Quiz anxiety — remind the learner: pointing and drawing count, and the adult writes. |
| 16 — Assessment | Four performance tasks, unhurried. Record per-objective levels; do not average into a single grade. | Rushing a "not yet" — a "not yet" is a teaching plan, not a verdict. |

## Safety notes

- Never taste any plant, seed, or bean. Celery water with food coloring
  is not for drinking. Wash hands after soil, plant, and bean work.
- Handle living plants gently; return a lifted plant to soil and water
  it immediately. If a plant may not be disturbed, use pictures only.
- Never touch a wild animal; observe from a distance. A family pet is
  observed only if calm, and never held up, poked, or chased.
- Tweezers and scissors are adult-supervised. Fan work is lowest
  setting, never at faces. Water work is over a tray or towel.
- Allergy check: if the learner has pollen or plant allergies, the
  adult handles plants and the learner observes with the hand lens.
- Observation-only alternatives: every outdoor hunt has an indoor
  (plants, toys, pictures) version; every touching task has a
  looking-and-pointing version.

## Common misconceptions and reteaching

| Misconception | What to say / do |
|---|---|
| The flower is the whole plant. | Point top-to-bottom on a real plant: flower, stem, leaves, roots. Four parts, one plant. |
| Roots eat soil. | "Roots drink water that is in the soil — like a straw in a drink, not like eating the cup." Re-run the celery observation. |
| Big part = important part; small part = unimportant. | "Roots are hidden and leaves are big — both are needed. A plant with no roots dies, and a plant with no leaves starves." |
| Every animal has the same parts. | Fish vs. duck, side by side: fins in water, legs on land. Same job (moving), different tool. |
| Hidden means missing (duck ears). | "Your ears are on the sides of your head; a duck's are under its feathers. We hear with them either way." |
| Fins are for walking. | "Fins push water. On land they cannot push anything — that is why a fish stays in water." |
| Leaves protect the plant. | "Leaves are soft and animals eat them. Leaves make food; shells protect." |
| Copying nature means looking like the animal. | "We copy the working shape — a beak that pinches, a scoop that drains — not a duck face." |
| One win decides the best design. | Count all three tries together. "Two wins beats one win." |
| A failed test means I failed. | "A failed test is data. It tells us what to change. Scientists love failed tests — they are how designs get better." |
| A match is right because the cards look nice together. | "The test is: does this part really do that job? If the answer is 'not really,' try another need." |

## Reteaching pathways

- **Plant parts shaky (Objective 1):** re-run Lesson 01's pointing round
  with the real plant, then Practice A with two new plants. Two short
  sessions, not one long one.
- **Animal parts shaky (Objective 2):** re-run Lesson 02's toy round
  with three animals, then Practice D's picture questions.
- **Matching shaky (Objective 3):** re-run Lesson 03 with only three
  cards (roots, webbed feet, shell) until the reason pattern is fluent,
  then add cards back.
- **Design shaky (Objective 4):** re-run Lesson 04 with Problem B only
  (the most concrete: a scoop with holes), sketching together.
- **Comparing shaky (Objective 5):** re-run Lesson 05's worked example
  with two spoons, counting wins on fingers, before re-testing the
  learner's own designs.
- **Safety shaky (Objective 6):** re-teach as rules with actions
  (mime washing hands, mime watching from a distance) rather than as
  words to memorize.

## Readiness and next steps

- **Ready for Unit 05** (5–6 objectives Secure): proceed. Unit 05
  (parent–offspring similarities and behavior) reuses part-naming and
  the design cycle only lightly; no extra preparation needed.
- **Ready with re-teaching** (3–4 Secure): use the flexible weeks to
  re-teach the Developing objectives with the pathways above, then
  proceed. Do not delay the whole track for one Developing objective.
- **Not yet ready** (fewer than 3 Secure): re-teach Lessons 01–03 and
  the design cycle across two weeks of flexible time, then re-assess
  only the "not yet" objectives — never the whole assessment.
- **Extension for a secure learner:** version 3 of the nature-tool
  (combine the best of both versions), the cactus-spines question from
  Lesson 03, or a second problem choice from the project (build and
  test it the same way).

## What not to do

- Do not teach internal anatomy, food chains, or evolution vocabulary
  in this unit — those belong to later grades. Stay at outside parts
  and their jobs.
- Do not present the generated illustration as a photograph or as
  evidence from a real experiment. It is labeled an AI illustration in
  its caption and in [`assets/README.md`](assets/README.md).
- Do not record learner names, grades, or completed work in this
  repository. Assessment results stay in private storage.
