# Practice Sessions — Unit 04

Named sessions from the [unit pacing](README.md#16-session-pacing).
All responses may be oral, pointing, drawing, or adult-scribed. Answers
are in the [answer key](answer-key.md) — this file contains no answers.

## Session A — Plant-part hunt (Session 2)

**Goal:** find and name plant parts in the real world.

1. Go outdoors with the adult, or walk among indoor plants. Find a plant
   and point to its stem, a leaf, and (if it has one) a flower.
2. Find a second plant that looks different (a tree, a bush, grass, a
   weed). Point to its stem and a leaf. Are its leaves the same shape as
   the first plant's leaves?
3. The adult lifts a small weed or seedling (only where pulling weeds is
   allowed) or shows a picture of roots. Point to the roots. Where do
   roots live — above or below the soil?
4. Draw the two plants you found in the journal. The adult labels the
   parts from dictation.
5. Question: a tree has a trunk. Is a trunk a stem? (Discuss: yes — a
   trunk is a tree's big woody stem. It holds the tree up and carries
   water, just like a small plant's stem.)
6. Question: grass has no flower right now. Is grass still a plant with
   parts? (Yes — it has roots, stems, and leaves even when it is not
   flowering.)

**Success criterion:** names stem, leaf, and (where present) flower on
two different plants, and says roots live below the soil.

## Session B — Roots drink: celery setup (Session 3)

**Goal:** set up a fair observation that water climbs into a plant.

1. The adult cuts the bottom off a celery stalk and stands it in a
   glass of water with a few drops of food coloring (adult handles the
   coloring).
2. Predict, before anything happens: "What will happen to the stem and
   the leaves by tomorrow — will they change color, stay the same, or
   something else?" The adult writes the prediction down exactly.
3. Draw the setup in the journal: glass, colored water, celery stalk.
   Label "Day 1" (adult-scribed).
4. Question: why do we use one celery stalk and one glass, and not
   three different stalks in three different drinks? (So only time
   changes — a fair test, like Unit 03's beam tests.)
5. Question: in a whole plant, which part does the job of the cut end
   of this celery? (The roots — they take water in from the soil.)
6. The adult places the glass where it will not be knocked over. Do not
   taste the celery or the water.

**Success criterion:** makes a prediction and can say roots are the
part that takes water in. The result is observed in Session C.

## Session C — Celery check + plant-part sort game (Session 4)

**Goal:** report what changed, and sort plant parts without help.

1. Look at the celery. What changed since yesterday? Draw the celery
   again, labeled "Day 2," showing any color in the stem or leaves.
2. Question: did the color climb up from the water, or fall down from
   the top? (Up from the water.)
3. Question: what does this tell us about roots? (Roots take water in,
   and water travels up through the plant.)
4. Sort game: the adult lays out 8 picture cards — two roots, two
   stems, two leaves, two flowers (mixed). Sort them into four piles by
   part, without help.
5. Question: one card shows an apple. Which pile — roots, stem, leaf,
   or flower? (None of the four exactly: an apple is a fruit, which
   holds seeds. If the learner puts it with flower, accept it with the
   reason "flowers make fruits and seeds" — then add a fifth pile,
   FRUIT, and move it there.)
6. Question: if the celery had stayed white, would that mean roots do
   not drink? (No — one test can fail for many reasons: not enough
   time, not enough coloring, a stalk that was already dry. A good
   scientist re-tests or waits longer, and does not throw away the
   whole idea on one try.)

**Success criterion:** reports the celery change correctly and sorts
at least 7 of 8 cards correctly (the apple card is handled as above).

## Session D — Picture investigation (Session 6)

**Goal:** read parts and jobs from the generated illustration.

Use [`assets/plant-animal-parts.png`](assets/plant-animal-parts.png)
or its text alternative in
[`assets/README.md`](assets/README.md#text-only-alternative).

1. Find the plant. Name its four parts, top to bottom, then bottom to
   top.
2. Find the duck. Name three of its parts (beak, wing, tail, webbed
   feet, eyes).
3. Find the fish. Name two of its parts (fins, tail, eye).
4. Question: which part in the picture helps a plant drink? (Roots, in
   the soil.)
5. Question: which part in the picture helps an animal swim? (The
   duck's webbed feet, or the fish's fins and tail — accept either
   with the animal named.)
6. Question: the duck's ears are not visible in this picture. Does
   that mean the duck cannot hear? (No — its ears are hidden under its
   feathers. Hidden does not mean missing.)
7. Draw one part from the picture in the journal and write (adult-
   scribed) its job beside it.

**Success criterion:** names the plant's four parts and at least three
duck parts and two fish parts, and answers questions 4–6 correctly.

## Session E — Beak tools test (Session 7)

**Goal:** show that the tool must fit the food.

Materials: tweezers (adult-supervised), a spoon, dried beans, a bowl of
blue pom-poms or beads ("pond water" with bits in it).

1. Round 1 — single beans on a table. Pick up beans one at a time with
   tweezers, then try with the spoon. Which tool wins for single
   beans?
2. Round 2 — scooping. Scoop pom-poms out of the bowl with the spoon,
   then try with tweezers. Which tool wins for scooping many bits at
   once?
3. Question: which bird beak is like tweezers — a beak that picks up
   single seeds? (A small seed-eating bird's beak.)
4. Question: which bird beak is like a spoon — a beak that scoops?
   (A duck's flat beak.)
5. Question: would tweezers help a duck scoop pond water? Why not?
   (No — tweezers pick one thing at a time; scooping needs a wide
   flat shape.)
6. Draw the winning tool for each round in the journal, with the adult
   writing "picks single seeds" or "scoops many bits" beside it.

**Success criterion:** says tweezers win for single beans and the spoon
wins for scooping, and matches each tool to its beak shape.

## Session F — Animal-part sort + journal check (Session 8)

**Goal:** sort new animal parts without help, and review the journal.

1. Sort game: the adult lays out 8 new cards — beak, fin, tail, wing,
   webbed foot, ear, eye, leg. Sort them into piles by part word.
   (Cards may show different animals; the part word is what matters.)
2. Question: pick one card and name an animal that has that part.
3. Question: pick the fin card. Which animal in our picture has fins,
   and what job do fins do? (The fish; fins help it swim and steer.)
4. Question: pick the wing card. Does a duck use its wings only for
   flying? (No — wings also cover and warm the duck's body.)
5. Journal check: the adult and learner look back through the journal
   together. Find one drawing you are proud of and one drawing to
   improve. What makes the proud one clear — labels? The right place
   for each part?
6. Question: which part have you found hardest to remember, and what
   will help you remember it? (Any honest answer counts; the adult
   notes it for reteaching.)

**Success criterion:** sorts at least 7 of 8 cards correctly and names
an animal for a chosen part.

## Session G — Part-to-need matching game (Session 10)

**Goal:** match six parts to needs, with reasons.

Part cards: roots, leaves, webbed feet, long beak, rabbit ears, turtle
shell. Need cards: FOOD, WATER, MOVING, STAYING SAFE.

1. Match all six part cards to need cards.
2. For each match, say one reason: "___ matches ___, because ___."
3. Challenge round: the adult adds two new part cards — sharp claws
   and a flower. Match them. (Claws: FOOD or STAYING SAFE, with a
   reason; flower: making seeds / new plants — accept "growing.")
4. Question: can one part match two needs? Give an example. (Yes —
   roots match WATER and staying put; ears match hearing and STAYING
   SAFE.)
5. Question: the adult swaps two matches on purpose. Find the swap and
   fix it, explaining why the swapped matches were wrong.

**Success criterion:** matches at least 5 of 6 original cards correctly
with reasons; the challenge cards are accepted with any defensible
reason.

## Session H — Midyear revisit, Units 01–04 (Session 15)

**Goal:** retrieve and connect the first four units before assessment.

1. Unit 01: ask one "I notice / I wonder" question about the plant or
   an animal in the room.
2. Unit 02: what makes sound? (Vibrating materials.) Show one vibration
   with hands or a rubber band (adult-supervised).
3. Unit 03: we see something only when…? (Light shines on it.) Name one
   light source and one object that only reflects light.
4. Unit 04: name one plant part and one animal part, and one job for
   each.
5. Question: which unit's idea did your nature-tool use most — asking
   questions, sound, light, or parts? (Parts — copying a part's shape;
   accept "design cycle" from Unit 02 as well, with a reason.)
6. Complete the [formative quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md).

**Success criterion:** answers at least 4 of 5 revisit questions, and
completes the quiz. Anything missed is re-taught before Session 16.
