# Project — The Nature-Tool Investigation

**Unit:** [U04](README.md) · **Sessions 11–14** (design, build, test and
compare, improve and re-test) · **Standards:** 1-LS1-1, K-2-ETS1-1,
K-2-ETS1-2, K-2-ETS1-3

## The challenge

A human has a problem. A plant or animal already solved a problem like
it, with the shape of one of its outside parts. **Copy the shape — not
the looks — and build a tool that solves the human problem.**

## Choose one problem (Session 11, with Lesson 04)

- **Problem A — Seed pickup:** pick up tiny seeds from a table without
  them rolling away and without dirty fingers. Nature part: a bird's
  beak (pinches and grips).
- **Problem B — Pond scoop:** scoop leaves and bits out of a bowl of
  water without scooping all the water too. Nature part: a duck's beak
  (flat and wide; lets water drain, keeps bits in).
- **Problem C — Sign anchor:** hold a paper sign upright in the wind so
  it does not blow over. Nature part: plant roots (spread wide and hold
  steady).

## Deliverables

1. **A problem sentence** — one sentence, adult-scribed, at the top of
   the journal page: "I need to…"
2. **A sketch made before building** — the tool, with the copied nature
   shape circled or pointed to and labeled "copies a ___."
3. **Version 1, built from the sketch** — cardboard, tape, cups, craft
   sticks, string, foil, or other safe household materials.
4. **A comparison table** — version 1 and version 2 (version 2 changes
   **one thing** only), three tries each, each try recorded honestly as
   worked / partly worked / did not work.
5. **A comparison sentence** — "Version ___ works better, because ___."
6. **An improvement** — either the weaker design improved and re-tested,
   or a reason, from the test results, for keeping the stronger design
   as it is.

## Steps

1. **Define (Session 11).** Pick a problem, say it in one sentence,
   name the nature part, and say what it does.
2. **Sketch (Session 11).** Draw the tool. Circle the copied shape.
   Label it. Do not build yet.
3. **Build version 1 (Session 12).** Build from the sketch. If the build
   drifts from the sketch, note the change on the sketch — the sketch is
   a plan, and plans may be honestly revised.
4. **Test version 1 (Session 13, with Lesson 05).** Three tries, same
   materials, same setup. Record every try honestly, including failures.
5. **Build version 2 (Session 13).** Change one thing only. Say what
   changed and why you think it might help.
6. **Test version 2 (Session 13).** Three tries, same setup as version 1.
7. **Compare (Session 13).** Count the wins. Write the comparison
   sentence. The design with more wins is better — not the design the
   learner likes more.
8. **Improve and re-test (Session 14).** Improve the weaker design (or
   keep the stronger one, with a reason from the results) and run one
   re-test of the changed design. Record the result.

## Materials and safety

Cardboard, tape, cups, craft sticks, string, foil, dried beans, a bowl
of water with leaves or bits, a paper sign, a fan on its lowest setting
or gentle blowing from a marked spot. Adult supervises scissors, tape,
and all water work (tray or towel underneath). Never point a fan at
faces. A failed test is data, not a failure — record it and learn from
it.

## Rubric

Each criterion is scored by the adult from observation and the journal.
"Secure" in a criterion means the learner did it with at most one adult
hint; "developing" means with repeated hints; "not yet" means the adult
did most of it.

| Criterion | Secure | Developing | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem defined** (K-2-ETS1-1) | States the chosen problem in one clear sentence and names the nature part it copies, with its job. | States the problem or the nature part, but not both, or needs repeated hints. | Cannot state the problem or the part even with hints. |
| **Sketch before building** (K-2-ETS1-2) | Sketch made before building, showing the tool and the copied shape, circled and labeled. | Sketch made, but the copied shape is missing or unlabeled, or building started first. | No sketch, or a sketch unrelated to the build. |
| **Build matches the design** | Version 1 is built from the sketch (honest revisions noted on the sketch). | Build partly follows the sketch; major changes unnoted. | Build ignores the sketch entirely. |
| **Fair test and comparison** (K-2-ETS1-3) | Both versions tested three times the same way; only one thing changed; winner chosen by counting wins, with a reason. | Tests run but unfair (more than one thing changed) or winner chosen by liking, not results. | No comparison, or results invented rather than observed. |
| **Explanation** (1-LS1-1) | Can say, in their own words, how the copied shape's job solves the human problem. | Explains with heavy prompting, or names the part without its job. | Cannot explain the copy even with prompts. |
| **Care and honesty** | Records failures honestly; handles materials safely; tries the improvement step. | Records most results honestly; needs reminders on safety or honesty. | Hides failures, or unsafe handling after reminders. |

**Overall:** 5–6 criteria at Secure = project secure. 3–4 at Secure
with the rest Developing = nearly secure; re-teach the Developing
criteria in Session 14 before the unit assessment. Fewer than 3 at
Secure = re-teach the design cycle (Lessons 04–05) before Unit 05's
design work.

## What success looks like (examples, not required designs)

- Problem A: a two-arm gripper whose arms pinch like a beak picks up
  8 of 10 beans in three tries, while a plain cup picks up 2 of 10 —
  the gripper wins because pinching grips single small things.
- Problem B: a scoop with drain holes brings up leaves with little
  water in 3 of 3 tries, while a plain spoon brings up leaves with a
  spoonful of water each time — the holed scoop wins because water
  drains and leaves stay.
- Problem C: a sign with wide cardboard "roots" taped flat stays
  upright in 3 of 3 gentle blows, while a sign with a narrow base falls
  in 2 of 3 — the wide base wins because spreading holds steady, like
  roots.

Actual results will vary with materials and the learner's designs; the
adult scores the **process and the reasoning from real results**, never
a required winner.
