# Unit 04 — External Structures of Plants and Animals

**Grade 1 · Science · Weeks 15–18 (4 weeks, 16 sessions, 20–25 minutes each)**

Part of [Grade 1 Science](../../README.md) · [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) ·
Issue [#11](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/11). Draft curriculum —
not educator-approved, not aligned/certified by any state.

## Big idea

Every living thing has outside parts, and every part has a job. A plant's
roots drink and hold on, its stem lifts and carries, its leaves catch
sunlight, and its flower makes seeds. An animal's beak, feet, fins, ears,
eyes, and tail each help it find food and water, move, stay safe, or keep
steady. When people copy a plant or animal part, they can solve a human
problem — a scoop shaped like a duck's beak, a grip shaped like a bird's
beak, an anchor that spreads like roots. In this unit the learner names
the parts, shows what each part does, and then designs, builds, and tests
a nature-inspired tool of their own.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of this unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Name the external parts of a plant — roots, stem, leaves, flower
   (and fruit/seed where present)** — and tell what each part does for
   the plant: roots drink water and hold the plant steady; the stem
   holds the plant up and carries water; leaves catch sunlight to help
   make food; the flower makes seeds (Lessons 01, 03; 1-LS1-1, LS1.A).
2. **Name the external parts of an animal — legs/feet, ears, eyes,
   beak (or mouth), fins, tail, wings where present** — and tell how
   each part helps the animal see, hear, grasp, move, find food and
   water, or stay safe (Lessons 02–03; 1-LS1-1, LS1.A).
3. **Match a part to a need with evidence from observation** — e.g.,
   webbed feet help a duck swim; a long beak helps a hummingbird reach
   nectar; roots hold a plant when the wind blows — and explain the
   match in their own words (Lessons 03, 05; 1-LS1-1).
4. **Define a simple human problem, sketch a solution that mimics a
   plant or animal part, and build it from the sketch** (Lesson 04,
   [project](project.md); 1-LS1-1, K-2-ETS1-1, K-2-ETS1-2).
5. **Test two designs for the same problem, compare their results, and
   say which works better and why** — then improve the weaker design or
   keep the stronger one (Lesson 05, project; K-2-ETS1-3).
6. **Follow the unit's living-thing safety rules in every investigation:**
   handle plants and animals gently, never taste a plant, wash hands
   after soil or plant work, observe wild animals from a distance
   without touching, and use the observation-only alternative whenever
   touching would be unsafe (all lessons; track objective 10).

Oral, pointing, drawing, or adult-scribed responses count as evidence
throughout. The adult reads all prompts aloud; learner independence grows
only as far as writing stamina allows. Short independent recording tasks
are 4–6 items (draw + label, adult-scribed), reviewed by the adult the
same day.

## Prerequisites

- From Unit 01: asking questions that can be explored by looking or
  testing, observing with more than one sense, recording in the science
  journal, and the four science safety rules (adult supervision; nothing
  near the mouth; gentle handling; the safe-alternative question).
- From Unit 02: the design cycle — plan (sketch), build, test, compare.
  The Unit 02 sound device is not needed here, but the same cycle runs
  the project.
- From Unit 03: describing what was observed, not what was guessed;
  testing one thing at a time (fair test).
- No reading is required. The learner does not need the words
  "photosynthesis" or "adaptation" as vocabulary, and the unit never
  teaches internal anatomy (bones, muscles, organs) or life cycles —
  everything stays at the outside parts a child can see and touch.

## Standards and source notes

- **1-LS1-1** — *Use materials to design a solution to a human problem
  by mimicking how plants and/or animals use their external parts to
  help them survive, grow, and meet their needs.* Lessons 01–05 and the
  project. The framework's clarification examples are the direct model
  for the project choices: protecting by mimicking turtle shells, acorn
  shells, and animal scales; stabilizing structures by mimicking animal
  tails and roots on plants; keeping out intruders by mimicking thorns
  on branches and animal quills; detecting intruders by mimicking eyes
  and ears. Description re-verified 2026-10-29 against the NSTA NGSS
  standard display for 1-LS1; matches the track audit crosswalk (checked
  2026-10-02).
- **LS1.A: Structure and Function** — *All organisms have external
  parts. Different animals use their body parts in different ways to see,
  hear, grasp objects, protect themselves, move from place to place, and
  seek, find, and take in food, water and air. Plants also have different
  parts (roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits) that help them survive
  and grow.* This is the content behind Objectives 1–3; the unit teaches
  the parts at the observable level only.
- **K-2-ETS1-1** — *Ask questions, make observations, and gather
  information about a situation people want to change to define a simple
  problem that can be solved through the development of a new or improved
  object or tool.* The learner picks one human problem in Lesson 04 and
  states it in one sentence before sketching.
- **K-2-ETS1-2** — *Develop a simple sketch, drawing, or physical model
  to illustrate how the shape of an object helps it function as needed
  to solve a given problem.* The learner sketches the nature-tool before
  building (Lesson 04, project).
- **K-2-ETS1-3** — *Analyze data from tests of two objects designed to
  solve the same problem to compare the strengths and weaknesses of how
  each performs.* The project tests two versions of the learner's tool
  and compares them (Lesson 05, project). The scope-and-sequence lists
  K-2-ETS1-1 and K-2-ETS1-2 for this unit; K-2-ETS1-3 is added here
  because the project genuinely compares two tested designs.
- **Plant-part jobs** (roots drink and anchor; stem holds up and
  carries; leaves catch sunlight and help make food; flowers make
  seeds) follow the elementary teacher guide page on plants (see
  [resource-pack](resource-pack.md)) and the track's biology background
  guide, kept at observable level for grade 1.

## Vocabulary

Adult introduces each word in context during the lesson; learners are not
expected to spell them.

- **root** — the part of a plant that grows underground; drinks water and holds the plant steady
- **stem** — the stalk that holds a plant up and carries water from roots to leaves and flowers
- **leaf / leaves** — the flat green part that catches sunlight and helps the plant make food
- **flower** — the colorful part that makes seeds
- **fruit** — the part that holds seeds (for example, an apple, a tomato)
- **seed** — a tiny packet that can grow into a new plant
- **external part** — a part on the outside that you can see or touch
- **beak** — a bird's hard mouth part, shaped for the food it eats
- **fin** — a flat part on a fish that helps it swim and steer
- **tail** — the part at the back of an animal; helps with balance, steering, or holding on
- **wing** — the part a bird or insect uses to fly
- **webbed foot** — a foot with skin between the toes, like a paddle for swimming
- **ear** — the part an animal hears with (on the outside of the head, or hidden under feathers/fur)
- **survive** — to stay alive: to get food, water, air, and safety
- **need** — something a living thing must have to live and grow
- **mimic / copy nature** — to make something shaped like, or working like, a plant or animal part
- **design** — a plan for something you will build, to solve a problem
- **sketch** — a quick drawing of a plan, made before building
- **test** — to try something and watch what happens
- **compare** — to tell how two things are the same and different
- **improve** — to change something so it works better
- **gentle** — touching softly and carefully, so nothing is hurt

## Materials (whole unit)

Science journal, pencils, crayons; one potted plant with visible stem
and leaves (a bean seedling, a sunflower seedling, or any houseplant the
adult can lift from its pot briefly to show roots — or a picture of
roots if the plant must not be disturbed); one celery stalk, a glass of
water, and food coloring (adult handles the coloring); toy animals or
animal picture cards (duck, fish, rabbit, bird, turtle, cat or dog);
tweezers (adult-supervised), a spoon, dried beans, a bowl of blue
pom-poms or beads ("pond water"); cardboard, tape, cups, craft sticks,
string, and foil for the project; a hand lens; the generated illustration
[`assets/plant-animal-parts.png`](assets/plant-animal-parts.png) and the
labeled diagram [`assets/plant-animal-parts-diagram.svg`](assets/plant-animal-parts-diagram.svg).
All investigations are adult-supervised; see
[teacher-guide](teacher-guide.md) for safety notes. Never taste any
plant, never touch a wild animal, and wash hands after soil or plant work.

## 16-session pacing

Sessions are 20–25 minutes. Each names its activity and success criterion.

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lesson 01** — Plant parts you can see | Names roots, stem, leaves, flower on a real plant or the picture, and says one job for each. |
| 2 | Practice — Plant-part hunt | Finds and names 4 plant parts outdoors or on indoor plants; records them by drawing. |
| 3 | Observation — Roots drink (celery setup) | Sets up the celery-in-colored-water test and predicts what will happen to the stem and leaves. |
| 4 | Review — Celery check + plant-part sort game | Reports what changed in the celery; sorts 8 picture cards by plant part without help. |
| 5 | **Lesson 02** — Animal parts you can see | Names legs/feet, ears, eyes, beak, fins, tail on toy animals or the picture, and says one job for each. |
| 6 | Practice — Picture investigation with the generated illustration | Answers 5 questions from the image (or its text alternative); matches each part to its job. |
| 7 | Observation — Beak tools test | Shows tweezers beat the spoon for single beans and the spoon beats tweezers for scooping; says which beak shape fits which food. |
| 8 | Review — Animal-part sort + journal check | Sorts 8 new cards by animal part; journal reviewed same day. |
| 9 | **Lesson 03** — Parts help living things meet their needs | Matches 4 part-to-need pairs with a reason: food, water, moving, staying safe. |
| 10 | Practice — Part-to-need matching game | Matches 6 cards correctly (or explains a defensible alternative) and gives a reason for each. |
| 11 | **Lesson 04** — Copying nature: design a tool | States one human problem in a sentence, names the plant/animal part it copies, and sketches the tool before building. |
| 12 | Project — Build nature-tool, Part 1 | Builds version 1 from the sketch; first test succeeds or fails honestly. |
| 13 | **Lesson 05** — Test two designs and compare | Tests version 1 and version 2 the same way; records which works better and why. |
| 14 | Project — Improve and re-test, Part 2 | Completes the comparison table; improves the weaker design or keeps the stronger one, with a reason. |
| 15 | Review — Midyear revisit (Units 01–04) + formative quiz | Answers midyear review questions across all four units and completes the [formative quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md). |
| 16 | Assessment — [Unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md) + re-teach planning | Completes all 4 performance tasks; adult records readiness per objective. |

Weeks 15–16 introduce and practice plant and animal parts; Week 17 is
the design-and-build week; Week 18 is the track's midyear review week
(scope-and-sequence §5): the design comparison, the Units 01–04
revisit, re-teaching, the formative check, and the culminating
assessment. If the learner is not yet secure on an objective after
Session 16, use the diagnostic/catch-up guidance in the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md) before starting Unit 05 — the track's
flexible weeks exist for exactly this.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, drawing, or gesture responses; the adult
scribes. Each lesson includes a seated-table and a floor-play variant, a
language-access support, an alternate response mode, and an extension.
Part words are never taught by color alone: every part is also named by
where it is (underground / on the stalk / on the head / at the back) and
by what it does, so a learner who cannot distinguish colors can still sort
by place and job. The generated illustration ships with a text-only
alternative carrying every part and job the activities ask about. For a
learner who cannot go outdoors, the plant hunt uses indoor plants, toy
animals, and pictures; for a learner sensitive to soil or smells, the adult
handles the plant and the learner observes with the hand lens and points.
