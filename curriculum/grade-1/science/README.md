## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-1 science course organized around
asking questions and planning safe investigations; sound from vibrating
materials; light, visibility, shadows, and materials; plant and animal
external parts and how they help survival; parent–offspring patterns and
behaviors; sun, moon, and star patterns; seasonal daylight and weather;
and engineering solutions inspired by living things. Sessions are short
(20–25 minutes, about four per week) and built on hands-on investigation,
drawing and dictating observations, and adult-guided questioning — the way
grade-1 scientists actually work.

Every investigation is adult-supervised and age-appropriate, with
observation-only or simulation alternatives where a task would otherwise
involve a hazard (direct sun viewing, severe weather, living creatures, heat).

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Ask questions that can be explored by observing, touching, or testing;
   make careful observations with the senses and simple tools (hand lens,
   flashlight, ruler); record observations in words, drawings, or tallies;
   and communicate findings clearly.
2. Plan and carry out investigations showing that vibrating materials can
   make sound and that sound can make materials vibrate.
3. Make observations to show that objects in darkness can be seen only when
   illuminated; test transparent, translucent, opaque, and reflective
   materials in the path of a light beam; and describe the shadows each
   material makes.
4. Design and build a device that uses light or sound to communicate over a
   distance; test it; and compare two designs to say which performs better
   and why.
5. Describe the external parts of plants and animals (roots, stems, leaves;
   legs, ears, eyes, beaks, fins) and how those parts help the living thing
   survive, grow, and meet its needs; and design a solution to a human
   problem that mimics how a plant or animal part works.
6. Use texts and media to identify patterns in the behavior of parents and
   offspring that help the offspring survive.
7. Make observations to show that young plants and animals are like — but
   not exactly like — their parents.
8. Use observations of the sun, moon, and stars to describe patterns that
   can be predicted (sunrise and sunset position, the moon's changing shape).
9. Make observations at different times of year to relate the amount of
   daylight to the time of year.
10. Follow science safety practices: never look directly at the sun, keep
    lab materials away from the mouth, handle living things gently, and
    name the safe alternative when an investigation is unsafe to touch.

performance expectations and the K–2 engineering band (codes and descriptions
verified 2026-10-02 against the official framework at nextgenscience.org and
corroborated with the California NGSS DCI arrangement; not a claim of state
adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification). Full crosswalk in
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md#standards-crosswalk):

  1-PS4-1, 1-PS4-2, 1-PS4-3, 1-PS4-4
- From Molecules to Organisms: Structures and Processes: 1-LS1-1, 1-LS1-2
- Heredity: Inheritance and Variation of Traits: 1-LS3-1
- Earth's Place in the Universe: 1-ESS1-1, 1-ESS1-2
- Engineering Design (K–2 band): K-2-ETS1-1, K-2-ETS1-2, K-2-ETS1-3

## For the guiding adult

Grade-1 science is concrete: every concept first lives in things the child
can touch, shake, shine, plant, and watch over time. Read the scope-and-sequence
before starting; it lists safe household materials, safety boundaries, and the
diagnostic, catch-up, and review weeks that keep pacing humane. Oral, pointing,
drawing, and adult-scribed responses count as evidence throughout — writing
independently is not required to do science. Each future unit will include its
own teacher guide and answer key.
