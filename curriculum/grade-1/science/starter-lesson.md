# Starter Lesson — Observe and compare a shadow

**Status:** draft supplement. **Time:** three 20–25 minute sessions, with an observation repeated later in the day. **Goal:** Describe a shadow, compare its location/length at two times, and support a simple claim with observations.

## Adult preparation and readiness

Use a sunny window or outdoor spot, chalk/paper, and a safe object such as a cup or toy. Adult supervises outdoors and sets boundaries. Never look at the Sun. Readiness: ask what a shadow is and invite a drawing; teach that a shadow forms where light is blocked if needed. If sunshine is unavailable, use a flashlight and object indoors.

## Teach and model

Explain: “Light travels from a source. When an object blocks some light, a darker shape appears on the other side.” Put the object on paper and outline the shadow. Mark source direction with an arrow.

```text
flashlight (light source)  →  toy (blocks light)  →  shadow on paper
```

**Worked example 1:** Flashlight on the left, toy in the middle; shadow appears to the right. Move the flashlight to the right; the shadow now appears left.

**Worked example 2:** At 9 a.m., an outdoor object's shadow measures 30 cm; at noon it measures 12 cm. The noon shadow is shorter by 18 cm. These are sample values, not a guaranteed observation.

**Pacing and roles:** Session 1: adult models the diagram and guides tasks 1–2. Session 2: learner traces a shadow twice with adult supervision. Session 3: learner compares records, completes tasks 5–6, and reviews the result with an adult.

## Guided and independent tasks

1. Point to the light source, object, and shadow in a diagram.
2. Predict where a shadow will appear if light comes from the left.
3. Trace or draw the shadow of the chosen object. Record time and weather (sunny/cloudy).
4. Repeat later and compare: same/different place or longer/shorter.
5. Error check: adult says that moving the object while the light stays fixed will leave its shadow in the same place. Test or explain why that prediction may fail.
6. Application: complete “My shadow changed ___; I observed ___.”

**Answer checks:** With light from left, shadow is generally opposite/right; sample change 30−12=18 cm shorter. Real shadow position/length depends on source, object, and surface; use actual recorded evidence.

## Exit check and product

Learner labels source, object, and shadow and tells one comparison. Product: two labeled drawings or adult-scribed table with times and observations.

## Review rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model | Correctly identifies source, blocker, shadow | Identifies with pointing support | Re-model with flashlight |
| Observation | Records two actual or clearly labeled sample observations | One observation with prompting | Adult models a trace |
| Claim | Uses a recorded difference as evidence | Describes difference without evidence | Compare drawings side by side |

Offer flashlight indoors, high contrast paper, verbal description/touch-safe outlined shape, seated observation, or adult scribing. Avoid hot bulbs and never direct light at eyes. Sample data must be identified as sample. This one investigation is not a full explanation of daily/yearly Sun patterns.
