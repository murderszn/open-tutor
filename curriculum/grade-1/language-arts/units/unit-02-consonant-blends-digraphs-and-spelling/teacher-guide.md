# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Consonant Blends, Digraphs, and Spelling

Adult-facing guidance for all 16 sessions. Learner materials live in
[lessons/](lessons/), [practice.md](practice.md),
[project.md](project.md), and [assessments/](assessments/); every
solution lives in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## Session-by-session guidance

- **Sessions 1–4 (blends):** blend reading is new in print even though
  Unit 01 practiced it orally. Slow down on Session 1 — the
  point–say–push–finish routine is the whole unit's engine. If a
  learner cannot push the pair fast, practice bare pairs (fr, sw, cl,
  st, nd, mp) before re-adding vowels.
- **Sessions 5–8 (digraphs):** keep the mouth cues front and center
  (lips for sh, choo-choo for ch, tongue tip for th, back-of-mouth hum
  for ng). The one-sound/two-sound chant is worth repeating every
  session; it is also the project's teach-back core.
- **Sessions 9–10 (spelling):** dictation is the unit's hardest new
  skill. Dictate slowly, repeat words, and praise mapping over
  correctness. Never let a phonetic attempt go uncelebrated — the key
  marks them as wins.
- **Session 11 (lines):** read each line yourself first with real
  expression, then choral-read, then solo. The brain-check habit
  (self-correction) matters more than speed.
- **Sessions 12–14 (project):** the book is the unit's proof of
  transfer. Let the learner choose teams they are most confident in;
  struggling learners may build 2 pages per team type and still meet
  the rubric at "Developing."
- **Sessions 15–16 (quiz + assessment):** give the quiz, re-teach the
  weakest team type the same session (use the re-teach pointers in
  Session F), then assess. A learner can be re-tested on one team type
  alone the next day if the full assessment overwhelms.

## Common misconceptions (all lessons)

1. **Swallowed second consonant** (*frog* → "fog"): exaggerate the
   stretch, then blend fast; mirror check (mouth moves twice).
2. **Vowel inserted between blend consonants** ("fuh-rog"): push the
   pair with no vowel between — one mouth movement run together.
3. **Digraph stretched apart** (*sh* → "suh-huh"): one mouth movement,
   one sound; feel the air on a hand for sh, the tongue tip for th.
4. **ng split** (*sing* → "sing-guh"): hum ladder sing–sang–song–sung.
5. **ck at the start of a word:** ck is an END team after a short
   vowel; at the start we write c or k.
6. **Counting letters instead of sounds** (*lunch* = 5 letters, 4
   sounds): sound dots / finger mapping before writing.
7. **Guessing from the first letter:** always point under EVERY
   letter; the check step ("real word? does it make sense?") catches
   guesses.

## Readiness and re-teaching

- **Ready for Unit 03** when the learner scores 16+/20 on the unit
  assessment, or 12–15 with the missed team type re-taught. All five
  objectives must reach at least "Developing."
- **Not yet ready:** use the flexible/catch-up weeks. Re-teach
  sequence: (a) the weakest team type in isolation (bare pairs/teams
  before words); (b) the paired practice session; (c) one
  re-dictation or re-read of the failed assessment tasks only — never
  the whole assessment twice in one day.
- **Fast finishers:** extension tasks in each lesson plus the
  two-team words (*think, shock, drink, string*); let them design a
  second book page set for qu/x/doubled endings.

## Accessibility notes for the adult

- Face the learner for every sound model; keep the mirror within
  reach. For th, the tongue-tip cue is visual — exaggerate it.
- Color is never the only cue in sorts; trays are labeled with the
  team letters in large print.
- All written tasks may be tiled instead of written; all reading may
  be echoed instead of solo-read. Record the accommodation in your
  notes so Unit 03's adult knows what worked.

## Record-keeping

Note per session: which teams the learner read fluently, which needed
prompts, dictation error patterns (which step failed — stretch, map,
write, or check), and any accommodation used. This log is what makes
the R00 review honest.
