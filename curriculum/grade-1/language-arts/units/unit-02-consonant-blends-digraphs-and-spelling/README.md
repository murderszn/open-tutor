# Unit 02 — Consonant Blends, Digraphs, and Spelling

**Grade:** 1 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #12](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/12), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

In Unit 01 the learner sounded out single-consonant words like *cat* and
*fish*. Real reading is rarely that tidy: consonants love to team up. In
this unit the learner reads one-syllable words where consonants cluster
at the front (*frog*, *stand*, *swim* — **consonant blends**: hear both
sounds, say them fast together), at the end (*hand*, *jump*, *nest*),
and in **digraphs** — two letters that make ONE new sound (*ship, chick,
thumb, whale, back, sing*). The unit ends with spelling: writing the
patterns the learner can now read, and sounding out new words the
honest way — one sound at a time, in order.

## Essential question

When two letters stand together, how do I know whether to hear two
sounds (blends) or one brand-new sound (digraphs) — and how do I write
what I hear?

## Prerequisites

From Unit 01 and the diagnostic weeks (1–2):

- Knows letter names and the common sounds of consonants and short
  vowels; can blend and segment spoken CVC words (RF.1.2.b, RF.1.2.d)
- Decodes regularly spelled one-syllable short-vowel words left to
  right (RF.1.3.b); knows that every syllable has a vowel sound
- Marks sentence features (first word, capital, ending punctuation)
- Orally segments CVC words into three sounds (needed for dictation
  spelling)

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed;
oral, pointing, and drawing responses accepted everywhere; short written
practice of 4–6 tasks is reviewed by the adult the same day):

1. **Read initial blends:** decode one-syllable words beginning with
   consonant blends (bl, cl, fl, gl, pl, sl; br, cr, dr, fr, gr, pr,
   tr; sm, sn, st, sw, sc, sp; str, spl) by saying each sound and
   blending fast, left to right.
2. **Read final blends:** decode one-syllable words ending with
   consonant blends (nd, nt, st, sk, mp, nk, lt, ft, sp, ct) the same
   way.
3. **Read digraphs:** know the spelling–sound correspondences for
   **sh, ch, th, wh, ck, ng, qu** and decode one-syllable words using
   them — two letters, one sound (qu spells /kw/).
4. **Blend and segment orally:** push separate sounds together and
   stretch words apart when the words contain blends or digraphs
   (e.g., /f/ /r/ /ŏ/ /g/ → "frog"; "think" → /th/ /ĭ/ /ng/).
5. **Spell what is heard:** spell common spelling patterns
   conventionally (ck after a short vowel: *back, duck*; ff/ll/ss at
   the end after a short vowel: *off, well, miss*; qu for /kw/:
   *quit*; x for /ks/: *box*) and spell untaught words phonetically —
   every sound in order, with phonetic attempts accepted and praised.

## Vocabulary (taught with mouths, tiles, and pictures first, word second)

blend · consonant blend · initial blend · final blend · digraph (two
letters, one sound) · team · sound out · stretch · map (every sound in
order) · spelling pattern · phonetic spelling · sentence (review:
first word, capital, ending mark)

### Unit word sets (authored for this unit; reused across lessons and practice)

Decodable with Unit 01 + Unit 02 skills (all short-vowel, one-syllable,
unless marked *taught whole word*).

- **Initial blends:** stop, step, still, stand; frog, from, front;
  clap, clip, clock; black, block, blend; flag, flip; glad; plan,
  plug; sled, slip; crab, crisp, crack; drum, drag; grin, grab; press,
  prop; trip, trap; snap, sniff, smell; snack, snip; swim, swing;
  scab, skip; spin, spot, span; strip, strap; split
- **Final blends:** and, hand, sand, pond, band; end, tent, sent, ant,
  bent; fast, last, nest, best, just; ask, desk, mask; jump, lamp,
  bump, camp, pump; bank, drink, think, sink, pink; belt, melt; gift,
  lift; crisp, grasp; act, fact; next
- **Digraph sh:** ship, shop, shut, shell, fish, dish, wish, brush,
  smash, cash, rush
- **Digraph ch:** chat, chick, chop, chip, much, such, rich, lunch
- **Digraph th:** thin, thick, bath *(unvoiced th)*; this, that, with
  *(voiced th — the throat buzzes)*
- **Digraph wh:** when*, which*, whale* *(wh spells /w/ before these
  vowels in everyday speech)*
- **Digraph ck:** back, chick, duck, sock, rock, sick, clock, black,
  brick, crack, shock
- **Digraph ng:** sing, king, ring, wing, thing, song, long, bang,
  sang, lung, hung
- **qu:** quit, quick, quiz *(qu spells /kw/)*
- **Doubled endings (spelling pattern):** off, well, miss, class, kiss
- **x:** box, fox, mix, next *(x spells /ks/)*
- **Taught whole words (used in decodable lines):** the, a, is, in, on,
  I, to, we, and, that, this, when, go (reviewed from Unit 01; *and,
  that, this, when* taught here because they appear before the unit's
  patterns would decode them)

\* *when, which, whale*: high-frequency words taught by sight this unit
because their vowels are not yet decodable (long-vowel spellings arrive
in Unit 03). The **wh** itself is exactly as taught.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, or drawing responses; the adult
scribes dictated writing. Color is never the only cue (digraph cards
carry a distinct border and the digraph letters themselves; blend tiles
are numbered 1–2–3 as well as colored). Each lesson includes a
seated-table and a floor-play variant, a language-access support, an
alternate response mode, and an extension. The generated illustration
ships with a text-only alternative naming all six pictured objects.
Hearing support: face the learner when modeling sounds; mirror work and
visual mouth-shape cues supplement auditory discrimination (especially
the th tongue-tip and the ng back-of-mouth sound). Movement support:
sessions are short by design; blend/digraph relays may be done seated.
