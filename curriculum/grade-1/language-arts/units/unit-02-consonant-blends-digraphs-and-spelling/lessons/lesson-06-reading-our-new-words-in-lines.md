# Lesson 06 — Reading Our New Words in Lines

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can read whole LINES of writing using
everything I know: blends, digraphs, sentence marks, and checking with
my brain."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** 3 decodable line strips
(large print, adult-made), pointer, word cards from the unit sets.
**Standards:** RF.1.3.a; RF.1.3.b; RF.1.1.a (review).

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Card review race: the adult flashes *frog, hand, ship, chick, back,
sing, swim, lunch* — the learner reads each with point–say–blend.
8/8 with teams pushed fast means the tools are sharp.

## Explicit teaching

Reading a line is reading one word at a time, with three jobs:

1. **Decode each word** — point under it, say every sound, push the
   teams, blend.
2. **Watch the sentence marks** — capital at the start, ending mark
   at the end (period = telling voice, question mark = asking voice,
   exclamation = excited voice). Unit 01 skills, still on duty.
3. **Check with your brain** — does it make sense? If "The crab sat
   in the sand" comes out wrong, go back and re-decode the tricky
   word. Smart readers self-correct.

*Adult reads a line first, tracking with the pointer, slightly
exaggerating expression. Then they read together (choral), then the
learner reads alone.*

### Modeled example 1 — "The crab sat in the sand."

*Adult points word by word:* "**The** — taught whole. **crab** — /k/
/r/ /ă/ /b/ — cr team, push fast: crab! **sat** — old friend from
Unit 01. **in** — old friend. **the** — taught whole. **sand** — /s/
/ă/ /n/ /d/ — nd end team: sand! Period — telling voice." *Adult
rereads with a smile in the voice.* "Check with my brain: a crab
sitting in sand — that makes sense!"

### Modeled example 2 — "That fish is quick!"

*Adult points:* "**That** — taught whole. **fish** — /f/ /ĭ/ /sh/ —
sh team: fish! **is** — old friend. **quick** — /kw/ /ĭ/ /k/ — qu AND
ck, two teams in one word! Exclamation point — excited voice:
'That fish is quick!'" *Adult asks:* "Brain check — can a fish be
quick?" (Yes!) "Good. We read it AND we understood it."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Choral read: "A frog can jump in the pond." (All decodable or
   taught.)
2. Learner reads alone: "This ship can go fast." Adult assists only
   the taught whole words (*This, go*) if needed.
3. "Find the digraph team in each word of 'A chick sat with the
   hen.'" (chick: ch + ck; with: th; the: th.)
4. Expression game: read "That fish is quick!" three ways — telling,
   asking, excited. Which ending mark matches which voice?
5. Brain check: the adult deliberately misreads — "A frog can jump
   in the... pong?" The learner catches it and fixes the word
   (*pond*).

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Read all three line strips alone, tracking with a pointer
   (adult notes fluency: accurate, steady, expressive).
2. Circle (with a crayon) one blend team and one digraph team in
   each line.
3. Write one line of the learner's own using at least two unit words
   (adult scribes if needed; phonetic spelling accepted).
4. Draw the funniest line and read it to a stuffed animal.

## Application

**News anchor:** the learner is the TV news anchor reading the day's
"blend news." The adult holds the line strips like a teleprompter;
the anchor reads each line with anchor voice (clear, a little slow,
big expression at the ending marks), then summarizes the news in one
sentence: "The big news today: a quick fish!"

## Exit check

Learner reads a NEW line: "A chick can swim in the pond." (All words
decodable/taught.) Success: every word decoded with teams handled,
period read as a telling voice, one-sentence retell correct.

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** learner slides a word-window (a card with
  a cut-out) across the strip, revealing one word at a time.
- **Floor-play variant:** line strips taped to the floor; learner
  walks the line, stepping on each word as they read it.
- **Language access:** act out each line before reading (be the crab,
  be the quick fish); pictures beside each line if helpful.
- **Alternate response mode:** the adult reads the line first and the
  learner echoes phrase by phrase; the learner points to each team as
  they echo.
- **Extension:** add a second sentence to a strip and read the pair;
  or hunt teams in a library picture book with the adult reading the
  hard parts.

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **Word-by-word robot reading:** reread lines chorally with
  expression before solo attempts; fluency grows from hearing the
  music of the line.
- **Skipping the check step:** celebrate every self-correction
  ("You fixed it yourself — that's what real readers do!").
- **Ending-mark voice:** if the learner reads "That fish is quick!"
  flatly, cover the line and reveal only the mark: "What voice does
  THIS want?"
