# Lesson 01 — Blends Are Sound Teams

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can read words where two consonants team
up at the front, hearing BOTH sounds fast — like *frog* and *swim*."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** letter tiles (lowercase), 8
word cards: *frog, swim, clap, stop, crab, sled, flag, drink*.
**Standards:** RF.1.3.b; RF.1.2.b.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Lay out tiles **s t o p**. "Say each sound slowly: /s/ ... /t/ ...
/ŏ/ ... /p/. Now say them fast together." If the learner pushes the
four sounds into "stop," the ear is ready. If not, review Unit 01's
blending first.

## Explicit teaching

In Unit 01 every word started with ONE consonant: *cat* starts with
/k/. Today we meet **blends** — two consonants that stand together and
team up. A blend is special: you still hear **both** sounds, you just
say them **fast**, one right after the other.

Two rules for the Sound Team:

1. **Point and say every sound.** In *frog*, don't skip: /f/ ... /r/
   ... /ŏ/ ... /g/.
2. **Push the first two together fast.** /f/ ... /r/ run into each
   other — "fr" — then add the rest: "frog!"

Compare *f-o-g* (fog) with *f-r-o-g* (frog): the blend team adds one
more consonant, and your mouth says both, fast.

*Adult writes the team's new word on a card while the learner watches:*
**bl, cl, fl · br, cr, dr, fr · sm, sn, st, sw, sc, sp, str, spl** — the
Front Team. (Read through them chorally, fast-pushing each.)

### Modeled example 1 — *swim*

*Adult points at tiles s-w-i-m:* "Point: /s/. Point: /w/. Push the
team together — /s/ ... /w/ — 'sw'! *(smile, say it fast)* Now finish:
/ĭ/ ... /m/ — **swim!** Check: is it a real word? Swim is a real word —
check!" *Adult mimes swimming one stroke.*

### Modeled example 2 — *crab*

*Adult points at c-r-a-b:* "Point: /k/. Point: /r/. Push the team:
'k-r' — **cr!** Now /ă/ ... /b/ — **crab!** Real word? Yes — a crab
is a real animal. Check!" *Adult holds up the card and the learner
finds the /k/ and /r/ sounds with a finger under each.*

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Tiles **f-l-a-g**. "Point each sound. Push the team: /f/ /l/ →
   'fl'. Finish the word." (*flag*)
2. Tiles **s-n-a-p**. "Which two letters team up? Say them fast."
   (*snap*)
3. Tiles **s-t-r-a-p**. "Three letters team up — str! Stretch, then
   push fast." (*strap*)
4. Card **clap**: "Read it the Sound Team way. Point–say–push–finish."
5. Card **drink**: "Where is the team? At the front. Which letters?"
   (dr)

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Read the cards *sled, stop, plan, trip* aloud with pointing.
2. Adult segments "/g/ /r/ /ă/ /b/"; the learner says the word.
3. Learner segments "flag" for the adult; adult blends it back.
4. Draw one initial-blend word (e.g., a frog) and copy its word card
   under the drawing (adult scribes if the learner only dictates).

## Application

**Blend detective walk:** the adult reads a short line slowly — "The
big black cat sat in the grass." The learner claps each time they hear
a word that STARTS with a blend team (*black*, *grass*). (Adult
pre-reads the line; all blends are initial.)

## Exit check

Adult shows three cards: *crab, flag, sled*. The learner reads all
three with point–say–push–finish, naming the team in each ("cr", "fl",
"sl"). Success: 3/3 with both consonant sounds heard.

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** tiles laid in a row on the table; learner
  slides a finger under each sound.
- **Floor-play variant:** big tiles or cards on the floor; learner hops
  from letter to letter, saying each sound, then jumps to the "blend
  pad" (a cushion) to say the team fast.
- **Language access:** name each picture card before reading its word
  ("This is a frog. It says frog. Where is the fr team?"); act out
  verbs (swim, clap, stop).
- **Alternate response mode:** learner points to each letter and nods
  at the fast push instead of saying it, if speech is still forming —
  the adult says the word, the learner judges real/non-real.
- **Extension:** three-letter teams *str, spl, squ* — read *strip,
  split, squint*... (hold *squint* until Lesson 05's qu).

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **The swallow:** learners often drop the second consonant (*frog* →
  "fog"). Fix: exaggerate the stretch — /f/ ... /r/ — then blend fast.
  Mirror check: the mouth moves twice.
- **The vowel peek:** learners sometimes insert a vowel between the
  consonants ("fuh-rog"). Say the two sounds with no vowel between —
  "fr" is one mouth movement run together.
- **Too slow to blend:** if the learner segments perfectly but cannot
  push, practice pushing pairs alone (fr, sw, cl, st) before adding
  the vowel.
