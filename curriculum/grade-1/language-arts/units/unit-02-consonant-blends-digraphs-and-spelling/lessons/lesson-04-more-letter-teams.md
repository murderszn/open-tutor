# Lesson 04 — th, wh, ck, ng: More Letter Teams

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can read four more letter teams: *th*,
*wh*, *ck*, and *ng* — in *thumb, whale, back, and sing*."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** the picture shelf
[`../assets/digraph-shelf.png`](../assets/digraph-shelf.png), letter
tiles (t+h, w+h, c+k, n+g pairs), 8 word cards: *this, that, when,
whale, back, duck, sing, song*.
**Standards:** RF.1.3.a; RF.1.3.b.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Picture shelf again: "Point to the card that starts with /th/." (The
thumbs-up hand — thumb.) "Point to the card that starts with /wh/."
(The whale.) "Point to the cards with /sh/." (Ship — start; fish —
end.) Yesterday's teams are still here; today we meet four new ones.

## Explicit teaching

Four more **digraphs** — two letters, one sound:

- **th** — stick your tongue tip BETWEEN your teeth and blow:
  "*thhh*." That's **thumb**. Now say **this** — feel your throat
  buzz? Same letters, two sounds: a quiet th (*thumb, bath*) and a
  buzzy th (*this, that, with*). Your mouth does the same job either
  way.
- **wh** — round your lips and blow gently: "*whhh*." **whale**,
  **when**. (In words like *who* it says /h/ — not this unit.)
- **ck** — says /k/, and it almost always comes **after a short
  vowel** at the end: ba-ck, du-ck. (You heard it in *chick*
  yesterday!)
- **ng** — the back-of-the-mouth hum: "**si-ng**." Say *sing* and
  feel the sound sit in the back: n-g is ONE sound, not n then g.

The chant: *"Two letters, one sound — th, wh, ck, ng!"*

### Modeled example 1 — *duck*

*Adult holds c and k as one pair:* "ck is one sound: /k/. Point:
/d/ /ŭ/ /k/ — **duck!** Notice: short vowel u, then ck. That's the
ck rule you'll spell in Lesson 05."

### Modeled example 2 — *sing*

*Adult holds n and g as one pair:* "ng is one sound — hum it: nggg.
Point: /s/ /ĭ/ /ng/ — **sing!** Only three sounds. Real word? Yes —
sing a song! (And *song* has the same team at the end: /s/ /ŏ/ /ng/.)"

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Card **thumb**: "Where's the team? What does the mouth do?"
   (Tongue between teeth.) Read.
2. Card **this**: "Quiet th or buzzy th? Put a hand on your throat."
   Read.
3. Card **back**: "Point to ck. Which vowel comes before it?" (a —
   short.) Read.
4. Card **whale**: "Name the team. Read the word." (Whale is taught
   whole — the vowel team comes in Unit 03.)
5. Card **song**: "How many sounds? Name the end team." (/s/ /ŏ/
/ng/ — three sounds.)

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Read the cards *that, thick, when, which, sock, rock, king, wing*
   aloud with pointing.
2. Four-tray sort: **th-team, wh-team, ck-team, ng-team** — the adult
   deals *thumb, when, back, sing, bath, whale, duck, thing* (6–8
   cards).
3. With tiles, build *sock* and *sing*; the adult checks pairs travel
   together.
4. Thumbs-up/thumbs-down game: the adult says "thumb, when, song,
   brick, fish" — learner shows thumbs UP for /th, wh, ck, ng/ words,
   DOWN for others.

## Application

**Picture-shelf quiz show:** the learner is the teacher. Holding the
shelf, the learner points at each card and announces: "Ship — /sh/ at
the start! Fish — /sh/ at the end! Chair — /ch/ at the start! Peach —
/ch/ at the end! Whale — /wh/ at the start! Thumb — /th/ at the
start!" Then the adult names a team and the learner points. Six for
six is a star round.

## Exit check

Cards *this, back, sing, whale*. The learner reads all four, naming
each team and where it sits. Success: 4/4 with each digraph as one
sound; *whale* may be read as taught whole word.

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** learner traces each digraph pair on sand
  while saying the single sound — one motion, one sound.
- **Floor-play variant:** four hula hoops labeled th / wh / ck / ng;
  the adult deals word cards, the learner hops each card to its hoop.
- **Language access:** act out each target (thumb up, whale spout
  with arms, duck waddle, sing a line); name pictures before reading
  the words.
- **Alternate response mode:** learner points at the shelf cards while
  the adult says teams; the learner never has to decode the four new
  teams aloud until ready.
- **Extension:** find TWO teams in one word — *think* (th + ng),
  *drink* (dr + nk), *shock* (sh + ck), *strength*? (hold — long
  vowel e; try *string* only if ready).

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **The ng split** (*sing* → "sing-guh"). Hum the team: one nasal
  sound at the back. Practice *sing–sang–song–sung* as a hum ladder.
- **th vs. f:** some learners substitute /f/ for th. Mirror + tongue
  between teeth is the fix; never let it slide, or spelling breaks
  too.
- **ck everywhere:** learners may spell *back* as "bak." Hold that
  thought for Lesson 05 — today, just read it right.
- **Whale's vowel:** *whale* is a long-a word taught whole here. Do
  not teach the a-e pattern yet — Unit 03 owns it. Praise the wh, not
  the guess at the vowel.
