# Lesson 05 — Spell It Out: Writing What You Hear

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can SPELL the sounds I hear — mapping
every sound in order, using the spelling patterns we know, and writing
new words the phonetic way."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** letter tiles, whiteboard or
paper and pencil, sand tray, the unit word sets.
**Standards:** L.1.2.d; L.1.2.e; RF.1.2.d.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Adult says "fish." Learner stretches it on fingers: /f/ /ĭ/ /sh/ —
three sounds. "You just MAPPED the word: every sound in order. Today
we write each sound down. Mapping is spelling's first step."

## Explicit teaching

Spelling is reading backwards. Four steps — **stretch, map, write,
check:**

1. **Stretch** the word slowly. 2. **Map** each sound on your fingers
   (one finger per sound — digraph teams take ONE finger!). 3. **Write**
   the letter(s) for each sound. 4. **Check** by reading what you
   wrote.

And the **spelling patterns** — grown-up rules that make writing
easier:

- **ck rule:** after a SHORT vowel at the end, /k/ is spelled **ck**:
  ba-ck, du-ck, clo-ck. (After other vowels we spell k differently —
  that's later.)
- **Double rule:** after a SHORT vowel at the end, f, l, and s usually
  **double**: o-ff, we-ll, mi-ss, cla-ss, ki-ss.
- **qu:** the /kw/ sound is spelled **qu**, and u is qu's best friend:
  qu-it, qu-ick.
- **x** at the end spells /ks/: bo-x, mi-x.

And the bravest rule of all: **phonetic spelling is real spelling.**
If you haven't learned a pattern yet, write what you hear — "truk" for
*truck* is a WIN (every sound, in order!). The adult circles the part
you got right and teaches the pattern later. Never fear a spelling —
map it.

### Modeled example 1 — *duck* (a pattern word)

*Adult stretches:* "/d/ /ŭ/ /k/ — three sounds. Map on fingers: d ...
u ... k. Write: d-u-?" *Adult pauses.* "Short vowel u, /k/ at the
end — the ck rule! d-u-ck. Check by reading: duck. The ck rule paid
off!"

### Modeled example 2 — *frog* (a map-it word)

*Adult stretches:* "/f/ /r/ /ŏ/ /g/ — four sounds. Map: f-r-o-g —
the fr team travels together but counts as TWO fingers, because we hear
both. Write: f-r-o-g. Check: frog. No special pattern — just honest
mapping."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Dictate **ship**. Learner: stretch, map (3 fingers — sh is one),
   write, check. (Adult praise: "sh is ONE sound, ONE finger!")
2. Dictate **sock**. "Short vowel o, /k/ at the end — which pattern?"
   (ck rule.)
3. Dictate **swim**. Learner maps 4 sounds, writes. (Phonetic win if
   the vowels are right.)
4. Dictate **well**. "Short vowel e, /l/ at the end — double rule?"
   (well.)
5. Dictate **box**. "Map it: /b/ /ŏ/ /ks/. The /ks/ team is x. Write:
   box."

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Dictation list (adult reads each twice, learner writes): *back,
   fish, lamp, chick, well.* (5 words — adult notes which step each
   word missed, if any.)
2. **Pattern hunt:** the learner finds the ck in *clock, brick, shock*
   cards and underlines the ck pair.
3. **Phonetic bravery:** the adult says "truck" (un-taught pattern —
   accept TRUK/TRUCK). Celebrate the mapping; the adult writes the
   conventional spelling beside it for later.
4. The learner writes one dictated sentence (adult scribes if needed):
   "The frog sat in the pond." — checks capital, spacing, period
   (Unit 01 sentence work returns).

## Application

**Store game:** the learner is the shopkeeper. The adult "buys" items
("I'd like a ship, please... a clock... a fish..."). The shopkeeper
writes each item on a receipt (phonetic spelling accepted), reads it
back to confirm the order, and stamps it SOLD. Five items per round.

## Exit check

Dictate **chick** and **jump**. Success: both mapped sound-by-sound
in order; *chick* shows ch and ck as teams; phonetic attempts (e.g.,
JUM P spaced) are accepted if every sound is present.

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** sound dots (Elkonin boxes) on paper — one
  box per sound, digraph teams share one box.
- **Floor-play variant:** learner stamps each sound with a hand stamp
  or beanbag toss onto floor letter cards, then writes at the table.
- **Language access:** dictate words whose meaning is known and acted
  (jump, swim, clap) before abstract ones.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner builds the word with tiles
  instead of writing; the adult writes it on the receipt while the
  learner watches each letter form.
- **Extension:** dictate two-team words — *think* (th + ng), *shock*
  (sh + ck), *drink* (dr + nk) — mapping 4 fingers for 5–6 letters.

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **Writing letters, not sounds** ("chick" → C-H-I-C-K as five
  finger-counts). Always count SOUNDS first (3 for chick), then
  letters.
- **Random ck:** learners may write "ck" at the START of words.
  Reinforce: ck is an END team, after a short vowel.
- **Fear of wrong spelling:** if the learner freezes, say "map it
  phonetically — brave spelling counts." Rushing to correct kills the
  willingness to write. Mark phonetic attempts as WINS in the key.
