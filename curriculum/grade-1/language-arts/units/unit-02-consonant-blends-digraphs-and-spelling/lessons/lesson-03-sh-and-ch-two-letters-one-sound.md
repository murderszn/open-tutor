# Lesson 03 — sh and ch: Two Letters, One Sound

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can read *sh* and *ch* — two letters that
team up to make ONE brand-new sound. *ship, fish, chick, lunch.*"
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** the generated illustration
[`../assets/digraph-shelf.png`](../assets/digraph-shelf.png) (printed
or on screen), letter tiles (incl. s+h and c+h pushed together), 8 word
cards: *ship, fish, shop, wish, chick, chat, chip, lunch*.
**Standards:** RF.1.3.a; RF.1.3.b.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Show the [digraph shelf](../assets/digraph-shelf.png). "Name the six
picture cards with me: ship ... fish ... chair ... peach ... whale ...
thumb." (Text-only alternative: the cards show a ship, a fish, a chair,
a peach, a whale, and a thumbs-up hand.) "Two cards share the same
team sound: ship and fish — both have /sh/! Where is the /sh/ in each
one?" (ship: start; fish: end.) "Today we meet the first digraph teams:
**sh** and **ch**. Th and wh arrive in Lesson 04."

## Explicit teaching

Yesterday's teams (blends) let you hear **both** sounds: *f-r-o-g*.
Today's teams are **digraphs** — two letters that join into **ONE new
sound** you cannot guess from the parts.

- **sh** — put a finger on your lips: "*shhh*!" Say *shhh-ip*.
  The word **ship** starts with ONE sound, written with TWO letters.
  At the end of **fish**: one sound again — "fishhh."
- **ch** — the choo-choo sound: "*chugga-chugga-CHOO-CHOO*!" Say
  *chick*: the first sound is /ch/, written with TWO letters.
  **lunch** ends with the same ONE sound: "lun-ch."

The test: "If you hear two sounds, it's a blend. If you hear ONE sound
from two letters, it's a **digraph**. Two letters, one sound!"

Point at the picture shelf: **ship** and **fish** both carry /sh/ —
at the start and at the end. **chair**... (today we read *ch* words;
the chair picture is a clue for the /ch/ sound — say "ch-air" and hear
the /ch/ at the front, just like *chick*).

### Modeled example 1 — *fish*

*Adult pushes f-i-s-h tiles together, holding s and h as one pair:*
"Two letters, one sound: s-h says /sh/. So the sounds are /f/ /ĭ/
/sh/ — just THREE sounds! Blend: **fish!**" *Adult points at the fish
card on the shelf.* "The picture says it: fish!"

### Modeled example 2 — *chick*

*Adult holds c and h as one pair:* "Two letters, one sound: c-h says
/ch/. Sounds: /ch/ /ĭ/ /k/ — THREE sounds. Blend: **chick!**"
*Adult points at the chick card... (no chick card — the chair shows
the /ch/ sound too: "chair starts with the same sound: ch-air").*
Tricky bit: *chick* ends with ck — another two-letters-one-sound team
(Lesson 04 will name it: ck spells /k/).

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Card **ship**: "Is sh one sound or two? Read the word: /sh/ /ĭ/ /p/."
2. Card **wish**: "Where is the digraph — front or back?" (Back.)
   Read.
3. Card **chat**: "Name the digraph. What ONE sound does it say?"
4. Card **lunch**: "How many sounds? Hold up fingers as you say
   them." (/l/ /ŭ/ /n/ /ch/ — 4 sounds, though it has 5 letters.)
5. Picture shelf: "Point to the card whose name ENDS with /ch/."
   (Peach.) "Point to the one that STARTS with /sh/." (Ship.)

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Read the cards *shop, dish, shut, chip* aloud with pointing.
2. Sort 6 cards (*ship, fish, chick, lunch, shop, much*) into
   **sh-team** and **ch-team** trays.
3. Build *ship* and *chat* with tiles; the adult checks the s+h and
   c+h pairs stay together.
4. Draw a fish; the adult labels it *fish* while saying "/f/ /ĭ/
   /sh/ — the sh is ONE sound."

## Application

**Shelf hunt:** with the picture shelf in front of the learner, the
adult says a sound — "/sh/ at the END" → the learner points to the
fish card; "/ch/ at the START" → chair... (and in word cards, *chick,
chip, chat*). Score one point per correct point; six rounds.

## Exit check

Cards *ship, chick, wish, lunch*. The learner reads all four, naming
the digraph and its position in each. Success: 4/4 with the digraph
said as ONE sound.

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** learner slides the s+h tile pair as one
  unit — "they travel together, like friends."
- **Floor-play variant:** two carpet squares marked sh and ch; the
  adult calls words, the learner hops to the right digraph's square.
- **Language access:** say each word's meaning first (a ship sails, a
  chick is a baby chicken); act out "shhh" (library finger) and
  "choo-choo."
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner sorts word cards onto two
  trays while the adult reads each aloud; the learner only has to find
  the digraph team.
- **Extension:** *smash, brush, such, rich* — longer digraph words;
  can the learner find BOTH teams in *smash* (sm + sh)?

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **Stretching the digraph apart** (*sh* → "suh-huh"). Model once:
  "sh is one mouth movement — shhh." Have the learner say it while
  feeling the air on a hand.
- **Adding ch to sh and vice versa:** keep mouth cues — lips push out
  and round for /sh/; /ch/ has a little explosion behind the lips.
  Mirror work helps.
- **Counting letters as sounds:** *lunch* has 5 letters but 4 sounds.
  Use sound dots (Elkonin boxes): 4 dots for lunch.
