# Lesson 03 — ee and ea Say Long e

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can read words where ee and ea team up
to say long e — like *see* and *beach*."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** letter tiles (lowercase),
8 word cards: *see, bee, tree, feet, sea, tea, leaf, beach*.
**Standards:** RF.1.3.c.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

"Say the long e sound: /ē/ — e says its name. Now read *rain*."
If the learner reads the ai word with a long a, the team idea is
still there. Then: "Today two new teams make the /ē/ sound."

## Explicit teaching

For long e, the teams are **ee** and **ea**. Same walking rule:
the first vowel talks (/ē/), the second walks quietly. In *see*,
the first e talks and the second e walks. In *beach*, e talks and
a walks.

Unlike ai/ay, ee and ea do not have a simple position rule — both
can sit in the middle or at the end (*see, tree, sea, leaf*). So
for long e, the learner's job is simpler: **see the team, say
/ē/**, and check that the word is real.

*Adult writes the teams on a card while the learner watches:*
**ee · ea** — both say /ē/.

### Modeled example 1 — *see*, step by step

*Adult points at tiles s-e-e:* "Point: /s/. Now the team: e and e
walk together. The first one talks — /ē/. The second one walks
quietly. **See!** Check: is it a real word? See is real — check!"
*Adult points to their own eyes.*

### Modeled example 2 — *beach*, and the honest exception note

*Adult points at b-e-a-ch:* "Point: /b/. Team: e talks, /ē/, a
walks quietly. Finish: /ch/ — **beach!**"
"Honest note: ea SOMETIMES says something else — *bread* and
*head* say short e. Those are words to learn by heart later. Today,
ea says long e, and every word on our cards follows the rule."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Tiles **s-e-e**. "Find the team. Who talks? Read the word."
   (*see*, ee)
2. Card **beach**: "Find the team. Who talks?" (ea, e talks)
3. "Which team — *feet* or *seat* — has ea?" (*seat*)
4. Tiles **t-r-e-e**. "Read it the team way." (*tree*)
5. Sort: *green* goes to the ee pile, *leaf* goes to the ea pile.

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Read the cards *sleep, sea, sheep, dream* aloud with pointing.
2. Adult segments "/s/ /ē/ /l/"; the learner says the word.
   (*seal*)
3. Learner segments "feet" for the adult; adult blends it back.
4. Draw a tree and copy its word card under the drawing (adult
   scribes if the learner only dictates).

## Application

**ee/ea hunt:** the adult reads a short line slowly — "I see a bee
on a leaf by the sea." The learner claps each time they hear a word
with an ee or ea team (*see, bee, leaf, sea*). (Adult pre-reads
the line; all four words use the teams taught.)

## Exit check

Adult shows three cards: *bee, beach, feet*. The learner reads all
three, naming the team in each ("ee," "ea," "ee"). Success: 3/3
with long e heard in every word.

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** tiles laid in a row; learner covers the
  walking vowel with a finger and reads only the talking vowel,
  then the whole word.
- **Floor-play variant:** big team cards on the floor; learner
  steps on the talking vowel and whispers the walking vowel.
- **Language access:** name each picture card before reading its
  word ("This is a beach. It says beach. Where is the ea team?");
  act out verbs (see, sleep, dream).
- **Alternate response mode:** learner points to the team and nods
  at the talking vowel instead of saying the rule, if speech is
  still forming.
- **Extension:** *three, free, keep, bean, seal, peach* — read and
  sort by team; the learner explains why *three* still counts
  (th team + ee team in one word).

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **Sounding both vowels** (*see* → "seh-eh"): same fix as Lesson
  02 — only the first vowel talks.
- **ea overgeneralized:** if the learner meets *bread* or *head*
  in other reading, do not call it a mistake — call it a
  rule-breaker and park it for the heart-word list. Testing ea
  only on long-e words keeps this lesson fair.
- **ee/ea spelling swaps** (*se* for *sea*): praise the long e,
  then show the team. Spelling teams exactly is a Unit 03 goal,
  not a Lesson 03 gate.
