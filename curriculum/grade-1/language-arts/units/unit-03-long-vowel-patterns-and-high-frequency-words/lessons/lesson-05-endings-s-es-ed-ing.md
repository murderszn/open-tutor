# Lesson 05 — Endings: -s, -es, -ed, -ing

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can read words with endings — -s, -es,
-ed, and -ing — and I know when to drop the e."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** letter tiles (lowercase),
10 word cards: *cats, dogs, dishes, boxes, liked, played, wanted,
jumping, singing, making*.
**Standards:** RF.1.3.f; RF.1.3.c (drop-e uses the magic-e words).

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

"Read *cake*. Now read *cakes*." If the learner reads both, the
-s ending is already emerging. Then: "Today endings do four jobs:
more than one, past time, and happening now."

## Explicit teaching

An **ending** is a small part added to the END of a word that
changes what the word means. This unit's four endings:

- **-s** — more than one, or happening now: *cat* → *cats*,
  *dog* → *dogs*.
- **-es** — more than one, when the word ends in s, sh, ch, or x
  (sounds that need an extra beat): *dish* → *dishes*, *box* →
  *boxes*, *watch* → *watches*.
- **-ed** — happened in the past. It has THREE sounds: /t/ in
  *liked, jumped, helped, watched*; /d/ in *played, rained,
  showed, cleaned*; /ĭd/ in *wanted, painted, landed, counted*.
  (Put a hand on your throat: /d/ and /ĭd/ buzz, /t/ does not.)
- **-ing** — happening right now: *jump* → *jumping*, *sing* →
  *singing*. And the **drop-e rule**: when a word ends in magic e,
  drop the e before -ing — *make* → *making*, *hope* → *hoping*,
  *ride* → *riding*. (The e already did its magic; it steps aside
  for -ing.)

One more spelling note for -ed: when a word already ends in e,
just add d — *like* → *liked*, *hope* → *hoped*. No double e.

*Adult writes the endings on a card while the learner watches:*
**-s · -es · -ed (/t/ /d/ /ĭd/) · -ing (drop the e)**.

### Modeled example 1 — *cats* and *dishes*, step by step

*Adult points at c-a-t-s:* "Base word: cat. Ending: -s. More than
one cat — **cats!**"
*Adult points at d-i-sh-e-s:* "Base word: dish. It ends in sh, so
it needs -es, not just -s. **Dishes!** Say it: dish-iz. The extra
beat is the es doing its job."

### Modeled example 2 — *-ed* has three sounds, and drop-e

*Adult shows liked, played, wanted in a row:* "Liked — listen at
the end: /t/. Played — /d/. Wanted — /ĭd/, an extra beat. Same
ending, three sounds. Your throat tells you which one."
*Adult shows m-a-k-e, then covers the e and adds i-n-g:*
"Make. Drop the e. Add -ing. **Making!** The e steps aside."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Card **cats**: "Base word? Ending? What does it mean?"
   (*cat*, -s, more than one)
2. Card **dishes**: "Base word? Why -es and not -s?"
   (*dish*; sh needs the extra beat)
3. Card **liked**: "Base word? Which -ed sound?" (*like*, /t/)
4. Card **wanted**: "Base word? Which -ed sound?" (*want*, /ĭd/)
5. Tiles **m-a-k-e**: "Drop the e, add -ing. Read it." (*making*)

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Read the cards *dogs, boxes, played, jumping* aloud with
   pointing.
2. Adult dictates "helped"; the learner writes it (phonetic
   attempts accepted and praised; conventional spelling is the
   goal, not a gate).
3. Learner segments "singing" for the adult; adult blends it back.
4. Draw someone jumping and copy its word card under the drawing
   (adult scribes if the learner only dictates).

## Application

**Ending detective:** the adult reads a short line slowly — "The
dogs played and jumped in the rain." The learner names each
ending they hear: *dogs* (-s), *played* (-ed), *jumped* (-ed).
(Adult pre-reads the line; *rain* is the ai team from Lesson 02.)

## Exit check

Adult shows three cards: *dishes, making, played*. The learner
reads all three, naming the ending in each ("-es," "-ing with
drop-e," "-ed, /d/"). Success: 3/3 with base word and ending
both named.

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** base-word card and ending card laid
  apart, then slid together; learner reads the base, then the
  whole word.
- **Floor-play variant:** base words on one side of the room,
  endings on the other; learner carries the ending to its base
  word and reads the result.
- **Language access:** act out every ending (more than one finger
  for -s, yesterday-pointing-back for -ed, right-now marching for
  -ing).
- **Alternate response mode:** learner points to the ending and
  holds up 1, 2, or 3 fingers for the -ed sound (/t/ = 1, /d/ = 2,
  /ĭd/ = 3) instead of naming it, if speech is still forming.
- **Extension:** doubling preview (adult note only, not assessed):
  *run* → *running*, *hop* → *hopping* — "short word, short
  vowel, double the last letter before -ing." Learners who notice
  it get praise; nobody is tested on it.

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **-ed always spelled "ed" but never one sound:** learners often
  read every -ed as /ĭd/ ("lik-ed"). Throat check + the three
  piles (liked / played / wanted) fixes this faster than rules.
- **Forgetting drop-e** (*makeing*): praise the -ing, then show
  the e stepping aside. Spelling drop-e exactly is a Unit 03 goal;
  reading *makeing* as "making" still earns the reading point.
- **-es added everywhere** (*catses*): the -es rule is only for
  s/sh/ch/x endings. The dishes/cats contrast pair teaches it.
- **Reading the base wrong:** if the base is a long-vowel word
  (*making* from *make*), re-check the magic-e word first.
