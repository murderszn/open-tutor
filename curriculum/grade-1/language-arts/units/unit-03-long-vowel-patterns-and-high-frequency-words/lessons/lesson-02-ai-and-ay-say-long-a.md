# Lesson 02 — ai and ay Say Long a

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can read words where ai and ay team up
to say long a — like *rain* and *play*."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** letter tiles (lowercase),
8 word cards: *rain, snail, train, mail, play, day, say, stay*.
**Standards:** RF.1.3.c.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

"Say the long a sound: /ā/ — a says its name. Now read *cape*."
If the learner reads the magic-e word with a long a, the ear is
ready. Then: "Today two vowels team up to make that same /ā/
sound."

## Explicit teaching

A **vowel team** is two vowels that stand together and make ONE
sound. For long a, the teams are **ai** and **ay**. The rule
children remember: "When two vowels go walking, the first one does
the talking." In *rain*, a talks (/ā/) and i walks quietly. In
*play*, a talks and y walks quietly.

Where does each team live? **ai usually hides in the middle** of a
word (*rain, snail, train*). **ay usually plays at the end**
(*play, day, say*). That position clue is the fastest way to tell
them apart when spelling.

*Adult writes the teams on a card while the learner watches:*
**ai (middle) · ay (end)** — both say /ā/.

### Modeled example 1 — *rain*, step by step

*Adult points at tiles r-a-i-n:* "Point: /r/. Now the team: a and
i walk together. The first one talks — a says /ā/. The i is
quiet. Finish: /n/ — **rain!** Check: is it a real word? Rain is
real — check!" *Adult mimes rain falling with fingertips.*

### Modeled example 2 — *play*, and the position check

*Adult points at p-l-a-y:* "Point: /p/ ... /l/. Now the team at
the END: a talks, /ā/, y walks quietly — **play!**"
"Where does ay live? At the end! Would *paly* be right?" (Swap
the tiles.) "No — ay plays at the end, not in the middle."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Tiles **r-a-i-n**. "Find the team. Who talks? Read the word."
   (*rain*, ai)
2. Card **play**: "Find the team. Where does it live?" (ay, at the
   end)
3. "Which team — *rain* or *play* — has ay at the end?" (*play*)
4. Tiles **t-r-a-y**. "Read it the team way." (*tray*)
5. Sort: *snail* goes to the ai pile, *day* goes to the ay pile.

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Read the cards *train, say, mail, stay* aloud with pointing.
2. Adult segments "/t/ /r/ /ā/ /n/"; the learner says the word.
   (*train*)
3. Learner segments "play" for the adult; adult blends it back.
4. Draw rain and copy its word card under the drawing (adult
   scribes if the learner only dictates).

## Application

**ai/ay detective line:** the adult reads a short line slowly —
"The snail plays in the rain all day." The learner claps each time
they hear a word with an ai or ay team (*snail, plays, rain,
day*). (Adult pre-reads the line; *plays* is *play* + -s, which
keeps the ay team.)

## Exit check

Adult shows three cards: *snail, play, train*. The learner reads
all three, naming the team in each ("ai," "ay," "ai"). Success:
3/3 with long a heard in every word.

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** tiles laid in a row; learner slides a
  finger under the team and says only the talking vowel.
- **Floor-play variant:** two learners (or learner and adult) walk
  together as the "team" — the first one talks, the second one
  walks quietly.
- **Language access:** name each picture card before reading its
  word ("This is a snail. It says snail. Where is the ai team?");
  act out verbs (play, stay, wait).
- **Alternate response mode:** learner points to the team and nods
  at the talking vowel instead of saying the rule, if speech is
  still forming.
- **Extension:** *brain, chain, spray, gray* — read and sort by
  team; the learner explains the position clue for each.

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **Sounding both vowels** (*rain* → "ra-in"): the walking chant
  fixes this — only the first vowel talks.
- **ai/ay swapped in spelling** (*rane* for *rain* is a phonetic
  attempt — praise the long a, then show the team): position clue
  first ("middle or end?"), team second.
- **Rule-breaker preview:** *said* and *again* have ai but do NOT
  say long a. They are heart words (Lesson 06), not pattern words.
  Do not test them here.
