# Lesson 06 — Two Syllables and Heart Words

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can break big words into syllables and
read them, and I can read heart words fast, by sight."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** letter tiles (lowercase),
8 word cards: *sunset, lemon, tiger, robot, said, was, of, you*;
heart-word cards: *said, was, of, have, some, come, do, you, are,
they, what, want*.
**Standards:** RF.1.3.d; RF.1.3.e; RF.1.3.g.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

"Clap your name. How many claps?" (Adult models with their own
name first.) "Words break into beats. Those beats are called
**syllables**." If the learner can clap a two-beat word, the ear
is ready.

## Explicit teaching

**Part 1 — Two syllables.** A **syllable** is one beat of a word,
and here is the rule that makes breaking words possible: **every
syllable has a vowel sound** (RF.1.3.d). So to break a word, find
the vowel sounds, clap once for each, and read each part:

- *sunset*: sun-set — two vowel sounds, two claps, two parts.
- *lemon*: lem-on — two vowel sounds, two claps, two parts.
- *tiger*: ti-ger — two vowel sounds, two claps, two parts.

Read each part, then put the parts together fast: "sun ... set ...
sunset!"

**Part 2 — Heart words.** Some words break the rules. *Said* has
ai but does not say long a. *Was* has a but does not say /ă/ or
/ā/. *Of* has f but the f says /v/. You cannot sound these out
— you learn them **by heart**. The heart-word way:

1. Say the word. 2. Spell the word. 3. Find the **tricky part**
   (the part that breaks the rule). 4. Draw a heart over the
   tricky part. 5. Read the word fast, by sight.

This unit's heart words: *said, was, of, have, some, come, do,
you, are, they, what, want*. (Extension: *from, there, one,
again*.)

*Adult writes the rule on a card while the learner watches:*
**Every syllable has a vowel sound. Heart words: say it, spell it,
heart the tricky part, read it fast.**

### Modeled example 1 — *sunset*, step by step

*Adult points at s-u-n-s-e-t:* "Find the vowel sounds: u ... e.
Two vowel sounds, two claps: sun (clap) set (clap). Read part 1:
sun. Read part 2: set. Put them together fast: **sunset!** Check:
is it a real word? Sunset is real — check!"

### Modeled example 2 — *said*, the heart-word way

*Adult holds up the said card:* "Say it: said. Spell it: s-a-i-d.
Tricky part: ai — it should say long a, but it says /ĕ/! Draw a
heart over the ai. Now read it fast, no sounding out: **said!**"

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Card **sunset**: "Clap the parts. Read each part. Put it
   together." (*sun-set*, sunset)
2. Card **lemon**: "How many vowel sounds? How many claps?"
   (2 and 2; lem-on)
3. Card **tiger**: "Break it. Read it." (*ti-ger*, tiger)
4. Heart card **said**: "Say it, spell it, heart the tricky part."
   (ai says /ĕ/)
5. Heart card **was**: "Say it, spell it, heart the tricky part."
   (a says /ŭ/)

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Read the two-syllable cards *robot, basket* aloud with
   pointing (break first, then together).
2. Adult says "napkin"; the learner claps its syllables (2) and
   reads it (*nap-kin*).
3. Read the heart cards *have, some, come, do* fast, by sight.
4. Draw a tiger and copy its word card under the drawing (adult
   scribes if the learner only dictates).

## Application

**Heart-word quick read:** the adult flashes four heart cards —
*said, was, of, you* — one at a time. The learner reads each fast,
with no sounding out. (If the learner starts sounding out, the
adult covers the word and says, "Heart words — read it fast, like
a friend's name.")

## Exit check

Adult shows two-syllable cards *sunset, lemon* and heart cards
*they, what*. The learner breaks and reads the two-syllable words
and reads the heart words by sight. Success: 4/4 (two-syllable
words broken into correct parts; heart words read without
sounding out).

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** draw a line between syllables with a
  finger before reading; heart cards get a real drawn heart over
  the tricky part.
- **Floor-play variant:** hop once per syllable across the room;
  heart words are "fast feet" — run past them while reading.
- **Language access:** name each picture card before reading its
  word ("This is a tiger. It says tiger. Where are the two vowel
  sounds?").
- **Alternate response mode:** learner claps syllables and points
  to the tricky part instead of explaining it, if speech is still
  forming.
- **Extension:** *rocket, pocket, dinner, winter, picnic, muffin*
  — break and read; extension heart words *from, there, one,
  again* — say, spell, heart the tricky part, read fast.

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **Counting letters instead of vowel sounds:** *basket* has 6
  letters but 2 vowel sounds (a, e) — 2 syllables. Vowel sounds,
  not letters, make the beats.
- **Sounding out heart words:** sounding out *said* as "s-ā-d"
  is the pattern working correctly and the word refusing. That is
  exactly why it is a heart word — celebrate the catch, then
  teach it by sight.
- **One-syllable words clapped as two** (*rain* → "ra-in"):
  rain has ONE vowel sound (/ā/, the team counts once). Teams are
  one sound, so one clap.
