# Lesson 01 — The Magic e

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can read words where a silent e at the
end makes the vowel say its own name — like *cape* and *kite*."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** letter tiles (lowercase, with
a spare e tile), 10 word cards: *cap, cape, tap, tape, kit, kite, hop,
hope, cub, cube*.
**Standards:** RF.1.3.c; RF.1.2.a (review: long vs. short by ear).

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Lay out tiles **c a p**. "Read it: cap. What sound does the a make?
Short a, /ă/." Then lay out **k i t**. "Read it: kit. Short i, /ĭ/."
If the learner reads both short-vowel words, the ear is ready. If
not, review Unit 01's short-vowel words first.

## Explicit teaching

A **long vowel** is a vowel that **says its own name**: a says /ā/,
e says /ē/, i says /ī/, o says /ō/, u says /ū/. A **short vowel**
says its short sound: /ă/, /ĕ/, /ĭ/, /ŏ/, /ŭ/.

Today's pattern is the **magic e** — also called **final e**. When
a word ends in vowel–consonant–e, the e jumps over the consonant,
gives the vowel its power, and then says nothing itself:

- The vowel says its **name** (long sound).
- The final e stays **silent**.

Magic-e chant (say it together, with actions): "E jumps over,
vowel gets power, vowel says its name, e says nothing!" (Jump a hand
over the tiles, wiggle fingers for power, point to your mouth for
the name, finger on lips for silent.)

*Adult writes the pattern on a card while the learner watches:*
**a_e · i_e · o_e · u_e** — the vowel, one consonant, silent e.

### Modeled example 1 — *cap* becomes *cape*, step by step

*Adult points at tiles c-a-p:* "Read: cap. Short a, /ă/."
*Adult adds the e tile:* "Now the magic e jumps over the p. The a
gets power and says its name: /ā/. The e says nothing. Read: cape!
Check: is it a real word? A cape is a real thing — check!"

### Modeled example 2 — *kit* becomes *kite*, and a wrong-e check

*Adult points at k-i-t, then adds e:* "Kit, short i. Add the magic e
— kite! The i says its name, /ī/. The e is silent."
"Now — would *kite* with NO e say kite?" (Cover the e.) "No! Kit,
short i. The e is the magic. No e, no long vowel."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Tiles **c-a-p**. "Add the magic e. Read the new word." (*cape*)
2. Tiles **h-o-p**. "Add the magic e. What does the o say now?"
   (*hope*, long o)
3. Card **kite**: "Which vowel says its name? What does the e do?"
   (i, /ī/; the e is silent)
4. Cards **cub** and **cube**: "Which one has the magic e?"
   (*cube*)
5. "Which word says 'made' — *mad* or *made*?" (*made*)

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Read the cards *tape, pine, note, tune* aloud with pointing.
2. Adult segments "/b/ /ī/ /t/"; the learner says the word.
   (*bite*)
3. Learner segments "hope" for the adult; adult blends it back.
4. Draw a kite and copy its word card under the drawing (adult
   scribes if the learner only dictates).

## Application

**Magic-e sort:** the adult lays out six cards — *cap, cape, pin,
pine, hop, hope*. The learner sorts them into two piles: "short
vowel" and "magic e (long vowel)," reading each word as it is
placed.

## Exit check

Adult shows three cards: *cape, kite, hope*. The learner reads all
three, naming the vowel that says its name in each ("a says /ā/,"
"i says /ī/," "o says /ō/"). Success: 3/3 with the long vowel heard
and the final e silent.

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** tiles laid in a row; learner slides the
  e tile over the consonant with a finger while chanting.
- **Floor-play variant:** big cards on the floor; learner hops from
  the vowel to the e, saying the long sound on the landing.
- **Language access:** act out the pairs (hop, then hope with hands
  on heart; a cap, then a cape swept around shoulders).
- **Alternate response mode:** learner points to the vowel that says
  its name and covers the silent e, instead of saying the rule, if
  speech is still forming.
- **Extension:** u_e pairs (*cub/cube, cut/cute*) and o_e pairs
  (*not/note, hop/hope*) read as mixed pairs; the learner explains
  which pile each word belongs in and why.

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **Reading the e aloud** (*cape* → "cap-ee"): cover the e with a
  finger — "e says nothing." The chant's last line fixes this.
- **Magic-e everywhere:** some words end in e but do NOT follow the
  rule (*have, some, come, love*). Name these now as "rule-breakers"
  — they return as heart words in Lesson 06. Never punish a learner
  for reading *have* as /hāv/ on a first try; it is exactly what
  the pattern predicts.
- **Vowel-name confusion:** if the learner says the short sound in
  a magic-e word, go back to the pair (*cap/cape*) and let the
  contrast teach it.
