# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Long-Vowel Patterns and High-Frequency Word Practice

Adult-facing guidance for all 16 sessions. Learner materials live in
[lessons/](lessons/), [practice.md](practice.md),
[project.md](project.md), and [assessments/](assessments/); every
solution lives in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## Session-by-session guidance

- **Sessions 1–2 (magic e):** slow down on Session 1 — the chant
  ("E jumps over, vowel gets power, vowel says its name, e says
  nothing!") is the whole unit's engine. If a learner reads the
  final e aloud, cover it with a finger and re-chant before
  re-reading.
- **Sessions 3–6 (vowel teams):** keep the walking chant front and
  center ("the first one talks, the second walks quietly"). Teach
  one long-vowel sound per lesson (a, then e, then o); do not
  introduce all six teams in one sitting.
- **Sessions 7–8 (oa/ow and the Train):** the Train picture is a
  review tool, not a test — name every picture together before
  any sorting. The snowman stands for *snow*; say so explicitly
  the first time.
- **Sessions 9–10 (endings):** dictation is the unit's hardest new
  skill after heart words. Dictate slowly, repeat words, and
  praise mapping over correctness. The -ed three-sound piles
  (liked / played / wanted) matter more than any rule statement.
- **Session 11 (two syllables and heart words):** teach the two
  halves separately — clap-and-break first, heart words second.
  Never let a learner sound out a heart word for more than a few
  seconds; cover it and say, "Heart word — read it fast."
- **Sessions 12–14 (project):** the book is the unit's proof of
  transfer. Let the learner choose words they are most confident
  in; struggling learners may write 2 words per pattern page and
  still meet the rubric at "Developing."
- **Sessions 15–16 (quiz + assessment):** give the quiz, re-teach
  the weakest pattern the same session (use the re-teach pointers
  in Session H), then assess. A learner can be re-tested on one
  pattern or ending type alone the next day if the full
  assessment overwhelms.

## Common misconceptions (all lessons)

1. **Reading the silent e** (*cape* → "cap-ee"): cover the e,
   re-chant, re-read.
2. **Magic-e everywhere** (*have* read as /hāv/): not a mistake —
   the pattern predicts it. Name *have, some, come* as
   rule-breakers and park them for Lesson 06.
3. **Sounding both team vowels** (*rain* → "ra-in"): only the
   first vowel talks; the walking chant fixes this.
4. **ai/ay and oa/ow position swaps** in spelling: position clue
   first ("middle or end?"), team second. Phonetic attempts that
   keep the long vowel earn praise; exact team spelling is the
   goal, not a gate, until the project.
5. **ea/ow overgeneralized:** *bread, head, cow, now* are
   rule-breakers, not errors. Park them; do not test them here.
6. **Every -ed as /ĭd/** ("lik-ed"): throat check + the three
   piles (liked / played / wanted).
7. **Forgetting drop-e** (*makeing*): praise the -ing, show the e
   stepping aside. Reading *makeing* as "making" still earns the
   reading point.
8. **Counting letters instead of vowel sounds** for syllables:
   *basket* has 6 letters but 2 vowel sounds — 2 syllables.
9. **Sounding out heart words:** sounding out *said* as "s-ā-d"
   proves the pattern works and the word refuses. That is exactly
   why it is a heart word — celebrate the catch, teach it by
   sight.
10. **Guessing from the first letter:** always point under EVERY
    letter; the check step ("real word? does it make sense?")
    catches guesses.

## Readiness and re-teaching

- **Ready for Unit 04** when the learner scores 16+/20 on the unit
  assessment, or 12–15 with the missed pattern or ending type
  re-taught. All six objectives must reach at least "Developing."
- **Not yet ready:** use the flexible/catch-up weeks. Re-teach
  sequence: (a) the weakest pattern in isolation (pairs and
  teams before words); (b) the paired practice session; (c) one
  re-dictation or re-read of the failed assessment tasks only —
  never the whole assessment at once.
- **What "ready" looks like per objective:** (1) reads 4/5
  magic-e pairs correctly; (2) sorts 5/6 team words correctly;
  (3) names the ending on 3/4 words; (4) breaks 3/4 two-syllable
  words correctly; (5) reads 9/12 heart words by sight; (6) reads
  the story with at most 2 self-corrected errors per page.

## Accessibility and differentiation

- Every check accepts oral, pointing, or drawing responses; the
  adult scribes dictated writing.
- Large-print, high-contrast word cards; textured/tactile letters
  for low-vision learners; face the learner when modeling sounds
  and use mirror work for mouth shapes.
- Short sessions with movement breaks; every lesson includes a
  seated-table and a floor-play variant.
- The Train picture ships with a text-only alternative naming all
  six pictures; color is never the only cue.
- Language support: vocabulary taught with objects and pictures
  first, word second; home-language labels welcomed alongside
  English.
