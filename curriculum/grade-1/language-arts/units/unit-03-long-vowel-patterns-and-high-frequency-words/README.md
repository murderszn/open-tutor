# Unit 03 — Long-Vowel Patterns and High-Frequency Word Practice

**Grade:** 1 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #12](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/12), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

In Unit 01 the learner read short-vowel words like *cat* and *pin*.
In Unit 02 consonants teamed up in blends and digraphs. Now the vowels
take their turn: a vowel that **says its own name** is a **long vowel**.
In this unit the learner meets the two big ways to spell a long vowel —
the **magic e** at the end (*cap* becomes *cape*, *kit* becomes *kite*)
and **vowel teams** (*rain, play, see, beach, boat, snow*) — then adds
**endings** (*-s, -es, -ed, -ing*), breaks **two-syllable words** into
parts (*sun-set, lem-on*), and learns a set of **heart words** — words
like *said* and *was* that break the rules and must be learned by
heart. The unit ends with the learner's own Long-Vowel Book and a
decodable story, "Jake and the Kite," that uses only patterns taught
so far.

## Essential question

When a vowel says its own name, how do I know which letters spell
that sound — and what do I do when a word breaks the rules?

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–02 and the diagnostic weeks (1–2):

- Knows letter names and the common sounds of consonants and short
  vowels; can blend and segment spoken CVC words (RF.1.2.b, RF.1.2.d)
- Decodes regularly spelled one-syllable words with short vowels,
  initial/final blends, and digraphs sh, ch, th, wh, ck, ng, qu
  (RF.1.3.a–b)
- Can hear the difference between a long and a short vowel sound in a
  spoken word (RF.1.2.a, from Unit 01)
- Marks sentence features (first word, capital, ending punctuation)
- Short independent written practice (4–6 tasks) reviewed by the adult
  the same day

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed;
oral, pointing, and drawing responses accepted everywhere; short
written practice of 4–6 tasks is reviewed by the adult the same day):

1. **Read final-e words:** decode CVCe words (a_e, i_e, o_e, u_e) and
   contrast short/long pairs (*cap/cape, kit/kite, hop/hope,
   cub/cube*), saying the vowel's name and leaving the final e
   silent.
2. **Read vowel teams:** decode one-syllable words with the common
   vowel teams **ai, ay** (long a), **ee, ea** (long e), and **oa,
   ow** (long o), and sort words by their team.
3. **Read endings:** read words with inflectional endings **-s, -es,
   -ed, -ing**, including the drop-e rule before -ing (*make* →
   *making*), and say the three sounds of -ed (/t/, /d/, /ĭd/).
4. **Read two-syllable words:** use the rule "every syllable has a
   vowel sound" to clap, count, and break two-syllable words into
   syllables, then read each part and put the word together
   (*sun-set, lem-on, ti-ger*).
5. **Read heart words by sight:** recognize and read grade-appropriate
   irregularly spelled high-frequency words (*said, was, of, have,
   some, come, do, you, are, they, what, want*) without sounding
   them out letter by letter.
6. **Read connected decodable text:** read decodable lines and the
   unit story using Units 01–03 patterns, checking that each word
   makes sense and self-correcting with context.

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full
safe-materials list): letter tiles or magnetic letters (lowercase,
with a spare final-e tile), blank word cards and a marker, a small
mirror (mouth shapes for long vowels), sand tray or whiteboard,
notebook and pencil, drawing paper and crayons, the generated
illustration [`assets/long-vowel-train.png`](assets/long-vowel-train.png),
decodable word cards from the unit word sets below. No sharp tools;
adult supervises small parts in shared settings.

### Unit word sets (authored for this unit; reused across lessons and practice)

Decodable with Unit 01 + Unit 02 + Unit 03 skills (all one-syllable
unless marked *two-syllable* or *taught whole word*).

- **Short/long magic-e pairs:** cap/cape, tap/tape, kit/kite,
  hop/hope, cub/cube, mad/made, pin/pine, not/note, bit/bite,
  cut/cute
- **Magic-e words:** cake, cape, cane, game, made, rake, tape, name,
  plate, snake; bike, kite, pine, fine, line, time, hide, ride, bite,
  mine; home, hope, rope, nose, stone, bone, note, rose; cube, cute,
  mule, tune, dune
- **ai (long a, usually in the middle):** rain, snail, train, mail,
  paint, tail, sail, chain, wait, brain
- **ay (long a, usually at the end):** play, day, say, stay, tray,
  gray, clay, way, spray, hay
- **ee (long e):** see, bee, tree, feet, green, sleep, sheep, three,
  free, keep
- **ea (long e):** sea, tea, leaf, beach, dream, seat, read, bean,
  seal, peach
- **oa (long o):** boat, road, coat, soap, toast, loaf, goat, toad,
  foam, roam
- **ow (long o):** snow, blow, low, grow, show, slow, crow, flow,
  glow, tow
- **Endings -s:** cats, dogs, pigs, hats, runs, jumps, sings, reads
- **Endings -es:** dishes, watches, boxes, kisses, fixes, passes,
  buzzes
- **Endings -ed:** liked, jumped, helped, watched (/t/); played,
  rained, showed, cleaned (/d/); wanted, painted, landed, counted
  (/ĭd/)
- **Endings -ing:** jumping, singing, reading, playing, looking;
  drop-e: making, hoping, riding, smiling, baking, shaking
- **Two-syllable words:** sunset, lemon, tiger, robot, basket,
  pencil, napkin, cabin, seven, melon, rocket, pocket, dinner,
  winter, picnic, muffin
- **Heart words (taught whole; see Lesson 06):** said, was, of, have,
  some, come, do, you, are, they, what, want (extension: from, there,
  one, again)
- **Taught whole words (used in decodable lines and the story):**
  the, a, is, in, on, I, to, we, and, that, this, when, go, goes,
  have (reviewed from Units 01–02; *goes* taught with the story)
