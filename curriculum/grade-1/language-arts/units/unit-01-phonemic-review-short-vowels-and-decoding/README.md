# Unit 01 — Phonemic Review, Short Vowels, and Decoding

**Grade:** 1 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #12](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/12), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Before children can decode print on their own, they must hear the sounds
inside words, stretch them apart, and push them back together — then connect
each sound to its letter and read one-syllable short-vowel words left to
right. In this unit the learner blends and segments spoken words (including
words with consonant blends), tells long from short vowel sounds by ear,
names the first, middle, and last sounds in spoken words, decodes regularly
spelled CVC words by sounding out, builds word families with letter tiles,
and marks the features that make a sentence a sentence (first word, capital
letter, ending punctuation).

## Essential question

How do the sounds inside a spoken word connect to the letters on the page,
so we can read new words we have never seen before?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (1–2) and the Kindergarten track:

- Talks in multi-word sentences; follows two-step oral directions
- Knows uppercase and lowercase letter names and the common sounds of
  consonants and short vowels
- Can rhyme, clap syllables, and blend simple CVC words with support
  (the K track's end-of-year objectives — this unit re-teaches, not assumes)

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed; oral,
pointing, and drawing responses accepted everywhere; short written practice
of 4–6 tasks is reviewed by the adult the same day):

1. **Blend and segment:** push separate sounds together to say a spoken
   single-syllable word (including words that begin with consonant
   blends), and stretch a spoken word into its full sequence of separate
   sounds.
2. **Hear vowels:** tell long from short vowel sounds by ear in spoken
   single-syllable words (e.g., *cap/cape*, *pin/pine* differ in the vowel
   sound, not just the letters).
3. **Isolate sounds:** name the first, middle (vowel), and last sound in a
   spoken single-syllable word.
4. **Decode:** read regularly spelled one-syllable short-vowel (CVC)
   words by saying each sound left to right and blending — including new
   words never seen before.
5. **Mark sentences:** point out the first word, the capital letter, and
   the ending punctuation that distinguish a sentence in print.

## Vocabulary (taught with mouths, tiles, and pictures first, word second)

sound · blend (push sounds together) · segment (stretch a word into
sounds) · stretch · first sound · middle sound · last sound · vowel
(a, e, i, o, u) · consonant · short vowel · long vowel · decode (sound it
out) · word family · sentence · capital letter · period · question mark ·
exclamation point

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials
list): letter tiles or magnetic letters (lowercase), blank word cards and a
marker, a small mirror (mouth shapes for sounds), sand tray or whiteboard,
notebook and pencil, drawing paper and crayons, the generated illustration
[`assets/sound-detective.png`](assets/sound-detective.png), decodable word
cards from the unit word lists below. No sharp tools; adult supervises
small parts in shared settings.

### Unit word sets (authored for this unit; reused across lessons and practice)

- **Short-a:** cat, sat, map, bag, jam, cap, mat, ran, pan, tap, hat, van
- **Short-i:** pig, big, sit, pin, win, dig, fin, mix, bit, lid, six, fix
- **Short-o:** hot, pot, top, box, fox, log, mop, dog, hop, pop
- **Short-u:** sun, run, cup, bus, mud, tub, bug, rug, hug, pup
- **Short-e:** bed, red, ten, pen, men, leg, pet, get, wet, net
- **Blend starters (oral only, this unit):** stop, frog, clap, grin, sled, drum
- **Long/short pairs (by ear):** cap/cape, pin/pine, hop/hope, cub/cube, pet/Pete
- **Taught high-frequency words in decodable lines:** the, a, is, in, on,
  I, to, we (learned as whole words; everything else in the lines is
  decodable with this unit's skills)

week: games, re-teaching, the formative check, and the culminating

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, or drawing responses; the adult scribes
dictated writing. Color is never the only cue (vowel cards carry a distinct
shape and the vowel letter itself; sound dots are numbered 1-2-3 as well
as colored). Each lesson includes a seated-table and a floor-play variant,
a language-access support, an alternate response mode, and an extension.
The generated illustration ships with a text-only alternative naming all
three pictured objects. Hearing support: face the learner when modeling
sounds; mirror work and visual mouth-shape cues supplement auditory
discrimination.
