# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Phonemic Review, Short Vowels, and Decoding

Adult-facing. Learner materials never link here with answers visible; keep
this file and [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) out of the learner's reach.

## How this unit runs

Four 20–25-minute sessions per week. Every session opens with the same
30-second ritual — "reading hands ready?" (tiles or pointer in hands,
eyes on the words) — and closes with one sentence of specific praise
tied to the objective ("You caught every middle sound today"). Short,
warm, and repetitive beats long and novel at this age.

**Pacing judgment:** the [16-session pacing](README.md#16-session-pacing-4-weeks--4-sessions-20-25-minutes-each)
alternates lesson and practice days. If a lesson runs long, move its
application to the next practice session rather than rushing the exit
check — the exit check is your data.

**Sound-first rule:** Lessons 02–04 are ear work. Cover or remove print
whenever the task is about sounds (vowel sorts, detective questions),
and bring print back for Lessons 05–06. Children who peek at spelling
during ear tasks learn to guess from letters instead of listening.

## Session-by-session guidance

- **Sessions 1–2 (sentences):** chant the check ("first word, capital
  letter, ending mark") until the learner says it unprompted. The three
  ending marks need daily naming for a week.
- **Sessions 3–4 (blend/segment):** model the pure sounds — no "buh" for
  /b/. If blending stalls, drop back to onset-rime ("s...at") for a day,
  then return to full phoneme blending.
- **Sessions 5–6 (vowels):** keep print covered. If the learner keeps
  answering from spelling, switch to nonsense-free minimal pairs you say
  aloud only.
- **Sessions 7–8 (isolation):** stretch the middle vowel extra long.
  Three sound dots beat fingers for children who lose count.
- **Sessions 9–10 (decoding):** enforce all 4 steps every time for two
  weeks, then let the check become silent and quick. Never skip it
  entirely — it is the self-correction habit.
- **Sessions 11–12 (families + project):** one letter per rung is the
  law of the ladder. Reject non-words kindly and immediately.
- **Session 13 (dictation):** invented spelling is the goal — circle
  what the learner heard correctly before naming what was missed.
- **Session 14 (project share):** the teach-back matters more than the
  book's neatness. If the learner can explain the chunk idea, the unit
  worked.
- **Sessions 15–16 (check + assess):** quiz in the morning slot, project
  share after a break. Never quiz a tired first-grader and trust the
  result.

## Misconceptions — quick reference

| You see | It usually means | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| "buh-ig" for "big" | Schwa puff on consonants | Mirror work: crisp /b/, lips barely move; praise the clean version |
| "sop" for "stop" | Blend collapsing | Slow the blend: "s-t, s-t, stop", then speed up |
| Guesses from first letter | Skipping the middle | "Detectives read every clue" — require all sounds before the word |
| "tap" read as "pat" | Right-to-left reading | Arrow strip; always point at the first letter first |
| Calls /ā/ "wrong" | Over-learned short vowels | Celebrate: "You found the other team!" (Lesson 03) |
| Peeks at spelling during ear tasks | Letter-guessing habit | Cover all print; ears only, every time |
| Names the letter, not the sound | Letter-name dominance | Accept, then reshape: "The letter is C; the sound is /k/" |
| "zat" on the ladder | Pattern without meaning | "Is it a real word? Ladders climb on real words." |
| Counts letters, not sounds | Print/sound confusion | Accept either count this unit; the point is hearing every sound |

## Re-teach triggers

Use these after Session 13 observations and the Session 15 quiz:

- **Blending below 3 of 4:** repeat
  [Session A](practice.md#session-a-blending-ladder-relay) with 3-sound
  words only; add the floor-hopscotch variant from Lesson 02.
- **Long/short confusion persists:** repeat
  [Session C](practice.md#session-c-vowel-sort-game) with mouth covered
  every round; keep the five short-vowel anchor words (apple, egg,
  igloo, octopus, umbrella) visible.
- **Middle sound missed more than half the time:** repeat
  [Session D](practice.md#session-d-sound-dots) with stretched middles
  ("c-ăăă-t"); use the mirror.
- **Decoding without the check step:** re-run
  [Session E](practice.md#session-e-tile-building-and-decodable-lines)
  with the referee announcement required after every word.
- **Word-family pattern not verbalized:** rebuild two families side by
  side with tiles; ask "what never moved?" until the learner names the
  chunk unprompted.
- **Sentence marks forgotten:** re-run the Session 1 chant at the start
  of every session for a week; find sentences in a picture book daily.

## Readiness criteria — moving to Unit 02

The learner is ready when the unit assessment totals 15+, **or** totals
11–14 with the re-teach above completed. Below 11: use a flexible week
(the track provides diagnostic/catch-up weeks) to re-teach the matching
lessons — Unit 02's consonant blends and digraphs in print build directly
on secure blending, segmenting, and CVC decoding, so do not skip the
re-teach.

## Accessibility notes for the adult

- Face the learner when modeling speech; mouth shapes in the mirror
  support the auditory discrimination this whole unit depends on.
- Large-print, high-contrast word and letter cards; textured letters for
  low-vision learners.
- Every check accepts pointing/gesture/drawing; scribe dictated writing.
- Short written practice (4–6 tasks) is reviewed the same day — never
  assigned as unsupervised homework at this age.
- Movement breaks between sessions, not within — a 20-minute session
  should not need one, but watch the learner, not the clock.
- Home-language labels for vocabulary (first/middle/last, blend/stretch)
  are welcome alongside English; note them in your records for
  consistency.
