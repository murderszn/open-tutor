# Project — Word-Family Flip Book

**Type:** investigation / creation project across Sessions 12 and 14,
with the share in Session 14.
**Driving question:** "Can I build a book that proves I can read new
words by changing just one letter?"

The learner authors a flip book of 3–4 word families (4–5 words each),
illustrates one word per family, and proves the decoding works by reading
every page aloud with the 4 decoding steps — then teaches one family to
someone else.

## Materials

8–10 strips of sturdy paper or index cards (one per word), 2 binder rings
or yarn (adult use), crayons/markers, letter tiles for drafting, the
unit word sets (see [unit README](README.md#unit-word-sets-authored-for-this-unit-reused-across-lessons-and-practice)).

## Part 1 — Plan and draft (Session 12)

1. Choose 3–4 word families from different vowel teams, e.g. **-at,
   -in, -ot, -ug** (at least two different vowels — no all--at book).
   The adult writes each family chunk on a planning strip.
2. For each family, build 4–5 real words with tiles by changing only the
   first letter: -at → cat, sat, mat, hat, bat; -in → pin, win, fin;
   -ot → hot, pot, not, cot; -ug → bug, rug, hug.
3. Draft-check each word: read it with the 4 decoding steps
   (point–say–blend–check). Any non-word ("zat") is rejected — the book
   only climbs on real words.
4. Draw one quick picture per family showing one of its words (a cat for
   -at, a pin for -in). Label the picture with the word, adult scribes
   if needed.

## Part 2 — Assemble, rehearse, share (Session 14)

1. Stack each family's cards with the chunk card on top; bind with rings
   or yarn so pages flip. Make a cover: title ("My Word Families"),
   author name (first name only — privacy), one drawing.
2. Rehearsal: the learner reads every page to a stuffed animal —
   pointing under each word, sounding out left to right, blending, and
   announcing the check ("real word!").
3. **Teach-back:** the learner teaches one family to the adult (or a
   family member): "The chunk is -ot. Watch — hot, pot, not. Only the
   first letter changes!" The audience asks one question ("How did you
   read a word you'd never seen?"), which the learner answers.

## Deliverables

1. The flip book: cover + 3–4 family sections, 4–5 real decodable words
   each, at least 2 different vowel teams, one illustration per family.
2. An observed fluent-enough reading of every page (4 decoding steps;
   self-corrections welcome).
3. The teach-back: one family explained with the "only the first letter
   changes" idea, plus one answered audience question.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Getting started (1) | Developing (2) | Secure (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Word choice** | Some non-words or repeated words; one vowel team | All real words; 2+ vowel teams; a few repeats | 3–4 families, 4–5 real words each, 2+ vowel teams, no repeats |
| **Decoding** | Reads some words by guessing; skips steps | Uses the 4 steps with reminders; self-corrects some | Point–say–blend–check on every page, independently |
| **Family understanding** | Cannot say what stays the same | Says "the end is the same" with prompting | Explains unprompted: "only the first letter changes; the chunk stays" |
| **Teach-back** | Reads the family but cannot explain it | Explains with adult questions guiding | Teaches the family and answers the audience question clearly |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = secure — ready for Unit 02. 7–9 = developing — note
the lowest criterion and re-teach it in Session 15 before moving on.
Below 7 = re-teach the matching lessons during a flexible week; the
track's diagnostic/catch-up weeks exist for this.

## Adult notes

- Reject non-words kindly: "Great pattern — but is 'zat' a real word?
  The book only climbs on real words. What real word fits?"
- If the learner's handwriting is still forming, dictated labels scribed
  by the adult fully count — the project tests decoding, not penmanship.
- Keep the flip book: Unit 02 reuses it when blends and digraphs join
  the families (e.g., -at → "chat"? no — that's Unit 02's lesson to
  discover).
