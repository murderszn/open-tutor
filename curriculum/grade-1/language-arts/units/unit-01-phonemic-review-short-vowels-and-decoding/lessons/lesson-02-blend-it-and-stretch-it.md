# Lesson 02 — Blend It and Stretch It

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can push sounds together to make a word, and
stretch a word apart into its sounds — even words that start with blends."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** none required; optional: 3 sound
dots or chips per word, a small mirror.
**Standards:** RF.1.2, RF.1.2.b, RF.1.2.d.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

"I'm going to say some sounds slowly, like a robot: /m/ ... /ă/ ... /p/.
Can you say it fast, the smooth way?" If the learner says "map", blending
is online. If not, echo each sound back with them once — no correction
lecture.

## Explicit teaching

Words are made of sounds, and we can play with those sounds two ways:

- **Blend** means push the sounds together, smooth and fast, until they
  become a word. We *blend* when we read: /s/ /ŭ/ /n/ → "sun".
- **Segment** means stretch a word apart into its separate sounds, like
  pulling taffy. We *segment* when we spell or check sounds: "sun" →
  /s/ /ŭ/ /n/.

Teach the two hand moves: **blending** = two fists start apart and slide
together ("push!"); **segmenting** = one fist opens finger by finger, one
sound per finger ("stretch!").

Some words begin with a **blend** — two consonant sounds that stick
together at the start, like /s/ /t/ in "stop" or /f/ /r/ in "frog". We
still say every sound; we just say the two beginning sounds quickly, like
best friends holding hands. "Stop" has four sounds: /s/ /t/ /ŏ/ /p/.

### Modeled example 1 — blending, with and without a blend

*Adult uses the push-hands move, pausing between sounds, then says the
word fast:*
"/b/ ... /ĭ/ ... /g/. Push — 'big'." *(Fists slide together.)*
"Now a trickier one with a blend: /s/ /t/ ... /ŏ/ ... /p/. The /s/ and
/t/ hold hands: 'st'. Push — 'stop'." *(Adult counts the sounds on four
fingers: s-t-o-p.)*
**Say it back to me:** "Your turn to be the robot: give me the sounds
for 'pin' and I will push them together." (Adult supplies /p/ /ĭ/ /n/
only if the learner stalls.)

### Modeled example 2 — segmenting with finger counting

*Adult holds up a fist:* "'Frog.' Now watch my fingers stretch it out."
*/f/ (thumb) /r/ (pointer — "the blend, two sounds") /ŏ/ (middle) /g/
(ring).* "Four sounds: /f/ /r/ /ŏ/ /g/."
*Adult does "mud":* "/m/ /ŭ/ /d/ — three sounds. My turn is done. Now
you stretch 'cup' and count the sounds on your fingers."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Blend: "/r/ /ŭ/ /n/." → "run" (push-hands together).
2. Blend with a blend: "/k/ /l/ ... /ă/ /p/." → "clap".
3. Segment: "grin" → /g/ /r/ /ĭ/ /n/ — count 4 sounds on fingers.
4. Segment: "bed" → /b/ /ĕ/ /d/ — 3 sounds.
5. Coach's choice: the adult blends a word wrong on purpose ("/h/ /ŏ/
/t/ → 'top'"... said slowly as "huh-aw-tuh" with extra vowel puffs);
   the learner catches it: "Say it smooth, no extra uh!" and re-blends.

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

1. Blend: /d/ /ĭ/ /g/ → "dig".
2. Blend with a blend: /s/ /l/ ... /ĕ/ /d/ → "sled".
3. Segment "fox" → /f/ /ŏ/ /ks/ (accept /f/ /ŏ/ /k/ /s/ or the /ks/
   together — note which the learner hears).
4. Segment "drum" → /d/ /r/ /ŭ/ /m/ — 4 sounds.
5. **Error-analysis:** the adult segments "hug" as "/h/ /ŭ/" (leaving off
   /g/). The learner fixes it: "You forgot /g/! /h/ /ŭ/ /g/ — three
   sounds."

## Application

"Robot telephone." The learner whispers a segmented word to a stuffed
animal (or the adult turns away), the listener blends it aloud, and the
learner confirms: "Yes! /t/ /ŏ/ /p/ is 'top'!" Swap roles for two rounds.
The learner must both segment (as speaker) and blend (as listener).

## Exit check

1. Blend: "/w/ /ĭ/ /n/." → ?
2. Blend with a blend: "/g/ /r/ ... /ĭ/ /n/." → ?
3. Segment "map": say all the sounds and count them.
4. Segment "stop": say all the sounds and count them.

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** do the first blends with the learner's name and
  family words ("stretch your name — how many sounds?") before word-list
  words.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may answer with the hand moves
  (push/stretch) plus pointing to sound dots instead of speaking every
  sound.
- **Seated-table variant:** sound dots or chips in a row — touch one dot
  per sound.
- **Floor-play variant:** "sound hopscotch" — one hop per sound, then a
  big jump that lands on the blended word.
- **Extension:** segment 4- and 5-sound words ("plant": /p/ /l/ /ă/ /n/
/t/; "first": /f/ /ĭ/ /r/ /s/ /t/) and blend them back.

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *The "schwa puff":* children say /b/ as "buh", turning "big" into
  "buh-ig". Model the pure sound with the mirror — lips barely move for
  /b/ — and praise the crisp version.
- *Blends collapse:* "stop" becomes "sop" (the /t/ vanishes). Slow the
  blend down: "s-t, s-t, stop" — then speed up.
- *Segmenting by syllable instead of sound:* "sunset" is two syllables
  but this unit works one syllable at a time. Keep every word one
  syllable; save "sunset" for Unit 03.
- *Counting letters, not sounds:* "fox" has 3 letters but 4 sounds if you
  split /ks/ — accept either count this unit; the point is hearing all
  the sound, not the letter count.
