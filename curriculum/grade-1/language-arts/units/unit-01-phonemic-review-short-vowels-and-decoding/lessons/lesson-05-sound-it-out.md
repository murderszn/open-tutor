# Lesson 05 — Sound It Out: Decoding CVC Words

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can read a new word I have never seen by
saying each sound left to right and blending them together."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** letter tiles or magnetic letters
(lowercase); 8 word cards from the unit word sets (e.g., cat, pig, hot,
sun, bed, map, dig, cup).
**Standards:** RF.1.2.b, RF.1.3, RF.1.3.b.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Lay out tiles c-a-t (mixed order: t-a-c). "These letters are in the
wrong order. Build 'cat' the right way." Watch: can the learner order
left to right and name each sound? That is today's launch pad.

## Explicit teaching

**Decoding** means reading a word by turning its letters back into
sounds. The detective work (Lessons 02–04) was with your ears; now your
eyes join in. The steps never change:

1. **Point** under the first letter (left side).
2. **Say** each sound, left to right, without stopping: /k/ /ă/ /t/.
3. **Blend** them smooth and fast: "cat!"
4. **Check:** does it sound like a real word you know? If yes — you
   read it! If no, try again slower.

Two reading rules: **every letter gets a sound** (no skipping the middle
— the vowel is the engine), and **go left to right, never backwards**.
Short-vowel words are the friendliest kind: one vowel, one syllable,
each letter doing its usual job — CVC words like *cat, pig, hot, sun,
bed*.

### Modeled example 1 — "map" (think-aloud)

*Adult builds m-a-p with tiles, pointing under each letter:*
"New word — I have never read this one. Point at the first letter: m.
Say the sounds left to right: /m/ ... /ă/ ... /p/." *(Finger slides
under the tiles.)* "Now blend it smooth: 'map'! Check: is 'map' a real
word? Yes — like the detective's treasure map! I read it."
*Adult scrambles the tiles:* "Watch me mess up on purpose: /m/ /p/ /ă/
— 'mpa'? That's not a word. My check failed, so I go back: left to
right, /m/ /ă/ /p/ — 'map'. The check is part of reading, not extra."

### Modeled example 2 — "fix" and "hug" (transfer to new words)

*Adult shows the card "fix" (a word not yet practiced):* "Never seen it.
First letter f: /f/. Next: /ĭ/. Last: /ks/ — x says /ks/ at the end.
Blend: 'fix'! Check: to fix a broken toy — real word. I read a brand-new
word."
*Adult shows "hug":* "/h/ /ŭ/ /g/ — 'hug'! Check: a hug is real. Two new
words, same steps. The steps work on ANY short-vowel word."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Build s-a-t with tiles. "Point, say the sounds, blend." ("sat")
2. Card "pen": read it with the 4 steps; say the check aloud ("a pen
   writes — real word").
3. Card "mop": read; then the adult asks "what was the middle sound?"
   (/ŏ/ — "the vowel engine").
4. **Fix my reading:** the adult reads "lid" as "/l/ /ĭ/ ... 'li'?"
   (stops early). The learner corrects: "You skipped the last letter!
   /l/ /ĭ/ /d/ — 'lid'!"
5. Build b-i-g, then swap the first tile to d: "Read the new word."
   ("dig" — same steps, new first sound.)

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

Write or lay out these 5 never-drilled-together words (all from the unit
word sets): **van, win, rob, nut, get**.

1. Read "van" with the 4 steps (point-say-blend-check).
2. Read "win".
3. Read "rob".
4. Read "nut".
5. Read "get".
6. **Explain-your-reasoning (bonus):** "Which step helps you most when
   the word is brand new? Why?" (Any defended answer; listen for
   "saying each sound" or "the check".)

## Application

"Word hospital." Five "sick" words are written with one wrong letter
each (adult-prepared): *cag* (should be *cat*), *pog* (should be *pig*),
*hut* (should be *hot*), *run* written as *ran*? No — keep it clean:
sick words: **cag, pog, sut, beg→bed?** Use: cag→cat, pog→pig,
sut→sun, beg→bed, hop→hot. The learner diagnoses each ("this says
'cag' — not a word!"), prescribes the one-letter fix, rebuilds with
tiles, and reads the healed word with the 4 steps.

## Exit check

1. Read "jam" (new card): point, sounds, blend, check.
2. Read "mix".
3. "What are the 4 decoding steps?" (point, say each sound left to
   right, blend, check it's a real word)
4. "Why can't we skip the middle letter?" (The vowel is the engine —
   without it you can't blend the word.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** pre-teach the meaning of each new word with a
  picture or object before decoding it ("a lid covers a pot — now read
  'lid'").
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may build every word with
  tiles and blend aloud without writing; pointing counts as the
  "point" step.
- **Seated-table variant:** tiles in a tray; finger slides on a printed
  arrow strip (left-to-right arrow under each word).
- **Floor-play variant:** giant floor letters — the learner steps on
  each letter saying its sound, then jumps and shouts the blended word.
- **Extension:** decode 4-letter short-vowel words with final blends
  ("fast": /f/ /ă/ /s/ /t/; "jump": /j/ /ŭ/ /m/ /p/) — every letter
  still gets a sound.

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Guessing from the first letter:* "s... snake? sun? sat?" — insist on
  all three sounds before the word: "detectives read every clue."
- *Reading right to left:* "tap" becomes "pat". The arrow strip and the
  point-first habit fix this; praise every left-start.
- *The blend at the end:* "mix" → "mik" (dropping /s/). Remind: x says
  /ks/ — two sounds hiding in one letter.
- *No check step:* children who skip the check "read" non-words happily.
  Make the check a celebration: "Real word? Then YOU read it!"
