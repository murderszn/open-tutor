# Lesson 03 — Long Vowel or Short Vowel?

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can hear whether the vowel sound in a word is
long (it says its name) or short (a quick sound) — with my ears, not my
eyes."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** a small mirror; picture/word
cards for: cap, cape, pin, pine, hop, hope, cub, cube, pet, Pete.
**Standards:** RF.1.2, RF.1.2.a.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

"Say the alphabet's vowels with me: a, e, i, o, u." Now: "Say 'cat'
slowly. What sound is in the middle?" (/ă/.) "Say 'cake' slowly. What
sound is in the middle?" (/ā/.) Note whether the learner hears the
difference — today is all about ears.

## Explicit teaching

Every syllable has a **vowel sound** — the open-mouth sound in the middle
of the word. Vowel sounds come in two teams:

- A **long vowel** says its own **name**: the /ā/ in "cake" sounds like
  the letter A; the /ī/ in "pine" sounds like the letter I.
- A **Short vowel** is a quick, different sound: the /ă/ in "cap" (like a
  cat's meow chopped short: "ă"), the /ĭ/ in "pin" (a quick "ih").

The trick: **listen, don't look**. The spelling comes later (Units 02–03);
today your ears decide. Say the word slowly, find the middle sound, and
ask: "Does it say the letter's name? Then it's long. Is it the quick
sound? Then it's short."

The five short vowels to know cold: /ă/ as in **a**pple, /ĕ/ as in
**e**gg, /ĭ/ as in **i**gloo, /ŏ/ as in **o**ctopus, /ŭ/ as in **u**mbrella.

### Modeled example 1 — cap vs. cape (listening, no letters shown)

*Adult says both words slowly, exaggerating the middle sound, and thinks
aloud:*
"Cap — c-ă-p. The middle sound is /ă/, the quick sound. Does it say the
letter A's name? No. So **cap has a short a**."
"Cape — c-ā-p. The middle sound is /ā/ — that IS the letter A's name!
So **cape has a long a**."
*Adult covers her mouth:* "Now you can't see my mouth — only your ears
get to vote. Cap... cape. Which one says A's name?" *(Waits for the
learner.)*

### Modeled example 2 — pin vs. pine, and a same-vowel pair

*Adult:* "Pin — p-ĭ-n. Quick /ĭ/. Short i. Pine — p-ī-n. It says I's name.
Long i."
"Now a harder one: 'hop' and 'hot'. Say them slowly. Hop — /ŏ/. Hot —
/ŏ/. Same team! Both short o. Not every pair is different — sometimes the
answer is 'same'."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. "Cub... cube." Long or short u? (cub = short /ŭ/; cube = long /ū/
   — it says U's name.)
2. "Pet... Pete." Long or short e? (pet = short /ĕ/; Pete = long /ē/.)
3. "Sit... sun." Same or different vowel teams? (Same — both short: /ĭ/,
   /ŭ/ are both short vowels. Accept "both short".)
4. Mirror check: say /ā/ and /ă/ watching the mouth. "Which one makes
   your mouth open wider and longer?" (/ā/.)
5. The adult says a word with the WRONG label on purpose ("'man' —
   that's a long a!"). The learner corrects: "No! /ă/ is short — it
   doesn't say A's name."

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 5 tasks)

1. "Fin." Long or short? (short /ĭ/)
2. "Hope." Long or short? (long /ō/ — says O's name)
3. "Red... run." Same team or different? (same — both short)
4. "Tube." Long or short? (long /ū/)
5. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "Is the vowel in 'box' long or short?
   How do you know?" (Short /ŏ/ — because it does NOT say the letter
   O's name; it's the quick sound.)

## Application

"Vowel umpire." The learner is the umpire: the adult (or a stuffed animal
"player") says five words slowly, and the learner calls "LONG!" (arms up,
big stretch) or "SHORT!" (arms in, quick chop) for each, then names the
middle sound: "Long — /ī/!" Words: *made, bit, note, cut, these*.
(Answers: made /ā/ long; bit /ĭ/ short; note /ō/ long; cut /ŭ/ short;
these /ē/ long.)

## Exit check

1. "Cap." Long or short a? (short)
2. "Pine." Long or short i? (long)
3. "Pet... Pete." Which one says E's name? (Pete)
4. "How do you tell a long vowel from a short vowel?" (The long vowel
   says the letter's name; the short vowel is the quick sound — ears,
   not eyes.)

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** anchor each short vowel to a body cue first
  ("ă — like you're at the doctor saying 'ah' quickly") before the
  word cards.
- **Alternate response mode:** LONG/SHORT hand signals (arms up / arms
  in) accepted for every check; the adult voices the reasoning.
- **Seated-table variant:** sort word cards into two labeled trays
  (LONG TEAM / SHORT TEAM).
- **Floor-play variant:** two floor hoops labeled with a big ear icon —
  hop into LONG or SHORT as the adult calls words.
- **Extension:** find the vowel team in two-syllable words heard aloud
  ("sunset" — /ŭ/ short, /ĕ/ short); predict which unit will teach the
  spellings (Unit 03).

## Teacher note — likely misconceptions

- *Reading the letters instead of listening:* children peek at spelling
  ("cape has an e, so it's long"). Cover all print during the sort —
  ears only, every time.
- *Every "a" sound is short:* after lots of CVC practice, children call
  /ā/ "wrong". Celebrate the discovery: "You found the other team!"
- *Confusing long with "loud":* long means "says its name", not "said
  loudly". The umpire arms (big stretch vs. quick chop) keep the meaning
  physical.
- *The letter name IS the sound:* for /ē/ vs. letter E, say both: "the
  letter is E; the long sound says 'ee' — its name."
