# Unit 04 — Fluency, Retelling, Characters, and Key Details

**Grade:** 1 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #12](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/12), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

In Units 01–03 the learner learned to sound words out. Now the
learner learns to sound like a reader: reading the same short story
again and again until it flows, pausing at the right places, making
a sad voice sad and a happy voice happy — and then telling the
story back in their own words, with the characters, the place, the
big events, and the lesson it teaches. Three short original stories
carry the whole unit: Milo and his dog Pip at the pond, Ana and a
lost mitten in the snow, and a slow race between a hare and a
turtle. The unit ends with the learner's own Retelling Storybook
and a read-aloud performance for the adult.

## Essential question

How do I read a story smoothly — and then tell it back so someone
who never heard it knows what happened, who it happened to, and
what it teaches?

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–03 and the diagnostic weeks (1–2):

- Decodes regularly spelled one-syllable words with short vowels,
  blends, digraphs, final-e, and common vowel teams ai, ay, ee, ea,
  oa, ow; reads two-syllable words by breaking them into syllables
  and words with endings -s, -es, -ed, -ing (RF.1.3.b–f)
- Recognizes the unit heart words by sight (RF.1.3.g)
- Retells a familiar story orally from Units 01–03 read-alouds
  (characters, setting, events, in rough order)
- Marks sentence features (first word, capital, ending punctuation)
- Short independent written practice (4–6 tasks) reviewed by the
  adult the same day

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed;
oral, pointing, and drawing responses accepted everywhere; short
written practice of 4–6 tasks is reviewed by the adult the same day):

1. **Read fluently:** read a grade-level story orally with accuracy,
   appropriate rate, and expression on successive readings, and use
   context to confirm or self-correct word recognition, rereading
   as necessary.
2. **Ask and answer key-detail questions:** ask and answer who,
   what, where, when, and how questions about key details in a
   story read aloud or read by the learner.
3. **Retell with key details:** retell a story in order —
   beginning, middle, end — including key details, and state its
   central message or lesson.
4. **Describe story parts:** describe characters, settings, and
   major events in a story, using key details from the text.
5. **Use words and pictures as evidence:** identify words and
   phrases that suggest feelings or appeal to the senses; identify
   who is telling the story; use illustrations and details to
   describe characters, settings, and events.
6. **Compare and classify:** compare and contrast the adventures
   and experiences of characters in two stories, and explain the
   difference between a book that tells a story and a book that
   gives information.

## Unit stories (original, written for this unit)

All three stories use Units 01–03 decoding patterns plus the small
taught-word list below. Read them aloud first; the learner rereads
them across the unit. "The Slow Race" is an original retelling of
the public-domain Tortoise and Hare tale, not a copy of any
published version.

**Taught whole words (used in the stories):** the, a, is, in, on,
to, of, and, go, goes, says, said, they, are, was, you, have,
good, happy, sad, look, looks, little, under, over, one, cold, soft.

### Story 1 — Milo and the Red Boat

Milo has a red boat. Pip is his dog. Milo and Pip go to the pond.
Milo puts the boat in the water. The wind blows. The boat sails
fast. "Go, boat, go!" says Milo. The wind blows hard. The boat
tips over! Splash! Milo is sad. Pip jumps in. Pip gets the boat.
Pip brings it back. Milo hugs Pip. "Good dog, Pip!" says Milo.
They go home happy.

### Story 2 — Ana and the Lost Mitten

Ana has a soft red mitten. One cold day, Ana drops her mitten in
the snow. She looks in the snow. She looks by the big tree. She
looks on the path. She can not find it. Ana is sad. Then she sees
a little cat. The cat is cold. Ana picks up the cat. The cat sits
on something soft. Ana lifts the cat. There is the mitten! Ana
puts on the mitten. The cat is warm in Ana's arms. They go home
happy.

### Story 3 — The Slow Race

Hare runs fast. Turtle walks slow. "I am fast! You are slow!"
says Hare. "Let us race," says Turtle. Hare runs far ahead. Hare
naps under a tree. Turtle walks on. Step, step, step. Turtle
passes Hare. Turtle wins the race! Hare wakes up. "Slow and
steady wins the race," says Turtle.

## 16-session pacing (4 weeks × 4 sessions, 20–25 minutes each)

Sessions rotate as: modeled fluent-reading lesson → partner/echo
rereading practice → retelling and story-talk practice → review.
The project runs across Sessions 12 and 14, with the read-aloud
share in Session 14. Week 18 (track week 18) is the midyear review
week: cumulative decoding, fluency, and retelling; re-teach of the
highest-need objective; formative check of Units 01–04.
