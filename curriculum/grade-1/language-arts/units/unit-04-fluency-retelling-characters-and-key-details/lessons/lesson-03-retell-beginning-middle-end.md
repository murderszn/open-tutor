# Lesson 03 — Retell Beginning, Middle, End

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can tell a story back in order —
first what happens at the beginning, then the middle, then the
end — using the important details."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** "Milo and the Red Boat"
(unit README), retelling rope (paper strip with Beginning, Middle,
End boxes).
**Standards:** RL.1.2 (retell with key details); RL.1.3 (major
events in order).

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Adult: "Tell me what you did this morning — first, then, last."
If the learner gives three things in order, they have the shape
of a retelling. If not, model your own morning in three parts
first.

## Explicit teaching

**Retell** means telling a story back in your own words after you
hear or read it. A good retelling has three parts, in order:

- **Beginning** — who the story is about, and where it starts.
  ("Milo and Pip go to the pond.")
- **Middle** — the big thing that happens, the problem.
  ("The wind blows hard. The boat tips over!")
- **End** — how it gets fixed, and how everyone feels.
  ("Pip brings the boat back. They go home happy.")

A retelling is not every word of the story. It is the key details
in order — enough that someone who never heard the story knows
what happened. If you skip the middle, the listener is lost: "Milo
goes to the pond... they go home happy." Wait — what happened in
between? The boat tipped over! That is why order matters.

### Modeled example 1 — Adult retells the Milo story (short version)

*Adult retells, touching each box on the rope:* "Beginning: Milo
and his dog Pip go to the pond, and Milo puts his red boat in the
water. Middle: The wind blows hard and the boat tips over — splash!
Milo is sad. End: Pip jumps in, gets the boat, and brings it back.
Milo hugs Pip, and they go home happy." "Three parts, in order,
key details only."

### Modeled example 2 — A mixed-up retelling, fixed

*Adult retells out of order:* "Pip brings the boat back. Milo
goes to the pond. The boat tips over." "Did that make sense? No!
The boat cannot come back before it tips over. Let me fix the
order: beginning — Milo goes to the pond; middle — the boat tips
over; end — Pip brings it back. Order is part of the retelling."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Learner names the beginning of the Milo story (one sentence).
   (*Milo and Pip go to the pond / Milo puts the boat in.*)
2. Learner names the middle (the problem). (*Wind blows; boat
   tips over; Milo is sad.*)
3. Learner names the end (the fix and feeling). (*Pip brings the
   boat back; they go home happy.*)
4. Adult gives three sentence cards out of order (boat tips over
   / Milo goes to pond / Pip brings boat back); learner puts
   them in order on the rope.
5. Learner retells the whole story in three sentences, one per
   rope box, with adult prompting only ("And then...?").

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Learner retells the Milo story to a stuffed animal or the
   adult, using the rope boxes, without looking at the story.
2. Learner draws one picture for each rope box (beginning,
   middle, end) and tells what is in each picture.
3. Learner answers: "What happens if you leave out the middle?"
   (orally or dictated: *the listener does not know the problem*).
4. Learner listens to the adult retell with one key detail
   missing (no Pip!) and names what was left out.

## Application

**Retelling rope build:** the learner makes their own rope for
the Milo story: three boxes, one quick drawing in each, and one
dictated or written sentence under each drawing (adult scribes if
needed). The rope becomes the project model for Session 12.

## Exit check

Learner retells the Milo story in order, unprompted except for
the rope boxes. Success: all three parts present, in order, each
with at least one key detail (pond/boat; wind/tip over; Pip
brings it back/happy).

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** rope boxes on the table; learner
  moves a token from box to box while retelling.
- **Floor-play variant:** three hoops or paper circles on the
  floor labeled Beginning, Middle, End; learner stands in each
  while telling that part.
- **Language access:** adult supplies sentence starters: "First...,
  Then..., At the end..."
- **Alternate response mode:** learner retells by ordering three
  drawings, then pointing through them, instead of speaking
  sentences.
- **Extension:** learner retells with two details per part, and
  adds how Milo feels in each part.

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **Retelling every word:** praise short retellings. If the
  learner recites the whole story, say: "Now tell it in just
  three sentences — the most important parts."
- **Ending without the feeling:** the end needs the fix AND the
  feeling ("go home happy"). Ask: "How does Milo feel now?"
- **Middle = everything:** if the middle swallows the story,
  split it: "What is the problem?" (one sentence) vs. "What
  happens because of it?"
