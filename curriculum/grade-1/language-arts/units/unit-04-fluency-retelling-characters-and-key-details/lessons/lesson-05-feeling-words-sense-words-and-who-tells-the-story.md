# Lesson 05 — Feeling Words, Sense Words, and Who Tells the Story

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can find words that tell feelings or
use the senses, I can tell who is telling the story, and I can
use pictures and words together to describe what happens."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** all three unit stories
(unit README); generated illustration
[`../assets/milo-red-boat-pond.png`](../assets/milo-red-boat-pond.png).
**Standards:** RL.1.4; RL.1.6; RL.1.7.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Adult: "Show me a happy face. Show me a sad face." Then: "What
does 'splash' sound like?" If the learner can act a feeling and
hear a sound word, they are ready.

## Explicit teaching

Stories use special words to make you feel and sense things:

- **Feeling words** tell how a character feels: *sad, happy,
  cold, warm*. ("Milo is sad." "They go home happy.")
- **Sense words** appeal to your senses — what you would see,
  hear, touch, or feel: *splash* (hear it!), *soft* (touch it!),
  *cold* (feel it!), *fast* wind (feel it on your face).

**Who tells the story?** Most stories in this unit are told by a
**narrator** — someone outside the story who tells what happens
("Milo has a red boat."). But sometimes a character speaks, and
you see their words in quotation marks: '"Go, boat, go!" says
Milo.' The narrator tells the story; the characters speak inside
it. If the story says "I," the teller is a character in the story
— none of our three stories does that, which is itself a clue.

**Pictures help too.** An illustration can show you the setting
(the pond), a character's feeling (Milo's smile), or an event
(the boat on the water) — and the words confirm it. Use both
together: "The picture shows the wind bending the tree, and the
words say 'The wind blows hard.'"

### Modeled example 1 — Feeling and sense words in two stories

*Adult reads:* "Milo is sad." "Sad is a feeling word — how does
Milo feel? Sad." *Then:* "Splash! — that is a sense word. What
do you hear? A splash! And 'soft red mitten' — soft is touch.
Feel it in your mind: soft." Learner repeats each word with its
sense or feeling.

### Modeled example 2 — Narrator or character?

*Adult reads:* '"Good dog, Pip!" says Milo.' "Who is talking
here? Milo — the words are inside quotation marks, and 'says
Milo' tells us. But who tells the whole story? The narrator —
the voice that says 'Milo has a red boat' without quotation
marks." *Adult points:* "Narrator words: no marks. Character
words: marks, plus 'says.'"

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. "Find a feeling word in the Milo story." (*sad, happy*)
2. "Find a sense word in the Ana story." (*soft, cold, snow*)
3. "Who says 'Slow and steady wins the race'?" (*Turtle — a
   character speaking*)
4. Look at the illustration: "What does the picture tell you
   about the setting?" (*A pond, a tree bending in the wind*)
5. "Does the picture or the words tell you Milo is happy? Both?"
   (Words: "go home happy"; picture: Milo's smile as he launches
   the boat — accept either with evidence.)

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Learner hunts the three stories for two feeling words and one
   sense word, pointing to each (adult lists them).
2. Learner draws a face for "sad" and a face for "happy" and
   matches each to the right story moment.
3. Learner answers: "Who tells the Milo story — Milo or a
   narrator?" (*A narrator; Milo only speaks in quotes*)
4. Learner describes the illustration's setting in one sentence,
   using a detail from the picture and a detail from the words.

## Application

**Picture detective:** the learner studies the generated
illustration and lists three things it shows (character, setting,
event), then finds one story sentence that matches each. The
adult records the matches.

## Exit check

Learner names one feeling word and one sense word from any unit
story, and answers "Who tells the story?" for the Milo story
(*a narrator*). Success: 3/3 with story evidence for the words.

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** feeling-word cards and sense-word
  cards sorted into two piles while the adult reads.
- **Floor-play variant:** learner acts out *splash*, *soft*,
  *cold*, *sad*, *happy* as the adult reads the words.
- **Language access:** adult pre-teaches each word with an
  action before the hunt begins.
- **Alternate response mode:** learner points to the word in the
  story text or the picture detail instead of naming the sense.
- **Extension:** learner finds a word that is BOTH a feeling and
  a sense word in a story ("cold" — the cat is cold, and you feel
  cold) and explains both uses.

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **Every adjective is a feeling word:** *red* tells color, not
  feeling. Ask: "Does it tell how someone feels, or what your
  senses notice?"
- **Quotation marks confusion:** learners often say "Milo" tells
  the whole story because Milo speaks. Re-read a narrator sentence
  with no marks: "Who says THIS part? Nobody in the story — the
  narrator."
- **Picture vs. words disagreement:** if the learner claims the
  picture shows something the words contradict, check both: in
  this unit the illustration matches the story's beginning
  (launching the boat), not its end.
