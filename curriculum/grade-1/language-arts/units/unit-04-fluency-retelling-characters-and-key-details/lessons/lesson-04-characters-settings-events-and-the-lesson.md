# Lesson 04 — Characters, Settings, Events, and the Lesson

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can name who is in a story, where it
happens, the big things that happen — and the lesson the story
teaches."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** "The Slow Race" and "Ana
and the Lost Mitten" (unit README).
**Standards:** RL.1.3; RL.1.2 (central message or lesson).

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Adult: "In the Milo story, who was in it? Where did it happen?"
If the learner names Milo, Pip, and the pond, they are ready. If
not, re-ask Lesson 02's who/where questions first.

## Explicit teaching

Every story has four things to find:

- **Characters** — who is in the story. (Milo, Pip; Ana, the cat;
  Hare, Turtle.)
- **Setting** — where (and sometimes when) it happens. (At the
  pond; in the snow, one cold day; on the race path, under a tree.)
- **Major events** — the big things that happen, in order.
  (Boat tips over; mitten is lost, then found; Hare naps, Turtle
  wins.)
- **Central message or lesson** — what the story teaches. It is
  often something a character learns, or a sentence the story
  says out loud.

The lesson of "The Slow Race" is said out loud at the end:
"Slow and steady wins the race." Not every story says its lesson
out loud. In the Ana story, nobody says the lesson — but we can
find it: keep looking, and a kind hand helps. A lesson you figure
out yourself still counts, as long as the story shows it.

### Modeled example 1 — The Slow Race, part by part

*Adult works through the four parts aloud:* "Characters: Hare
and Turtle. Setting: the race path, under a tree where Hare naps.
Major events: Hare runs far ahead; Hare naps; Turtle walks on,
step, step, step; Turtle wins. Lesson: slow and steady wins the
race — Turtle says it himself!"

### Modeled example 2 — Finding a lesson the story does not say

*Adult reads the Ana story's end:* "Ana puts on the mitten. The
cat is warm in Ana's arms. They go home happy." "Nobody says a
lesson here. But look: Ana kept looking, and she helped a cold
cat — and then she found her mitten. So the lesson could be: keep
looking, and helping feels good. Two learners might say it a
little differently — that is fine, if the story shows it."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. "Who are the characters in The Slow Race?" (*Hare, Turtle*)
2. "Where does the Ana story happen?" (*In the snow; by the big
   tree; on the path*)
3. "What is the biggest event in The Slow Race?" (*Hare naps
   while Turtle walks on; Turtle wins*)
4. "What lesson does the Turtle say out loud?" (*Slow and steady
   wins the race*)
5. Learner picks one story and names its characters, setting, and
   one major event, with adult prompting.

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Learner draws the Ana story's setting (snow, big tree, path)
   and labels it by dictating one sentence (adult scribes if
   needed).
2. Learner lists the major events of the Ana story in order:
   drops mitten → looks → finds cat → finds mitten → goes home
   happy (oral or picture-ordered).
3. Learner answers: "What lesson does the Ana story teach?"
   (acceptable: *keep looking; helping others feels good; look
   carefully* — any story-supported version).
4. Learner sorts cards into Characters / Setting / Event piles
   for the Milo story (Milo, Pip; pond; boat tips over).

## Application

**Story-parts chart:** the learner fills a four-row chart for a
story of their choice (Milo, Ana, or Slow Race): Characters /
Setting / Major events / Lesson. The adult writes the learner's
dictated answers; the learner reads the chart back.

## Exit check

For "The Slow Race": learner names both characters, one setting
detail, the major event (Hare naps, Turtle wins), and the lesson.
Success: 4/4 parts, with the lesson in the learner's own words
("slow and steady wins" counts).

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** four labeled cups; learner drops a
  token in the right cup for each story fact the adult reads.
- **Floor-play variant:** learner acts out each major event
  (run fast, nap, walk slow) while naming it.
- **Language access:** adult reads the story once more, slowly,
  before the learner names the parts.
- **Alternate response mode:** learner points to pictures (hare,
  turtle, tree, finish) instead of naming parts in sentences.
- **Extension:** learner explains how the lesson would change if
  Hare had not napped ("Then fast would have won — so the lesson
  needs the nap").

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **Setting = only a place:** "one cold day" is also setting
  (when). Accept place-only answers first, then add "and when?"
- **Lesson = what happened:** "Turtle wins" is an event, not a
  lesson. Ask: "What does that teach us?" to move from event to
  message.
- **One right lesson wording:** accept any story-supported
  wording for unstated lessons; correct only lessons the story
  contradicts ("give up quickly" is wrong for Ana).
