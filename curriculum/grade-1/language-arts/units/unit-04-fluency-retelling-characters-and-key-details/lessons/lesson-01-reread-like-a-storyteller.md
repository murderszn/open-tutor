# Lesson 01 — Reread Like a Storyteller

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can read a story again and again until
it sounds smooth — not too fast, not too slow, with a voice that
matches the story — and I can fix a word that does not make sense."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** "Milo and the Red Boat"
(unit README, Unit stories), printed large.
**Standards:** RF.1.4, RF.1.4.a–c.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Adult reads: "Milo has a red boat." Learner repeats it, pointing
at each word. Then: "Pip is his dog." If the learner can point and
read most words, the ears and eyes are ready. If not, reread Unit
03's decodable lines first — fluency practice needs words the
learner can mostly decode already.

## Explicit teaching

**Fluency** is reading that sounds like talking: the words come
out smooth, at a good speed, and with feeling. It has three parts:

- **Accuracy** — reading the words right.
- **Rate** — not too fast, not too slow. Racing is not reading.
- **Expression** — your voice matches the story. A happy part
  sounds happy. A surprise sounds surprised. A question goes up
  at the end.

The secret of fluency is **rereading**. The first read is for
figuring out the words. The second read is smoother. The third
read sounds like a storyteller.

And when a word does not make sense, a good reader stops and
**self-corrects**: "Did that make sense? No? Let me reread that
part and fix it."

### Modeled example 1 — Robot voice vs. storyteller voice

*Adult reads flat and choppy, one word at a time:* "Milo. Puts.
The. Boat. In. The. Water." "Did that sound like a story? No —
that was a robot!" *Adult reads again, smooth and warm:* "Milo
puts the boat in the water." "Now it sounds like a story. Same
words — but grouped together, like talking."

### Modeled example 2 — Catching a word that does not make sense

*Adult reads on purpose:* "The wind blows hard. The boat tips
over! Splash! Milo is... *sand*?" "Wait — Milo is sand? That
does not make sense! Let me look again: s-a-d. Sad! Milo is sad.
I fixed it by checking what makes sense. That is self-correcting."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. Adult reads "The wind blows." Learner echoes it, smooth, like
   talking. (*Echo read*)
2. Learner reads "Milo has a red boat." Adult: "Too fast or just
   right?" Learner reads it again, slower, grouped. (*Rate*)
3. Adult reads "Go, boat, go!" in a flat voice. Learner: "Say it
   like Milo is cheering!" Learner rereads it with a cheering
   voice. (*Expression*)
4. Adult reads "Pip jumps in. Pip gets the... *bat*?" Learner
   stops the adult: "Bat does not make sense — boat!" (*Self-correct*)
5. Learner reads the first four sentences of the Milo story; adult
   marks one smooth part to praise ("That sentence sounded just
   like talking!").

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Learner rereads the Milo story's first six sentences twice;
   the adult listens for smoother second read.
2. Learner points to a word they fixed by self-correcting and
   tells the adult what did not make sense.
3. Learner draws a star next to (adult scribes) the sentence they
   read most smoothly.
4. Learner reads "Splash!" three ways: surprised, happy, quiet —
   and picks the one that fits the story.

## Application

**Three-read race (against yourself, not a clock):** the learner
reads the first six sentences of the Milo story three times. After
each read, the adult asks one question: "Smoother? More like
talking?" The learner keeps the best read for the last one — and
says what got better.

## Exit check

Learner reads "Milo puts the boat in the water. The wind blows.
The boat sails fast." twice. Success: second read is smoother
than the first, words are accurate, and the learner can name one
thing that got better (speed, smoothness, or voice).

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** learner tracks words with a finger or
  a pointer card with a window cut out.
- **Floor-play variant:** learner steps on a word card for each
  phrase, not each word, to feel phrasing.
- **Language access:** adult models the cheering voice first;
  learner copies before reading alone.
- **Alternate response mode:** learner points to "robot" or
  "storyteller" cards to judge a read, instead of explaining.
- **Extension:** learner reads the whole Milo story twice and
  marks the sentence where expression matters most, explaining why.

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **Fast equals good:** praise smoothness and sense, never speed
  alone. If the learner races, cover all but one sentence and
  reread for sense.
- **Skipping the fix:** a learner who reads past a nonsense word
  needs the stop-and-check habit modeled often: "Did that make
  sense?" before any correction of the word itself.
- **Flat reading as careful reading:** expression is part of
  comprehension, not showing off. Model it every lesson.
