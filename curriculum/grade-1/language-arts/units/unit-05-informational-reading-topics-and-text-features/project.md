# Project — My Information Book

**Product:** a 4-page information book about beavers, made by the
learner with adult support (adult scribes dictated words; the
learner draws, labels, and reads back every page).
**Time:** Sessions 12 and 14 (20–25 minutes each), plus the share
in Session 14.
**Standards:** W.1.8; RI.1.2; RI.1.5; RI.1.7; SL.1.4; SL.1.5.

## Why this project

A reader who can use text features should also be able to make
them. Building a book forces every unit skill into one product:
topic, headings, contents, labels, caption, glossary — and the
facts must be true, checked against the unit texts.

## Materials

Drawing paper (4 pages) or folded booklet paper, crayons, pencil,
marker, the generated illustration for reference, Unit Texts A
and B. No sharp tools.

## Part 1 (Session 12) — Cover, contents, first section

1. **Cover:** title ("Beavers" or the learner's own true title,
   for example "Beavers Build!") and a drawn beaver picture.
2. **Table of contents:** list the book's parts with page
   numbers: "Beaver Homes — page 2," "Beaver Food — page 3,"
   "Words to Know — page 4." (Adult checks the numbers match
   the real pages.)
3. **Section 1 (page 2) — heading + facts:** heading "Beaver
   Homes," then two true facts from Text A or B (for example:
   "A beaver builds a dam of sticks, mud, and stones." "A lodge
   is the beaver's home. Its door is under the water."). The
   learner reads both facts back.

## Part 2 (Session 14) — Picture page, glossary, share

4. **Section 2 (page 3) — picture page:** a drawing of the
   beaver pond with at least two labels (*dam*, *lodge*) and
   one caption sentence ("A beaver stands on its dam."). The
   learner may copy the layout (not the image) of the generated
   illustration.
5. **Glossary (page 4):** two words with meanings in the
   learner's own words (for example: *dam* — "a wall across a
   stream"; *kit* — "a baby beaver"). Meanings must match the
   unit glossaries' sense; wording may differ.
6. **Share:** the learner "reads" the book aloud to the adult
   (reading, retelling from pictures, or a mix), pointing at
   each text feature as it is used: "This is my table of
   contents. It tells you where Beaver Homes is."

## Deliverables

- Cover with a true title and a beaver picture
- Table of contents with correct page numbers
- Section 1: heading + two true facts
- Section 2: labeled picture + caption sentence
- Glossary: two words with correct meanings
- Share: learner presents the book, naming each feature used

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (3) | Developing (2) | Starting (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| True facts (RI.1.2) | Both section-1 facts are true and match Texts A/B | One fact true; the other vague or unchecked | Facts are made up or missing |
| Text features (RI.1.5) | Contents page numbers match; heading present; two labels + caption all correct | One feature missing or one page number wrong | Features missing or mislabeled |
| Picture + caption (RI.1.7) | Drawing shows a beaver fact; caption sentence matches the drawing | Drawing or caption only partly matches | No drawing, or caption unrelated to drawing |
| Glossary (RI.1.4) | Both words defined with correct sense in own words | One definition correct; the other vague | Definitions wrong or copied without understanding |
| Share (SL.1.4) | Learner presents all pages and names each feature used | Presents most pages; names some features | Needs adult prompting for every page |

**Scoring:** 12–15 = ready to move on (celebrate; file the book
in private storage, never in this repo). 8–11 = review one weak
feature with a practice game, then re-share that page. Below 8 =
re-teach with Lesson 02 or 03 and rebuild the weak pages before
the unit assessment.

## Teacher notes

- **Fact-checking is the lesson:** if a learner writes a fact
  neither text supports (for example, "Beavers eat fish"), do
  not mark it wrong and move on — look it up together in Text
  A/B or the resource pack. Beavers eat plants; fish is a
  common mix-up. Correcting it teaches what information books
  are for.
- **Scribing is allowed:** the adult writes dictated words;
  the learner must still read the page back. The skill being
  assessed is informational reading, not handwriting stamina.
- **Privacy:** the finished book stays in private storage. Do
  not photograph it into this repository.
