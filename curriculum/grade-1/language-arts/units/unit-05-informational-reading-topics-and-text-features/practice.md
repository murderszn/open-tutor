# Practice Sessions — Unit 05: Informational Reading

Each session is 20–25 minutes. The adult reads unit texts aloud;
the learner reads with support. Oral, pointing, and drawing
responses count everywhere; the adult reviews written work the
same day.

## Session A — Question-and-retell cards

1. The adult writes (or the learner dictates) six question cards:
   Who builds dams? What is a lodge? Where is the lodge door?
   What do beavers eat? Why is deep water good? What is a kit?
2. The learner draws a card, asks the question aloud, then
   answers it from Text A.
3. Retell round: the learner retells Text A in three sentences
   (builders → dam → lodge, or any three key details in order).
4. Swap: the learner asks the adult one question; the adult
   answers, then asks: "Was that a key detail?"
5. Finish: the learner picks the hardest question and answers it
   a second time, faster.

## Session B — Feature scavenger hunt

Use Text A and any household information book the adult has
checked first.

1. Find a heading. Read it aloud (adult helps). Say what its
   part is about.
2. Find the table of contents. Use it to open the "Beaver Food"
   part without flipping page by page.
3. Find the glossary. Look up *lodge* and read its meaning.
4. Find a picture with a caption. Read the caption (adult helps)
   and say one thing the caption adds.
5. In the household book, find one heading, one picture, and
   one caption. Name each feature as you point to it.
6. Speed round: the adult names a feature ("glossary!"); the
   learner points to it in under ten seconds.

## Session C — Label the beaver pond

Uses [`assets/beaver-dam-scene.png`](assets/beaver-dam-scene.png).

1. Look at the picture. Name five things you see (beaver, dam,
   lodge, duck, cattails, trees, pond, stick).
2. The adult writes labels: *dam, lodge, tail, duck, pond*.
   The learner places each label on the right part.
3. Picture-only round: "What does the picture show that the
   words in Text A never say?" (Duck, cattails, what the dam
   looks like.)
4. Word-only round: "What do the words say that the picture
   cannot show?" (Door under the water; teeth keep growing;
   winter food storage.)
5. The learner draws their own beaver pond and adds two labels
   and one caption sentence (adult scribes as needed).
6. Text-only alternative: if the picture cannot be shown, use
   the text alternative in
   [Lesson 03](lessons/lesson-03-pictures-labels-and-captions.md)
   — every task above works from the description alone.

## Session D — Glossary game

1. Word cards face down: *dam, lodge, gnaw, kit, bark, pond*.
   The learner turns one over and says its meaning (glossary
   allowed — using it is the skill).
2. Definition round: the adult reads a definition; the learner
   names the word ("A wall built across a stream" → dam).
3. Affix round: *rebuild* (build again), *lodges* (more than
   one lodge), *swimming* (root *swim* + *-ing*).
4. Sentence-clue round: "The beaver gnaws the branch. Gnaw
   means…?" (Bite and chew again and again.)
5. The learner teaches the adult one word, using a sentence
   clue, a word part, or the glossary — learner's choice.
6. Make a mini-glossary: the learner picks two unit words and
   writes (or dictates) a meaning for each in their own words.

## Session E — Because chains

1. Card chain 1: beaver piles sticks → water slows → pond
   forms → beaver is safe. Learner orders the cards and reads
   the chain.
2. Card chain 2: teeth keep growing → beaver gnaws wood → teeth
   stay the right length. Learner orders and reads.
3. Card chain 3: fall comes → beaver stores branches under
   water → winter food is ready. Learner orders and reads.
4. Reason match: the adult reads a point; the learner gives its
   reason. Point: "Deep water keeps beavers safe." Reason:
   "Hunters cannot reach them easily." Point: "Cold pond water
   keeps food fresh." Reason: "It works like a refrigerator."
5. The learner invents a because-sentence about beavers and
   checks it against Text A (adult confirms or corrects).
6. Broken-chain fix: the adult lays a chain out of order; the
   learner fixes it and explains the fix.

## Session F — Same or different game

1. Fact cards sorted into A only / B only / Both piles:
   "Teeth keep growing" (A), "Kits stay two years" (B),
   "Lodge is made of sticks and mud" (Both), "Tail slap warns"
   (B), "Beavers eat bark" (A), "Family lives in the lodge"
   (Both).
2. The learner defends one sort: "Why is this card in Both?"
   (Both books say it — show both sentences.)
3. New fact round: "Kits are born in spring." (B only.)
   "The lodge door is under the water." (A only.)
4. Picture round: which book's facts does the generated
   illustration match best? (Text A — dam, lodge, pond; Text B
   has no dam picture. The illustration supports Text A.)
5. The learner asks the adult one same/different question and
   checks the answer in the texts.

## Session G — Reread and retell Text B

1. The adult reads Text B aloud; the learner follows along.
2. The learner retells Text B in three parts: born → growing
   up → staying safe.
3. Key-detail hunt: "What are two key details about kits?"
   (Born with fur, eyes open; stay about two years.)
4. Word work: find *warning* in the glossary; use the sentence
   clue ("Slap! That is a warning.") to explain it.
5. Connect: "How does the tail slap connect to staying safe?"
   (Slap warns → beavers dive deep → deep water protects.)
6. The learner tells one thing Text B taught that Text A did
   not, and one thing both books teach.

## Session H — Review games and re-teach

Use after the quiz in Session 15, or any time a skill is shaky.

1. Feature flash: the adult names a feature; the learner finds
   it in Text A in under ten seconds (heading, contents,
   glossary, caption, label).
2. Question ball: the adult tosses a soft ball (or points);
   whoever catches it asks a key-detail question; the other
   answers.
3. Chain race: rebuild all three Session E chains from mixed
   cards.
4. Picture detective replay with the generated illustration:
   one picture-only fact, one word-only fact.
5. Re-teach as needed: if the quiz showed a gap (for example,
   glossary use), replay that session's game once, with the
   adult modeling first.
6. Celebrate: the learner names the unit skill they are proudest
   of, and the adult names one observed growth.
