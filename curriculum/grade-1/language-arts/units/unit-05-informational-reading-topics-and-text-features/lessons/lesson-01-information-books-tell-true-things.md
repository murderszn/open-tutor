# Lesson 01 — Information Books Tell True Things

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can tell a storybook from an
information book, name the main topic, and retell key details."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** [Unit Text A](../README.md#unit-text-a--beavers-build-a-home)
("Beavers Build a Home"), two cards labeled "STORYBOOK" and
"INFORMATION BOOK" (adult writes them; the learner need not read
them yet).
**Standards:** RI.1.2; RI.1.1; RL.1.5.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Adult: "Tell me one thing from a story we read before." (Any
retelling from Unit 04 counts.) "Now — was that story true, or
made up for fun?" If the learner can retell one detail, the ear is
ready. If not, reread a familiar Unit 04 passage together first.

## Explicit teaching

Some books tell **stories**. A story has made-up characters and
things that did not really happen. We read stories for fun and
for feelings.

Some books give **information**. An information book teaches
**true things** about the real world. It has a **topic** — what
the whole book is about. And it has **key details** — the important
true facts that teach about the topic.

Today's book is an information book. Its topic is **beavers**.
Listen for true facts. After we read, you will tell me the topic
and two key details.

*Adult reads Text A aloud, one section at a time, pointing at
each heading before reading its part.*

### Modeled example 1 — The topic is beavers

*Adult:* "What is this whole book about? Every part is about one
animal. The animal builds dams. The animal builds a lodge. The
animal eats bark. So the **main topic** is **beavers**. A topic
is the answer to: 'What is this book mostly about?'"

### Modeled example 2 — A key detail, and a not-key detail

*Adult:* "Here is a key detail: 'A beaver builds a dam of sticks,
mud, and stones.' That teaches us about beavers. Here is a
not-key detail: the book says the water is slow. Slow water
matters, but the big fact is the dam. When you retell, keep the
big facts: beavers build dams, beavers live in lodges, beavers
eat bark."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. "Is 'Beavers Build a Home' a storybook or an information
   book? How do you know?" (Information book — it teaches true
   things about real beavers.)
2. "What is the main topic?" (Beavers.)
3. "Tell me one key detail from 'A Dam Across the Stream.'"
   (A beaver piles sticks, mud, and stones to build a dam; the
   dam makes a pond.)
4. "Tell me one key detail from 'A Lodge in the Pond.'" (The
   lodge is a pile of sticks and mud; its door is under the
   water; the family lives there.)
5. "Which is a key detail: 'Beavers eat bark' or 'The pond is
   nice'? Why?" (Beavers eat bark — a true fact about the topic.)

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Point to the "INFORMATION BOOK" card and say why Text A
   belongs there (it teaches true things).
2. Say the main topic in a full sentence: "This book is about
   beavers."
3. Draw a beaver dam and tell the adult one key detail about it
   (adult scribes the detail under the drawing if needed).
4. Answer: "What do beavers eat?" (Bark, twigs, and leaves.)
5. Retell two key details from any two sections, in order.

## Application

**True-or-made-up sort:** the adult says six short lines; the
learner points to the STORYBOOK card or the INFORMATION BOOK card
for each: "A beaver builds a dam." (information) / "A beaver
wears a crown and sings." (story) / "A beaver's teeth keep
growing." (information) / "A beaver drives a car to school."
(story) / "A beaver family lives in a lodge." (information) /
"A beaver talks to the moon." (story)

## Exit check

Adult asks: "Is this book a storybook or an information book?
What is its topic? Tell me two key details." Success: correct
book type, topic "beavers," and two true key details.

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** cards on the table; learner taps the
  card for each answer instead of speaking the label.
- **Floor-play variant:** two hoops labeled with picture cards
  (a crown for story, a magnifying glass for information); the
  learner steps into the right hoop.
- **Language access:** act out "true" (nod, thumbs up) and
  "made up" (shake head, silly face) before the sort.
- **Alternate response mode:** learner points to the dam picture
  in the generated illustration for a dam detail, or draws
  instead of writing.
- **Extension:** the learner names the topic of a household
  information book the adult shows (title and pictures only).

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **"It has pictures, so it is a storybook":** both kinds of
  books have pictures. Ask: "Does it teach true things, or tell
  a made-up story?" That question, not the pictures, decides it.
- **Retelling everything:** a retell keeps key details, not
  every word. If the learner lists tiny details, ask: "What
  would a friend most need to know about beavers?"
- **Topic too narrow:** "dams" alone is not the topic — the
  whole book covers beavers. Ask: "What are ALL the parts
  about?"
