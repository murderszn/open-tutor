# Lesson 05 — Why Beavers Build

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can explain how ideas in a book
connect, and name a reason the author gives."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Materials:** [Unit Text A](../README.md#unit-text-a--beavers-build-a-home),
three arrow cards (→) or a drawn chain on paper.
**Standards:** RI.1.3; RI.1.8.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

Adult: "Why do you wear a coat in winter?" (To stay warm.) "That
is a reason. Today we find the author's reasons in the book."
If the learner gives any sensible because-answer, the ear is
ready.

## Explicit teaching

Ideas in an information book **connect** like links in a chain.
One thing causes the next thing. Watch the beaver chain:

**Beaver piles sticks, mud, and stones → the dam slows the
water → slow water makes a pond → the deep pond keeps the beaver
safe.**

Each link answers "what happens next?" or "why?"

An author also gives **reasons** — facts that support a point.
The book's point is: "Deep water keeps the beaver safe." The
reason is: animals that hunt beavers cannot reach it easily in
deep water. A reason answers "why is that true?" or "how do you
know?"

The word **because** is a clue: the words after *because* are
usually a reason.

### Modeled example 1 — The dam chain

*Adult, laying out arrow cards:* "Beaver builds a dam. What
happens because of the dam? The water slows down. What happens
because the water slows? A pond forms. What happens because
there is a deep pond? The beaver is safe. So: dam → slow water
→ pond → safe beaver. If one link breaks, the chain breaks: no
dam, no pond, no safe home."

### Modeled example 2 — The author's reason

*Adult:* "The book says: 'Deep water keeps the beaver safe.'
How does the author prove it? Next sentence: 'Animals that hunt
beavers cannot reach it easily.' That is the reason. Point and
reason, like this: POINT — deep water keeps beavers safe.
REASON — hunters cannot reach them easily. When you read, ask:
'What is the point, and what reason backs it up?'"

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 5 tasks)

1. "Why does the beaver build a dam? Give the chain." (To slow
   the water and make a deep pond, so it stays safe.)
2. "Point: 'The cold pond water keeps food fresh.' Reason?"
   (It works like a refrigerator — the book says so in 'Beaver
   Food.' Accept: cold water keeps the branches fresh.)
3. "Connect: teeth keep growing → beaver gnaws wood → ?"
   (Teeth stay worn down / the right length — gnawing wears
   the ever-growing teeth down.)
4. "Why is the lodge door under the water?" (So the beaver can
   swim in safely and hunters cannot walk in.)
5. "Which sentence is a reason: 'Beavers eat bark' or 'Animals
   that hunt beavers cannot reach it easily'?" (The second —
   it explains why deep water keeps beavers safe.)

## Independent practice (4–6 short tasks; adult checks same day)

1. Draw the dam → pond → safe chain with three pictures and
   arrows (adult scribes labels if needed).
2. Finish: "Beavers store food in fall because ______."
   (They need food in winter / they stay in the lodge in winter.)
3. Finish: "A beaver's teeth keep growing, so ______." (It must
   gnaw wood to wear them down.)
4. Name the point and the reason: "Beavers build lodges of
   sticks and mud." — is that a point or a reason? (A fact/point;
   the reason lodges matter is safety — deep water and a strong
   home protect the family.)
5. Ask a "why" question about Text A and answer it with a
   because-sentence.

## Application

**Chain builder:** the adult gives four mixed-up fact cards:
"pond is deep," "beaver piles sticks," "hunters cannot reach,"
"water slows." The learner orders them into a chain and reads
it aloud: beaver piles sticks → water slows → pond is deep →
hunters cannot reach. Then the learner says the point in one
sentence: "The dam keeps the beaver safe."

## Exit check

Adult asks: "Why does a beaver build a dam? Tell me the chain
and one reason." Success: at least two chain links in order
(dam → pond → safety) and one reason (hunters cannot reach /
deep water protects).

## Supports

- **Seated-table variant:** fact cards on the table; learner
  orders them left to right with arrow cards between.
- **Floor-play variant:** chain links as floor cards; learner
  walks the chain, saying each link.
- **Language access:** act out each link (pile, slow, swim
  deep, hide) before chaining.
- **Alternate response mode:** learner draws the chain or points
  to ordered cards instead of speaking full sentences.
- **Extension:** the learner builds a second chain: teeth grow
  → gnaw → teeth stay short; or: fall comes → store branches
  → winter food ready.

## Teacher note — misconceptions

- **Reason vs. detail:** a detail tells more about the topic;
  a reason explains why a point is true. If the learner offers a
  detail as a reason, ask: "Does that tell us WHY, or just
  tell us MORE?"
- **Backward chains:** young learners often start at the end
  ("Beavers are safe because…"). That is fine — a because-answer
  is a chain read backward. Help them flip it into forward
  order once, then accept either direction.
- **One-link answers:** "Beavers build dams to be safe" skips
  the middle. Ask: "What happens in between?" to stretch the
  chain.
