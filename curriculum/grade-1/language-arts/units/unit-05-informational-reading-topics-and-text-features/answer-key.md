# Answer Key — Unit 05: Informational Reading

Adult-facing. Every solution below was worked independently from
the unit texts and the generated illustration, then reconciled
with the lessons. Accept reasonable wording that keeps the same
fact; for open responses, the sample reasoning is the standard,
not the only wording.

## Lesson 01 — Information books tell true things

**Guided practice**

1. Information book — it teaches true things about real beavers.
2. Beavers.
3. Any one: dam of sticks, mud, and stones; dam slows water and
   makes a pond.
4. Any one: lodge is a pile of sticks and mud; door under the
   water; beaver family lives there.
5. "Beavers eat bark" — a true fact about the topic; "The pond
   is nice" is an opinion, not a key detail.

**Independent practice**

1. INFORMATION BOOK card; reason: teaches true things.
2. "This book is about beavers."
3. Drawing of a dam + one key detail (dam of sticks/mud/stones;
   dam makes a pond).
4. Bark, twigs, and leaves.
5. Any two true key details in order.

**Application (true-or-made-up sort):** information, story,
information, story, information, story (in the order given in
the lesson).

**Exit check:** information book; topic beavers; two true key
details.

refrigerator (page 4).

**Exit check:** "A Lodge in the Pond" (page 3); sticks and mud.

## Lesson 03 — Pictures, labels, and captions

**Guided practice**

1. Dome-shaped (like a round hill).
2. A stick; the picture (words never say it).
3. Dam → stick pile; lodge → dome behind; tail → flat brown
   shape behind the beaver; duck → yellow bird in the water.
4. The caption names the pile as a dam; the picture alone shows
   only a pile.
5. Picture fact: duck swims here / cattails at the edge. Word
   fact: lodge door under water / beavers eat bark.

**Independent practice**

1. Drawing with *dam* and *lodge* labels pointing correctly.
2. Caption sentence about the drawing (for example, "A beaver
   stands on its dam.").
3. Picture-only: duck, cattails, what the dam looks like.
4. Word-only: underwater door, stored winter food, ever-growing
   teeth.
5. Tail pointed to; one use: swimming rudder, balance, or
   warning slap.

**Application (picture detective):** any three true picture facts
(beaver on stick dam; dome lodge; duck; cattails/trees; beaver
holds a stick). Caption adds: it names the dam and the lodge.

**Exit check:** one picture-only fact + one word-only fact.

## Lesson 04 — Words we do not know yet

**Guided practice**

1. The rough outer skin of a tree.
2. "A wall built across a stream to slow the water."
3. Heat again.
4. *Swim*; *kit*.
5. Protected from animals that hunt it (sentence clue + earlier
   facts).

**Independent practice**

1. A small body of still water.
2. Keep/save for later.
3. Grow again.
4. Roots: look, lodge, build. With parts: looks, lodges, rebuild.
5. Any genuine word-meaning question (asking is the skill).

**Application (word detective):** *huddle* = to crowd close
together for warmth (adult-supplied; learner restates in own
words). Asking a person counts as a valid last tool.

**Exit check:** bite and chew again and again; tool named
(sentence clue or glossary).

## Lesson 05 — Why beavers build

**Guided practice**

1. To slow the water, make a deep pond, and stay safe.
2. It works like a refrigerator (cold water keeps branches fresh).
3. Teeth stay worn down / the right length.
4. So the beaver can swim in safely and hunters cannot walk in.
5. "Animals that hunt beavers cannot reach it easily" — it
   explains why deep water keeps beavers safe.

**Independent practice**

1. Three-picture chain with arrows: dam → pond → safe.
2. "…because they need food in winter / they stay in the lodge
   in winter."
3. "…so it must gnaw wood to wear them down."
4. A fact/point; the reason lodges matter is safety (deep water
   and a strong home protect the family).
5. Any why-question about Text A answered with a
   because-sentence.

**Application (chain builder):** beaver piles sticks → water
slows → pond is deep → hunters cannot reach. Point sentence:
"The dam keeps the beaver safe."

**Exit check:** at least two chain links in order + one reason.

## Lesson 06 — Two books, one topic

**Guided practice**

1. Beavers.
2. Any shared fact: lodge is the home of sticks and mud;
   family/babies live in the lodge; deep water keeps beavers
   safe.
3. Any A-only fact: teeth keep growing; dam of sticks, mud,
   stones; food stored under water; beavers eat bark.
4. Any B-only fact: kits born in spring; kits stay two years;
   tail slap warns of danger.
5. Either book — both glossaries define *kit*; Text B also has
   the "Kits Are Born" section.

**Independent practice**

1. Chart: SAME = lodge home (or another shared fact);
   DIFFERENT = A: teeth / B: tail slap (or equivalents).
2. Two-sentence retell of Text B (born → growing → safety;
   any two key details).
3. Yes.
4. Drawing of a B-only fact (kit, or tail slapping water) with
   label.
5. Any honest preference + reason (no single right answer).

**Application (two-book quiz show):** A, B, Both, B, A, A (in
the order given; see lesson note on defending "Both" for the
last item).

**Exit check:** one true likeness + one true difference.

## Practice sessions

- **A:** all six questions answered from Text A (Who: beavers.
  Lodge: the beaver's home of sticks and mud. Door: under the
  water. Eat: bark, twigs, leaves. Deep water: keeps beavers
  safe from hunters. Kit: a baby beaver.) Retell: any three key
  details in order.
- **B:** heading, contents ("Beaver Food," page 4), glossary
  (*lodge* = a beaver's home, built of sticks and mud in a
  pond), caption (adds a name or fact to its picture), plus one
  heading/picture/caption found in the household book.
- **C:** five namings; labels placed correctly; picture-only
  facts (duck, cattails, dam's look); word-only facts
  (underwater door, growing teeth, winter food storage);
  drawing with two labels + caption.
- **D:** all six words defined (dam: wall across a stream;
  lodge: beaver's home of sticks and mud; gnaw: bite and chew
  again and again; kit: baby beaver; bark: rough outer skin of
  a tree; pond: small body of still water). Affixes: rebuild =
  build again; lodges = more than one lodge; swimming root =
  swim.
- **E:** chains ordered as listed in the lesson; reasons matched
  as listed; learner's because-sentence checked against Text A;
  broken chain fixed with explanation.
- **F:** sorts as listed (A: teeth, bark; B: two years, tail
  slap; Both: lodge of sticks and mud, family in lodge).
  Illustration matches Text A best (dam, lodge, pond).
- **G:** retell in three parts (born → growing → safety); kit
  details (born with fur, eyes open; stay about two years);
  *warning* = a signal that tells of danger; tail slap → dive
  deep → safety.
- **H:** features found in under ten seconds each; chains
  rebuilt; picture detective repeated; re-teach as noted.
