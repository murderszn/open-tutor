# Unit 05 — Informational Reading: Topics and Text Features

**Grade:** 1 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 19–22
**Backlog:** [issue #12](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/12), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Stories are fun to read. Information books are a different kind of
fun: they teach true things about the real world. In this unit the
learner reads about one real animal — the **beaver** — and learns
how information books work. A **heading** tells what a part is
about. A **table of contents** tells where each part lives. A
**glossary** explains hard words. A **caption** tells what a
picture shows. A **label** names a part of a picture. By the end,
the learner can find a fact fast, tell what a picture shows that
the words do not, retell key details, and make a small information
book of their own.

## Essential question

How do I find a true fact in an information book — and how do I
know what the book is teaching me?

  two-syllable words; reads grade-appropriate irregular words by
  sight (RF.1.3)
- Reads grade-level text orally with accuracy and self-correction
  (RF.1.4, from Unit 04)
- Asks and answers questions about key details and retells stories
  with key details in order (RL.1.1, RL.1.2, from Unit 04)
- Prints upper- and lowercase letters and spells common patterns
  conventionally, untaught words phonetically (L.1.2, from Units

- Short independent written practice (4–6 tasks) reviewed by the
  adult the same day

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed;
oral, pointing, and drawing responses accepted everywhere; short
written practice of 4–6 tasks is reviewed by the adult the same day):

1. **Ask and answer key-detail questions:** ask and answer who,
   what, where, and why questions about key details in an
   informational text read aloud or read with support.
2. **Name the topic and retell key details:** identify the main
   topic of an informational text and retell two or more key
   details in order.
3. **Use text features to find facts:** use headings, a table of
   contents, a glossary, labels, captions, and icons to locate a
   named fact without reading the whole book.
4. **Use pictures and words together:** distinguish information
   given by a picture from information given by words, and use
   illustrations and details to describe key ideas.
5. **Explain connections and reasons:** describe a connection
   between two ideas in a text (for example, dam → pond → safe
   lodge) and name a reason the author gives for a point.
6. **Compare two texts and clarify words:** identify one likeness
   and one difference between two texts on the same topic; ask and
   answer questions that clarify a word's meaning using sentence
   clues, a known affix, or a known root word; tell a storybook
   apart from an information book and explain the difference.

## Unit texts (original passages authored for this unit; read aloud by the adult, read with support by the learner)

Facts in these texts were checked against the Smithsonian's National
Zoo beaver page and the Britannica beaver article (see
[resource-pack](resource-pack.md); checked 2026-11-05) and simplified
for grade 1. The texts are teaching passages, not quotations from

### Unit Text A — "Beavers Build a Home"

**Table of contents**

- Beaver Builders — page 1
- A Dam Across the Stream — page 2
- A Lodge in the Pond — page 3
- Beaver Food — page 4
- Glossary — page 5

**Beaver Builders**

Beavers are animals that build. A beaver has big orange teeth in
front. Its teeth keep growing all its life. The beaver gnaws wood
with its teeth. Gnaw means to bite and chew again and again. A
beaver has a wide, flat tail. Its back feet have skin between the
toes, like paddles. That helps it swim.

**A Dam Across the Stream**

A beaver builds a dam. A dam is a wall across a stream. The beaver
piles up sticks, mud, and stones. The dam slows the water down.
Slow water makes a pond. The pond is deep. Deep water keeps the
beaver safe. Animals that hunt beavers cannot reach it easily.

**A Lodge in the Pond**

A beaver builds a lodge in the pond. A lodge is the beaver's home.
It is a big pile of sticks and mud, shaped like a dome. Inside is
a dry room above the water. The door is under the water. The
beaver swims under the water and comes up inside its home. A
beaver family lives in the lodge: a mother, a father, and their
babies.

**Beaver Food**

Beavers eat plants. They eat bark, twigs, and leaves. Bark is the
rough skin of a tree. In fall, beavers store food. They push
branches down into the mud at the bottom of the pond. In winter,
the beaver swims out, takes a branch, and eats it inside the lodge.
The cold pond water keeps the food fresh, like a refrigerator.

**Glossary**

- **bark** — the rough outer skin of a tree.
- **dam** — a wall built across a stream to slow the water.
- **gnaw** — to bite and chew again and again.
- **kit** — a baby beaver.
- **lodge** — a beaver's home, built of sticks and mud in a pond.
- **pond** — a small body of still water.

### Unit Text B — "A Beaver Family"

**Table of contents**

- Kits Are Born — page 1
- Growing Up — page 2
- Staying Safe — page 3
- Glossary — page 4

**Kits Are Born**

Baby beavers are called kits. Kits are born in spring. A kit is
born with fur, and its eyes are open. Soon the kit can swim. The
mother feeds the kit milk. The family keeps the kit warm and safe
inside the lodge.

**Growing Up**

Kits stay with their parents for about two years. They learn to
swim well. They learn to gnaw wood. They help fix the dam and the
lodge. When a kit is two years old, it leaves and builds its own
home. Then new kits are born in the old lodge.

**Staying Safe**

Beavers stay safe in deep water. A beaver can slap its flat tail
on the water. Slap! That is a warning. The other beavers dive down
deep. Deep water and the strong lodge keep the family safe from
animals that hunt them.

**Glossary**

- **kit** — a baby beaver.
- **lodge** — a beaver's home, built of sticks and mud in a pond.
- **warning** — a signal that tells of danger.
