# Starter Lesson — Blend short-vowel words and retell

**Status:** draft supplement; one early decoding lesson, not a complete reading unit or intervention. **Time:** three short 15–20 minute sessions. **Goal:** Review spoken phoneme segmentation, decode selected short-a and short-i words with taught final consonant clusters, and retell an original decodable text.

## Adult preparation and readiness

Use counters, letter tiles/cards `s, t, m, p, a, i, n`, and paper. This lesson assumes letter names and the listed letter-sounds are introduced; if not, explicitly teach a few correspondences first and use oral sound games. Readiness: ask the learner to blend /s/ /a/ /t/ and name the first sound in *map*. If readiness is not shown, pause the printed text and work orally; this lesson does not claim to assess reading disability.

## Teach and model

Tell the learner that each sound in a spoken word can be mapped to a letter. Model with *sat*: /s/ /a/ /t/; move three counters, then replace each with its letter. For *taps*, say /t/ /a/ /p/ /s/: the final consonants **ps** represent two separate sounds. Blend smoothly without omitting either sound.

**Worked example 1:** *map* → /m/ /a/ /p/ → m-a-p → *map*.

**Worked example 2:** *tips* → /t/ /i/ /p/ /s/ → t-i-p-s → *tips*. Four sounds, four letters; /p/ and /s/ stay separate.

## Original short text

The text uses only short **a/i** and taught consonants, plus high-frequency words **a**, **the**, and **is**. Preview all three as known words before reading; **is** is especially important because its **s** spelling represents /z/, which this lesson has not taught. Do not ask the learner to decode these words using this lesson's patterns.

> Sam sat. Pam taps a tin. A map is in the tin. Pam tips the tin. Sam taps the map.

**Pacing and roles:** Session 1: adult models and guides sound boxes. Session 2: read the passage together, then learner independently marks familiar decodable words. Session 3: complete sequence/error-check tasks and exit retell with adult review.

## Guided and independent tasks

1. Tap sounds in *map*, *taps*, and *tips*; model /t/ /a/ /p/ /s/ for *taps* and /t/ /i/ /p/ /s/ for *tips*.
2. Read the passage together; adult points under each word and learner blends decodable words.
3. Ask: “What is in the tin?” and “What does Pam do before Sam taps the map?”
4. Put events in order: Pam taps the tin; Pam tips the tin; Sam taps the map.
5. Error check: adult reads *taps* but omits /s/. Ask learner which sound is missing.
6. Write/dictate a new sentence using practiced words, such as “Sam sat.”

**Answer checks:** A map is in the tin; Pam tips the tin immediately before Sam taps the map (she also taps the tin earlier); the event sequence follows the passage. In task 5, /s/ is missing. **a**, **the**, and **is** are previewed known words, not decoding targets.

## Exit check and product

Ask learner to decode *map* and orally retell two events. Expected: *map*; Pam taps the tin, then tips it. Product: marked passage, event strip/drawing, and one dictated or written sentence.

## Review rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phoneme work | Segments/blends CVC and taught final-cluster words | Accurate after adult sound stretch | Return to 2–3 phoneme oral words |
| Text reading | Decodes taught patterns; recognizes pre-taught *a*, *the*, and *is* | Reads with modeling | Reduce to taught word cards |
| Comprehension | Retells events in order | Retells with sequence prompts | Reread one line at a time |

Use counters, sound boxes, large type, adult read-aloud, and home-language discussion. Student may point, dictate, or use letter tiles instead of handwriting. Follow a systematic phonics sequence; do not generalize this small word set to all blends or vowel patterns. No internet is required; keep learner names and work private.
