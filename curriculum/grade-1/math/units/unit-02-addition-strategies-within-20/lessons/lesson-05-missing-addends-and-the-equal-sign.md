# Lesson 05 — Missing Addends and the Equal Sign

**Student goal:** I can find the missing number in 8 + ? = 11, and I know
the equal sign means "is the same amount as."
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Unit:** [U02](../README.md) · **Session 13 of 16**

## Materials

- 20 counters
- Equation cards: `8 + ? = 11`, `5 + ? = 13`, `6 + 6 = _`, `_ + 4 = 12`
- True/false cards: `6 = 6`, `5 + 2 = 2 + 5`, `7 + 7 = 15`, `9 = 4 + 5`

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"7 + 5 — use any strategy." (Expected: make-ten 10 + 2 = 12, or count on.)
Strategies from Lessons 01–04 must be available before they're applied to
missing-addend puzzles.

## Teaching

Two big ideas today. First: the equal sign `=` does NOT mean "write the
answer here." It means **"is the same amount as."** Both sides balance like
a seesaw. Second: a **missing addend** (`8 + ? = 11`) is a puzzle we can
solve with the strategies we already know — count on from 8 to 11 and count
the hops.

> "8 + ? = 11. Start at 8 and count up: 9 (one hop), 10 (two hops), 11
> (three hops). Three hops — so the missing number is **3.** Check: 8 + 3 =
> 11. Both sides match."

**Key vocabulary:** *equal sign* (means "is the same amount as"),
*missing addend* (the number we don't know yet), *true* / *false* (whether
the equation balances).

## Worked example 1 — 8 + ? = 11 by counting up

The adult covers the `?` with a sticky note and builds 8 counters.

> "We need 11. Count up from 8: 9, 10, 11 — that's 3 more counters."
> *(adds 3)* "Now count everything: 11. The missing number is **3.** Check
> it: 8 + 3 = 11. Same amount on both sides — balanced."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** counting up from the known addend to the total
turns a mystery into a counting-on problem we already own (Lesson 01).

## Worked example 2 — true or false? Reading the equal sign

The adult lays out `5 + 2 = 2 + 5` with counters on each side of a drawn
equal sign.

> "Left side: 5 + 2 = 7. Right side: 2 + 5 = 7. Both sides are the same
> amount — **true.** They're turn-around pairs (Lesson 02), so of course
> they match."

Then `7 + 7 = 15`: "Left side: 7 + 7 = 14. Right side: 15. 14 is not the
same amount as 15 — **false.**"

**Reasoning to name aloud:** always compute (or reason) each side, then ask
"same amount?" The equal sign is a balance check, not a command to compute.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds.

1. `5 + ? = 13`: count up from 5. (Expected: 6, 7, …, 13 — 8 hops; the
   missing addend is 8.)
2. `6 + 6 = _`: (Expected: 12 — a double.)
3. `_ + 4 = 12`: count up from 4 to 12. (Expected: 8 hops → 8.)
4. True or false: `6 = 6`? (Expected: true — same amount, obviously!)
5. True or false: `9 = 4 + 5`? (Expected: true — 4 + 5 = 9, same amount.
   The "answer" can sit on the left; the equal sign doesn't care.)
6. Error analysis: the adult reads `4 + 1 = 5 + 2` as "four plus one equals
   five, plus two" and announces "true, because 4 + 1 is 5." Ask: "Did I
   read it right?" (Expected: no — you only checked the left side. Right
   side: 5 + 2 = 7. 5 is not the same as 7, so the equation is **false.**
   This is the standard's own example of a false equation.)

## Independent practice

The learner gets `7 + ? = 12` and `3 + 4 = 4 + 3` (true/false). They solve
the missing addend by counting up and judge the equation by comparing sides.
The adult observes silently: do they compute both sides before judging?

*Alternate response mode:* build both sides with counters on either side of
a drawn equal sign; point to the heavier/lighter side.

## Application

Mystery bag: the adult shows a bag with 6 visible marbles and says 10 are
inside total. "How many are hiding?" (`6 + ? = 10` → count up: 7, 8, 9, 10 —
4 hiding.) Missing addends are everywhere something is hidden.

## Exit check

"`9 + ? = 14`. And: is `8 + 2 = 9 + 1` true or false?" Expected: "? = 5
(10, 11, 12, 13, 14 — five hops). True: both sides are 10." Record: secure
/ developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** "is the same amount as" first, symbol `=` second.
  A balance/seesaw gesture anchors the meaning.
- **Alternate response mode:** thumbs up/down for true/false; show the hop
  count on fingers for missing addends.
- **Floor-play variant:** the learner stands on one side of a tape equal
  sign holding counters; a partner holds the other side's counters; the
  group checks the balance.

## Extension

Missing addend with a turn-around twist: `? + 9 = 14` — turn it around to
9 + ? = 14 first, then count up. Or: write your own false equation and dare
the adult to catch it.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) `=` means "the answer comes next" — the
`9 = 4 + 5` example breaks this fast; (2) judging an equation from one side
only — insist on "both sides, then compare"; (3) counting up including the
start number — same hop-count fix as Lesson 01.
