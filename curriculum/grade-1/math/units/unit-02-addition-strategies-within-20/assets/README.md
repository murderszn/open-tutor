## make-ten-ten-frames.png

- **File:** `make-ten-ten-frames.png` (PNG, raster, 1920 × 1280)
- **Prompt (verbatim):** "A flat educational illustration for a first-grade
  math lesson on the make-ten addition strategy, plain white background,
  clean simple cartoon style, high contrast. TWO ten-frames side by side
  (each ten-frame is a white rectangle divided into 2 rows of 5 empty boxes
  with thin gray outlines). LEFT ten-frame: exactly 9 large red dots (top
  row has 5 dots, bottom row has 4 dots, the last bottom-right box is empty
  and drawn with a dashed outline to highlight it). RIGHT ten-frame: exactly
  4 large blue dots in the top row only (4 dots, then one empty box; bottom
  row all 5 boxes empty). Count the dots very carefully: 9 red dots and 4
  blue dots, 13 total. A curved orange arrow goes from one of the blue dots
  in the right frame toward the dashed empty box in the left frame, showing
  one dot moving to complete the ten. All dots clearly separated and
  individually countable. No text, no numbers, no letters, no people, no
  clutter."

- **Date generated:** 2026-10-16 (first accepted version; no rejected

  [Lesson 03](../lessons/lesson-03-make-ten-with-ten-frames.md) (teaching

  the addition and the arrow's meaning). The learner counts the 9 red dots
  and 4 blue dots, traces the arrow, and reasons that moving one dot makes
  10 + 3 = 13 without changing the total.
