# Practice Sessions — Unit 02: Addition Strategies Within 20

Session-type practice, game, and review plans for the unit's 16-session
pacing. Every session opens with the Unit-01 counting retrieval warm-up
(count by tens from any decade; read/write one teen numeral).

## Session A — Counting-on games (Session 2)

**Goal:** make counting on from the bigger number automatic for sums to 15.

1. **Dice count-on:** roll two dice (1–6). Name the bigger number, count on
   the smaller. 8 rounds; the adult counts hops silently and checks.
2. **Cover the start:** the adult shows 7 counters, then covers them with a
   cloth and adds 3 more beside the cloth. "How many in all?" (Expected:
   count on from 7: 8, 9, 10.) 6 rounds, sums to 15.
3. **Hop-count check:** the learner says each hop aloud ("that's one more,
   that's two more") — the adult listens for the off-by-one error where the
   start number gets counted as a hop.

*Alternate response mode:* point to hop numbers on the number line instead
of saying them.

## Session B — Turn-around pairs (Session 6)

**Goal:** build and verify turn-around pairs with counters; choose the
easier counting direction.

1. **Bowl swap:** build 6 + 5 in two bowls, flip, name both orders and the
   total (11). 5 rounds with different pairs.
2. **Which order?** The adult shows `2 + 9` and `9 + 2` cards. "Which would
   you count? Why?" (Expected: 9 + 2 — fewer hops.)
3. **Draw the pair:** the learner draws two dot groups, then draws them
   swapped, and writes both equations (adult scribes if needed).
4. **Convince me:** the learner proves `4 + 7 = 7 + 4` to the adult with
   counters and the "nothing appeared or disappeared" argument.

## Session C — Number-line hop races (Session 3)

**Goal:** physicalize counting on; connect hops to the written equation.

1. **Floor number line:** the learner stands on the bigger addend, hops the
   smaller addend forward while the adult counts hops. Land on the total;
   write it. Sums: 8 + 3, 5 + 9, 6 + 6, 4 + 7.
2. **Hop-count journal:** after each race, the learner says "I started at
   ___, I hopped ___, I landed on ___."
3. **Race the adult:** the adult "counts on" slowly from the smaller addend
   while the learner uses the bigger-start shortcut — the shortcut should
   win every time. Debrief: fewer hops wins.

*Alternate response mode:* hop a toy car along a table number line.

## Session D — Review circles (Sessions 4, 8)

**Goal:** re-check the lessons so far; re-teach from evidence.

Structure (repeat for L1, then L1–L2):

1. Re-ask 2–3 exit checks from the lessons (see answer key).
2. Each learner task is tried orally first; written only when secure.
3. Any task missed twice becomes tomorrow's warm-up.

L1 re-checks: 9 + 2 (count on from 9 → 11); 4 + 8 (start at 8 → 12).
L2 re-checks: turn `3 + 9` around (9 + 3 = 12); is `6 + 5 = 5 + 6` true?

## Session E — Strategy choice stations, round 1 (Session 7)

**Goal:** begin choosing between count-on and turn-around deliberately.

Three stations, 5 minutes each, learner rotates:

1. **Count-on corner:** sums with a small second addend (9 + 2, 7 + 3, 8 + 4).
2. **Turn-around table:** sums written small-first (2 + 8, 3 + 9, 4 + 6) —
   flip, then count.
3. **Convince-a-friend:** the learner teaches one solved pair to a stuffed
   animal, naming the strategy used.

Debrief: "Which station felt fastest? Why?" Strategy choice, not speed, is
the point.

## Session F — Make-ten stations (Session 10)

**Goal:** make-ten becomes a choice, not just a trick; connect to the
generated illustration.

1. **Frame fill:** the adult sets a ten frame with 8 or 9 dots; the learner
   names the borrow ("8 needs 2") and completes the make-ten rewrite aloud.
   Sums: 9 + 3, 8 + 5, 9 + 6, 7 + 4.
2. **Picture study:** revisit
   [`assets/make-ten-ten-frames.png`](assets/make-ten-ten-frames.png):
   "Point to the empty box. How many dots must move? After moving, what is
   the new addition?" (Expected: 1 dot moves; 10 + 3 = 13.)
3. **Which is closer to ten?** For 7 + 5 and 8 + 6, the learner picks which
   frame to fill and explains the pick.
4. **Move-check:** after every make-ten, recount ALL dots/counters — the
   total never changes by moving.

## Session G — Doubles fluency games (Session 12)

**Goal:** instant doubles to 10 + 10; near-doubles via the smaller anchor.

1. **Doubles snap:** doubles cards (5+5 through 10+10) face down; flip, say
   the total. Keep the ones known cold; rebuild the shaky ones with counters.
2. **One more:** the adult shows 6 + 6 = 12 built in counters, then slides
   one extra counter in: "Now?" (13.) Repeat with 7 + 7 → 7 + 8, 8 + 8 → 8 + 9.
3. **Anchor hunt:** for 5 + 6, 6 + 7, 4 + 5, the learner names the smaller
   double first, then the answer.
4. **Mirror doubles:** hold a mirror (or folded paper) against a row of 7
   counters to "see" 7 + 7 = 14, then add one more for 7 + 8 = 15.

## Session H — True/false equation sorts (Session 14)

**Goal:** judge equations by comparing both sides; cement the equal-sign
meaning.

1. **Sort the cards:** equation cards sorted into TRUE and FALSE piles.
   Cards: `6 = 6` (T), `5 + 2 = 2 + 5` (T), `7 + 7 = 15` (F), `9 = 4 + 5`
   (T), `8 + 3 = 12` (F — 8 + 3 = 11), `4 + 1 = 5 + 2` (F — 5 ≠ 7),
   `10 = 10` (T), `6 + 4 = 9 + 1` (T — both 10).
2. **Fix the false ones:** for each false card, the learner changes ONE
   number to make it true and explains the fix.
3. **Balance check chant:** before judging, both sides are computed or
   reasoned aloud — "left side…, right side…, same amount?"
