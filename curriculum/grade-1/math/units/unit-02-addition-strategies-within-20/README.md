# Unit 02 — Addition Strategies Within 20

**Grade:** 1 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #10](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/10), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Adding can be smarter than counting everything from one. In this unit the
learner builds four reliable addition strategies for sums within 20 —
counting on from the bigger number, turning around the order of addends,
making a ten, and using doubles and near-doubles — and learns what the equal
sign really means by solving missing-addend equations and judging true/false
equations. Subtraction strategy work (Unit 03) builds directly on the fact
relationships made here.

## Essential question

How can we add two numbers without counting every single thing from one?

## Prerequisites

From Unit 01 (track weeks 3–6) and the diagnostic weeks:

- Counting to 120 from any starting number; reading and writing numerals to 120
- Teen numbers as a ten plus ones; tens-and-ones builds with ten frames and bundles
- Comparing numbers; the one-to-one counting routines from kindergarten

No subtraction instruction is required before this unit (subtraction facts
appear only as true/false equation partners, e.g. "7 = 8 − 1," never as
computed differences).

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Add within 20 by **counting on** from the larger addend — e.g. for
   7 + 3, start at 7 and count "8, 9, 10" to land on 10 — explaining that
   the first number is already counted (1.OA.C.5).
2. Explain and use three structure strategies: **turn-around pairs**
   (order doesn't change the total: 3 + 8 = 8 + 3), **make-ten**
   (9 + 4 = 9 + 1 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13), and **doubles/near-doubles**
   (6 + 7 = 6 + 6 + 1) to add within 20 (1.OA.B.3, 1.OA.C.6).
3. Find the missing addend in equations within 20 — `8 + ? = 11`,
   `5 + ? = 13`, `6 + 6 = _` — and check the answer by addition
   (1.OA.D.8).
4. Explain that the equal sign means "is the same amount as" (not "the answer
   goes here"), and judge whether addition equations are true or false,
   e.g. `5 + 2 = 2 + 5` (true), `7 + 7 = 15` (false) (1.OA.D.7).

Fluency target for this unit: within 10 by unit's end; within 20 through
strategy use. No timed tests.

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials
list): two ten frames per learner (print or draw; blank frames are also
created in the project), 20+ small counters in two colors, a number line to
20 (print or draw), number cards 0–20, dice or spinner cards 1–6, paper and
crayons, the generated illustration
[`assets/make-ten-ten-frames.png`](assets/make-ten-ten-frames.png).
Strategy-choice cards from [practice.md](practice.md) are reused in Week 10.
No sharp tools; supervise small parts.

## Differentiation (every session)

- **Oral/pointing response modes** for all checks; adult scribes written work
  when the learner isn't ready to write independently.
- Learners may answer with counters or drawings instead of written numerals at
  any point; the strategy explanation matters more than the recording.
- Extensions: write the equation for every strategy used; find two different
  strategies for the same sum; near-doubles beyond 10 + 10.
