# Project — My Addition Strategy Book

**Unit:** [U02](README.md) · **Runs across Sessions 9, 12, 14, 15**
**Type:** individual book-making project (oral/drawing/manipulative; adult
scribes written words when the learner isn't ready to write)

## The job

The learner authors a 5-page **Addition Strategy Book** — a real book with a
cover and one page per strategy — that teaches a younger friend (or a
stuffed animal) how to add. Each strategy page shows an original example the
learner invented, a drawing or counter arrangement of that example, and an
explanation in the learner's own words of why the strategy works.

## Materials

- 5 sheets of paper (or folded booklet), crayons/markers
- Counters and a ten frame for planning each page
- Stapler or binder clip (adult operates)

## Parts

### Part 1 — Cover and count-on page (Session 9)

- **Cover:** book title, "by [learner]" (adult may scribe the name), one
  drawing of adding.
- **Page 1 — Count on:** the learner picks their own sum (within 15), draws
  the bigger group and the counted-on hops (arrows on a number line, or dots
  added one by one), and explains: "I start at the bigger number because…"

### Part 2 — Turn-around and make-ten pages (Session 12)

- **Page 2 — Turn-around pair:** the learner's own pair (e.g., 4 + 7 and
  7 + 4), drawn both ways, with the explanation "the total stays the same
  because…"
- **Page 3 — Make ten:** the learner's own sum (one addend 7–9), drawn on a
  ten frame with the "moving dot" arrow, and the rewrite "___ + ___ is
  10 + ___ = ___."

### Part 3 — Doubles and equal-sign pages (Session 14)

- **Page 4 — Doubles:** the learner's own double and its near-double
  (e.g., 6 + 6 = 12, so 6 + 7 = 13), drawn as two rows plus one extra.
- **Page 5 — The equal sign:** the learner draws a balance/seesaw with the
  same amount on both sides and writes (or dictates) what `=` means:
  "is the same amount as." Includes one true equation and one false
  equation of their own invention, labeled.

### Part 4 — Strategy showcase (Session 15)

The learner "reads" the book to the adult (or a small audience), pointing to
each page and explaining the strategy. The adult asks one question per page:
"Why does this strategy work?" and "When would you use it?" The learner's
answers — not reading fluency — are what count.

## Deliverables

1. A 5-page book: cover + 4 strategy pages + equal-sign page
   (5 pages total including cover).
2. One original invented example per strategy page (not copied from lessons).
3. An oral explanation of each strategy at the showcase.

## Rubric (adult-scored; share the language with the learner in advance)

| Criterion | Strong (3) | Developing (2) | Starting (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strategy accuracy** | Every page's example is computed correctly and the drawing matches the numbers | 1–2 pages have a number/drawing mismatch, corrected with help | Several mismatches; learner re-builds with counters before finishing |
| **Explanation** | Each page explains *why* the strategy works in the learner's own words | Some pages explain; others only show the answer | Pages show answers without explanations; adult re-asks "why?" |
| **Equal-sign page** | True/false equations labeled correctly; `=` explained as "same amount" | Equations correct but explanation is "the answer goes here"-flavored | `=` treated only as "write the answer"; re-teach with balance demo |
| **Showcase** | Names the strategy and when to use it for all 5 pages | Names strategies but can't say when to use 1–2 of them | Reads pages without naming strategies; adult prompts each one |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = ready to move on; 7–9 = one re-teach session on the
weakest page, then re-showcase; below 7 = revisit Lessons 01–05 practice
sessions before Unit 03.

## Differentiation

- Adult scribes all dictated words; the learner's job is the math thinking.
- Pages may be built with glued-down counters or stickers instead of drawn.
- A learner who finishes early adds a "Which strategy is fastest?" page
  comparing two strategies on the same sum.
