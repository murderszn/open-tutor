# Lesson 02 — The Missing Start and the Missing Change

**Student goal:** I can find the number a story hides — the start or
the change — by using the number I do know.
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Unit:** [U04](../README.md) · **Session 5 of 16**

## Materials

- 20 counters in two colors, a ten frame, a number line to 20
- The story-map diagram
  [`assets/story-map.svg`](../assets/story-map.svg): start → change →
  result, with the `?` moving to each position

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Result-unknown, quick: 9 + 6." The learner: 15. "12 − 5." The learner:
7. Then: "? + 4 = 13 — what is the missing number?" The learner counts
up from 4 or subtracts: 13 − 4 = 9. Missing-number equations from
Unit 03 are the engine of this lesson; if they are shaky, re-run the
Unit 03 missing-number sort before continuing.

## Teaching

Yesterday's stories told us the start and the change and asked for the
result. Today the story hides a different part. The story-map shows the
three parts — **start**, **change**, **result** — and the `?` can sit in
any of them. The rule never changes: act out what you know, and let the
missing part show itself.

> "Listen: 'Sam had some marbles. He found 4 more. Now he has 13. How
> many marbles did Sam have at the start?' The start is hidden. Act
> what we know: the change is 4, the result is 13. If 4 joined to make
> 13, the start must be 13 − 4 = **9.** Check: 9 + 4 = 13. The check
> tells the same story forward."

**Key vocabulary:** *start-unknown* (the beginning number is hidden),
*change-unknown* (the joining or leaving number is hidden),
*result-unknown* (the ending number is hidden, from Lesson 01).

**Why the inverse operation appears (say it aloud):** when the start
is hidden in an add-to story, we undo the joining — subtract the change
from the result. When the change is hidden, we ask "what gets me from
the start to the result?" — and that question is answered by
subtracting too, or by counting up. The story decides; the equation
follows the story, not the other way around.

## Worked example 1 — Start-unknown add-to: ? + 4 = 13, so ? = 9

> "Sam had some marbles. He found 4 more. Now he has 13. How many did
> he have at the start? The `?` is the start. Build the result: 13
> counters. Take the 4 that joined back off — 9 are left. Those 9 were
> the start. Equation: ? + 4 = 13, and ? = **9.** Check forward:
> 9 + 4 = 13."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** we did not add 13 + 4. The 4 is already
inside the 13. Taking it back out shows what was there before.

## Worked example 2 — Change-unknown take-from: 11 − ? = 5, so ? = 6

> "Lena had 11 stickers. She gave some to a friend. Now she has 5.
> How many stickers did she give away? The `?` is the change. Build
> the start: 11 counters. Take away until only 5 are left — count what
> you took: 6. Equation: 11 − ? = 5, and ? = **6.** Check: 11 − 6 = 5."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** "gave some away" means take-from, and the
missing piece is how many left. Counting up from 5 to 11 (6, 7, 8, 9,
10, 11 — six hops) gives the same answer: the unknown-addend move from
Unit 03, now inside a story.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds. For each, name which part is hidden before solving.

1. "Ana had some crayons. She got 6 more. Now she has 14. How many
   crayons did Ana have at the start?" (Expected: start-unknown;
   ? + 6 = 14, ? = 8; check 8 + 6 = 14.)
2. "Ben had 9 blocks. He got some more. Now he has 17. How many blocks
   did Ben get?" (Expected: change-unknown add-to; 9 + ? = 17,
   ? = 8; check 9 + 8 = 17.)
3. "Mia had some cookies. She ate 3. Now she has 12 left. How many
   cookies did Mia have at the start?" (Expected: start-unknown
   take-from; ? − 3 = 12, ? = 15; check 15 − 3 = 12.)
4. "There were 16 apples in a basket. Some were eaten. Now there are
   9 left. How many apples were eaten?" (Expected: change-unknown
   take-from; 16 − ? = 9, ? = 7; check 16 − 7 = 9.)
5. Error analysis: the adult solves "? + 5 = 12" by computing 12 + 5 =
   17 and announcing the start was 17. Ask: "Does the check work?"
   (Expected: no — 17 + 5 = 22, not 12. The start must be smaller than
   the result when something joined: 12 − 5 = 7, and 7 + 5 = 12.)
6. The learner models "Some birds were on a branch. 6 flew away. Now
   there are 8 left. How many birds were on the branch at the start?"
   alone with counters while the adult watches.
   (Expected: start-unknown take-from; ? − 6 = 8, ? = 14;
   check 14 − 6 = 8.)

## Independent practice

The learner gets two fresh stories, names the hidden part, acts each
out, and writes the equation. The adult observes silently: do they
name the hidden part before computing?

1. "Kai had some pencils. He found 7 more. Now he has 15. How many
   pencils did Kai have at the start?" (Expected: start-unknown;
   ? + 7 = 15, ? = 8; check 8 + 7 = 15.)
2. "There were 13 fish in a tank. Some swam away. Now there are 4
   left. How many fish swam away?" (Expected: change-unknown;
   13 − ? = 4, ? = 9; check 13 − 9 = 4.)

*Alternate response mode:* cover the hidden part with a cloth — the
learner lifts the cloth only after the equation is written.

## Application

Button jar: "A jar had some buttons. 5 buttons were added. Now the jar
has 18 buttons. How many buttons were in the jar at the start?" The
learner names the hidden part, acts it out, writes the equation, and
checks it forward. (Expected: start-unknown; ? + 5 = 18, ? = 13;
check 13 + 5 = 18.)

## Exit check

"Kai had some pencils. He gave away 7. Now he has 6 left. How many
pencils did Kai have at the start? Tell me which part is hidden, then
solve it." Expected: "The start is hidden. ? − 7 = 6, so ? = 13,
because 6 + 7 = 13. Check: 13 − 7 = 6." Record: secure / developing /
not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** "some" in a story is the signal that a number
  is hidden — teach the learner to point at the word "some" and say
  "that's my `?`."
- **Alternate response mode:** draw three boxes — start, change,
  result — and put a `?` in the hidden box before touching counters.
- **Floor-play variant:** three floor mats labeled START, CHANGE,
  RESULT; the learner stands on the `?` mat and says what is missing.

## Extension

A start-unknown story where the change is bigger than the result's
tens: "? − 9 = 11" ("Some stickers, 9 given away, 11 left") — ? = 20,
the top of the unit's range, solved by 11 + 9 = 20. Or: invent a
change-unknown story whose answer is 0 ("had 8, gave some away, 8
left — gave away 0") and defend it.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) adding the two visible numbers no
matter which part is hidden — always ask "is the `?` bigger or smaller
than the result?"; (2) in start-unknown take-from stories, subtracting
instead of adding (the start is the whole — it must be bigger than
what is left); (3) confusing change-unknown with result-unknown when
the story says "some" twice — re-read and point at each "some."
