# Assets — Unit 04: Story Problems

## `apple-basket-story.png` (generated raster illustration)

- **Prompt:** "Simple flat children's picture-book illustration for a
  first-grade math lesson, top-down view on a plain light background:
  a woven basket holding exactly 8 shiny red apples on the left, and a
  small neat pile of exactly 5 green apples on the right, apples
  clearly separated and easy to count, no text, no numbers, no
  letters, no people, bright friendly colors."
- **Generation tool:** Muse built-in image generation
  (`media.generate_image`), 2026-10-29.
- **Learning purpose:** Lesson 03 picture activity — one picture
  serving three story types (put-together total, compare difference,
  take-apart missing part) from the same two groups.
- **Factual review (inspected 2026-10-29 before linking):** the image
  shows exactly 8 red apples in the basket (rows of 3, 2, 3) and
  exactly 5 green apples in the pile (rows of 2, 2, 1). No text,
  numbers, or people appear. Counts verified by direct inspection;
  the lesson's questions use these verified counts (8 + 5 = 13;
  8 − 5 = 3; 13 − 8 = 5).
- **Caption:** "A basket of 8 red apples and a pile of 5 green
  apples — count each group, then answer the three questions."
- **Alt text:** "A woven basket holding 8 red apples, with 5 green
  apples piled beside it."
- **Text-only alternative (in Lesson 03):** "There are 8 red apples
  in a woven basket on the left, and 5 green apples in a pile on the
  right. No apples are hidden."
- **Known limitations:** AI illustration, not a photograph; apple
  counts are the teaching content and were verified, but the image
  must not be reused with different numbers. If the picture is ever
  replaced, Lesson 03's questions must be re-checked against the new
  counts.

## `story-map.svg` (deterministic diagram, not a generated image)

- **Purpose:** Lesson 02 visual — the three parts of a story
  (start → change → result) with the `?` able to sit in any position.
  Drawn as reproducible SVG text/boxes so positions are exact; it
  supplements, and does not replace, the generated illustration above.
- **Factual review:** a schematic, not a measurement, map, or data
  chart — no quantities are depicted, so no count verification applies.
- **Text alternative (in Lesson 02):** "Three boxes in a row: START,
  CHANGE, RESULT, with arrows between them. The question mark can sit
  in any one of the three boxes."
