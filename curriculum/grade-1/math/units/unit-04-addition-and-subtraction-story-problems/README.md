# Unit 04 — Addition and Subtraction Story Problems

**Grade:** 1 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #10](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/10), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Units 02 and 03 built the tools: addition strategies, then subtraction
strategies. This unit asks the question those tools were for: *what is
this story asking, and which tool answers it?* The learner meets every
situation type in 1.OA.A.1 — add to, take from, put together, take apart,
and compare — with the unknown in every position, plus three-addend
stories (1.OA.A.2). The hard part of this unit is not computing; it is
reading the story, acting it out, and naming what is missing before
choosing an operation. A learner who can retell a story with counters and
write the matching equation has the skill; a learner who only hunts for
"key words" does not, and this unit teaches against key-word hunting
from the first lesson.

## Essential question

How do we know whether a story wants us to add or subtract — and what
is the story not telling us yet?

## Prerequisites

From Unit 02 (track weeks 7–10) and Unit 03 (track weeks 11–14):

- Adding within 20 by counting on, turn-around pairs, make-ten, and
  doubles/near-doubles; fluency within 10
- Subtracting within 20 by unknown-addend, count back/count up,
  back-to-ten, and fact families; fluency within 10
- Missing-addend and missing-subtrahend/minuend equations
  (`8 + ? = 11`, `? − 3 = 12`, `11 − ? = 5`) and judging true/false
  equations; the equal sign means "is the same amount as"
- Ten frames, number lines to 20, counters in two colors

No new computation strategy is introduced here. Every answer is found
with a Unit 02/03 strategy; the new learning is *modeling* — turning a
story into counters, a drawing, and an equation.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Solve **add-to and take-from result-unknown** stories within 20 by
   retelling the action and writing the matching equation (1.OA.A.1).
2. Solve **start-unknown and change-unknown** add-to and take-from
   stories within 20, explaining the missing part with counters or a
   drawing (1.OA.A.1, 1.OA.D.8).
3. Solve **put-together/take-apart** stories (total unknown, addend
   unknown, both addends unknown) and **compare** stories (difference
   unknown, bigger unknown, smaller unknown), choosing addition or
   subtraction and justifying the choice (1.OA.A.1).
4. Solve **three-addend** stories (sum ≤ 20) by grouping two numbers
   first — especially a pair that makes ten — and explaining the
   grouping (1.OA.A.2, 1.OA.B.3).
5. Judge whether an **equation matches a story** and whether an
   equation is **true or false**, and **write a story** for a given
   equation, with a matching drawing or counters (1.OA.A.1, 1.OA.D.7,
   1.OA.D.8).

Fluency target for this unit: no new fluency target — maintain
within-10 fluency and within-20 strategy use from Units 02–03. No timed
tests. A story answered with the wrong operation but correct arithmetic
is a modeling error, not a computation error; re-teach the story, not
the fact.

## Vocabulary (taught with objects first, word second)

story problem · add to · take from · put together · take apart ·
compare · how many more · how many fewer · in all · left · start ·
change · result · unknown · missing number · equation · matches ·
true · false · group · the same amount as

"Key words" are never taught as a rule: *more* appears in both addition
stories ("5 more join") and subtraction stories ("how many more does
Ben have?"), and this unit proves that in Lessons 03 and 05.

## Differentiation (every session)

- **Language access:** every story is acted out with counters before
  it is written as an equation. "How many more" and "how many fewer"
  are taught by matching two rows of counters, not by memorizing which
  word means which operation.
- **Alternate response modes:** retell the story aloud, point to hops
  on the number line, or draw dots instead of writing equations; the
  adult scribes written work when the learner isn't ready to write.
- **Support:** keep every new situation type inside 15 until its
  modeling is fluent, then extend to 20; a ten frame and a number line
  stay on the table all unit. Learners who grab numbers and compute
  without retelling re-run Lesson 01's act-it-out routine before new
  situation types are added.
- **Extension:** two-step stories (e.g. "9 + 5, then 3 are eaten — how
  many now?"), start-unknown compare stories, and three-addend stories
  where no pair makes ten, as enrichment only — never as a gate for
  moving on.
- **Error-analysis habit:** every lesson includes one adult-made
  mistake for the learner to catch; catching the mistake counts as
  evidence, not as a trick.
