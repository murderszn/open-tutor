# Practice Sessions — Unit 04: Story Problems

Each session is 20–25 minutes and opens with a Units 01–03 retrieval
warm-up (one counting or strategy flash). Every task is solved in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md#practice-sessions). Stories use invented
names and everyday objects only; no real learner information appears.

## Session A — Act-it-out stories (Session 2; game round in Session 3)

Read each story aloud. The learner acts it out with counters, says what
happened, then writes the equation. No equation before acting.

1. "Mia has 5 ribbons. Her friend gives her 8 more. How many ribbons
   does Mia have now?" (5 + 8 = 13.)
2. "There are 16 ants on a log. 7 crawl away. How many ants are left?"
   (16 − 7 = 9.)
3. "Ben has 9 toy trucks. He gets 9 more. How many toy trucks in all?"
   (9 + 9 = 18.)
4. "There are 13 apples in a basket. 6 are eaten. How many apples are
   left?" (13 − 6 = 7.)
5. "Sara has 7 hair clips. She buys 6 more. How many hair clips does
   Sara have now?" (7 + 6 = 13.)
6. "There are 18 fish in a pond. 9 swim away. How many fish are left?"
   (18 − 9 = 9.)

**Game round (Session 3 — Story relay):** two teams of counters (or
one learner racing the adult's timer). The adult reads a story; the
learner acts it and calls the equation before the timer runs out.
Swap roles: the learner reads, the adult acts — and the learner must
catch any acting mistake. Five stories from the list above, then five
fresh ones the adult invents on the spot (same range, result-unknown
only).

## Session B — Start/change detective cards (Session 6)

Each card hides one part. The learner names the hidden part (start or
change), writes the equation with `?`, solves it, and checks it
forward. Cut these into cards or read them aloud.

1. "Sam had some marbles. He found 3 more. Now he has 11. How many
   marbles did Sam have at the start?" (? + 3 = 11, ? = 8.)
2. "There were 12 cookies. Some were eaten. Now there are 8 left. How
   many cookies were eaten?" (12 − ? = 8, ? = 4.)
3. "Mia had some stickers. She gave away 5. Now she has 9 left. How
   many stickers did Mia have at the start?" (? − 5 = 9, ? = 14.)
4. "Ben had 7 blocks. He got some more. Now he has 16. How many blocks
   did Ben get?" (7 + ? = 16, ? = 9.)
5. "Ana had some crayons. She got 8 more. Now she has 17. How many
   crayons did Ana have at the start?" (? + 8 = 17, ? = 9.)
6. "There were 15 birds. Some flew away. Now there are 6 left. How
   many birds flew away?" (15 − ? = 6, ? = 9.)

Detective rule, posted on the table: "If the start is hidden in an
add-to story, subtract. If the start is hidden in a take-from story,
add. If the change is hidden, count up or subtract — then check."

## Session C — Situation sort (Session 7)

Read each story. The learner sorts it into **add-to**, **take-from**,
**put-together/take-apart**, or **compare**, then solves it. The sort
matters more than the answer: a right answer in the wrong pile is a
re-sort, not a pass.

1. "There are 8 red balls and 7 blue balls in a box. How many balls
   are in the box in all?" (Put-together; 8 + 7 = 15.)
2. "Lena had 14 stickers. She gave 6 to a friend. How many stickers
   does Lena have left?" (Take-from; 14 − 6 = 8.)
3. "Tom has 17 marbles. Sam has 9 marbles. How many more marbles does
   Tom have than Sam?" (Compare; 17 − 9 = 8.)
4. "Some ducks are on a pond. 5 more ducks join. Now there are 13
   ducks. How many ducks were on the pond at the start?"
   (Add-to, start-unknown; ? + 5 = 13, ? = 8.)
5. "There are 15 children at a party. 6 are wearing hats. The rest are
   not. How many children are not wearing hats?"
   (Take-apart; 15 − 6 = 9.)
6. "Ana has 7 dolls. Ben has 4 more dolls than Ana. How many dolls
   does Ben have?" (Compare, bigger unknown; 7 + 4 = 11.)

## Session D — Review circles (Sessions 4 and 8)

Two rounds of mixed re-checks. Round 1 (Session 4) revisits Lesson 01;
round 2 (Session 8) revisits Lessons 01–02. The learner answers orally
or with counters; the adult records secure / developing / not yet per
item.

Round 1 (Session 4):

1. "8 + 7 = ?" (15.)
2. "14 − 5 = ?" (9.)
3. "There are 10 pencils. Mia gets 5 more. How many pencils now?"
   (10 + 5 = 15.)
4. "There are 12 grapes. Ben eats 4. How many grapes are left?"
   (12 − 4 = 8.)
5. "9 + 6 = ?" (15.)
6. "17 − 8 = ?" (9.)

Round 2 (Session 8):

1. "? + 6 = 15. What is the missing number?" (9.)
2. "17 − ? = 10. What is the missing number?" (7.)
3. "Some fish are in a tank. 4 more are added. Now there are 16. How
   many fish were in the tank at the start?" (? + 4 = 16, ? = 12.)
4. "There were 18 apples. Some were eaten. Now there are 11 left. How
   many apples were eaten?" (18 − ? = 11, ? = 7.)
5. "6 + 9 = ?" (15.)
6. "15 − 7 = ?" (8.)

## Session E — Compare language games (Session 10)

"More" and "fewer" are matching questions. Build both rows, match them,
count the leftover — every time.

1. "Ben has 19 blocks. Ana has 12 blocks. How many more blocks does
   Ben have than Ana?" (19 − 12 = 7.)
2. "Mia has 11 ribbons. Zoe has 17 ribbons. How many fewer ribbons
   does Mia have than Zoe?" (17 − 11 = 6.)
3. "Sam has 9 cars. Leo has 4 more cars than Sam. How many cars does
   Leo have?" (9 + 4 = 13.)
4. "Leo has 16 cars. He has 5 more cars than Sam. How many cars does
   Sam have?" (16 − 5 = 11.)
5. "There are 14 red cups and 8 blue cups. How many fewer blue cups
   are there than red cups?" (14 − 8 = 6.)
6. Trap card (adult reads, learner judges): "Kim has 13 shells. She
   has 3 more shells than Pat. Pat has 13 + 3 = 16 shells." Is that
   right? (No — Kim's number is the bigger one and it is given, so
   Pat's is smaller: 13 − 3 = 10. "More than" with the bigger number
   known means subtract.)

## Session F — Three-addend grouping games (Session 12)

Circle the ten pair first, then add the third number. Write both steps.

1. "4 + 6 + 5" (4 + 6 = 10, + 5 = 15.)
2. "8 + 3 + 7" (3 + 7 = 10, + 8 = 18.)
3. "9 + 9 + 2" (9 + 9 = 18, + 2 = 20 — no ten pair; doubles first.)
4. "1 + 9 + 6" (1 + 9 = 10, + 6 = 16.)
5. "5 + 4 + 5" (5 + 5 = 10, + 4 = 14.)
6. "7 + 7 + 6" (7 + 7 = 14, + 6 = 20 — doubles first, then add on.)

Then one story, acted with three piles: "A picnic basket has 3 apples,
7 bananas, and 6 oranges. How many pieces of fruit in all?"
(3 + 7 = 10, + 6 = 16.)

## Session G — Equation-match game (Session 14)

For each story, the adult offers an equation. The learner verdicts
**fits** or **does not fit**, and if it fits, **true** or **false**.
Both questions, every time.

1. Story: "There are 8 ducks. 7 more ducks join." Offered: 8 + 7 = 15.
   (Fits; true.)
2. Story: "There are 15 stickers. 6 are given away." Offered:
   15 + 6 = 21. (Does not fit — the action is take-from; fitting
   equation 15 − 6 = 9.)
3. Story: "Mia has 12 pens. Jon has 9 pens. How many more pens does
   Mia have?" Offered: 12 − 9 = 3. (Fits; true.)
4. Story: "A tray has 4 muffins, 6 cookies, and 5 brownies. How many
   treats in all?" Offered: 4 + 6 + 5 = 14. (Does not fit — the
   arithmetic is wrong: 4 + 6 = 10, + 5 = 15. Right operation, false
   equation; corrected: 4 + 6 + 5 = 15.)
5. Story: "Some marbles are in a bag. 6 more are added. Now there are
   14." Offered: 8 + 6 = 14. (Fits; true — the start was 8.)
6. Story: "There are 17 crayons. 10 are blue. The rest are red."
   Offered: 17 + 10 = 27. (Does not fit — the 10 is part of the 17,
   not added to it; fitting equation 17 − 10 = 7.)

True/false lightning round (no story): 10 + 10 = 20 (true); 13 − 8 = 4
(false — 13 − 8 = 5); 6 + 9 = 15 (true); 17 − 8 = 10 (false — 17 − 8 = 9).

## Session H — Write-your-own workshop (Session 15, with the project)

The learner writes one story of each family below (or dictates it for
the adult to scribe), acts it out, and writes the equation. Sample
stories are in the answer key; any story with matching action,
numbers, and answer counts.

1. An add-to story with a result unknown.
2. A take-from story with a start unknown.
3. A compare story with a difference unknown.
4. A three-addend story whose sum is 20 or less.

Workshop rule: read the story back against the equation, number by
number, before calling it done. Stories that fail the read-back get
revised, not graded down — revision is the skill.
