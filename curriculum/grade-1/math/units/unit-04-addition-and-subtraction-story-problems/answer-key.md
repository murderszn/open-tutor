# Answer Key — Unit 04: Story Problems

**For the guiding adult only.** Every item below was solved
independently of the lesson text and reconciled with it; strategies
shown are the unit's strategies (make-ten, back-to-ten, count up,
doubles, fact families). Open responses accept any story whose action,
numbers, and answer match the equation.

## Lesson 01

**Readiness:** 9 + 4 = 13 (9 + 1 = 10, + 3 = 13); 14 − 6 = 8
(14 − 4 = 10, − 2 = 8).

**Worked examples:** (1) 7 + 5 = 12 — check 7 + 3 = 10, + 2 = 12.
(2) 14 − 6 = 8 — check 14 − 4 = 10, − 2 = 8.

**Guided practice:**

1. 9 + 4 = 13 (9 + 1 = 10, + 3 = 13).
2. 12 − 5 = 7 (12 − 2 = 10, − 3 = 7).
3. 8 + 8 = 16 (double).
4. 17 − 9 = 8 (17 − 7 = 10, − 2 = 8).
5. Error: 6 + 9 = 15, not 14 (6 + 4 = 10, + 5 = 15). The acting was
   right; the computation was wrong — always check a second way.
6. 11 − 4 = 7 (11 − 1 = 10, − 3 = 7).

**Independent practice:** (1) 6 + 7 = 13 (6 + 4 = 10, + 3 = 13).
(2) 15 − 8 = 7 (15 − 5 = 10, − 3 = 7).

**Application:** 9 + 6 = 15 (9 + 1 = 10, + 5 = 15).

**Exit check:** 8 + 5 = 13 (8 + 2 = 10, + 3 = 13).

## Lesson 03

**Readiness:** 13 − 8 = 5 (13 − 3 = 10, − 5 = 5); 8 + 5 = 13
(8 + 2 = 10, + 3 = 13).

**Worked examples:** (1) 9 + 6 = 15 (9 + 1 = 10, + 5 = 15).
(2) 13 − 8 = 5 (match 8 pairs, 5 left over; count up 8 → 13, five hops).

**Picture activity (8 red apples, 5 green apples):**
(1) 8 + 5 = 13 in all. (2) 8 − 5 = 3 more red than green.
(3) 13 − 8 = 5 green — matches the picture.

**Guided practice:**

1. Take-apart; 16 − 9 = 7 blue blocks (16 − 6 = 10, − 3 = 7).
2. Compare, bigger unknown; 8 + 5 = 13 cars.
3. Compare, smaller unknown; 13 − 5 = 8 cars. Note the contrast with
   round 2: same "5 more" words, different unknown, different
   operation.
4. Take-apart, both addends unknown; any two pairs summing to 14, e.g.
   7 + 7, 9 + 5, 10 + 4.
5. Error: 12 + 7 = 19 is wrong. Matching gives 12 − 7 = 5 (12 − 2 = 10,
   − 5 = 5). Adding counts Jon's 7 twice.
6. Compare; 15 − 9 = 6 pages (15 − 5 = 10, − 4 = 6).

**Independent practice:** (1) Take-apart; 17 − 10 = 7 red crayons.
(2) Compare; 11 − 6 = 5 fewer dolls.

**Exit check:** 18 − 11 = 7 more marbles (match 11 pairs, 7 left over).

## Lesson 04

**Readiness:** 6 + 4 = 10; 10 + 5 = 15; 2 + 6 + 4 = 12
(6 + 4 = 10, + 2 = 12).

**Worked examples:** (1) 6 + 5 + 4 = 15 — group 6 + 4 = 10, + 5 = 15;
check left to right 6 + 5 = 11, + 4 = 15. (2) 3 + 7 + 8 = 18 — group
3 + 7 = 10, + 8 = 18; check 7 + 8 = 15, + 3 = 18.

**Guided practice:**

1. 5 + 5 + 6 = 16 (5 + 5 = 10, + 6 = 16).
2. 2 + 9 + 8 = 19 (2 + 8 = 10, + 9 = 19).
3. 4 + 7 + 3 = 14 (7 + 3 = 10, + 4 = 14).
4. 9 + 1 + 7 = 17 (9 + 1 = 10, + 7 = 17).
5. Error: 11 + 4 = 15, not 14 — a counting slip. Grouping first
   avoids it: 6 + 4 = 10, + 5 = 15.
6. 8 + 2 + 9 = 19 (8 + 2 = 10, + 9 = 19).

**Independent practice:** (1) 4 + 8 + 6 = 18 (4 + 6 = 10, + 8 = 18).
(2) 2 + 6 + 10 = 18 (2 + 6 = 8, + 10 = 18; or 10 + 6 = 16, + 2 = 18).

**Application:** 5 + 5 + 9 = 19 (5 + 5 = 10, + 9 = 19).

**Exit check:** 7 + 6 + 3 = 16 (7 + 3 = 10, + 6 = 16).

## Lesson 05

**Readiness:** 9 + 4 = 13; 9 − 4 = 5 — same numbers, different stories.

**Worked examples:** (1) 9 + 4 = 13 does not fit the fish story
(swim away = take-from); fitting equation 9 − 4 = 5. (2) 7 + 6 = 13
is true (7 + 3 = 10, + 3 = 13); 12 − 5 = 8 is false — 12 − 5 = 7
(12 − 2 = 10, − 3 = 7).

**Guided practice:**

1. Does not fit — giving means add-to; fitting equation 10 + 3 = 13.
   (The offered 10 − 3 = 7 is true arithmetic, which is why the fit
   check comes first.)
2. 8 + 7 = 15 true (8 + 2 = 10, + 5 = 15); 16 − 9 = 6 false —
   16 − 9 = 7 (16 − 6 = 10, − 3 = 7).
3. Fits — start was 9; 9 + 5 = 14 is true.
4. Any take-from story with start 11, change 3, result 8, e.g.
   "There were 11 cookies. Mia ate 3. Now there are 8 left."
5. Error: 6 + 6 = 11 is false — doubles give 6 + 6 = 12.
6. 14 − 7 = 7 true (14 − 4 = 10, − 3 = 7); story for 5 + 9 = 14, e.g.
   "There are 5 red balloons and 9 blue balloons. How many balloons
   in all? 14."

**Independent practice:** (1) 13 − 6 = 7 fits; 13 + 6 = 19 is true
arithmetic that tells the wrong story. (2) 9 + 8 = 17 true
(9 + 1 = 10, + 7 = 17); 15 − 6 = 10 false — 15 − 6 = 9
(15 − 5 = 10, − 1 = 9).

**Application:** Any add-to or put-together story with parts 12 and 5
and total 17, e.g. "There are 12 crayons in a box. Mia adds 5 more.
How many crayons are there now? 17."

**Exit check:** False. 11 − 4 = 7 (11 − 1 = 10, − 3 = 7), so the sides
are 7 and 8 — not the same amount.
