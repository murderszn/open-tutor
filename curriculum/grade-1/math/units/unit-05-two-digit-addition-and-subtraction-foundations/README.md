# Unit 05 — Two-Digit Addition and Subtraction Foundations

**Grade:** 1 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 19–22
**Backlog:** [issue #10](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/10), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Units 01–03 taught the learner to read a two-digit number as tens and
ones, and Units 02–03 taught addition and subtraction within 20. This
unit finally puts those two powers together: add a one-digit number to a
two-digit number, add a multiple of ten, jump ten more or ten less in
the head, and subtract one multiple of ten from another. The rule that
runs through every lesson is short enough to say aloud: **tens go with
tens, ones go with ones — and when the ones reach ten, they trade for
one new ten.**

Two-digit + two-digit addition (for example 34 + 27) is **not** taught
here. The grade-1 standard covers a two-digit number plus a one-digit
number and a two-digit number plus a multiple of ten; two-digit +
two-digit work is a later grade's job and appears in this unit only as
optional enrichment with models, never as a gate for moving on.

## Essential question

How do tens and ones tell us what changes — and what stays the same —
when we add or subtract?

## Prerequisites

From Unit 01 (track weeks 3–6), Unit 02 (weeks 7–10), and Unit 03
(weeks 11–14):

- Two-digit numbers as tens and ones: teen numbers as a ten plus ones,
  decade numbers as groups of ten with zero ones
- Counting to 120, counting by tens, and the hundred chart (one row
  down is ten more, one row up is ten less)
- Addition within 20 by counting on, make-ten, doubles, and fact
  families; fluency within 10
- Subtraction within 20 by unknown-addend, count back / count up, and
  back-to-ten; the equal sign means "is the same amount as"
- Story problems of every situation type (Unit 04, in draft) are
  **not** required here: this unit's problems are bare-number equations
  and one-sentence situations, so the place-value reasoning stays in
  focus.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. **Add a two-digit number and a one-digit number within 100** using
   tens/ones models or drawings — without regrouping when the ones
   stay below ten, and by composing a ten when they reach ten or more
   (for example 27 + 5 = 32) (1.NBT.C.4).
2. **Add a two-digit number and a multiple of ten within 100**,
   explaining that the tens change and the ones stay the same (for
   example 34 + 20 = 54) (1.NBT.C.4).
3. **Mentally find 10 more or 10 less** than a two-digit number,
   without counting, and explain that only the tens digit changes
   (1.NBT.C.5).
4. **Subtract multiples of ten** (10–90) from multiples of ten
   (10–90), with positive or zero differences, using tens models or
   drawings (for example 70 − 30 = 40, 50 − 50 = 0) (1.NBT.C.6).
5. **Relate a model or drawing to a written method and explain the
   reasoning**, including checking a subtraction with addition (for
   example 40 + 30 = 70, so 70 − 30 = 40) (1.NBT.C.4, 1.NBT.C.6).

Fluency target for this unit: two-digit + one-digit and two-digit +
multiple-of-ten problems solved by place-value reasoning, not by
counting from one. No timed tests.

## Vocabulary (taught with objects first, word second)

tens · ones · tens rod · ones cube · decade number (10, 20, 30, …) ·
compose a ten · trade ten ones for one ten · multiple of ten · 10 more ·
10 less · hundred chart · row · written method · check · the same amount
as · equation

The word *regrouping* is adult vocabulary for composing a ten; the
learner may say "make a new ten" and still meet every objective.

## Differentiation (every session)

- **Language access:** teach each move with rods and cubes first,
  then name it. "Make a new ten" comes before *compose* or
  *regrouping*; "the tens stay the same" is always acceptable for
  "the tens digit is unchanged."
- **Alternate response modes:** point to rods/cubes, move them, or
  draw bars and dots instead of writing equations; the adult scribes
  written work when the learner isn't ready to write.
- **Support:** keep every new move inside 60 until its steps are
  fluent, then extend to 100; a hundred chart and tens/ones trays stay
  on the table all unit. Learners who still count from one re-run
  Unit 01's bundle games before new moves are added.
- **Extension:** two-digit + two-digit problems with models only
  (for example 34 + 27 by tens and ones, no written algorithm required),
  10 more/less across 100 (90 → 100 → 110 on the hundred chart), and
  "invent a problem where the ones exactly make ten" — enrichment
  only, never a gate for moving on.
- **Error-analysis habit:** every lesson includes one adult-made
  mistake for the learner to catch; catching the mistake counts as
  evidence, not as a trick.
