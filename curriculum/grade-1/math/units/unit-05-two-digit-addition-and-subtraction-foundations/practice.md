# Practice Sessions — Unit 05: Two-Digit Addition and Subtraction Foundations

Session-type practice, game, and review plans for the unit's 16-session
pacing. Every session opens with the Units 01–03 retrieval warm-up
(count by tens from any decade; one within-20 strategy flash; one
teen-number decomposition).

## Session A — Add-ones trays (Session 2)

**Goal:** make "tens stay the same, ones change" automatic for
two-digit + one-digit problems with no new ten.

1. **Build and add:** the adult calls a number; the learner builds it
   and adds the ones. Problems: 33 + 4 (3 tens, 3 ones + 4 ones = 7
   ones → 37); 52 + 6 (→ 58); 71 + 8 (→ 79); 20 + 9 (→ 29).
2. **Say it first:** before touching the cubes, the learner says
   "tens stay, ones change" and predicts whether a new ten will
   appear (none of these do — every ones total is 9 or less).
3. **Zero-ones round:** 60 + 7 → 67; 30 + 5 → 35. The learner names
   the empty ones pile before adding.

*Alternate response mode:* point to the ones pile and say the new
ones count; adult scribes the equations.

## Session B — Compose-ten stations (Session 6)

**Goal:** make the trade physical before it becomes mental.

1. **Trade tray station:** 29 + 6 → 6 ones + 9 ones = 15 ones = 1 ten
   and 5 ones; 2 tens + 1 ten = 3 tens → 35. Then 47 + 8 → 55 (7 + 8
   = 15 ones → 1 ten and 5 ones; 4 tens + 1 ten = 5 tens).
2. **Bridge station:** 68 + 4 → 68 + 2 = 70, + 2 = 72. Then 53 + 9 →
   53 + 7 = 60, + 2 = 62. The learner says the split aloud before
   jumping.
3. **Exactly-ten round:** 24 + 6 = 30 (4 + 6 = 10 ones = 1 ten, 0
   ones left; 2 tens + 1 ten = 3 tens). The learner explains why the
   ones digit becomes 0.

*Alternate response mode:* draw the trade — circle ten ones, draw an
arrow to one tens rod — instead of moving cubes.

## Session C — Tens-and-ones race (Session 3)

**Goal:** physicalize the tens/ones split; connect piles to the
written equation.

1. **Floor race:** the learner stands beside a floor tens/ones mat.
   The adult calls "45 + 3": the learner adds 3 cubes to the ones
   pile only and calls "48 — tens stayed, ones changed." Problems:
   45 + 3 → 48; 62 + 5 → 67; 81 + 6 → 87.
2. **Race the adult:** the adult adds ones slowly by counting from
   one; the learner uses the piles and should finish first every
   time. Debrief: trusting the piles beats counting from one.

*Alternate response mode:* move a toy car to the ones pile only.

## Session D — Review circles (Sessions 4 and 8)

**Goal:** re-check the last lesson's exit items in a low-stakes
circle before new material arrives.

1. The adult re-asks each exit check from the previous lesson; the
   learner answers with piles, chart, or words — any mode counts.
2. **Error hunt:** the adult replays the lesson's error-analysis
   mistake and one new one (for Session 8: "56 + 7 = 53" — the
   learner catches the forgotten ten: 6 + 7 = 13 ones = 1 ten and 3
   ones; 5 tens + 1 ten = 6 tens, so 56 + 7 = **63**).
3. The adult records secure / developing / not yet per objective
   and re-teaches the weakest move in the next session's warm-up.

## Session E — Shop game (Session 7)

**Goal:** use Lessons 01–02 moves in a playful buying game; start
the project.

1. **Price tags:** items cost two-digit prices (24, 37, 48). The
   learner "buys" an item and pays with ones: 24 + 6 = 30 (compose a
   ten); 37 + 2 = 39 (no new ten); 48 + 5 = 53 (compose a ten).
2. **Change check:** after each purchase, the learner says whether a
   new ten was made and proves it with piles.
3. **Project link:** the learner drafts the project's add-ones page
   (see [project.md](project.md), Part 1) from one shop purchase.

*Alternate response mode:* point to price tags and piles instead of
writing; adult scribes.

## Session F — Add-tens jumps (Session 10)

**Goal:** make "tens change, ones stay" automatic for two-digit +
multiple-of-ten problems.

1. **Rod jumps:** 24 + 40 → 2 tens + 4 tens = 6 tens, 4 ones
   unchanged → 64. Then 55 + 30 → 85; 38 + 50 → 88; 70 + 20 → 90.
2. **Chart jumps:** the same problems on the hundred chart — the
   learner jumps down one row per ten and names each landing number
   (24 → 34 → 44 → 54 → 64).
3. **Wrong-pile hunt:** the adult offers "36 + 40 = 310" (adding tens
   as ones, then mashing digits) and "36 + 40 = 39" (adding 3 to the
   ones). The learner rejects both: 3 tens + 4 tens = 7 tens, 6 ones
   unchanged → **76**.

## Session G — Hundred-chart races (Session 12)

**Goal:** make 10 more / 10 less a one-step mental move.

1. **Row races:** the adult calls a number; the learner jumps one
   row and calls the landing number. Problems: 10 more than 44 → 54;
   10 less than 71 → 61; 10 more than 89 → 99; 10 less than 30 → 20.
2. **Round trip:** 10 more and then 10 less from 57 (→ 67 → 57);
   the learner explains why the trip returns to the start.
3. **Edge round:** 10 more than 90 → 100 (10 tens = 100); 10 less
   than 10 → 0. The learner says the tens sentence for each.

## Session H — Subtract-tens stations (Session 14)

**Goal:** make tens subtraction automatic and always checked by
addition.

1. **Remove rods:** 60 − 30 → 3 tens → 30, check 30 + 30 = 60. Then
   80 − 60 → 20, check 20 + 60 = 80. Then 90 − 90 → 0, check
   0 + 90 = 90.
2. **Check-catches-error:** the adult offers "70 − 40 = 20" and the
   learner runs the check: 20 + 40 = 60, not 70 — so the answer is
   wrong; correct is 70 − 40 = **30** (check 30 + 40 = 70).
3. **True-or-false:** 50 + 20 = 70 (true); 80 − 30 = 40 (false —
   80 − 30 = 50, check 50 + 30 = 80).

*Alternate response mode:* jump up the hundred chart instead of
removing rods; adult scribes equations and checks.
