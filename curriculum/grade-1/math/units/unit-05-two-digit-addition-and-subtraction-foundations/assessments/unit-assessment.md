# Unit Assessment — Unit 05: Two-Digit Addition and Subtraction Foundations

**Culminating assessment** for the five unit objectives. Oral,
pointing, drawing, or manipulative responses are all valid; the adult
scribes written work when the learner isn't ready. Scored with the
teacher guide's rubric. Not a standardized or validated assessment.

## Task 1 — Add ones (Objective 1)

Build each with piles and say whether a new ten is made.

a. 34 + 5 = ___ (Expected: 39 — 4 ones + 5 ones = 9 ones, no new ten;
   3 tens unchanged.)
b. 57 + 6 = ___ (Expected: 63 — 7 ones + 6 ones = 13 ones = 1 ten and
   3 ones; 5 tens + 1 ten = 6 tens.)

## Task 2 — Add tens (Objective 2)

Name what changes before solving.

a. 46 + 30 = ___ (Expected: 76 — 4 tens + 3 tens = 7 tens, 6 ones
   unchanged.)
b. 18 + 40 = ___ (Expected: 58 — 1 ten + 4 tens = 5 tens, 8 ones
   unchanged.)

## Task 3 — Ten more, ten less (Objective 3)

Answer in your head; say the tens-digit sentence.

a. 10 more than 68 = ___ (Expected: 78 — 6 tens + 1 ten = 7 tens, 8
   ones unchanged.)
b. 10 less than 53 = ___ (Expected: 43 — 5 tens − 1 ten = 4 tens, 3
   ones unchanged.)

## Task 4 — Subtract tens (Objective 4)

Remove rods and write the addition check for each.

a. 70 − 20 = ___ (Expected: 50 — 7 tens − 2 tens = 5 tens; check
   50 + 20 = 70.)
b. 60 − 60 = ___ (Expected: 0 — 6 tens − 6 tens = 0 tens; check
   0 + 60 = 60.)

## Task 5 — Explain a worked problem (Objective 5)

The adult shows: "27 + 5 = 32, because 7 ones + 5 ones = 12 ones =
1 ten and 2 ones, and 2 tens + 1 ten = 3 tens."

a. Explain in your own words why the answer is 32 and not 212 or 27
   with 12 ones. (Expected: 12 ones cannot stay in the ones pile —
   ten of them trade for one ten, leaving 2 ones; the tens pile
   grows from 2 to 3. "212" writes the ones total as if it were a
   separate number.)
b. A friend says "70 − 30 = 40 because 40 + 30 = 70." Is the friend
   right, and why does the check prove it? (Expected: yes — the
   check rebuilds the start number: taking 30 away and putting 30
   back must return 70, and 40 does that.)

## Task 6 — Picture study (Objectives 1 and 5)

Look at the unit illustration
[`assets/tens-and-ones-blocks.png`](../assets/tens-and-ones-blocks.png):
"How many tens bars and how many ones cubes are shown? What number
is that? Add 5 ones to it — is a new ten made, and what is the new
number?"

(Expected: 4 blue bars = 4 tens; 5 red cubes = 5 ones; the number is
45. Adding 5 ones: 5 ones + 5 ones = 10 ones = 1 ten and 0 ones, so a
new ten IS made: 4 tens + 1 ten = 5 tens, 0 ones — the new number is
50. This is the picture's Lesson 01 case (45 + 3 = 48, no new ten)
pushed one step further.)

## Scoring (adult)

| 2 — Add tens | Both 2a, 2b correct, tens sentence stated | One correct | Tens and ones confused |
| 3 — Ten more/less | Both 3a, 3b correct in the head | One correct, or counting by ones | Cannot find 10 more/less mentally |
| 4 — Subtract tens | Both 4a, 4b correct with checks | One correct, or check missing | Cannot subtract tens by piles |
| 5 — Explain and check | Both 5a, 5b explained with piles/check reasoning | One explained | Cannot explain a method or a check |
| Picture study (supports 1, 5) | Counts 45 correctly and explains the new ten to 50 | Counts 45, new-ten step shaky | Cannot read the piles |

**Ready to move on:** at least 9 of 12 points, with Objective 1 at
Secure or Developing. **Re-teach** per the teacher guide's map;
Unit 06 (length measurement) does not depend on this unit's
computation, but the tens/ones reasoning returns in every later
