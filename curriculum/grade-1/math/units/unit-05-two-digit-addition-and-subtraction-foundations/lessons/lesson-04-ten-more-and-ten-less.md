# Lesson 04 — Ten More, Ten Less

**Student goal:** I can find 10 more or 10 less than a two-digit
number in my head: only the tens digit changes, up or down by one.
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Unit:** [U05](../README.md) · **Session 11 of 16**

## Materials

- A hundred chart to 120
- Number cards 0–100

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"34: what digit is in the tens place? What is one more ten than 3
tens?" The learner says "3" and "4 tens." Then: "So 10 more than 34
should be…?" If the learner counts by ones to answer, show the
hundred-chart row jump first, then re-ask.

## Teaching

Ten more and ten less are Lesson 03's moves with the rods put away.
Adding 10 means adding exactly one ten, so the tens digit grows by
one and the ones digit does not move at all. Subtracting 10 — finding
10 less — means taking one ten away, so the tens digit shrinks by
one and the ones digit does not move.

> "34: 3 tens and 4 ones. Ten more: one more ten, so 4 tens and 4
> ones — **44**. Ten less: one fewer ten, so 2 tens and 4 ones —
> **24**. Look at the digits: 34 → 44 → 24. Only the first digit
> moved. The 4 ones sat still both times."
>
> "On the hundred chart, ten more is one jump straight down, ten
> less is one jump straight up. Same column, every time — because
> the ones never change."

**Key vocabulary:** *10 more*, *10 less*, *tens digit*, *ones digit*.
The standard's own words: "mentally find 10 more or 10 less than the
number, without having to count."

## Worked example 1 — 58: ten more and ten less

> "58 is 5 tens and 8 ones. Ten more: 5 tens + 1 ten = 6 tens, 8 ones
> unchanged — **68**. Ten less: 5 tens − 1 ten = 4 tens, 8 ones
> unchanged — **48**."
>
> "Check on the chart: 58 sits in the 8-column. One row down is 68,
> one row up is 48. Same column, same ones digit."

## Worked example 2 — Edge cases: 90 and 10

> "90 is 9 tens and 0 ones. Ten more: 9 tens + 1 ten = 10 tens. Ten
> tens is a hundred, so 10 more than 90 is **100**. The ones digit
> is still 0."
>
> "10 is 1 ten and 0 ones. Ten less: 1 ten − 1 ten = 0 tens, 0 ones
> unchanged — **0**. Taking the only ten away leaves nothing."

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds, chart available but counting by ones not allowed
for the answer — the learner must name the tens-digit change.

1. 10 more than 27: 2 tens + 1 ten = 3 tens, 7 ones unchanged.
   (Expected: 37.)
2. 10 less than 63: 6 tens − 1 ten = 5 tens, 3 ones unchanged.
   (Expected: 53.)
3. 10 more than 80: 8 tens + 1 ten = 9 tens, 0 ones unchanged.
   (Expected: 90.)
4. 10 less than 41: 4 tens − 1 ten = 3 tens, 1 one unchanged.
   (Expected: 31.)
5. Error analysis: the adult says "10 more than 46 is 47 — I added 1."
   Ask: "Did I add ten, or did I add one?" (Expected: one. 10 more
   means one more *ten*: 4 tens + 1 ten = 5 tens, 6 ones unchanged,
   so 10 more than 46 is **56**. Adding 1 to the ones digit is
   today's classic slip.)
6. The learner answers, chart covered: 10 less than 20, then 10 more
   than 72. (Expected: 10; 82.)

## Independent practice

The learner gets: 10 more than 35, and 10 less than 58. They say the
tens-digit sentence before writing each answer, chart covered if
ready.

*Alternate response mode:* point to the tens digit on a number card
and say "up one" or "down one"; adult scribes the answers.

## Application

Game points: "Jae has 52 points. She earns 10 more points. How many
points does she have now? Then she spends 10 points. How many are
left?" The learner answers in the head: 5 tens + 1 ten = 6 tens, 2
ones unchanged → 62; then 6 tens − 1 ten = 5 tens → 52.
(Expected: 62 points, then 52 points. The round trip proves the move:
10 more and then 10 less returns to the start.)

## Exit check

"69. First 10 more, then 10 less — both in your head." Expected:
"10 more: 6 tens + 1 ten = 7 tens, 9 ones unchanged, so 79. 10 less
than 69: 6 tens − 1 ten = 5 tens, 9 ones unchanged, so 59." Record:
secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** teach "one more ten" with a rod first, then
  shorten it to "ten more." The phrase "ten more" must never be
  heard as "one more."
- **Alternate response mode:** slide a finger down or up one row on
  the hundred chart instead of computing; say the landing number.
- **Floor-play variant:** a floor hundred chart; the learner steps
  down one row for ten more, up one row for ten less.

## Extension

Chain three jumps in the head (for example 34 → 44 → 54 → 44: ten
more, ten more, ten less) and say the tens digit at each stop. Or:
explain why 10 more than 90 is 100, using "10 tens = 100" with rods.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) adding 1 to the ones digit for "10
more" (46 → 47); (2) counting by ones from the start number; (3)
changing both digits ("10 more than 34 is 45"). The chart column is
the cure: same column, same ones digit. **Answer-key reference:**
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-04).
