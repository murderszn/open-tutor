# Lesson 05 — Subtracting Multiples of Ten

**Student goal:** I can subtract a multiple of ten from a multiple of
ten: take tens away from tens, and check my answer by adding.
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Unit:** [U05](../README.md) · **Session 13 of 16**

## Materials

- Tens rods (at least 9) and ones cubes (kept aside — this lesson has
  no ones to move)
- A hundred chart to 120

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"70 is how many tens? 30 is how many tens?" The learner says "7 tens"
and "3 tens." Then: "7 tens take away 3 tens?" — "4 tens." If decade
numbers as tens are shaky, re-run Lesson 03's rod counting before
continuing.

## Teaching

Both numbers here are pure tens — decade numbers with zero ones. So
subtracting is just taking tens rods away from tens rods. Nothing
happens to the ones, because there are no ones to take.

> "Here is 70: 7 tens rods, 0 ones. Take away 30, which is 3 tens
> rods. 7 tens − 3 tens = 4 tens. 4 tens is **40**. So 70 − 30 = 40."
>
> "**Check it by adding:** if 70 − 30 really is 40, then putting the
> taken part back must rebuild 70: 40 + 30 = 70. It does — so 40 is
> right. Every subtraction in this lesson gets an addition check."

**Key vocabulary:** *multiple of ten*, *take away*, *check*. Say the
sentence every time: "Tens take away tens; then add back to check."

## Worked example 1 — 70 − 30, with the check

> "Build 7 tens rods. Remove 3 rods. Count what is left: 4 rods, 4
> tens, 0 ones — **40**. Check: 40 + 30: 4 tens + 3 tens = 7 tens =
> **70**. The check rebuilds the start number, so 70 − 30 = 40 is
> correct."

## Worked example 2 — 80 − 50, and the zero case 50 − 50

> "80 is 8 tens. Take away 50 (5 tens): 8 tens − 5 tens = 3 tens =
> **30**. Check: 30 + 50 = 80. ✓"
>
> "50 − 50: 5 tens − 5 tens = 0 tens = **0**. Taking all the tens
> away leaves nothing — and zero is a correct answer, not a mistake.
> Check: 0 + 50 = 50. ✓"

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds, removing real rods and checking every answer by
addition.

1. 60 − 20: 6 tens − 2 tens = 4 tens. Check 40 + 20 = 60.
   (Expected: 40.)
2. 90 − 40: 9 tens − 4 tens = 5 tens. Check 50 + 40 = 90.
   (Expected: 50.)
3. 40 − 40: 4 tens − 4 tens = 0 tens. Check 0 + 40 = 40.
   (Expected: 0.)
4. 80 − 10: 8 tens − 1 ten = 7 tens. Check 70 + 10 = 80.
   (Expected: 70.)
5. Error analysis: the adult solves 90 − 60 by saying "9 − 6 = 2,
   so 20." Ask: "Is 9 − 6 really 2?" (Expected: no — 9 − 6 = 3, so
   9 tens − 6 tens = 3 tens = **30**. Check: 30 + 60 = 90. A small
   within-10 fact slip, not a place-value slip — and the addition
   check catches it: 20 + 60 = 80, not 90.)
6. The learner models 70 − 60 alone: 7 tens − 6 tens = 1 ten.
   Check 10 + 60 = 70. (Expected: 10.)

## Independent practice

The learner gets 50 − 20 and 90 − 70, with rods available. They write
the subtraction, the tens sentence, and the addition check for each.

*Alternate response mode:* jump up the hundred chart — one row per
ten taken away — and say each landing number; adult scribes the
equations and checks.

## Application

Marble jar: "A jar holds 80 marbles. Ana takes 30 marbles out for a
game. How many marbles are left in the jar?" The learner builds 8
tens, removes 3: 8 tens − 3 tens = 5 tens. (Expected: 50 marbles;
80 − 30 = 50. Check: 50 + 30 = 80.)

## Exit check

"60 − 40, with its addition check." Expected: "6 tens − 4 tens = 2
tens = 20. Check: 20 + 40 = 60. So 60 − 40 = **20**." Record:
secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** teach "take tens away" with rods first, then
  name the numbers as multiples of ten.
- **Alternate response mode:** remove rods and count what is left
  without writing; adult scribes the equation and the check.
- **Floor-play variant:** a floor tens row; the learner walks away
  with the taken rods and counts the rods still standing.

## Extension

True-or-false with checks: judge "80 − 30 = 40" (false — 80 − 30 =
50; check 40 + 30 = 70, not 80) and "50 + 20 = 70" (true). Or: solve a
missing-tens problem — "70 − ? = 40" — by asking "40 + what tens
makes 70?" (? = 30).

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) a within-10 fact slip (9 − 6 = 2)
disguised as a tens problem — the addition check is the cure;
(2) refusing zero as an answer (50 − 50 "can't be nothing"); (3)
subtracting the ones digit (there is none — both numbers end in 0).
Celebrate zero answers explicitly. **Answer-key reference:**
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-05).
