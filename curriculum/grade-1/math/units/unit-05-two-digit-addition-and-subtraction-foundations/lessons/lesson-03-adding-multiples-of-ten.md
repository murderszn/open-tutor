# Lesson 03 — Adding a Multiple of Ten

**Student goal:** I can add 10, 20, 30, or more tens to a two-digit
number: the tens change, the ones stay exactly the same.
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Unit:** [U05](../README.md) · **Session 9 of 16**

## Materials

- Tens rods and ones cubes
- A hundred chart to 120

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"34 is how many tens and how many ones? What is 20 as tens?" The
learner says "3 tens and 4 ones" and "2 tens." Then: "If I add 2 tens
to 3 tens, how many tens?" — "5 tens." If decade numbers as tens are
shaky, re-run Unit 01's decade-number game before continuing.

## Teaching

A multiple of ten — 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90 — is pure tens:
it has zero ones. So when we add one to a two-digit number, we touch
**only the tens pile**. The ones pile does not move, so the ones digit
does not change. It is the mirror image of Lesson 01.

> "Here is 34: 3 tens and 4 ones. Add 20, which is 2 tens and 0 ones.
> Tens: 3 tens + 2 tens = 5 tens. Ones: 4 ones + 0 ones = 4 ones. So
> 34 + 20 = **54**."
>
> "On the hundred chart, adding 20 is two jumps down — each jump is
> one ten. 34 → 44 → 54. The ones column never changes: 34, 44, 54
> all end in 4."

**Key vocabulary:** *multiple of ten*, *decade number*. Say the
sentence every time: "Tens change, ones stay the same."

## Worked example 1 — 34 + 20 with rods

> "Build 34: 3 tens rods, 4 ones cubes. Add 2 tens rods. Count tens:
> 3, 4, 5 — 5 tens. Count ones: still 4. So 34 + 20 = **54**."
>
> "Written method, tens first in words: 30 + 20 = 50, then put the 4
> ones back: 50 + 4 = 54. Same answer."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** we added tens to tens. Adding 20 never
adds 20 to the ones digit — that would be adding 20 ones, a different
problem.

## Worked example 2 — 25 + 30, and 60 + 20

> "25 is 2 tens and 5 ones. Add 30 (3 tens): 2 tens + 3 tens = 5 tens,
> 5 ones unchanged. So 25 + 30 = **55**."
>
> "60 is 6 tens and 0 ones. Add 20 (2 tens): 6 tens + 2 tens = 8 tens,
> 0 ones unchanged. So 60 + 20 = **80**."

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds, with rods and a hundred chart on the table.

1. 41 + 30: 4 tens + 3 tens = 7 tens; 1 one unchanged. (Expected: 71.)
2. 17 + 40: 1 ten + 4 tens = 5 tens; 7 ones unchanged. (Expected: 57.)
3. 52 + 20: 5 tens + 2 tens = 7 tens; 2 ones unchanged. (Expected: 72.)
4. 30 + 50: 3 tens + 5 tens = 8 tens; 0 ones unchanged. (Expected: 80.)
5. Error analysis: the adult solves 42 + 30 by saying "add 3 to the
   ones: 2 ones + 3 = 5 ones, tens stay 4, so 45." Ask: "Did I add to
   the right pile?" (Expected: no — 30 is 3 tens, not 3 ones. 4 tens
   + 3 tens = 7 tens, 2 ones unchanged, so 42 + 30 = **72**. Adding
   tens to the ones pile is today's classic slip — the mirror of
   Lesson 01's error.)
6. The learner models 26 + 40 alone on the hundred chart: 26 → 36 →
   46 → 56 → 66, four jumps down. (Expected: 66.)

## Independent practice

The learner gets 63 + 20 and 19 + 50, with rods available. They say
the tens-change sentence before writing each answer.

*Alternate response mode:* jump a toy along the hundred chart, one
row per ten, and say each landing number; adult scribes the equations.

## Application

Library books: "The class shelf has 26 books. The teacher adds 30
more books. How many books are on the shelf now?" The learner builds
26, adds 3 tens rods: 2 tens + 3 tens = 5 tens, 6 ones unchanged.
(Expected: 56 books; 26 + 30 = 56.)

## Exit check

"47 + 40. Tens change, ones stay — what is it?" Expected: "4 tens +
4 tens = 8 tens, 7 ones unchanged, so 47 + 40 = **87**." Record:
secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** teach "multiple of ten" with rods first — a
  pile of only tens rods — then name it.
- **Alternate response mode:** cover the ones pile with a cloth
  while adding tens, then uncover it to prove it never moved.
- **Floor-play variant:** a floor hundred chart; the learner jumps
  down one row per ten added, calling out each landing number.

## Extension

Add two multiples of ten to a number in a row (for example 24 + 30 +
20 = 74: 24 → 54 → 74), and say why the ones digit never changed.
Or: predict 10 more than 90 on the hundred chart (100) and explain
that 9 tens + 1 ten = 10 tens = 100.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) adding the tens number to the ones
digit (42 + 30 = 45); (2) counting by ones to add 20 or 30 instead of
jumping by tens; (3) thinking 30 means "3" because the zero is
ignored. Keep rods on the table until the tens jump is automatic.
