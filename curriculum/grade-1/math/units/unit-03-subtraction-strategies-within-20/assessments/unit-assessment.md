# Unit Assessment — Unit 03: Subtraction Strategies Within 20

**Culminating assessment** for the five unit objectives. Oral, pointing,
drawing, or manipulative responses are all valid; the adult scribes written
work when the learner isn't ready. Scored with the teacher guide's rubric.
Not a standardized or validated assessment.

## Task 1 — Unknown addend (Objective 1)

Flip each subtraction to addition and count up aloud.

a. 11 − 5 = ___ (Expected: 6 — 5 + 6 = 11; count up 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11)
b. 14 − 8 = ___ (Expected: 6 — 8 + 6 = 14; count up 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14)

## Task 2 — Count back or count up (Objective 2)

Name the move before solving; say each hop aloud.

a. 17 − 3 = ___ (Expected: 14 — count back: "16, 15, 14")
b. 19 − 17 = ___ (Expected: 2 — count up: "18, 19," 2 hops)

## Task 3 — Back to ten (Objective 3)

Show both steps on ten frames and write the chain.

a. 14 − 6 = ___ (Expected: 8 — split 6 into 4 + 2; 14 − 4 = 10; 10 − 2 = 8)
b. 16 − 7 = ___ (Expected: 9 — split 7 into 6 + 1; 16 − 6 = 10; 10 − 1 = 9)

## Task 4 — Fact family (Objective 4)

a. 8 + 7 = 15. Write both subtractions. (Expected: 15 − 8 = 7 and
   15 − 7 = 8.)
b. Explain why both subtractions must use the numbers 8, 7, and 15.
   (Expected: the total minus one part leaves the other part — no new
   numbers appear.)

## Task 5 — Missing numbers and true or false (Objective 5)

a. 15 − ? = 8 (Expected: ? = 7 — count up 9…15, seven hops; check
   15 − 7 = 8)
b. ? − 5 = 12 (Expected: ? = 17 — rebuild 12 + 5 = 17; check 17 − 5 = 12)
c. Judge, computing both sides: 12 − 6 = 6 (Expected: true — 12 − 6 = 6)
d. Judge, computing both sides: 14 − 5 = 8 (Expected: false —
   14 − 5 = 9, and 9 ≠ 8; fix by changing 8 to 9)
e. What does the equal sign mean? (Expected: "is the same amount as" —
   both sides balance)

## Task 6 — Picture study (Objectives 2–3)

Look at the unit illustration
[`assets/count-back-ten-frames.png`](../assets/count-back-ten-frames.png):
"How many dots are there? How many are looped in orange? If the looped
dots are taken away, how many are left — and which subtraction is that?"
(Expected: 13 dots; 2 looped — one red, one blue; 13 − 2 = 11 left.)

## Scoring (adult)

| Objective | Secure (2) | Developing (1) | Not yet (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Unknown addend | Both 1a, 1b correct with hop counts | One correct, or hop-count slip | Cannot flip to addition |
| 2 — Count back / count up | Both 2a, 2b correct with the shorter move named | One correct, or always uses one move | Counts from one, or off-by-one both ways |
| 3 — Back to ten | Both 3a, 3b correct with chains landing on ten | One correct, or split not by loose ones | Stops at ten, or splits arbitrarily |
| 4 — Fact family | 4a both correct and 4b explained | One subtraction correct, or explanation missing | Invents numbers outside the family |
| 5 — Missing / true-false | 5a–5d correct and `=` explained | 2–3 of 5a–5d correct | Judges one-sided, or rebuilds by subtracting |

**Picture study (Task 6)** is reported alongside Objectives 2–3, not
scored separately: a correct count (13, 2 looped, 11 left) confirms the
learner can read a ten-frame picture; a miscount sends the learner back
to Lesson 02's picture study with real counters.
