# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Subtraction Strategies Within 20

Adult-facing guidance for the unit. Learner materials never include answer
keys; keep this file separate from the learner.

## How the unit fits

Unit 03 (track weeks 11–14) turns Unit 02's addition power into
subtraction power. The strategies arrive in a deliberate order:
**unknown-addend** (L1) reuses the missing-addend skill and makes every
later strategy checkable; **count back / count up** (L2) handles the
easy ends of the range (small subtrahends, close numbers);
**back-to-ten** (L3) is the workhorse for teen subtractions;
**fact families** (L4) convert known additions into free subtractions;
**missing numbers and true/false** (L5) prove the learner understands
equations, not just procedures. Unit 04 (story problems) will demand all
five at once, so Objectives 1 and 3 must be secure before moving on.

## Session-by-session notes

- **Sessions 1–4 (Week 11):** Keep differences within 15 while the
  unknown-addend flip stabilizes. The off-by-one hop error (counting the
  start number) is the unit's most common bug — catch it in Session 2's
  hop-count check, not in the quiz.
- **Sessions 5–8 (Week 12a):** The "shorter move" choice must be defended
  aloud at least once — a learner who always counts back will stall on
  19 − 17. Watch for subtraction read backwards ("take 14 away from
  12"); re-anchor: subtraction starts at the bigger number. The
  generated illustration is a study object, not decoration — Lesson 02's
  picture study is mandatory, and its honest counts (13 dots, 2 looped)
  are recorded in the assets README.
- **Sessions 9–10 (Week 12b):** Back-to-ten only works if "how many loose
  ones?" is instant (Lesson 03 readiness check). If it isn't, drill
  loose-ones identification for two days before modeling the split. The
  second step is the one learners forget — always demand the full chain.
- **Sessions 11–12 (Week 13a):** Fact families must be physical at least
  once — a learner who only hears "total minus a part" will invent
  numbers. The cover-and-say move (Session G) is the proof the family
  is understood.
- **Sessions 13–16 (Week 13b–14):** Missing-minuend problems break the
  "subtract to find a missing number" habit. Use the rebuild demo every
  time: put the parts back together. Learners who judge equations
  one-sided need Session H's both-sides sort with mandatory computation.

## What "ready to move on" looks like

- Flips any subtraction within 20 to an unknown-addend question and
  counts up without recounting the start.
- Chooses count back for small subtrahends and count up for close
  numbers, and says why.
- Splits a subtrahend by the loose ones and lands on ten first, every time.
- Reads two subtractions off any known addition fact.
- Solves missing-subtrahend and missing-minuend equations with a check,
  and judges true/false equations by computing both sides; explains `=`
  as "is the same amount as."

## Re-teaching map

| Evidence | Re-teach with |
|---|---|
| Hop-count off-by-one | Session A/C hop-count checks; hand-over-start-number demo |
| Always counts back, even for close numbers | Lesson 02 "shorter move" races; Session B choose-and-defend |
| Back-to-ten split not by loose ones | "How many loose ones?" ritual; Session F frame station |
| Stops at ten, forgets second step | Demand the full chain aloud; Lesson 03 guided rounds |
| Fact-family invented numbers | Session G triangle race; cover-and-say with counters |
| Missing-minuend solved by subtracting | Lesson 05 rebuild demo; "put the parts back together" chant |
| `=` read as "write the answer" | Balance/seesaw demo; `9 = 14 − 5` and `6 = 6` cards |
| Judges equations one-sided | Session H sort with mandatory both-sides computation |

## Project scoring

Use the rubric in [project.md](project.md): 10–12 = ready to move on;
7–9 = one re-teach session on the weakest page, then re-showcase; below 7
= revisit Lessons 01–05 practice sessions before Unit 04. The showcase's
invented missing-number problem must include a check — an unchecked
answer does not earn Strong on that criterion.
