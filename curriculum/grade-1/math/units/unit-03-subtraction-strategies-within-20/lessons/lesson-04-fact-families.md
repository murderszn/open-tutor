# Lesson 04 — Fact Families: One Addition Fact, Two Subtractions

**Student goal:** I can use one addition fact to answer two subtractions
— if 8 + 4 = 12, then 12 − 8 = 4 and 12 − 4 = 8.
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Unit:** [U03](../README.md) · **Session 11 of 16**

## Materials

- Fact-family triangle cards (three numbers per card: two parts, one
  total), counters in two colors, paper and crayons

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"8 + 4 = ?" (Expected: 12.) "Now, without counting: 12 − 8 = ?"
If the learner hesitates, rebuild 8 + 4 with counters and cover the 8:
"What's left?" (Expected: 4.) The addition fact is the key; the
subtractions are already inside it.

## Teaching

Numbers that add together form a family of four facts — two additions and
two subtractions — all using the same three numbers. Learn one addition
fact and you get two subtractions free.

> "Build 8 red and 4 blue: 8 + 4 = 12. Now cover the 8 red ones. What's
> left? The 4 blue ones — so 12 − 8 = **4.** Uncover, cover the 4 blue
> ones instead. What's left? The 8 red ones — so 12 − 4 = **8.** Same
> three numbers: 8, 4, 12. One family, four facts."

**Key vocabulary:** *fact family* (the four facts made from the same
three numbers), *parts* (the two smaller numbers), *total* (the biggest
number).

**Why it works (say it aloud):** subtraction undoes addition. The total
minus one part must leave the other part — there is nowhere else for it
to go.

## Worked example 1 — 8 + 4 = 12, both subtractions

The adult writes the triangle: parts 8 and 4 at the bottom corners,
total 12 at the top.

> "Additions: 8 + 4 = 12 and 4 + 8 = 12. Subtractions: total minus a
> part leaves the other part. 12 − 8 = **4.** 12 − 4 = **8.** Check:
> 4 + 8 = 12 and 8 + 4 = 12 — both check out."

## Worked example 2 — 9 + 6 = 15, worked backward from a subtraction

> "A problem asks 15 − 9 = ? Ask: '9 plus what is 15?' — that's the
> addition fact 9 + 6 = 15. So 15 − 9 = **6.** And the family gives one
> more for free: 15 − 6 = **9.**"

**Reasoning to name aloud:** when a subtraction feels hard, find its
addition fact first. Fact families turn Lesson 01's flipped question
into a habit: every subtraction has a home addition fact.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds.

1. 7 + 5 = 12: write both subtractions. (Expected: 12 − 7 = 5 and
   12 − 5 = 7.)
2. 6 + 9 = 15: write both subtractions. (Expected: 15 − 6 = 9 and
   15 − 9 = 6.)
3. Given 14 − 6 = 8, write the addition fact. (Expected: 6 + 8 = 14,
   and 8 + 6 = 14.)
4. Triangle card with 5, 8, 13: write all four facts. (Expected:
   5 + 8 = 13, 8 + 5 = 13, 13 − 5 = 8, 13 − 8 = 5.)
5. Error analysis: the adult, starting from 8 + 4 = 12, claims
   12 − 4 = 6. Ask: "Did I use the family right?" (Expected: no —
   12 − 4 must leave the other part, 8, and 4 + 8 = 12 checks it.
   12 − 4 = 8, not 6.)
6. The learner builds 7 + 8 = 15 alone and writes both subtractions.
   (Expected: 15 − 7 = 8 and 15 − 8 = 7.)

## Independent practice

The learner gets 9 + 7 = 16 and 4 + 11 = 15. For each, they write both
subtractions and check each with its addition partner. The adult observes
silently: do the subtraction answers match the *other* part, not a
guessed number?

- 9 + 7 = 16 → 16 − 9 = 7 and 16 − 7 = 9.
- 4 + 11 = 15 → 15 − 4 = 11 and 15 − 11 = 4.

*Alternate response mode:* build each family with two colors of
counters and cover one part at a time instead of writing; adult scribes
the facts.

## Application

Domino facts: the adult shows a domino with 6 dots on one side and 7 on
the other. "Total? 13. Now name the whole family." (Expected:
6 + 7 = 13, 7 + 6 = 13, 13 − 6 = 7, 13 − 7 = 6.) Any domino, any
two-part counter build, any triangle card can start a family — the
learner hunts for families at home with dice and coins.

## Exit check

"6 + 7 = 13. What is 13 − 6?" Expected: "7 — the other part. And
13 − 7 = 6." Record: secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** "parts" and "total" are shown by covering parts of
  a build before the words *fact family* are used. Triangle cards make
  the three numbers visible at once.
- **Alternate response mode:** cover-and-say — cover one part, say
  what's left — instead of writing all four facts.
- **Support:** start with totals ≤ 12 until the cover-and-say move is
  fluent, then extend to totals 13–20.

## Extension

Build a family for 9 + 9 = 18 (note: the two subtractions are the same,
18 − 9 = 9 — a doubles family has only three distinct facts). Or: given
only 17 − 9 = 8, write the other three family facts (9 + 8 = 17,
8 + 9 = 17, 17 − 8 = 9).

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) subtracting the total from a part
(e.g. 8 − 12) — re-anchor: subtraction always starts at the total;
(2) inventing a fourth number instead of reusing the other part;
(3) thinking turn-around pairs are different families — they are the
same family, same three numbers.
