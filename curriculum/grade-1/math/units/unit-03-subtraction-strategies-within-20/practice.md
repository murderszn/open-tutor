# Practice Sessions — Unit 03: Subtraction Strategies Within 20

Session-type practice, game, and review plans for the unit's 16-session
pacing. Every session opens with the Unit 01/Unit 02 retrieval warm-up
(count by tens from any decade; one addition strategy flash).

## Session A — Unknown-addend games (Session 2)

**Goal:** make flipping a subtraction into a counting-up question
automatic for differences within 15.

1. **Cover and count:** the adult builds the subtrahend, covers the rest
   of the total with a cloth, and asks "how many are hiding?" Problems:
   10 − 7 (7 + 3 = 10 → 3), 11 − 8 (8 + 3 = 11 → 3), 13 − 6 (6 + 7 = 13
   → 7), 12 − 9 (9 + 3 = 12 → 3). The learner counts up aloud; the adult
   listens for the off-by-one slip.
2. **Flip-it cards:** the adult shows a subtraction card; the learner
   says the flipped addition before answering. Cards: 14 − 6 → "6 + ? =
   14" → 8; 15 − 9 → "9 + ? = 15" → 6; 11 − 5 → "5 + ? = 11" → 6.
3. **Hop-count check:** the learner says each hop aloud ("that's one
   more, that's two more") — the adult listens for the start number
   being counted as a hop.

*Alternate response mode:* point to hop numbers on the number line
instead of saying them.

## Session B — Count-back / count-up stations (Session 6)

**Goal:** choose the shorter move — count back for small subtrahends,
count up for close numbers — and execute it without the off-by-one slip.

1. **Small take-aways (count back):** 18 − 3 → "17, 16, 15" → 15;
   14 − 2 → "13, 12" → 12; 17 − 4 → "16, 15, 14, 13" → 13.
2. **Close numbers (count up):** 20 − 18 → "19, 20" → 2; 13 − 11 →
   "12, 13" → 2; 15 − 14 → "15" → 1.
3. **Choose and defend:** the adult shows 16 − 13 and 16 − 3. The
   learner picks a move for each and defends it. (Expected: 16 − 13 by
   counting up — "14, 15, 16," 3 hops → 3; 16 − 3 by counting back —
   "15, 14, 13" → 13. The wrong move still works; the short move wins.)

*Alternate response mode:* hop a toy car along a table number line.

## Session C — Number-line hop-back races (Session 3)

**Goal:** physicalize counting back; connect hops to the written equation.

1. **Floor number line:** the learner stands on the minuend, hops
   backward the subtrahend while the adult counts hops. Land on the
   difference; write it. Problems: 12 − 3 → 9; 16 − 2 → 14; 19 − 17 by
   counting *up* instead ("18, 19" → 2 — the race teaches that the
   shorter direction wins); 11 − 8 by counting up ("9, 10, 11" → 3).
2. **Hop-count journal:** after each race, the learner says "I started
   at ___, I hopped ___, I landed on ___."
3. **Race the adult:** the adult counts back slowly from the minuend
   while the learner uses the shorter move — the shortcut should win
   every time. Debrief: fewer hops wins.

*Alternate response mode:* hop a toy car along a table number line.

## Session D — Review circles (Sessions 4, 8)

**Goal:** re-check the lessons so far; re-teach from evidence.

Structure (repeat for L1, then L1–L2):

1. Re-ask 2–3 exit checks from the lessons (see answer key): L1's
   15 − 9 = 6; L2's 16 − 14 = 2.
2. Each learner task is tried orally first; written only when secure.
3. Any task missed twice becomes tomorrow's warm-up.

## Session E — Strategy stations, round 1 (Session 7)

**Goal:** choose between unknown-addend, count back, and count up for
each problem; name the strategy before solving.

Stations (learner rotates; adult checks the named strategy, not just the
answer):

1. 12 − 6 by unknown-addend: 6 + 6 = 12 → 6.
2. 17 − 3 by counting back: "16, 15, 14" → 14.
3. 15 − 13 by counting up: "14, 15" → 2.
4. 11 − 4 by unknown-addend: 4 + 7 = 11 → 7.

*Debrief:* "Which station was fastest? Which would you use again
tomorrow?" There is no single right strategy — only a well-chosen one.

## Session F — Back-to-ten stations (Session 10)

**Goal:** split every subtrahend by the loose ones so the first step
lands exactly on ten.

1. **Frame station:** build each on ten frames, remove loose ones first,
   then the rest. 14 − 5: split 5 into 4 + 1; 14 − 4 = 10; 10 − 1 = 9.
2. **Chain station:** write the full chain for each: 15 − 8:
   15 − 5 = 10, 10 − 3 = 7 → 7. 12 − 5: 12 − 2 = 10, 10 − 3 = 7 → 7.
   16 − 9: 16 − 6 = 10, 10 − 3 = 7 → 7. 13 − 6: 13 − 3 = 10, 10 − 3 = 7
   → 7. (Four different problems, one answer — a good moment to notice
   that back-to-ten lands on ten every time.)
3. **Check station:** check each answer by unknown-addend: 7 + 8 = 15 ✓;
   7 + 5 = 12 ✓; 7 + 9 = 16 ✓; 7 + 6 = 13 ✓.

*Alternate response mode:* draw the two-stage cross-out instead of
moving counters.

## Session G — Fact-family games (Session 12)

**Goal:** read two subtractions off every addition fact; cover-and-say
until the family is automatic.

1. **Triangle race:** the adult shows a triangle card; the learner names
   all four facts before the adult finishes counting to ten. Cards:
   (4, 9, 13): 4 + 9 = 13, 9 + 4 = 13, 13 − 4 = 9, 13 − 9 = 4.
   (7, 6, 13): 7 + 6 = 13, 6 + 7 = 13, 13 − 7 = 6, 13 − 6 = 7.
   (8, 5, 13): 8 + 5 = 13, 5 + 8 = 13, 13 − 8 = 5, 13 − 5 = 8.
2. **Domino families:** roll two dice (or draw a domino); total the
   pips, then name both subtractions. Example roll 9 and 8: total 17;
   17 − 9 = 8 and 17 − 8 = 9.
3. **Missing-part cover:** the adult builds a family with counters and
   covers one part; the learner names the covered part and the
   subtraction it answers.

## Session H — True/false and missing-number sorts (Session 14)

**Goal:** judge equations by computing both sides; solve both kinds of
missing-number problems with a check.

1. **True pile / false pile:** sort these cards, computing both sides
   first. True: 11 − 4 = 7; 8 = 13 − 5; 16 − 9 = 7. False: 14 − 7 = 6
   (14 − 7 = 7); 12 − 8 = 5 (12 − 8 = 4); 15 − 6 = 8 (15 − 6 = 9). For
   each false card, say how to fix it (change the answer to the true
   value).
2. **Missing-number hunt:** 17 − ? = 8 → count up 9…17, nine hops → 9
   (check 17 − 9 = 8). ? − 7 = 6 → rebuild 6 + 7 = 13 (check 13 − 7 = 6).
   4 = _ − 9 → rebuild 4 + 9 = 13 (check 13 − 9 = 4).
3. **Fix-it round:** the adult writes 13 − 5 = 7 (false; 13 − 5 = 8).
   The learner fixes it two ways: change the answer (→ 8), or change a
   number on the left (e.g. 12 − 5 = 7). Both fixes are accepted when
   the learner can prove the repaired equation true.
