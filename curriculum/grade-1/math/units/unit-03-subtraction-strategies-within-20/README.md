# Unit 03 — Subtraction Strategies Within 20

**Grade:** 1 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #10](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/10), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Addition gave the learner a toolbox. Subtraction asks the same toolbox a new
question: how much is left, or how far apart are two numbers? In this unit the
learner builds five reliable subtraction strategies for differences within
20 — thinking addition (the unknown addend), counting back and counting up,
going back to ten, and using fact families — and learns to find missing
numbers and judge true/false equations with subtraction. Story problems that
mix addition and subtraction arrive next, in Unit 04, so the strategies here
must be secure before moving on.

## Essential question

How can we subtract without counting every single thing from one — and how
does addition help us do it?

## Prerequisites

From Unit 02 (track weeks 7–10) and Unit 01 (track weeks 3–6):

- Adding within 20 by counting on, turn-around pairs, make-ten, and
  doubles/near-doubles; fluency within 10
- Missing-addend equations (`8 + ? = 11`) solved by counting up
- The equal sign means "is the same amount as"; judging true/false equations
  by computing both sides
- Teen numbers as a ten plus ones; ten frames, number lines to 20, and
  counting forward and backward within 20

No story-problem instruction is required before this unit: every problem here
is a bare-number equation or a one-sentence take-away situation. Full story
problems of every situation type are Unit 04.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Solve subtraction within 20 as an **unknown-addend** problem — e.g. for
   10 − 8, find the number that makes 10 when added to 8 — by counting up
   from the subtrahend (1.OA.B.4, 1.OA.C.6).
2. **Count back** when the number taken away is small, and **count up**
   when the two numbers are close, and explain which choice fits the problem
   (1.OA.C.5, 1.OA.C.6).
3. **Decompose to a ten** ("back to ten") — e.g.
   13 − 4 = 13 − 3 − 1 = 10 − 1 = 9 — splitting the subtrahend so the first
   step lands exactly on ten (1.OA.C.6).
4. Use a **known addition fact and fact families** — e.g. knowing
   8 + 4 = 12, one knows 12 − 8 = 4 and 12 − 4 = 8 (1.OA.B.3, 1.OA.C.6).
5. Find the **missing number** in subtraction equations (missing subtrahend
   and missing minuend) and judge whether subtraction equations are **true
   or false**, explaining that the equal sign means "is the same amount as"
   (1.OA.D.7, 1.OA.D.8).

Fluency target for this unit: subtraction within 10 fluent by unit's end;
subtraction within 20 through strategy use. No timed tests.

## Vocabulary (taught with objects first, word second)

subtract · take away · difference · how many are left · count back ·
count up · unknown addend · back to ten · decompose · fact family ·
minuend (the number you start with) · subtrahend (the number taken away) ·
missing number · equal sign · the same amount as · true · false · equation

The words *minuend* and *subtrahend* are adult vocabulary for naming the
parts of a problem; the learner may say "the start number" and "the number
taken away" and still meet every objective.

## Differentiation (every session)

- **Language access:** teach each strategy with counters first, then name
  it. "Take away" and "how many are left" come before *difference*;
  "the start number" and "the number taken away" are always acceptable for
  *minuend* and *subtrahend*.
- **Alternate response modes:** point to hops on the number line, move
  counters, or draw dots instead of writing equations; the adult scribes
  written work when the learner isn't ready to write.
- **Support:** keep every new strategy inside 15 until its steps are fluent,
  then extend to 20; a number line and a ten frame stay on the table all
  unit. Learners who still count all the way from one re-run Lesson 01's
  cover-and-count game before new strategies are added.
- **Extension:** two-step strategy chains (e.g. solve 15 − 8 by back-to-ten
  and then check it by unknown-addend), "invent a problem where counting up
  beats counting back and prove it," and missing-minuend problems above 20
  with counters (e.g. ? − 9 = 14) as enrichment only — never as a gate for
  moving on.
- **Error-analysis habit:** every lesson includes one adult-made mistake
  for the learner to catch; catching the mistake counts as evidence, not
  as a trick.
