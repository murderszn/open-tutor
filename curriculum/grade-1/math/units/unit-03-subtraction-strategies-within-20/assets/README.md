# Assets — Unit 03: Subtraction Strategies Within 20

## count-back-ten-frames.png

- **File:** `count-back-ten-frames.png` (PNG, raster, 1920 × 1280)
- **Prompt (verbatim, first render — the version kept):** "A flat
  educational illustration for a first-grade math lesson on the
  back-to-ten subtraction strategy, plain white background, clean simple
  cartoon style, high contrast. TWO ten-frames side by side (each
  ten-frame is a white rectangle divided into 2 rows of 5 empty boxes
  with thin gray outlines). LEFT ten-frame: exactly 10 large red dots
  filling every box (top row 5 dots, bottom row 5 dots). RIGHT ten-frame:
  exactly 3 large blue dots in the top row only (3 dots, then 2 empty
  boxes; bottom row all 5 boxes empty). Count the dots very carefully:
  10 red dots and 3 blue dots, 13 total. A thick orange loop circles all
  3 blue dots in the right frame, and a second thick orange loop circles
  exactly 1 red dot in the left frame, showing the 4 dots that will be
  taken away for 13 minus 4. All dots clearly separated and individually
  countable. No text, no numbers, no letters, no people, no clutter."
- **Tool:** Muse built-in image generation (`media.generate_image`).
- **Date generated:** 2026-10-22 (first render kept; one rejected second
  render, see Factual review).
- **Learning purpose:** Used in
  [Lesson 02](../lessons/lesson-02-count-back-or-count-up.md) (teaching
  and Guided practice 4: picture study) and in the
  [unit assessment](../assessments/unit-assessment.md) (Task 6: picture
  study). The learner counts the dots, counts the looped dots, and
  reasons that taking the looped dots away leaves 11: 13 − 2 = 11.
- **Factual review (2026-10-22, inspected before linking):** dot counts
  verified against the prompt — left frame: 10 red dots (5 top, 5
  bottom) ✓; right frame: 3 blue dots in the top row, rest empty ✓;
  total 13 dots ✓; both frames are true 2 × 5 ten-frames ✓. The loops
  did NOT match the prompt: instead of one loop around all 3 blue dots
  and one loop around 1 red dot (4 dots marked), the render has one loop
  around 1 red dot and one loop around 1 blue dot (plus an empty box) —
  2 dots marked in total. The picture is therefore used ONLY for
  13 − 2 = 11, which is what it actually shows, and never for 13 − 4.
  A second render attempt, prompted with one ring per dot, was rejected
  and deleted: it drew each frame with 20 boxes instead of 10, so it was
  not a ten-frame picture at all. The 13 − 4 back-to-ten decomposition
  is taught with physical counters in Lesson 03, not with this picture.
  No text, numbers, letters, or people in the image. Red vs. blue dots
  are high-contrast and distinct.
- **Caption:** "An AI-generated picture of two ten frames. Left: 10 red
  dots. Right: 3 blue dots. Two dots have orange loops around them —
  one red, one blue. How many dots are left if the looped dots are taken
  away?"
- **Alt text:** "Two ten-frames on white. Left frame: 10 red dots. Right
  frame: 3 blue dots. One red dot and one blue dot each have an orange
  loop around them."
- **Text-only alternative** (carries every count the activities need, so
  the activity works without the image): "The picture shows 10 red dots
  in the left ten frame and 3 blue dots in the right ten frame — 13 dots
  in all. Two dots are looped in orange: one red dot and one blue dot.
  Taking those 2 away leaves 11 dots: 13 − 2 = 11."
- **Known limitations:** AI illustration, not a photograph — stylized
  flat shapes. The picture does not prove anything about subtraction;
  the learner verifies 13 − 2 = 11 with real counters in Lesson 02
  before trusting the loops. Color is never the only cue in unit
  activities: the same tasks are also done with physical counters and
  spoken counts. The original prompt's 13 − 4 marking was not delivered
  by the generator; the lesson was rewritten to match the picture that
  was actually produced, rather than describing a picture that does not
  exist.
