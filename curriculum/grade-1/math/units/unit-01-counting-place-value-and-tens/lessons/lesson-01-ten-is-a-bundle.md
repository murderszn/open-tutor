# Lesson 01 — Ten Is a Bundle

**Student goal:** I can make one bundle of ten out of ten loose ones, and I
know that one bundle counts as ten.
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Unit:** [U01](../README.md) · **Session 1 of 16**

## Materials

- 30 loose straws (or craft sticks, pencils, or linking cubes)
- Rubber bands or short pieces of string
- A tray to keep things from rolling

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

Count loose straws with the learner, one-to-one, up to 15. Ask "how many?"
and confirm the last number said is the total. If counting to 15 is not yet
stable, keep the lesson at 10–12 straws and re-check on Session 2; note it
for re-teaching.

## Teaching

Counting one by one gets slow when there are many things. Mathematicians have
a shortcut: **when we have ten ones, we bundle them into one ten.**

> "Ten is special. Whenever we collect ten loose straws, we tie them into one
> bundle. That bundle is one ten. We can count bundles instead of counting
> every straw."

Hold up ten loose straws. "Count with me: one, two, …, ten. Now I tie them.
This is one bundle. The bundle is ten straws, but I only have to count it
once: one ten." Repeat: show the loose straws inside the bundle, then the
single bundled object.

**Key vocabulary:** *bundle of ten* (ten ones tied together), *ten*
(one bundle), *ones* (loose single straws).

## Worked example 1 — bundling ten straws

The adult counts 10 straws aloud with the learner, one at a time, moving each
counted straw into a "counted" pile.

> "One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten. Ten loose
> straws — ten ones." *(ties the rubber band around them)*
> "Now it is one bundle. One bundle = ten straws. Count the bundles: **one
> ten.**"

**Reasoning to name aloud:** nothing changed about how many straws there
are — only how we hold them. Ten ones and one ten are the same amount in
different packaging.

## Worked example 2 — counting bundles and loose straws

The adult prepares 2 bundles and 3 loose straws in advance.

> "Count the bundles first: one ten, two tens — twenty." *(points to each
> bundle)*
> "Now the loose ones: one, two, three. Twenty and three more is
> **twenty-three.**"
> "So 2 bundles and 3 loose straws = 23 straws. We wrote '2' for the tens
> and '3' for the ones."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** count the big groups first (tens), then the
small ones (ones). The tens digit tells how many bundles; the ones digit
tells how many loose straws.

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds.

1. Count 10 straws with the adult, then tie them into a bundle together.
   *Hint: "Move each one into the counted pile as you say its number."*
2. The adult shows 1 bundle: "How many straws?" (Expected: ten.)
3. Build 2 bundles with the adult: "How many tens? How many straws?"
   (Expected: 2 tens, 20 straws.)
4. Show 1 bundle + 4 loose: "Count the bundles, then the loose ones. How
   many in all?" (Expected: 14.)
5. Error analysis: the adult holds 10 loose straws and 1 bundle and says
   "That's 11 tens." Ask: "Did I count right? What did I do wrong?"
   (Expected: the loose straws are ones, not tens — it's 1 ten and 10 ones,
   which is really 2 tens.)
6. The learner bundles 10 straws alone while the adult watches.

## Independent practice

The learner takes a pile of 23 straws, bundles as many tens as they can
(2 bundles), counts the loose ones (3), and announces "two tens and three
ones — twenty-three." The adult observes silently: did they count ten into
each bundle? Did loose ones stay loose?

*Alternate response mode:* point to bundles and loose straws with a pointer
stick instead of touching; for limited mobility, the learner directs the
adult's hands ("tie those ten").

## Application

Laundry math: the learner bundles 10 socks from the clean laundry into a
pair-stack "ten," then counts remaining singles. "How many socks? How many
tens and ones?" Bundling now has a purpose: keeping count without recounting.

## Exit check

The adult holds up 1 bundle of ten straws and 6 loose straws. "How many tens?
How many ones? How many straws in all?" Expected: "1 ten, 6 ones, 16 straws."
Record: secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** teach "bundle" by doing it first, then naming it.
  Accept home-language words for ten/ones alongside English.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing, or moving counted straws into a
  "done" bowl one at a time.
- **Floor-play variant:** sit on the floor and bundle ten blocks into a
  tower of ten; knock it down and rebuild.

## Extension

Bundle 3 tens and say the total (30). Or: "I have 4 bundles. How many
straws?" (40) — previews counting by tens.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) treating a bundle as "one straw" — always
follow with "one ten is how many straws?"; (2) re-counting bundled straws one
by one — that's fine early, then model counting bundles as units; (3) loose
straws mixed into the bundle count — keep bundles and singles in separate
zones on the tray.
