# Lesson 03 — Teen Numbers: Ten and Ones

**Student goal:** I can show any teen number (11–19) as one ten plus loose
ones, and I can read and write those numbers.
**Time:** 20–25 minutes · **Unit:** [U01](../README.md) · **Session 9 of 16**

## Materials

- 1 bundle of ten plus 9 loose straws (or a tens rod and single cubes)
- A ten frame drawn on paper
- Number cards 11–19

## Readiness check (2 minutes)

"Show me one ten" (learner holds up the bundle). "How many straws?" (ten.)
"Show me 5 ones." If the bundle-ten idea is shaky, re-do Lesson 01's
exit check before starting.

## Teaching

Teen numbers are tricky because their names don't sound like their meaning.
But every teen number is easy once you see it: **one ten, plus some loose
ones.**

> "Thirteen. Say it slowly: thir-teen. It hides the secret: **three** plus
> **ten**. One bundle and three loose straws — that's thirteen."
> "Fourteen is four and ten. Fifteen is five and ten. Every teen is a ten
> with ones beside it."

Build each teen with the learner as you say it: place the bundle, then add
loose straws one at a time, counting "eleven, twelve, thirteen…" Lay the
number cards 11–19 in order under the builds.

**The pattern to name:** the "1" in a teen number means **one ten**; the
other digit means **how many loose ones**.

**Key vocabulary:** *teen*, *ten and ones* (e.g., "13 is 1 ten and 3 ones").

## Worked example 1 — building thirteen

> "I want 13. First the ten." *(places the bundle)* "Now count ones with me:
> one, two, three." *(lays 3 loose straws beside the bundle)*
> "One ten and three ones. Count all of them: 10, 11, 12, 13." *(point to
> the bundle for 10, then each loose straw)*
> "Write: **13**. The 1 is the ten. The 3 is the ones."

**Reasoning to name aloud:** counting from 10 by ones connects the bundle
picture to the counting sequence.

## Worked example 2 — eighteen and nineteen

Build 18, then add one straw.

> "One ten and eight ones is **eighteen**. Write: 18."
> "Now one more straw: one ten and nine ones — **nineteen**. Write: 19."
> "What if I add one more?" *(lets the learner try 10 loose ones)* "Ten ones
> becomes another ten! Two tens is **twenty**." *(ties them; previews Lesson 02's decade numbers)*

**Reasoning to name aloud:** ten ones can always be traded for one more
ten — that's why 19 is followed by 20, not by "nineteen-teen."

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

Do 4–6 short rounds.

1. "Build 12." Learner places bundle + 2 loose, writes 12.
   *Hint: "Start with the ten."*
2. "Build 16." Learner builds and writes 16.
3. The adult builds 1 bundle + 7 loose: "What number?" (Expected: 17.)
4. Show card "14": learner builds it with the bundle and straws.
5. Error analysis: the adult builds 1 bundle + 5 loose and says "That's 51."
   Ask: "Right or wrong? Fix it." (Expected: wrong — 51 would be 5 bundles
   and 1 loose; this is 15, one ten and five ones.)
6. Count from 10 to 19 pointing at the ordered builds: 10, 11, 12 … 19.

## Independent practice

The learner draws the bundle as a rectangle with a band and loose straws as
short lines for 13, 15, and 17, writing each numeral beside the drawing. The
adult checks: one rectangle + 3 lines = 13; one rectangle + 5 lines = 15; one
rectangle + 7 lines = 17.

*Alternate response mode:* build with cubes only (no writing); the adult
records the numerals the learner dictates.

## Application

Crayon box: "Your crayon box holds exactly 16 crayons. Show me with a ten
train and loose crayons. How many tens? How many ones?" (1 ten, 6 ones.)
Packaging with fixed counts makes tens-and-ones visible in real life.

## Exit check

The adult shows 1 bundle + 6 loose straws. "What number? Tell me the tens and
the ones, then write it." Expected: "16 — one ten and six ones." Record:
secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** say the teen names slowly and link each to its
  parts ("sixteen = six-ten"); accept home-language number words.
- **Alternate response mode:** build and say only; adult writes numerals.
- **Floor-play variant:** the learner is "the ten" standing with arms wide
  while the adult adds "ones" children one at a time; count the group.

## Extension

"What number is 1 ten and 0 ones?" (10 — previews decade numbers.) Or build
all nine teens in order, 11 to 19, and read the whole row.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) reversed digits — "31" for thirteen: always
build first, then write, reading the build as "1 ten and 3 ones"; (2) the
"teen" names sounding like the ones digit comes first (they do!) — naming the
bundle "ten" aloud fixes the order; (3) counting the bundle as "one" instead
of ten — point and say "ten" for the whole bundle.

expected responses for the guided error-analysis round and exit check.
