# Project — The Ten-Bundle Shop

**Sessions:** 11 (Part 1), 14 (Part 2), 15 (open house + formative quiz)
**Time:** 20–25 minutes per part · **Unit:** [U01](README.md)

The learner opens a pretend shop where everything is stocked in bundles of
ten and loose singles. They build the inventory, write price cards as
tens-and-ones, "sell" items to the adult customer, and prove every sale by
counting tens first, then ones. The project applies all four unit objectives
in one sustained, playful investigation.

## Materials

- 100+ small counters (buttons, beans, blocks, or straw bundles)
- Rubber bands or string for bundling
- Price-card blanks (paper slips), crayons, a tray per "shelf"
- The learner's hundred chart from Lesson 04

## Part 1 — Stock the shelves (Session 11)

1. **Choose 5 shop items** (e.g., red buttons = apples, blue blocks = toy
   cars, beans = cookies). Give each a name and draw a small sign.
2. **Stock each shelf** with a two-digit count the learner chooses
   (11–99): bundle the tens, lay out the loose ones beside each bundle.
   Write a stock card for each shelf: "3 tens and 4 ones = 34 apples."
3. **Check:** the adult counts one shelf with the learner to verify the
   card matches the stock. Fix any mismatch by re-bundling, not by erasing.

*Deliverable: 5 shelves, each with bundles, loose ones, a stock card showing
tens, ones, and the total.*

## Part 2 — Price and sell (Session 14)

1. **Price cards:** each item costs its stock count's "tens and ones"
   (e.g., 34 apples cost "3 tens and 4 ones"). Write the price card as a
   numeral and as tens-and-ones words.
2. **Sell:** the adult customer "buys" quantities: "I'd like 25 apples —
   show me 2 tens and 5 ones." The learner counts out bundles and singles
   from the shelf, narrating tens first.
3. **Compare sale:** the adult buys two items and asks, "Which cost more —
   34 apples or 25 toy cars? Prove it with tens first." The learner states
   the comparison and writes the symbol.

*Deliverable: price cards for 5 items; a sale log of 3 purchases with the
quantities counted out and one written comparison.*

## Open house (Session 15)

The learner gives the adult a 3-minute shop tour: points to each shelf,
reads the stock card ("2 tens and 5 ones — 25"), and answers one customer
question ("Do you have more apples or cars? How do you know?"). The adult
scores with the rubric below.

## Rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bundling (Obj. 1) | Every ten loose items is bundled; loose singles < 10 on every shelf | Most shelves bundled correctly; 1–2 shelves need re-bundling | Bundles missing or loose piles of 10+ remain |
| Reading/writing numerals (Obj. 2) | All stock and price cards show correct numerals to 99 | 1–2 numeral errors, corrected with a hint | Numerals don't match the stock |
| Tens-and-ones explanation (Obj. 3) | Every card and sale narrated as "tens and ones" correctly | Narration needs prompting ("how many tens?") | Cannot separate tens from ones |
| Comparison (Obj. 4) | Compares two prices tens-first with the correct symbol | Correct answer after re-checking the ones | Compares ones first or wrong symbol |
| Shopkeeping (communication) | Tour is clear; answers the customer question with evidence | Tour needs 1–2 prompts | Cannot explain the stock |

**Ready to move on:** 7+ points with at least 1 in every row. **Re-teach:**
any 0 — revisit the matching lesson's guided practice before Unit 02.

## Safety and accessibility

All materials are household items; supervise small parts. Every part of the
project can be done orally or by pointing, with the adult scribing cards.
Color is never the only cue — shelves are also labeled with the item name
and count.
