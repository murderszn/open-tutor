# Assets — Unit 01: Counting, Place Value, and Tens

## tens-and-ones-compare.png

- **File:** `tens-and-ones-compare.png` (PNG, raster)
- **Prompt (verbatim):** "A flat educational illustration for a first-grade
  math lesson on place value, plain white background, clean simple cartoon
  style, high contrast. TOP ROW: exactly 3 red bundles (each bundle is a
  tight stack of horizontal straws tied with one yellow band) side by side on
  the left, then a gap, then exactly 4 single loose red straws standing
  upright on the right. BOTTOM ROW: exactly 2 blue bundles (each bundle is a
  tight stack of horizontal straws tied with one yellow band) side by side on
  the left, then a gap, then exactly 5 single loose blue straws standing
  upright on the right. Count the loose straws very carefully: 4 in the top
  row, 5 in the bottom row. All straws clearly separated and individually
  countable. No text, no numbers, no letters, no people, no clutter."
- **Tool:** Muse built-in image generation (`media.generate_image`).
- **Date generated:** 2026-10-09 (final accepted version; three earlier
  attempts were rejected during factual review — counts of 10 and 11 loose
  straws in the bottom row instead of the requested 9 — and were discarded,
  not kept in the repo).
- **Learning purpose:** Used in
  [Lesson 05](../lessons/lesson-05-comparing-two-digit-numbers.md) (Worked
  example 1: count bundles and singles to find 34 and 25, then compare by
  tens first) and in the
  [unit assessment](../assessments/unit-assessment.md) (Task 4: identify the
  two numbers and record the comparison). The learner points to bundles,
  counts by tens, counts the singles, and records `34 > 25` with reasoning.
- **Factual review (2026-10-09, inspected before linking):** counts verified
  against the prompt — top row: 3 red bundles, 4 loose red straws ✓; bottom
  row: 2 blue bundles, 5 loose blue straws ✓. Every item clearly separated
  and individually countable; no text, numbers, letters, or people in the
  image. Red vs. blue rows are high-contrast and distinct. Each bundle is
  understood as one ten per the unit's teaching (the learner verified ten
  straws per bundle with their own manipulatives in Lesson 01).
- **Caption:** "An AI-generated picture of straw bundles. Top row: red
  bundles and loose straws. Bottom row: blue bundles and loose straws.
  How many in each row — and which row has more?"
- **Alt text:** "Two rows of straw bundles on white. Top row: 3 red bundles
  of straws and 4 loose red straws. Bottom row: 2 blue bundles of straws and
  5 loose blue straws."
- **Text-only alternative** (carries every count the activities need, so the
  activity works without the image): "The picture shows 3 bundles and 4
  loose red straws (34 straws) in the top row, and 2 bundles and 5 loose
  blue straws (25 straws) in the bottom row."
- **Known limitations:** AI illustration, not a photograph — stylized flat
  shapes. A bundle is treated as one ten by agreement (Lesson 01); the
  picture itself does not prove each bundle holds exactly ten straws, so the
  learner first bundles their own ten straws by hand. Color is never the only
  cue in unit activities: the same tasks are also done with size/arrangement
  sorts (banded stacks vs. singles) and the text alternative. Do not use this
  image as evidence of real observations; counts in learner activities come
  from the learner's own manipulatives.
