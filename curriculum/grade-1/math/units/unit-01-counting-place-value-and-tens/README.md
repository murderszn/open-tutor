# Unit 01 — Counting, Place Value, and Tens

**Grade:** 1 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #10](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/10), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Counting big numbers is easier when we bundle. In this unit the learner
discovers that ten loose ones can be bundled into one ten, counts by tens to
120, reads and writes teen and decade numerals as "ten(s) and ones," counts
forward from any number within 120, and compares two-digit numbers by tens
first, then ones, recording with `>`, `=`, and `<`.

## Essential question

How does grouping things in tens help us count big numbers and compare them?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the kindergarten track:

- Counting to 100 by ones and tens; numerals written 0–20
- One-to-one counting of sets to 20; comparing numbers 1–10
- Composing/decomposing 11–19 as ten ones + some ones (re-taught here, not assumed)

No new addition/subtraction instruction is required before this unit.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Bundle 10 loose ones into one ten (straws, cubes, or drawings), count by
   tens to 120, and read and write the decade numerals 10–120.
2. Count forward from any starting number within 120; read and write numerals
   to 120.
3. Explain any two-digit number as tens and ones: teen numbers as one ten plus
   ones, decade numbers as groups of ten with zero ones.
4. Compare two two-digit numbers using the meanings of the tens and ones
   digits, and record the comparison with `>`, `=`, or `<`.

## Vocabulary (taught with objects first, word second)

bundle of ten · ten · ones · tens · teen · decade number · hundred chart ·
more than / less than / equal to · greater than / less than (symbols `>`, `<`) ·
count by tens · zero ones · compare

## Materials

Household or dollar-store items (see the track
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) for the full safe-materials
list): 120+ small counters (dried beans, buttons, or blocks), straws or
craft sticks with rubber bands for bundling, tens rods or linked-cube trains
of 10, a hundred chart (print or draw), number cards 0–120, the generated
illustration [`assets/tens-and-ones-compare.png`](assets/tens-and-ones-compare.png),
paper and crayons. No sharp tools; supervise small parts; check food-allergen
safety before using food as counters.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts oral, pointing, or drawing responses; the adult scribes
when the learner isn't ready to write. Bundle and loose groups are also
distinguishable by arrangement (banded stacks vs. singles), not only by
color. Each lesson includes a seated-table and a floor-play variant, a
language-access support, an alternate response mode, and an extension. The
generated illustration ships with a text-only alternative containing every
count the activities need.
