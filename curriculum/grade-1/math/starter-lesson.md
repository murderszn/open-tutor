# Starter Lesson — Make a ten to solve within 20

**Status:** draft supplement. **Time:** three 20–25 minute sessions. **Goal:** Represent addition/subtraction within 20 with objects or drawings and explain how making a ten can help.

## Adult preparation and readiness

Gather 20 counters or draw dots, two-color pencils, and a number line 0–20. Readiness check: ask learner to show 8 counters and count on from 8 to 12. If counting is not stable, use smaller totals and touch each item once. Read aloud and accept manipulatives, drawings, oral explanation, or written equations.

## Teach and model

Explain: “Ten is a useful group. I can break a number apart to fill a ten, then add the leftovers.” Model 8 + 5: take 2 from 5 to fill 8 to 10; 10 + 3 = 13. For subtraction, explain that removing 4 from 13 can first remove 3 to reach 10, then 1 more to reach 9.

**Worked example 1:** 9 + 4. Move 1 of the 4 counters to make 10 with 9. Three remain. 10 + 3 = 13.

**Worked example 2:** 14 − 6. Remove 4 from 14 to reach 10; remove 2 more. 10 − 2 = 8. Check: 8 + 6 = 14.

**Pacing and roles:** Session 1: adult models examples and guides tasks 1–2. Session 2: guide tasks 3–4 with counters, then learner independently solves task 5. Session 3: complete task 6 and exit check with adult review.

## Guided and independent tasks

1. Build 7 + 6 with counters; move some to make ten. Record the equation.
2. Solve 8 + 5 using a drawing or number line.
3. Solve 12 − 3 by counting back or crossing out counters.
4. Solve 15 − 7 by reaching ten first.
5. Error analysis: a learner says 9 + 4 = 14 because “9 plus 1 is 10, and 3 are left, so 10 + 3 is 14.” Find the arithmetic error and correct it.
6. Story: “There are 8 red leaves and 5 yellow leaves. How many leaves altogether?” Show a model and answer.

**Answer checks:** 1) 7 + 6 = 10 + 3 = 13; 2) 13; 3) 9; 4) 8; 5) 10 + 3 = 13; 6) 13 leaves. Other strategies are acceptable if accurate and explained.

## Exit check and product

Ask learner to solve 9 + 6, show a model, and describe one step. Expected: 9 + 1 + 5 = 10 + 5 = 15. Product: one word problem with model/equation and a dictated or written explanation.

## Review rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model | Counters/drawing match the quantities | Model needs adult setup | Rebuild with smaller numbers |
| Computation | Correct sum/difference within 20 | Correct after a hint/check | Re-teach with objects |
| Explanation | Describes making a ten or another valid strategy | States steps with prompting | Oral modeling needed |

**Adult guidance:** Watch for counting a moved counter twice or treating an equation as a symbol puzzle without quantities. Use two colors and a ten-frame. Offer large counters, tactile marks, short sets, movement breaks, and oral/pointing responses. Household manipulatives are enough; supervise small pieces and do not use food if allergies are unknown. This is a selective practice lesson, not the grade's scope-and-sequence.
