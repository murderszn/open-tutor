# Unit 04 — Teacher Guide

For the guiding adult (teacher, tutor, or family member). Learner-facing pages
never link here without a clear "for the adult" label. Pair with
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md), which holds every solution.

## How to use this unit

- **Session length:** 15–20 minutes, four sessions a week. Stop while energy
  is high; a split session beats a forced one.
- **Response modes:** accept oral, pointing, drawing, and acted-out answers
  everywhere. You scribe dictated words. Written output stays at 2–3 word
  labels plus dictated sentences.
- **Language:** teach each vocabulary word with a person, picture, or
  role-play first and the word second. Welcome the home language alongside
  English.
- **Pacing:** the 16-session table in the [unit README](README.md) is the
  order that matters. Combine or split sessions freely; do not skip the
  project (Sessions 11–12) — it is where Objectives 5–6 are demonstrated,
  not just described.
- **Neutrality:** keep every example fictional and local (Willow Street).
  Never name a real politician, party, or election; never ask how anyone in
  the learner's home votes. One neutral sentence answers a real-world
  question ("grown-ups vote to choose people like the mayor"), then return
  to the classroom decision.
- **Safety:** role-play only. Never ask the learner to call an emergency
  number, approach a stranger in uniform, or visit a government building
  alone. Any real-helper visit is arranged and supervised by the adult.
- **Privacy:** keep the learner's vote, proposal cards, quiz notes, and
  assessment evidence in private storage. Nothing with the learner's work
  or name enters this public repository. The only class-shareable artifact
  is the decision poster, which names the decision — not who voted which
  way.
- **The generated picture is not a photograph:** say so out loud in
  Practice B. The town scene is an invented drawing that helps us practice
  naming helpers.

## Misconception map (all five lessons)

- **L1 — "In charge means bossy."** Reframe with responsibility: the
  principal's job is the school's safety and learning, not ordering people
  around for fun.
- **L1 — Mayor vs. principal.** Place-size chant: classroom → teacher,
  school → principal, town → mayor.
- **L2 — "Helpers matter less."** Consequence test: "what breaks if nobody
  does this job?" Every helper breaks something real.
- **L3 — "Government is one person."** Always "a *group* of people" — the
  mayor leads it; workers do the jobs.
- **L3 — "Government does everything."** Shared/personal test: lunchbox vs.
  road.
- **L4 — Sorting by where the rule was heard.** Place test: "whose place
  is the hall — one class, or the whole school?"
- **L4 — "Fair means I like it."** Fairness is about everyone: the swings
  example catches this.
- **L5 — "Losing means I was wrong."** A vote says what the group will
  *try*, not who is right.
- **L5 — "Consensus means nobody objected."** Always ask "does anyone want
  something different?" out loud before calling it consensus.

## Midyear review (Week 18) — how to run it

Session 13 stations (adult prepares one prompt per station):

- **Unit 01 station:** "Name one right and one responsibility in our
  classroom; show me our discussion rule in action (one listening turn)."
- **Unit 02 station:** "Point to the library and the park on this simple
  Willow Street map; is the park near or far from the school?"
- **Unit 03 station:** "Order these 3 day-cards; tell me one thing that
  changed from long ago and one thing that stayed the same."
- **Unit 04 station:** "Put the decision steps in order; name one
  government job and one rule that protects fairness."

Session 14: reteach the single weakest objective across the four stations,
using that unit's original lesson example (not a new one), for 10 minutes;
then run the project gallery share (learner presents the decision poster
and explains how the class decided).

## Reteach stations (for Sessions 14–15 follow-up)

- **Objective 1 (roles):** classroom/school pair only (teacher, principal),
  then add town roles one at a time with place-size chant.
- **Objective 2 (helpers):** bus driver + crossing guard pair — the two
  most visible helpers — with the consequence test.
- **Objective 3 (government):** road/pothole example only; repeat the
  one-sentence definition three times across the session.
- **Objective 4 (rules):** hall rule only, then re-sort with the place test.
- **Objective 5 (decision steps):** bear/space vote story only, acted out
  with tokens.
- **Objective 6 (reflection):** sentence frames: "It went well when ___"
  and "Next time we could ___."

## Readiness and next steps

- **Unit assessment 16–20:** secure — start Unit 05 with its planned
  opening retrieval (needs/wants sort warm-up).
- **11–15:** developing — reteach the weakest objective in Unit 05 Week
  19's first session, then proceed.
- **0–10:** emerging — reteach the two weakest objectives before Unit 05,
  using the stations above; keep sessions short and role-play heavy.
- **Midyear note for the adult:** Week 18's quiz also re-checks Units
  01–03. If a Unit 01–03 objective is the weakest, that is the Session 14
  reteach — the midyear review exists to catch exactly that.
