# Project — Our Classroom Decision

**Sessions 11–12 (Week 17) · about 15–20 minutes each, plus one week of
carrying out the decision.**

## The investigation

The class makes one **real** decision together — not a pretend vote, but a
choice the class will actually live with for a week. The decision must be
safe either way and small enough to carry out: examples are which book to
read aloud next, how to arrange the reading corner, which song opens the
morning, or which fair rule to add to the reading corner (from Practice D).
The adult vetoes only unsafe or unkind options, and says so plainly
("that one is not safe, so it cannot be voted on").

## Materials

- Proposal cards (blank cards, 2–3 words each, adult-scribed)
- One token per voter (buttons, counters, or pebbles — identical tokens)
- A cup or box per choice, or one cup with color-coded tokens
- The decision poster (large paper): *Our decision / How we decided /
  How it went*
- The learner's reflection sheet (a drawing box + one dictated sentence)

## Steps

**Session 11 — propose and discuss.**

1. The adult frames the question: "We need to decide ___." (For example:
   "We need to decide how to arrange the reading corner.")
2. Each person may propose one idea, on one card. The adult scribes every
   idea exactly as spoken — no editing, no ranking.
3. The group narrows to two or three choices. If there are more, the adult
   combines duplicates ("two people said more pillows — that is one idea")
   and drops ideas that are unsafe or impossible, saying why.
4. Each proposer says one reason for their idea. Everyone else listens
   quietly — Unit 01's discussion rule, enforced kindly.
5. The adult checks for consensus: "Does anyone want something different?"
   If everyone agrees on one choice, record it as a consensus decision and
   skip to step 7. Otherwise, go to Session 12.

**Session 12 — vote, tally, carry out, reflect.**

6. **Vote:** each voter places exactly one token for exactly one choice.
   The adult counts aloud, twice, and writes the tally on the poster.
7. **Announce:** the adult names the winner and the count. Every voter,
   including voters for losing choices, says one thing they will enjoy
   about the winner.
8. **Do:** the class carries out the decision the same day (move the
   pillows, start the book, post the rule).
9. **Live with it for a week.** The adult notes one observation per day
   (one line each) on the poster: what worked, what snagged.
10. **Reflect (end of the week, 5 minutes):** the learner draws or dictates
    one thing that went well and one thing to change next time. The adult
    scribes the reflection onto the poster.

## Deliverables

1. Proposal cards (one per idea, scribed verbatim).
2. The decision poster: question, choices, tally (or "consensus"), the
   decision, and the week-long observation notes.
3. A reflection drawing + one dictated sentence per learner ("went well /
   change next time").
4. (Optional) A photo-free gallery share: the learner presents the poster
   to another class or adult and explains how the class decided — no
   learner names, votes, or work leaves private storage.

## Rubric (20 points, adult-scored)

| Criterion | 5 — strong | 3 — developing | 1 — emerging |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listening and discussion (5) | Listened quietly to at least two other ideas; did not interrupt | Listened to one idea; needed one reminder | Interrupted repeatedly or refused to listen |
| Proposing or supporting (5) | Proposed an idea with a reason, or supported another's idea with a reason | Proposed or supported an idea without a reason | Did not propose or support any idea |
| Voting fairly (5) | Cast exactly one vote; accepted the result without re-arguing | Voted once but grumbled or needed prompting to accept | Tried to vote twice, voted for someone else, or refused the result |
| Carrying out + reflecting (5) | Helped carry out the decision; named one thing that went well and one change | Helped carry out; named only a "went well" or only a change | Did not help carry out, or could not reflect on the week |

**Scoring note:** the rubric scores *participation in a fair process*,
not whether the learner's idea won. A learner whose idea lost can score
full marks. If the decision reached consensus, score "voting fairly" on
accepting the consensus and the "does anyone want something different?"
check.
