# Lesson 5 — Making a Decision Together

**Session 9 · about 15–20 minutes.**

**Learner goal:** I can list the steps of a group decision and take part in
one fairly.

**Materials:** 4 step cards the adult draws or prints (ear = listen, hand
raised = propose, ballot box = vote, running shoes = do — adult reads labels
aloud); picture ballots for the mock vote (3 choices: read a bear story, read
a space story, read a dinosaur story — the adult reads them aloud).

**Readiness check (1 minute):** "What is one fair rule, and why is it fair?"
(Any fair rule + any everyone-reason counts.) Retrieval: "What do we call
the steps listen → propose → vote → do?" (Not taught yet — accept any guess,
then teach.)

## Explicit teaching

*(Adult reads or paraphrases; pause at the bold words.)*

A **decision** is what a group chooses after listening and voting. In our
classroom, decisions work in four steps. Say them with me:

1. **Listen.** Everyone gets a turn to speak, and everyone else listens
   quietly. No interrupting. (This is Unit 01's discussion rule, doing real
   work.)
2. **Propose.** Someone suggests an idea: "I **propose** we read the bear
   story." Proposing is not deciding — it is offering an idea for the group
   to consider.
3. **Vote.** Every person gets **one vote** — one choice, counted once.
   The choice with the most votes wins. Sometimes everyone agrees on the
   same choice; that is called **consensus**, and it is the happiest kind
   of decision.
4. **Do.** The group carries out the decision, even the people who voted
   differently. Then the group checks: did it work? What would we change
   next time?

Two rules keep voting fair. First: **one person, one vote** — nobody votes
twice, and nobody votes for someone else. Second: **losing gracefully** —
when your choice does not win, you do not re-argue it or spoil it. You try
the winner honestly, because next time the vote might go your way. That is
how a group stays friends while disagreeing.

A decision is not "the loudest voice wins" and not "the teacher always
chooses." Sometimes the teacher must decide for safety — that is authority
from Lesson 1. But when a choice is safe either way, the group decides
together. That is the difference between being told and deciding.

## Worked examples (adult models the reasoning aloud)

**Example 1.** "Our class must choose a story for Friday. Mia proposes the
bear story. Jon proposes the space story. We listen to both, then we vote:
bear gets 4 votes, space gets 3 votes. What happens? The **bear story**
wins, and on Friday we read the bear story — including Jon, who voted for
space. Jon was heard, he voted, and now he tries the winner. That is a fair
decision."

**Example 2.** "The same vote, but Mia votes twice — once for herself and
once 'for Jon, who is absent.' Is that fair? No. **One person, one vote.**
Jon is absent, so Jon does not vote today. If we let one person vote twice,
the count lies about what the group wants."

**Example 3.** "Everyone in the class proposes the same idea: more pillows
in the reading corner. No vote is even needed — that is **consensus**.
Consensus is not 'nobody disagreed because nobody spoke.' It is 'everyone
spoke and everyone agreed.' The adult checks: 'Does anyone want something
different?' Silence after a real question is agreement; silence because
nobody was asked is not."

**Example 4 (the deliberate error).** "The vote chooses the dinosaur story.
Sam, who wanted the bear story, says, 'That's dumb, I'm not listening on
Friday.' Is Sam deciding fairly? No. Sam's job after losing is to try the
winner honestly and say later what he thought of it. Losing a vote is not
losing a friend — and Friday's story is still a story."

## Guided practice (adult prompts, learner responds; 4 tasks)

1. "Put the four step cards in order: listen, propose, vote, do. What
   comes first? What comes last?"
2. "Our class votes: bear story 4, space story 3. Which story wins? What
   does the space-story voter do on Friday?"
3. "Can one person vote twice? Why not?" *(Hint if stuck: "Would the count
   still tell the truth about the group?")*
4. "Everyone agrees on more pillows, without anyone disagreeing. What is
   that called?" *(Accept "consensus" or "everyone agreed.")*

## Independent practice

The learner runs a real mini-decision with the adult: choose tomorrow's
opening song from two picture choices. The learner proposes one choice
aloud (or points), the adult proposes the other, they listen to each other's
one reason, then each casts one vote (a token in a cup), the adult counts
aloud, and the learner says what the group will do tomorrow.
**Text alternative:** the learner votes by placing a token instead of
speaking, and points to the winning card.

## Application

"At home or in class today, notice one decision someone makes alone and one
decision a group makes together. Tell someone which kind each one was."

## Exit check

The learner lists the four steps in order (cards face-up as support) and
says what a person who loses a vote should do. The adult records: *secure*
(4 steps in order + graceful losing), *developing* (3 steps or losing
unclear), *emerging* (0–2 — reteach with the bear/space vote story only).

## Supports

- **Language access:** pre-teach *propose / vote / decision* with the
  learner's home language alongside English; accept answers in any language.
- **Alternate response mode:** ordering step cards, voting with tokens,
  or pointing instead of speaking.
- **Movement:** cup an ear for listen, raise a hand for propose, drop a
  token for vote, march in place for do — the steps become a chant.

## Extension

"Decision reporter": the learner tells the class one decision they saw a
group make (at home, at play, in a story) and names which steps the group
used — a bridge into the project sessions.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** learners think voting means "my side must win" or
that the loser was "wrong." Repeat the frame every time: a vote does not
say who is right; it says what the group will *try*. Also watch for
"consensus = nobody objected": always ask "does anyone want something
different?" out loud before calling it consensus. **Answer-key reference:**
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) §L5.
