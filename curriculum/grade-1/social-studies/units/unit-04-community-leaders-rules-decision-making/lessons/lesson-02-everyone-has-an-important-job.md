# Lesson 2 — Everyone Has an Important Job

**Session 3 · about 15–20 minutes.**

**Learner goal:** I can name helpers who are not leaders and say why their
jobs matter.

**Materials:** 6 helper cards the adult draws or prints (bus driver, school
librarian, custodian, crossing guard, baker, mail carrier — adult reads
labels aloud); the 5 Lesson 1 role cards for comparison.

**Readiness check (1 minute):** "Name one person in authority from yesterday
and one job they do." (Any of the five counts.) Retrieval: "Who is in
charge of our classroom? Who is in charge of the whole school?"

## Explicit teaching

*(Adult reads or paraphrases; pause at the bold words.)*

Yesterday we met people in authority — people in charge of a place. But a
community does not run on leaders alone. Most of the work is done by
**community helpers**: people whose jobs help everyone, even though they are
not in charge of a place.

Meet six helpers on Willow Street:

- The **bus driver** drives children to school safely. Without her, many
  children could not get to school at all.
- The **school librarian** takes care of the books and helps children find
  stories. Without him, the library would be a room full of books nobody
  could find.
- The **custodian** cleans the school, fixes small problems, and keeps the
  building safe and clean. Without her, the school would get dirty and
  unsafe fast.
- The **crossing guard** stops traffic so children can cross the street
  safely. Without him, crossing the road to school would be dangerous.
- The **baker** makes bread and food for the town. Without her, one of the
  town's shops would have empty shelves.
- The **mail carrier** brings letters and packages to homes and shops.
  Without him, news and packages would not arrive.

Here is the big idea: **all people, not just official leaders, play
important roles in a community.** A leader decides; a helper does. Both are
needed. A mayor cannot drive every bus, shelve every book, and sweep every
hall — and a bus driver cannot make town rules. The town works because
everyone does their own important job.

## Worked examples (adult models the reasoning aloud)

**Example 1.** "It rains hard on Willow Street. The school floor gets muddy.
Who fixes that — the mayor? No. The **custodian** mops the floor and puts
out a wet-floor sign. Her job matters because a muddy floor is slippery and
unsafe. The mayor leads the town; the custodian keeps the school safe to
walk in. Different jobs, both important."

**Example 2.** "A child wants a book about dinosaurs but cannot find it on
the shelf. Who helps? The **school librarian**. He knows where the dinosaur
books live and helps the child choose one. His job matters because a library
only helps people if someone keeps it in order and helps readers."

**Example 3.** "The class is going to the library on foot. Who stops the cars
so the class can cross Main Street? The **crossing guard**. He is not the
mayor and not the principal — but without him, the walk would not be safe.
Important does not mean in charge."

**Example 4 (the trap).** "Someone says, 'Only leaders matter, because
leaders decide everything.' Is that right? No. Leaders decide some things —
but the decision only works if helpers carry it out. The mayor can decide
the town needs clean streets; the helpers sweep them. Deciding and doing are
both important."

## Guided practice (adult prompts, learner responds; 4 tasks)

1. "Who drives children to school safely — a leader or a helper? Why does
   that job matter?" *(Hint if stuck: "What would happen if no bus came?")*
2. "Who keeps the school clean and safe to walk in? Is that person in
   charge of the school, or a helper?"
3. "Name a helper from the cards and tell me one thing the town could not
   do without them."
4. "Is the crossing guard a leader? Why or why not?" *(Accept: "No — he
   does an important job, but he is not in charge of a place.")*

## Independent practice

The learner sorts all 11 cards (5 authority + 6 helper) into two piles:
"in charge of a place" and "helper job." Then the learner picks one helper
card and finishes the sentence (adult scribes): "Without the ___, we could
not ___."
**Text alternative:** the learner points to the pile and acts out the helper
job instead of speaking the sentence.

## Application

"Today, notice one helper doing their job — at school, on the street, or in
a shop. Tonight, tell someone what that helper did and why it mattered."
The adult notes whether the learner reports one the next day.

## Exit check

The learner names 2 helpers who are not leaders and says why each job
matters. The adult records: *secure* (2 helpers + reasons), *developing*
(2 helpers, reasons shaky), *emerging* (0–1 — reteach with the bus driver
and crossing guard pair, the two most visible helpers).

## Supports

- **Language access:** pre-teach *helper / important* with the learner's
  home language alongside English; accept answers in any language.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing to cards, acting out jobs, or
  drawing the helper instead of naming aloud.
- **Movement:** mime driving a bus, shelving a book, sweeping a floor —
  one motion per helper.

## Extension

"Helper hunt": the learner draws one helper they saw outside school (with
adult help) and labels it with 2–3 words, to add to the class helper wall
in the project sessions.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** learners rank jobs — "the mayor matters most."
Never rank; compare by consequence instead: "what would break if nobody did
this job?" Every job in the lesson breaks something real if it stops. Also
watch for "helper = less important": correct gently every time — "helpers
do most of the work in a town." **Answer-key reference:**
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) §L2.
