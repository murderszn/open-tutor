# Unit 04 — Practice Sessions

Adult-led practice for Grade 1 Social Studies, Unit 04. All responses may be
oral, pointing, drawing, or adult-scribed. Written recording stays to 2–3
word labels and dictated sentences. Solutions are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md) — **for the guiding adult only**.

## Practice A — Role–responsibility match (Session 2)

Lay out the 5 Lesson 1 role cards and 5 responsibility cards (teaches and
keeps the class safe; helps all teachers and keeps the school safe; helps
lead the town and looks after shared things; puts out fires and rescues
people; keeps people safe and helps when someone is lost). The learner
matches each role to its responsibility.

1. Teacher → ?
2. Principal → ?
3. Mayor → ?
4. Firefighter → ?
5. Police officer → ?

**Challenge round:** the adult swaps two responsibility cards and asks,
"Is this match right? How do you know?" (The learner must fix the swap with
a reason, not just a guess.)

**Movement version:** the learner stands behind the role card they would
ask for help in each spoken situation ("a fire," "a lost child," "a broken
swing in the park").

## Practice B — Who helps in the town scene? (Session 4)

Look together at the unit's generated illustration:

![A friendly children's-book drawing of a small town street: a teacher reads a picture book to two children on a bench, a firefighter in a yellow helmet holds a coiled hose beside a red fire truck, a police officer in a dark blue uniform helps a child cross at a striped crosswalk, a librarian carries a stack of books outside a stone building, and a person in a suit waves from the steps of a town hall with a flag.](assets/community-helpers-town-scene.png)

*Caption: Community helpers at work on Willow Street — a practice
illustration, not a photograph of real people.*

**Say this out loud before the activity:** "This picture is a drawing an
artist made up to help us practice. The people in it are invented, like
Mayor Sam. Real helpers are real people we meet with a grown-up."

**Tasks:**

1. Point to the teacher. What is she doing? (Reading to children.)
2. Point to the firefighter. What is he holding, and what is his job?
3. Point to the police officer. Who is she helping, and how?
4. Point to the librarian. What is she carrying, and where is she going?
5. Point to the person waving on the town hall steps. Which role from
   Lesson 1 is he? (The mayor — he helps lead the town.)
6. Which helper in the picture is NOT in authority? (The librarian — she
   does an important job but is not in charge of a place.)

**Text-only alternative (answer the same 6 questions without the picture):**
A teacher reads a book to two children on a bench. A firefighter in a yellow
helmet holds a coiled hose beside a red fire truck. A police officer in a
dark blue uniform holds a child's hand at a striped crosswalk. A librarian
carries a stack of books out of a stone building. A person in a suit waves
from the steps of a town hall with a flag. Ask: who is each person, and what
is each person's job?

## Practice C — Government does / does not do (Session 6)

Sort these 6 cards into "the government does this" and "the government
does not do this":

1. Fix a road with a pothole.
2. Buy a child new shoes.
3. Keep the library open.
4. Choose what one family eats for dinner.
5. Put streetlights on a dark street.
6. Run the fire department.

**Discussion after the sort:** "What test did you use?" (Accept: "shared
thing or one person's thing." The government looks after shared things.)

## Practice D — Rules sort + make a fair rule (Session 8)

**Part 1 — sort.** Sort these 6 rule cards onto the classroom / school /
town mats:

1. Raise your hand to speak. (Classroom)
2. Walk in the hall. (School)
3. Do not litter in the park. (Town)
4. Listen while others talk. (Classroom)
5. Dogs on leashes. (Town)
6. Use a quiet voice near other classes. (School)

For each card, the learner also says safety or fairness (or both).

**Part 2 — make a fair rule.** The learner invents one fair rule for the
reading corner, says whether it protects safety or fairness, and the adult
scribes it on a card. (Example: "One book at a time, so everyone gets a
turn" — fairness.) The card joins the project wall for Sessions 11–12.

## Practice E — Mock vote with picture ballots (Session 10)

The class (or the learner + adult, with stuffed animals as extra voters
only if the learner wants them — every vote still counts once per voter)
chooses Friday's read-aloud from three picture ballots: a bear story, a
space story, a dinosaur story.

**Steps (the adult runs them in order):**

1. **Listen:** each voter says one reason for their choice; everyone else
   listens quietly.
2. **Propose:** the choices are already proposed — the adult names all
   three before voting, so no choice is hidden.
3. **Vote:** each voter places exactly one token in the cup for their
   choice. The adult counts aloud, twice.
4. **Do:** the adult announces the winner and writes it on the Friday plan.
   Everyone says one thing they will enjoy about the winning story —
   including voters who chose differently.

**Debrief questions:**

1. Did everyone get exactly one vote? How do we know?
2. What should a voter whose story lost do on Friday?
3. Was this a consensus or a vote? (A vote — unless everyone chose the
   same story, in which case: consensus.)

## Read-aloud A — *Mayor Sam's Busy Morning* (Session 8)

*(Adult reads aloud with voices; about 150 words.)*

> Mayor Sam woke up early on Willow Street. First she walked to the park,
> where a swing was broken. "That swing belongs to everyone," she said.
> "We must fix it." She called the town's workers, and they promised to fix
> it by Friday.
>
> Next she visited the library, where Ms. Patel the librarian was stacking
> books. "The library is open late tonight," said Ms. Patel. "Everyone is
> welcome." Mayor Sam smiled. "A library for everyone — that is a shared
> thing done right."
>
> Last she stopped at the school crossing, where Mr. Ortiz the crossing
> guard was stopping cars. "Thank you," said Mayor Sam. "You keep children
> safe, and I do not do your job — you do."
>
> On the way home, Mayor Sam thought: "I help lead the town. But the town
> works because everyone does their job — the workers, the librarian, the
> crossing guard, and me."

**Response tasks:**

1. Name two shared things in the story and who looks after each.
2. Is Ms. Patel a leader or a helper? How do you know?
3. What does Mayor Sam mean by "you do your job — I do not do it"?
4. Draw one scene from the story and label it with 2–3 words (adult
   scribes a dictated sentence if the learner wants one).

## Read-aloud B — *Our Class Chooses* (Session 10)

*(Adult reads aloud with voices; about 140 words.)*

> On Monday, Teacher Ms. Rosa said, "Our reading corner needs a new rule.
> We will decide it together."
>
> First, everyone listened. Ana proposed, "One book at a time, so everyone
> gets a turn." Ben proposed, "Shoes off in the corner, so it stays clean."
> The class listened to both ideas without interrupting.
>
> Then came the vote. Each child placed one token in a cup. Ms. Rosa counted
> aloud, twice: "One book at a time — five votes. Shoes off — four votes."
>
> "One book at a time wins," said Ms. Rosa. Ben smiled. "I voted for shoes
> off, but I will try one book at a time. It is fair — everyone gets a
> turn."
>
> On Tuesday, the class tried the new rule. It worked. Ana said, "Next time
> we can vote on shoes off too." And Ben said, "That is how deciding
> together works."

**Response tasks:**

1. List the four decision steps in the story, in order.
2. How many votes did each idea get? Did anyone vote twice? How do you
   know?
3. Ben lost the vote. What did he do? Was that fair deciding? Why?
4. What will the class do next time, according to Ana? (Vote on the other
   idea — losing ideas are not thrown away forever.)
