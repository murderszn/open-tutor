# Unit 04 — Community Leaders, Rules, and Decision Making

Grade 1 · Social Studies · Weeks 15–18 · issue #13, section U04 (draft for review).

## Essential question

*Who helps our community run, and how do we decide things together?*

## What this unit teaches

A first grader already knows that grown-ups are in charge of different places:
a teacher runs the classroom, a principal runs the school, a mayor helps run
the town. This unit turns that everyday knowing into civics. The learner meets
five people in **authority** — teacher, principal, mayor, firefighter, police
officer — and names one **responsibility** of each; learns that people who are
not leaders also do important jobs (the bus driver, the librarian, the
custodian, the crossing guard); learns what a **government** is in one
sentence — a group of people whose job is to make rules for a whole town and
to run the things everyone shares — and names one thing it does; sorts
**rules** by where they belong (classroom, school, town) and explains why one
rule exists; and then practices real democracy at classroom size: listen,
propose, **vote**, carry out the decision, and reflect on how it went. The
unit closes with "Our Classroom Decision," a project in which the class makes
one real decision together. Week 18 is the track's **midyear review**: the
learner re-checks Units 01–04 objectives, and the adult re-teaches the
highest-need objective before the second half of the year.

**Fictional-town rule (from the track scope-and-sequence):** all examples are
set in the fictional town of Willow Street, already familiar from Unit 03, and
use invented characters (Mayor Sam, Principal Lee, Teacher Ms. Rosa). No real
politician, election, or town is named, and no learner is asked about their
own family's voting or civic life. The mock votes in this unit are about
classroom choices only — which book to read, how to arrange the reading
corner — never about real candidates or parties. If the learner asks about a
real election they heard about, the adult answers briefly and neutrally
("grown-ups vote to choose leaders") and returns to the classroom decision.

**Family-neutral rule:** nothing in this unit assumes a family structure.
Characters are named by role (teacher, neighbor, librarian), never by family
position. Any story the learner volunteers about their own home stays in
private storage — it is never written into this public repository.

## Prerequisites

From the Week 1–2 diagnostic and Units 01–03
([scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md), Section 5): the learner
follows turn-taking discussions and discussion rules (Unit 01), uses everyday
location words and reads a simple picture map (Unit 02), and orders events and
uses a source to answer a question (Unit 03). This unit re-uses Unit 01's
rules language on purpose: "rules" from Unit 01 return here sorted by setting
and explained by purpose. No reading level is assumed; all written recording
stays to 2–3 word labels, dictated sentences, drawings, and picture ballots.
An AI-generated picture in this unit is an **illustration of a fictional
town, never a photograph of real people** — Practice B says this out loud.

## Measurable objectives (adult-assessed by end of Week 18)

1. Name at least 4 of the 5 authority roles taught here — teacher, principal,
   mayor, firefighter, police officer — and say one responsibility of each
   ("the principal is in charge of the whole school").
2. Explain that people who are not leaders also do important jobs: name at
   least 2 community helpers who are not in authority and say why each job
   matters ("the bus driver gets children to school safely").
3. Explain in their own words what a government is ("a group of people whose
   job is to make rules for the town and run the things we share") and name
   at least one thing a government does (keeps people safe, takes care of
   roads and parks, runs the library, runs the schools).
4. Sort at least 5 of 6 rule cards into the right setting (classroom, school,
   town) and explain why one rule exists, using safety or fairness ("we walk
   in the hall so nobody gets hurt").
5. Take part in a real group decision: listen while others speak, propose or
   support an idea, cast one vote, and accept the result without re-arguing
   it (the adult may prompt "the vote decided; we try it and see").
6. Carry out the class decision and say one thing that went well and one
   thing to change next time ("reading corner worked; we need more pillows").

## Vocabulary

Introduced with people, pictures, and role-play first; the word follows the
idea.

| Word | Grade-1 meaning used in this unit |
|---|---|
| **authority** | Being the person in charge of a place or a job |
| **leader** | A person other people look to for decisions and help |
| **teacher** | The person in charge of learning in a classroom |
| **principal** | The person in charge of a whole school |
| **mayor** | The person chosen to help lead a town or city |
| **firefighter** | A helper who puts out fires and rescues people |
| **police officer** | A helper who keeps people safe and enforces rules |
| **responsibility** | A job someone is counted on to do |
| **community helper** | Any person whose job helps the community |
| **government** | A group of people whose job is to make rules for a town and run the things everyone shares |
| **rule** | An agreement about what to do (or not do) in a place |
| **law** | A rule for a whole town, state, or country |
| **fair** | Treating people in a way that is right and equal |
| **propose** | To suggest an idea for the group to consider |
| **vote** | To choose by marking or saying a choice; each person gets one |
| **decision** | What the group chooses after listening and voting |
| **consensus** | When everyone in the group agrees on the same choice |

**A note on "government" in this unit:** the only definition used is the
one-sentence version above, with town-level examples (roads, parks, library,
fire department, schools). State and national government, branches, and
elections of real officials are out of band and are not taught here; the
adult answers curious questions briefly and neutrally and returns to the
classroom decision.

## For the guiding adult — read before Week 15

- **Retrieval warm-up (Session 1, 2 minutes):** re-use Unit 01's rules
  language — "What is a rule we agreed on in our classroom? Who reminds us
  about it?" — then ask, "Who is in charge of the whole school? Who helps
  lead the whole town?" This links rules (Unit 01) to authority (this unit)
  before any new word is taught.
- **Neutrality:** keep every example fictional and local. Never name a real
  politician, party, or election, and never ask how anyone in the learner's
  home votes. If a real-world question comes up, answer in one neutral
  sentence and return to Willow Street.
- **Safety:** role-play only. Never ask the learner to call an emergency
  number, approach a stranger in uniform, or visit a real government
  building alone. A visit or interview with a real helper happens only with
  the adult arranging and supervising it.
- **The generated picture is not a photograph:** Practice B says this out
  loud. The town scene is a drawing an artist (here, an AI tool) made up to
  help us practice naming helpers. Real helpers are real people we meet with
  an adult, not pictures.
- **Privacy:** the learner's vote, proposal cards, and reflection stay in
  private storage. The class-shareable artifact is the decision poster from
  the project, which names the decision — not who voted which way.
