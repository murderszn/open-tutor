# Unit 04 — Answer Key

**For the guiding adult only. Never place beside a learner question.**
Every item below was worked independently against the lesson text; open
responses list the acceptable range, not a single "correct" sentence. When
the learner's answer is defensible but different, accept it and note the
reasoning.

## §L1 — Lesson 1: People in authority

- **Guided 1** ("in charge of learning in a classroom"): **teacher**.
- **Guided 2** ("in charge of a whole school"): **principal**.
- **Guided 3** ("fire in a wastebasket"): **firefighter** — putting out
  fires is exactly his responsibility; the mayor leads the town and the
  teacher teaches, neither fights fires.
- **Guided 4** ("lost, needs help finding a grown-up"): **police officer**
  — keeping people safe and helping when someone is lost is her
  responsibility; the principal's responsibility is the school.
- **Independent** (5 cards, role + one job each): teacher — teaches and
  keeps the class safe; principal — helps all teachers and keeps the school
  safe; mayor — helps lead the town and looks after shared things;
  firefighter — puts out fires and rescues people; police officer — keeps
  people safe and helps when someone is lost or hurt.
- **Exit check**: 3 roles + jobs = secure; 3 roles, jobs shaky =
  developing.

## §L2 — Lesson 2: Everyone has an important job

- **Guided 1** ("drives children to school safely"): a **helper** (bus
  driver). It matters because without her, many children could not get to
  school at all.
- **Guided 2** ("keeps the school clean and safe to walk in"): the
  **custodian**, a **helper** — not in charge of the school (that is the
  principal).
- **Guided 3** (any helper + consequence): accept any of bus driver /
  librarian / custodian / crossing guard / baker / mail carrier with a
  true "without them, we could not ___" consequence.
- **Guided 4** ("is the crossing guard a leader?"): **No** — he does an
  important job (stopping traffic so children cross safely) but is not in
  charge of a place.
- **Independent** (11-card sort): "in charge of a place" pile — teacher,
  principal, mayor, firefighter, police officer; "helper job" pile — bus
  driver, school librarian, custodian, crossing guard, baker, mail
  carrier. Sentence frame example: "Without the bus driver, we could not
  get to school."
- **Exit check**: 2 helpers + reasons = secure.

## §L3 — Lesson 3: What is a government?

- **Guided 1** ("in your own words, what is a government?"): accept any
  version of **"a group of people whose job is to make rules for the town
  and run the things we share."**
- **Guided 2** ("road with a big hole"): **shared thing**; the **town
  government** fixes it (road workers sent by the town).
- **Guided 3** ("child's shoes wear out — does the government buy new
  ones?"): **No** — shoes belong to one person, not everyone; the
  government looks after shared things.
- **Guided 4** (one thing a government does): any of keeps people safe
  (fire department, police), takes care of shared places (roads, parks,
  streetlights), runs the library and schools, makes rules for the whole
  town (laws).
- **Independent** (7-card sort): shared pile — road, park, library, fire
  truck, school, streetlight; personal pile — house. Sentence example:
  "The government fixes the road."
- **Exit check**: definition + one job = secure.

## §L4 — Lesson 4: Rules in different places

- **Guided 1** ("raise your hand to speak"): **classroom**; **fairness**
  (everyone gets a turn to speak).
- **Guided 2** ("drive on the right side of the road"): **town** (a law);
  **safety** (if everyone drove on any side, cars would crash).
- **Guided 3** ("stay with your grown-up in a parking lot"): **town**
  (a parking lot is a shared town place); **safety** (cars cannot see
  small children well).
- **Guided 4** ("only the tallest children may use the swings"): **not
  fair** — it leaves some children out for no safety reason; fairness is
  about everyone, not one child.
- **Independent** (6 lesson cards): raise your hand → classroom;
  walk in the hall → school; stay with a grown-up in a parking lot →
  town; do not litter in the park → town; return library books on time →
  town or school (accept either with a reason); drive on the right side →
  town. Sentence example: "The rule walk in the hall exists because
  running could hurt someone."
- **Exit check** (3 new cards): "listen while others talk" → classroom;
  "dogs on leashes" → town; "use a quiet voice near other classes" →
  school; plus one safety-or-fairness reason for any of them.

## §L5 — Lesson 5: Making a decision together

- **Guided 1** (step order): **listen → propose → vote → do**.
- **Guided 2** ("bear 4, space 3"): the **bear story** wins; the
  space-story voter reads the bear story on Friday and tries it honestly.
- **Guided 3** ("can one person vote twice?"): **No** — one person, one
  vote; otherwise the count lies about what the group wants.
- **Guided 4** ("everyone agrees on more pillows"): **consensus** (or
  "everyone agreed" — but only after the adult asked "does anyone want
  something different?").
- **Independent** (mini-decision): procedure check — learner proposed or
  pointed to a choice, listened to the adult's reason, cast exactly one
  token, and stated what the group will do tomorrow. Any choice may win;
  score the process, not the choice.
- **Exit check**: 4 steps in order + graceful losing ("try the winner
  honestly") = secure.

## §Practice A — Role–responsibility match

1. Teacher → teaches and keeps the class safe.
2. Principal → helps all teachers and keeps the school safe.
3. Mayor → helps lead the town and looks after shared things.
4. Firefighter → puts out fires and rescues people.
5. Police officer → keeps people safe and helps when someone is lost.
   **Challenge round:** any fixed swap is correct if the learner gives the
   place-size or job reason ("the principal's job is the whole school, not
   one classroom").

## §Practice B — Who helps in the town scene?

1. Teacher — reading a picture book to two children on a bench.
2. Firefighter — holding a coiled hose beside the red fire truck; his job
   is putting out fires and rescuing people.
3. Police officer — holding a child's hand at the striped crosswalk; she
   keeps people safe crossing the street.
4. Librarian — carrying a stack of books out of the stone building; she
   takes care of books and helps readers find stories.
5. The waving person on the town hall steps is the **mayor** — he helps
   lead the town.
6. NOT in authority: the **librarian** — an important helper, not in
   charge of a place. (The teacher is in charge of a classroom, so the
   librarian is the intended answer; if the learner argues the teacher is
   "not a leader like the mayor," accept the reasoning and re-teach the
   place-size idea.)

## §Practice C — Government does / does not do

Does: fix a road with a pothole; keep the library open; put streetlights
on a dark street; run the fire department. Does not: buy a child new
shoes; choose what one family eats for dinner. Test: shared thing vs. one
person's thing.

## §Practice D — Rules sort + make a fair rule

Part 1: raise your hand → classroom (fairness); walk in the hall → school
(safety); do not litter in the park → town (safety and fairness); listen
while others talk → classroom (fairness); dogs on leashes → town
(safety); use a quiet voice near other classes → school (fairness — or
safety of learning; accept either with a reason). Part 2: any fair rule
for the reading corner with a safety or fairness reason (example: "One
book at a time, so everyone gets a turn" — fairness).

## §Practice E — Mock vote

Debrief: (1) Yes — one token per voter, counted aloud twice. (2) The
losing voter tries the winning story on Friday and says one thing they
enjoy about it. (3) A vote — unless every voter chose the same story, in
which case consensus. The winning story is whatever the count says; score
the process.

## §Read-aloud A — *Mayor Sam's Busy Morning*

1. Two shared things: the park swing (looked after by the town's workers)
   and the library (kept open by the town government; Ms. Patel the
   librarian runs it day to day). Also accept: the school crossing
   (crossing guard) and the roads.
2. Ms. Patel is a **helper** — she does an important job but is not in
   charge of a place.
3. The mayor leads the town but does not do everyone's jobs; the town
   works because each person does their own job.
4. Drawing: any scene from the story, labeled with 2–3 words (adult may
   scribe a dictated sentence).

## §Read-aloud B — *Our Class Chooses*

1. Steps in order: **listen → propose → vote → do**.
2. "One book at a time" — 5 votes; "shoes off" — 4 votes. Nobody voted
   twice: one token per child, counted aloud twice.
3. Ben accepted the result and will try "one book at a time"; yes, that is
   fair deciding — losing a vote is not losing a friend, and the winner is
   tried honestly.
4. Next time the class can vote on the shoes-off idea — losing ideas are
   not thrown away forever.

## §Project — Our Classroom Decision

Score with the 20-point rubric in [project.md](project.md). Key rule: score
participation in a fair process, not whether the learner's idea won. A
learner whose idea lost can score full marks. For a consensus decision,
score "voting fairly" on accepting the consensus after the "does anyone
want something different?" check.

1. 5 role + job pairs (see §L1 Independent), 1 point each = 5.
2. **Firefighter** + reason (fires are his responsibility; the mayor
   leads the town and does not fight fires) = 1.
3. 2 helpers + reasons (see §L2), 1 point each = 2.
4. Government definition, any version of the one-sentence definition = 1.
5. One government job (see §L3 Guided 4) = 1.
6. 4 rule cards: raise your hand → classroom; stay in your line → school;
   do not litter → town; return library books → town or school with a
   reason = 4.
7. One safety-or-fairness reason with evidence ("running could hurt
   someone"; "everyone gets a turn") = 1.
8. Full order listen → propose → vote → do = 1.
9. **Bear story** wins (5 vs. 2); the space-story voter reads the bear
   story and tries it honestly = 1.
10. **Not fair** — one person, one vote; voting for an absent friend breaks
    the count = 1.
11. The class's actual decision + the learner's part in carrying it out
    (any true pair) = 1.
12. One "went well" + one "change next time" (any honest pair) = 1.
    **Total 20.** 16–20 secure; 11–15 developing; 0–10 emerging (see
    [teacher-guide.md](teacher-guide.md) §Readiness and next steps).
