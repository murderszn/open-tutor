# Unit 05 — Needs, Wants, Producers, and Consumers

Grade 1 · Social Studies · Weeks 19–22 · issue #13, section U05 (draft for review).

## Essential question

*When we cannot have everything, how do we choose — and who makes the things we use?*

## What this unit teaches

A first grader already makes little choices every day: which snack, which
game, which book first. This unit turns those everyday choices into
economics. The learner sorts **needs** (things our bodies and lives must
have: food, water, a safe place to sleep, clothes that fit) from **wants**
(things we would like but can live without: a second toy, a treat, a new
game); learns that **scarcity** — not enough of something for every want —
is why choices are necessary; compares a **benefit** (a good thing about a
choice) with a **cost** (what is given up by that choice) for two real
decisions, spend-or-save and share-or-keep; meets **producers** — people
who make goods or do services — and tells a **good** (a thing you can hold,
like bread or apples) from a **service** (help someone does, like a haircut
or a ride); takes the role of **consumer** — a person who uses or buys
goods and services — and traces one familiar product's journey in order
from where it is grown or made to where it is used; and learns how people
**earn** income by working and why people **save** some of what they earn
for later. The unit closes with "Our Class Market," a pretend market in
which the class makes goods, offers services, earns play tokens, and chooses
to spend, save, or share them.

All examples are set in the fictional town of Willow Street, already
familiar from Units 03–04, and use invented characters (Maya, a child on
Willow Street; the baker; the farmer; the barber; the shopkeeper). No real
money changes hands anywhere in this unit: the market runs on play tokens
(counters), and no prices in dollars are taught or used. That limit is
deliberate — the track scope-and-sequence reserves real currency and
market-price data for later grades.

**Family-neutral rule:** nothing in this unit assumes a family structure.
Characters are named by role or first name only, never by family position.
Nothing the learner volunteers about their own home is written into this
public repository; learner work stays in private storage.

## Prerequisites

From the Week 1–2 diagnostic and Units 01–04
([scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md), Section 5): the learner
follows turn-taking discussions (Unit 01), reads a simple picture map and
uses location words (Unit 02), orders events on a timeline strip and uses a
source to answer a question (Unit 03), and has taken part in a group
decision (Unit 04). This unit re-uses Unit 03's timeline strip on purpose:
a product's journey is sequenced the same way a day's events were. No
reading level is assumed; all written recording stays to 2–3 word labels,
dictated sentences, drawings, and picture cards. An AI-generated picture in
this unit is an **illustration of a fictional market, never a photograph
of real people** — Practice D says this out loud.

## Measurable objectives (adult-assessed by end of Week 22)

1. Sort at least 5 of 6 picture/object cards into needs and wants, and
   explain one sort in their own words ("food is a need because our bodies
   need it to grow and stay strong").
2. Explain scarcity in their own words ("there is not enough for everyone
   to have everything they want") and, when two wants compete, make a
   choice and name what is given up.
3. For two personal decisions (spend now or save; share or keep), name one
   benefit and one cost of the choice made.
4. Name at least 3 producers and, for each, say whether they make a good
   or do a service, and name the good or service ("the baker makes bread
   — a good, because you can hold it").
5. Explain what a consumer is ("a person who uses or buys a good or a
   service") and put a familiar product's journey in order in at least
   3 steps (grown or made → moved → sold or used).
6. Explain how people earn income ("people work, and their work is paid
   for") and why people save ("to have something for later, or for
   something bigger"), and sort play tokens into earn–spend–save jars
   with a reason for each jar.

## Vocabulary

Introduced with objects, pictures, and play first; the word follows the
idea.

| Word | Grade-1 meaning used in this unit |
|---|---|
| **need** | Something our bodies or lives must have: food, water, a safe place to sleep, clothes that fit |
| **want** | Something we would like but can live without: a second toy, a treat, a new game |
| **scarcity** | Not enough of something for everyone to have everything they want |
| **choice** | Picking one thing when you cannot have everything |
| **benefit** | A good thing about a choice |
| **cost** | What you give up when you make a choice |
| **producer** | A person who makes a good or does a service |
| **consumer** | A person who uses or buys a good or a service |
| **good** | A thing you can hold and use: bread, apples, a chair |
| **service** | Help someone does for you: a haircut, a ride, a lesson |
| **earn** | To get pay or tokens for work you do |
| **income** | The pay people get for their work |
| **save** | To keep something for later instead of using it now |
| **spend** | To use tokens or pay to get a good or a service |
| **share** | To let someone else use something you have |
| **market** | A place where producers sell goods and services to consumers |

**A note on "cost" in this unit:** cost never means a dollar price here.
A cost is what you give up: if you spend your tokens on stickers now, the
cost is the puzzle you cannot get later; if you keep your crayons, the
cost is a friend who does not get to draw with them. Real prices, coins,
and bills are out of band and are not taught here.

## For the guiding adult — read before Week 19

- **Retrieval warm-up (Session 1, 2 minutes):** re-use Unit 03's timeline
  strip — "Put these three pictures in order: waking up, eating breakfast,
  coming to class." Then ask, "Where did your breakfast come from before it
  reached your table?" This links sequencing (Unit 03) to a product's
  journey (this unit) before any new word is taught.
- **No real money, ever, in this unit.** Tokens are counters. Never ask
  the learner what things cost in dollars, what anyone in their home
  earns, or what their home buys. If the learner volunteers a price, the
  adult answers briefly ("grown-ups handle paying") and returns to tokens.
- **Needs are not a test of a child's life.** Sort cards are about
  pictured objects, never about what the learner does or does not have at
  home. If a sort touches a sensitive spot, the adult moves to the next
  card without comment.
- **The generated picture is not a photograph:** Practice D says this out
  loud. The market scene is a drawing an artist (here, an AI tool) made up
  to help us practice naming producers and consumers. Real markets are
  real places we visit with an adult, not pictures.
- **Privacy:** the learner's token jars, choice reflections, and market
  cards stay in private storage. The class-shareable artifact is the
  market poster, which names the goods and services — not who chose what.
