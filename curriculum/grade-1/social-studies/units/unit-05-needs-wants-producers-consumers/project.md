# Project — Our Class Market

**Sessions 11–12 (Week 21) · about 15–20 minutes each, plus one market day
inside Session 12.** Play tokens only — no real money, no dollar prices,
no real buying or selling.

## The investigation

The class runs a pretend market for one session. Each learner is both a
producer and a consumer: first they make a good or offer a service, then
they earn play tokens for a market job, then they choose what to do with
their tokens — spend them at a classmate's stall, save them for a bigger
class project, or share one with a classmate who has none. The investigation
question, posted on the market poster, is: *"Who made what, who used it,
and what did you choose to do with your tokens — and why?"*

## Materials

- Drawing paper, crayons, and picture cards for making goods (paper
  apples, bread cards, bookmarks, woven-paper mats — anything flat,
  safe, and quick to make)
- Service offer cards (blank cards, 2–3 words each, adult-scribed): a
  song, a story told aloud, help sorting cards, a drawing done to order
- Play tokens (identical counters, 6 per learner) and 3 jars labeled with
  pictures: *earn*, *spend*, *save*, plus a share cup
- The market poster (large paper): *Our Market / Who made what / Who
  used it / What we chose*
- The learner's choice sheet (a drawing box + one dictated sentence:
  "I chose to ___ because ___.")

## Steps

**Session 11 — make goods, offer services, earn tokens.**

1. The adult frames the question: "Our market opens tomorrow. What will
   you make, or what help will you offer?" Each learner picks one good to
   make or one service to offer, and makes or rehearses it.
2. Each learner names their producer role in the frame: "I am a producer.
   I make ___ (a good)" or "I am a producer. I do ___ (a service)." The
   adult scribes the stall card (good/service + maker).
3. Market jobs: each learner does one job — sorting apple cards,
   stacking stall cards, counting tokens — and earns 6 play tokens for
   the job. The adult says out loud: "You earned these tokens by working.
   That is how people earn income."
4. The adult posts the stall cards on the market poster under *Who made
   what*.

**Session 12 — market day: spend, save, or share.**

5. **Shop:** each learner, as a consumer, visits stalls. Spending costs 1
   token per good or service (play-token counts only — never dollars).
   The learner may spend 0–3 tokens.
6. **Choose:** each learner sorts their remaining tokens into *save* (for
   a bigger class project later) and the share cup (1 token for a
   classmate who has none, if they wish). The learner completes the choice
   sheet: "I chose to ___ because ___." The adult scribes.
7. **Record:** the adult posts under *Who used it* (which goods/services
   found a consumer) and *What we chose* (how many tokens were spent,
   saved, and shared in total — counted aloud, twice).
8. **Reflect (5 minutes):** each learner draws or dictates one thing that
   went well at the market and one thing to change next time. The adult
   scribes the reflection onto the poster.

## Deliverables

1. Stall cards (one per learner: good or service + maker, scribed).
2. The market poster: investigation question, who made what, who used it,
   token totals (spent / saved / shared), and reflections.
3. A choice sheet per learner (drawing + one dictated "I chose to ___
   because ___" sentence).
4. (Optional) A photo-free gallery share: the learner presents the poster
   to another class or adult and explains one producer, one consumer, and
   one choice — no learner names, token counts per child, or work leaves
   private storage.

## Rubric (20 points, adult-scored)

| Criterion | 5 — strong | 3 — developing | 1 — emerging |
|---|---|---|---|
| Making a good or offering a service (5) | Made a finished good or rehearsed a service, and named it as a good or a service correctly | Made a good or offered a service, but the good/service label was wrong or missing | Did not make a good or offer a service |
| Naming producer and consumer roles (5) | Named a producer (including themselves) and a consumer (including themselves) with the good/service each used | Named one role correctly; the other was missing or swapped | Could not name either role |
| Earning and choosing with tokens (5) | Earned tokens for a job, and chose spend, save, or share with a reason ("I saved because ___") | Earned tokens and chose, but gave no reason | Did not earn tokens or could not choose |
| Fair play and sharing (5) | Took turns at stalls, accepted sold-out goods without argument, and shared or offered help at least once | Needed one reminder about turns or sharing | Refused turns, argued over goods, or would not share |

**Scoring note:** the rubric scores *participation in the market process*,
not what the learner "bought." A learner who saved every token can score
full marks, as can a learner who spent every token — what matters is the
reason given for the choice. Token totals on the poster are class totals
only; no individual child's token count is posted.
