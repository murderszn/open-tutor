# Lesson 5 — Consumers and Where Things Come From

**Session 9 · about 15–20 minutes.**

**Learner goal:** I can say what a consumer is, trace a product's journey,
and explain earning and saving.

**Materials:** a timeline strip (from Unit 03) and 4 journey cards for an
apple (apple tree in an orchard, a truck on a road, a market stall, a child
eating an apple); 6 play tokens and 3 jars labeled with pictures: *earn*,
*spend*, *save* (the adult reads the labels aloud; the learner need not
read them).

**Readiness check (1 minute):** "The baker makes bread. Good or service?"
(Good — Lesson 4 retrieval.) Retrieval: "Put these in order: waking up,
eating breakfast, coming to class." (Unit 03 timeline-strip retrieval.)

## Explicit teaching

*(Adult reads or paraphrases; pause at the bold words.)*

Producers make goods and do services. Who uses them? **Consumers** — people
who use or buy a good or a service. When you eat the baker's bread, you are
a consumer. When you ride the bus, you are a consumer of a service. When
you get a haircut, you are a consumer of a service. Everyone is both, at
different times: the baker is a producer when she bakes, and a consumer
when she buys apples from the farmer. Producer and consumer are jobs
people do, not kinds of people.

Goods travel before they reach us. Follow an apple on Willow Street. First,
the farmer **grows** it on an apple tree in an orchard — that is where it
is made. Then a truck **moves** it along the road to town — goods move
from place to place, just as Unit 02's maps showed people moving. Then the
shopkeeper **sells** it at the market stall. Then Maya, the consumer,
**uses** it — she eats it. Grown → moved → sold → used. Four steps, in
order, on the timeline strip. Bread travels the same way: wheat grows in a
field, the baker bakes it, the shop sells it, a family eats it. Every
familiar thing has a journey; the steps are always in order, and skipping
a step breaks the story.

How do people get the tokens or pay to be consumers? They **earn** it.
**Income** is the pay people get for their work: the baker earns pay for
baking bread; the bus driver earns pay for driving; the farmer earns pay
for growing apples. And people do not use everything they earn at once.
They **save** some of it — they keep it for later, or for something
bigger. The baker saves part of her pay to buy a new oven later. Saving is
not losing: saved tokens sit in the save jar, waiting for their job.

## Worked examples (adult models the reasoning aloud)

**Example 1.** "Trace the apple. Step 1: the farmer grows it on a tree.
Step 2: a truck moves it to town. Step 3: the shopkeeper sells it at the
market. Step 4: Maya eats it. Who is the producer here? The **farmer** —
she grew the apple. Who is the consumer? **Maya** — she uses the apple by
eating it. What if I swap steps 2 and 3 — sell it, then move it? That
cannot work: the apple must arrive in town before anyone can sell it.
Order matters."

**Example 2.** "Trace the bread. Step 1: wheat grows in a field. Step 2:
the baker bakes bread. Step 3: the shop sells the bread. Step 4: a family
eats it. Producer? The **baker** — and the farmer who grew the wheat, too;
one product can have more than one producer along its journey. Consumer?
The **family** that eats the bread. Good or service? Bread is a **good** —
you can hold it."

**Example 3.** "The baker earns pay for baking bread. She spends some of
her pay on flour for tomorrow's loaves, and she saves some of her pay for
a new oven later. Which part is earning? **Baking bread for pay** — her
work is paid for. Which part is saving? **Keeping some pay for the oven**
— not using it now, so it is there later. And the flour? That is spending
for her work — using pay now for something she needs tomorrow. Earning,
spending, and saving are three different jars, and grown-ups use all
three."

**Example 4.** "Maya helps sort apples at the market stall — a pretend
market job in our class, with play tokens, not real work. She earns 6
tokens. She spends 2 tokens on a paper apple for her stall, saves 3 tokens
for a bigger project later, and shares 1 token with a friend who has none.
Is Maya a producer or a consumer here? **Both**: a producer when she
sorts apples (a service), a consumer when she spends tokens on the paper
apple. And her save jar? Those 3 tokens are not lost — they are waiting."

## Guided practice (adult prompts, learner responds; 4 tasks)

1. "Put the 4 apple journey cards in order. Who is the producer? Who is
   the consumer?" *(Hint if stuck: "What happens first — growing or
   eating?")*
2. "Bread journey: wheat field, baker baking, shop stall, family eating.
   Which step is 'made'? Which step is 'used'?"
3. "The bus driver earns pay for driving. Is driving earning, spending,
   or saving? What about keeping some pay for later?"
4. "Maya earns 6 tokens and saves 3. Are the 3 saved tokens lost? Where
   are they, and what are they waiting for?"

## Independent practice

The learner orders the 4 apple journey cards on the timeline strip, then
orders a second set — bread cards (wheat field, baker baking, shop stall,
family eating) — and for each journey names one producer and one consumer.
Then the learner sorts 6 tokens into the earn–spend–save jars: 2 to spend
(on a paper apple), 3 to save (for a bigger project), 1 to share (put in
the share cup beside the jars), and dictates one reason per jar. The adult
scribes.
**Text alternative:** the learner points to journey cards in order and
drops tokens into jars instead of explaining aloud.

## Application

"Tonight, pick one thing you used today — a snack, a book, a ride. Tell
someone where you think it came from before it reached you, in 2–3 steps."
The adult notes whether the learner tries it unprompted the next day.

## Exit check

The learner orders a 3-step journey (grown/made → moved → sold/used) and
says what a consumer is. The adult records: *secure* (journey in order +
consumer explained), *developing* (journey ordered, consumer shaky),
*emerging* (journey out of order — reteach with the apple cards only).

## Supports

- **Language access:** pre-teach *consumer / earn / income / save* with
  the learner's home language alongside English; accept answers in any language.
- **Alternate response mode:** ordering picture cards and dropping tokens
  into jars instead of speaking sentences.
- **Movement:** crouch small for "grown," drive a pretend truck for
  "moved," hold out hands for "sold," mime eating for "used" — one motion
  per journey step.

## Extension

"Journey detective": the learner picks one more familiar thing (a pencil,
a banana, a library book) and guesses its journey in 3 steps — with the
question "does every journey have a truck?" held open for the project
(some journeys are short: the baker's bread travels from the bakery next
door, not across the country).

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** learners think producer and consumer are fixed
kinds of people ("bakers are producers, children are consumers"). Return
to Example 4: the same person is both at different times. Another common
mix-up: saving means losing — stress the jar: saved tokens are kept and
visible. **Answer-key reference:** [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) §L5.
