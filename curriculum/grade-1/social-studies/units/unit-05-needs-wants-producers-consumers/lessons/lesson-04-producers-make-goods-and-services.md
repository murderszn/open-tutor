# Lesson 4 — Producers Make Goods and Services

**Session 7 · about 15–20 minutes.**

**Learner goal:** I can name producers and tell a good from a service.

**Materials:** 6 producer cards the adult draws or prints before the session
(baker with bread, farmer with apples, carpenter with a chair, barber with
scissors, teacher with a book, bus driver at a bus); two labeled mats,
*good* and *service* (the adult reads the labels aloud; the learner need
not read them).

**Readiness check (1 minute):** "Maya saves her tokens for a puzzle. What
is the benefit? What is the cost?" (Benefit: puzzle sooner. Cost: no
stickers today — Lesson 3 retrieval.)

## Explicit teaching

*(Adult reads or paraphrases; pause at the bold words.)*

Where do the things we use come from? People make them or do them. A
**producer** is a person who makes a good or does a service. A **good**
is a thing you can hold and use: bread, apples, a chair. A **service** is
help someone does for you: a haircut, a ride, a lesson. The test is simple:
can you hold it in your hands? If yes, it is a good. If it is help that
someone does, and when they finish, nothing is left in your hands except
the result — a neat haircut, a safe ride, a new idea — it is a service.

Meet producers on Willow Street. The **baker** makes bread — a good, because
you can hold a loaf. The **farmer** grows apples — a good, because you can
hold an apple (and eat it). The **carpenter** makes chairs — a good,
because you can sit on it and touch it. The **barber** gives haircuts — a
service, because a haircut is help someone does; you cannot hold a haircut
in your hands. The **teacher** teaches lessons — a service, because
teaching is help someone does. The **bus driver** drives children to
school — a service, because a ride is help someone does.

Producers are not only shopkeepers. Anyone whose work makes something or
does something helpful is a producer — including the grown-ups whose jobs
we met in Unit 04, when they are making or doing rather than leading.

## Worked examples (adult models the reasoning aloud)

**Example 1.** "The baker takes flour and water, bakes all morning, and
puts warm loaves on the table. Good or service? A **good** — bread. You
can hold a loaf in your hands, carry it home, and eat it. The baker is a
producer because she made something."

**Example 2.** "The barber snips, combs, and brushes. When he finishes,
the child has a neat haircut. Good or service? A **service** — a haircut
is help someone does. You cannot hold a haircut in your hands; you wear
the result. The barber is a producer because he did something helpful."

**Example 3.** "The farmer picks apples from the trees and fills baskets.
Good or service? A **good** — apples. You can hold an apple, count apples,
bite an apple. The farmer is a producer because she grew something."

**Example 4.** "The bus driver drives the bus, stops at every corner, and
gets children to school safely. Good or service? A **service** — a ride is
help someone does. When the ride ends, you do not carry the ride home in
your hands; you carry the result — you arrived safely. The bus driver is a
producer because he did something helpful. And here is the Unit 04 link:
the bus driver is a helper, not a leader — and helpers can be producers
too."

## Guided practice (adult prompts, learner responds; 4 tasks)

1. "The carpenter makes a chair. Good or service? How does the hold-it
   test answer it?" *(Hint if stuck: "Can you hold a chair?")*
2. "The teacher teaches a lesson about apples. Good or service? Why?"
3. "The farmer grows apples and the barber gives a haircut. Which one
   makes a good, and which one does a service?"
4. "Name a producer you have seen at work, and say whether they made a
   good or did a service."

## Independent practice

The learner sorts the 6 producer cards onto the *good* and *service*
mats: goods — baker/bread, farmer/apples, carpenter/chair; services —
barber/haircut, teacher/lesson, bus driver/ride. Then the learner points
to one card on each mat and says the producer, the good or service, and
the hold-it test result ("I can hold bread, so it is a good"). The adult
scribes the two sentences.
**Text alternative:** the learner mimes the producer's work (kneading
bread, snipping scissors, steering a bus) instead of naming it aloud.

## Application

"Tonight, notice one producer at work — someone making something or doing
something helpful. Tell someone who it was and whether it was a good or a
service." The adult notes whether the learner tries it unprompted the next
day.

## Exit check

The learner names 2 producers and, for each, says good or service with the
thing or help named. The adult records: *secure* (2 producers + good/
service + item each), *developing* (2 producers, good/service shaky),
*emerging* (0–1 — reteach with the baker/barber pair first).

## Supports

- **Language access:** pre-teach *producer / good / service* with the
  learner's home language alongside English; accept answers in any language.
- **Alternate response mode:** sorting producer cards, or miming the
  work, instead of explaining aloud.
- **Movement:** pretend to hold a loaf (good) vs. pretend to snip scissors
  (service) — one motion per kind, every time.

## Extension

"Producer detective": the learner watches for one more producer during the
day — a cook, a cleaner, a singer — and brings back the good-or-service
verdict, with the question "can a person do both?" held open for Lesson 5's
consumer discussion. (Yes — the baker both bakes bread and sells it; the
farmer both grows and carries apples to market.)

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** learners call everything a shop sells a service,
or call every job a good. Return to the hold-it test every time. Another
common mix-up: thinking only shopkeepers are producers — stress that
teachers, drivers, and barbers produce services. **Answer-key reference:**
