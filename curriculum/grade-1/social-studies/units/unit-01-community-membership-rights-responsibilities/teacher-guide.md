# Unit 01 — Teacher Guide

For the guiding adult (teacher, tutor, or family member). Learner-facing pages
never link here without a clear "for the adult" label. Pair with
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md), which holds every solution.

## How to use this unit

- **Session length:** 18–20 minutes, four sessions a week. Stop while energy
  is high; a split session beats a forced one.
- **Response modes:** accept oral, pointing, drawing, and acted-out answers
  everywhere. You scribe dictated words. Written output stays at 2–3 word
  labels plus dictated sentences.
- **Language:** teach each vocabulary word with an object, picture, or walk
  first and the word second. Welcome the home language alongside English.
- **Pacing:** the 16-session table in the [unit README](README.md) is the
  order that matters. Combine or split sessions freely; do not skip the
  project (Sessions 12–13) — it is where the objectives come together.
- **Privacy:** keep the learner's drawings, dictated sentences, quiz notes,
  and assessment evidence in private storage. Nothing with the learner's work
  or name enters this public repository.

## Session-by-session adult directions

| Session | Do this | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (L1) | Teach *community/member* with the learner's real day; draw the circles together | The place-vs-people confusion — use "is it a person who spends time with us?" |
| 2 (Practice A) | Show the illustration; ask the three spotting questions; then the drawing | Learners who name only family — prompt "who do you learn with?" |
| 3 (L2) | Teach the three rights with gestures (ear / arms-around-self / welcoming arms) | "Rights = anything I want" — rehearse "everyone has it at the same time" |
| 4 (Read-aloud A) | Read *Maya's Raised Hand* with voices; do 3–4 response tasks | Monday-vs-Tuesday contrast is the whole point — slow down there |
| 5 (L3) | Teach responsibilities; run the detective question ("whose right does it protect?") | "Jobs are punishments" — narrate your own adult responsibilities all week |
| 6 (Practice B) | Sort game; keep the three challenge fixes for the charter | Rushing the "why" — the sentence matters more than the sort |
| 7 (L4) | Teach the two jobs of rules (one finger: safe; two fingers: learn-and-play) | Agreement-vs-action blur — rehearse "the promise / keeping the promise" |
| 8 (Practice C) | Three baskets; celebrate rules that fit two baskets | Invented rule must do one of the two jobs — coach it into shape |
| 9 (L5) | Fairness demo with cups; make the discussion-rules poster together | "Fair = identical" — the ankle-chair example is the antidote |
| 10 (Read-aloud B) | Read *The Block Tower Vote*; run a REAL vote and honor the result | If the adult overrides the vote, the lesson un-teaches itself |
| 11 (Review) | Community-circle review game: toss a soft ball, catcher answers a prompt from Lessons 1–5 | Note which objectives wobble — they set Session 15 stations |
| 12 (Project 1) | Gather earlier work; learner chooses charter items; draw + dictate | Keep labels in the learner's own words — resist editing into adult prose |
| 13 (Project 2) | Gallery walk-through, signing, one same-day kept responsibility | The reflection answers seed Session 16 |
| 14 (Quiz) | [week-04_quiz.md](assessments/week-04_quiz.md), gently — "show me what you know" | Mark *got it / getting there / not yet* per item, plus one evidence line |

## Misconception map (all five lessons)

1. Community = place, not people (L1) → the "is it a person?" test.
2. Right = anything I want / something earned (L2) → "everyone has it at the
   same time; membership, not behavior, is the source."
3. Responsibilities = punishments; only kids have jobs (L3) → narrate adult
   responsibilities; frame jobs as belonging.
4. Rules = grown-ups saying no; rules identical everywhere (L4) → two jobs;
   shoes-on/shoes-off example.
5. Fair = identical shares; respect = never disagreeing (L5) → ankle-chair;
   model respectful disagreement.
6. Rights and responsibilities blurred (all) → the detective question, every
   time: find the job, then find the right it protects.

## Reteach guidance

- **Rights/responsibilities still blurred after Session 8:** replay Practice B
  with only four cards (two clear rights, two clear responsibilities), then add
  one challenge card per round. Use the sentence frame every time.
- **"Why rules?" answers stay at "because the teacher said so":** walk the
  learner through one rule removal ("what if we ran inside today?") and let
  them supply the consequences; then re-ask.
- **Discussion rules collapse in real talks:** shrink the group (even to two),
  shorten the talk (60 seconds), and coach one rule at a time — listening
  first, then turn-taking, then responding.
- **Charter choices are all adult-fed:** that is fine for Session 12 — offer
  two real choices per column ("heard or safe?") and let ownership grow in
  Unit 04's real decision-making work.

## What "ready to move on" looks like

Per the unit README: objectives 1–4 at least *developing*, objective 3's sort
*secure*. Concretely: the learner can name two communities, state one right
and one responsibility in their own words, sort rights from responsibilities
with reasons, follow the discussion rules with at most one reminder, and has
carried out one real responsibility. If the sort is not secure, re-teach
Sessions 5–8 before Unit 02 — Unit 02's retrieval warm-up assumes the
vocabulary but not mastery.

## Accessibility notes

- Textured or raised materials for low-vision learners; large-print, high
  contrast cards; color never the only cue (pair every color sort with a shape
  or label).
- Face the learner during discussions; visual sentence frames supplement
  listening; allow extra wait time before prompting.
- Every lesson has a table variant and a floor-play/movement variant — use the
  one that fits the learner's body today.
