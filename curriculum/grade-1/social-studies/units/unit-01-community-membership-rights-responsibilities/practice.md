# Unit 01 — Practice Sessions and Read-Alouds

Three practice sessions (Sessions 2, 6, 8) and two original read-aloud stories
(Sessions 4, 10). All response modes may be oral, pointing, or drawing; the
adult scribes dictated words. Solutions and acceptable responses are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md) §P.

## Practice A — Session 2: Spotting community in the picture

Uses [assets/classroom-community-scene.png](assets/classroom-community-scene.png)
(AI illustration; alt text, caption, and text alternative in
[assets/README.md](assets/README.md)).

1. **Look and find.** "This is a classroom community — people who spend time
   together and look after each other. Point to three members of this
   community." *(Anyone in the picture counts.)*
2. **Find a responsibility.** "Someone here is doing a job for the community.
   Point to them. What job are they doing?" *(Expected: the children tidying
   toys together; the child helping with the shoe.)*
3. **Find a right.** "Someone here is using a right — being heard, being safe,
   or being included. Point to them. Which right is it?" *(Expected: the child
   with the raised hand — right to be heard; children listening — protecting
   that right.)*
4. **My communities drawing.** The learner draws themselves in two of their
   own communities (Lesson 1 independent practice) and the adult labels the
   drawings with dictated words.

**Text-only alternative** (for learners who cannot see the image): "The
picture shows a teacher reading to seven children sitting in a circle on a
rug. One child has a hand raised. In the back, two children put toys on a
shelf together, and one child helps another child with a shoe." The same three
questions are asked about this description.

## Read-aloud A — Session 4: *Maya's Raised Hand* (original story)

*An original story for this unit — not a reproduction of any published work.*

> Maya loved circle time, but her words were fast and her hand was faster. On
> Monday she called out, "I know! I know!" while Jalen was still talking.
> Jalen's shoulders drooped. Their teacher, Ms. Okafor, waited until Jalen
> finished, then said, "Maya, Jalen had the floor. Your turn is coming."
> On Tuesday Maya raised her hand high and waited. Ms. Okafor nodded. "Maya,
> we are listening." Maya told the class about the robin's nest outside her
> window, and everyone learned something new. After circle time Jalen said,
> "I liked your robin story." Maya grinned. "I liked waiting. It felt good
> when you listened to me, too."

**Response activities** (choose 3–4):

1. "What right did Maya use on Tuesday?" *(Right to be heard.)*
2. "What responsibility did Maya practice on Tuesday?" *(Listening — waiting
   her turn.)*
3. Act it out: two learners replay Monday and Tuesday; the group names what
   changed. *(Monday: right broken. Tuesday: right used, responsibility kept.)*
4. Rights sort: the adult shows four picture cards (raised hand, child alone
   outside a game, warm blanket, child being read to). "Which three show a
   right? Which right is each?" The fourth (left-out child) starts the
   conversation: "Whose right is missing here? What could the community do?"

## Practice B — Session 6: Right-or-responsibility sort game

Ten picture cards (adult draws or prints simple scenes): *raised hand; child
listening; shared crayons; child alone at lunch; toy bin being tidied; warm
coat on a hook; child helping another up; broken toy left on the floor; two
children voting with hands up; spilled juice being wiped.*

1. The learner sorts each card onto the **RIGHT** mat or the **RESPONSIBILITY**
   mat, saying one sentence: "This is a ___ because ___."
2. Challenge cards (broken toy, spilled juice, child alone): "What
   responsibility would fix this? Whose right would it protect?" The learner
   draws or describes the fix.
3. The adult keeps the three challenge fixes — they become ideas for the
   charter project (Session 12).

## Practice C — Session 8: Rules in three places

Three labeled baskets or floor zones: **HOME**, **CLASSROOM**, **PARK**.
Six rule cards: *walk inside; raise your hand; take turns on the slide; wash
hands before dinner; shoes off at the door; clean up toys.*

1. The learner places each card in a basket. (Two cards — *clean up toys* —
   fit more than one basket; celebrate that: "Some rules travel!")
2. For each placed card: "Which job — keep safe, or help everyone learn and
   play?" (Two-finger chant from Lesson 4.)
3. "Invent one rule for our classroom that does one of the two jobs. Tell me
   the rule and the job." The adult writes it on an index card — it goes to
   the charter project.

## Read-aloud B — Session 10: *The Block Tower Vote* (original story)

*An original story for this unit — not a reproduction of any published work.*

> On Friday the class had fifteen blocks and one big question: what should
> they build? "A castle!" said Theo. "A bridge!" said Amara. "A zoo!" said
> Luis. Their teacher said, "We have one tower of blocks and three good
> ideas. Let's discuss, then vote."
> They used their discussion rules. They listened. One speaker at a time.
> Amara said, "I agree with Theo about a castle because we can all add a
> tower." Luis said, "I think differently — a zoo needs cages, and cages are
> hard." Then they voted by raising hands. Castle won, eight to five. Luis
> sighed — then smiled. "I voted zoo, but I will build the tallest tower."
> The castle had thirteen towers, one from every builder. It was the best
> castle on the block.

**Response activities** (choose 3–4):

1. "Which discussion rules did the class use? Show me each one."
2. Vote game (real): the adult poses a real tiny classroom choice (which song,
   which book, which job order). The group discusses with the three rules,
   votes by raised hands, and the adult counts aloud. **The result stands** —
   the class follows through, so voting means something.
3. "Luis did not get his zoo. Was that fair? Why?" *(Expected: yes — same
   rules for everyone, everyone got what they needed: a tower to build. This
   is the Lesson 5 ankle-chair idea in story form.)*
4. "What is one decision our class could vote on next week?" (Seeds Unit 04's
   decision-making work.)

## Observation notes for the adult

After each practice session, note one line per objective in the learner's
private log (kept outside this repository): what the learner did, in their
words where possible. These notes feed the formative quiz (Session 14) and the
unit assessment (Session 16).
