# Unit 01 — Community Membership, Rights, and Responsibilities

Grade 1 · Social Studies · Weeks 3–6 · issue #13, section U01 (draft for review).

## Essential question

*What do the communities I belong to give me — and what do they ask of me?*

## What this unit teaches

A first grader already belongs to several communities: a family or household, a
classroom, and a neighborhood. This unit makes that membership visible. The
learner names their communities, meets two big civics words — **right** (what
membership gives you) and **responsibility** (what membership asks of you) —
learns why rules exist in different places, and practices the discussion rules
that let a group think together: listening, one speaker at a time, and
responding to others. The unit closes with a class-community charter the
learner helps write, draw, and sign.

## Prerequisites

From the Week 1–2 diagnostic ([scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md),
Section 5): the learner can follow a turn-taking discussion, use everyday
location words, talk about people at home and school, draw and label with adult
help, and dictate a sentence for the adult to scribe. Units 01–02 re-teach
discussion rules rather than assuming them secure. No reading level is assumed;
all written responses are 2–3 word labels or adult-scribed dictation.

## Measurable objectives (adult-assessed by end of Week 6)

1. Name at least two communities they belong to (for example classroom, home,
   neighborhood) and name at least one right and one responsibility in each.
2. Explain, in their own words, why rules exist — "rules keep us safe and help
   everyone learn" counts — and give one example rule for home, one for the
   classroom, and one for a public place.
3. Sort five picture actions as **rights** or **responsibilities** with at least
   four correct, and explain one choice aloud ("that one is a responsibility
   because someone has to do the work").
4. Follow the agreed discussion rules (listen, one speaker at a time, respond
   to what someone said) during a group talk, and give an example of fairness
   and an example of respecting a person in authority.
5. Take on a real classroom responsibility, carry it out, and say how the action
   helped the classroom community.

## Vocabulary

Introduced with objects, pictures, and walks first; the word follows the idea.

| Word | Grade-1 meaning used in this unit |
|---|---|
| **community** | A group of people who spend time together and look after each other (our class, our family, our neighborhood) |
| **member** | Someone who belongs to a community |
| **right** | Something every member gets to have — being heard, being safe, being included |
| **responsibility** | Something every member helps with — listening, helping, cleaning up, following rules |
| **rule** | An agreement everyone follows so the community works |
| **fair** | Everyone gets what they need and the same rules apply to everyone |
| **respect** | Treating people and their words as important |
| **authority** | A person the community trusts to keep things safe and fair (teacher, principal, firefighter) |
| **agree** | Saying "yes, I will" to a plan together |
| **discuss** | Talking and listening about an idea with others |

review game, per the track's session model. The adult may combine or split
sessions; what matters is the order, not the calendar.

## Differentiation

- **Supports:** oral, pointing, and drawing response modes for every check; the
  adult scribes dictated sentences; vocabulary taught with objects and pictures
  before words; home-language labels welcome alongside English; textured or
  raised materials for low-vision learners; visual sentence frames for votes and
  retellings; every lesson includes a table/floor-play movement variant.
- **Extensions:** "community reporter" interviews a household adult about a
  responsibility they had as a child; write the unit's essential question in a
  notebook and revisit it at the final review; design one more charter rule and
  defend it to the adult.
- K–2 rule: all investigations are adult-supervised and classroom-scale; no
  neighborhood walks with strangers, no contact with outside authorities.

## Readiness to move on (Week 6)

The learner is ready for Unit 02 when objectives 1–4 are at least *developing*
and objective 3's sort is secure. If rights and responsibilities still blur,
re-teach Sessions 6–7 before Unit 02; Unit 02's retrieval warm-up revisits
community vocabulary on a map, so the ideas keep living there.
