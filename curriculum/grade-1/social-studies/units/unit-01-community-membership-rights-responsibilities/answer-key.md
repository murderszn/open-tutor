# Unit 01 — Answer Key

**For the guiding adult only. Never place beside a learner question.**
Every item below was worked independently against the lesson text; open
responses list the acceptable range, not a single "correct" sentence. When the
learner's answer is defensible but different, accept it and note the reasoning.

## §L1 — Lesson 1: My communities

- **Guided 1** ("one community you belong to"): accept class, family/household,
  neighborhood, team, congregation (as community, not doctrine). Redirect
  places without people ("the school building" → "who is in it with you?").
- **Guided 2** ("two people in it"): any two real members; accept roles
  ("my teacher", "my sister").
- **Guided 3** ("a different community"): must differ from Guided 1; if the
  learner repeats, prompt "where else do you spend time with people?"
- **Guided 4** (arm circles): big = neighborhood, small = class. Accept any
  sincere attempt — this is embodied vocabulary, not a test.
- **Independent** (drawing): secure = self + two labeled communities with a
  person in each; developing = one community or people missing (prompt once);
  emerging = cannot start — switch to the pointing-cards alternative.
- **Exit check**: two communities named (or pointed to) = secure; one = developing.

## §L2 — Lesson 2: What is a right?

- **Guided 1** (raised-hand card): a right — the right to be **heard**.
- **Guided 2** (blanket card): a right — the right to be **safe** (accept
  "to be warm/protected" — same idea in child words).
- **Guided 3** ("a right you have at home"): accept being fed, being safe,
  being listened to, being included. **Redirect** treats and rewards
  ("ice cream", "a new toy"): ask "does EVERY member get that?" If yes in
  their household, accept it — do not argue with their family's reality.
- **Guided 4** (thumbs: "a right is something you have to win"): **thumbs
  down.** Reason: you have it because you belong.
- **Independent** (draw one right): any of the three rights depicted or
  described; dictated label must name the right ("my right to be heard").
- **Exit check**: completes "a right is something every member gets to
  **have**" (accept "get", "keep") and names one own right.

## §L3 — Lesson 3: What is a responsibility?

- **Guided 1** (listening card): **responsibility** — protects others' right
  to be **heard**. Accept the frame with blanks filled: "This is a
  responsibility. It protects ___'s right to be heard."
- **Guided 2** (toy-bin card): **responsibility** (taking care of our things)
  — protects the next child's right to be **included** / to play with a
  complete set.
- **Guided 3** (sharing-crayons card): **responsibility** (helping/sharing)
  — protects others' right to be **included**.
- **Guided 4** ("hardest responsibility"): any honest answer; there is no
  wrong pick. Note it for charter and reteach planning.
- **Guided 5** ("is brushing teeth a responsibility?"): **yes** — it keeps
  you healthy; taking care of yourself is part of what members owe their
  community. Accept "it keeps me healthy" as full credit.
- **Independent**: the done job + "I did ___. It helped ___ because ___."
  Secure = job done and a who-it-helped named; developing = job done, help
  unclear (prompt: "who feels better because you did it?").
- **Exit check**: "a responsibility is a **job** (accept 'work', 'thing we
  do') that keeps our community working" + names today's job.

## §L4 — Lesson 4: Why we have rules

- **Guided 1** ("raise your hand" — which job?): **learn-and-play** (protects
  everyone's right to be heard). Accept "safety" only with a coherent reason
  (there is none obvious — coach back to the two jobs).
- **Guided 2** ("hold the railing"): **safety**.
- **Guided 3** (home rule + job): accept any real household rule with a
  plausible job mapping (hand-washing → safety; bedtime → safety/rest;
  "ask before taking" → learn-and-play/fairness).
- **Guided 4** (rule vs. responsibility): "We clean up toys" = the **rule**
  (the promise/agreement); the picture of tidying = the **responsibility**
  (keeping the promise). Accept "the words / the doing."
- **Guided 5** ("no rules for a day"): accept silly answers; secure = names a
  safety consequence AND a fairness consequence ("someone gets hurt", "nobody
  gets a turn").
- **Independent**: three drawings (home / class / park) with dictated rules.
  Secure = one rule per place, each doing a recognizable job. **Alternate
  mode** (basket sort): *walk inside* → classroom; *raise your hand* →
  classroom; *take turns on the slide* → park; *wash hands before dinner* →
  home; *shoes off at the door* → home; *clean up toys* → home AND classroom
  (celebrate the double fit).
- **Exit check**: "rules keep us **safe** and help everyone **learn and
  play**" (any own-words version) + one rule per place.

## §L5 — Lesson 5: Fairness and discussion rules

- **Guided 1** (fair-or-not): "everyone gets one cookie" = **fair**; "only the
  tallest get cookies" = **not fair** (rule not applied to everyone); "hurt
  ankle sits closest" = **fair** (everyone gets what they need). The third one
  is the point — let the argument run.
- **Guided 2** (firefighter): person in **authority**; respect = listen,
  follow safety rules, say thank you. Accept any two.
- **Guided 3** (authority at school): teacher / principal / crossing guard /
  nurse — with their job ("keeps us safe", "helps us learn", "makes fair
  rules"). Any real school adult with a real job counts.
- **Guided 4** (practice discussion): observe the three rules; coach live.
  Secure = all three shown with at most one reminder.
- **Guided 5** ("what happens when someone shouts over others?"): **not
  fair — not everyone is heard.** This answer connects rules to rights;
  note it as evidence for Objective 4.
- **Independent**: fairness picture with dictated "fair means ___." Secure =
  picture shows a fair share/turn AND the label captures same-rules or
  needs-met. **Alternate mode** (crackers on plates): level plates = fair;
  explanation required.
- **Exit check**: (a) three discussion rules in any order — listen, one
  speaker, respond to what was said; (b) one fairness example; (c) one person
  in authority + their job. Score each part secure/developing/emerging.

## §P — Practice sessions and read-alouds

**Practice A.**
1. Three members pointed to: any three people in the illustration.
2. Responsibility spotted: children tidying toys together, or the child
   helping with the shoe. Accept either with "they are helping / taking
   care."
3. Right spotted: raised hand = **heard** (expected); children listening =
   protecting heard; children playing together = **included**.
4. Drawing: same criteria as §L1 independent.

**Read-aloud A** (*Maya's Raised Hand*).
1. Tuesday's right: **heard** ("we are listening").
2. Tuesday's responsibility: **listening** / waiting her turn.
3. Act-out: Monday = Jalen's right to be heard broken; Tuesday = right used
   AND responsibility kept. Secure = the learner can say what changed and why
   it mattered ("Jalen got to finish").
4. Rights sort: raised hand → heard; warm blanket → safe; being read to →
   heard/included (accept either with reason); the left-out child = right to
   be **included** missing. "What could the community do?" — accept any
   inclusive action ("make room", "invite them", "ask what they want to play").

**Practice B** (10-card sort).
- RIGHT mat: raised hand (heard); warm coat on a hook (safe); two children
  voting with hands up (heard — accept "fair" as child words for the same idea).
- RESPONSIBILITY mat: child listening; toys being put away; child sharing
  crayons; child helping another up; spilled juice being wiped.
- Challenge cards (need a fix, then sort the fix): child alone at lunch →
  invite/include (protects **included**); broken toy left on floor → put away
  / tell the teacher (protects **safe**); spilled juice → wipe it (already on
  the responsibility mat — note the double as a teaching moment, not an error).
- The "why" sentence matters more than mat placement: a thoughtful wrong-mat
  answer with a good reason outranks a lucky right-mat guess. Re-sort together
  and move on.

**Practice C** (three baskets). See §L4 independent alternate mode for
placements. Invented rule: must do one of the two jobs — coach vague rules
("be good") into observable ones ("we use quiet voices when someone reads").

**Read-aloud B** (*The Block Tower Vote*).
1. Discussion rules used: listened, one speaker at a time, responded to ideas
   (Amara agreed with Theo *because…*; Luis disagreed *with a reason*).
2. Vote game: the count is what it is — **honor the result**. If the learner
   protests, re-teach on the spot: "fair means the same rules for everyone,
   even when we wish it went the other way."
3. "Was it fair that Luis lost?" **Yes** — same rules for everyone; everyone
   got what they needed (a tower to build). Accept "yes, because we all
   voted" as full credit.
4. Next-week vote idea: any real, safe, classroom-scale decision. Bank it for
   Unit 04.

## §Q — Formative quiz (Session 14)

1. Two communities + one person each: same criteria as §L1 exit.
2. Raised-hand card → right to be **heard**; one own right named.
3. Toy-bin card → **responsibility**; protects the next child's right to be
   **included** (accept "so others can play").
4. Why rules: the two-jobs idea in own words; one home rule + one classroom
   rule. Secure = both rules real and at least one job named.
5. Crackers: **not fair**; fix = split evenly (accept "give the empty plate
   some"). If the learner says "the hungry one gets more" — that is the
   needs-based fairness from Lesson 5; accept and celebrate.
6. Three discussion rules: listen / one speaker / respond. Gestures accepted.

## §UA — Unit assessment (Session 16)

- **Task 1**: secure = two communities drawn with a person in each + one
  right and one responsibility per community (frames allowed); developing =
  communities named but right/responsibility missing in one.
- **Task 2**: secure = 4–5 of 5 sorted correctly with a reason for the
  explained card (see §P Practice B for placements); developing = 3 correct.
- **Task 3**: secure = the two-jobs idea in own words for ≥2 of 3 rules;
  developing = one rule explained or "because the teacher said so" with
  prompting toward consequences.
- **Task 4**: secure = all three discussion rules with ≤1 reminder + a
  fairness explanation (same-rules or needs-met); developing = 2 of 3 rules.
- **Task 5**: secure = job done + names who it helped; developing = done with
  reminders.
- **Reflection**: not scored; note the proudest-part and the wonder — the
  wonder opens Unit 02.

## Reconciliation note

Every question, prompt, and task in the lessons, practice sessions, quiz, and
assessment above has a worked acceptable response here. Open responses accept
the learner's own words when the reasoning matches; when in doubt, ask the
follow-up question in parentheses rather than marking wrong.
