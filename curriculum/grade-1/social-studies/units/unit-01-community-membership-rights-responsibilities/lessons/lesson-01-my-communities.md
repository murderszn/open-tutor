# Lesson 1 — My Communities

**Session 1 · about 18–20 minutes.**

**Learner goal:** I can name two communities I belong to and say who is in them.

**Materials:** crayons, paper; the [classroom illustration](../assets/classroom-community-scene.png) (printed or on screen) for the adult to preview before Session 2.

**Readiness check (1 minute):** "Who did you spend time with yesterday? Tell me
one person." (Any answer counts — the adult is just warming up naming people.)

## Explicit teaching

*(Adult reads or paraphrases; pause at the bold words.)*

A **community** is a group of people who spend time together and look after
each other. You are in more than one community. A person who belongs to a
community is called a **member**.

Think about your day. In the morning you might be with the people at home —
that is your **home community**. Then you come to class — the people who learn
together are your **classroom community**. Outside your door are neighbors and
helpers — your **neighborhood community**.

Communities are different sizes, but they all work the same way: members help
each other, and every member matters.

## Worked examples (adult models the reasoning aloud)

**Example 1.** *The adult points to the learner, then to themselves, then to
the room.* "Am I in your classroom community? Yes — we spend time together
and we look after each other. Is the mailbox on the street in our classroom
community? No — it is not a person who spends time with us. Members are
people, not things."

**Example 2.** "Is your grandmother, who lives in another town but calls you
every Sunday, in a community with you? Yes — she spends time with you and
looks after you, even from far away. Communities are not only about places;
they are about people who care about each other."

## Guided practice (adult prompts, learner responds; 4 tasks)

1. "Tell me one community you belong to." *(Hint if stuck: "Where do you eat
   breakfast?")*
2. "Who else is a member of that community? Name two people."
3. "Name a different community. Who is in that one?"
4. "Show me with your arms: make a big circle for a big community
   (neighborhood) and a small circle for a smaller one (your class)."

## Independent practice

The learner draws "Me in my communities": themselves in the middle, and at
least two community circles around them with a person drawn in each. The adult
labels with the learner's words (2–3 word labels, e.g. "my class", "my
family"). **Text alternative:** the learner can point to printed community
cards (class / home / neighborhood) and place a photo of themselves next to
each one they belong to — no drawing required.

## Application

"Tonight, tell someone at home: 'I learned I am a member of my classroom
community. I belong here.'" The adult notes whether the learner says it
unprompted the next day.

## Exit check

The learner names two of their communities aloud (or points to two labeled
circles). The adult records: *secure* (two communities, names members),
*developing* (one community or needs the hint), *emerging* (cannot name yet —
reteach with the learner's own day as the only example).

## Supports

- **Language access:** pre-teach *community* and *member* with the learner's
  home language alongside English; accept answers in any language.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing to labeled circles or picture cards
  instead of naming aloud.
- **Movement:** stand and physically step into hula-hoop "communities" labeled
  class / home / neighborhood.

## Extension

"Community reporter": the learner asks a household adult, "What communities
did you belong to when you were six?" and reports one answer back the next
day.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** learners think a community is a *place* ("the
school building") rather than *people*. Keep correcting gently with Example 1's
test: "Is it a person who spends time with us?" **Answer-key reference:**
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) §L1 for acceptable naming variations
(family, household, class, team, congregation as "faith community" stays
separate from core civics per repo boundaries).
