# Lesson 3 — What Is a Responsibility?

**Session 5 · about 18–20 minutes.**

**Learner goal:** I can say what a responsibility is, name one of mine, and do it today.

**Materials:** picture cards: child listening, child putting toys in a bin,
child sharing crayons, child washing hands; a real classroom job the learner
can do (water a plant, stack books, feed a class pet, tidy the rug).

**Readiness check (1 minute):** "Name one right you have. Now — who makes sure
that right happens?" (Bridge: rights are protected when members do their part.)

## Explicit teaching

A **right** is what the community gives you. A **responsibility** is what you
give back. A responsibility is a job that keeps the community working — and
every member has them, including grown-ups.

Our classroom responsibilities:

1. **Listen** when someone is speaking — this protects everyone's right to be heard.
2. **Help** — carry, share, comfort, show.
3. **Take care of our things and our place** — put toys back, clean spills, be
   gentle with books. This protects everyone's right to be safe.
4. **Follow our agreements** — the rules we made together.

See the pattern? Every responsibility on this list *protects somebody's
right*. That is the big idea of this unit: **rights and responsibilities fit
together like two hands.**

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** "Leo finishes a puzzle and puts the pieces back in the box.
That is the responsibility of **taking care of our things**. Whose right does
it protect? The next child's right to a *complete* puzzle — to be included in
the fun. Work it backwards like a detective: find the job, then find the right
it protects."

**Example 2.** "During clean-up, Ana sees Ben struggling with a heavy bin and
holds the door. That is the responsibility of **helping**. Whose right does it
protect? Ben's right to be **safe** — the bin will not fall on him. One small
job, one protected right."

## Guided practice (5 tasks)

1. Hold up the listening card. "Right or responsibility? Whose right does it
   protect?" *(Sentence frame: "This is a responsibility. It protects ___'s
   right to ___." — adult supplies the frame; learner fills the blanks.)*
2. Hold up the toy-bin card. Same two questions.
3. Hold up the sharing-crayons card. Same two questions.
4. "Which responsibility is the hardest for you? Why?" *(Honest answers build
   the charter in Lesson 6's project — note them.)*
5. "Is brushing your teeth a responsibility? Who does it help?" *(Tests
   transfer: yes — it keeps you healthy and is part of taking care of yourself,
   which every member owes their community too.)*

## Independent practice

The learner chooses a real classroom responsibility card and does the job
*now* (2–3 minutes), then tells the adult: "I did ___. It helped ___ because
___." The adult scribes the sentence on the job card and posts it on the
classroom "responsibilities wall." **Alternate mode:** the learner points to
the job card they chose and pantomimes the job for the adult to name.

## Application

The job card stays posted for the week. The learner repeats their
responsibility daily and the adult checks in: "Who did your work protect
today?"

## Exit check

"Finish my sentence: a responsibility is a ___ that keeps our community
working." Plus: name one responsibility they personally did today. Record
secure / developing / emerging.

## Supports

- **Language access:** *responsibility* is a long word — teach the short form
  "my job for us" alongside it; keep the full word on the wall card.
- **Alternate response mode:** sentence frames with blanks; pointing to wall
  cards.
- **Movement:** "responsibility relay" — act out a card, the partner guesses
  which job it is.

## Extension

"Responsibility map": draw the path of one job (toy → bin → shelf) and label
whose right each step protects.

## Teacher note

**Common misconceptions:** (a) "Responsibilities are punishments" — they are
not; nobody is in trouble, the work is how we belong. (b) "Only kids have
jobs" — point to adult responsibilities all week (the teacher listens,
prepares, keeps everyone safe). **Watch for:** learners who always pick the
easiest job — celebrate the pick, then nudge: "Which job would stretch you?"
