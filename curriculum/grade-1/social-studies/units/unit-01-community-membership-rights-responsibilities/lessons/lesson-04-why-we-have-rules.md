# Lesson 4 — Why We Have Rules

**Session 7 · about 18–20 minutes.**

**Learner goal:** I can explain why rules exist and give one rule for home, class, and a public place.

**Materials:** three labeled picture scenes (or simple drawings): a home
kitchen, the classroom, a park/playground; rule picture cards: "walk inside,"
"raise your hand," "hold the railing," "take turns on the slide," "clean up
toys," "say please when you ask."

**Readiness check (1 minute):** "Name one responsibility you did this week. Who
did it help?" (Retrieval from Lesson 3.)

## Explicit teaching

A **rule** is an agreement everyone follows so the community works. Rules are
not there to spoil fun — they are there for two jobs:

1. **Keep everyone safe.** ("Walk inside" keeps bodies safe; "hold the
   railing" keeps bodies safe on stairs.)
2. **Help everyone learn and play.** ("Raise your hand" protects everyone's
   right to be heard; "take turns on the slide" protects everyone's right to
   be included.)

Rules live in every community, but the *same place* can have different rules,
and different places have different rules. At home you might take shoes off at
the door; at school you keep shoes on for fire drills. The rules change
because the **needs of the community** change — but the two jobs (safe +
everyone can learn and play) stay the same everywhere.

**Rule or responsibility?** A rule is the *agreement* ("we walk inside").
A responsibility is *doing* it (walking inside, every day). The rule is the
promise; the responsibility is keeping the promise.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** "Why do we walk inside the classroom instead of running?
Think of the two jobs. First — safety: someone could fall and get hurt, and
that breaks their right to be safe. Second — learning: a running, shouting room
is a room where nobody can hear the story, and that breaks everyone's right
to be heard. One rule, two jobs, three rights protected. That is why the rule
exists — it is not because grown-ups like saying no."

**Example 2.** "At the park there is a rule: take turns on the slide. At home
there is no slide, so there is no turn-taking rule about slides — but there
*might* be a rule about taking turns with the tablet. Different places, same
job: the rule protects everyone's right to be included. If there were no
turns, the biggest or fastest child would get everything, and that would not
be fair." (Fairness previews Lesson 5.)

## Guided practice (5 tasks)

1. Show "raise your hand." "Why does this rule exist — safety, or
   learning-and-play?" *(Accept either with a reason: "learning, because
   everyone gets heard.")*
2. Show "hold the railing." Same question. *(Safety — bodies.)*
3. "Tell me a rule from your home. Which of the two jobs does it do?"
   *(Prompt: "What happens at home before dinner?" — hand-washing emerges.)*
4. Rule-or-responsibility sort, two cards: "We clean up toys" (rule = the
   agreement) vs. a picture of a child putting toys away (responsibility =
   doing it). "Which is the promise, and which is keeping the promise?"
5. "What would our classroom be like with NO rules for one day?" *(Accept
   silly answers, then anchor: "Who would feel unsafe? Who would not get a
   turn?" — the adult writes the two or three best answers on the board.)*

## Independent practice

The learner draws one rule for each of three places: home, classroom, park
(simple scenes — a shoe by the door, a raised hand, a slide with a line).
Adult labels each with the learner's dictated rule. **Alternate mode:** sort
the six rule picture cards into three labeled place-baskets instead of
drawing.

## Application

"Rule detective": for the rest of the day, the learner spots one rule being
followed in real life and reports it: "I saw ___ following the rule ___."

## Exit check

"Why do rules exist?" Accept any version of: *to keep us safe and help
everyone learn/play*. Plus one home rule, one classroom rule, one public-place
rule. Record secure / developing / emerging.

## Supports

- **Language access:** the "two jobs" chant — adult holds up one finger
  ("safe!") then two ("learn and play!") — becomes the class shorthand for the
  rest of the year.
- **Alternate response mode:** sort cards into baskets; point to the finger
  (1 = safe, 2 = learn/play) to answer "which job."
- **Movement:** "rule statues" — freeze showing a rule being followed; partner
  names the rule and its job.

## Extension

"What if" debate (oral, adult-facilitated): "Should the park rule be 'first
come, first served forever'? Why not?" — first taste of evaluating rules,
revisited in Unit 04.

## Teacher note

**Common misconceptions:** (a) "Rules are punishments for bad kids" — rules
protect *everyone*, including the rule-follower; (b) "Rules are the same
everywhere" — use the shoes-on/shoes-off example; (c) confusing the agreement
with the action — keep rehearsing "the promise / keeping the promise."
