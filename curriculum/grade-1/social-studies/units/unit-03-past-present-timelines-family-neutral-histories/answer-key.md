# Unit 03 — Answer Key

**For the guiding adult only. Never place beside a learner question.**
Every item below was worked independently against the lesson text; open
responses list the acceptable range, not a single "correct" sentence. When
the learner's answer is defensible but different, accept it and note the
reasoning.

## §L1 — Lesson 1: Past, present, future

- **Guided 1** ("brushed teeth this morning"): **past** — already happened.
- **Guided 2** ("sitting together now"): **present** — happening now.
- **Guided 3** ("eat lunch later"): **future** — has not happened yet.
- **Guided 4** ("washed clothes in a basin, long ago"): **past**, and
  **long ago** (before the learner was born). Accept "past" alone as
  correct; "long ago" is a bonus.
- **Independent** (5 spoken events): "we woke up" → past; "we are learning
  now" → present; "we will go home later" → future; "yesterday it rained"
  → past; "tomorrow is a new day" → future.
- **Exit check**: 3 new events sorted correctly = secure; 2 = developing.

## §L2 — Lesson 2: Ordering events on a timeline

- **Guided 1** (3 morning cards): waking up → breakfast → class.
- **Guided 2** ("after breakfast"): class.
- **Guided 3** ("before" sentence): accept "Breakfast comes before class" or
  "Waking up comes before breakfast" (any true before-sentence about the
  strip).
- **Guided 4** (deliberate error, class before breakfast): **wrong.**
  Breakfast happened earlier; the fix is breakfast → class, because class
  comes *after* breakfast.
- **Independent** (4-event strip + "playing outside later"): waking up →
  getting dressed → eating breakfast → coming to class → playing outside
  later. Accept any order the learner defends with before/after reasoning
  (for example, if their day dresses after breakfast, accept
  waking → breakfast → dressed → class, with the defense scribed).
- **Exit check**: correct order + one before/after explanation = secure.

## §L3 — Lesson 3: Life long ago and today

- **Guided 1** (lamps): changed — how light is made (oil flame → electric
  switch). Stayed the same — people need light when it is dark.
- **Guided 2** (washing): changed — the tool and the work (hands in a basin
  → machine). Stayed the same — clothes get dirty and someone washes them.
- **Guided 3** (travel): changed — the vehicle and the road (horse cart on
  dirt → car on pavement). Stayed the same — people travel to get somewhere
  and carry things.
- **Guided 4** (error: "nobody needed light at night long ago"): **wrong.**
  The need stayed the same; only the lamp changed. Fixed sentence: "Long
  ago, people needed light at night too, but they used oil lamps."
- **Independent** (6-card sort, washing area): changed pile — basin, machine
  (tools); stayed pile — soapy water, dirty clothes, clean clothes (the job
  and its materials); clothesline — accept either pile with reasoning
  ("changed" if compared with a dryer, "stayed" if compared with hanging
  clothes today — both are defensible; ask "why?" and accept the reason).
- **Exit check**: one change + one stay-the-same for one area = secure.

## §L4 — Lesson 4: Kinds of historical sources

- **Guided 1** (second printed photo): **photograph** — picture taken with a
  camera of a real moment.
- **Guided 2** (candle): **object** — a real thing you can hold.
- **Guided 3** (told sentence about the lamp-lighter): **story people
  tell** — words, no picture shown, no thing handed over.
- **Guided 4** (error: "this Willow Street drawing proves people used oil
  lamps"): **wrong.** A made-up drawing cannot prove a real past fact. Fixed
  sentence: "This drawing helps us practice, but it does not prove anything
  about the real past."
- **Independent** (6 source cards): photographs ×2, objects ×2, stories ×2
  as labeled in the lesson; the Willow Street illustration is **none** of
  the three — it is an illustration for practice.
- **Exit check**: three kinds named + 2 new examples sorted = secure.

## §L5 — Lesson 5: Using a source to answer a question

- **Guided 1** (session source + question card): accept any sentence of the
  form "The [photograph/object/story] shows [visible evidence], so
  [answer]" where the evidence is actually visible/audible in the source.
  Example (photo of an old street): "The photograph shows a horse pulling a
  cart, so people traveled by horse and cart."
- **Guided 2** ("one question this source cannot answer"): any question the
  source does not show — names, thoughts, exact dates, what happened next.
  "I cannot tell from this source" is the correct form.
- **Guided 3** (Nia and the washing machine): accept surprised, curious, or
  unsure — **with a reason tied to Nia's normal** ("surprised, because she
  washes by hand every morning"). Reject feelings with no reason; prompt
  "why would she feel that way?"
- **Guided 4** (error: "the picture shows a horse, so the horse's name was
  Brownie"): **guessing.** The picture shows no name. Fixed sentence: "The
  picture shows a horse, so I know people used horses — but I cannot tell
  the horse's name from this picture."
- **Independent**: evidence sentence (as Guided 1) + one perspective answer
  (a long-ago child on a modern thing, or vice versa) with a reason.
- **Exit check**: evidence-based answer + one perspective comparison with a
  reason = secure.

## §Practice A — My-day strip (Session 2)

Order: waking up → eating breakfast → coming to class → what we will do
after this lesson. Accept the learner's real morning order if it differs,
provided the "after this lesson" card is last and the learner can say why
with "after."

## §Timeline game — Willow Street morning (Session 4)

Defensible order: lamp-lighter puts out lamps at dawn → baker opens the
shop → cart driver loads crates → children walk to school. Accept swaps of
the middle two (baker/cart) if defended ("the baker opens before the cart
leaves because the cart carries bread" or the reverse with a reason — the
anchor facts are dawn first and school last). Before-sentence example:
"The baker opens before the children walk to school." After-sentence
example: "The children walk to school after the cart driver loads the
crates." Swap round: the learner must spot the swapped pair and restore
the defended order.

## §Practice B — Willow Street illustration (Session 6)

- **Differences (any 3):** oil lamp vs. electric streetlight; dirt road +
  horse cart vs. paved road + car; washing by hand in a basin vs. washing
  machine; clothesline vs. laundry basket (accept); open doorway showing
  machine vs. closed doorway (accept if described).
- **Stayed the same (any 1):** the small house; the big tree; the hills;
  smoke from the chimney; washing as a job people do; the sky/clouds.
- **Which side is long ago:** the left side — evidence: horse cart, oil
  lamp, dirt road (any one counts).
- **Sentences:** one change sentence + one stay-the-same sentence, any
  accurate pair from above.

## §Read-aloud A — *The Lantern on Willow Street* (Session 7)

1. **Retell order:** evening comes → Sam lights the lamps → Nia asks why →
   Sam explains (light helps people feel safe finding their way) → today
   lights switch on by themselves. Order matters; wording does not.
2. **What changed:** how lights are lit (oil flame lit by a person walking
   the street → electric lights that switch on by themselves).
3. **What stayed the same:** people want light when it is dark; people want
   to feel safe finding their way home.
4. **Question for Sam:** any genuine question about the past counts
   ("How heavy was the oil can?" "Did it rain on the lamps?"). Scribe one.

## §Changed / stayed sort + review game (Session 8)

- **Sort:** changed — oil lamp, electric light, wash basin, washing machine
  (as tools; accept either lamp card in "changed" with tool reasoning).
  Stayed — needing light at night, clothes getting dirty.
- **Game ("Change or same?", 8 items):** changed — horse cart, car, oil
  lamp, electric light (the tools and vehicles of daily life changed);
  stayed — house, tree, dirty clothes, clean clothes (places, nature, and
  the job of washing itself). 6 of 8 = secure.

## §Source sort (Session 10)

1. Photograph. 2. Object. 3. Story people tell. 4. Photograph. 5. Object.
6. Story people tell.
- **Illustration question:** **none** — the Willow Street picture is a
  drawing made for practice, not a photograph, object, or told story.
- **One thing each kind can/cannot teach:** photograph — can show what
  things looked like; cannot show thoughts. Object — can show materials and
  use; cannot show who held it (unless told). Story — can show feelings and
  reasons; can change a little each telling, so check it when possible.

## §Read-aloud B — *Two Pictures of One Street* (Session 12)

1. **Travel long ago:** by horse and cart. Evidence: the first picture
   shows a horse pulling a cart on a dirt road.
2. **Stayed the same (any 2):** the small house, the big tree, the corner,
   the street itself.
3. **Cannot answer (any 1):** the horse's name, the driver's name, what
   people were thinking, what happened after the picture. "I cannot tell
   from these pictures" is correct.
