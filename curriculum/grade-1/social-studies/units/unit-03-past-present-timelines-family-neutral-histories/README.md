# Unit 03 — Past, Present, Timelines, and Family-Neutral Histories

Grade 1 · Social Studies · Weeks 11–14 · issue #13, section U03 (draft for review).

## Essential question

*How do we know what happened before, and how is life long ago different from life today?*

## What this unit teaches

A first grader already lives in time: yesterday happened, today is happening,
tomorrow has not happened yet. This unit turns that everyday knowing into
history. The learner meets the words **past**, **present**, and **future**,
orders events on a strip **timeline** using **before** and **after**, compares
life long ago with life today (lighting, washing, travel, and communication),
meets three kinds of historical **sources** — photographs, objects, and stories
people tell — and uses one source to answer a question about the past. The unit
closes with a "Willow Street Through Time" project: a three-panel timeline of
a fictional street, built from picture sources. The week-14 review compares a
child's day long ago with a child's day today, so the learner practices seeing
the past through another person's eyes.

**Family-neutral rule (from the track scope-and-sequence):** nothing in this
unit assumes a family structure. Read-alouds are set in the fictional town of
Willow Street and name caregivers, neighbors, elders, and storytellers without
assuming who lives with whom. If the learner wants to make a timeline of their
own life or family, the adult helps them do it in **private storage only** —
that timeline is never written into, photographed for, or posted to this public
repository. The project in this unit deliberately uses a fictional street so
every learner's work can be shared in class without sharing private history.

## Prerequisites

From the Week 1–2 diagnostic and Units 01–02
([scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md), Section 5): the learner
follows turn-taking discussions, uses everyday location words, draws and labels
with adult help, dictates a sentence for the adult to scribe, and counts to
about 10 (used to label timeline positions). Unit 02's map skills return here
only as retrieval: the Willow Street picture is read like a map of one street
("what is near the house?"), and the timeline strip is laid out left-to-right
the way a route map is read. No reading level is assumed; all written recording
stays to 2–3 word labels, dictated sentences, drawings, and timeline cards.
An AI-generated picture in this unit is an **illustration of a fictional
street, never a historical source** — Lesson 4 teaches that difference
explicitly.

## Measurable objectives (adult-assessed by end of Week 14)

1. Use **past**, **present**, and **future** correctly: sort at least 4 of 5
   spoken events (for example "we ate breakfast," "we are reading now," "we
   will play outside later") into past / present / future.
2. Order 3–5 events on a strip timeline from earliest to latest and explain
   one placement aloud with **before** or **after** ("lunch comes after
   morning circle").
3. Compare life long ago with life today in at least two areas (lighting,
   washing, travel, communication): name one thing that changed and one thing
   that stayed the same in each area.
4. Name the three kinds of historical sources taught here — **photograph**,
   **object**, **story people tell** — and sort at least 5 of 6 example cards
   into the right kind.
5. Use one source to answer a question about the past, pointing to or naming
   the evidence in the source ("the picture shows a horse pulling the cart,
   so people traveled by horse").
6. Compare a child's day long ago with a child's day today and say one way the
   two children might see the same thing differently (for example, a wash basin
   feels normal to one child and strange to the other).

## Vocabulary

Introduced with objects, pictures, and walks first; the word follows the idea.

| Word | Grade-1 meaning used in this unit |
|---|---|
| **past** | Time that already happened (yesterday, long ago) |
| **present** | Time happening now (today, right now) |
| **future** | Time that has not happened yet (tomorrow, later) |
| **long ago** | A long time in the past, before the learner was born |
| **timeline** | A line of events in order, earliest first |
| **order** | Putting things in the sequence they happened |
| **before / after** | Earlier than / later than another event |
| **change** | Something that is different now from how it was |
| **stayed the same** | Something that is still the way it was |
| **source** | Something that gives information about the past |
| **photograph** | A picture taken with a camera of a real moment |
| **object** | A real thing from the past you can look at or hold (a lamp, a wash basin) |
| **story people tell** | Words passed from person to person about the past |
| **evidence** | Something you can see or hear in a source that answers a question |
| **perspective** | How something looks or feels to a particular person |

daily life (lamps, washing, carts, letters) in a fictional town or in
undated, adult-vetted photographs of everyday objects. The unit makes no claims
about dates, places, or named historical people; dating and named figures
belong to Unit 07, where the adult vets every story and source.

## For the guiding adult — read before Week 11

- **Retrieval warm-up (Session 1, 2 minutes):** place Unit 01 community members
  on the Unit 02 map idea — "who lives near the school on Willow Street?" —
  then ask, "was that map about today, or about long ago?" This links maps
  (space) to timelines (time) before any new word is taught.
- **Privacy:** the learner's own-day strip (Session 2) and any family stories
  they volunteer stay in private storage. The class-shareable artifact is the
  fictional Willow Street strip from the project.
- **The generated picture is not evidence:** Lesson 4 says this out loud.
  The Willow Street illustration is a drawing an artist (here, an AI tool)
  made up to help us practice spotting differences. Real evidence comes from
  real photographs, real objects, and real stories people tell.
