# Lesson 1 — Past, Present, Future

**Session 1 · about 15–20 minutes.**

**Learner goal:** I can tell whether something already happened, is happening
now, or has not happened yet.

**Materials:** three baskets or hoops labeled with picture cards (a backward
arrow for past, a star for present, a forward arrow for future — the adult
reads the labels aloud; the learner need not read them); 5 spoken-event cards
the adult reads aloud (no reading required).

**Readiness check (1 minute):** "What did you do first this morning?" (Any
answer counts — the adult is warming up talking about time. Retrieval: "Who
are the members of our classroom community?" — one quick Unit 01 recall.)

## Explicit teaching

*(Adult reads or paraphrases; pause at the bold words.)*

Time moves in one direction, like walking down a path. Things that already
happened are in the **past**. Things happening right now are in the
**present**. Things that have not happened yet are in the **future**.

Yesterday is past. Today, right now, is present. Tomorrow is future.

Hold up three fingers. Point behind you: "Past — already happened." Point at
your feet: "Present — happening now." Point ahead of you: "Future — not yet."

**Long ago** is a special kind of past: so long ago that it happened before
you were born. Your grandmother's childhood is long ago. A horse pulling a
cart down a street is long ago for most streets today — but the street itself
may still be there.

## Worked examples (adult models the reasoning aloud)

**Example 1.** "We ate breakfast this morning. Did breakfast already happen?
Yes — it is behind us on the path. So breakfast is **past**."

**Example 2.** "We are reading this lesson right now. Is reading happening
now? Yes — we are doing it. So reading is **present**."

**Example 3.** "We will play outside later. Has outside play happened yet? No
— it is ahead of us on the path. So playing outside later is **future**."

**Example 4 (long ago).** "Long ago, before you were born, many streets had no
electric lights. People lit oil lamps. That is past — and it is *long ago*
past, because it happened before you were born."

## Guided practice (adult prompts, learner responds; 4 tasks)

1. "We brushed our teeth this morning. Past, present, or future?" *(Hint if
   stuck: "Point behind you for past, at your feet for present, ahead for
   future.")*
2. "We are sitting together now. Past, present, or future?"
3. "We will eat lunch later. Past, present, or future?"
4. "Long ago, people washed clothes in a basin. Is that past, present, or
   future? And is it long ago or just yesterday?" *(Accept "past" and "long
   ago" — the second part is a bonus, not required.)*

## Independent practice

The learner sorts 5 spoken events the adult reads aloud into the three
baskets by placing a token (or pointing) — "we woke up," "we are learning
now," "we will go home later," "yesterday it rained," "tomorrow is a new
day." The adult scribes the learner's sorting as a row of 5 marks.
**Text alternative:** the learner holds up a backward-arrow card (past), a
star card (present), or a forward-arrow card (future) instead of speaking.

## Application

"Tonight, tell someone: one thing from your past today, one thing in your
present right now, and one thing in your future tonight." The adult notes
whether the learner tries it unprompted the next day.

## Exit check

The learner sorts 3 new spoken events (one past, one present, one future)
correctly, or points to the right basket for each. The adult records:
*secure* (3 correct), *developing* (2 correct or needs the pointing hint),
*emerging* (0–1 — reteach with the learner's own day as the only examples).

## Supports

- **Language access:** pre-teach *past / present / future* with the learner's
  home language alongside English; accept answers in any language.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing to baskets or holding up arrow/star
  cards instead of naming aloud.
- **Movement:** walk three steps along a floor line — step back for past,
  stand still for present, step forward for future.

## Extension

"Time detective": the learner listens during the day for time words (already,
now, later, yesterday, tomorrow) and reports one of each the next session.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** learners treat "past" as only "yesterday" and miss
long ago, or treat "future" as "tomorrow only." Keep both scales alive: every
past example gets the follow-up "yesterday-past or long-ago-past?" **Answer-key
reference:** [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) §L1.
