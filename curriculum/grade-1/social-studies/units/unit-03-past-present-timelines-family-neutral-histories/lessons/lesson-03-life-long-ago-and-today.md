# Lesson 3 — Life Long Ago and Today

**Session 5 · about 15–20 minutes.**

**Learner goal:** I can compare life long ago with life today and name one
thing that changed and one thing that stayed the same.

**Materials:** picture pairs the adult shows (no reading required): an oil
lamp and an electric lamp; a wash basin and a washing machine; a horse cart
and a car; a hand-written letter and a phone call (adult explains each
picture). The [Willow Street illustration](../assets/willow-street-past-and-present.png)
is previewed by the adult here and used fully in Session 6.

**Readiness check (1 minute):** "Point to the first event on this timeline"
(the adult shows yesterday's 3-event strip). Quick Lesson 2 retrieval.

## Explicit teaching

*(Adult reads or paraphrases; pause at the bold words.)*

**Long ago**, before you were born, everyday life worked differently in some
ways — and exactly the same in others. A **change** is something that is
different now from how it was. **Stayed the same** means something is still
the way it was.

Look at light. Long ago, many people lit an oil lamp — a small lamp you fill
with oil and light with a flame. Today, most homes switch on an electric
light. The *way we make light* changed. But the *need* stayed the same:
people still want light when it is dark.

Look at washing. Long ago, many people washed clothes by hand in a basin of
soapy water, then hung them on a line. Today, many people use a washing
machine. The *tool* changed. But the *job* stayed the same: clothes still get
dirty, and people still wash them.

Look at travel. Long ago, a horse could pull a cart down a dirt road. Today, a
car drives down a paved road. The *vehicle and the road* changed. But the
*reason* stayed the same: people still need to go places and carry things.

Not everything changed at the same time, and not everywhere. Some people
still wash by hand today. Some streets still have horses. "Long ago" and
"today" are about what is *usual*, not about what every single person does.

## Worked examples (adult models the reasoning aloud)

**Example 1 (lighting).** "Oil lamp, electric light. What changed? The lamp
itself — oil and flame became electricity and a switch. What stayed the
same? People still need light at night. So: changed — how we make light;
stayed the same — needing light when it is dark."

**Example 2 (washing).** "Basin, washing machine. What changed? The tool and
the work — hands in soapy water became a machine doing the scrubbing. What
stayed the same? Clothes get dirty and someone washes them. A machine did not
make dirty clothes disappear."

**Example 3 (travel).** "Horse cart, car. What changed? The animal pulling
became an engine, and the dirt road became a paved road. What stayed the
same? People travel to get somewhere, and they carry things with them."

**Example 4 (communication — modeled, not assumed).** "Long ago, a letter
traveled slowly, carried from place to place. Today, many people talk on a
phone right away. What changed? The speed. What stayed the same? People still
want to tell each other news."

## Guided practice (adult prompts, learner responds; 4 tasks)

1. Show the lamp pair. "What changed? What stayed the same?" *(Hint if
   stuck: "Point to what is different. Now point to what people still
   need.")*
2. Show the washing pair. "What changed? What stayed the same?"
3. Show the travel pair. "What changed? What stayed the same?"
4. Error-analysis: the adult says, "Long ago, nobody needed light at night."
   "Is that right? How do you know? Fix the sentence." *(The need stayed the
   same — only the lamp changed.)*

## Independent practice

The learner sorts 6 picture cards into "changed" and "stayed the same" piles
for one area the adult chooses (for example, washing: basin / machine /
soapy water / dirty clothes / clothesline / clean clothes — the adult
accepts defensible sorts and asks "why?" for one card). **Text alternative:**
the learner points to the changed pile or the stayed pile for each card
while the adult moves it.

## Application

"Find one thing in this room that would also have been here long ago (a
chair, a book, a cup) and one thing that would not (a phone charger, a
plastic toy with batteries). Tell me why you chose each." The adult notes
whether the learner's reasoning uses "changed" or "stayed the same."

## Exit check

The learner names, for one area, one change and one stay-the-same. The adult
records: *secure* (both, unprompted), *developing* (one, or both with
prompts), *emerging* (cannot name either — reteach with lighting only).

## Supports

- **Language access:** pre-teach *change / stayed the same* with gestures
  (swap hands for change, steady hands for stayed) and the home language.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing to picture pairs and sorting cards
  instead of speaking sentences.
- **Movement:** stand for "changed," sit still for "stayed the same" as the
  adult reads a list of things about daily life.

## Extension

"One more area": the learner picks an area not yet discussed (cooking,
school, games) and, with the adult, guesses one change and one stay-the-same
— labeled clearly as a guess to check later, not a fact.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** learners conclude "everything was worse long ago"
or "everything is new now." Keep returning to the stay-the-same column — the
needs (light, clean clothes, travel, news) are the anchor. Also watch for
"long ago = 100 years exactly": long ago is deliberately undated here; dates
belong to later grades. **Answer-key reference:**
