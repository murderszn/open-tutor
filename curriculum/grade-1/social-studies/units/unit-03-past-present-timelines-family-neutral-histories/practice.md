# Unit 03 — Practice Sessions

For the guiding adult. Learner responses are oral, pointing, drawing, or
adult-scribed throughout. Nothing the learner makes here that is personal
goes into the public repository; the class-shareable work is the fictional
Willow Street material.

## Practice A — My-day strip (Session 2)

**Goal:** place 3 picture cards of the learner's own day in order.

1. The adult draws or prints 3 simple cards for the learner's real day so
   far: waking up, eating breakfast, coming to class (adapt to the learner's
   actual morning; if the learner's morning differs, use their real events).
2. Shuffle. The learner orders them earliest first on a strip, then reads
   the strip back: "first… next… last…"
3. Add a fourth card: "what we will do after this lesson." The learner
   places it last and says why with "after."
4. **Check:** 3 in order = secure; 2 in order or order with one prompt =
   developing; fewer = reteach Lesson 2 with 2 cards. This strip stays in
   private storage.

## Timeline game — Willow Street morning, mixed up (Session 4)

**Goal:** re-order a 4-event fictional strip without adult ordering.

The adult reads these four Willow Street morning events (fictional town,
invented for this unit) out of order:

- The lamp-lighter puts out the street lamps at dawn.
- The baker opens the shop and puts bread in the window.
- Children walk to school with their satchels.
- The cart driver loads crates and drives down the street.

1. The learner orders all four on a strip, earliest first. (Defensible
   order: lamps out → baker opens → cart loads → children walk; the adult
   accepts any order the learner defends with "before/after" reasoning about
   dawn, opening, and school start — see the answer key.)
2. The learner says one "before" sentence and one "after" sentence about the
   strip.
3. **Swap round:** the adult swaps two cards. "What is wrong now? Fix it and
   tell me why." Repeat twice.
4. **Check:** correct order + one defended sentence = secure.

## Practice B — Spotting in the Willow Street illustration (Session 6)

**Goal:** find 3 differences and 1 stay-the-same in the generated picture.

Show the [Willow Street illustration](assets/willow-street-past-and-present.png)
(printed or on screen). Read the caption and the text alternative in
[assets/README.md](assets/README.md) aloud if the learner cannot see the
picture clearly. Remind the learner: this is a **drawing made to help us
practice, not a photograph of a real street**.

1. "Point to something that is different on the two sides." Repeat until the
   learner has found 3 differences (lamp, road/vehicle, washing method are
   the clearest).
2. "Point to something that stayed the same on both sides." (House, big
   tree, hills, washing as a job, smoke from the chimney.)
3. "Which side is long ago? How do you know?" (The horse cart, oil lamp, and
   dirt road are the evidence — accept any one.)
4. "Tell me one change and one stay-the-same in a full sentence each."
5. **Check:** 3 differences + 1 stay-the-same = secure; 2 differences =
   developing; fewer = re-look with the adult pointing at one pair first.

## Read-aloud A — *The Lantern on Willow Street* (Session 7)

*Original story, written for this unit. Fictional town; no family structure
is assumed. Adult reads aloud with voices; pause at the bold words.*

> On Willow Street, long ago, there were no electric lights. When evening
> came, Sam the lamp-lighter walked the street with a long pole. *Click,
> hiss* — one lamp, then another, then another, until the whole street glowed
> warm yellow.
>
> One evening, a child named Nia watched Sam work. "Why do you light the
> lamps?" Nia asked.
>
> "So everyone can find their way home in the dark," Sam said. "Light helps
> people feel safe."
>
> Nia helped carry Sam's oil can to the last lamp. "Will the lamps ever light
> themselves?" Nia asked.
>
> Sam laughed. "Maybe one day, someone will invent a light that switches on
> all by itself!"
>
> Many years later — in the present, on that same street — lights do switch
> on by themselves. But people still want light when it is dark. That part
> never changed.

**Response activities (4 tasks):**

1. Retell the story in order: evening comes → Sam lights the lamps → Nia
   asks why → Sam explains → lights today switch on by themselves. (Picture
   cards allowed; order matters, exact words do not.)
2. "What changed between Sam's time and today?" (How lights are lit: oil
   flame and a person walking vs. electric lights that switch on.)
3. "What stayed the same?" (People want light in the dark; people want to
   feel safe finding their way.)
4. "What question would you ask Sam if you met him?" (Any genuine question
   counts; the adult scribes one and notes whether it is about the past.)

## Changed / stayed-the-same sort + review game (Session 8)

**Goal:** sort 6 cards; then play the review game.

Sort cards (adult reads each aloud): oil lamp / electric light / needing
light at night / wash basin / washing machine / clothes getting dirty.

1. The learner sorts each card into "changed" or "stayed the same" piles
   for its area. (Changed: oil lamp, electric light, wash basin, washing
   machine — as tools; stayed: needing light at night, clothes getting
   dirty. Accept "the lamp changed" reasoning for either lamp card placed
   in "changed.")
2. For one card in each pile, the learner says why it belongs there.
3. **Review game — "Change or same?":** the adult reads 8 quick items (a
   horse cart, a car, a house, a tree, an oil lamp, an electric light,
   dirty clothes, clean clothes). The learner stands for "changed" and sits
   still for "stayed the same" — or points to pile cards. 6 of 8 = secure.
4. **Check:** 5 of 6 sort cards + 6 of 8 game items = secure.

## Source sort practice (Session 10)

**Goal:** sort 6 source examples into photograph / object / story.

The adult reads each example aloud and shows a picture card for it:

1. A printed photo of an old street (photograph).
2. An old oil lamp you can hold (object).
3. An elder neighbor telling how the street looked when they were young
   (story people tell).
4. A printed photo of a horse cart (photograph).
5. A wash basin from long ago (object).
6. A storyteller telling the tale of the lamp-lighter (story people tell).

1. The learner sorts all six into three piles and names each pile.
2. "Which pile is the Willow Street illustration in?" (None — it is a
   drawing made for practice, not a source. This is the key item; reteach
   Lesson 4's illustration test if missed.)
3. "Pick one pile. Tell me one thing that kind of source can teach us and
   one thing it cannot." (Any reasonable pair counts; see the answer key.)
4. **Check:** 5 of 6 sorted + the illustration answer = secure.

## Read-aloud B — *Two Pictures of One Street* (Session 12)

*Original story, written for this unit. Adult reads aloud; the learner plays
source detective.*

> Jai found two pictures in a library book. Both showed the same street.
> In the first picture, a horse pulled a cart down a dirt road. A tall lamp
> with a flame stood at the corner. Clothes hung on a line behind a small
> house.
>
> In the second picture, a car drove down a paved road. A tall electric light
> stood at the same corner. The same small house stood behind a big tree.
>
> "Are these the same street?" Jai asked the librarian.
>
> "Look for what stayed the same," the librarian said. "The house. The tree.
> The corner. The street is the same place — but the time is different."
>
> Jai pointed at the horse. "So long ago, people traveled by horse cart?"
>
> "The picture shows a horse and a cart," the librarian said, "so that is
> good evidence. But the picture does not tell us the horse's name, or who
> drove the cart. Some questions a picture cannot answer — and that is all
> right."

**Source detective (3 questions):**

1. "How did people travel on this street long ago? What in the picture is
   your evidence?" (Horse pulling a cart.)
2. "What stayed the same in both pictures? Name two things." (House, tree,
   corner — any two.)
3. "What is one question the pictures cannot answer?" (The horse's name, the
   driver's name, what people were thinking — any question the pictures do
   not show.)
4. **Check:** 2 of 3 with evidence or a good "cannot tell" = secure.
