# Project — My Neighborhood Map

**Sessions 13–14 · about 15–20 minutes each, adult-supervised.**

The learner makes a bird's-eye map of their own neighborhood (or, if that is
not possible, their street, block, or the area around the school) using
everything from the unit: the above view, symbols, a key, land and water, and
cultural and environmental features. On Session 14 the maps go up in a gallery
walk and each learner-presents theirs.

## Materials

- Large paper (11×17 or two taped sheets), crayons/markers (blue and green required)
- The [bird's-eye illustration](assets/neighborhood-from-above.png) as a mentor example
- The learner's route map from Sessions 7–8 (to reuse landmarks)
- Sticky notes for gallery-walk comments (drawn stars/checks are fine)

## Steps

### Session 13 — Draft our map

1. **Plan (3 min):** "What will your map show?" The learner names the area
   ("my street," "around my school"), one water or land feature if any, and
   three landmarks. The adult writes the plan as 2–3 word labels.
2. **Draw from above (8 min):** the learner draws the area like a bird sees
   it — tops only. Roads as lines, houses as roof shapes, water blue, land
   green. The adult reminds: "Would a bird see a door? Then do not draw
   one."
3. **Key (4 min):** the learner adds a key box with at least three symbols
   and their meanings, dictated or labeled.
4. **Check (3 min):** the adult asks the three project questions: "Is it from
   above? Does every symbol have a key entry? Is water blue?" The learner
   fixes what is missing.

### Session 14 — Finish, gallery walk, share

1. **Finish (5 min):** the learner adds one cultural feature and one
   environmental feature with symbols, both in the key.
2. **Gallery walk (5 min):** maps are displayed; everyone walks, looks, and
   leaves one sticky-note comment (a star, a check, or a dictated word like
   "clear key!").
3. **Share (5 min each, or across the week):** the learner presents: "This is
   my map of ___. This symbol stands for ___. The water here is ___. One
   cultural feature is ___ and one environmental feature is ___." The adult
   asks one follow-up question ("How would a visitor get from here to the
   park on your map?").

## Deliverables

1. A finished bird's-eye neighborhood map (paper).
2. A key with at least three symbols, every map symbol explained in it.
3. A 4-sentence oral presentation (adult may scribe it as the learner's
   "map talk" card).
4. One gallery-walk comment left on someone else's map (a mark counts).

## Rubric — observable criteria

| Criterion | Secure (3) | Developing (2) | Emerging (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Above view** | All features drawn as tops (roofs, road lines); no side-view doors/windows | Mostly tops; 1–2 side-view slips | Drawn from the side throughout |
| **Symbols + key** | ≥3 symbols, every one explained in the key | 2 symbols, or one symbol missing from the key | Symbols with no key, or key with no matching symbols |
| **Land and water** | Water blue, land green; at least one of each labeled or pointed to | Colors correct but neither labeled | Blue/green agreement broken |
| **Features** | Names 1 cultural + 1 environmental feature on the map | Names one of the two kinds | Cannot sort features |
| **Presentation** | 4-sentence talk covering area, a symbol, water, and both feature kinds | 2–3 sentences with prompting | Cannot describe the map aloud |

**Scoring:** 13–15 = secure overall; 9–12 = developing (reteach the lowest
row); below 9 = emerging (redo Session 13's draft with the adult drawing
alongside). The rubric evidence feeds objectives 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6.

## Accessibility and safety

- **Low-vision:** raised-line drawing (glue lines), textured symbol stickers,
  high-contrast blue/green; the presentation may be given entirely orally with
  the adult pointing.
- **Fine-motor:** the learner places pre-cut symbol shapes and dictates the
  key; drawing quality is never scored — only the above view, symbols, key,
  and colors.
- **Safety:** maps show public places only — never the learner's home address,
  house number, or full street name ("home" and "the park" are enough). Walks
  that informed the map were adult-accompanied on a defined route.
