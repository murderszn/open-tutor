# Unit 02 — Local Maps, Land, Water, and Location

Grade 1 · Social Studies · Weeks 7–10 · issue #13, section U02 (draft for review).

## Essential question

*How do maps help us find our way and describe the places we live?*

## What this unit teaches

A first grader already knows where things are — the toy is *under* the bed,
the park is *near* the school. This unit turns that everyday knowing into
geography. The learner practices location words until they are automatic, meets
the **bird's-eye view** (what the world looks like from above), learns that a
**map** is a picture of a place seen from above, reads simple **symbols** with
a **key**, draws a route map from the front door to a neighborhood landmark,
and finds **land** and **water** on maps and a globe. The unit closes with a
"My Neighborhood Map" project the learner draws, keys, and presents.

## Prerequisites

From the Week 1–2 diagnostic and Unit 01 ([scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md),
Section 5): the learner follows turn-taking discussions, uses everyday words
like in/out/up/down/near/far, talks about home and neighborhood, draws and
labels with adult help, and dictates sentences for the adult to scribe.
Left/right and above/below are taught here, not required on entry. Unit 02's
retrieval warm-up places Unit 01 community members on a map, so civics
vocabulary keeps living. No reading level is assumed; written recording stays
to 2–3 word labels, dictated sentences, drawings, and maps.

## Measurable objectives (adult-assessed by end of Week 10)

1. Use location and direction words — near, far, left, right, above, below,
   next to — to describe where things are, with at least 4 of 5 correct in the
   Session 2 scavenger hunt.
2. Explain in their own words what a map is ("a picture of a place seen from
   above" counts) and name one difference between the bird's-eye view and the
   standing view.
3. Use a simple map key: match at least 4 of 5 symbols to what they stand for.
4. Draw a picture-map of a familiar room or route that shows at least three
   labeled features and includes a simple key with at least two symbols.
5. On a globe or simple map, point to land and point to water; name one
   landform and one water body they have seen (hill, river, pond, lake, and
   similar all count).
6. Name one cultural feature (made by people — school, park bench, road) and
   one environmental feature (not made by people — river, hill, tree) of their
   own neighborhood.

## Vocabulary

Introduced with objects, pictures, and walks first; the word follows the idea.

| Word | Grade-1 meaning used in this unit |
|---|---|
| **map** | A picture of a place drawn as if seen from above |
| **bird's-eye view** | What something looks like from high above, like a bird sees it |
| **symbol** | A small drawing on a map that stands for a real thing |
| **key** | The part of a map that tells what each symbol means |
| **land** | The solid ground part of Earth |
| **water** | Rivers, lakes, ponds, and oceans — shown blue on maps and globes |
| **globe** | A ball-shaped model of Earth |
| **route** | The way you go from one place to another |
| **landmark** | A building or place that helps you know where you are |
| **near / far** | Close to / a long way from |
| **left / right** | The two sides of your body (hold up hands: the L-hand is left) |
| **above / below** | Higher than / lower than |
| **next to** | Right beside, touching or almost touching |
| **cultural feature** | Something in a place that people made (school, road, bench) |
| **environmental feature** | Something in a place people did not make (river, hill, tree) |

## Differentiation

- **Supports:** oral, pointing, and drawing response modes for every check; the
  adult scribes dictated labels; vocabulary taught with objects, walks, and
  pictures before words; home-language labels welcome alongside English;
  large-print, high-contrast symbol cards; textured shapes for low-vision
  learners; every lesson includes a table and a floor-play movement variant.
- **Extensions:** map the route to a second landmark and compare the two
  routes; add a "future" symbol to the key (something the learner wishes were
  in the neighborhood) and defend the choice; interview a household adult
  about how they learned their way around as a child.
- K–2 rule: all investigations are adult-supervised; neighborhood walks stay on
  a defined, adult-chosen route with no contact with strangers; maps show only
  public places, never the learner's home address.

## Readiness to move on (Week 10)

The learner is ready for Unit 03 when objectives 1–4 are at least *developing*
and the learner can point to land and water on a globe. If symbols and keys
still blur, re-teach Sessions 5–6 before Unit 03; Unit 03's retrieval warm-up
revisits the map by placing past/present pictures on a timeline strip, so map
language keeps living there.
