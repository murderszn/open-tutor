# Lesson 1 — Where Is It?

**Session 1 · about 15–20 minutes.**

**Learner goal:** I can use location words — near, far, left, right, above,
below, next to — to say where things are.

**Materials:** a soft ball or stuffed animal; 3–4 familiar objects (book, cup,
shoe).

**Readiness check (1 minute):** "Point to something *near* you. Now point to
something *far* from you." (Near/far are already familiar; this lesson adds
the rest.)

## Explicit teaching

*(Adult reads or paraphrases; pause at the bold words.)*

You already know how to say where things are. Today we learn the exact words
map-makers use, because maps need exact words.

**Near** means close to you. **Far** means a long way from you. **Next to**
means right beside — touching or almost touching.

**Above** means higher than. **Below** means lower than. The light is *above*
the table; the rug is *below* the table.

**Left** and **right** are the two sides of your body. Hold up both hands,
thumbs out — the hand that makes an **L** is your **left** hand. Everything on
that side is on your left; the other side is your right.

Map-makers use these words so anyone reading the map can find things. By the
end of this unit, you will use them on your own maps.

## Worked examples (adult models the reasoning aloud)

**Example 1.** *The adult puts the ball on the table and stands beside it.*
"Where is the ball? It is *next to* me — right beside me, almost touching.
Is it *above* the table or *below* the table? It is sitting *on* the table,
so it is *above* the floor and *below* the light. One thing can be above one
thing and below another at the same time."

**Example 2.** *The adult puts the book on the learner's left and the cup on
the learner's right.* "Where is the book? It is on your *left* — the L-hand
side. Where is the cup? It is on your *right*. Now *I* move to the other side
of the table. Is the book still on *my* left? No! Left and right move with
the person. That is why map-makers always say whose left they mean — or they
draw it on the map so everyone sees the same picture."

## Guided practice (adult prompts, learner responds; 6 tasks)

1. "Put the ball *above* the book." *(Check: ball is higher than the book.)*
2. "Now put the ball *below* the book." *(Ball is lower than the book.)*
3. "Put the shoe *next to* the cup." *(Touching or almost touching.)*
4. "Point to something *far* from you with your *left* hand." *(Two words at
   once — praise the combination.)*
5. "Is the ceiling *above* us or *below* us?" *(Above.)*
6. "Stand up. Take one step to your *right*. Now you moved right — good."

## Independent practice

The learner arranges the four objects on the table however they like, then
tells the adult where two of them are using location words ("The cup is next
to the shoe. The book is below the ball."). The adult scribes the learner's
sentences (2–3 word labels are fine: "cup next to shoe"). **Text alternative:**
the learner points to each pair and the adult says the sentence for them to
confirm with "yes" or fix.

## Application

"At dinner, use one location word to tell someone where something is: 'The
salt is next to your plate.' Report back tomorrow what you said."

## Exit check

The adult places the ball somewhere new and asks, "Where is the ball? Use one
of our words." Then: "Point left. Point right." The adult records: *secure*
(uses 4+ words correctly across the lesson), *developing* (2–3 words, or
left/right confusion), *emerging* (0–1 words — reteach with only near/far/
next to/above/below first, add left/right tomorrow).

## Supports

- **Language access:** pre-teach the six words with gestures and the home
  language alongside English; accept answers in any language.
- **Alternate response mode:** pointing and placing objects instead of naming.
- **Movement:** whole-body game — "jump *above* the line, crouch *below* your
  hands, step *left*."

## Extension

"Secret directions": the learner hides the ball and gives the adult three
location-word clues to find it ("It is below the table. It is near the door.
It is to your right."). The adult follows exactly what is said, modeling why
exact words matter.

## Teacher note

**Common misconception:** learners treat left/right as fixed ("the book is on
the left side *of the table*") rather than body-relative. Keep returning to
the L-hand check; on maps, the drawn picture removes the confusion, which is
one reason maps are useful. **Answer-key reference:** [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md)
§L1 for acceptable wordings (on/under count as above/below at this age; beside
counts as next to).
