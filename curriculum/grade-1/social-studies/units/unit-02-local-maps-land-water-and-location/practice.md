# Unit 02 — Practice Sessions and Read-Alouds

All sessions are adult-led, ~15–20 minutes. The learner responds orally, by
pointing, or by drawing; the adult scribes anything longer than a 2–3 word
label. Both stories below are **original** — written for this unit, no
copyrighted text.

## Practice A — Session 2: Location-word scavenger hunt

**Goal:** use all six location words to find hidden things.

1. The adult hides five soft objects around one room before the session.
2. The adult gives one location-word clue per object: "Find something *below*
   the chair." "Find something *next to* the lamp." "Find something *above*
   the shelf you can reach." "Find something to the *left* of the door."
   "Find something *far* from the window."
3. The learner finds each object, then describes where it was in a full
   sentence: "It was below the chair."
4. **Picture-map:** the learner draws their bedroom or the classroom as a
   picture-map — the room's shape with at least three features placed where
   they really are (bed, door, window). The adult labels with the learner's
   words. This is the learner's first real map: praise "drawn from above"
   placements ("your bed is in the corner of the paper, like a bird sees
   it").

**Success:** 4 of 5 objects found from clues; picture-map shows 3 features in
sensible positions. **Answer-key reference:** [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) §P.

## Read-aloud A — Session 4: *Where Is Bear?* (original story)

*(Adult reads aloud with expression; the learner tracks Bear with a finger on
an imaginary map or simply listens and answers.)*

> Bear woke up early. Bear's honey pot was gone! "Where is it?" growled Bear.
> Bear looked **below** the table. No honey pot. Bear looked **above** the
> shelf. No honey pot. Bear looked **next to** the door. No honey pot!
> Then Bear's friend Rabbit hopped in. "It is **far** from the door," said
> Rabbit, "and **near** the window. Look to your **left**." Bear looked left.
> There it was — the honey pot, **below** the window, **next to** the flower
> pot! "Hooray!" shouted Bear. "Location words found my honey!"

**Response activity:** the adult re-reads one paragraph at a time; the learner
acts out each location word with Bear (a stuffed animal): put Bear below the
table, above the shelf (as high as safe), next to the door. Then the learner
retells where the honey pot was using at least three location words.

## Practice B — Session 4 (after the read-aloud): Tabletop bird's-eye drawing

**Goal:** draw one arrangement twice — from the side and from above — and feel
the difference.

1. The adult arranges three objects on the table (cup, book, ball).
2. The learner draws the arrangement "like you see it sitting here" (side
   view — sides showing).
3. Then the learner stands on a sturdy chair with the adult holding them safe,
   looks straight down, and draws the same arrangement "like a bird sees it"
   (tops only — circles and rectangles).
4. Side by side: "Which drawing is more like a map? How do you know?" (The
   from-above one — no sides, everything fits on the page.)

**Success:** the two drawings differ (one shows sides, one shows tops); the
learner names the above-view drawing as map-like.

## Practice C — Session 6: Symbol match game

**Goal:** read a key and match symbols fast.

1. The adult lays out the five symbol cards from Lesson 3 (blue wavy line,
   green triangle, red square, gray curving line, blue circle) and five
   meaning cards (river, tree, house, road, pond), all face up.
2. Round 1 — match: the learner pairs each symbol with its meaning. The adult
   reads the growing key aloud: "blue wavy line *stands for* river."
3. Round 2 — speed: the adult shuffles the meaning cards and holds them up one
   at a time; the learner points to the matching symbol.
4. **Make-your-own key:** the learner invents three symbols for three things
   in the classroom (door, rug, shelf) and the adult writes the key exactly as
   dictated. Test it: can the adult "read" a tiny map drawn with only those
   symbols?

**Success:** 4 of 5 matches in Round 1; a 3-symbol key where every symbol has
a stated meaning.

## Walk practice — Session 8: Landmark-collecting walk

**Goal:** collect real landmarks to finish the Session 7 route map.

*(Adult-accompanied, on a defined route the adult chose in advance — e.g.,
front door to the park. No contact with strangers; the walk is observation
only.)*

1. Before leaving: "Our route is home → [landmark]. We are collecting
   landmarks in order. First, then, last."
2. On the walk, the learner calls out landmarks; the adult photographs or
   sketches each and numbers them in order.
3. Back home: the learner finishes the route map from Lesson 4 — symbols for
   each collected landmark, connected by the line of travel, key completed.
4. **If no walk is possible** (weather, safety): the adult walks a route
   *inside* the home (front door → kitchen → bedroom), and the learner maps
   that instead. The skills are identical.

**Success:** route map shows at least 3 landmarks in order with a line of
travel and a key of at least 2 symbols.

## Read-aloud B — Session 8 (after the walk): *Mia's Walk to the Park* (original story)

> Mia loved Saturdays, because Saturday was park day. "First," said Mom,
> "we leave our **home**." Mia drew a little square for home. "Then we pass
> the **big oak tree**." Mia drew a green triangle. "Then the **corner
> store** with the blue door." Mia drew a blue rectangle. "**Last**, the
> **park**!" Mia drew a green circle with a swing. Mom smiled. "You just made
> a route map, Mia. First, then, last — and we never got lost."

**Response activity:** the learner retells Mia's route in order (first/then/
last), then compares it with their own walk: "What was *your* first landmark?
What was last?" The adult notes whether the learner sequences without
prompting.

## Globe practice — Session 10: Land-water sort

**Goal:** read the blue/green agreement on a real globe.

1. The adult spins the globe slowly and stops it at random three times.
2. Each stop: the learner points and says "land" or "water," then names what
   they see if they can ("That blue is an ocean. That green is land.").
3. **Color-a-map:** the adult draws a simple outline (one winding river, one
   round pond, land around); the learner colors all water blue and all land
   green, then dictates one label for a water body and one for a land shape.
4. Stretch: "Is there more blue or more green on the globe?" (More blue —
   most of Earth is water. Accept "more blue" with any wording.)

**Success:** 3 of 3 land/water identifications correct; coloring follows the
agreement.

## Observation notes for the adult

After each practice, jot one line per objective: which location words are
automatic, whether the above-view is stable, whether the key habit holds
(every symbol explained), and whether route order survives without prompting.
These notes are the evidence for the Session 15 quiz and the Session 16
assessment — and for the "ready to move on" call in the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md).
