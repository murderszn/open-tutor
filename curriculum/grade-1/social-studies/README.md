# Grade 1 Social Studies

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are delivered;
the eight units are planned, not yet written. See [scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained for
one grade-1 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 1 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-1 social studies course built on the four
disciplines of the NCSS C3 Framework — civics, economics, geography, and history —
organized around the learner's own communities: classroom, home, and
neighborhood. Units cover community membership with rights and responsibilities;
simple maps of familiar places with land and water; past–present timelines told
through family-neutral histories; community leaders, rules, and group decision
making; needs, wants, producers, and consumers; cultures and celebrations with
diverse perspectives; historical-figure stories built from simple sources; and a
closing civic-inquiry project where the learner identifies a real classroom or
community problem and helps take action on it.

Sessions are short (15–20 minutes, about four per week) and built on discussion,
read-alouds, drawing, simple map and timeline making, neighborhood observation,
and adult-scribed recording; the learner's own written recording stays light
(2–3 word labels, dictated sentences).

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Describe the communities they belong to (classroom, home, neighborhood) and
   name at least one right and one responsibility they hold in each.
2. Explain the need for rules in different settings; follow agreed-upon rules in
   discussions; explain how rules function in classroom and school settings.
3. Describe the roles and responsibilities of people in authority (teacher,
   principal, mayor, firefighters, police officers); explain what a government
   is and name one thing it does; make a classroom decision with others using
   listening, consensus, or voting, and take part in the resulting action.
4. Construct simple maps and picture representations of familiar places; use
   location and direction words (near, far, left, right, above, below, next to);
   describe places using cultural and environmental characteristics.
5. Order events on a past–present–future timeline; compare life in the past to
   life today; name kinds of historical sources (photographs, objects, stories)
   and use one source to answer a question.
6. Explain that scarcity makes choices necessary; name a benefit and a cost of a
   personal decision; sort needs and wants; describe goods and services people
   in the local community produce; explain how people earn income and why people
   save.
7. Describe celebrations and cultural practices — their own and others' — with
   respect; compare their own point of view with another person's; compare
   perspectives of people in the past with those of people today.
8. Ask and answer questions about a historical figure from a simple source;
   name the maker, date, or place of origin of a historical source when it is
   evident; gather relevant information from one or two sources.
9. With the guiding adult, identify a classroom or community problem, name a
   way people are trying to address it, and help take a small, safe action.

## Standards reference

Objectives reference the NCSS **C3 Framework** inquiry indicators for the K–2
band (indicators written "by the end of grade 2" — there is no separate grade-1
indicator set; this track treats them as year-1 progressions toward the band
target). Descriptions were verified against the framework's indicator text
2026-10-02; this is a reference use only, not a claim of state adoption,
accreditation, or alignment certification. Full crosswalk in
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md#4-standards-crosswalk):

- Civics: D2.Civ.1–3, D2.Civ.5–12, D2.Civ.14 (K–2)
- Economics: D2.Eco.1, D2.Eco.2, D2.Eco.4, D2.Eco.6, D2.Eco.10 (K–2)
- Geography: D2.Geo.1–4, D2.Geo.6–8 (K–2)
- History: D2.His.1–4, D2.His.6, D2.His.9–12, D2.His.14, D2.His.16 (K–2)
- Inquiry and informed action: D1.1–5, D3.1–2, D4.1–8 (K–2)

## Structure

- [Scope and sequence](scope-and-sequence.md) — audit, standards crosswalk,
  36-week pacing with weekly goals, session model, materials, accessibility.
- `units/` — eight units, planned (not yet written):
  1. Community membership, rights, and responsibilities
  2. Local maps, land, water, and location
  3. Past, present, timelines, and family-neutral histories
  4. Community leaders, rules, and decision making
  5. Needs, wants, producers, and consumers
  6. Cultures, celebrations, and diverse perspectives
  7. Historical figures, stories, and simple sources
  8. Community problem solving and civic inquiry

## For the guiding adult

Grade-1 social studies is an oral, observation-based course: the diagnostic
weeks (Weeks 1–2) verify listening, discussion, drawing, and location-word
skills so the units never assume them. Read the scope-and-sequence before
starting; it lists safe household materials, accessibility supports, and the
diagnostic, catch-up, and review weeks that keep pacing humane. Histories stay
family-neutral — nothing in the units assumes a family structure, and any
personal or family timelines are kept in private storage, never in this public
repository. All read-alouds and figure stories are vetted by the adult for age
suitability and advertising-free access, and every unit will ship with its own
teacher guide and answer key.
