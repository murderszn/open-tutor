# Grade 4 Language Arts

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are delivered;
the eight units are planned, not yet written. See [scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained for
one grade-4 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository. All research uses adult-selected
sources; see the safety practices in the scope-and-sequence.

See also: [Grade 4 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-4 language arts course: reading evidence and
inference with details from the text; theme, character, setting, and point of view
in stories, drama, and poems; informational main ideas, text structures, and source
comparison; grade-4 grammar, sentence combining, punctuation, and spelling; narrative
craft with dialogue, description, and revision; opinion writing with reasons and
evidence; short research projects with note-taking, paraphrase, and explanatory
writing; and poetry, figurative language, presentations, and a reading–writing
portfolio. Sessions are about 30–35 minutes, four per week, rotating across an
explicit lesson, guided practice, reading or writing practice, and review — with
oral, pointing, and drawing response modes available for every check and adult
directions for every research step.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Read unfamiliar multisyllabic words accurately using combined letter-sound
   knowledge, syllabication patterns, and morphology (roots and affixes); read
   grade-level prose and poetry orally with accuracy, appropriate rate, and
   expression; and use context to confirm or self-correct (RF.4.3, RF.4.3.a,
   RF.4.4.a–c).
2. Clarify unknown and multiple-meaning words flexibly — context clues, common
   Greek and Latin affixes and roots, print and digital dictionaries — and
   explain simple similes and metaphors, common idioms, adages, and proverbs in
   context (L.4.4.a–c, L.4.5.a–c, L.4.6).
3. In literature: refer to details and examples to explain explicit meaning and
   draw inferences; determine a theme and summarize; describe a character,
   setting, or event in depth; compare and contrast first- and third-person
   narration; and compare similar themes and topics in stories, myths, and
   traditional literature from different cultures (RL.4.1–4.7, RL.4.9, RL.4.10).
4. In informational text: explain events, procedures, ideas, or concepts
   (what happened and why) from specific information; describe overall text
   structures (chronology, comparison, cause/effect, problem/solution); explain
   how an author uses reasons and evidence; and integrate information from two
   texts on the same topic (RI.4.1–3, RI.4.5, RI.4.8, RI.4.9, RI.4.10).
5. Write narratives: establish a situation and introduce a narrator and/or
   characters; organize a natural event sequence with transitional words; use
   dialogue and description with concrete words and sensory details; provide a
   sense of closure; and plan, revise, and edit with guidance (W.4.3.a–e,
   W.4.4, W.4.5).
6. Write opinion pieces: introduce the topic clearly, state an opinion, group
   related ideas, support reasons with facts and details, link opinion and
   reasons with transitional phrases, and conclude; draw evidence from literary
   or informational texts (W.4.1.a–d, W.4.9.a–b).
7. Conduct short research projects: recall or gather information from print
   and digital sources, take notes, categorize information, list sources,
   paraphrase without copying, and write informative/explanatory texts with
   grouped paragraphs, formatting, precise domain vocabulary, and a concluding
   section (W.4.2.a–e, W.4.6–8).
8. Use grade-4 conventions — relative pronouns and adverbs, progressive verb
   tenses, modal auxiliaries, prepositional phrases, conventional adjective
   order, complete sentences (fragments and run-ons corrected), frequently
   confused words, dialogue punctuation, the comma in compound sentences, and
   grade-appropriate spelling — and participate in prepared discussions,
   paraphrase across media, present organized reports, and distinguish formal
   from informal English (L.4.1.a–g, L.4.2.a–d, L.4.3.a–c; SL.4.1–6).

## For the guiding adult

Grade-4 language arts turns grade-3 fluency into evidence-based reading and
independent writing: every unit teaches before it asks — the legacy prompts
contain no instruction, models, or keys, so always read the scope-and-sequence
and each unit's teacher guide before assigning. Short research projects use
adult-selected sources only, never open web searching by the learner. Writing
is revised with guidance, not graded for perfection on a first draft. Each
future unit will include its own teacher guide and answer key; the legacy
quizzes have no keys yet, so always work solutions yourself before assigning.

  every assignment is a bare prompt with no instruction, and **no answer key or
  rubric exists anywhere** — the units will supply the missing teaching,
  models, and keys.
- **Revise before use (7 quizzes):** the two great-books quizzes (grade-band —
  high-school texts such as *To Kill a Mockingbird*, *Animal Farm*, and "The
  Tell-Tale Heart" are not age-appropriate; the Shakespeare "1,700 invented
  words" item repeats a disputed claim), the figurative-language quiz (split in
  two; reduce original-generation load), the story-elements quiz (halve volume;
  dramatic irony and extended theme statements move to enrichment), and the
  three sentence-grammar quizzes (participial/gerund phrases, noun clauses,
  compound-complex sentences, predicate nominative/adjective, and correlative
  conjunctions are above the grade-4 band — grade-4 grammar stops at
  simple/compound sentences with prepositional phrases).
- **Gaps:** no lessons anywhere (instruction is entirely missing), no reading
  passages or read-aloud sets, no foundational phonics/fluency instruction, no
  explanatory-writing or paraphrase instruction, no text-structure teaching, no
  poetry craft beyond one rhyme workshop, and no discussion or portfolio
  guidance.
