# Practice — Unit 05: Narrative Craft

Work a set after its lesson. The adult checks the set the same day.
Full solutions: [answer key](answer-key.md) § Practice.

## Set A — Story beginnings (after Lesson 01)

Read this opening first:

> On Friday afternoon, Jamal and his grandmother planted tomato
> seeds in pots on the porch. Jamal held the watering can while his
> grandmother showed him how deep to press each seed.

1. Name the situation in one sentence: when, where, and what is
   going on.
2. First person or third person? How do you know?
3. Put these events from "The Spilled Paint" in order (number
   them 1–4): (a) Mia wiped the paint off the table. (b) Mia
   knocked over the blue paint. (c) Mia painted a picture of her
   house. (d) Mia cleaned her brush.
4. Write a situation sentence for this idea: a boy, a library, a
   storm. It must name who, where, when, and what.
5. Which opening is stronger, and why? (a) "There was a kid. He
   did things." (b) "On the first day of summer, Rosa opened the
   old gate to her aunt's garden and found a tiny green door in
   the fence."
6. Who is the narrator of this line, and how do you know? "I could
   hear the rain on the roof, and I knew the game was canceled."
7. Error analysis: "Then the dog ran away. First, we opened the
   gate. In the end, we called his name." What is wrong with the
   order — and what should come first?
8. Write a beginning (2 sentences) for a story about finding
   something surprising in a backpack. Include a situation and at
   least one named character.

## Set B — Transitions (after Lesson 02)

1. Fill each gap with the best transition (use each word once):
   *first · then · after that · finally*
   "___ we packed our bags. ___ we walked to the bus stop.
   ___ we waited an hour. ___ the bus came at last."
2. Choose the best transition: "The sun was shining. ___ dark
   clouds covered the sky." (a) First (b) Suddenly (c) Finally.
   Explain your choice in one sentence.
3. Fix this bare list. Add transitions and one more detail so the
   reader knows *how* it happened: "We built the tower. It fell
   down. We built it again."
4. Which sentence uses "finally" correctly? (a) "Finally, I woke
   up and brushed my teeth." (b) "Finally, after three tries, I
   tied my shoes by myself." Explain in one sentence.
5. Write three sentences about making a sandwich, using "first",
   "then", and "finally" correctly — one transition per sentence.
6. Error analysis: "Later, I woke up. First, I ate breakfast.
   Then, I went to school." What is wrong with the transitions?
   Rewrite the three sentences in the right order.
7. Match each transition to its job: *first · meanwhile ·
   suddenly · in the end* → start / at the same time / unexpected
   change / closing.
8. Write a three-sentence mini-story about a lost-and-found
   lunchbox. Use one transition for a sudden change and one for
   the ending.

## Set C — Dialogue (after Lesson 03)

1. Punctuate this line correctly: *sam said i found your mitten*
2. Punctuate this line correctly: *ana whispered look its hiding
   under the bench*
3. These two speakers share one paragraph. Rewrite with correct
   paragraph breaks: "I lost my kite," Mara said. "I can help,"
   Theo said.
4. Write a 4-line dialogue between Mara and Theo at the moment in
   the picture ([park-story-scene.png](assets/park-story-scene.png)
   or its text-only version in Lesson 03). Start a new paragraph
   each time the speaker changes. Use at least one exclamation and
   one question.
5. Which line is punctuated correctly? (a) "Let's go." said Mara
   (b) "Let's go," said Mara. (c) Lets go, said Mara. Explain the
   errors in the other two.
6. What does this dialogue show about Mia? "I can't do it," Mia
   whispered, looking at the floor. Answer in one sentence.
7. Choose the best speaker tag: "We won the game!" ___ Leo.
   (a) whispered (b) shouted (c) murmured. Explain your choice.
8. Error analysis: a classmate wrote *Mara said, the kite is
   stuck.* Explain in two sentences what is missing — and write
   the line correctly.

## Set D — Description (after Lesson 04)

1. Sort these words into **concrete** or **vague**: *crunchy ·
   nice · red · things · squeaky · big · wooden · stuff.*
2. Rewrite each vague sentence with concrete words: (a) "The soup
   was good." (b) "The room was big."
3. Name the sense each detail uses: (a) "The popcorn smelled
   buttery." (b) "The bell rang sharp and loud." (c) "The ice was
   cold and slippery." (d) "The lemonade was sour." (e) "The
   sunset painted the sky orange and pink."
4. Describe the picture ([park-story-scene.png](assets/park-story-scene.png)
   or its text-only version in Lesson 03) in three sentences. Each
   sentence must have at least one concrete word (a color, a
   number, a shape, a material), and the three sentences together
   must use two different senses.
5. Show, don't tell: write two sentences that show a character is
   excited **without** using the word "excited" (or "happy").
6. Describe Theo in depth in two sentences — one from his words
   and one from his actions in the anchor story.
7. Error analysis: "The very, very, very big dog was super nice."
   Explain in two sentences why this is weak — and rewrite it as
   one concrete sentence.
8. Replace the vague word in each sentence: (a) "She wore a nice
   dress." (b) "He ate some stuff." (c) "It was a good trip."

## Set E — Revision and closure (after Lesson 05)

1. Write a closure (2 sentences) for this unfinished story: "Lena
   found a lost puppy under the school steps. She fed it her
   sandwich and hid it in her backpack. The puppy barked during
   math class." Your closure must tell what changed or what Lena
   learned.
2. Revise this weak paragraph with two named moves (label each
   move: Add / Remove / Move / Swap): "The beach was nice. We did
   stuff. It was fun. Then we left."
3. Which is revising and which is editing? (a) fixing spelling
   (b) adding a sensory detail (c) moving a sentence to the
   beginning (d) fixing a capital letter.
4. Peer conference: read this draft paragraph, then write one
   strength and one specific suggestion for it: "On Monday, I went
   to the park. I played. It was fun. I went home."
5. Name the revision move in each sentence: (a) "I added a
   sentence about the smell of rain." (b) "I cut the sentence that
   repeated the same idea." (c) "I put the ending sentence at the
   end instead of the middle." (d) "I changed 'nice' to 'bright
   yellow'."
6. Write a closure (2 sentences) for the anchor story if it ended
   at this line: "Theo caught the kite as it floated down."
7. Error analysis: "My story is finished because I fixed all the
   spelling." Explain in two sentences why that is editing, not
   revising — and name one revision move the draft still needs.
8. Choose one story starter and write a 3-sentence beginning with
   a situation, a named character, and one transition:
   (1) "The library book was overdue — and it was talking."
   (2) "On the last day of school, the classroom hamster escaped."
   (3) "A red umbrella appeared on our porch, and it wasn't ours."
