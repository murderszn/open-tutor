# Unit Assessment — Narrative Craft: Description, Dialogue, and Revision

**Unit 05, Grade 4 Language Arts · 12 items, 30 points.**
Read the passage first, then answer. The adult scores with the
[answer key](../answer-key.md) § Unit assessment. 25–30 = unit
objectives met; 19–24 = met with reteaching notes; below 19 =
reteach and re-assess with a fresh passage. This is a teacher-made
check, not a standardized or validated assessment.

## The passage

Read it aloud together first.

**"The Rainy-Day Library" (original, written for this unit)**

> On a rainy Tuesday afternoon, Ana hurried into the library with
> her wet umbrella dripping on the mat. She needed one more book
> for her report on volcanoes, and the library was closing in an
> hour.
>
> "Can you help me find a book about volcanoes?" Ana asked the
> librarian, Ms. Patel.
>
> Ms. Patel smiled. "Follow me," she said. "The science shelf is
> at the back, next to the big window."
>
> As they walked past the tall shelves, Ana heard the rain
> pattering on the roof and smelled the warm, dusty smell of old
> books. Her shoes squeaked on the shiny floor.
>
> "Here," Ms. Patel said, pulling out a thick blue book. "This one
> has photographs of real eruptions. But it is heavy. Can you
> carry it?"
>
> "I can try," Ana said, hugging the book to her chest. It smelled
> like new paper.
>
> Just then, a flash of lightning lit up the window, and thunder
> rumbled like a giant's stomach. Ana jumped.
>
> "Don't worry," Ms. Patel said softly. "The library is the safest
> place in a storm. Stay and read for a while."
>
> Ana sat in the big green chair by the window and opened the book.
> The photographs showed orange lava bubbling like soup. She forgot
> all about the storm.
>
> When the rain stopped, Ana checked out the book and thanked Ms.
> Patel. She walked home under a pale blue sky, already planning
> the first sentence of her report.

## Questions

1. (2 points) Who narrates this story — Ana, or someone outside
   the story? How do you know? Answer in one sentence.
2. (2 points) Put these events in order (number them 1–4): (a) Ana
   asks Ms. Patel for help. (b) Ana and Ms. Patel walk to the
   science shelf. (c) Lightning flashes and thunder rumbles.
   (d) Ana sits in the green chair and reads.
3. (2 points) Name two transition words or phrases in the passage
   and say what job each one does.
4. (3 points) Quote one line of dialogue from the passage and
   explain in one sentence what it shows about Ms. Patel.
5. (2 points) Name two sensory details from the passage and the
   sense each one uses (sight, hearing, smell, touch, or taste).
6. (2 points) Rewrite this vague sentence with concrete words:
   "The book was good." Your rewrite must name something specific
   about the book.
7. (3 points) Describe Ana in depth: use two details from the text
   — one from her words and one from her actions — and say what
   they show about her.
8. (2 points) What is the closure sentence of the story? What does
   it tell the reader? Answer in one or two sentences.
9. (2 points) Fix the punctuation in these two dialogue lines:
   (a) *can you help me asked ana*
   (b) *follow me said ms patel*
10. (3 points) Revise this weak paragraph by adding one sensory
    detail and one line of dialogue (punctuated correctly):
    "The library was quiet. Ana read her book. It was good."
11. (4 points) Write a short narrative paragraph (5–7 sentences)
    from this starter: "On the last day of school, the classroom
    hamster escaped." Your paragraph must include: a transition
    word, one line of dialogue, one sensory detail, and a closure
    sentence. (1 point for each element.)
12. (3 points) Look at the paragraph you wrote for item 11. Name
    one revision move you would make to it — add, remove, move, or
    swap — and explain why in one sentence.
