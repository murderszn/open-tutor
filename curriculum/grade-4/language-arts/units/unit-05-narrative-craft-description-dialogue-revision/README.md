# Unit 05 — Narrative Craft: Description, Dialogue, and Revision

**Grade:** 4 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 19–22
**Backlog:** [issue #24](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/24), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Stories are how people remember what happened — and how they make
someone else feel it. In this unit the learner writes a real story
of their own: they **set up a situation** with a narrator and
characters, **order events** so they unfold naturally, **connect
them with transition words**, use **dialogue and description** to
show how characters respond, choose **concrete words and sensory
details**, give the story a **sense of closure**, and then **revise
and edit** it with a partner's help before reading it aloud. Every
model passage in this unit is original, written for this unit; no
published text is reproduced.

## Essential question

How does a writer turn something that happened — real or imagined —
into a story someone else can see, hear, and feel?

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–04 and the track's diagnostic weeks:

- Quote exact text evidence to answer questions (answer → point →
  quote routine); determine a theme and describe characters,
  settings, and events in depth with text details (RL.4.3)
- Compare first- and third-person narration (RL.4.6); distinguish
  literal from nonliteral language
- Write in complete sentences; combine sentences; recognize and
  correct fragments and run-ons; use commas and quotation marks for
  direct speech (L.4.2.b); choose words precisely (L.4.3.a)
- Plan, revise, and edit short writing with peer and adult guidance
  (W.4.5); write routinely over short and extended time frames
  (W.4.10)

New in this unit: establishing a situation and introducing a
narrator and/or characters; organizing a natural event sequence;
transitional words and phrases; dialogue and description as craft
tools; concrete words and sensory details; a sense of closure;
revision as a separate step from editing; reading a finished story
aloud to an audience.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Establish a **situation** and introduce a **narrator** and/or
   **characters**; organize a **natural event sequence** that unfolds
   in order (W.4.3.a).
2. Use a variety of **transitional words and phrases** to manage the
   sequence of events (W.4.3.c).
3. Use **dialogue and description** to develop experiences and
   events or show the **responses of characters** to situations
   (W.4.3.b); punctuate dialogue correctly with **commas and
   quotation marks** (L.4.2.b).
4. Use **concrete words and phrases** and **sensory details** to
   convey experiences and events precisely (W.4.3.d); **choose
   words and phrases precisely** (L.4.3.a); describe a character
   **in depth**, drawing on specific details in a text (RL.4.3).
5. Provide a **sense of closure** (W.4.3.e); **plan, revise, and
   edit** writing with guidance and support from peers and adults
   (W.4.5); produce **clear and coherent** writing appropriate to
   task, purpose, and audience (W.4.4).
6. **Write routinely** over extended time frames (a single sitting
   or a day or two) and shorter time frames (W.4.10); **tell a
   story** in an organized manner, using appropriate facts and
   relevant, descriptive details, speaking clearly at an
   understandable pace (SL.4.4).

## 16-session pacing (4 sessions per week, about 30–35 minutes each)

| Session | Focus | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| W19 S1 | Lesson 01 — situation, narrator, characters, natural sequence | Learner names the situation, narrator, and characters of the anchor story and retells its events in order |
| W19 S2 | Practice A + project launch (choose a story idea, story map) | Learner completes a story map: situation, two characters, setting, beginning–middle–end |
| W19 S3 | Lesson 02 — transitional words and phrases | Learner inserts correct transitions into a bare event list and explains what each one does |
| W19 S4 | Practice B + draft the project beginning | Learner drafts a beginning with a situation, a narrator or characters, and two transitions |
| W20 S5 | Lesson 03, part 1 — dialogue shows how characters respond; punctuation rules | Learner writes two correctly punctuated dialogue lines and says what each one shows about the speaker |
| W20 S6 | Practice C + image dialogue activity | Learner writes a 4-line dialogue for the park picture, punctuated correctly, with a new paragraph per speaker |
| W20 S7 | Lesson 03, part 2 — dialogue inside the anchor story; draft project middle | Learner adds at least one dialogue exchange to the project draft that shows a character's response |
| W20 S8 | Practice C continued + paired read-aloud of drafts | Learner reads a draft aloud and a listener names one thing the dialogue showed |
| W21 S9 | Lesson 04 — concrete words and sensory details; show, don't tell | Learner rewrites three vague sentences with concrete words and names the sense in each sensory detail |
| W21 S10 | Practice D + revise project description | Learner adds at least two sensory details and replaces two vague words in the project draft |
| W21 S11 | Lesson 05 — endings and revision (closure; add/remove/move/swap) | Learner writes a closure for the anchor story's ending and revises the weak paragraph with two named moves |
| W21 S12 | Practice E + peer conference on project draft | Learner gives and receives two specific revision suggestions and records the changes made |
| W22 S13 | Project work session — final copy + drawn illustration (optional) | Final copy has a beginning–middle–end, dialogue, sensory detail, closure, and corrected punctuation |
| W22 S14 | Share circle presentations (SL.4.4) + listener feedback | Learner reads the story aloud in order at an understandable pace; listeners name one description they could picture |
| W22 S15 | Review + formative week-22 quiz | Learner can name the five craft moves (situation, sequence, dialogue, description, closure) without prompting |
| W22 S16 | Culminating unit assessment + reflection | Assessment complete; learner names one strength and one next step |

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for English
Language Arts**, grade 4, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/).
Standard wordings were verified 2026-11-06 against the published
grade-4 Writing standards (W.4.3.a–e, W.4.4, W.4.5, W.4.10),
Language standards (L.4.2.b, L.4.3.a), Reading: Literature standard
RL.4.3, and Speaking and Listening standard SL.4.4, corroborated
across the published standards listings and the track's audit
crosswalk. Paraphrases in the objectives above follow those
wordings; the full standard texts are not reproduced. No state
adoption or accreditation is claimed.

- **W.4.3** Write narratives to develop real or imagined
  experiences or events using effective technique, descriptive
  details, and clear event sequences.
- **W.4.3.a** Orient the reader by establishing a situation and
  introducing a narrator and/or characters; organize an event
  sequence that unfolds naturally.
- **W.4.3.b** Use dialogue and description to develop experiences
  and events or show the responses of characters to situations.
- **W.4.3.c** Use a variety of transitional words and phrases to
  manage the sequence of events.
- **W.4.3.d** Use concrete words and phrases and sensory details
  to convey experiences and events precisely.
- **W.4.3.e** Provide a sense of closure.
- **W.4.4** Produce clear and coherent writing in which the
  development and organization are appropriate to task, purpose,
  and audience.
- **W.4.5** With guidance and support from peers and adults,
  develop and strengthen writing as needed by planning, revising,
  and editing.
- **W.4.10** Write routinely over extended time frames (time for
  research, reflection, and revision) and shorter time frames (a
  single sitting or a day or two) for a range of discipline-specific
  tasks, purposes, and audiences.
- **L.4.2.b** Use commas and quotation marks to mark direct speech
  and quotations from a text.
- **L.4.3.a** Choose words and phrases to convey ideas precisely.
- **RL.4.3** Describe in depth a character, setting, or event in
  a story or drama, drawing on specific details in the text (e.g.,
  a character's thoughts, words, or actions).
- **SL.4.4** Report on a topic or text, tell a story, or recount
  an experience in an organized manner, using appropriate facts and
  relevant, descriptive details to support main ideas or themes;
  speak clearly at an understandable pace.

Factual notes: the anchor story and the assessment passage are
**original fiction written for this unit** — the people, dog, kite,
and library events are invented, not reports of real events. The
dialogue punctuation rules taught here (new paragraph per speaker,
comma before a closing quotation mark when a speaker tag follows,
capital letter at the start of the quoted words) follow standard
American usage for grade-4 writing; the unit teaches the common
cases only and does not cover interrupted quotations or quotations
inside quotations.
