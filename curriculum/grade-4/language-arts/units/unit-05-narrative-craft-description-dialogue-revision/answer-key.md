# Answer Key — Unit 05

Teacher only. Do not hand this file to the learner. Accept any
answer that matches the key's reasoning, even if the wording
differs. Sample open responses are models, not the only correct
answers — the requirement (a transition used for the right job, a
correctly punctuated line, a concrete picture, a closure that
answers the story's question) is what is graded.

## Lesson 01 — Situation, narrator, characters, sequence

### Guided practice

1. The narrator is **Mara**. She says "my little brother Theo",
   "I carried", "I ran", and "I shouted" — the narrator is a
   character in the story, so this is first person.
2. Situation: on a windy Saturday morning, Mara and her little
   brother Theo take their yellow kite to the park (with their
   small brown dog, Biscuit).
3. First four events in order: (1) Mara and Theo carry the kite to
   the park, Biscuit trotting beside them. (2) Theo holds the kite
   high while Mara runs with the string. (3) The wind catches the
   kite and it shoots up into the gray sky. (4) A strong gust
   pushes the kite sideways into the branches of the maple tree.
4. "On a windy Saturday morning, my little brother Theo and I
   carried our yellow kite to the park." Place: the park. Weather:
   windy.

### Independent practice

1. Sample: "On a windy Saturday morning, Mara and Theo take their
   yellow kite to the park to fly it." (Any one sentence with who,
   where, when, and what.)
2. **Mara** — holds the string, runs, pulls the kite free.
   **Theo** — holds the kite, points at the branch, counts
   "One… two… three!" **Biscuit** — trots beside them, grabs the
   loose end of the string and tugs.
3. Order: (c) 1 — Theo holds the kite while Mara runs; (a) 2 — the
   kite drops into the maple tree; (d) 3 — Biscuit grabs the loose
   end; (b) 4 — Theo and Mara pull together and free the kite.
4. Missing: a named character, a place, a situation, and events in
   order — "a kid," "somewhere," and "things" tell the reader
   nothing. Sample better first sentence: "On Saturday morning,
   Maya carried her red kite to the park with her brother Sam."

## Lesson 02 — Transitions

### Guided practice

1. Transitions in order: **First** (start), **Then** (next step),
   **Suddenly** (sudden change), **Then** (next step),
   **In the end** (closing).
2. (b) **Meanwhile** — Biscuit grabbing the string and the kite
   being stuck happen at the same time. "First" would put it at
   the start of the story and "In the end" at the close; neither
   fits.
3. Sample fix: "We pulled the string gently, slow and steady.
   Then the kite wiggled free and floated down like a big yellow
   leaf." (Transition "Then" plus the detail of pulling gently.)
4. Both "Finally" (at the very start) and "First" (in the middle)
   are wrong. Correct order: "First, Theo woke up. Then he brushed
   his teeth. Finally, he ate breakfast."

### Independent practice

1. Sample: "First, Mara carried the kite to the park. Then the
   wind caught it. Suddenly, a strong gust pushed it into the
   tree. In the end, they pulled it free." (Any correct set, with
   "then" used at most once.)
2. Sample: "First, I woke up and stretched. After that, I got
   dressed and ate breakfast with my family."
3. "Suddenly" is for unexpected things. Used three times, nothing
   feels sudden — a morning routine becomes a string of surprises.
   Rewrite: "First, I woke up. Then, I ate breakfast. Finally, I
   went to school."
4. Sample: "I lost my lunchbox on the bus. Suddenly, I saw it
   under the back seat. In the end, I carried it home like a
   prize." (Must include a sudden-change transition and an ending
   transition.)

## Lesson 03 — Dialogue

### Guided practice

1. Mara upset: "It's stuck!" I shouted. Theo thinking clearly:
   "Don't pull too hard or the string will snap." (Also accepted:
   "Let's pull together, slow and steady.")
2. Mara said, "Biscuit, drop it!"
3. Mara feels worried or upset — she frowns, holds the tangled
   string in both hands, and looks up at the tree. Theo feels
   excited or cheerful — he smiles and points up at the kite.
4. Sample: Theo shouted, "Look! The kite is caught on the highest
   branch!" (Any speaker tag other than "said", correctly
   punctuated.)

### Independent practice

1. Sample 4-line dialogue (new paragraph per speaker; one
   exclamation, one question):
   "Oh no!" Mara cried. "The kite is stuck!"
   "Don't worry," Theo said. "We can pull it down."
   "How?" Mara asked.
   "Slow and steady," Theo said. "On three!"
2. (a) "Look out!" shouted Theo. "The string is tangling!"
   (b) Mara said, "I have an idea."
3. The three lines are in one paragraph, so the reader cannot tell
   who is speaking when. Correct version, one speaker per
   paragraph:
   "We should pull it," Mara said.
   "No, wait," Theo said.
   "It will snap," Mara said.
4. "Let's pull together, slow and steady" shows Theo is calm, has
   a plan, and leads — he does not panic the way Mara does; he
   solves the problem.

## Lesson 04 — Description

### Guided practice

1. Sight: the bright yellow kite caught in the tree (or the orange
   leaves on the grass). Sound: leaves rattling in the wind (or
   Biscuit barking). Touch: the cold wind on your face (or the
   rough string in your hands). (Any one detail per sense.)
2. (b) is concrete — it names the color (bright yellow), the place
   (the high branch), and the detail (its string dangling).
   (a) is vague — "up there" could be anywhere.
3. Words: "Don't pull too hard or the string will snap" — Theo
   thinks before he acts. Actions: he points at the branch and
   counts "One… two… three!" — he leads the plan.
4. Sample: "The small brown dog wagged his tail and licked my
   hand, his ears flopping."

### Independent practice

1. Sample: "A bright yellow kite hangs in the branches of a big
   tree with orange leaves. A girl in a red jacket holds a tangled
   ball of string while a small brown dog tugs the loose end. A
   boy in a blue cap points up at the kite and smiles as leaves
   rustle around them." (Concrete colors, shapes, and numbers;
   sight and hearing.)
2. Concrete: crunchy, red, squeaky, wooden. Vague: nice, things,
   big, stuff. Sample replacements: nice → "sweet-smelling";
   things → name them ("the kite, the string, and the bench");
   big → "as tall as the tree"; stuff → name it ("the kite string
   and the picnic basket").
3. "Very" only makes a vague word louder — it adds no picture.
   Sample rewrites: "Mara's shoulders slumped and her eyes filled
   with tears." / "Orange leaves covered the grass like a blanket."
4. Sample: "Lena's hands shook as she opened the letter. Her
   heart thumped so loudly she was sure everyone could hear it."
   (No "nervous", "scared", or "worried".)

## Lesson 05 — Endings and revision

### Guided practice

1. (b) is a closure — it tells what changed (they flew the kite
   again) and what Mara learned. (a) only stops the story.
2. Sample moves: **Add** a sensory detail — "the garden smelled
   of wet soil after the rain" or "bees buzzed around the
   flowers." **Swap** a vague word — "nice" → "green and buzzing
   with bees."
3. What changed: they flew the kite again, lower this time, away
   from the trees. What Mara learned: a kite needs open sky and a
   steady wind — and two pullers are better than one.
4. "Your story is good" is not useful: it does not say what is
   good or what to change. More useful: "Your beginning is clear,
   but I can't picture the park — add one thing you could see or
   hear there."

### Independent practice

1. Sample closure: "In the end, Lena told her teacher, and
   together they found the puppy's owner. Lena learned that a lost
   puppy needs a grown-up's help — and that sandwiches make good
   puppy food." (Must tell what changed or what was learned.)
2. Sample revised paragraph with labeled moves: "The beach was hot
   and bright, and the waves crashed loud on the sand. We built a
   tall sandcastle with shells for windows. Then we left as the
   sun went down." Moves: **Add** (sensory detail — waves
   crashing), **Swap** ("nice" → "hot and bright"; "did stuff" →
   "built a tall sandcastle"), **Remove** ("It was fun" — cut; the
   details show it).
3. Fixing spelling is editing — it changes the spelling, not the
   story. A revision move still needed: e.g., add a sensory
   detail, swap a vague word, or move an event into the right
   order.
4. Sample peer-conference note — strength: "The ending answers
   the story's own question: they get the kite back and fly it
   again." Suggestion: "Add one sensory detail to the middle — for
   example, what the wind sounded like when the gust hit." (Any
   specific strength + specific suggestion.)

## Practice

### Set A

1. Situation: on Friday afternoon, Jamal and his grandmother plant
   tomato seeds in pots on the porch.
2. Third person — the narrator says "Jamal" and "his
   grandmother", not "I".
3. Order: (c) 1 — Mia painted a picture of her house; (b) 2 — Mia
   knocked over the blue paint; (a) 3 — Mia wiped the paint off
   the table; (d) 4 — Mia cleaned her brush.
4. Sample: "On a stormy night, Ben ran into the library with his
   wet coat dripping." (Must name who, where, when, and what.)
5. (b) is stronger — it names a character (Rosa), a place (her
   aunt's garden), a time (the first day of summer), and a specific
   event (a tiny green door in the fence). (a) has no names, no
   place, and no event.
6. First person — the narrator says "I".
7. The transitions are in the wrong order: "Then" opens the story,
   "First" sits in the middle, and the events are scrambled.
   Correct order: "First, we opened the gate. Then the dog ran
   away. In the end, we called his name."
8. Sample: "On Tuesday morning, Leo opened his backpack and found
   a tiny green frog sitting on his math book. He stared at it,
   and the frog stared back." (Situation + named character.)

### Set B

1. "First we packed our bags. Then we walked to the bus stop.
   After that we waited an hour. Finally the bus came at last."
2. (b) Suddenly — dark clouds covering a sunny sky is unexpected.
   "First" belongs at a start and "Finally" at an end.
3. Sample: "First, we built a tall tower out of wooden blocks.
   Suddenly, it wobbled and fell down with a crash. Then we built
   it again, stronger this time, with a wider base."
4. (b) — "finally" comes after three tries, at the end of a
   sequence. (a) uses "finally" at the very start.
5. Sample: "First, I spread peanut butter on the bread. Then I
   added banana slices. Finally, I cut the sandwich in half."
6. The order words fight the event order: "Later" opens the story
   and "First" sits in the middle. Rewrite: "First, I woke up.
   Then, I ate breakfast. Later, I went to school."
7. first → start; meanwhile → at the same time; suddenly →
   unexpected change; in the end → closing.
8. Sample: "I left my lunchbox on the bus. Suddenly, I saw it
   under the back seat. In the end, I carried it home and washed
   it." (Must include a sudden-change transition and an ending
   transition.)

### Set C

1. Sam said, "I found your mitten."
2. Ana whispered, "Look! It's hiding under the bench."
3. "I lost my kite," Mara said.
   "I can help," Theo said.
4. Sample (new paragraph per speaker; one exclamation, one
   question):
   "Oh no!" Mara cried. "The kite is stuck!"
   "Don't worry," Theo said. "We can pull it down."
   "How?" Mara asked.
   "Slow and steady," Theo said. "On three!"
5. (b) is correct. (a) has a period before the closing quotation
   mark instead of a comma ("Let's go." said Mara — the comma
   belongs inside the quotes when a speaker tag follows). (c) has
   no quotation marks and no capital letter.
6. Mia is nervous or unsure — she whispers and looks at the floor
   instead of speaking up.
7. (b) shouted — "We won the game!" is excited and loud;
   whispering or murmuring does not match.
8. The exact words spoken have no quotation marks. Correct: Mara
   said, "The kite is stuck."

### Set D

1. Concrete: crunchy, red, squeaky, wooden. Vague: nice, things,
   big, stuff.
2. (a) Sample: "The tomato soup was hot and salty, and it warmed
   my hands through the bowl." (b) Sample: "The room was as wide
   as our classroom, with tall windows along one wall."
3. (a) smell (b) hearing (c) touch (d) taste (e) sight.
4. Sample: "A bright yellow kite hangs in the branches of a big
   tree with orange leaves. A girl in a red jacket holds a tangled
   ball of string while a small brown dog tugs the loose end. A
   boy in a blue cap points up at the kite and smiles as leaves
   rustle around them." (Concrete colors, shapes, and numbers;
   sight and hearing.)
5. Sample: "Lena jumped up and down, clapping her hands. 'We did
   it!' she shouted, her eyes wide." (No "excited" or "happy".)
6. Sample: "Theo's words — 'Don't pull too hard or the string
   will snap' — show he thinks before he acts. His actions —
   pointing at the branch and counting 'One… two… three!' — show
   he leads the plan."
7. "Very" only makes vague words louder; "big" and "nice" still
   paint no picture. Rewrite: "The shaggy brown dog wagged his
   tail and licked my hand."
8. (a) "She wore a red dress with white flowers." (b) "He ate two
   warm rolls with butter." (c) "It was a trip with sandy beaches
   and a lighthouse we could climb."

### Set E

1. Sample closure: "In the end, Lena told her teacher, and
   together they found the puppy's owner. Lena learned that a lost
   puppy needs a grown-up's help — and that sandwiches make good
   puppy food." (Must tell what changed or what was learned.)
2. Sample: "The beach was hot and bright, and the waves crashed

   windows. Then we left as the sun went down." Moves: **Add**
   (sensory detail — waves crashing), **Swap** ("nice" → "hot and
   bright"; "did stuff" → "built a tall sandcastle"),
   **Remove** ("It was fun" — cut; the details show it).
3. (a) editing (b) revising (c) revising (d) editing.
4. Sample — strength: "The events are in a clear order — park,
   play, home." Suggestion: "Add one sensory detail (what did you
   play? what did the park sound or look like?) or one line of

5. (a) Add (b) Remove (c) Move (d) Swap.
6. Sample closure: "In the end, we flew the kite again, lower this
   time, away from the trees. I learned that two pullers are better
   than one." (Any 2-sentence closure telling what changed or what
   was learned.)
7. Fixing spelling is editing — it changes the spelling, not the
   story. A revision move still needed: e.g., add a sensory detail,

8. Sample (starter 2): "On the last day of school, the classroom

## Unit assessment (30 points)

1. (2 points) Third person — the narrator calls her "Ana" and
   "she", never "I". (1 point for third person, 1 for the
   evidence.)
2. (2 points) (a) 1, (b) 2, (c) 3, (d) 4.
3. (2 points) Any two with the right job: "As they walked"
   (meanwhile — at the same time); "Just then" (sudden change);
   "When the rain stopped" (later — toward the ending). "On a
   rainy Tuesday afternoon" (sets the time) is also accepted.
4. (3 points) Sample: "Don't worry," Ms. Patel said softly. "The
   library is the safest place in a storm." — it shows she is
   kind, calm, and reassuring. (Any quoted line + a correct
   inference about Ms. Patel.)
5. (2 points) Any two: "rain pattering on the roof" (hearing);
   "warm, dusty smell of old books" (smell); "shoes squeaked"
   (hearing); "orange lava bubbling like soup" (sight); "smelled
   like new paper" (smell).
6. (2 points) Sample: "The thick blue book had photographs of
   real eruptions." (Any rewrite naming something specific about
   the book.)
7. (3 points) Sample: her words — "Can you help me find a book
   about volcanoes?" — show she is polite and asks for help when
   she needs it. Her actions — hugging the book to her chest and
   sitting down to read until she forgets the storm — show she
   loves reading and is curious. (1 point per detail, 1 for the
   inference.)
8. (2 points) Closure: "She walked home under a pale blue sky,
   already planning the first sentence of her report." It tells
   the reader the storm is over, Ana has the book she needed, and
   she is ready to write her report — the story's question is
   answered.
9. (2 points) (a) "Can you help me?" asked Ana. (b) "Follow me,"
   said Ms. Patel.
10. (3 points) Sample revision: "The library was quiet except for
    the rain pattering on the roof. 'Can I sit by the window?' Ana
    asked. She opened her book and forgot all about the storm."
    (1 point sensory detail, 1 point dialogue, 1 point correct
    punctuation.)
11. (4 points — 1 per element) Sample paragraph:
    "On the last day of school, the classroom hamster, Pickles,
    squeezed out of his cage. First, he scampered across the
    reading corner. 'There he goes!' shouted Maya, pointing under
    the teacher's desk. The class heard tiny feet pattering on the
    floor. Finally, Maya lured him out with a sunflower seed. In
    the end, Pickles was back in his cage, and Maya learned that
    hamsters love snacks more than freedom."
    (Transition: First / Finally / In the end. Dialogue: "There he
    goes!" Sensory detail: tiny feet pattering. Closure: the last
    sentence.)
12. (3 points) Any named move with a matching reason. Sample:
    "Swap — change 'tiny feet pattering' to 'tiny feet tapping
    like rain on a window' for a clearer sound picture."

## Project — scoring notes

Score with the rubric in [project.md](project.md). Accept any
story that meets the listed requirements; the topic is the
learner's choice. Common borderline calls:

- **Dialogue row:** an exchange counts only if it has 2 or more
  lines and shows a response (a feeling, a decision, a change of
  mind). "Hello," said Sam. "Hi," said Ana. is greeting, not a
  response — score 3, not 5.
- **Description row:** a sensory detail counts only if it names a
  sense the reader can feel (a sound, a smell, a texture). "It
  was very nice" is not a sensory detail, however many times it
  appears.
- **Revision row:** the moves must be visible in the final copy
  (a new detail, a removed repeat, a moved sentence, a swapped
  word) — a note that says "I revised" without a visible change
  scores 1.
