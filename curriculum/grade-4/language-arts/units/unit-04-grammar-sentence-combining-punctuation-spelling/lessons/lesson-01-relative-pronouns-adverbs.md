# Lesson 01 — Relative Pronouns and Relative Adverbs

**Goal:** I can join two sentences with a relative pronoun or relative
adverb and explain what the new clause describes.
**Time:** about 30–35 minutes · **Materials:** notebook, pencil
**Standard:** L.4.1.a

## Readiness check

Say or point: which word names a person — *teacher, quickly, happy*?
Which word names a place — *library, loudly, tomorrow*? If either is
hard, name two more people and two more places together first.

## Teach

A **clause** is a group of words with a subject and a verb. A
**relative clause** is a clause that describes a noun — it tells more
about a person, a thing, a place, a time, or a reason. It starts with a
**relative pronoun** or a **relative adverb**, and it cannot stand
alone as a sentence: it needs the rest of the sentence to make sense.

**Relative pronouns** — use them for people and things:

| Word | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *who* | a person doing the action | the student **who** won the race |
| *whom* | a person receiving the action | the teacher **whom** we thanked |
| *whose* | something belonging to a person or thing | the dog **whose** ball rolled away |
| *which* | a thing or an animal | the kite **which** flew highest |
| *that* | a person or a thing | the book **that** you lent me |

**Relative adverbs** — use them for place, time, and reason:

| Word | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *where* | a place | the park **where** we fly kites |
| *when* | a time | the morning **when** we planted seeds |
| *why* | a reason | the reason **why** we left early |

The test for the right word is simple: ask what the clause describes.
A person doing something → *who*. A place → *where*. A time → *when*.
A reason → *why*. Ownership → *whose*. If you can swap in *that*
for a thing, *that* works too.

## Worked example 1 — joining with *who*

Two short sentences:

> Maya is a student. Maya won the spelling bee.

The second sentence tells more about *Maya*, a person doing the
action. Join them with *who* and drop the repeated name:

> Maya is the student **who won the spelling bee**.

Check: *who won the spelling bee* has a subject (*who*) and a verb
(*won*), but it is not a complete sentence on its own — try saying it
alone and it hangs in the air. That is how you know it is a relative
clause, not a second sentence.

## Worked example 2 — joining with *where* and *whose*

Two short sentences:

> This is the park. We fly kites in the park.

The second sentence tells more about *the park*, a place. Join them
with *where*:

> This is the park **where we fly kites**.

Now ownership:

> Jonah has a dog. The dog's ball rolled away.

The second sentence tells whose ball it is. Join them with *whose*:

> Jonah has a dog **whose ball rolled away**.

A common error: using *who* for a place or a time. "The park who we
visit" is wrong — a park is a place, so the word is *where*:
"The park **where** we visit" is still awkward; the natural sentence
is "The park **where** we play." Always read the joined sentence
aloud and ask: does the clause describe a person, a thing, a place, a
time, or a reason?

## Guided practice

Work together; the adult may read the choices aloud.

1. Join: "Ms. Reed is the teacher. Ms. Reed reads to us every Friday."
   Choose *who, which,* or *where*.
2. Join: "That is the house. We lived in the house last year." Choose
   *who, where,* or *when*.
3. Join: "This is the day. We planted the garden on that day." Choose
   *where, when,* or *why*.
4. Join: "Sam is the boy. We gave the ball to Sam." Choose *who, whom,*
   or *whose*. (Hint: Sam receives the ball.)
5. Join: "That is the reason. We stayed inside because of the rain."
   Choose *when, why,* or *where*.
6. Fix the error: "The library who has new books is on Main Street."
   Name the right word and rewrite the sentence.

## Independent practice

1. Fill in the blank: "The girl ___ rides the red bicycle is my
   friend." (*who / where / when*)
2. Fill in the blank: "Do you remember the afternoon ___ it rained
   during the picnic?" (*who / when / why*)
3. Fill in the blank: "That is the shop ___ sells fresh bread."
   (*which / where / whose*)
4. Combine: "Lena found a shell. The shell was pink and smooth." Use
   *that* or *which*.
5. Combine: "This is the friend. I lent my book to this friend." Use
   *whom*.
6. Combine: "Mr. Alvarez is our neighbor. His garden grows tomatoes."
   Use *whose*.

## Applied task

Write three sentences of your own: one about a person (use *who* or
*whom*), one about a place (use *where*), and one about a time or a
reason (use *when* or *why*). Read each one aloud and name what its
clause describes: person, place, time, or reason.

## Exit check

Complete the sentence and explain your choice in one sentence:

> "The library ___ we read every Friday is quiet."

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** cover the relative word and ask, "Person, thing, place,
  time, or reason?" before choosing. Dictation is fine: say the
  sentence, then the adult scribes it and the learner reads it back.
- **Extension:** write a sentence with two relative clauses, for
  example: "Maya is the student who won the spelling bee in the
  library where we read every Friday." Then check each clause against
  the person/place test.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: learners use *who* for everything, or use
*that* for a place ("the park that we play"). Reteach with the
person/place/time/reason question, not with memorizing a list. Full
solutions are in the [answer key](../answer-key.md) under Lesson 01.
