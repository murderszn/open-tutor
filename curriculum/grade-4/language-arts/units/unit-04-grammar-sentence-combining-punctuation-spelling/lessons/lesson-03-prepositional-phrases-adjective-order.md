# Lesson 03 — Prepositional Phrases and Adjective Order

**Goal:** I can add a prepositional phrase that tells where, when, or
how, and I can put adjectives in conventional order before a noun.
**Time:** about 30–35 minutes · **Materials:** notebook, pencil
**Standards:** L.4.1.e, L.4.1.d

## Readiness check

Name three prepositions: words that show place or time, like *in, on,
under, after, before, with*. Then finish: "The cat sat ___ the rug."
Any preposition that makes sense is a correct start.

## Teach

### Prepositional phrases

A **preposition** is a word that shows a relationship — usually where,
when, or how. A **prepositional phrase** is the preposition plus the
noun (or pronoun) that follows it, called its object, plus any words
describing that object. A phrase has no subject and verb of its own,
so it cannot be a sentence by itself.

| Question it answers | Prepositions | Example phrase |
|---|---|---|
| Where? | *in, on, under, beside, across, behind* | **under the old oak tree** |
| When? | *after, before, during, at* | **after lunch** |
| How? | *with, without, by* | **with great care** |

So: "Maya read **under the old oak tree** **after lunch**." The first
phrase tells where; the second tells when. Add a phrase to a plain
sentence and the picture sharpens: "The dog ran" becomes "The brown
dog ran **across the green grass** **after lunch**."

### Adjective order

When more than one adjective comes before a noun, English puts them
in a conventional order. Grade 4 uses this shortened order:

**number or opinion → size → age → color → material → noun**

- Number or opinion first: *two, three, beautiful, favorite*
- Then size: *small, big, tiny, huge*
- Then age: *old, new, young*
- Then color: *red, blue, yellow*
- Then material: *wooden, glass, leather*

Examples: *a small red bag* (size, then color — never *a red small
bag*); *two small red balls* (number, size, color); *the beautiful
old wooden bench* (opinion, age, material); *three tiny yellow
flowers* (number, size, color).

A common error is putting color before size: "the red small bag"
sounds wrong to a reader even before they can say why. The order test
is: opinion, size, age, color, material — say the adjectives in that
order and the sentence sounds natural.

## Worked example 1 — adding phrases

Plain sentence: "The dog ran."

Step 1 — add *where*: "The dog ran **across the grass**."
Step 2 — add *when*: "The dog ran **across the grass** **after
lunch**."
Step 3 — add *how* and adjectives: "The **brown** dog ran **across
the green grass** **after lunch** **with great speed**."

Each phrase answers one question — where, when, or how — and the
sentence stays complete because the subject (*dog*) and verb (*ran*)
are still there. A phrase alone, like "Across the grass after lunch,"
is not a sentence: it has no subject and no verb.

## Worked example 2 — fixing adjective order

Wrong order: "Maya carried a red small bag and sat on a wooden old
beautiful bench."

Fix it with the order test:

- *red small bag* → size before color → **small red bag**.
- *wooden old beautiful bench* → opinion, then age, then material →
  **beautiful old wooden bench**.

Fixed sentence: "Maya carried a **small red bag** and sat on the
**beautiful old wooden bench**." Read both versions aloud: the fixed
one sounds like the English a reader expects.

## Guided practice

Work together.

1. Add a *where* phrase to: "Jonah read his book."
2. Add a *when* phrase to: "We planted seeds."
3. Name the object of the preposition in: "The kite flew over the
   tall trees." (Hint: the noun right after *over*.)
4. Reorder: "Lena wore a blue tiny dress." Explain the order you used.
5. Reorder: "He found an old rusty red bicycle." (Hint: age before
   color; *rusty* is an opinion-like describing word that comes
   before age here — accept "rusty old red bicycle" or "old rusty
   red bicycle" and discuss why both sound natural; the key point is
   that color comes last before the noun.)
6. Fix the fragment: "Under the old oak tree." Add a subject and a
   verb to make it a sentence.

## Independent practice

1. Add a *where* phrase to: "The ducks swam."
2. Add a *how* phrase to: "Sam carried the box." (Use *with*.)
3. Underline the prepositional phrase(s) in: "After school, Maya
   walked to the library with her brother."
4. Reorder: "She bought three red tiny apples."
5. Reorder: "They sat on a wooden new bench."
6. Write one sentence with two adjectives in correct order and one
   prepositional phrase. Underline the phrase and circle the
   adjectives.

## Applied task

Describe the old wooden bench in a park in two sentences: one
sentence must contain a prepositional phrase telling where, and the
other must contain three adjectives in conventional order before a
noun. Read both sentences aloud and check the adjective order with
the opinion → size → age → color → material test.

## Exit check

Fix this sentence, then explain the fix in one sentence:

> "The dog chased a yellow small ball across the grass."

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** use the question cards *Where? When? How?* — point to
  the card before adding a phrase. For adjective order, chant the
  short chain: "size, age, color" and check color last.
- **Extension:** write a sentence with two prepositional phrases
  answering two different questions, then move one phrase to the
  front of the sentence ("After lunch, the brown dog ran across the
  green grass.") and explain that the meaning stays the same.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: treating a prepositional phrase as a sentence
("Under the tree."), and putting color before size. Reteach with the
subject-and-verb test for fragments and the order chant. Note on
guided item 5: *rusty* is an evaluative adjective; the acceptable
answers are recorded in the key with reasoning. Full solutions are in
the [answer key](../answer-key.md) under Lesson 03.
