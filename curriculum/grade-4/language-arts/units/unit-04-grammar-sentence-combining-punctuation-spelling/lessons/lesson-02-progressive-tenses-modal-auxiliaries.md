# Lesson 02 — Progressive Verb Tenses and Modal Auxiliaries

**Goal:** I can write a sentence in the past, present, and future
progressive tenses, and I can choose *can, may,* or *must* to show
ability, permission, or necessity.
**Time:** about 30–35 minutes · **Materials:** notebook, pencil
**Standards:** L.4.1.b, L.4.1.c

## Readiness check

Act out or name: what are you doing right now? What did you do
yesterday at this time? What will you do tomorrow at this time? Each
answer needs a verb with *-ing* to be a progressive answer — for
example, "I am reading," not just "reading."

## Teach

### Progressive tenses: actions in progress

A **progressive tense** shows an action in progress at a time. It is
made with a form of *be* plus a verb ending in *-ing*:

| Tense | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Past progressive | *was / were* + verb-*ing* | I **was walking** home at 5 o'clock. |
| Present progressive | *am / is / are* + verb-*ing* | I **am walking** home right now. |
| Future progressive | *will be* + verb-*ing* | I **will be walking** home at 5 o'clock tomorrow. |

The time words are the clue: *yesterday, last night, at 5 o'clock
yesterday* → past; *right now, today, at this moment* → present;
*tomorrow, next week, at noon tomorrow* → future. The verb form must
match its time: "Yesterday I am walking" mixes a past time with a
present verb, and a reader feels the bump.

### Modal auxiliaries: can, may, must

A **modal auxiliary** is a helping verb that comes before the main
verb and changes what the sentence means. Grade 4 uses three:

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *can* | ability — what someone is able to do | Maya **can** swim across the pool. |
| *may* | permission or possibility — what is allowed, or what might happen | You **may** borrow my book. / It **may** rain later. |
| *must* | necessity or obligation — what has to happen | You **must** wear a helmet when you ride. |

After a modal, the main verb stays in its plain form: "She can
swims" is wrong; "She can **swim**" is right. And *can* is not the
same as *may*: "Can I go?" asks whether you are able; "May I go?"
asks whether you have permission. In polite requests, *may* is the
careful choice.

## Worked example 1 — one action, three tenses

Base action: Maya reads a book.

- Past progressive (last night at 7 o'clock): "At 7 o'clock last
  night, Maya **was reading** her book."
- Present progressive (right now): "Right now, Maya **is reading**
  her book."
- Future progressive (tomorrow at noon): "Tomorrow at noon, Maya
  **will be reading** her book."

Each sentence keeps the same action (*reading*) and changes only the
*be* form and the time words. Read them in order and the time moves:
last night → now → tomorrow.

## Worked example 2 — choosing a modal

Situation: Jonah wants to borrow a kite, and the rule is that every
rider wears a helmet.

- Ability: "Jonah **can** fly a kite." (He knows how.)
- Permission: "Jonah **may** borrow my kite." (He is allowed to.)
- Necessity: "Jonah **must** wear a helmet when he rides his bike."
  (The rule requires it.)

A common error: "Jonah must borrow my kite" when you mean permission.
*Must* turns a favor into a rule. Ask: "Is this able, allowed, or
required?" Ability → *can*; allowed → *may*; required → *must*.

## Guided practice

Work together; the adult may read the sentences aloud.

1. Rewrite in the past progressive: "Right now, Sam is planting
   seeds." (Time: yesterday afternoon.)
2. Rewrite in the future progressive: "Right now, Sam is planting
   seeds." (Time: tomorrow morning.)
3. Choose the modal: "You ___ wash your hands before lunch." (The
   school rule requires it.)
4. Choose the modal: "You ___ sit with us if you like." (Permission.)
5. Choose the modal: "Lena ___ play the piano beautifully." (Ability.)
6. Fix the error: "Yesterday, we are walking to the library." Name
   what is wrong and rewrite the sentence.

## Independent practice

1. Write the present progressive of "They play soccer" for "right
   now."
2. Write the past progressive of "They play soccer" for "yesterday at
   4 o'clock."
3. Write the future progressive of "They play soccer" for "tomorrow
   at 4 o'clock."
4. Fill in the blank with *can, may,* or *must*: "You ___ return the
   book by Friday." (The library rule requires it.)
5. Fill in the blank with *can, may,* or *must*: "___ I use your
   pencil, please?" (Asking permission politely.)
6. Fill in the blank with *can, may,* or *must*: "My little brother
   ___ count to one hundred." (Ability.)

## Applied task

Write a three-sentence note about tomorrow's park trip: one sentence
in the future progressive ("Tomorrow at noon, we will be …"), one
sentence with *may* (permission or possibility), and one sentence
with *must* (a rule for the trip). Read the note aloud and check that
every modal is followed by a plain verb.

## Exit check

Rewrite this sentence twice: "Maya is riding her bicycle."

1. As a past-progressive sentence (yesterday afternoon).
2. As a future-progressive sentence (tomorrow morning).

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** use a time-word card for each tense (*yesterday /
  right now / tomorrow*) and match the card to the *be* form before
  writing. Act out the action while saying the sentence.
- **Extension:** explain the difference between "It can rain later"
  (ability is wrong here — rain has no ability) and "It may rain
  later" (possibility). Rewrite "We can visit the park" as a
  permission sentence and as a necessity sentence, and explain how
  the meaning changes.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: matching the wrong *be* form to the time
("Yesterday I is walking"), and adding *-s* or *-ing* after a modal
("She can swims"). Reteach with the time-word test and the plain-verb
rule. Full solutions are in the [answer key](../answer-key.md) under
Lesson 02.
