# Lesson 05 — Capitalization and Direct-Speech Punctuation

**Goal:** I can capitalize correctly and punctuate direct speech with
commas and quotation marks.
**Time:** about 30–35 minutes · **Materials:** notebook, pencil
**Standards:** L.4.2.a, L.4.2.b

## Readiness check

Write your first name and the name of your town. Which letters are
capital, and why? Names of people and places are proper nouns — they
always start with a capital letter, wherever they appear in a
sentence.

## Teach

### Capitalization

Capitalize:

- The first word of every sentence.
- The pronoun *I*, wherever it appears.
- Names of people and pets: *Maya, Jonah, Ms. Reed*.
- Names of specific places: *Maple Park, Main Street, Chicago*.
- Days, months, and holidays: *Friday, October, Halloween*.
- Titles before a name: *Ms. Reed, Mr. Alvarez, Dr. Patel*.
- The first word inside quotation marks when it starts a quoted
  sentence (see below).

Do not capitalize ordinary nouns just because they feel important:
*school, park, dog, book* stay lowercase unless they are part of a
specific name.

### Direct speech: quotation marks and commas

**Direct speech** is a speaker's exact words. Put quotation marks
around exactly those words — not around the dialogue tag (the part
that says who spoke).

There are two main patterns. Watch where the comma goes:

**Pattern A — tag first.** A comma comes after the tag, before the
opening quotation mark, and the quoted sentence starts with a capital:

> Maya said, **"**The kite is stuck in the tree.**"**

**Pattern B — speech first.** The comma (or question mark, or
exclamation mark) goes **inside** the closing quotation mark, and the
tag after it stays lowercase unless it is a name:

> **"**The kite is stuck in the tree**,** Maya said.
> **"**Can you help me**?** Jonah asked.

A quoted question or exclamation keeps its own mark inside the quotes:
"Can you help me?" — never "Can you help me"? with the mark outside.
And a period at the end of quoted speech becomes a comma when a tag
follows: "The kite is stuck in the tree," Maya said — not "…tree.",
Maya said.

The same rules mark a **quotation from a text**: "The author wrote,
'The garden grew all summer.'" In this unit, short quotations use
double quotation marks, as in dialogue.

## Worked example 1 — punctuating a conversation

Unpunctuated draft:

> maya said the kite is flying high jonah shouted look out

Step 1 — capitalize names and sentence starts: *Maya, Jonah*.
Step 2 — put quotation marks around the exact words, and commas
where the patterns require them:

> Maya said, "The kite is flying high!"
> "Look out!" Jonah shouted.

Check each line: the quoted sentence starts with a capital; the
exclamation mark sits inside the closing quotation mark; the tag
(*Jonah shouted*) is outside the quotes.

## Worked example 2 — fixing capitalization

Wrong: "on friday, maya and jonah went to maple park with ms. reed."

Fixes, one by one: *On* (first word) → capital *O*; *friday* (a day)
→ *Friday*; *maya, jonah* (names) → *Maya, Jonah*; *maple park* (a
specific place) → *Maple Park*; *ms. reed* (a title before a name)
→ *Ms. Reed*.

Fixed: "On Friday, Maya and Jonah went to Maple Park with Ms. Reed."
A common error is capitalizing *park* alone ("the Park") — only the
full specific name, *Maple Park*, takes capitals.

## Guided practice

Work together.

1. Punctuate (tag first): maya whispered the ducks are swimming
2. Punctuate (speech first): the ball is rolling away sam cried
3. Punctuate a question (speech first): will you help me jonah asked
4. Fix the capitals: "in october, my friend lena visited chicago."
5. Fix the capitals: "ms. reed said, open your books to page ten."
   (Hint: the quoted sentence also starts with a capital.)
6. Find the error: '"The kite is high". Maya said.' Explain what is
   wrong and rewrite it.

## Independent practice

1. Punctuate (tag first): Jonah said the wind is perfect today
2. Punctuate (speech first): I can see two ducks Maya said
3. Punctuate (speech first, question): Are we going home now Lena
   asked
4. Fix the capitals: "on tuesday, mr. alvarez took his dog to the
   park."
5. Write one dialogue line of your own in each pattern (tag first,
   then speech first) about the park picture from Lesson 04.
6. Quote from a text: punctuate this quotation — the author wrote
   the garden grew all summer (tag first).

## Applied task

Write a four-line conversation between two friends at the park. Use
both patterns at least once, include one question, and capitalize
every name, place, day, and sentence start correctly. Read it aloud
with a partner (or with the adult reading one part) and check that
every quoted sentence starts with a capital and ends with its mark
inside the quotes.

## Exit check

Punctuate this line and explain the comma in one sentence:

> jonah asked can we fly the kite again

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** use two colors or underlines: underline the exact
  spoken words first, then add quotation marks around only the
  underlined part. Say the tag in a normal voice and the quoted words
  in a "speaking" voice to feel the boundary.
- **Extension:** rewrite a speech-first line as a tag-first line
  ("'The kite is stuck,' Maya said" → 'Maya said, "The kite is
  stuck."') and explain what changed: the comma moves from inside
  the closing quote to after the tag, and the tag's verb stays
  lowercase in the speech-first version.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: putting the comma or period outside the
closing quotation mark; capitalizing the tag after a speech-first
comma ("…tree," Maya Said); capitalizing common nouns. Reteach with
the underline-the-exact-words routine. Full solutions are in the
[answer key](../answer-key.md) under Lesson 05.
