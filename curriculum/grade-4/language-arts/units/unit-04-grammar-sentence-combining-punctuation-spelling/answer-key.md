# Answer Key — Unit 04 (Teacher Only)

Do not hand this key to the learner. Accept any answer that matches
the reasoning below, even if the wording differs. Where a sample
sentence is given for an open task, any sentence meeting the stated
conditions earns full credit.

## Lesson 01 — Relative pronouns and adverbs

**Guided:** 1. "Ms. Reed is the teacher who reads to us every Friday."
2. "That is the house where we lived last year." 3. "This is the day
when we planted the garden." 4. "Sam is the boy whom we gave the ball
to." (Also acceptable: "Sam is the boy to whom we gave the ball.")
5. "That is the reason why we stayed inside." 6. *who* is wrong for a
thing; use *which* or *that*: "The library that has new books is on
Main Street."

**Independent:** 1. *who* 2. *when* 3. *which* 4. "Lena found a shell
that was pink and smooth." (or *which*) 5. "This is the friend whom
I lent my book to." 6. "Mr. Alvarez is our neighbor whose garden
grows tomatoes."

**Applied (sample; any correct set earns credit):** "Jonah is the
friend who lent me his kite." (person) / "Maple Park is the place
where we fly kites." (place) / "That was the morning when we planted
seeds." (time)

**Exit:** "The library **where** we read every Friday is quiet." —
*where* because a library is a place.

## Lesson 02 — Progressive tenses and modal auxiliaries

**Guided:** 1. "Yesterday afternoon, Sam was planting seeds."
2. "Tomorrow morning, Sam will be planting seeds." 3. *must*
4. *may* 5. *can* 6. Wrong *be* form for the time: "Yesterday, we
were walking to the library."

**Independent:** 1. "They are playing soccer right now." 2. "Yesterday
at 4 o'clock, they were playing soccer." 3. "Tomorrow at 4 o'clock,
they will be playing soccer." 4. *must* 5. *May* 6. *can*

**Applied (sample):** "Tomorrow at noon, we will be eating lunch in
the park. We may fly kites if the wind is strong. We must stay on the
paths when other people are walking." Each modal is followed by a
plain verb (*fly, stay*).

**Exit:** 1. "Yesterday afternoon, Maya was riding her bicycle."
2. "Tomorrow morning, Maya will be riding her bicycle."

## Lesson 03 — Prepositional phrases and adjective order

**Guided:** 1. Sample: "Jonah read his book under the old oak tree."
(Any sensible *where* phrase earns credit.) 2. Sample: "We planted
seeds after lunch." (Any sensible *when* phrase earns credit.)
3. *trees* — the noun after *over*. 4. "Lena wore a tiny blue dress."
(Size *tiny* before color *blue*.) 5. Accept "He found a rusty old
red bicycle" or "He found an old rusty red bicycle": *rusty* is an
evaluative word that may come before age; the key point is that color
(*red*) comes last, right before the noun. 6. Sample: "We sat under
the old oak tree." (Any sentence with a subject and a verb earns
credit.)

**Independent:** 1. Sample: "The ducks swam in the pond." 2. Sample:
"Sam carried the box with great care." 3. Three phrases: *After
school, to the library, with her brother*. 4. "She bought three tiny
red apples." 5. "They sat on a new wooden bench." (Age *new* before
material *wooden*.) 6. Sample: "Maya carried two small red apples in
her basket." Phrases: *in her basket*; adjectives: *two, small, red*
in number → size → color order.

**Applied (sample):** "The beautiful old wooden bench stands under
the tall oak tree. Three tiny yellow flowers grow beside the bench."
Check: *beautiful* (opinion), *old* (age), *wooden* (material);
*three* (number), *tiny* (size), *yellow* (color); *under the tall
oak tree* and *beside the bench* are prepositional phrases.

**Exit:** "The dog chased a **small yellow** ball across the grass."
Size (*small*) comes before color (*yellow*).

## Lesson 04 — Fragments, run-ons, combining, confused words

**Guided:** 1. Fragment — no subject or verb. Sample fix: "After
lunch, we played in the park." 2. Complete — subject *ducks*, verb
*swam*. 3. Split: "Maya read her book. Jonah flew his kite."
Combined: "Maya read her book, and Jonah flew his kite." 4. *or*:
"We can walk to the park, or we can ride our bikes." 5. "We are
going **to** the library, and Sam is coming, **too**." 6. "**Their**
dog is over **there**, by the bench."

**Independent:** 1. Sample: "Because the wind was strong, we stayed
inside." 2. Sample: "The boy with the red kite is my friend."
3. Sample: "The sun shone, and the children played all afternoon."
(Splitting into two sentences is also correct.) 4. "Maya packed a
lunch, but she forgot her water bottle." 5. "It was hot, so we drank
cold water." 6. "I have **two** tickets, and I want to go, **too**."

**Applied (sample):** Short sentences: "The girl rides her blue
bicycle." / "The boy flies his red kite." / "The brown dog chases the
yellow ball." / "Two ducks swim in the pond." Combined pairs, for
example: "The girl rides her blue bicycle, and the boy flies his red
kite." / "The brown dog chases the yellow ball, and two ducks swim in
the pond." Any two correct combined pairs earn credit.

**Exit:** "The dog chased the ball, and the girl rode her bike."
(Splitting into two sentences is also correct.) The fix works because
each half is a complete sentence joined by a comma and a conjunction.

## Lesson 05 — Capitalization and direct speech

**Guided:** 1. Maya whispered, "The ducks are swimming." 2. "The ball
is rolling away," Sam cried. 3. "Will you help me?" Jonah asked.
4. "In October, my friend Lena visited Chicago." 5. Ms. Reed said,
"Open your books to page ten." 6. The period is outside the closing
quotation mark and a tag follows, so it must be a comma inside:
"The kite is high," Maya said.

**Independent:** 1. Jonah said, "The wind is perfect today."
2. "I can see two ducks," Maya said. 3. "Are we going home now?"
Lena asked. 4. "On Tuesday, Mr. Alvarez took his dog to the park."
5. Sample: tag-first: Jonah said, "My kite is flying high!"
speech-first: "My kite is flying high!" Jonah said. 6. The author
wrote, "The garden grew all summer."

**Applied (sample):** Maya said, "Look at the ducks!" / "They are
swimming fast," Jonah said. / "Can we stay a little longer?" Maya
asked. / Jonah said, "Yes, we can stay until lunch." Both patterns,
one question, capitals for names and sentence starts.

**Exit:** Jonah asked, "Can we fly the kite again?" The comma comes
after the tag (*asked*) and before the opening quotation mark; the
question mark sits inside the closing quotation mark.

## Lesson 06 — Spelling, precise words, punctuation for effect, formal vs. informal

**Guided:** 1. *freind* is misspelled; correct is **friend**
(*fri-end*). 2. **beautiful** — *beau-ti-ful*; the *eau* spelling is
the part most often guessed wrong, so check it letter by letter.
3. Sample: "She pedaled home." (Any verb showing bicycle travel earns
credit.) 4. Excited: "The dog caught the ball!" Question: "Did the
dog catch the ball?" 5. Sample: "Excuse me, may I have my ball back,
please?" 6. Swapping *big* for *enormous* is **revising** (word
choice); fixing a capital is **editing** (conventions).

**Independent:** 1. **tomorrow, together, carefully, favorite**
(checked letter by letter). 2. (a) "The dog was enormous." (Any
precise size word earns credit.) (b) "Maya whispered the news."
(*Whispered* fits news told quietly; *announced* is acceptable if
the learner explains the news was told to a group.) 3. Calm: "The
kite flew over the trees." Excited: "The kite flew over the trees!"
Curious: "Did the kite fly over the trees?" 4. (a) formal (b)
informal. 5. "Last Friday, we went to the library and read books."
(Errors fixed: *Last, Friday, library*.) 6. Sample: "It was a sunny
day, and we pedaled to the park and flew kites." Changes named:
*nice* → *sunny* (precise adjective); *did fun stuff* → *pedaled to
the park and flew kites* (precise verbs and detail).

**Applied (the "My Park Visit" draft):** Sample revised paragraph:
"Yesterday, Sam and I went to the park. It was a sunny day. We saw an
enormous dog, and it ran quickly. We had a wonderful time. Good
morning, class. The park was beautiful!" Fixes: capital *Y*; *Sam
and I* order; *nice* → *sunny*; *big* → *enormous*; *went* → *ran*;
*fun* → *a wonderful time*; "hey class" → "Good morning, class";
*cool* → *beautiful*. Any revision with at least 3 spelling/capital
fixes, 2 precise-word swaps, and a formal last sentence earns credit.

**Exit:** Sample: "Good morning. May I borrow that wonderful book,
please?" Spelling strategy for *beautiful*: look it up in the
dictionary, break it into *beau-ti-ful*, and copy it letter by letter.

## Practice A — Relative pronouns and adverbs

1. *who* 2. *where* 3. *why* 4. *whom* 5. "I read a book that was
about space." (or *which*) 6. "That is the girl whose bicycle is
red." 7. "It was a
morning when we planted seeds." 8. *who* is wrong for a place; use
*where*: "The park where we visit on Saturdays is near our house."
(Also acceptable: "…that we visit…" — *that* for a thing/place is
acceptable here; *where* is the lesson answer.) 9–10. Samples:
"The teacher who helps us is Ms. Reed." / "The library where we read
is on Main Street." Any correct sentence earns credit.

## Practice B — Progressive tenses and modal auxiliaries

1. "Maya is reading a book right now." 2. "Maya was reading a book
yesterday evening." 3. "Maya will be reading a book tomorrow
evening." 4. *must* 5. *May* 6. *can* 7. *may* (possibility)
8. "She can **swim** across the pool." (Plain verb after a modal.)
9. "Tomorrow, they **will be** playing soccer." (Future time needs
the future progressive; "were playing" would fit yesterday, not
tomorrow.) 10. Sample: "We must wear helmets on our bikes." /
"You may borrow my pencil."

## Practice C — Prepositional phrases and adjective order

1. Sample: "The children played in the park." 2. Sample: "We ate
lunch after school." 3. *on the wooden shelf* 4. *After the rain, in
the pond* 5. "She wore a small red coat." 6. "He carried two big
blue bags." (Number *two*, size *big*, color *blue*.) 7. "They sat
on a beautiful old wooden bench." (Opinion, age, material.)
8. Sample: "Lena opened the box with great care." 9. Sample: "Three
tiny yellow flowers grew by the fence." 10. Sample: "In the morning,
before school, we ate breakfast." (Any subject + verb earns credit.)

## Practice D — Fragments, run-ons, combining, confused words

1. Fragment — a dependent clause alone. Sample fix: "Because the kite
got stuck, we went home." 2. Complete — subject *boy*, verb *flew*.
3. Sample: "The girl rides her blue bicycle." + "The brown dog chases
the yellow ball." Combined: "The girl rides her blue bicycle, and
the brown dog chases the yellow ball." 4. "The ducks swam in the
pond. The dog ran across the grass." 5. "Maya rode her bike, and
Jonah read his book." 6. "The boy wanted to fly his kite, but the
wind was weak." 7. **to, too**: "We walked **to** school, and we were
**too** excited to go." (Note: the second blank is *too* = very.)
8. **Their, there**: "**Their** house is over **there**." 9. **two,
too**: "I have **two** brothers, and they want to come, **too**."
10. Sample: "We can stay at the park, or we can go home for lunch."
(Any compound sentence with a comma before *or* or *so* earns
credit.)

## Practice E — Capitalization and direct speech

1. "On Monday, Maya visited her aunt in Austin." 2. Dr. Patel said,
"Wash your hands before lunch." 3. Lena said, "The pond is full of
ducks." 4. "Watch out!" Jonah shouted. (An exclamation mark inside
the quotes is also correct for a shout; the comma version — "Watch
out," Jonah shouted — is acceptable.) 5. "Is that your ball?" Maya
asked. 6. Sam asked, "Are we staying long?" 7. The period must be a
comma inside the quotes: "I found a shell," Lena said. 8–9. Samples:
tag-first: "Maya said, \"The kite is stuck!\"" / speech-first: "\"The
kite is stuck!\" Maya said." 10. The author wrote, "Ducks swim in
ponds in spring."

## Practice F — Spelling, precise words, punctuation for effect, formal vs. informal

1. (a) *beautiful* (b) *library* (c) *tomorrow* 2. **together,
carefully** (checked letter by letter). 3. Sample: "He pedaled to
the store." (Any sensible verb of travel earns credit.) 4. Sample:
"It was a delicious cake." (meaning: taste) or "It was a beautiful
cake." (meaning: looks) — credit requires naming the meaning.
5. Excited: "The fireworks lit up the sky!" Calm: "The fireworks lit
up the sky." 6. (a) formal (b) informal. 7. Informal sample: "Hey,
can I use your pencil?" Formal sample: "Excuse me, may I borrow your
pencil, please?" 8. "Last week, we visited the library, and Maya
read a book." (Errors fixed: *Last, library, Maya,* and the comma
before *and*.) 9. Sample: "We pedaled to the park and flew kites all
afternoon." (Precise verb *pedaled*; precise detail *flew kites all
afternoon*.) 10. "Yesterday, Lena and I went to the park, and it was
sunny." (Fixes: capital *Y*, *Lena and I* order, *nice* → *sunny*,
comma before *and*.)

## Unit assessment — 30 points

**Part A (1 point each):** 1. (b) *where* 2. (a) *is* 3. (c) *must*
4. (b) "under the old oak tree"

**Part B (2 points each):** 5. Sample: "After school, we played in
the park." (Any subject + verb earns credit.) 6. Sample: "The sun was
warm, and the children played outside." (Splitting is also correct.)
7. "Lena wanted to read outside, but the rain started." 8. "She
carried a small red bag to school."

**Part C (4 points each):** 9. Jonah said, "The wind is strong today."
/ "I can see the kite," Maya said. (2 points per line: quotation
marks, comma placement, capitals.) 10. "Last Tuesday, Maya and Jonah
went to the library, and they read books." (Errors fixed: *Last,
Tuesday, library,* comma before *and*.)

**Part D (4 points):** Sample: "Good morning. May I borrow that book,
please? I found it very interesting." Credit any formal version with
a greeting or polite frame, *may* or *borrow*, and no slang.

**Part E (6 points):** Sample paragraph (labels in brackets):
"The girl [who] rides a blue bicycle is in the park [relative clause:
*who rides a blue bicycle*]. She rides along the path [prepositional
phrase: *along the path*], and the brown dog chases a yellow ball
[compound sentence with comma before *and*]. The boy is flying a red
kite [progressive: *is flying*]. Two ducks are swimming in the pond."
Score: relative clause 2, compound sentence 2, progressive/modal 1,
prepositional phrase 1. Accept any paragraph meeting the four
conditions, even if the sentences differ.
