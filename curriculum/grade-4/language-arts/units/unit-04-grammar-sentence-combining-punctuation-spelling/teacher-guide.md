# Teacher Guide — Unit 04

Read this before teaching. All solutions are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md). Do not hand the answer key to the
learner; keep it separate from every student page.

## Teaching the unit in order

**Week 15 — describing words and time.** Lesson 01's question —
"Person, thing, place, time, or reason?" — is the unit's most reusable
tool; use it again whenever a relative word is disputed. Lesson 02's
time-word test (*yesterday / right now / tomorrow*) and the plain-verb
rule after modals prevent the two most common errors in this unit.
Do not rush Lesson 02: progressive tenses return in the project and
in Unit 05's narrative dialogue.

**Week 16 — building sentences.** Lesson 03's adjective-order chant
("size, age, color" — with opinion and material at the ends) is worth
repeating at the start of every Week 16 session. Lesson 04's
stand-alone test ("Who or what? Did what?") is the applied task's
real assessment: if a group of words fails either question, it is a
fragment, however long it looks.

**Week 17 — polishing.** Lesson 05's underline-the-exact-words routine
is the only reliable way to teach quotation marks: learners who skip
it put marks around the tag. Lesson 06's revise-first order matters:
do not let a learner polish spelling in a sentence they are about to
delete. The spelling list is a checking tool, not a memorization test
— keep it open during writing.

**Week 18 — project, quiz, assessment.** The quiz is formative:
reteach anything it exposes before the unit assessment. The project
presentation doubles as the SL.4.6 formal-English check: listeners
must hear a greeting, full sentences, and a polite request or
closing, not just correct grammar on paper.

## Misconceptions and reteaching

| If the learner… | Reteach by… |
|---|---|
| Uses *who* for places and times | Asking "Person, thing, place, time, or reason?" before choosing; covering the clause and asking what noun it describes |
| Uses *that* for a place ("the park that we play") | The same question: a place takes *where*; read the joined sentence aloud and ask whether it sounds like a place description |
| Matches the wrong *be* form to the time ("Yesterday I is walking") | Time-word cards (*yesterday / right now / tomorrow*) matched to *was / were, am / is / are, will be* before writing |
| Adds *-s* or *-ing* after a modal ("She can swims") | The plain-verb rule: a modal is always followed by the plain verb; chant "can swim, may swim, must swim" |
| Treats a prepositional phrase as a sentence ("Under the tree.") | The two-question test: "Who or what? Did what?" — a phrase answers neither |
| Puts color before size ("a red small bag") | The order chant: opinion → size → age → color → material; read both orders aloud |
| Fixes a run-on with a comma alone | Covering the second half: if the first half is already a sentence, a comma alone is not a join — add *and, but, or,* or *so,* or split |
| Calls a long sentence a run-on | The join test: length is not the test; missing punctuation or a missing conjunction is |
| Mixes up *their* and *there* by sound | The swap tests: *their* ↔ *our* (belonging); *there* ↔ *here* (place); *too* ↔ *also* or *very* |
| Puts the comma or period outside the closing quotation mark | Underlining the exact spoken words first, then putting marks around only the underlined part |
| Capitalizes the tag after a speech-first comma ("…tree," Maya Said) | The lowercase-tag rule: after a comma inside the quotes, the tag starts lowercase unless it is a name |
| Capitalizes common nouns ("the Park") | The specific-name test: only the full specific name (*Maple Park*) takes capitals |
| Guesses a spelling instead of checking it | The dictionary routine: look it up, break it into parts, copy it letter by letter; keep the list open |
| Thinks revising and editing are the same job | The revise-first order: change words and ideas first, fix capitals and spelling second |
| Thinks formal English means "longer words" | The situation test: who is listening, and what does the situation call for? — then choose the register |

## Scoring guidance

- **Lessons and practice:** credit any answer that matches the key's
  reasoning, even if the wording differs. For combining tasks, accept
  splitting a run-on into two sentences as correct unless the item
  asks for a combined sentence.
- **Quiz (10 items):** 9–10 = ready for the assessment; 7–8 = brief
  reteach of the missed skill; below 7 = reteach the lesson for that
  skill before the assessment.
- **Unit assessment (30 points):** 27–30 = objectives met strongly;
  21–26 = objectives met, with reteaching notes for missed items;
  below 21 = reteach the missed lessons and re-assess with a fresh
  passage and picture sentences.
- **Project:** use the rubric in [project.md](project.md). A first
  draft is never graded for perfection; record one strength and one
  next step for every learner.

## Readiness and next steps

**Ready for Unit 05 (narrative craft: description, dialogue, and
revision) when the learner can:** join a sentence with a relative
clause, write in all three progressive tenses, choose *can, may,* or
*must* with a reason, fix a fragment and a run-on, punctuate two
dialogue lines correctly, and edit a draft paragraph for capitals,
spelling, and commas. If dialogue punctuation is still shaky, repeat
Lesson 05's underline routine on a new conversation before starting
Unit 05 — narrative writing will not fix a punctuation gap.

**If the learner is ahead:** the Lesson 04 extension (a sentence
with a relative clause and a compound join), the Lesson 05 extension
(rewriting speech-first as tag-first), and the Lesson 06 extension
(the same sentence in formal and informal registers, word by word)
are the enrichment path. Do not accelerate into Unit 06's opinion
writing early; depth in sentence craft pays off there.

## Accessibility and safety notes

- Every check has an oral, pointing, or drawing mode: the learner
  may dictate sentences and paragraphs while the adult scribes, then
  read them back for checking.
- The park illustration's text-only alternative (Lesson 04) carries
  every fact the picture activities need; no activity requires
  seeing the picture.
- All research uses adult-selected sources only; the learner never
  searches the open web. All passages are original to this unit;
  no published text is reproduced.
- No investigation in this unit involves materials, tools, or
  hazards; the project is a writing task with a spoken presentation.
