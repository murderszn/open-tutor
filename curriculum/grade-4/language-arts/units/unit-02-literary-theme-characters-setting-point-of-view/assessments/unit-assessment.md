# Unit Assessment — Unit 02

**30 points.** Adult-scheduled within a week after Session 16; untimed.
The learner may re-read any unit text. All items are solved in the
[answer key](../answer-key.md); open responses accept any defensible
answer with matching evidence (see scoring notes).

## New passage (original, written for this unit — read together first)

**"The Garden Wall"**

> Every spring, Mr. Abernathy planted tomatoes along the garden wall he
> shared with the Okafor family. Every spring, the Okafor twins, Ada and
> Ben, picked the ripest ones through the fence slats and ate them warm
> from the vine.
>
> "Those are *my* tomatoes," Mr. Abernathy grumbled each June, shaking
> his rake. He began building the wall higher — one row of bricks, then
> another — until the twins could no longer reach through.
>
> The next spring was the hottest anyone could remember. Mr. Abernathy's
> well ran low, and his tomato leaves curled brown at the edges. From
> her porch, Mrs. Okafor watched him carry bucket after bucket from the
> kitchen tap. Without a word, she sent the twins over with their wagon
> full of water jugs.
>
> "Why help me?" Mr. Abernathy asked, wiping his forehead. "After the
> tomatoes?"
>
> Ada grinned. "The wall's high enough now," she said. "Somebody had to
> water the man who built it."
>
> They watered the tomatoes together all summer. In August, Mr.
> Abernathy set a basket of the ripest ones on the Okafors' porch steps
> — and this time, nobody had to reach through a fence.

## Part A — Theme and summary (RL.4.2) · 8 pts

**A1.** (3 pts) State the theme of "The Garden Wall" as a full sentence
about people in general (not just these characters), and quote two
details that support it.

**A2.** (3 pts) Summarize "The Garden Wall" in 3–4 sentences:
beginning, middle, end, in order, in your own words.

**A3.** (2 pts) A learner says the theme is "tomatoes." Explain in two
sentences why that is a topic, not a theme, and give the theme in a full
sentence.

## Part B — Character in depth (RL.4.3) · 6 pts

**B1.** (3 pts) Describe **Mr. Abernathy** in depth: name one trait and
support it with one thought *or* feeling, one word he says, and one
action — quote or closely follow the text.

**B2.** (3 pts) Describe **Ada** in depth: name one trait and support it
with one word she says and one action — quote or closely follow the
text. (Her thoughts are not shown; say what the narrator gives you
instead.)

## Part C — Setting, events, and forms (RL.4.3, RL.4.5) · 6 pts

**C1.** (2 pts) Describe the setting of "The Garden Wall" (place and
time/season) with two text details.

**C2.** (2 pts) Is "The Garden Wall" poem, drama, or prose? Name two
structural clues.

**C3.** (2 pts) If "The Garden Wall" were rewritten as a drama scene,
name the four structural parts you would need to add or convert, and
give one example of a stage direction for the wall-building moment.

## Part D — Point of view (RL.4.6) · 5 pts

**D1.** (2 pts) What point of view is "The Garden Wall" told in? Quote
two pronouns that prove it.

**D2.** (3 pts) Rewrite this sentence in first person as Mr. Abernathy,
adding one thought only he could know (mark it ★): "He began building
the wall higher — one row of bricks, then another — until the twins
could no longer reach through."

## Part E — Words and cross-cultural themes (RL.4.4, RL.4.9) · 5 pts

**E1.** (2 pts) "Carrying water all summer was a **Herculean** chore."
What does *Herculean* mean here? What two clues (myth + context) unlock
it?

**E2.** (3 pts) "The Garden Wall" shares a theme with one of this
unit's traditional tales. Name the tale, state the shared theme in a
full sentence, and give one supporting detail from each story.
