# Unit 02 — Literary Theme, Characters, Setting, and Point of View

**Grade:** 4 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #24](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/24), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Stories are built from the same five parts the way houses are built from
wood and nails: a **theme** (the big idea the story is really about),
**characters** (the people or animals who live it), a **setting** (where
and when it happens), **events** (what happens, in order), and a
**point of view** (whose eyes tell it). In this unit the learner takes
stories apart and puts them back together — finding themes in details,
knowing characters by their thoughts, words, and actions, describing
settings and events from exact text evidence, telling poems from dramas
from prose, cracking mythology allusions like *Herculean*, hearing the
difference between "I" stories and "she" stories, and comparing how
different cultures tell the same kinds of tales.

The anchor texts are all original to this unit: the story **"The Lantern
on Gray Hill,"** the poem **"October Wind,"** the drama scene **"The Lost
Compass,"** two tellings of the same market scene in different points of
view, and original retellings of two public-domain traditional tales —
Aesop's "The Tortoise and the Hare" and the Japanese folktale "Momotaro,
the Peach Boy." No published text is reproduced.

## Essential question

How do theme, characters, setting, and point of view work together to
make a story mean something?

## Prerequisites

From Unit 01 and the track's diagnostic weeks:

- Quote exact text evidence to answer questions (answer → point →
  quote routine); separate explicit meaning from inference
- Read grade-level prose and poetry orally with accuracy, rate, and
  expression; use context clues and reference materials for unknown words
- Retell a story's beginning, middle, and end in order

New in this unit: theme vs. topic, character analysis from
thoughts/words/actions, setting-and-event description, poem/drama/prose
structures, first- vs. third-person narration, and cross-cultural theme
comparison.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Determine a **theme** of a story, drama, or poem from details in the
   text, and **summarize** the text (RL.4.2).
2. Describe a **character** in depth, drawing on specific details about
   the character's thoughts, words, and actions (RL.4.3).
3. Describe a **setting** and key **events** in depth from specific text
   details, and connect a story's text to a visual presentation of the
   same scene (RL.4.3, RL.4.7).
4. Determine the meaning of words and phrases as used in a text,
   including words that **allude to mythology** (e.g., *Herculean*)
   (RL.4.4); explain major differences among **poems, drama, and
   prose** and name their structural elements — verse, rhythm, meter;
   casts of characters, settings, descriptions, dialogue, stage
   directions (RL.4.5).
5. Compare and contrast **first- and third-person narration** and
   explain how the narrator's point of view changes what the reader
   knows (RL.4.6).
6. Compare and contrast the treatment of similar **themes** in stories,
   myths, and traditional literature from **different cultures**;
   read literature in the grades 4–5 text complexity band with
   scaffolding (RL.4.9, RL.4.10); acquire and use literary academic
   vocabulary accurately (L.4.6).

## 16-session pacing

Four sessions per week, 30–35 minutes each. Each session names its
activity and success criterion; repeated labels are never reused without
a specific new task.

| Session | Week | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7 | Lesson 01: Theme — the big idea hiding in the details ("The Lantern on Gray Hill") | States the story's theme in a full sentence and backs it with 2 text details; summarizes in 3–4 sentences |
| 2 | 7 | Practice A: theme vs. topic sort, theme statements, summaries | 6 of 8 correct; theme statements are full sentences, not one word |
| 3 | 7 | Lesson 02: Characters in depth — thoughts, words, actions | Names a trait for Nora and Grandmother with one thought/word/action detail each |
| 4 | 7 | Practice B: character evidence charts; error-analysis repair | 3 complete thought/word/action triples; repairs the flawed character claim |
| 5 | 8 | Lesson 03: Setting and events in depth (with the lantern-hill illustration) | Describes the setting with 4+ text details; orders 5 events correctly |
| 6 | 8 | Practice C: setting-event map; text vs. illustration comparison (RL.4.7) | Names 2 things the words tell that the picture cannot and 2 the picture adds |
| 7 | 8 | Fluency reading: repeated expressive read of "The Lantern on Gray Hill" | Reads with ≤4 uncorrected errors; marks 3 places where voice shows feeling |
| 8 | 8 | Lesson 04: Poems, drama, prose — how to tell them apart; mythology allusions | Sorts 6 excerpts by form naming the structural clue; explains 3 of 4 allusions |
| 9 | 9 | Practice D: form identification, verse/rhythm/meter, stage directions, allusions in context | 10 of 12 correct; reads "October Wind" marking the beat |
| 10 | 9 | Lesson 05: Point of view — "I" stories vs. "she" stories ("The Market Bell" × 2) | Identifies narrator POV in 4 of 5 passages; explains one thing each POV hides/reveals |
| 11 | 9 | Practice E: POV sort, rewrite a paragraph in the other POV | Rewritten paragraph keeps the events but changes pronouns and inner access correctly |
| 12 | 9 | Project workshop 1: Story Architect — first-person rewrite of the tower scene + theme statement draft | Draft rewrite uses I/me/my throughout; theme is a full sentence with 1 detail |
| 13 | 10 | Lesson 06: Same themes, different cultures — Tortoise & Hare × Momotaro | Names the shared theme and one culture-specific difference with evidence |
| 14 | 10 | Project workshop 2: Story Architect — stage scene with stage directions; peer check | Scene has cast, setting description, dialogue, and 3+ stage directions; peer check complete |
| 15 | 10 | Review: mixed retrieval relay — theme, character, setting, forms, POV | Team scores ≥ 8/12 on the relay board |
| 16 | 10 | Formative quiz ([week-10_quiz](assessments/week-10_quiz.md)) + read-aloud check | Quiz ≥ 9/12; reads a new paragraph with ≤2 uncorrected errors |

Weeks 1–2 of the track are diagnostic/placement and are not part of this
unit. Each session opens with a 2-minute retrieval warm-up from an
earlier session (e.g., quoting one evidence sentence from Unit 01's
routine before Lesson 01's theme work).

## How lessons, practice, reading, investigation, and review fill the unit

- **Lessons (6):** 30–35-minute explicit-teaching sessions with modeled
  reasoning, guided practice, independent practice, an application, and an
  exit check. Each lesson teaches one objective cluster; no lesson stands
  in for a full week.
- **Practice (5 sets):** targeted follow-up sessions (Practice A–E in
  [practice.md](practice.md)) that convert each lesson's teaching into
  independent skill, including one error-analysis repair task.
- **Reading:** every lesson centers on an original anchor text read aloud
  together first (adult and learner alternate paragraphs); Session 7 is a
  dedicated repeated-reading fluency session (RL.4.10).
- **Investigation/project:** the **Story Architect** project
  ([project.md](project.md)) spans Sessions 12 and 14 — a first-person
  POV rewrite, a drama scene with stage directions, and an evidence-backed
  theme statement, with a 40-point rubric.
- **Review:** retrieval warm-ups open every session; Session 15 is a
  full mixed review; Session 16 carries the formative quiz; the
  [unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md) is given within a
  week after Session 16 (adult-scheduled, untimed).

## Differentiation

- **Reading access:** all anchor texts are read aloud together before
  any analysis; the adult reads harder paragraphs while the learner
  follows. The illustration's text-only alternative serves learners who
  cannot see the image.
- **Response modes:** every written task accepts dictated/oral responses
  scribed by the adult, drawings with labels, or sentence frames ("The
  theme is ___ because ___").
- **Extensions:** each lesson and the project include a stretch task
  (e.g., comparing *two* themes, writing an original fable with a stated
  theme, performing the drama scene).
- **Language:** vocabulary is taught with examples before definitions;
  the [resource pack](resource-pack.md) lists free read-aloud and
  captioned supports the adult previews first.
