# Project — Story Architect

**Unit 02 investigation (Sessions 12 and 14, track weeks 9–10).**
Take "The Lantern on Gray Hill" apart and rebuild it three ways — as a
first-person story, as a drama scene, and as a proven theme. You are the
architect; the story's parts are your materials.

## Deliverables (all three required)

### 1. First-person rewrite — "The Tower Door" (RL.4.6)

Rewrite the tower-door paragraph of "The Lantern on Gray Hill" in
**first person as Nora** (8–12 sentences):

> Original (third person): "At the top, the tower door groaned on its
> hinges — it had swollen in the damp, and Nora had to brace her
> shoulder against it. Inside, the narrow stair smelled of oil and dust.
> The lantern room's glass was fogged; she wiped it with her sleeve
> until it shone."

Rules: keep **every event** (door groans, swollen with damp, shoulder
push, stair smell, fogged glass, wiping it clean); change pronouns to
**I/me/my** throughout; add **two thoughts or feelings only Nora could
know** (mark them with a star ★). Do not invent new events.

### 2. Drama scene — "The Lighting" (RL.4.5)

Turn the lantern-lighting moment into a short **drama scene** that could
be performed:

- **Cast of characters** (Nora; Grandmother may appear in a memory or
  voice-over — label it)
- **Setting description** (2–3 sentences: the lantern room at dusk)
- **Dialogue** (at least 6 spoken lines, speaker names first)
- **Stage directions** (at least 4, in brackets or italics: what actors
  do, not say)
- Keep every story fact true to the original (shaking hands, the match,
  the boat's horn).

### 3. Theme statement with evidence (RL.4.2)

On one page: state the story's **theme in a full sentence**, then list
**three text details** (quoted or closely paraphrased, with the part of
the story each comes from: beginning / middle / end) that prove it.

## Presentation

Read your drama scene aloud with the adult (reader's theater — the adult
takes one part), or perform it with simple props. Then explain in 2–3
sentences how changing the point of view (deliverable 1) changed what
the audience gets to know.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | 10 — Strong | 7 — Developing | 4 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **POV rewrite** (RL.4.6) | All events kept; I/me/my throughout; 2 ★ inner thoughts clearly only Nora could know | Events kept; 1–2 pronoun slips; 1 ★ thought, or thoughts anyone could know | Events changed or missing; mixed POV; no ★ thoughts |
| **Drama scene** (RL.4.5) | Cast + setting description present; 6+ dialogue lines; 4+ true stage directions; all story facts accurate | Missing one structural part; or 2–3 stage directions; one story fact off | Two+ structural parts missing; dialogue confused with directions; facts invented |
| **Theme + evidence** (RL.4.2) | Full-sentence theme about people in general; 3 quoted details, one from each story part | Theme is a topic or only about Nora; 2 details, or details unquoted | Theme missing or plot retelling; fewer than 2 details |
| **Presentation & reflection** | Fluent read-aloud; reflection names one specific thing POV changed (inner access or wider view) | Read-aloud hesitant; reflection vague ("it was different") | No read-aloud; no reflection |

**Scoring:** 36–40 exemplary · 28–35 proficient · 20–27 developing ·
below 20 — revisit with the adult (see teacher guide reteach).

## Stretch (optional, ungraded)

Write an original fable (8–12 sentences, animal characters) with a
stated moral, then write its theme as a full sentence. Trade with the
adult: can they find your theme from your details alone?
