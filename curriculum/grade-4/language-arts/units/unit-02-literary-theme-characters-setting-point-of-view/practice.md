# Practice Sets — Unit 02

Targeted follow-ups for Lessons 01–06. Every item is solved in the
[answer key](answer-key.md). The adult reviews practice the same day;
scores stay in private storage, never in this repository.

## Practice A — Theme and summary (after Lesson 01)

1. Sort into **Topic** or **Theme**: (a) "a lost compass," (b) "Small
   kindnesses can change a whole day," (c) "Gray Hill," (d) "Courage
   grows when you act scared anyway."
2. Which detail from "The Lantern on Gray Hill" best supports the theme
   "Being brave means doing the hard thing when someone is counting on
   you"? (a) "The path climbed the hill in lazy S-curves." (b) "'The
   boats are counting on the light,' she whispered." (c) "Fireflies
   drifted over the grass like slow sparks." Explain your pick in one
   sentence.
3. Write a different full-sentence theme for the story (not the modeled
   one) and give two supporting details.
4. Summarize "The Lantern on Gray Hill" in exactly four sentences:
   beginning, middle, end — events only, no theme.
5. A learner wrote: "The theme is when Nora lights the lantern." Name
   two things wrong with this statement and rewrite it correctly.
6. **Error analysis:** "The summary of the story is that bravery is
   doing the hard thing when someone counts on you." What's confused
   here? Write one sentence fixing it.
7. Read "October Wind" (Lesson 04). Write its topic (a word or two) and
   a possible theme (a full sentence).
8. **Challenge:** Could "The Lantern on Gray Hill" have *two* true
   themes? Name a second one with one supporting detail, and say which
   theme the final paragraph supports most.

## Practice B — Characters in depth (after Lesson 02)

Use "The Lantern on Gray Hill" for items 1–5.

1. Quote one **thought** of Nora's and name the trait or feeling it
   shows.
2. Quote one **word** (spoken line) of Grandmother's and name the trait
   it shows.
3. Quote one **action** of Nora's that shows responsibility. Explain in
   one sentence why that action — not just any action — proves it.
4. Is "frightened" a trait of Nora or a feeling? Use one detail to
   defend your answer.
5. **Error analysis:** "Grandmother is the bravest character because she
   is the oldest." What's wrong with this reasoning? Rewrite the claim
   with real evidence (or explain why it can't be proven).
6. New character — read this original paragraph and complete a
   thought/word/action triple for Jun:
   > Jun stared at the empty bird feeder and sighed. "I forgot *again*,"
   > he muttered. He filled the feeder to the brim, then set his alarm
   > for the same time tomorrow.
7. **Application:** Pick a character from any book or show. Write one
   trait and prove it with one thought, one word, and one action.

## Practice C — Setting, events, and text vs. illustration (after Lesson 03)

1. Write the setting of "The Lantern on Gray Hill" in 2–3 sentences
   using one detail from each box: place, time, light/weather,
   sound/smell/touch.
2. Number these events 1–5 in story order: ___ Nora wipes the lantern
   glass clean / ___ Grandmother presses the matchbox into Nora's hand
   / ___ A boat's horn answers / ___ Nora braces her shoulder against
   the swollen door / ___ Nora touches the oak's broken branch for luck.
3. Which event is the story's **problem**? Which is the **resolution**?
4. Name the setting detail the *illustration* shows most clearly that
   the *words* describe least (hint: the path's exact shape).
5. With the [illustration](assets/lantern-hill-setting.png): list two
   things the **words** tell you that the **picture** cannot show.
6. With the illustration: list two things the **picture** shows that
   the **words** never say.
7. **Application:** Describe the setting of your bedroom in four details
   (one per box). Then write one sentence an illustrator would add that
   your words don't say.

## Practice D — Forms of literature and allusions (after Lesson 04)

1. Label each as **poem**, **drama**, or **prose**, and name the
   structural clue: (a) "The Lost Compass," (b) "October Wind,"
   (c) "The Lantern on Gray Hill."
2. In "The Lost Compass," quote one **stage direction** and explain what
   it tells the actor to do.
3. How many **stanzas** does "October Wind" have? How many **verses**
   per stanza? Which lines rhyme?
4. "Lifting the crates was a **Herculean** task." What does *Herculean*
   mean? Name the myth it points to and the context clue you used.
5. Use **Achilles' heel** in an original sentence that shows its
   meaning (one weak spot).
6. Use **Pandora's box** in an original sentence that shows its meaning
   (an action causing unexpected problems).
7. **Error analysis:** "The Lost Compass is a poem because the
   characters' lines rhyme." What's wrong? Name the form and two
   structural parts that prove it.
8. **Challenge:** Write a 4-verse stanza about rain that follows the
   shape of an "October Wind" stanza (4 verses, AABB rhyme, tappable
   beat).

## Practice E — Point of view and cross-cultural themes (after Lessons 05–06)

1. Label each as **first** or **third** person; quote the proving
   pronoun: (a) "She struck the match." (b) "I wiped the glass."
   (c) "We climbed the hill." (d) "The boats waited."
2. Rewrite "She tied her red scarf tight and started up the path" in
   first person as Nora. Add one thought only Nora could know.
3. What does the first-person "Market Bell" reveal that the
   third-person version hides? (One sentence.)
4. What can the third-person narrator show that first-person Maya
   cannot? (One sentence.)
5. State the shared theme of "The Tortoise and the Hare" and "Momotaro"
   in one full sentence, with one detail from each tale.
6. Name one way the Greek and Japanese tales tell their stories
   **differently** (characters, setting, or events) with a detail.
7. Which pattern of events does Momotaro follow — a race or a **quest**?
   Name its three quest stops.
8. **Challenge:** Rewrite the *ending* of "The Tortoise and the Hare"
   (the finish line) in first person as Tortoise. Keep every event;
   add two thoughts only Tortoise could know.
