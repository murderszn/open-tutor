# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Literary Theme, Characters, Setting, and Point of View

For the guiding adult. Read the [unit README](README.md) and the
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) before starting.
Sessions are 30–35 minutes, four per week (track weeks 7–10). Review
practice the same day; keep all learner work, names, and scores in
private storage — never in this public repository.

## How to use this guide

Each lesson below lists: the misconception to watch for, what "good
enough" looks like, and the exact reteach move if the learner struggles.
The [answer key](answer-key.md) solves every item. Nothing here is shown
to the learner.

## Lesson 01 — theme {#lesson-01}

**Watch for:** one-word "themes" ("bravery"); retelling plot when asked
for theme; themes that fit only Nora ("Nora is brave") instead of people
in general; confusing summary (events) with theme (idea).
**Good enough:** a full-sentence theme about people generally, backed by
2 quoted details; a 3–4 sentence ordered summary without theme language.
**Reteach:** use the topic/theme sort alone for a session — 10 cards,
the learner sorts and must defend each theme card with one detail.
Then rebuild: "What did it cost her? What did she gain?" before any
sentence-writing.

## Lesson 02 — character {#lesson-02}

**Watch for:** feelings presented as traits ("Nora is scared" — redirect:
feelings change; traits persist); details that don't match the claimed
trait; describing the illustration instead of quoting text.
**Good enough:** 3 complete triples for Nora (thought/word/action, each
with a quoted detail and a fitting trait); 2 for Grandmother.
**Reteach:** the adult pre-marks 6 details in the story; the learner
only sorts Thought / Word / Action, then matches each to a trait from a
bank of four. Drop the bank once sorting is automatic.

## Lesson 03 — setting and RL.4.7 {#lesson-03}

**Watch for:** describing the picture when asked for text details
("finger on the sentence"); four random details instead of one per box;
treating the illustration as proof rather than a second presentation.
**Good enough:** 4 details, one per box (place, time, light/weather,
sound/smell/touch); 2 text-not-picture and 1 picture-not-text items for
the RL.4.7 comparison.
**Reteach:** cover the illustration; build the setting purely from text
with the four-box chart. Then reveal the picture and play "only in the
picture" — the learner hunts 3 picture-only details. The contrast
teaches the RL.4.7 move.

## Lesson 04 — forms and allusions {#lesson-04}

**Watch for:** calling anything rhyming a poem (check verse/stanza
structure); memorizing allusion definitions without using context;
confusing stage directions with dialogue.
**Good enough:** 6 excerpts sorted by form with the structural clue
named; 3 of 4 allusions explained from myth + context.
**Reteach:** three-column chart (Poem / Drama / Prose) with the
structural parts as column headers; the learner places 9 new excerpts
(one structural clue per card). For allusions, teach only *Herculean*
deeply first — myth story, then 3 sentence contexts — before adding the
other four.

## Lesson 05 — point of view {#lesson-05}

**Watch for:** labeling by who is *in* the scene ("Maya is in it, so
first person"); believing first person is always more truthful;
changing events (not just pronouns/inner access) when rewriting POV.
**Good enough:** 4 of 5 passages labeled with the proving pronoun
quoted; the rewrite keeps every event with correct pronouns and one
genuine inner thought.
**Reteach:** pronoun-hunt session — the adult reads 8 sentences, the
learner holds up "I" or "SHE" cards. Then rewrite one sentence at a
time with the rule "change the camera, not the events."

## Lesson 06 — cross-cultural themes {#lesson-06}

**Watch for:** restating the moral word-for-word ("slow and steady wins
the race") instead of building a theme sentence; claiming the tales are
"the same story"; treating the retellings as quoted ancient texts.
**Good enough:** a shared theme in a full sentence with one detail per
tale; one same / one different with details.
**Reteach:** sentence frame "Both stories show that ___ because in the
first ___ and in the second ___." Remind: these are the curriculum's
own retellings of traditional plots, in our words.

## Project — Story Architect {#project}

**Watch for:** inventing new events in the POV rewrite; dialogue written
as stage directions (and vice versa); theme statements that are topics.
**Good enough:** see the [rubric](project.md) — 28/40 is proficient.
**Reteach:** split the project across three short sessions (one
deliverable each); model the first two sentences of each deliverable
aloud before the learner continues. Reader's theater can be done with
puppets or stuffed animals to lower performance pressure.

## Assessments {#assessments}

- **Week-10 quiz:** 9/12 shows readiness for the unit assessment.
  Below 9: reteach the missed objective's lesson (not the whole unit)
  using the reteach move above, then re-quiz with 4 fresh items from
  [practice.md](practice.md).
- **Unit assessment:** 24/30 is proficient. Open responses accept any
  defensible answer with matching evidence — score the *evidence*,
  not the theme wording. If Part D (POV) is weak but the rest is
  strong, the learner is ready for Unit 03; circle back to POV during
  Unit 05's narrative writing.
- **Readiness for Unit 03:** the learner can state a theme with
  evidence, describe a character from thoughts/words/actions, and tell
  first from third person. Everything else can keep growing.

## Pacing notes

- Sessions 12 and 14 are workshops: 5 minutes of modeling, 25 minutes
  of drafting, 5 minutes of read-back. Do not let drafting bleed into
  Session 13 (Lesson 06) or 15 (review).
- If a session runs long, cut the *extension*, never the exit check —
  the exit check is your data for tomorrow's warm-up.
