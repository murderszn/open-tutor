# Answer Key — Unit 02: Literary Theme, Characters, Setting, and Point of View

For the guiding adult only. Every practice, quiz, assessment, and project
item is solved here. Open responses accept any defensible answer whose
evidence matches — score the evidence, not the wording. Each solution was
worked independently and reconciled against its lesson's teaching.

## Practice A — Theme and summary

1. Topic: (a) "a lost compass," (c) "Gray Hill." Theme: (b) "Small
   kindnesses can change a whole day," (d) "Courage grows when you act
   scared anyway." Rule: topics are words you point at; themes are full
   sentences about people in general.
2. **(b)** "'The boats are counting on the light,' she whispered." It
   is the only choice about someone *depending* on Nora — the heart of
   the theme. (a) and (c) are setting details.
3. Sample: "Kindness means helping people you will never meet."
   Details: "the riverboats below found the safe channel by its light";
   "A boat's horn answered — low and grateful." (Accept any full-sentence
   theme with 2 matching details.)
4. Sample: "Nora's grandmother, the keeper of the Gray Hill signal
   lantern, falls sick with fever. Nora climbs the hill alone at dusk,
   pushes through the swollen tower door, and lights the lantern with
   shaking hands. The light shines, a boat's horn answers gratefully,
   and Nora learns she can do the hard thing."
5. Wrong: (1) it names an event, not an idea — a theme must be a full
   sentence about people in general; (2) "when Nora lights the lantern"
   is plot summary. Rewrite: "Being brave means doing the hard thing
   when someone is counting on you."
6. Confused: that sentence *is* a theme, not a summary. Fix: "A summary
   retells the events in order — for example, 'Nora climbs Gray Hill
   and lights the lantern.' The theme is the big idea: 'Being brave
   means doing the hard thing when someone is counting on you.'"
7. Topic: "the October wind" (or "an autumn wind"). Sample theme:
   "Wild autumn weather is powerful but playful." (Accept any
   full-sentence idea the poem's details support.)
8. Second theme: "Kindness to strangers matters — Nora risks the climb
   for boat crews she will never meet." Detail: "the riverboats below
   found the safe channel by its light." The final paragraph ("she had
   done the hard thing") supports the bravery theme most strongly.

## Practice B — Characters in depth

1. Thought: "Nora's stomach knotted" — shows fear/nervousness. (Also
   accepted: "She was still a little afraid of the dark walk home.")
2. Word: "'The boats are counting on the light,' she whispered" —
   shows Grandmother is dutiful/caring (duty first, even sick).
3. Action: "she wiped it with her sleeve until it shone" — she does the
   job carefully and completely though no one is watching; that
   thoroughness proves responsibility. (Also accepted: "she tied her
   red scarf tight... and started up the stone path" — follows through
   despite fear.)
4. "Frightened" is a **feeling**: it changes — "Nora's stomach knotted"
   at the start, but "Nora laughed out loud" at the end. Traits persist;
   feelings pass.
5. Wrong: age proves nothing about bravery, and no detail is quoted —
   it fails the thoughts/words/actions test. Rewrite: "Grandmother is
   dutiful: sick with fever, her first words are 'The boats are counting
   on the light.'" (Or: the claim can't be proven — no detail shows
   Grandmother facing fear.)
6. Jun triple — Thought: "Jun stared at the empty bird feeder and
   sighed" (regret/guilt). Word: "'I forgot *again*,' he muttered"
   (honest, self-aware). Action: "He filled the feeder to the brim,
   then set his alarm for the same time tomorrow" (responsible, caring).
   Trait: **responsible**.
7. Application: score the triple, not the character chosen. Sample:
   trait "loyal" — thought: "she worried about him all night"; word:
   "'We have to help him,' she said"; action: "she stayed up brewing
   the remedy."

## Practice C — Setting, events, text vs. illustration

1. Sample: "The story happens at dusk on Gray Hill above Miller's Ford,
   where a stone path climbs in S-curves to a round stone tower. The sky
   deepens to evening blue with a thin crescent moon, tall grass
   whispers around the climber, and the tower door groans on its
   hinges." (Place ✓, time ✓, light ✓, sound ✓.)
2. Order: **2** Grandmother presses the matchbox into Nora's hand;
   **4** Nora touches the oak's broken branch for luck — wait,
   recheck: the branch-touch happens during the climb, *before* the
   door. Correct order: 1 Grandmother presses matchbox; 2 Nora touches
   oak branch; 3 Nora braces shoulder against door; 4 Nora wipes glass;
   5 boat's horn answers. (Items listed: matchbox → 1, glass → 4,
   horn → 5, door → 3, branch → 2.)
3. Problem: Grandmother's fever — Nora must light the lantern alone.
   Resolution: the lantern is lit, the boat's horn answers, Nora
   succeeds.
4. The path's exact S-shape — the words say "lazy S-curves" but the
   picture lets the eye trace each curve; also the girl's exact look
   (braid, blue coat, boots), never described in words.
5. Words-not-picture (any two): "the tower door groaned on its hinges
   — it had swollen in the damp" (sound + cause); "the narrow stair
   smelled of oil and dust" (smell); "The lantern room's glass was
   fogged" (before wiping); "A boat's horn answered" (sound off-page);
   the brass matchbox; "Nora's stomach knotted" (inner feeling).
6. Picture-not-words (any two): the girl's braid, blue coat, brown
   boots; the exact number and placement of stars; the stone-by-stone
   pattern of the path; the tower's conical roof shape.
7. Application: check one detail per box; the illustrator sentence must
   name something visual the words omit.

## Practice D — Forms and allusions

1. (a) **drama** — cast of characters + stage directions; (b) **poem**
   — verses in stanzas + rhythm/rhyme; (c) **prose** — sentences in
   paragraphs.
2. "*(lifting the compass)*" — tells the actor playing Nora to lift the
   compass as she speaks. (Any of the four accepted.)
3. 2 stanzas; 4 verses each. Rhymes: low/fro (slant), tails/rails,
   moan/alone, rain/again.
4. *Herculean* = extremely difficult, needing enormous strength/effort.
   Myth: Hercules, the Greek hero of the twelve impossible labors.
   Context clue: "repairing the stone wall" is heavy physical work
   framed as a huge "task."
5. Sample: "I am good at math, but word problems are my Achilles'
   heel." (Shows: one weak spot.)
6. Sample: "Bringing up last year's argument at dinner was opening
   Pandora's box — we argued about three new things." (Shows: unexpected
   problems released.)
7. Wrong: the scene's lines don't rhyme — they are **dialogue**, and the
   piece opens with a cast list and setting description and uses stage
   directions like *(grinning)*. It is **drama**.
8. Challenge sample:
   > Rain, rain, you tap the pane,
   > You drum the roof and flood the lane.
   > You fill the gutters, splash and pour —
   > Rain, rain, I can't ask for more!
   Check: 4 verses, AABB rhyme, tappable beat. (Accept any equivalent.)

## Practice E — POV and cross-cultural themes

1. (a) third — "She"; (b) first — "I"; (c) first — "We"; (d) third —
   no I/me/we; the narrator describes from outside ("The boats waited").
2. Sample: "I tied my red scarf tight and started up the path. ★ My
   legs felt wobbly, but I told myself Grandmother was counting on me."
   (Check: I/my throughout; events unchanged; ★ thought only Nora could
   know.)
3. Maya's secret: she spent the flour money on a carved wooden bird —
   the third-person version only shows her ducking.
4. The wider scene: Jon waving across the square, the market around
   them — things Maya, stuck in her own worry, might not notice.
5. Sample: "What looks weak can win when it keeps going with a brave
   heart." Tortoise detail: "Step, step, step — slow and steady, all
   the way to the riverbank." Momotaro detail: he and his small
   companions faced the huge ogres "with courage and cleverness."
6. Sample difference: the Greek tale uses **talking animals** in a
   single-afternoon **race**; the Japanese tale uses a **human boy with
   animal helpers** on a long **quest** to an island.
7. A **quest**: leaves home → journeys to Ogre Island → faces the ogres
   → returns with treasure for the village.
8. Challenge sample: "I kept going — step, step, step. ★ My legs ached,
   but I remembered Hare's laugh and kept my eyes on the riverbank. ★
   Then I heard cheering, and I knew before I looked: I had won."
   (Check: events kept — Tortoise crosses while Hare sleeps, animals
   cheer; 2 ★ inner thoughts.)

## Week-10 quiz (12 pts)

1. **Topic.** A topic is what the story is *about* (a word or two);
   a theme is what the story *says* about the topic (a full sentence).
2. Sample: "Being brave means doing the hard thing when someone is
   counting on you." Detail: "Her hands shook as she struck the match."
   (Any defensible full-sentence theme + matching quoted detail.)
3. Thought: "Nora's stomach knotted." Word: "'What if I can't?' Nora
   asked." Action: "she wiped it with her sleeve until it shone."
4. Sample: "Gray Hill at dusk: a stone path winds in S-curves up to a
   round stone tower with a glowing lantern. The sky turns evening blue
   with a crescent moon while tall grass whispers." (3+ details.)
5. (a) poem (b) drama (c) prose.
6. They are **stage directions** — they tell the actors what to *do*
   (not say).
7. *Herculean* = extremely difficult. It points to Hercules and his
   twelve labors.
8. **First** person — "I."
9. "I wiped the glass with my sleeve." (Accept "my sleeve.")
10. Sample: "What looks weak can win when it keeps going with a brave
    heart." (Accept any defensible shared theme.)
11. Sample: the Greek tale uses talking animals in a race; the Japanese
    tale sends a boy with animal helpers on a quest.
12. Sample: "A text can give sounds, smells, and thoughts that a still
    picture cannot show — comparing both ways reveals what each version
    adds." (Accept: words carry the unpicturable; pictures add the
    unseen-described.)

## Unit assessment (30 pts)

### Part A — Theme and summary (8 pts)

**A1.** Sample theme: "Kindness repaid with kindness — helping a
neighbor comes back to you." Details: "she sent the twins over with
their wagon full of water jugs" (middle); "Mr. Abernathy set a basket
of the ripest ones on the Okafors' porch steps" (end). (Also accepted:
"Generosity can heal a quarrel," with matching details. 1 pt theme
sentence + 1 pt per detail.)

**A2.** Sample: "Mr. Abernathy builds his shared garden wall higher so
the Okafor twins can't reach his tomatoes. When a hot spring dries his
well, Mrs. Okafor sends the twins over with water jugs. They water the
tomatoes together all summer, and in August he leaves a basket of the
ripest tomatoes on their porch — freely given this time." (1 pt per
ordered part; deduct for theme language or copied sentences.)

**A3.** "Tomatoes" is the **topic** — what the story is about, in a
word. A theme is the *idea* in a full sentence, e.g.: "Sharing with
neighbors can turn enemies into friends."

### Part B — Character in depth (6 pts)

**B1.** Sample — trait: **proud, then grateful** (a change; accept
"gruff but fair"). Feeling/word: "'Those are *my* tomatoes,' Mr.
Abernathy grumbled each June" (annoyed/possessive). Word: "'Why help
me?' Mr. Abernathy asked" (humbled/surprised). Action: "he set a basket
of the ripest ones on the Okafors' porch steps" (generous, grateful).
(1 pt trait + 1 pt per clue type with matching text.)

**B2.** Sample — trait: **kind** (accept "playful," "generous"). Word:
"'Somebody had to water the man who built it,' she said" (teasing
kindness). Action: "They watered the tomatoes together all summer"
(helpful, steady). Note for the adult: the narrator never shows Ada's
thoughts — the learner should say the evidence comes from **words and
actions only**, which is itself worth credit.

### Part C — Setting, events, forms (6 pts)

**C1.** Place: the garden wall shared by Mr. Abernathy and the Okafor
family (neighboring homes). Time: spring into summer — "Every spring...
planted," "the hottest [spring] anyone could remember," "In August."
(1 pt place + detail, 1 pt time + detail.)

**C2.** **Prose** — sentences grouped into paragraphs; dialogue in
quotation marks inside narration. Not a poem (no verses/stanzas) or
drama (no cast list, no stage directions).

**C3.** The four drama parts: **cast of characters, setting
description, dialogue, stage directions.** Sample stage direction for
wall-building: *[Mr. Abernathy stacks bricks, wiping sweat from his
forehead; the twins watch through the fence slats.]* (1 pt for naming
all four, 1 pt for a true stage direction — an action note, not a
spoken line.)

### Part D — Point of view (5 pts)

**D1.** **Third person.** Proving pronouns: "he/his/him" ("he shared,"
"his rake," "him carry"), "she/her" ("she sent," "her porch"),
"they/their" ("their wagon"). (Any two quoted.)

**D2.** Sample: "I began building the wall higher — one row of bricks,
then another — until the twins could no longer reach through. ★ My
hands were blistered, but I told myself they'd thank me when the
tomatoes were safe." (1 pt pronouns, 1 pt events kept, 1 pt ★ inner
thought only he could know.)

### Part E — Words and cross-cultural themes (5 pts)

**E1.** *Herculean* = extremely hard, an enormous effort. Clues: the
**myth** of Hercules' twelve impossible labors + the **context** —
"carrying water all summer" is a huge, exhausting chore.

**E2.** Expected: **Momotaro.** Shared theme: "Kindness shared comes
back to the giver." Momotaro detail: "Momotaro shared it [the treasure]
with the whole village." Garden Wall detail: "Mr. Abernathy set a
basket of the ripest ones on the Okafors' porch steps." (Also accepted:
**The Tortoise and the Hare** with "steady effort wins" — detail "They
watered the tomatoes together all summer" — if the reasoning is
defended; score the evidence.)

## Project — Story Architect (40 pts)

Score with the [rubric](project.md). Sample strong responses:

- **POV rewrite:** "I reached the top with my legs burning. The tower
  door groaned on its hinges — it had swollen in the damp. ★ For a
  terrible second I thought it wouldn't open at all. I braced my
  shoulder against it and shoved. Inside, the narrow stair smelled of
  oil and dust. The lantern room's glass was fogged. ★ My sleeve was
  already damp with sweat, but I wiped and wiped until it shone."
- **Drama scene:** cast (NORA, 10; GRANDMOTHER'S VOICE, offstage);
  setting description ("The lantern room of the Gray Hill tower. Dusk
  light fades through fogged glass."); dialogue (6+ lines, e.g. NORA:
  "Come on — catch, please catch."); stage directions (*[Her hands
  shake as she strikes the match]*, *[Golden light blooms]*, *[A low
  boat horn sounds, far below]*, *[She laughs]*).
- **Theme + evidence:** "Being brave means doing the hard thing when
  someone is counting on you." Beginning: "she pressed the brass
  matchbox into Nora's hand"; middle: "Her hands shook as she struck
  the match"; end: "A boat's horn answered — low and grateful."
