# Lesson 02 — Characters in Depth: Thoughts, Words, Actions

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can describe a character in depth using
their thoughts, words, and actions as evidence."
**Time:** 30–35 minutes · **Materials:** this lesson; Story Parts
notebook.
**Standards:** RL.4.3 (character in depth).

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult asks: "Yesterday we found the story's big idea. Today we meet
the people inside it. Quick check: if I say 'Nora is brave,' is that a
description or a *proven* description?" (A description — it needs proof.
Today's rule: **every trait needs a thought, a word, or an action to
back it up.**)

## Explicit teaching

Authors rarely write "Nora was brave." Instead they show you three kinds
of clues:

- **Thoughts** — what the character thinks or feels inside: *"Nora's
  stomach knotted."*
- **Words** — what the character says out loud: *"'What if I can't?'
  Nora asked, her voice small."*
- **Actions** — what the character does: *"she tied her red scarf
  tight... and started up the stone path."*

The character-evidence triple: **trait + clue type + quoted detail.**
Example: "Nora is **responsible** — shown by her **actions**: 'she
pressed the brass matchbox into Nora's hand' is Grandmother trusting
her, and Nora 'wiped [the glass] with her sleeve until it shone'
instead of rushing."

A **trait** is a lasting quality (brave, kind, responsible, stubborn).
A **feeling** is temporary (scared, tired). Traits are proven across
the story; feelings can change — Nora is scared *and* brave in the same
evening.

### Modeled example 1 — Nora's thoughts prove a trait

*Adult models aloud:* "Trait: **brave**. Thought-clue: 'Nora's stomach
knotted' — she feels fear. But bravery isn't the absence of fear; it's
acting anyway. Action-clue: 'Her hands shook as she struck the match.'
Shaking hands + striking the match anyway = brave. So: 'Nora is brave.
Her thoughts show fear ("Nora's stomach knotted"), yet her actions show
courage ("her hands shook as she struck the match").'"

### Modeled example 2 — Grandmother's words prove a trait

*Adult models aloud:* "Trait: **trusting** (she trusts Nora) or
**dutiful** (duty to the boats). Word-clue: 'The boats are counting on
the light,' she whispered. 'You know the way.' Even sick with fever, her
first thought is the boats and her confidence in Nora. Action-clue: 'she
pressed the brass matchbox into Nora's hand.' So: 'Grandmother is
dutiful and trusting. Her words put the boats first ("The boats are
counting on the light"), and her action hands Nora the keeper's job
("she pressed the brass matchbox into Nora's hand").' Two defensible
traits, both proven."

## Guided practice (together)

1. Find one **thought** of Nora's besides "stomach knotted." What trait
   or feeling does it show?
2. Find one **word** Grandmother says. What does it show about her?
3. A learner claims: "Nora is careless because she went up the hill
   alone at dusk." Use the thoughts/words/actions test — does any
   detail support "careless"? What trait *do* the details support?
4. **Error analysis:** "Nora is tall because the text says she climbed
   the hill." What's wrong with this evidence? (Climbing proves
   nothing about height.)

## Independent practice

1. Complete a character-evidence chart for **Nora**: three rows —
   one thought, one word, one action — each with the quoted detail and
   the trait it supports.
2. Complete two rows for **Grandmother** (one word, one action).
3. **Application:** Think of a character from a book or show you know.
   Write one trait and support it with one thought, one word, and one
   action (quote or describe the exact moment).

## Exit check

"Give me one trait for Nora, and prove it with either a thought, a
word, or an action — quote the words." (Accept any trait with a
matching quoted detail, e.g., brave/kind/responsible/determined.)

## Supports, alternatives, extensions

- **Support:** the adult pre-marks three details in the story; the
  learner only sorts each into Thought / Word / Action and names a
  trait.
- **Alternate response mode:** act out the trait — show "brave" in a
  frozen pose from the tower scene, then quote the matching detail;
  dictate the chart.
- **Extension:** explain how Nora **changes** from the beginning to the
  end using two details (fearful → confident); predict how the change
  proves the theme from Lesson 01.

## Teacher note

**Watch for:** confusing feelings with traits ("Nora is scared" as a
trait — redirect: feelings change, traits persist); quoting a detail
that doesn't match the claimed trait; describing the *illustration*
instead of the text.
