# Lesson 05 — Point of View: "I" Stories vs. "She" Stories

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can tell first-person from third-person
narration and explain how the narrator changes what the reader gets to
know."
**Time:** 30–35 minutes · **Materials:** this lesson; Story Parts
notebook.
**Standards:** RL.4.6 (point of view).

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult asks: "Who told 'The Lantern on Gray Hill' — Nora, or someone
watching Nora?" (Someone watching — the words say "she," never "I.")
"Today: the **narrator** is whoever tells the story, and the **point of
view** is where that narrator stands."

## Explicit teaching

- **First person:** the narrator is *inside* the story — a character
  telling it. Pronouns: **I, me, my, we, us, our.** You get the
  narrator's thoughts and feelings directly, but *only* what that
  character knows, sees, and feels.
- **Third person:** the narrator is *outside* the story — watching the
  characters. Pronouns: **he, she, they, him, her, them**, plus names.
  You can see several characters' actions, but you don't automatically
  get anyone's private thoughts (unless the narrator chooses to share
  them).
- The POV test: **what does this narrator know that the other one
  couldn't?** First person hides what the character doesn't know;
  third person can show you the whole scene but may hide inner
  thoughts.

### "The Market Bell" — third-person telling (original, written for this unit)

> The market bell rang at noon. Maya froze behind the bread stall. She
> had promised her mother she would buy flour, but the coin purse in her
> pocket felt too light. Across the square, her friend Jon waved. Maya
> ducked behind a barrel of apples and pretended not to see him.

### "The Market Bell" — first-person telling (same scene, Maya narrates)

> The market bell rang at noon, and my heart jumped into my throat. I
> had promised Mama I would buy flour, but my coin purse felt too light
> in my pocket. Across the square, Jon waved at me. I ducked behind a
> barrel of apples and pretended not to see him. I knew exactly why I
> was hiding — I had spent the flour money on a carved wooden bird — I
> just hoped Jon hadn't seen me do it.

### Modeled example 1 — spotting the POV

*Adult models aloud:* "Version one: 'Maya froze,' 'She had promised,'
'her friend Jon.' Pronouns she/her, and the narrator names Maya from
outside — **third person**. Version two: 'my heart jumped,' 'I had
promised,' 'I ducked.' Pronouns I/my — Maya herself is telling it —
**first person**. Same events, different eyes."

### Modeled example 2 — what each POV reveals and hides

*Adult models aloud:* "What does first person *reveal*? Maya's secret:
'I had spent the flour money on a carved wooden bird.' The third-person
version never tells us that — it only shows her ducking. What does
third person *reveal*? The whole square: 'Across the square, her friend
Jon waved' — we see Jon clearly. First person is stuck inside Maya's
head; third person is a camera that can pan. So: first person gives
**inner truth**; third person gives **wider view**."

## Guided practice (together)

1. "We packed our bags before sunrise." First or third person? Which
   pronoun proves it?
2. In the first-person "Market Bell," what does the reader learn that
   the third-person version hides?
3. In the third-person version, what can the narrator show that Maya
   herself might not notice?
4. "The Lantern on Gray Hill" says "she tied her red scarf tight" —
   what POV is the whole story? Rewrite that clause in first person.

## Independent practice

1. Label each as first or third person and quote the proving pronoun:
   (a) "They ran to the tower." (b) "I struck the match." (c) "Nora
   laughed out loud." (d) "Our lanterns glowed."
2. Rewrite the third-person "Market Bell" ending ("Maya ducked behind
   a barrel of apples and pretended not to see him") in first person
   as Maya. Keep every event; change pronouns and add one thought only
   Maya could know.
3. **Application:** Tell about your own morning in 3 sentences in first
   person, then retell the *same* morning in third person (use your
   name). Write one sentence explaining what changed.

## Exit check

"Give me one thing a first-person narrator can tell you that a
third-person narrator usually cannot — and one thing third person does
better." (Inner thoughts/feelings/secrets vs. showing multiple
characters or the wider scene.)

## Supports, alternatives, extensions

- **Support:** pronoun highlighter routine — the adult reads a passage
  while the learner raises a hand for I/me/my (first) or he/she/they
  (third).
- **Alternate response mode:** tell the morning retelling orally; act
  the two Market Bell versions with the adult narrating.
- **Extension:** "The Lantern on Gray Hill" is third person but shares
  Nora's thoughts ("Nora's stomach knotted"). Explain why an author
  might choose *this* middle ground — what would be lost in strict
  first person? (Adult note: she'd lose the tower's outside view; the
  boat's horn lands harder from outside.)

## Teacher note

**Watch for:** labeling by *who is in* the scene instead of *who tells*
it ("Maya is in it, so it's first person"); thinking first person is
always more truthful (narrators can be wrong or hide things); changing
events when rewriting POV instead of only pronouns and inner access.
