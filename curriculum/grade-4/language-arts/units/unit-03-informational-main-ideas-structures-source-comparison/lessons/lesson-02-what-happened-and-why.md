# Lesson 02 — What Happened and Why: Procedures and Causes

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can explain events, procedures, ideas,
or concepts in an informational text — what happened and why — using
specific information, and I can use domain-specific words correctly."
**Time:** 30–35 minutes · **Materials:** this lesson; a pencil and
paper.
**Standards:** RI.4.1, RI.4.3, RI.4.4.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult asks: "If I tell you 'the plant grew,' have I told you
everything? What would you still want to know?" (How it grew, step
by step — and why each step happened.) "Informational writers answer
two questions at once: **what** happened, and **why**. Today we
practice finding both, and learning the special words each topic
uses — those are called **domain-specific words**."

## Explicit teaching

- A **procedure** is a set of steps in order. A **cause** is why
  something happens; an **effect** is what happens because of it.
- To explain "what happened and why," find the steps or events in
  order, then find the sentence that gives the reason for each one.
  Quote both.
- **Domain-specific words** belong to one topic's world: *germinate*
  belongs to seeds, the way *pitch* belongs to baseball. Learn them
  from context first, then check a dictionary or glossary.

**Vocabulary for this lesson (taught with examples first):**

- **germinate** — when a seed starts to grow.
- **seedling** — a very young plant.
- **vine** — a long, trailing stem that grows along the ground.
- **pollinate** — when pollen moves to a flower so it can make seeds
  or fruit. Bees do much of the pollinating.
- **harvest** — to pick or gather a crop when it is ready.

### The lesson passage

Read it aloud together first.

**"From Seed to Pumpkin" (original, written for this unit)**

> In spring, after the last frost, farmers plant pumpkin seeds in
> warm soil. Within about a week, the seeds germinate: a tiny root
> grows down and a shoot pushes up through the soil. The shoot
> becomes a seedling with two small leaves. Over the summer, the
> seedling grows into a long vine that spreads across the ground,
> growing big leaves and yellow flowers. Bees visit the flowers and
> pollinate them, carrying pollen from flower to flower. After a
> flower is pollinated, a small green pumpkin begins to grow behind
> it. All summer the pumpkin grows bigger and turns from green to
> orange, because it is storing food and ripening in the sun. In
> autumn, farmers harvest the pumpkins when the skin is hard and
> deep orange.

### Modeled example 1 — steps in order

*Adult models:* "What happened, in order? (1) Seeds planted in warm
soil. (2) Seeds germinate — root down, shoot up. (3) Seedling grows
into a vine with leaves and flowers. (4) Bees pollinate the flowers.
(5) A small pumpkin grows and ripens to orange. (6) Farmers harvest
in autumn. Notice I did not skip pollination: without it, step 5
never happens. A procedure answer that skips a step is like a
recipe missing an ingredient."

### Modeled example 2 — what happened and why

*Adult models:* "Question: why does the pumpkin turn orange? Answer:
because it is storing food and ripening in the sun. Evidence: 'All
summer the pumpkin grows bigger and turns from green to orange,
because it is storing food and ripening in the sun.' The word
*because* is a clue — it points straight at the why. Question: why
do farmers plant after the last frost? The passage says seeds go
into 'warm soil' — cold soil after a frost would not let them
germinate well. That is an inference from the text's details, and I
say so: the text states the warm soil, and I infer the frost would
keep soil too cold."

### A common trap (model it)

A learner lists the steps out of order: "First the pumpkin grows,
then bees come." The adult answers: "Check the passage: bees visit
*before* the pumpkin appears — 'After a flower is pollinated, a
small green pumpkin begins to grow.' Order matters in a procedure;
swap two steps and the explanation breaks."

## Guided practice (adult hints allowed)

1. List the six steps of pumpkin growing in order, in your own
   words. (Hint: start with "plant" and end with "harvest.")
2. Why must bees visit the flowers? Quote the sentence that proves
   it, and explain what would happen without them.
3. Use *germinate* and *seedling* correctly in two sentences of your
   own about any plant.
4. What does "after the last frost" tell us about *when* to plant,
   and why does the timing matter? (Hint: look for "warm soil.")

## Independent practice

1. Explain what happens between "seedling" and "harvest" in three
   sentences, in order, quoting at least one sentence from the
   passage.
2. Cause and effect: the cause is "the pumpkin stores food and
   ripens in the sun." What is the effect? Write the cause and the
   effect as a pair, with the quoting sentence.
3. Error analysis: a classmate wrote, "Pumpkins turn orange because
   farmers paint them." Use the passage to correct this in two
   sentences, quoting the real reason.
4. Domain words: define *vine*, *pollinate*, and *harvest* in your
   own words, then check each in a dictionary or glossary and note
   any correction to your definition.

## Applied task

Explain a procedure you know well (making a sandwich, tying shoes,
planting a seed, brushing teeth) in 4–6 ordered steps, with a "why"
for at least two steps. Read it to the adult: can they follow it
without asking a question? If they must ask, a step or a reason is
missing — add it.

## Exit check

Without looking back: "In order, what are the first and last steps
of growing a pumpkin, and why does the pumpkin turn orange?" (First:
plant seeds in warm soil after the last frost. Last: harvest in
autumn when the skin is hard and deep orange. It turns orange because
it stores food and ripens in the sun.)

## Supports, alternate modes, extension

- **Reading support:** number the passage's sentences together
  before listing steps; each step maps to one or two numbered
  sentences.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may act out the steps in
  order (plant, sprout, vine, bee, pumpkin, harvest) while narrating,
  then write the list.
- **Extension:** write one sentence that explains *why* farmers
  wait for a hard, deep-orange skin before harvesting, using the
  passage plus one careful inference — and label which part is
  stated and which part is inferred.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: (1) listing facts without order; (2) giving
the *what* without the *why*; (3) treating an inference as if the
text stated it. Reteach by asking "does the passage say that, or did
we figure it out from what it says?" Full solutions:
