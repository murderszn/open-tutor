# Teacher Guide — Unit 03

Read this before teaching. All solutions are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md). Do not hand the answer key to the
learner; keep it separate from every student page.

## Teaching the unit in order

**Week 11 — main idea and what/why.** Lesson 01's removal test
("if I remove this detail, does the main idea fall apart?") is the
unit's most reusable tool — use it again whenever a main idea is
disputed. Lesson 02's order check ("can the adult follow your steps
without asking a question?") is the applied task's real assessment:
if the adult must ask, a step or a reason is missing.

**Week 12 — structures and accounts.** Teach Lesson 03 in two
sessions: structures first, accounts and the picture second. The
four mini-passages are deliberately short so the learner can hold
the whole passage in mind while naming its structure. When the
firsthand/secondhand discussion turns into "which is more true,"
steer to "which question does each answer better?" — that is the
durable understanding, and it returns in the project rubric.

**Week 13 — charts, reasons, integration.** Lesson 04's chart is
fictional practice data: say so out loud the first time, or the
learner will treat the numbers as real garden results. Lesson 05's
integration rule — "one paragraph where a fact from A sits next to
a fact from B, ending with a conclusion that needs both" — is the
project's grading standard; model it twice if the first attempt
comes back as two summaries.

**Week 14 — project, quiz, assessment.** The quiz is formative:
reteach anything it exposes before the unit assessment. The project
presentation doubles as the SL.4.3 listening assessment: listeners
must name a point, a reason, and evidence from the talk they heard.

## Misconceptions and reteaching

| If the learner… | Reteach by… |
|---|---|
| Says the topic ("leaves") when asked for the main idea | Asking for a full sentence: "Tell me in a sentence what the author says *about* leaves." |
| Calls the first sentence the main idea | Covering the first sentence and asking whether the rest still makes sense without it; then covering the last sentence and asking the same. |
| Calls every true detail a key detail | The removal test: cover the detail; if the main idea still stands, the detail is interesting, not key. |
| Lists procedure steps out of order | Numbering the passage's sentences together and mapping each step to its sentence number. |
| Gives the *what* without the *why* | Asking "because…?" after every step until the learner adds the reason sentence. |
| Calls comparison "differences only" | Pointing to the similarity sentence in the river passage ("both carry people across a river"). |
| Calls the longer/more official account "more true" | Asking two questions — one only the diary answers, one only the report answers — and showing both answers are needed. |
| Treats any number as proof of anything | Asking "evidence *for what*?" after every number, and checking whether the number's topic matches the reason's topic. |
| Copies sentences and calls them notes | Covering the text and asking the learner to say the fact from memory in five words or fewer. |
| Writes two summaries and calls it integration | Requiring one paragraph where sentences alternate sources and end with a conclusion sentence that neither text wrote. |
| Treats a speaker's confidence as evidence | Asking "what fact did the speaker give?" — confidence is delivery, not evidence. |

## Scoring guidance

- **Lessons and practice:** credit any answer that matches the key's
  reasoning, even if the wording differs. For main-idea items, accept
  any full sentence covering what most of the passage does; reject
  topic words, opinions ("leaves are pretty"), and single details.
- **Quiz (10 items):** 9–10 = ready for the assessment; 7–8 = brief
  reteach of the missed skill; below 7 = reteach the lesson for that
  skill before the assessment.
- **Unit assessment (12 items):** score items 1–9 and 11–12 as
  correct/partial/incorrect; item 10 (integrated paragraph) with the
  project rubric's integration row. 10–12 strong = unit objectives
  met; 7–9 = met with reteaching notes; below 7 = reteach and
  re-assess with a fresh passage.
- **Project:** use the rubric in [project.md](project.md). A first
  draft is never graded for perfection; record one strength and one
  next step for every learner.

## Readiness and next steps

**Ready for Unit 04 (grammar, sentence combining, punctuation,
spelling) when the learner can:** state a main idea in a sentence,
support it with key details, name all four structures, tell
firsthand from secondhand, read a simple chart, and integrate two
texts with a source list. If main-idea work is still shaky, repeat
Lesson 01's routine on a new passage before starting Unit 04 —
grammar work will not fix a main-idea gap.

**If the learner is ahead:** the Lesson 03 extension (rewrite a
cause/effect ending as problem/solution), the Lesson 04 extension
(rewrite a conclusion to match only its evidence), and the Lesson 05
extension (add a third source) are the enrichment path. Do not
accelerate into Unit 04's grammar content early; depth here pays
off in Units 06–07 (opinion and research writing).

## Accessibility and safety notes

- Every check has an oral, pointing, or drawing mode: the learner
  may dictate summaries and paragraphs while the adult scribes,
  then read them back for checking.
- The garden illustration's text-only alternative (Lesson 03)
  carries every fact the picture activities need; no activity
  requires seeing the picture.
- All research uses adult-selected sources only; the learner never
  searches the open web. All passages are original to this unit;
  no published text is reproduced.
- The harvest chart and the walking-to-school chart are fictional
  practice data, labeled in the lessons. Do not present them as
  real results.
