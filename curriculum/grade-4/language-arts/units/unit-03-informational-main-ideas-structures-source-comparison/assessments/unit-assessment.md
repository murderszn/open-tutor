## Passage 1 — "How the Moon Changes Shape" (original, written for this unit)

> The Moon does not make its own light. It shines because sunlight
> bounces off its surface toward Earth, the way a mirror bounces
> light. As the Moon travels around Earth, the amount of its lit
> half that we can see changes, and that is why the Moon seems to
> change shape. The table below shows the pattern over about 29
> days.

**Days since new moon and what we see (rounded for grade 4)**

| Day | What we see |
|---|---|
| 0 | New moon — the lit half faces away from us, so we see almost nothing |
| 7 | First quarter — we see the right half lit |
| 14 | Full moon — we see the whole lit face |
| 22 | Last quarter — we see the left half lit |
| 29 | New moon again — the cycle restarts |

> Because the cycle takes about 29 days, a full moon comes roughly
> once a month. People long ago used the full moon to mark time,
> which is why some of our months are named for moons.

1. (RI.4.2) What is the main idea of Passage 1, in one sentence of
   your own? Name two key details that support it.
2. (RI.4.3) Why does the Moon seem to change shape? Explain what
   happens and why, quoting one sentence.
3. (RI.4.4) What does *cycle* mean in this passage? Use the table
   and the text to explain it in your own words.
4. (RI.4.7) From the table: how many days pass between the new moon
   and the full moon? What pattern do the day numbers show?
5. (RI.4.5) What text structure does the middle of Passage 1 mainly
   use — chronology, comparison, cause/effect, or problem/solution?
   Quote one signal word or phrase that proves it.
6. (RI.4.2) Write a 2–3 sentence summary of Passage 1 in your own
   words. It must include the main idea and at least two key
   details, with no copied sentences.

## Passage 2 — Two texts about the new playground

**Text A — firsthand letter (original, written for this unit)**

> Dear Mayor, I was at the new playground on opening day, and I
> tried the tall slide three times. My favorite part was the climbing
> wall, because I finally reached the top on my third try and felt
> so proud. Please build more climbing walls in our town. — Sam, age 10

**Text B — secondhand report (original, written for this unit)**

> On Saturday, the town opened its new playground on Elm Street.
> The playground cost $40,000 and includes a slide, swings, a
> climbing wall, and a sandbox. According to the parks department,
> more than two hundred children visited on opening day.

7. (RI.4.6) Which text is firsthand and which is secondhand? How do
   you know? Name one thing each text tells you that the other does
   not.
8. (RI.4.8) What point does Sam make in Text A, and what reason and
   evidence does Sam give? Is Sam's evidence strong or weak for
   that point? Explain in two sentences.
9. (RI.4.6) If you wanted to know how much the playground cost,
   which text would you check, and why? If you wanted to know how
   it felt to climb the wall, which would you check?
10. (RI.4.9) Write one paragraph (4–6 sentences) that integrates
    both texts: tell what happened on opening day using facts from
    Text A and Text B, and end with your own conclusion sentence.
    Mark which facts came from which text.

## Vocabulary and structure check

11. (RI.4.5, RI.4.4) Match each passage to its main structure, and
    define the underlined word in your own words:
    - (a) "First the seed sprouts. Next the stem grows tall. Then
      flowers open." — structure? Define *sprouts*.
    - (b) "Cats and dogs both make good pets, but cats groom
      themselves while dogs need baths." — structure? Define
      *groom*.
    - (c) "The bridge was closed because a storm damaged it, so
      drivers took another road." — structure? Define *damaged*.
    - (d) "The hall was too loud for reading. The school added
      curtains and rugs to soften the sound." — structure? Define
      *soften* (as used here).

    Write a source list naming both, in the format: title — what
    kind of text it is — written for this unit. Then write two
    categorized notes (phrases, not copies) from Passage 1 under
    the headings *What the Moon does* and *Why it looks that way*.
