## Part 1 — "The Life of a Raindrop" (original, written for this unit)

> Water travels in a cycle. First, heat from the sun warms water in
> lakes and oceans, and the water evaporates — it rises into the air
> as an invisible gas. Next, high in the sky, the gas cools and
> condenses into tiny droplets, forming clouds. Then the droplets
> join together until they are heavy enough to fall as rain. Finally,
> the rain collects in lakes and oceans again, and the cycle starts
> over.

1. What is the main idea of the passage, in one sentence of your own?
2. List the four steps of the water cycle in order.
3. What text structure does this passage mainly use? Quote two
   signal words that prove it.
4. Why do the droplets fall as rain? Quote the sentence that gives
   the reason.

## Part 3 — Two accounts of one event

**Account A:** "I stood at the finish line with my heart pounding
and watched my friend cross first. I cheered so loud my throat hurt."

**Account B:** "The school fun run had eighty runners. The winner
finished in nine minutes, and the school raised money for new library
books."

7. Which account is firsthand, and how do you know? Which is
   secondhand?
8. If you wanted to know how many runners there were, which account
   would you check? If you wanted to know how cheering felt, which
   would you check? Explain each choice in one sentence.

## Part 4 — Reasons and evidence

**"Why Recess Matters" (original, written for this unit)**

> Schools should protect recess time. First, recess gives children
> exercise: running and playing build strong bodies. Second, recess
> gives children a break, so they return to class ready to focus.
> In one school, teachers reported that students concentrated
> better after recess than after a long stretch of lessons.

9. Name the author's point and its two reasons.
10. Which reason has evidence, and what is the evidence? Which
    reason has no evidence? Explain how you can tell.
