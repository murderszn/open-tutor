# Unit 03 — Informational Main Ideas, Structures, and Source Comparison

**Grade:** 4 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #24](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/24), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Stories tell you what happened to someone. Informational text tells you
how the world works — and it expects you to check it. In this unit the
learner reads to find the **main idea** and the **key details** that
prove it, explains **what happened and why**, names how a text is
**structured**, tells a **firsthand** account from a **secondhand**
one, reads charts and pictures as evidence, judges an author's
**reasons and evidence**, and puts **two texts together** to speak and
write knowledgeably about one topic. Every passage in this unit is
original, written for this unit, except where a public-domain retelling
is noted; no published text is reproduced.

## Essential question

How do readers figure out what informational text is really saying — and
check it against another source?

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–02 and the track's diagnostic weeks:

- Quote exact text evidence to answer questions (answer → point →
  quote routine); separate explicit meaning from inference
- Determine a theme, describe characters/settings/events, and compare
  first- and third-person narration in stories
- Read grade-level prose and poetry orally with accuracy, rate, and
  expression; use context clues and reference materials for unknown words
- Retell a text's beginning, middle, and end in order; summarize a story

New in this unit: main idea vs. topic, key details vs. interesting
details, text structures (chronology, comparison, cause/effect,
problem/solution), firsthand vs. secondhand accounts, reading charts and
images as information, author's reasons and evidence, integrating two
texts, note-taking with categories, and source lists.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Determine the **main idea** of an informational text, explain how
   **key details** support it, and **summarize** the text (RI.4.1,
   RI.4.2).
2. Explain **events, procedures, ideas, or concepts** in an
   informational text — including what happened and why — based on
   specific information in the text (RI.4.3); determine the meaning of
   general academic and **domain-specific** words relevant to a grade-4
   topic (RI.4.4).
3. Describe the **overall structure** of a text or part of a text —
   chronology, comparison, cause/effect, problem/solution (RI.4.5).
4. Compare and contrast a **firsthand** and a **secondhand** account
   of the same event or topic, describing the differences in focus and
   in the information provided (RI.4.6); interpret information presented
   **visually, orally, or quantitatively** (charts, diagrams, images)
   and explain how it contributes to understanding the text (RI.4.7).
5. Explain how an author uses **reasons and evidence** to support
   particular points (RI.4.8); **integrate information from two texts**
   on the same topic in order to write or speak about the subject
   knowledgeably (RI.4.9).
6. Gather relevant information from print and digital sources, **take
   notes and categorize** information, and **provide a list of
   sources** (W.4.8); identify the **reasons and evidence a speaker**
   provides to support particular points (SL.4.3); read informational
   texts in the grades 4–5 text complexity band with scaffolding at the
   high end (RI.4.10); acquire and use informational academic
   vocabulary accurately (L.4.6).

## 16-session pacing (4 sessions per week, about 30–35 minutes each)

| Session | Focus | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| W11 S1 | Lesson 01 — main idea, key details, summary | Learner states a main idea in one sentence and names 2 key details that support it, quoting at least one |
| W11 S2 | Practice A + reread of "Why Do Leaves Change Color?" | Learner sorts details into key vs. interesting and writes a 2–3 sentence summary |
| W11 S3 | Lesson 02 — what happened and why; domain words | Learner explains a procedure's steps in order and why each step matters, using 2 domain words correctly |
| W11 S4 | Practice B + independent reading of a short informational passage (adult-selected) | Learner answers what/why questions with quoted evidence |
| W12 S5 | Lesson 03, part 1 — four text structures | Learner names the structure of each mini-passage and points to its signal words |
| W12 S6 | Practice C (structures) + image study | Learner matches 4 new short passages to structures and describes 3 image details |
| W12 S7 | Lesson 03, part 2 — firsthand vs. secondhand accounts | Learner compares the diary and the news report: who wrote each, what each focuses on, what only one includes |
| W12 S8 | Practice C continued + discussion: which account would you trust for what? | Learner explains, with evidence, which account answers which question better |
| W13 S9 | Lesson 04 — visual/quantitative information; reasons and evidence | Learner reads the harvest chart (totals, comparisons) and names an author's point + 2 reasons + evidence |
| W13 S10 | Practice D + chart talk | Learner explains what the chart adds that the words alone do not |
| W13 S11 | Lesson 05 — integrating two texts; notes, categories, source lists; speaker evidence | Learner combines facts from both compost passages into one short paragraph and lists both sources |
| W13 S12 | Practice E + project launch (choose topic, adult selects two texts) | Learner files at least 6 categorized notes from the two project texts |
| W14 S13 | Project work session — draft the two-source report | Draft has a main idea, details from both texts, and a source list |
| W14 S14 | Project presentations + listening for speaker reasons (SL.4.3) | Learner presents in order and names one reason + evidence from the talk they heard |
| W14 S15 | Review + formative week-14 quiz | Learner can name all four structures and the firsthand/secondhand test without prompting |
| W14 S16 | Culminating unit assessment + reflection | Assessment complete; learner names one strength and one next step |

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for English Language
Arts**, grade 4, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/).
Standard wordings were verified 2026-10-24 against the published
grade-4 Reading: Informational Text standards (RI.4.1–RI.4.10),
Writing standard W.4.8, and Speaking and Listening standard SL.4.3,
corroborated across the published standards listings. Paraphrases in
the objectives above follow those wordings; the full standard texts
are not reproduced. No state adoption or accreditation is claimed.

- **RI.4.1** Refer to details and examples in a text when explaining
  what the text says explicitly and when drawing inferences from the
  text.
- **RI.4.2** Determine the main idea of a text and explain how it is
  supported by key details; summarize the text.
- **RI.4.3** Explain events, procedures, ideas, or concepts in a
  historical, scientific, or technical text, including what happened
  and why, based on specific information in the text.
- **RI.4.4** Determine the meaning of general academic and
  domain-specific words or phrases in a text relevant to a grade 4
  topic or subject area.
- **RI.4.5** Describe the overall structure (e.g., chronology,
  comparison, cause/effect, problem/solution) of events, ideas,
  concepts, or information in a text or part of a text.
- **RI.4.6** Compare and contrast a firsthand and secondhand account
  of the same event or topic; describe the differences in focus and
  the information provided.
- **RI.4.7** Interpret information presented visually, orally, or
  quantitatively (e.g., in charts, graphs, diagrams, time lines,
  animations, or interactive elements on Web pages) and explain how
  the information contributes to an understanding of the text in
  which it appears.
- **RI.4.8** Explain how an author uses reasons and evidence to
  support particular points in a text.
- **RI.4.9** Integrate information from two texts on the same topic
  in order to write or speak about the subject knowledgeably.
- **RI.4.10** By the end of year, read and comprehend informational
  texts, including history/social studies, science, and technical
  texts, in the grades 4–5 text complexity band proficiently, with
  scaffolding as needed at the high end of the range.
- **W.4.8** Recall relevant information from experiences or gather
  relevant information from print and digital sources; take notes
  and categorize information, and provide a list of sources.
- **SL.4.3** Identify the reasons and evidence a speaker provides to
  support particular points.
- **L.4.6** Acquire and use accurately grade-appropriate general
  academic and domain-specific words and phrases.

Factual notes: the leaf-color passage follows the standard
elementary account (chlorophyll fades in autumn, revealing yellow and
orange pigments already present; some trees produce red pigment in
autumn). The pumpkin procedure uses ordinary garden timing (seeds
sprout in about a week, vines grow through summer, harvest in
autumn). The harvest chart in Lesson 04 is **fictional practice
data**, labeled as such in the lesson — it teaches chart reading,
not real garden results. The moon passage in the assessment uses the
approximate 29-day cycle (new → first quarter → full → last quarter
→ new) rounded for grade 4.
