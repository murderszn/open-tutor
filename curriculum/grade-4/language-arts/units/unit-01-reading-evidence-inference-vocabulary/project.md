# Project — Evidence Case File

**Investigating question:** Can I prove everything I claim about a text
— and crack every hard word I meet?

Across the unit (sessions 1–15), the learner builds an **Evidence Case
File**: a detective's folder of six solved cases. Each case starts from
a question about one of the unit's texts or the window-scene
illustration and ends with quoted evidence, a verified word, and a
clearly labeled answer or inference. The finished file is the unit's
investigation product and is assessed with the rubric below.

## Materials

A folder or notebook (the case file); pencil; the unit passages and
[illustration](assets/inference-window-scene.png); root keyring cards
(Lesson 03); word warrant cards (Lesson 05); a print or
adult-supervised digital dictionary.

## Steps

1. **Open six cases (sessions 1–13, ongoing).** Choose at least one
   case from each source: "The Empty Bowl," "Beaver Builders," the
   window-scene illustration, "The Cracked Sidewalk," "The Porch
   Light"/"Rainy-Day Porch." At least two cases must be **inference**
   cases and at least two must be **explicit-evidence** cases.
2. **Work each case (as its lesson is taught).** Every case entry
   contains:
   - **The question** (in the learner's words).
   - **The evidence** — exact quoted words from the text, or exactly
     described details from the illustration.
   - **The detective work** — for explicit cases: the finger-point and
     quote; for inference cases: detail + background knowledge +
     check ("nothing contradicts it" or what would change the mind);
     for word cases: the tool used (root break, context clue type,
     dictionary verification) with the word broken/read/defined.
   - **The verdict** — the answer or inference, stated plainly.
   - **One word warrant** — one hard or interesting word from the
     case's text, cracked with a unit tool and used in an original
     sentence.
3. **Verify (session 14).** Re-check two cases against the text: is
   every quote word-for-word? Is every inference still supported? Fix
   any drift and note what the re-check caught.
4. **Present one case (session 15).** The learner presents their
   strongest case to the adult like a detective briefing: question,
   evidence, work, verdict — in under two minutes.
5. **File and close (session 15).** Six complete cases, numbered, with
   a cover page (title, detective's name line left blank/anonymized per
   repo privacy rules — first name or code only in private storage,
   never committed). The file stays in the learner's private
   materials — it is not published.

## Deliverables

- 6 case entries, each with: question, quoted/described evidence,
  detective work labeled by type, verdict, and one word warrant with
  an original sentence.
- Evidence of the session-14 verification (at least 2 re-checked or
  corrected items noted).
- 1 two-minute case briefing, presented orally.

## Rubric

| Criterion | 3 — Strong | 2 — Developing | 1 — Starting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence | Every claim quotes or precisely describes text/illustration details; quotes are word-for-word | Most claims have evidence; 1–2 quotes are paraphrased or slightly off | Evidence missing or vague ("the story says so") |
| Inference quality | Inferences name both detail and background knowledge; checkable; busted theories dropped | Inferences given but knowledge or check is missing | Guesses stated as facts; no detail named |
| Word work | Every word warrant shows the tool (root/clue/dictionary), correct break/read, and a sensible original sentence | Tool named but break or sentence has errors | Words listed without tools or sentences |
| Academic language | 4+ unit academic words (*evidence, inference, cite, predict, observe, conclude, compare, describe*) used precisely | 2–3 used, mostly correctly | 0–1, or used vaguely |
| Presentation & verification | Briefing is clear and under 2 minutes; verification notes show 2+ genuine re-checks | Briefing rambles or verification is superficial | No briefing or no verification |

**Scoring:** 13–15 = strong detective (ready for Unit 02); 9–12 =
developing (rework 1–2 weakest cases); 5–8 = starting (redo two cases
with the adult modeling first).
