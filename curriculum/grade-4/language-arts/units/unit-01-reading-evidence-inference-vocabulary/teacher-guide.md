# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Reading Evidence, Inference, and Vocabulary

For the guiding adult. Read the [unit README](README.md) and the
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) before starting. Sessions
are 30–35 minutes, four per week (track weeks 3–6). Review practice the
same day; keep all learner work, names, and scores in private storage —
never in this public repository.

## How to use this guide

Each lesson below lists: the misconception to watch for, what "good
enough" looks like, and the exact reteach move if the learner struggles.
The [answer key](answer-key.md) solves every item. Nothing here is shown
to the learner.

## Lesson 01 — explicit meaning and quoting {#lesson-01}

**Watch for:** answering from background knowledge ("dogs need water")
instead of the text; paraphrasing instead of quoting; quoting a whole
paragraph when one sentence answers.
**Good enough:** 6 of 8 guided questions answered with a correct
word-for-word quote; the learner can state the three-step routine
(answer → point → quote).
**Reteach:** drop to one passage; cover all but the target sentence and
play "hot and cold" — the learner points nearer/farther until the finger
lands on the sentence. Then require only a 3–5 word quoted phrase
before building to full sentences.

## Lesson 02 — inference {#lesson-02}

**Watch for:** guesses with no detail ("I just think so"); stating
inferences as certainties; ignoring the check step; confusing visible
facts with inferences ("the umbrella is wet" is explicit, not an
inference).
**Good enough:** 4 of 5 cases state inference + detail + background
knowledge; the learner can repair one busted theory when prompted.
**Reteach:** use the illustration only (no text) for a full session:
the adult models 5 triples aloud, then the learner does 3 with the
sentence frame "I see ___, and I know ___, so I infer ___." Add text
inferences only after the frame is automatic.

## Lesson 03 — Greek and Latin roots {#lesson-03}

**Watch for:** letter-matching without the meaning test (*port* in
*important*); breaking at the wrong place and freezing; treating every
long word as root-based (some are just long — *calendar*, *window*).
**Good enough:** 6 of 8 words broken, read, and assembled with sensible
meanings; the 8 root meanings recalled with the hooks.
**Reteach:** teach 4 roots only (*tele-, photo-, graph-, port-*) with
physical cards for a day; do the meaning test as a game ("Does 'carry'
make sense in *important*? No? Then it is not our root."). Re-add roots
only after those four are automatic.

## Lesson 04 — context clues {#lesson-04}

**Watch for:** naming a meaning without naming the clue type; calling
every comma phrase a definition; leaning on one clue type.
**Good enough:** 6 of 8 words solved with the clue type and signal
words named; the re-read check performed unprompted at least once.
**Reteach:** deal pre-sorted cards — 3 definition, 3 example, 3
restatement — and have the learner sort new sentences into the three
boxes before solving any meaning. Require the signal words to be
pointed at before any meaning is accepted.

## Lesson 05 — dictionary and academic words {#lesson-05}

**Watch for:** dictionary as first resort (skipping clue → parts →
verify order); grabbing numbered meaning #1; thesaurus swaps that break
meaning ("slim puppy").
**Good enough:** 4 of 5 lookups land on the sentence-correct numbered
meaning; 4 academic words used precisely in original sentences.
**Reteach:** enforce the order with a checklist card (1. clue? 2.
parts? 3. verify). Practice wrong-number traps deliberately: give 3
sentences for one multiple-meaning word and require the number + the
ruling-out reason each time.

## Lesson 06 — fluency {#lesson-06}

**Watch for:** speed = fluency (rushing); word-by-word dragging;
ignoring punctuation; silent skipping of errors instead of
self-correcting.
**Good enough:** third read of "The Porch Light" is smoother than the
first with ≤2 uncorrected errors; the poem's expression marks honored;
2+ self-corrections narrated with the context reason.
**Reteach:** echo reading (adult reads a phrase, learner repeats) for
the whole passage before any solo read; draw phrase scoops under the
text to break word-by-word habit; pre-teach all multisyllable words
with Lesson 03's break-and-blend.

## Practice sets

- **A (evidence):** if quotes drift from word-for-word, go back to the
  finger-point before any copying. Trap item 9 is the lesson's key
  idea — do not skip it.
- **B (inference):** accept any defensible triple; reject triples
  missing the knowledge half. Item 7 (busted theory) is the honesty
  check — praise a clean "drop it."
- **C (roots):** item 5 (*important*) is the meaning-test lesson in
  miniature; make sure the verdict is stated, not just felt.
- **D (mixed):** below 80%, identify the weakest of the three skills
  and replay that lesson's guided practice before continuing.
- **E (clues):** item 8 (*oases*) tests whether examples can teach a
  plural/meaning the learner has never seen — discuss what the list
  reveals (places in the desert with water).
- **F (dictionary):** item 6 (thesaurus trap) is the point of the
  whole set — a synonym that fails the meaning check is not a
  synonym for that sentence.

## Project — Evidence Case File

Check cases as they are filed (sessions 1–13), not all at session 14.
The most common failure is evidence drift — quotes that slowly become
paraphrases. The session-14 verification exists to catch exactly this;
model one full re-check aloud before the learner does theirs. For the
briefing, enforce the 2-minute limit kindly but firmly — concision is
part of the skill. Score with the project rubric; 9–12 means rework
the weakest cases, not the whole file.

## Assessments

- **Week-04 quiz (30 pts):** Part B items 5–6 accept any defensible
  inference triple; do not penalize a different-but-supported reading
  of the illustration. Part C: half credit for a correct break/read
  with a wrong assembly.
- **Unit assessment (40 pts):** Part D item 16 is scored by its rubric;
  a claim like "Maya is responsible" can be supported by "filled the
  bowl with kibble," "poured fresh water," "Leo never remembers the
  water" (she compensates), or "sat on the steps to wait." "Caring"
  works with the same evidence — judge the connection, not the
  adjective. The fluency check (item 17) is required but ungraded;
  more than 4 uncorrected errors triggers a Lesson 06 revisit.

## Reteach paths

- **Evidence weak after Lesson 01 + Practice A:** replay Lesson 01
  with "Beaver Builders" only, then re-take Practice A items 5–8.
- **Inference weak after Lesson 02 + Practice B:** one full session
  of illustration-only triples (see Lesson 02 reteach), then Practice
  B items 4–6 with the sentence frame.
- **Word parts weak after Lesson 03 + Practice C:** 4-root reteach
  (see Lesson 03), then Practice C items 1–4 only.
- **Clues weak after Lesson 04 + Practice E:** sort-first reteach
  (see Lesson 04), then Practice E items 1–7.
- **Dictionary weak after Lesson 05 + Practice F:** wrong-number
  traps (see Lesson 05 reteach), then Practice F items 1–4.
- **Fluency weak at session 16:** echo reading + phrase scoops (see
  Lesson 06 reteach) across 3 extra sessions before Unit 02; this is
  normal and not a failure.

## What "ready for Unit 02" looks like

Quiz ≥ 24/30, case file complete with verification notes, fluency
check ≤ 4 uncorrected errors, and the learner can state the inference
triple and the quote routine without prompting. Anything short of that
is a targeted reteach above — not a repeat of the whole unit.
