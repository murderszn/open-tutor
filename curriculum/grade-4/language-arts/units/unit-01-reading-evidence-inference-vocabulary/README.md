# Unit 01 — Reading Evidence, Inference, and Vocabulary

**Grade:** 4 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #24](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/24), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Good readers are detectives. In this unit the learner collects **evidence**
(exact words and details from the text), separates what a text **says
explicitly** from what it only **hints at**, and makes **inferences** by
combining text clues with what they already know — always able to point to
the sentence that proves it. Along the way the learner cracks big
multisyllable words with Greek and Latin roots and affixes, figures out
unknown words from context clues, verifies meanings in dictionaries and
glossaries, collects precise academic words, and reads grade-level prose
and poetry aloud with accuracy, rate, and expression.

## Essential question

How can I prove what a text says — and figure out what it only hints at?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the grade-3 track:

- Read and blend one-, two-, and some three-syllable words; common
  prefixes (un-, re-, dis-, pre-) and suffixes (-ful, -less, -ed, -ing,
  -tion); Latin suffixes (-tion, -sion, -able, -ible, -ment)
- Ask and answer questions referring explicitly to a text; recount stories
  and determine central messages; describe characters' traits and feelings
- Read grade-level text orally with developing accuracy, rate, and
  expression; use context to confirm or self-correct
- Sentence-level context clues; beginning-dictionary routines; shades of
  meaning among related words

The diagnostic re-teaches nothing here; Lesson 03 re-teaches morphology and
multisyllable strategies explicitly rather than assuming they are secure.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Refer to details and examples to explain what a literary or
   informational text says **explicitly**, quoting or naming the exact
   words that prove it (RL.4.1, RI.4.1).
2. Draw **inferences** from a text by combining text details with
   background knowledge, and support each inference with quoted text
   evidence (RL.4.1, RI.4.1).
3. Break unfamiliar multisyllabic words into syllables and read them
   accurately, using combined letter-sound knowledge, syllabication
   patterns, and morphology — roots and affixes (RF.4.3, RF.4.3.a).
4. Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown words using sentence-level
   context clues (definitions, examples, restatements) and common Greek
   and Latin affixes and roots (L.4.4.a, L.4.4.b).
5. Verify word meanings with print or digital dictionaries, glossaries,
   and thesauruses; choose and use grade-appropriate academic and
   domain-specific words precisely (L.4.4.c, L.4.6).
6. Read grade-level prose and poetry orally with accuracy, appropriate
   rate, and expression on successive readings; use context to confirm or
   self-correct word recognition and understanding (RF.4.4.a–c).

## Unit word sets (authored for this unit; reused across lessons and practice)

**Multisyllable decode set:** photograph, telescope, microscope, autograph,
transport, import, export, paragraph, biography, geography, thermometer,
enormous, generous, important, discover, adventure, calendar, window,
family, table, napkin, insect, magnet, problem, carpet

**Greek/Latin roots (meaning → example words):**
- tele- ("far"): telephone, television, telescope, telegraph
- photo- ("light"): photograph, photon
- graph- ("write"): autograph, photograph, paragraph, biography
- auto- ("self"): autograph, automobile, automatic
- phon- ("sound"): telephone, microphone, symphony
- scope- ("see"): telescope, microscope, periscope
- geo- ("earth"): geography, geology
- port- ("carry"): transport, portable, import, export

**Meaning affixes:** un- ("not"), re- ("again"), dis- ("not/opposite"),
pre- ("before"), mis- ("wrong"), -ful ("full of"), -less ("without"),
-tion/-sion ("act or state of"), -able/-ible ("can be")

**Context-clue practice words:** weary, brisk, enormous, generous,
murmur, cautious, triumph, shimmer, sturdy, vacant

**Academic words (collect and use):** evidence, inference, cite,
predict, observe, conclude, compare, contrast, describe, explain

All word sets are original curriculum-authored lists; no worksheet or
published list is reproduced. All reading passages in this unit are
original and written for it; no published text is reproduced.

## 16-session pacing

Four sessions per week, 30–35 minutes each. Each session names its
activity and success criterion; repeated labels are never reused without a
specific new task.

| Session | Week | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | Lesson 01: Say it with the text — explicit meaning and quoting evidence | Finds and quotes the exact sentence answering 6 of 8 explicit questions |
| 2 | 3 | Practice A: evidence hunts in "The Empty Bowl" (story) and "Beaver Builders" (informational) | Quotes correct evidence for 8 of 10 questions across both passages |
| 3 | 3 | Lesson 02: Inference detective — details + what you know (with the window-scene image) | States an inference, names the detail, and names the background knowledge for 4 of 5 cases |
| 4 | 3 | Practice B: inference cases with the image and "The Cracked Sidewalk" passage | 6 of 8 inferences supported by quoted details; no unsupported guesses |
| 5 | 4 | Lesson 03: Greek and Latin roots unlock big words (tele-, photo-, graph-, auto-, phon-, scope-, geo-, port-) | Breaks and reads 6 of 8 multisyllable words using root + syllables; explains 6 of 8 root meanings |
| 6 | 4 | Practice C: root sort, build-a-word, syllable surgery | Sorts 16 words by root with ≤2 errors; builds 6 correct root words |
| 7 | 4 | Practice D: mixed detective review — evidence, inference, and word parts | Mixed set: ≥80% correct across the three skills |
| 8 | 4 | Fluency reading 1: repeated reading of "The Empty Bowl" | Reads with ≤4 errors on first read; improves on the third read (fewer errors or smoother phrasing) |
| 9 | 5 | Lesson 04: Context clues — definition, example, restatement | Names the clue type and the meaning for 6 of 8 words |
| 10 | 5 | Practice E: context-clue investigations with unit word sets | 8 of 10 meanings correct, each with the clue type named |
| 11 | 5 | Lesson 05: Dictionary detective and academic words | Reads a dictionary entry correctly (word, part of speech, numbered meaning) for 4 of 5 words; uses 4 academic words precisely in sentences |
| 12 | 5 | Practice F: dictionary drills, multiple meanings, thesaurus swaps | Finds 4 of 5 words; picks the right numbered meaning 4 of 5 times; improves 4 sentences with stronger synonyms |
| 13 | 6 | Lesson 06: Fluency with purpose — accuracy, rate, expression, self-correction | Reads the poem "Rainy-Day Porch" with expression marks honored; self-corrects 2+ errors using context |
| 14 | 6 | Project workshop 1: Evidence Case File — draft 3+ cases | 3 cases each with question, quoted evidence, and answer or inference |
| 15 | 6 | Project workshop 2 + review game: inference relay | Completes 6-case file; team scores ≥7/10 in the relay |
| 16 | 6 | Formative quiz ([week-04_quiz](assessments/week-04_quiz.md)) + fluency check | Quiz ≥ 24/30; reads "The Empty Bowl" final timing with ≤2 uncorrected errors |

Weeks 1–2 of the track are diagnostic/placement and are not part of this
unit.

## How lessons, practice, reading, investigation, and review fill the unit

Six explicit lessons (sessions 1, 3, 5, 9, 11, 13) introduce each
detective tool; each is followed or paired with a practice session that
applies it (sessions 2, 4, 6, 7, 10, 12). Connected reading appears in
sessions 2, 4, 8, 13, and 16 — four original passages ("The Empty Bowl,"
"Beaver Builders," "The Cracked Sidewalk," "Rainy-Day Porch" and "The
Porch Light") re-read across the unit for evidence work and fluency.
The investigation is the [Evidence Case File project](project.md), built
across the unit and finished in sessions 14–15. Review is woven into
sessions 7, 13, and 15; retrieval practice opens most sessions with a
2-minute root-word or academic-word drill. Feedback happens the same day
the adult reviews practice.
