# Practice Sets A–F — Unit 01

Keyed to the [16-session pacing](README.md#16-session-pacing). Every item
is solved in the [answer key](answer-key.md). Default: 6–10 meaningful
tasks per set.

## Practice A — Evidence hunts (Session 2, after Lesson 01)

Re-read "The Empty Bowl" and "Beaver Builders" ([Lesson
01](lessons/lesson-01-evidence-quote-the-text.md)). Answer each with an
exact quote.

1. What did Maya call out when she got home?
2. What two things did the note say? (Quote both parts.)
3. Where did Maya put the bowls, and where did she sit?
4. What did Maya mutter about Leo?
5. What kind of animal is a beaver, and what does it reshape?
6. What three materials go into a dam? What does the dam create?
7. What is a lodge, and how do beavers enter it?
8. Why do beavers chew constantly?
9. **Trap:** "Maya was angry at Leo." Is there text evidence for this?
   What does the text actually say about how she felt or acted?
10. **Write one:** make up one explicit question about either passage
    that can only be answered by quoting, then answer it with the
    quote.

## Practice B — Inference cases (Session 4, after Lesson 02)

For each: state the inference, the detail (quoted or described), and
the background knowledge. Use the [window-scene
illustration](assets/inference-window-scene.png) for items 1–3 and "The
Cracked Sidewalk" ([Lesson 02](lessons/lesson-02-inference-detective.md))
for items 4–6.

1. What was the weather like just before this moment?
2. What will the girl probably do next with the umbrella?
3. How does the dog feel about being wet?
4. Why did Mr. Abernathy smile at the missing tomatoes?
5. What does "PLEASE LET THEM RIPEN" suggest about past years?
6. Why is the basket by the *front gate* and not by the back door?
7. **Busted theory:** "The rainbow means it will rain more." Check it
   against detail + knowledge. Keep or drop? Why?
8. **Reverse detective:** inference — "Mr. Abernathy expected the
   tomatoes to be taken." Supply two supporting details.

## Practice C — Root sort and syllable surgery (Session 6, after Lesson 03)

Use the unit root table ([Lesson
03](lessons/lesson-03-greek-latin-roots-affixes.md)).

1. Sort into root families (tele-, photo-, graph-, auto-, phon-,
   scope-, geo-, port-): telephone, photograph, autograph, telescope,
   microscope, portable, geography, microphone, telegraph, transport,
   symphony, paragraph.
2. Break and read: *television, periscope, biography, import*.
3. For each word in item 2, assemble the meaning from its parts.
4. Build: "see around" → ? ; "sound together" → ? ; "carry out" → ?
5. **Meaning test:** does *port-* = "carry" explain *important*? Show
   the check and the verdict.
6. Add 4 new words to your root keyring (from books or the unit sets),
   each broken, read, and defined.
7. **Speed sort:** the adult shuffles 12 root-word cards; sort by root
   in under 2 minutes with ≤1 error.

## Practice D — Mixed detective review (Session 7)

One mixed set across the three skills; aim for ≥80%.

1. (Evidence) "What did Elena do with the cracker crumb?" Quote it.
   ("The Porch Light," Lesson 06.)
2. (Inference) Why did Elena switch on the porch light? Detail +
   knowledge.
3. (Word parts) Break *automatic* and assemble its meaning.
4. (Evidence) "What hung on a hook, still dark with water?" Quote it.
5. (Inference) What time of day is it in "The Porch Light"? Detail +
   knowledge.
6. (Word parts) Which root is shared by *photograph* and
   *paragraph*? What does it mean?
7. (Evidence) "Where did the ants march?" Quote it.
8. (Inference) How does Elena feel about the ants? Detail +
   knowledge.
9. (Word parts) *Export* — break, read, assemble.
10. **Case review:** pick your weakest answer above; redo it with the
    full detective routine and note what changed.

## Practice E — Context-clue investigations (Session 10, after Lesson 04)

Name the clue type (definition / example / restatement), point to the
signal words, and give the meaning.

1. The *parched*, completely dried-out riverbed cracked in the sun.
2. Reptiles, such as turtles, snakes, and lizards, are *cold-blooded*.
3. The *summit*, or top of the mountain, was covered in snow.
4. She felt *reluctant*, unwilling, to leave the warm house.
5. Pack light layers, like T-shirts and light sweaters, for the
   *variable* spring weather.
6. His *vivid*, bright and lifelike imagination made stories feel
   real.
7. The *fragile*, easily broken vase sat on the highest shelf.
8. Desert travelers, for example camels' drivers and guides, know the
   *oases* well. (Bonus: what does the example list tell you about
   *oases*?)
9. **Write two:** author two sentences, each hiding a different clue
   type for the words *sturdy* and *vacant*; label the type and
   signal.
10. **Clue hunt:** find one real definition, example, or restatement
    clue in any book at home; copy the sentence and label it.

## Practice F — Dictionary drills and thesaurus swaps (Session 12, after Lesson 05)

Use a print beginning dictionary or adult-supervised
[Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/rebuild)
lookup (entry format verified 2026-10-11).

1. Look up *brisk*: entry word, part of speech, and the numbered
   meaning that fits "a brisk walk."
2. Look up *cautious*: which numbered meaning fits "a cautious driver"?
   What ruled the others out?
3. Look up *enormous*: copy one example phrase from the entry.
4. **Multiple meanings:** the word *fair* — find two different
   meanings; write one sentence for each.
5. **Thesaurus swap:** upgrade "The *fast* river made a *loud* noise."
   with two stronger synonyms, each meaning-checked.
6. **Thesaurus trap:** a thesaurus lists *slim* as a synonym for
   *small*. Would "a *slim* puppy" work? Why or why not?
7. Use *predict* and *observe* in two sentences about "Beaver
   Builders."
8. **Glossary race:** using a 6-word mini-glossary the adult provides,
   find and read aloud the definitions of *cite* and *explicit* in
   under a minute each.
