# Lesson 06 — Fluency with Purpose: Accuracy, Rate, Expression, Self-Correction

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can read aloud so a listener understands
and feels the text — and fix myself when something sounds wrong."
**Time:** 30–35 minutes · **Materials:** this lesson; the poem and
passage below; pencil and paper.
**Standards:** RF.4.4.a, RF.4.4.b, RF.4.4.c.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult reads one line two ways — flat and robot-like, then with
feeling: "Maya sighed, filled the bowl with kibble." "Which reading
helped you *feel* Maya's evening? Fluency is reading so the meaning
comes through."

## Explicit teaching

Fluent reading has four dials:

- **Accuracy** — the words right, no skips or swaps.
- **Rate** — not too fast, not too slow; like talking to a friend.
- **Expression** — your voice matches the meaning (excited parts lift,
  sad parts soften, questions rise).
- **Purpose** — you know *why* you are reading (here: to prove you
  understand and to enjoy the language).

And the detective's safety net: **self-correction**. When something
sounds wrong, you stop, reread, and fix it using **context** — the
words around it. Signs you need a fix: the sentence stops making sense,
a word you "read" is not a real word, or your voice and the punctuation
disagree (you read a question flat).

The repeated-reading method: read once for accuracy, read again for
smoothness, read a third time for expression. Each pass has a job.

### The unit's fluency texts (original, written for this unit)

**"Rainy-Day Porch" (poem)**

> Rain on the roof goes *pitter-pat, pat,*
> The dog shakes dry on the welcome mat.
> Mud on the boots by the open door —
> Who tracked the whole outdoors across the floor?
>
> Clouds roll off like a closing lid,
> Blue sky peeks out — the sun unhid!
> A rainbow leans where the rain has been…
> Quick! Name the clues. What have you seen?

**"The Porch Light" (prose)**

> After the rain, Elena wiped her boots on the mat and studied the
> porch. Muddy footprints led from the steps to the door, and a damp
> umbrella leaned against the railing, dripping. Her little brother's
> raincoat hung on a hook, still dark with water. "Someone had quite
> the adventure," she said, following the footprints with her eyes. On
> the railing, a line of ants marched toward a forgotten cracker crumb.
> Elena smiled, flicked the crumb into the grass, and switched on the
> porch light — a small yellow welcome in the gray afternoon.

### Modeled example 1 — expression marks

*Adult reads the poem's third stanza aloud twice:* first flat, then
honoring the marks — the dash in "Blue sky peeks out — the sun unhid!"
gets a lift and a grin; "Quick!" is quick and bright; the final
question rises. "The punctuation is the author's stage directions.
Dashes, exclamation points, and question marks tell your voice what to
do."

### Modeled example 2 — self-correction aloud

*Adult reads:* "Muddy *footprints* led from the steps to the door, and
a damp umbrella leaned against the *railing*…" then deliberately
misreads: "…leaned against the *raining*." *Adult stops:* "Wait —
'leaned against the raining'? That is not a thing. Reread: r-ai-l-i-n-g
… *railing*. The sentence makes sense now. I caught it because my ears
heard nonsense. That is self-correction — the detective's net."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 6 tasks)

1. **First read (accuracy):** the learner reads "The Porch Light"
   aloud; the adult tallies errors without interrupting.
2. **Fix pass:** for each error, the learner rereads the sentence and
   self-corrects using context before the adult supplies the word.
3. **Second read (smoothness):** the learner re-reads the same
   passage; the adult notes which phrases now flow.
4. **Expression marks:** the learner reads the poem's last stanza,
   honoring the dash, exclamation, and question mark; the adult
   models any missed mark once, then the learner retries.
5. **Rate check:** the adult times one poem stanza; together they
   judge: rushed, dragging, or "like talking"? The learner adjusts
   and re-reads.
6. **Error-analysis:** the adult reads "a line of ants marched toward
   a forgotten cracker *crumb*" as "*crumbs*" and keeps going. "Did I
   need to fix that?" (It changes singular to plural — small, but a
   detective reads what is there; fix and reread.)

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 6 tasks)

1. **Third read (expression):** the learner reads "The Porch Light"
   with feeling — Elena's smile warm, the discovery curious. The
   adult scores 1–3 on expression.
2. "The Porch Light" once more, self-correcting at least 2 errors
   (real or planted by the adult) using context, each fix narrated:
   "That didn't make sense because…"
3. Read the poem's first stanza twice; the second read must be
   smoother — the learner names what improved.
4. **Punctuation hunt:** the learner finds 3 marks in the two texts
   that change how the voice should sound, and demonstrates each.
5. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "Why read the same text three times
   instead of three different texts?" (Each pass has a job —
   accuracy, then smoothness, then expression; familiarity frees
   attention for meaning.)
6. **Fluency log:** the learner records today's error counts
   (read 1 vs. read 3) in the Evidence Case File — data for the
   session-16 fluency check.

## Application

"Porch performance." The learner performs "Rainy-Day Porch" for the
adult — standing, poem in hand, expression marks honored, one
self-correction narrated if needed. The adult scores with the lesson's
simple rubric (accuracy / rate / expression / self-correction, 1–2
each) and the learner files the score in the case file.

## Exit check

The adult points to any unpracticed paragraph of "The Porch Light."
The learner reads it aloud once, then answers: "What was your one
fix, and what told you to fix it?" Success: the read is
understandable with ≤2 uncorrected errors, and the learner can name
the context signal behind at least one self-correction.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** the adult reads first as a model
  (echo reading); difficult words are pre-taught; the learner may
  read along, not solo.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may record the reading
  (audio only, kept private) and self-score with the adult, or mark
  expression symbols on the text before reading.
- **Extension:** the learner writes two more lines for "Rainy-Day
  Porch" that keep the rhyme and rhythm, then performs the extended
  poem.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconception:** speed = fluency. Slow an over-fast reader
with the "talk to a friend" test; speed up a word-by-word reader with
phrase-chunking (draw scoops under phrases). **Answer-key reference:**
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-06). Fluency is never graded on
a single cold read — the unit's checks always allow the repeated-read
passes first.
