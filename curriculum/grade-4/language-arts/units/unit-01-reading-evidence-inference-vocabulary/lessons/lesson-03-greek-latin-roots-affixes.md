# Lesson 03 — Word-Part Detectives: Greek and Latin Roots Unlock Big Words

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can break a big word into its root and
parts, read it piece by piece, and figure out what it means."
**Time:** 30–35 minutes · **Materials:** this lesson; the unit word
sets ([unit README](../README.md#unit-word-sets-authored-for-this-unit-reused-across-lessons-and-practice));
word cards the adult writes by hand; pencil and paper.
**Standards:** RF.4.3, RF.4.3.a; L.4.4.b.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult writes *telephone* and asks: "This looks long, but you
already know two secret pieces. *Tele* means 'far' and *phon* means
'sound.' What does a telephone do?" (Carries sound far.) "Today every
long word is a locked box, and roots are the keys."

## Explicit teaching

A **root** is a word piece that carries the core meaning — many English
roots come from Greek and Latin, languages spoken thousands of years
ago. An **affix** is a piece added before (**prefix**) or after
(**suffix**) the root that changes the meaning. The detective method:

1. **Find the root.** Cover the beginning and end; the meaningful
   middle is usually the root.
2. **Name what each part means.** Say each piece's meaning out loud.
3. **Break into syllables and read.** Every syllable has one vowel
   sound — break, read each part, blend.
4. **Assemble the meaning.** Root meaning + affix meanings ≈ the
   word's meaning. Then check: does it make sense in the sentence?

Today's eight keys (Greek or Latin, with the memory hook):

| Root | Means | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| tele- | far | *telephone* — sound from far away |
| photo- | light | *photograph* — writing with light |
| graph- | write | *autograph* — self-writing (your signature) |
| auto- | self | *automobile* — moves by itself (no horses!) |
| phon- | sound | *microphone* — small sound made big |
| scope- | see | *telescope* — see far things |
| geo- | earth | *geography* — writing about the earth |
| port- | carry | *transport* — carry across |

### Modeled example 1 — telescope

*Adult writes* telescope. "Find the root pieces: *tele-* ('far') +
*scope* ('see'). Two pieces, two vowel sounds per piece — tel-e-scope,
three syllables: tel | e | scope. Read: *tel-uh-scope*. Assemble: 'see
far' — a tool for seeing far things. Check in a sentence: 'Galileo
pointed his telescope at the moon.' Does 'see-far tool' make sense?
Yes."

### Modeled example 2 — photograph, then autograph

*Adult writes* photograph. "Pieces: *photo-* ('light') + *graph*
('write'). Pho-to-graph — three syllables. Assemble: 'light-writing' —
a picture written by light. That is exactly what a camera does."
*Adult writes* autograph. "Pieces: *auto-* ('self') + *graph*
('write'). Au-to-graph. Assemble: 'self-writing' — writing by
yourself, your own signature. Notice *graph* shows up in both words —
roots are repeat keys that open many boxes."

### Modeled example 3 — portable (affix + root + syllables)

*Adult writes* portable. "Root *port-* ('carry'), suffix *-able*
('can be'). Port-a-ble — three syllables. Assemble: 'can be carried.'
Check: 'a portable radio' — a radio you can carry. The suffix *-able*
always whispers 'can be.'"

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 6 tasks)

1. "Break *microphone* into pieces and syllables." (*micro-* "small" +
   *phon* "sound" — mi-cro-phone. A small sound device; adult notes
   *micro-* is a bonus prefix meaning "small.")
2. "What does *telegraph* mean? Use the keys." (*tele-* far + *graph*
   write — writing sent far away; old-time texting.)
3. **Syllable surgery:** "Break *geography* and read it." (ge-og-ra-phy
   — four syllables; *geo* earth + *graph* write: writing about the
   earth.)
4. "Build a word: 'carry across' + the root *port*." (*transport* —
   trans- is a bonus prefix meaning "across"; trans-port.)
5. **Error-analysis:** the adult "reads" *photograph* as "photo-graf"
   with a hard *f* at the end and the wrong stress: PHO-to-graf. "Did
   that sound right?" (No — *ph* says /f/, and the word is
   PHO-tuh-graf; also *graph* is spelled with *ph*. Fix the blend and
   re-read.)
6. "Which two unit words share the root *auto-*? What does each mean?"
   (*autograph* — self-writing, signature; *automobile* — self-moving
   vehicle; *automatic* — working by itself.)

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 6 tasks)

1. Break, read, and define: *periscope, symphony*. (per-i-scope —
   *peri-* "around" + *scope* "see": see around; sym-pho-ny —
   *sym-* "together" + *phon* "sound": sounds together.)
2. Break, read, and define: *import, export*. (im-port, ex-port —
   carry in, carry out.)
3. "List every unit word containing *tele-* and tell what the words
   have in common." (telephone, television, telescope, telegraph —
   all involve distance.)
4. Build-a-word: combine *photo* + *graph* + *-y*? No — instead:
   "What word means 'write about one's own life'?" (*autobiography* —
   *auto* self + *bio* life + *graph* write; adult supplies *bio-*
   "life" as a bonus key.)
5. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "Why does knowing *port-* = 'carry' help
   you read *transport* on the first try?" (You expect the *port* chunk
   and its sound; the word breaks itself.)
6. Speed round: the adult shows 6 root-word cards from the unit set;
   the learner breaks, reads, and states the assembled meaning.
   Record the count correct.

## Application

"Root keyring." The learner makes 8 key cards — one per root in today's
table. Each card shows the root, its meaning, the hook word, and one
*new* word from the unit set that uses it (checked by breaking and
reading). The keyring lives in the Evidence Case File and is used in
every later word task.

## Exit check

The adult writes two unpracticed unit words — for example, *microscope*
and *television*. The learner breaks each into pieces and syllables,
reads it, and assembles the meaning. Success: both read correctly with
pieces named and sensible meanings.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** the adult reads each word aloud first;
  roots may be color-coded on cards (root in one color, affixes in
  another); syllable beats can be tapped.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may point to pieces, clap
  syllables, or dictate the assembly for the adult to write.
- **Extension:** hunt for *tele-*, *photo-*, *graph-*, *auto-*,
  *phon-*, *scope-*, *geo-*, *port-* in books at home; log three new
  finds with assembled meanings in the case file.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconception:** letter-matching without the meaning test
("port" in *important* is not the carry-root — it fails the meaning
check; teach the removal/meaning test: does 'carry' make sense in
*important*? No → not this root). **Answer-key reference:**
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-03). Keep Greek/Latin (meaning)
separate from pure syllable-breaking until both are solid — the unit
sets overlap on purpose.
