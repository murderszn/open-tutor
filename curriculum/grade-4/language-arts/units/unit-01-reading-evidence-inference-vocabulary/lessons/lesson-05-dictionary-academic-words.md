# Lesson 05 — Dictionary Detective and Academic Words

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can look up a word, pick the right meaning,
and use strong academic words in my own speaking and writing."
**Time:** 30–35 minutes · **Materials:** this lesson; a print
beginning dictionary or adult-supervised digital dictionary
([Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/rebuild)
entry format, verified 2026-10-11); pencil and paper.
**Standards:** L.4.4.c; L.4.6.

## Readiness check (1–2 min)

The adult writes *bank* and asks: "A river bank or a money bank — which
does the writer mean?" (Depends on the sentence.) "Context clues get
you close; today you learn to **verify** — check the exact meaning and
pick the right one. Detectives verify."

## Explicit teaching

A **dictionary entry** is a word's case file. Learn to read it:

- **Entry word** (often with syllable breaks: *re·build*)
- **Part of speech** (noun, verb, adjective…)
- **Numbered meanings** — one word can have several; the sentence
  decides which number fits
- **Example sentence** showing the word in action

A **glossary** is a mini-dictionary at the back of a book — same skill,
smaller file. A **thesaurus** lists **synonyms** (same-meaning words)
and antonyms — it upgrades your word choice but never replaces checking
the meaning.

**Multiple-meaning words** are the reason you never grab meaning #1
blindly. And **academic vocabulary** — words like *evidence,
inference, predict, observe, conclude, compare, describe* — are the
precise tools of school thinking. A detective who says "the thing
shows the stuff" gets ignored; one who says "the evidence supports my
inference" gets believed.

### Modeled example 1 — reading an entry

*Adult looks up* rebuild *(entry format verified 2026-10-11):* "Entry
word *rebuild*, transitive verb. Meaning 1a: 'to make extensive repairs
to' — example, 'rebuild a war-torn city.' Meaning 1b: 'to restore to a
previous state' — 'rebuild inventories.' Meaning 2: 'to make extensive
changes in' — 'rebuild society.' Sentence check: 'After the flood, the
town had to rebuild the bridge.' Which number? The bridge was damaged,
so 1a — repairs. I do not pick 2 ('changes in society') — the sentence
rules it out."

### Modeled example 2 — multiple meanings

*Adult looks up* light *in a beginning dictionary:* "Meanings include:
1. the brightness that lets us see; 2. not heavy; 3. pale in color.
Sentence: 'She packed a light bag for the trip.' Number 2 — not heavy.
A careless detective grabs #1 and gets nonsense: 'a brightness bag.'
The sentence is the judge."

### Modeled example 3 — thesaurus upgrade

"Original sentence: 'The dog was *big*.' Thesaurus synonyms for *big*:
*enormous, massive, huge, gigantic.* Upgraded: 'The dog was
*enormous*.' But — check the meaning first: *enormous* fits a dog;
*massive* might overstate it. A thesaurus suggests; the dictionary
confirms."

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 6 tasks)

1. "Look up *cautious*. What part of speech, and which meaning fits:
   'Be cautious near the edge'?" (Adjective; the "careful, watchful"
   meaning — not any other numbered sense.)
2. "Look up *triumph*. Read the entry parts aloud." (Noun/verb; "a
   great victory" — matches Lesson 04's clue.)
3. **Wrong-number trap:** the adult "looks up" *vacant* and picks
   "not thinking; empty-headed" for the sentence "a vacant lot."
   The learner corrects: (Wrong number — the lot needs "empty, not
   occupied." The sentence judges.)
4. "Use a thesaurus to upgrade: 'The *nice* garden grew *big*
   tomatoes.'" (Accept: "The *lovely* garden grew *enormous*
   tomatoes" or similar — each swap must survive a meaning check.)
5. "Which academic word fits: 'I ___ that it will rain because the
   clouds are dark'?" (*predict* — say why: a forecast from evidence.)
6. "Fix the vague sentence with two academic words: 'The story thing
   shows she is sad.'" (Accept: "The *evidence* shows she is sad" /
   "The *details* *describe* her sadness" — precise words named.)

## Independent practice (learner tries; adult observes, 6 tasks)

1. Look up *sturdy*; copy the entry word, part of speech, and the
   meaning number that fits "a sturdy bridge." 
2. Look up *shimmer*; which meaning fits "the shimmer of the lake"?
3. **Glossary drill:** the adult provides a 5-word mini-glossary
   (evidence, inference, explicit, cite, detail); the learner finds
   *cite* and *explicit* in under a minute each and reads the
   definitions aloud.
4. Upgrade with the thesaurus: "The *good* soup was *hot*." (Accept
   defensible pairs, e.g., "delicious/scalding" — meaning-checked.)
5. Write two sentences using *observe* and *conclude* correctly about
   the window-scene illustration.
6. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "Why check a dictionary *after* trying
   context clues and word parts?" (Clues get you close; the dictionary
   verifies — detectives confirm before they report.)

## Application

"Word warrant cards." The learner makes 8 academic-word cards
(*evidence, inference, cite, predict, observe, conclude, compare,
describe*): word, learner-written definition (verified against the
dictionary), and an original sentence. Cards join the Evidence Case
File and must be used — at least 4 of them — in the project write-ups.

## Exit check

The adult gives one new sentence with a multiple-meaning word (e.g.,
"The pitcher *poured* the water slowly" vs. "a baseball *pitcher*").
The learner looks it up, names the part of speech and the right
numbered meaning, and explains what ruled the other meanings out.
Success: correct meaning chosen with the sentence-based reason stated.

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** the adult navigates the dictionary;
  beginning-dictionary "kids' definitions" are preferred; the learner
  may answer orally.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may match pre-printed
  entry slips to sentences instead of writing full entries.
- **Extension:** "etymology corner" — look up the word history of one
  unit root word (e.g., *telephone*: Greek *tele-* + *phon-*) and add
  it to the root keyring from Lesson 03.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconception:** dictionary as first resort (skipping the
detective tools), or grabbing numbered meaning #1. Enforce the order:
clue → parts → verify. **Answer-key reference:**
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-05). Note for the adult: the
Merriam-Webster entry pages also show auto-compiled "recent examples
from the web" — scroll to the numbered dictionary definitions, not the
news examples, when verifying with the learner.
