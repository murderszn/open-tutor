# Resource Pack — Unit 01: Geographic Tools, U.S. Regions, and Map Evidence

**Goal (one sentence):** Give the guiding adult short, grade-4-appropriate
videos, references, and search routes that reinforce map parts and scale,
the four Census regions, the five oceans, state capitals, time zones, and
reading the states dataset.
**Age fit:** grade 4 (ages 9–10); the adult previews every video and
co-views with the learner.

**Fact-check record (2026-10-11):** four Census regions (Northeast,
Midwest, South, West) verified against the Census Bureau's Regions and
Divisions page (state lists: Northeast 9, Midwest 12, South 16 + DC, West
13); five oceans verified against NOAA ("How many oceans are there?" —
historically four named oceans; the U.S. now recognizes the Southern
(Antarctic) as the fifth); time-zone rule of thumb (one zone west = one
hour earlier) verified against the Learning Resources time-zones
explainer; dataset columns and values verified against
`resources/us_states.csv` on main (see Lesson 05). **Caveat for every
video below:** many children's videos teach a *five*-region U.S. system
(Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Southwest, West); this unit teaches the
Census Bureau's *four* regions (Northeast, Midwest, South, West), which
match the dataset's `subregion` column — the adult names the difference
out loud whenever a video splits the South or West.

## Web references

1. **Census Bureau — "Regions and Divisions" (Geographic Levels)** —
   https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/economic-census/guidance-geographies/levels.html
   *Why good:* the authoritative list of the four Census regions and
   which states sit in each — the system behind the dataset's
   `subregion` column and Lesson 03's sort. Adult background. *Found
   via search 2026-10-11; free, no account.*
2. **Britannica Kids — "United States: regions"** —
   https://kids.britannica.com/kids/assembly/view/241450
   *Why good:* kid-readable page stating the official Census regions
   are the West, the South, the Midwest, and the Northeast. The adult
   may read it aloud with the learner. *Found via search 2026-10-11;
   free content visible without login.*
3. **NOAA — "How many oceans are there?"** —
   http://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/howmanyoceans.html
   *Why good:* the authoritative source for the five-ocean list
   (Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic, Southern); short, plain
   language. Best for the adult's background and for answering "why
   five?" *Found via search 2026-10-11; free, no account.*
4. **Learning Resources — "Learning About Time Zones for Kids"** —
   https://www.learningresources.com/blog/learning-about-time-zones-for-kids/
   *Why good:* kid-facing explainer confirming the rotation model and
   the U.S. zones (including the Alaska/Hawaii examples) — backs
   Lesson 04's investigation. *Found via search 2026-10-11; free, no
   account.*
5. **National Geographic Education — "Map" (reference)** —
   https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/map/
   *Why good:* explains what maps are, symbols/keys, and scale — adult
   background for Lessons 01–02. General-audience reading level: adult
   reads and adapts; not assigned to the learner. *Verified in the
   sibling Grade 3 U01 delivery (2026-10-10); free, no account.*
