# Unit Assessment — Geographic Tools, U.S. Regions, and Map Evidence

**Coverage:** all six unit objectives (Lessons 01–05). **Time:** about 35
minutes, or two shorter sittings. The adult may read any item aloud;
spoken, drawn, and pointed answers are full response modes. Answers are
in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) — never beside the questions.

**Text-only note:** items 7–8 use the unit's illustration. A full
text-only alternative is printed with each item, so the assessment works
without the picture.

**Part 1 — Map tools (Objectives 1)**

1. A new student joins your class. Explain in 2–3 sentences what a
   map's **key** and **compass rose** do, and why a geographer needs
   both.
2. On the U.S. map: (a) Is Miami north or south of Chicago? (b) Name the
   two pieces of map evidence for your answer.
3. A map's scale bar says 1 cm = 200 miles. The map distance from Dallas
   to Atlanta measures 4 cm. About how far apart are the cities? Show
   the three steps (read, measure, multiply).

**Part 2 — Regions and environments (Objectives 2, 6)**

4. Name the four U.S. Census regions.
5. The dataset's `subregion` column says: Colorado → "United States
   West"; Wisconsin → "United States Midwest." Which Census region is
   each state in?
6. Write a compelling geographic question about ONE U.S. region, plus
   two supporting questions that would help answer it.
7. Look at the unit's illustration
   ([four-us-regions-landscapes.png](../assets/four-us-regions-landscapes.png)).
   (a) Which panel best matches the **South**? (b) Name two visible
   clues and explain what each clue tells you about the South's
   environment.
   *Text-only alternative: Panel A — rocky coastline with a lighthouse,
   pine trees, a sailboat on gray-blue water. Panel B — red barn and
   silo with flat cornfields under a big cloudy sky. Panel C —
   moss-draped cypress trees, still dark water, a heron at the water's
   edge, warm golden light. Panel D — red-rock mesas, a tall saguaro
   cactus, dry bushes, bright blue sky. (a) Which panel best matches
   the South? (b) Name two clues from its description and explain what
   each tells you about the South's environment.*
8. Using the illustration: (a) Which panel best matches the **West**?
   (b) In 2–3 sentences, explain how the panel's landforms connect to
   the West's location, naming your evidence.
   *Text-only alternative: same four panel descriptions as item 7.*

**Part 3 — Oceans, capitals, time zones (Objectives 3, 4)**

9. Name the five oceans. In one sentence, explain why the United States
   counts five instead of four, citing the source the unit used.
10. Look up two state capitals in the dataset (your choice, from
    different regions). Write each state, its capital, and whether the
    capital is also the state's biggest city.
11. It is 11:00 AM in Chicago (Central). (a) What time is it in Los
    Angeles (Pacific)? (b) What time is it in New York (Eastern)?
    (c) In two sentences, explain WHY the three cities show different
    times, using the words *rotation* and *time zone*.

**Part 4 — Data (Objective 5)**

12. What does `population_approx` mean? Why must you write "about"
    before the number?
13. Compare **Montana** and **Illinois** using the dataset: (a) which
    has the larger area — give both numbers; (b) which is more crowded
    — give both density numbers; (c) in one sentence, explain what this
    comparison proves about the claim "bigger states have more people."
14. Name one question the dataset CAN answer and one it CANNOT, and
    explain the difference in one sentence each.

**Scoring:** 14 items; items 1, 6, 8b, 11c, 13c, 14 are worth 2 points
(explanation quality), the rest 1 point — 20 points total. See
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) for acceptable responses and the
teacher guide for reteaching signals.
