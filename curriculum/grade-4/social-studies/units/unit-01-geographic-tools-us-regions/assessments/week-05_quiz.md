# Formative Quiz — End of Week 5

**Coverage:** Lessons 01–04 (map tools, scale/grids, regions, oceans and
time zones). **Time:** about 20 minutes. The adult may read any item
aloud. Answers are in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) — never beside
the questions.

**Text-only note:** item 4 uses the unit's illustration. The full
text-only alternative is printed below the item, so the quiz works
without the picture.

1. Name the six parts of a useful map.
2. A map's key says a red star means "state capital." You see a red star
   on the label "Austin." What city is it, and what is it the capital
   of? What is your map evidence?
3. On the U.S. map: is Chicago east or west of Denver? Name your map
   evidence.
4. Look at the unit's illustration
   ([four-us-regions-landscapes.png](../assets/four-us-regions-landscapes.png)).
   Which panel shows a landscape shaped mostly by DRY conditions? Name
   two visible clues.
   *Text-only alternative: Panel A — rocky coastline, lighthouse, pines,
   sailboat. Panel B — red barn, silo, flat cornfields. Panel C —
   moss-draped cypress, still dark water, heron. Panel D — red-rock
   mesas, saguaro cacti, bright dry sky. Which panel is shaped mostly by
   dry conditions? Name two visible clues from its description.*
5. Sort these states into Census regions: Washington, Alabama, Maine,
   Kansas.
6. Name the five oceans. Which one do older maps sometimes leave out?
7. It is 1:00 PM in New York (Eastern). What time is it in Denver
   (Mountain)? Show your thinking.
8. A friend says, "The capital of California must be Los Angeles because
   it is the biggest city." What is wrong with this reasoning, and how
   would you check?
9. A map's scale bar says 1 cm = 100 miles. Two cities measure 4 cm
   apart on the map. About how far apart are they in real life?
10. In one or two sentences: why do different U.S. cities show different
    times at the same moment?
