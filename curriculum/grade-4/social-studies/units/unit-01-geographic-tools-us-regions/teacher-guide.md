# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Geographic Tools, U.S. Regions, and Map Evidence

**For the guiding adult.** Student materials never contain answers; all
solutions live in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md). This guide gives
session-by-session direction, misconception alerts, and reteaching moves.

## How to use this unit

- **Pace:** 4 sessions/week, ~35 minutes. Each lesson is written for 1–2
  sessions; slow down rather than skip the investigation sessions (Week 3
  S3, Week 5 S3) — they are where the tools become habits.
- **Your role:** read teaching sections aloud, model the worked examples
  with your finger on the map, and require evidence for every claim
  ("show me the map part that proves it"). Scribe or read aloud whenever
  the learner needs it — the thinking is the goal, not the handwriting.
- **Dataset:** you hold `resources/us_states.csv`. Read rows aloud; never
  ask the learner to open the raw file. Always name the column you used.

## Misconceptions — quick reference

1. **"Up is north."** Require the compass-rose check every time.
2. **"The key is the same on every map."** Every map's key is read fresh.
3. **"About 800 miles" feels wrong.** Geographers estimate from maps;
   exactness lives on the ground.
4. **One shared feature = a match** (Panel D → Northeast "because
   mountains"). Evidence weighs ALL clues.
5. **The sun moves around Earth.** The globe-and-lamp model fixes this
   physically — let the learner turn the globe.
6. **Bigger state = more people.** Montana/Rhode Island exists to break
   this; area and population are different columns.
7. **Rounded numbers are exact.** "About" stays attached to every
   `population_approx` reading.
8. **Data proves opinions.** The dataset proves comparisons; "best state"
   is not in any column.

## Reteaching moves

- **Map parts shaky (Quiz items 1–3 < 2 pts):** replay Lesson 01's worked
  examples on a new map; make the learner teach YOU the six parts.
- **Scale/grid shaky (Quiz item 9, Practice A4/A7 wrong):** re-do Lesson
  02 with a hand-drawn map where YOU set a friendly scale (1 cm = 10
  steps); measure the room together.
- **Regions shaky (Quiz items 4–5 wrong):** re-sort 12 states with physical
  cards into four labeled boxes; re-match the four illustration panels
  with the text-only alternative.
- **Time zones shaky (Quiz items 7, 10 wrong):** re-run the lamp model;
  practice with only Eastern/Central first, then add Mountain/Pacific.
- **Data shaky (Practice D wrong):** work one comparison fully aloud
  together ("Texas vs. Florida"), naming every column before any
  conclusion.

## Readiness signals

- **Ready for Unit 02:** the learner sorts any 8 states into regions
  from the `subregion` column without prompting, estimates a map
  distance with a scale bar, and converts a time between two U.S. zones
  — each with evidence named.
- **Needs more time:** the learner still guesses capitals, treats
  rounded numbers as exact, or cannot name map evidence for a claim.
  Repeat Week 3 S3–S4 style investigations with new cities before
  moving on; Unit 02 reuses every tool taught here.

## Assessment scoring notes

- **Formative quiz (10 items):** 10 points, 1 each. Items 2, 3, 4, 7, 8
  require the evidence/reasoning, not just the answer — half credit for
  a bare correct answer with no evidence.
- **Unit assessment (20 points):** 2-point items (1, 6, 8b, 11c, 13c, 14)
  score explanation quality: 2 = correct + evidence/reasoning, 1 =
  correct but thin, 0 = wrong. 16+ = strong; 12–15 = developing, reteach
  the missed objective's lesson; below 12 = re-teach the unit's tool
  lessons (01, 02, 05) before Unit 02.
- **Project (12 points):** see [project.md](project.md) rubric. A strong
  project is the best evidence the unit's habits transferred.
- These are draft, educator-scored assessments — not standardized or
  validated instruments.
