# Project — Region Data Explorer: Five States, One Story

**Type:** investigation with a built product · **Time:** Weeks 5–6 (about
3 sessions plus a project share) · **Materials:** the `us_states.csv`
dataset (adult side); a U.S. map; paper or poster board, markers, a
ruler; the learner's four region profiles from Lesson 03

**Built on the kept assignment:** this project runs the track's kept
[Five States report](../../assignments/creed-five-states-report.md) as
its engine and adds the region-analysis layer. The learner's completed
report stays private; this page is the reusable prompt.

## The mission

You are a **region data explorer**. Pick **5 states you have never
visited**, from **at least two different Census regions**. Investigate
them with the dataset, map them, and answer the big question: **what
does the data say about how your states' regions shape them?**

## Steps

1. **Choose** your 5 states (adult approves; none you have visited; at
   least two regions represented).
2. **Ask** first: write your compelling geographic question (from Lesson
   03, or a new one about your states' regions) plus two supporting
   questions. Your questions steer the investigation.
3. **Gather**: for each state, copy these six columns into a comparison
   table — `capital`, `population_approx`, `area_sq_mi`,
   `population_density_per_sq_mi`, `statehood_year`, `state_fact`. Write
   "about" before every approximate number.
4. **Map**: on a U.S. outline map, label your 5 states, mark each capital
   with a star (check the key habit from Lesson 01!), and color or
   outline each state's region. Add a title, key, and compass rose.
5. **Compare**: answer the four "detective" questions — biggest by area,
   most people, most crowded (highest density), oldest statehood year —
   each with the column name and numbers.
6. **Explain**: write a 4–6 sentence "What the data says" section. For
   at least two of your states, connect a data fact to its region's
   environment (Lesson 03 profiles help: "Arizona's low density fits the
   West's deserts and mountains…"). Every claim needs its evidence
   named — the column, the map part, or the profile.
7. **Reflect**: 3–5 sentences — which state sounds most interesting to
   you and why? Separate what the DATA says from what YOU feel.
8. **Share**: present to the adult (3 minutes: your map, one
   data-backed claim, your compelling question).

## Deliverables

- One comparison table (5 states × 6 columns, "about" on approximations).
- One labeled U.S. map (5 states, capitals starred, regions marked,
  title/key/compass rose).
- Detective answers (biggest, most people, most crowded, oldest — with
  columns and numbers).
- "What the data says" (4–6 sentences, ≥2 region-environment
  connections, evidence named).
- Compelling question + two supporting questions.
- Final reflection (3–5 sentences).

## Rubric (adult scores; 12 points)

| Criterion | 3 — Strong | 2 — Developing | 1 — Starting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data table | All 5 states × 6 columns correct; "about" on approximations | Table present; 1–2 copied numbers wrong or "about" missing | Missing states or columns |
| Map | 5 states labeled, capitals starred, regions marked; title, key, compass rose present | Map present; 1–2 parts missing | Missing 3+ parts |
| Detective answers | All four correct with column names and numbers | 2–3 correct, or numbers without columns | 0–1 correct |
| Explanation & questions | 4–6 sentences; ≥2 region-environment connections with evidence named; compelling + 2 supporting questions | Sentences present; connections vague or evidence unnamed | Few sentences; no evidence |

**Safety/accessibility:** all work is desk-based. The adult scribes or
reads dataset rows on request; pointing, drawing, and oral presentation
are full response modes. The learner may answer the illustration-based
items from the text-only alternative.

## What "done well" looks like (for the adult)

A strong report picks states that CONTRAST (e.g., Alaska, Rhode Island,
Texas, Vermont, Hawaii), gets every number from the named column, marks
approximations honestly, and makes at least two claims of the form "the
data shows ___, which fits the ___ region's ___ because ___." The
reflection separates evidence from opinion out loud.
