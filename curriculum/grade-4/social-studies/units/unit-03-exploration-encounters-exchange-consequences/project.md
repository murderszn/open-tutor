# Project — Encounter Exhibit

**Product:** a five-panel mini-exhibit (poster board, paper panels, or
a slide deck the adult prints) that a visitor can walk through in order.
**Work time:** Week 13, Session 3 (draft) and Week 14, Session 3
(finish and present). **Present to:** the guiding adult, or a small
adult-supervised group. Nothing from the exhibit is published from this
track without the guiding adult's review.

## The five panels

**Panel 1 — Timeline strip.** The five voyages in order (Columbus
1492, Cabot 1497, Cartier 1534, de Soto 1541, Hudson 1609 — Cartier's
voyages spanned 1534–35), with the bracket "117 years, 1492–1609" and the
Lesson 01 sentence: "Before 1492, Indigenous nations already lived
across the Americas."

**Panel 2 — Route map.** A hand-drawn Atlantic route map for one
voyage of the learner's choice, with arrows, start and landfall
labeled, and the caption: "Sought: ___. Found: ___. Peoples already
living there: ___." Never write "nobody" for the third blank; write
"peoples already home" if a nation name is not known.

**Panel 3 — Exchange chart.** Two columns (Americas → Old World;
Old World → Americas) with at least three items in each column,
including one animal and one disease row. Under the chart, one
consequence chain of three links for one item.

**Panel 4 — Two perspectives.** Two short panels for one encounter
morning: one from a sailor's viewpoint, one from a shore watcher's
viewpoint, each clearly labeled "invented teaching sentences — not a
historical document." Below them, one agreement, one difference, and
one thing neither viewpoint proves.

**Panel 5 — Claim cards.** Two claim cards from Lesson 05: one claim
about what changed, one claim about what continued. Each card follows
the frame: claim + two different evidences + "So" sentence.

## Materials

Poster board or large paper, markers, the timeline strip and route map
from Lessons 01–02, exchange item cards from Lesson 03, the claim frame
card from Lesson 05. No materials beyond ordinary classroom supplies;
no live animals, no sharp tools beyond adult-supervised scissors.

## Steps

1. **Draft (Week 13, Session 3).** Sketch all five panels on scrap
   paper. The adult checks Panel 4's "invented" label and Panel 2's
   third caption blank before any final copy.
2. **Build (Week 14, Session 3).** Copy the draft onto the exhibit
   panels in order. Check every year against the Lesson 02 table and
   every exchange item against the Lesson 03 table.
3. **Present.** Walk a visitor through the panels in order, in about
   5 minutes: timeline → route → exchange → perspectives → claims.
   End by answering one visitor question with evidence ("Which panel
   proves that?").
4. **Reflect (one paragraph or spoken answer).** Answer: "What
   changed, what continued, and what are you still unsure about?"
   The "unsure" sentence is required — good historians name what they
   do not know.

## Deliverables

- The five-panel exhibit (all five panels present, in order).
- The reflection paragraph (written or adult-scribed).
- A source note on the back of Panel 1 listing the lesson tables used
  (Lesson 02 voyage table, Lesson 03 exchange table) and the sentence:
  "Passage-style panels are invented teaching sentences, not
  historical documents."

## Rubric (12 points total; 4 criteria × 0–3)

| Criterion | 3 — Strong | 2 — Good | 1 — Beginning | 0 — Missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Timeline and route accuracy | All five voyages in order with correct years; route arrows, start, and landfall labeled; sought/found kept separate | One year or label wrong; sought/found mostly separate | Two or more errors; sought and found swapped | No timeline or route panel |
| Exchange completeness and honesty | At least 3 items each way, including an animal and a disease; chain has 3 linked steps and names a harm | 3 items each way but a row missing, or chain has 2 steps | Items sorted wrongly, or harms omitted entirely | No exchange panel |
| Perspectives | Both viewpoints present and labeled invented; one agreement, one difference, and one unknown all stated | One element missing (agreement, difference, or unknown) or label missing | Only one viewpoint, or viewpoints presented as real documents | No perspectives panel |
| Claims and reflection | Both claim cards follow the frame with two different evidences each; reflection names a change, a continuation, and an uncertainty | One card breaks the two-evidence rule, or reflection misses one part | Claims given without evidence, or no reflection | No claim cards |

**Scoring guide for the adult:** 10–12 = exhibit ready to present as
is; 7–9 = present after fixing the one weakest panel; 4–6 = rework two
panels with the adult before presenting; 0–3 = rebuild with guided
support — the learner keeps the draft and tries again, never a failing
grade without a second attempt.

## Accessibility and safety

- Panels may be spoken instead of written; the adult scribes.
- The harbor illustration may be used on Panel 4 only with its
  caption ("AI illustration, not a record of a real encounter").
- Encounter, disease, and forced-labor content stays at the unit's
  plain, non-graphic level; the adult stops the presentation for
  discussion at any point.
