# Teacher Guide — Unit 03

**Audience:** the guiding adult only. Keep this guide and the
[answer key](answer-key.md) away from learner pages. This unit's
sensitive content (conquest, disease, forced labor) is adult-led at a
plain, non-graphic level; pre-read Lessons 03–05 before teaching them,
and pause for discussion whenever the learner needs it.

## Pacing and session notes

- **Week 11:** Lesson 01 (motives/tools) then Lesson 02 (timeline,
  route maps, harbor illustration). The illustration's caption must be
  read aloud: it is an AI imagining, not a record of a real encounter,
  and its dwellings/clothing are generic, not any specific nation's.
- **Week 12:** Lesson 03 (exchange). Lead the disease and forced-labor
  sections yourself; do not assign them as silent reading. Check that
  every consequence chain ends with survival/continuity, not erasure.
- **Week 13:** Lesson 04 (two invented passages — remind the learner
  at the start of each session that they are invented teaching
  passages, not historical documents), project draft, then the Week 13
  quiz.
- **Week 14:** Lesson 05 (claims), project finish and presentation,
  practice set D, then the unit assessment.

## Misconceptions and reteaching

| Misconception | What to say / reteach |
|---|---|
| "Explorers discovered empty land" | "Peoples already lived there, with governments and histories. Use 'encounter' and 'arrived,' not 'discovered.'" Re-teach Lesson 01's opening and Lesson 02's G3. |
| Sought swapped with found | Re-do one voyage's pair on the board: sought = what they hoped; found = what they actually reached. Check Lesson 02, I1. |
| "Exchange was a fair swap" | Point to the table's disease and forced-labor rows: foods are one row of three. Re-teach Lesson 03's G3. |
| "Horses changed everything instantly" | "Over time, through trade and generations." Re-teach Lesson 03's horse trace. |
| One writer's hope/fear treated as fact | Ask: "Who says so, and what did they actually see?" Re-teach Lesson 04's agree/differ/unknown sort. |
| Two "evidences" that are one fact twice | Count facts, not sentences. Re-teach Lesson 05's G2. |
| Indigenous history ending at contact | Every chain and claim needs a continuation sentence. Check project Panel 5 and assessment Task 12. |
| One death percentage quoted as fact | "Historians' estimates vary widely; records are incomplete." Do not accept a single pinned percentage. |

## Scoring the assessments

**Week 13 quiz (10 points):** Q1–Q6 are 1 point each (key in the
answer key). Q7: 1 point for 117 with subtraction shown. Q8: 1 point
for a non-trade motive plus a clue phrase. Q9: 1 point for naming
peoples already living in the Americas. Q10: 1 point only if both a
disease evidence and a forced-labor evidence are given.

**Unit assessment (12 tasks):** score each task as full (1), partial
(0.5), or missing (0) using the answer key's acceptable answers.
11–12 = ready for Unit 04; 8–10.5 = review the weakest objective with
its practice set, then move on; below 8 = re-teach the two weakest
objectives (use the lesson reteach rows above) and re-assess only
those tasks. Never report a single letter grade without the task
breakdown.

**Project (12 points):** use the rubric in [project.md](project.md).
10–12 = present as is; 7–9 = fix the weakest panel first; 4–6 = rework
two panels with adult support; 0–3 = rebuild with guided support and a
second attempt.

## Readiness and next steps

- **Ready for Unit 04** if the learner orders the five voyages, sorts
  exchange items by direction, compares two accounts without upgrading
  opinion to fact, and builds a claim with two different evidences.
- **Reteach before Unit 04** if sought/found are still swapped, or if
  claims still hide harms: repeat Lesson 02's applied task and Lesson
  05's claim cards with new voyages/items.
- **Unit 04 preview:** colonial regions build directly on this unit's
  claims, trade, and labor chains — keep the timeline strip and
  exchange chart for reuse; do not discard them.

## Source and accuracy notes (adult only)

- Voyage years: Columbus 1492, Cabot 1497, Cartier 1534–35, de Soto
  1541 (expedition about 1539–1542; sources differ on the end year),
  Hudson 1609 — checked 2026-10-24. If a reference the adult consults
  gives de Soto's expedition as 1539–1543, accept it and note the
  one-year variation; do not mark the learner wrong for it.
- Exchange items: Britannica Kids, "Columbian Exchange," checked
  2026-10-24. Disease totals are deliberately unpinned; the
  syphilis-origin question is unsettled and is not taught.
- The Lesson 04 passages are invented teaching passages. Real primary
  sources (Library of Congress, Columbus's writings) are adult-side
  only; never present an invented passage as a quotation.
- The generated harbor illustration is an AI reconstruction with
  generic dwellings and clothing. It must never be described as a
  depiction of a specific nation, place, or event.
