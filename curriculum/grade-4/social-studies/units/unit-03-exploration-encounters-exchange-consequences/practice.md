# Practice Sets — Unit 03

Weekly practice with hints. Answers are held in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md) — learner pages never contain answers.
The adult may read any item aloud; pointing and spoken answers are full
response modes.

## Week 11 — Timelines and routes

*After Lessons 01–02. Success: the learner orders the five voyages,
computes timeline gaps, and separates what each expedition sought from
what it found.*

**A1.** Put these voyages in order with years: Hudson, Columbus,
Cartier, Cabot, de Soto's Mississippi crossing.
*Hint: start with 1492.*

**A2.** How many years passed between Columbus (1492) and Cartier's
first voyage (1534)? Show the subtraction.
*Hint: later minus earlier.*

**A3.** How many years passed between Cabot (1497) and Hudson (1609)?
Show the subtraction.
*Hint: 1609 − 1497.*

**A4.** Which expedition sought a Northwest Passage, and what did it
find instead?
*Hint: the 1609 voyage.*

**A5.** For de Soto, write one "sought" sentence and one "found"
sentence. The two sentences must not say the same thing.
*Hint: gold and riches vs. the Mississippi River.*

**A6.** A route map shows an arrow from England to the Newfoundland
coast labeled "1497." Whose voyage is it, and what did it seek?
*Hint: the sailor's English name is Cabot.*

**A7.** Error-analysis: "A friend says the five voyages all reached
Asia." Which part is wrong, and what is the true sentence?
*Hint: none reached Asia.*

**A8.** Look at the harbor illustration in Lesson 02. Name one detail
that shows the sailors' viewpoint is NOT the picture's viewpoint, and
explain why in one sentence.
*Hint: where is the picture drawn from?*

## Week 12 — The Columbian Exchange

*After Lesson 03. Success: the learner sorts exchange items by
direction and builds consequence chains that include harms.*

**B1.** Sort these six items by direction: potato, cattle, tomato,
wheat, horse, maize. Write the two columns with three items each.
*Hint: count as you sort — 3 and 3.*

**B2.** Trace the potato: write where it came from, where it went, and
one probable effect, in three short sentences.
*Hint: Americas → Europe; cool soils.*

**B3.** Which exchange row is missing from this sentence: "The
Columbian Exchange gave both sides new foods"? Name the two missing
rows and give one example from each.
*Hint: animals and diseases.*

**B4.** Build a three-link chain for pigs: arrival → spread → effect.
Each link must be one sentence.
*Hint: pigs reproduce quickly and live off the land.*

**B5.** Why is "horses changed Plains life overnight" wrong? Rewrite
the sentence truthfully using the phrase "over time."
*Hint: spread took generations.*

**B6.** Name one crop from each direction and one probable effect for
each crop. (Two crops, two effects.)
*Hint: pick from the Lesson 03 table.*

**B7.** Error-analysis: "A friend says disease deaths can be counted
exactly from the records of the time." Why is that claim too strong?
*Hint: what do historians disagree about?*

**B8.** Write the sugarcane chain in your own words in three sentences:
crop → forced labor → profit. Use the word "forced" once and "not
free" once.
*Hint: who grew it, and who kept the money?*

## Week 13 — Sources: audience, purpose, fact, opinion

*After Lesson 04, before the quiz. Success: the learner infers
audience and purpose from inside a passage and sorts fact, opinion,
and unknown.*

**C1.** Passage A says, "I counted the canoes: twelve." Is that a
fact-shaped claim, an opinion, or unknown? Explain in one sentence.
*Hint: can it be counted?*

**C2.** Passage B says, "I think we were right to be careful." Is
that a fact or an opinion? Which word marks it as one?
*Hint: look for "I think."*

**C3.** Infer Passage B's audience in one sentence, quoting one clue
phrase from inside the passage.
*Hint: who would need to remember this morning?*

**C4.** Name one fact both passages agree on and one thing only
Passage B mentions.
*Hint: the morning itself vs. the elders.*

**C5.** Sort these four statements using only the two passages —
Fact / Opinion / Unknown: (1) The water was calm. (2) The bay will make
a good harbor. (3) The strangers came to take things. (4) Food was
offered from canoes.
*Hint: one is unknown, one is opinion, two are facts.*

**C6.** Why can't one passage alone prove what the strangers planned?
Answer in two sentences.
*Hint: plans live in heads, not in what a watcher sees.*

**C7.** Error-analysis: "A friend says Passage B proves the sailors
were dangerous." Why is that too strong? Write the weaker, truer
sentence.
*Hint: "were careful" is not "proved dangerous."*

**C8.** Write one sentence from a third viewpoint — a child watching
from the shore — that agrees with both passages on one fact and adds
one new observation. Label it "invented teaching sentence."

## Week 14 — Causes, effects, and claims

*After Lesson 05. Success: the learner builds claims with two
different evidences and names what continued.*

**D1.** Build a claim about the St. Lawrence fur trade using the
frame: claim + two different evidences + "So" sentence.
*Hint: Lesson 05's worked example is a model, not a copy — change the
claim or an evidence.*

**D2.** Name one probable cause and one probable effect of the fur
trade in the St. Lawrence region, in two sentences.
*Hint: want for pelts → trade grew.*

**D3.** Name one thing that changed and one thing that continued in
the St. Lawrence region after contact. Each needs a reason.
*Hint: use Lesson 05's three chains.*

**D4.** Error-analysis: a claim uses these evidences: (1) "Cartier
claimed land for France in 1534." (2) "France claimed the St. Lawrence
in 1534." Why do these fail the two-evidence rule? Give a genuinely
different second evidence.
*Hint: count facts, not sentences.*

**D5.** Repair this claim in 2–3 sentences: "Contact only helped
people because it brought new foods." Keep the food fact; add a harm.
*Hint: disease or forced labor.*

**D6.** Write a "probable cause / probable effect" pair about disease
in the St. Lawrence region, and finish with: "What we cannot prove
from this evidence alone is…" (complete the sentence honestly).
*Hint: exact death totals are one honest completion.*

**D7.** Which is stronger, and why in one sentence: Claim X — "Trade
changed the region" (no evidence given) vs. Claim Y — "Trade changed
the region: French traders wanted pelts, and Indigenous trappers
supplied them" (two evidences given)?
*Hint: a claim without evidence is an opinion.*

**D8.** Write a continuation sentence for your D1 claim: what stayed
true in the region after the change you claimed? One sentence.
*Hint: peoples, trade, or governance continuing.*
