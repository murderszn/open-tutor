# Practice Sets — Unit 04

The adult may read items aloud; spoken, pointed-to, and drawn answers
count. Answers are in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) — this page
contains no answers.

## Week 15 — Three regions

**A1.** Sort these colonies into regions: Massachusetts, Georgia,
Pennsylvania, Connecticut, Virginia, Delaware. Write the region
beside each.

**A2.** Which region fits? "Fertile soil, wheat fields, a port city
where flour is loaded." Give one environment clue and one work clue.

**A3.** A New England farmer and a Southern planter swap farms for a
year. Predict one difficulty each faces, and explain it with an
environment reason.

**A4.** True or false, with a correction if false: "Maine was one of
the thirteen colonies, separate from Massachusetts."

**A5.** Compare a colonial New England fishing village with a modern
fishing town: name one thing that continued and one thing that
changed. (Then/now.)

**A6.** A town has no meetinghouse, no common, and houses spread miles
apart along a river. Which region is it most likely in, and why?

**A7.** Error-analysis: "All Southern colonists lived on big
plantations." Correct the sentence and name two other Southern ways
of living.

**A8.** Draw the region strip from Lesson 01's applied task from
memory (three bands, colonies, one environment symbol and one work
symbol per band), then check it against the lesson table and fix
errors in a different color.

## Week 16 — Production chains

**B1.** Order this tobacco chain: "packed in barrels," "leaves cut
and dried," "loaded on a ship," "grown in a field." Number the steps
1–4.

**B2.** Name one natural, one human, and one physical-capital resource
for the wheat chain. Label each N, H, or P.

**B3.** A mill breaks down for a month. Name two jobs or groups hurt
beyond the miller, and explain the chain link for each.

**B4.** A rice price falls by half. Predict one decision a plantation
owner might make, and name the incentive. Then write one sentence
about who bears the cost of that decision if workers are enslaved.

**B5.** Explain specialization to a friend in two sentences, using a
New England shipbuilder and a Virginia tobacco grower as your example.

**B6.** A Middle Colony farmer can grow wheat or try to grow rice.
Use environment reasoning to advise her: which crop, and why? (Rice
needs long, warm, wet seasons and flooded fields — not a Middle
Colony pattern.)

**B7.** Error-analysis: "Profit from a plantation went to everyone who
worked on it." Correct the sentence, naming who received the profit
and who did not.

**B8.** Build a five-step chain for fish (sea to ship) with one
resource named at each step. Check backward: can each step happen
without the one before it?

## Week 17 — Trade webs and ports

**C1.** Trace a barrel of New England fish to the West Indies in four
steps, naming a worker at each step.

**C2.** Draw a trade web: New England port, Middle Colony port,
Southern port, Britain. Add one labeled arrow per connection (fish,
flour, tobacco, cloth — one good each, your choice, but each arrow
must match its origin region).

**C3.** A port town's harbor silts up so big ships cannot enter. Name
two consequences for the town and one for a farming region inland
that sold through that port.

**C4.** Using the Lesson 03 illustration's text alternative only,
list three goods visible on the dock and the region each most likely
came from, with a reason each. Name one item you cannot source from
the picture and what evidence would settle it.

**C5.** A merchant says: "Trade made everyone in the colonies richer."
Give one counterexample group and explain why the merchant's sentence
is incomplete.

**C6.** Why did Boston, New York, Philadelphia, and Charleston grow
large? Answer with one sentence using "port" and one using "trade
routes."

**C7.** A ship carries Middle Colony flour to the West Indies and
returns with sugar. Name the benefit for the flour farmer and the
benefit for the sugar buyer, in one sentence each.

**C8.** Error-analysis: "The Atlantic trade system carried only goods."
Correct the sentence in one sentence, naming what else it carried
and how.

## Week 18 — Money, self-rule, perspectives

**D1.** Solve with subtractions shown: (a) A candle costs 2 bits;
change from one piece of eight? (b) A basket costs 5 bits and a rope
costs 2 bits; total, and change from one piece?

**D2.** A colonist pays a debt with animal skins. Which payment kind
is this? Give one advantage and one problem for this kind of money.

**D3.** A cut Spanish coin is found in a Southern port town. Write one
evidence sentence (what it suggests about trade) and one limit
sentence (what it does not prove).

**D4.** Match: (a) Mayflower Compact, (b) town meeting, (c) House of
Burgesses — to: (i) elected representatives make colony laws,
(ii) settlers sign a written self-government agreement, (iii) townspeople
gather and vote directly.

**D5.** Order these events and give the gap in years between each
neighboring pair: Jamestown (1607), House of Burgesses (1619),
Mayflower Compact (1620). Show both subtractions.

**D6.** A town votes to build a road across land an Indigenous nation
has long used. Write two perspective sentences (settler; Indigenous
nation member), each labeled "invented teaching sentence," and one
"fair for whom?" sentence.

**D7.** Explain in 3–4 sentences why colonial self-rule was both real
and limited. Name at least two groups who could not vote in it.

**D8.** Error-analysis: "Colonial money was simple: everyone used
British pounds, and every trade was fair." Correct both halves of
the sentence (money kinds; fairness) in two sentences.
