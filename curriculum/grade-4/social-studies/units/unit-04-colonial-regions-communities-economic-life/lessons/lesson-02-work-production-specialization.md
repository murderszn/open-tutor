# Lesson 02 — Work, Production, and Specialization

**Goal:** I can trace a production chain, name the resources in it,
and explain why regions specialized and traded.
**Time:** two 35-minute sessions. **Materials:** chain cards or
strips of paper, the Lesson 01 region table.

**Readiness check (adult-led, 3 minutes):** Name one product from each
region from Lesson 01. What had to happen before that product could be
sold? (It had to be grown or caught, prepared, packed, and moved.)

## Teaching

### Three kinds of resources

Every good or service is made from resources, and economists sort
them into three kinds:

- **Natural resources** — from nature: soil, timber, fish, rivers,
  sunlight, and the long Southern growing season itself.
- **Human resources** — people's work and skills: the fisher, the
  sawyer, the miller, the shipbuilder, the farmer — and, in the
  Southern Colonies, the enslaved workers whose forced labor made
  plantation crops possible.
- **Physical capital** — the tools and buildings people use to
  produce: axes, saws, mills, barrels, workshops, and ships.

A production chain is useless if one kind is missing. Wheat without a
miller's skill is just grain; a mill without wheat is an empty
building; grain and a mill without a river or road cannot reach a
buyer. Keep all three kinds in view whenever you trace a chain.

### Production chains: four examples

- **Tobacco (Southern):** field → leaves cut and dried → packed in
  barrels (hogsheads) → rolled or shipped downriver to a port →
  loaded on a ship for Britain.
- **Wheat (Middle):** field → harvest → mill grinds grain into flour
  → flour packed in barrels → shipped from a port city → sold as
  flour or baked into bread.
- **Fish (New England):** sea → catch → salted or dried → packed in
  barrels → shipped to the West Indies or Europe.
- **Ships (New England):** forest → timber cut and sawn → planks
  shaped in a shipyard → ship launched → ship carries other regions'
  goods for a fee.

Notice the pattern: raw material, skilled work, packing, transport,
sale. If any step breaks, the chain stops.

### Specialization: why not make everything yourself?

**Specialization** means concentrating on what you can make best and
trading for the rest. A New England shipyard could build ships better
than a tobacco plantation could; a Virginia plantation could grow
tobacco better than a rocky New England farm could. If each region
tried to make everything itself, each would do every job badly. By
specializing and trading, each region got more of everything — fish
for tobacco, flour for lumber, ships for grain. Trade is the
partnership that specialization makes necessary.

### Incentives and profit — and whose profit?

An **incentive** is something that encourages a choice. A high price
for tobacco encourages a planter to plant more tobacco; a low price
encourages switching crops. A shipowner who can charge for carrying
goods has an incentive to build more ships. **Profit** is what remains
for a seller after costs are paid.

Here the unit insists on a hard truth: on a plantation, the "costs"
included human beings. Enslaved people were forced to work without pay
and without freedom, so the planter's profit was built on stolen labor.
Saying "plantations were profitable" without saying that is
misleading. The incentive belonged to the owner; the enslaved workers
had no choice, no wages, and no share of the profit — and many
resisted, in ways from slowing work to running away to keeping their
families and cultures alive under terrible pressure. When this lesson
says "profit," always ask: *profit for whom, at whose cost?*

### Worked example 1 — Order a chain

*Question: Put these wheat steps in order: "flour packed in barrels,"
"wheat harvested," "bread sold in a port town," "mill grinds grain."*

Reasoning: Follow the material. Wheat must exist before it can be
ground, flour must exist before it can be packed, and packed flour
must travel before bread can be sold. Order: harvested → mill grinds
→ packed in barrels → bread sold. A chain checked backward (can the
last step happen without the one before it?) catches most ordering
errors.

### Worked example 2 — Explain specialization

*Question: "A New England merchant buys tobacco from Virginia instead
of growing it at home. Explain his choice with specialization and with
an environment reason."*

Reasoning: Specialization: Virginia grows tobacco better and cheaper
than rocky-soil New England can, while New England builds ships and
catches fish better than Virginia does — so trading beats
self-sufficiency for both. Environment reason: tobacco needs a long,
warm growing season, which New England does not have. One answer,
two reasons: an economic reason and an environment reason.

## Guided practice (adult reads aloud if needed)

**G1.** Name one natural, one human, and one physical-capital resource
in the fish chain. If one were missing, which step would stop first?
*Hint: no salt, no packing; no fisher, no catch.*

**G2.** Error-analysis: "A friend says Southern plantations were
successful because enslaved workers liked the work." What is wrong
with that sentence? Rewrite it truthfully in one sentence.
*Hint: forced is not chosen; profit went to the owner.*

**G3.** A tobacco price rises sharply. Predict two decisions a
plantation owner might make, and name the incentive behind each.
*Hint: more of what pays, less of what does not.*

## Independent practice

**I1.** Trace the shipbuilding chain in five steps, naming at least
one resource of each kind somewhere in your chain. Label each resource
(N, H, or P).

**I2.** In 3–4 sentences, explain why a Middle Colony miller and a New
England shipbuilder would trade with each other rather than each doing
both jobs. Use the word "specialization."

**I3.** A port town's shipyard closes for a year. Name two other
colonial jobs hurt by the closure and explain the chain link for each.
(This is a cause-and-effect chain: one broken step ripples onward.)

## Applied task

Build one production-chain poster for a region of your choice: five
cards in order, each card naming the step and one resource used, with
an arrow between cards. At the bottom, write one sentence answering:
"Whose work made this chain possible, and were they paid and free?"
The adult checks that the sentence is honest for the chain chosen —
for a plantation chain, it must name forced labor.

## Exit check

Explain specialization in your own words, then give one example from
this lesson of two regions trading because each did one job better.
The adult checks: definition correct, example matched to regions.
