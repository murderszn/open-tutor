# Teacher Guide — Unit 04

**Audience:** the guiding adult only. Keep this guide and the
[answer key](answer-key.md) away from learner pages. This unit's
sensitive content (enslavement, land-taking, forced labor) is adult-led
at a plain, non-graphic level; pre-read Lessons 02, 03, and 05 before
teaching them, and pause for discussion whenever the learner needs it.

## Pacing and session notes

- **Week 15:** Lesson 01 (regions table, then/now). The region strip
  must carry words on every band — color alone is never a label.
  Check the Maine point explicitly: Maine was part of Massachusetts,
  not a separate colony.
- **Week 16:** Lesson 02 (resources, chains, specialization, profit).
  Lead the profit discussion yourself: "profit for whom, at whose
  cost?" must be asked aloud for every plantation chain. Do not let a
  chain poster end without its honesty sentence.
- **Week 17:** Lesson 03 (trade webs, port illustration, who
  benefited/harmed) then the Week 17 quiz. The illustration's caption
  must be read aloud: it is an AI imagining, not a record of a real
  port, ship, or day, and a barrel's contents cannot be read from the
  picture.
- **Week 18:** Lesson 04 (money, coins as evidence; the kept
  currency-history assignment begins with educator-provided images
  only) and Lesson 05 (self-rule, invented perspective passages —
  remind the learner at the start that they are invented teaching
  passages, not historical documents), project finish and
  presentation, then the unit assessment.

## Misconceptions and reteaching

| Misconception | What to say / reteach |
|---|---|
| "All thirteen colonies were the same" | Same ruler, different land. Re-teach Lesson 01's region table, one row at a time. |
| Maine as a separate colony | Maine was part of Massachusetts in this period. Re-check Lesson 01, Practice A4. |
| "Plantations were profitable, so they were fair" | Profit went to the owner; enslaved workers were forced, unpaid, and unfree. Re-teach Lesson 02's incentive/profit section and G2. |
| Triangular trade as "just goods" | The Atlantic system forcibly transported enslaved people. Re-teach Lesson 03's third scale and G3. |
| A picture proves its contents | A barrel's contents are not visible. Re-teach Lesson 03's applied task: name what extra evidence would settle it. |
| "Colonial money was just British pounds" | Spanish pieces of eight, barter, commodity money, paper, and credit were everyday reality. Re-teach Lesson 04's four kinds. |
| "Self-rule meant everyone was free and equal" | Self-rule for voting settlers coexisted with enslavement and land-taking. Re-teach Lesson 05's "who was left out" section and G3. |
| Sought/found or region/product mismatches | Re-do one row of the Lesson 01 table with environment → work reasoning, not memorized labels. |
| Bits arithmetic errors | Check price + change = 8. Re-teach Lesson 04's worked example 1. |
| One perspective stated as the fact | Ask: "Whose interests does that sentence serve, and who is missing?" Re-teach Lesson 05's worked example 2. |

## Scoring the assessments

**Week 17 quiz (10 points):** Q1–Q6 are 1 point each (key in the
answer key). Q7: 1 point for 5 bits with subtraction shown. Q8: 1 point
for a non-coin payment kind plus a real problem with it. Q9: 1 point
only if the sentence names forced, unpaid labor behind the profit.
Q10: 1 point total only if both a benefited group (with reason) and
a harmed group (with reason) are given.

**Unit assessment (12 tasks):** score each task as full (1), partial
(0.5), or missing (0) using the answer key's acceptable answers.
11–12 = ready for Unit 05; 8–10.5 = review the weakest objective with
its practice set, then move on; below 8 = re-teach the two weakest
objectives (use the lesson reteach rows above) and re-assess only
those tasks. Never report a single letter grade without the task
breakdown.

**Project (12 points):** use the rubric in [project.md](project.md).
10–12 = present as is; 7–9 = fix the weakest panel first; 4–6 = rework
two panels with adult support; 0–3 = rebuild with guided support and
a second attempt.

## Readiness and next steps

- **Ready for Unit 05** if the learner sorts colonies into regions
  from environment clues, traces a chain and a trade route, solves
  bits change, names three beginnings of self-rule with their limits,
  and states two perspectives without upgrading an invented passage
  into fact.
- **Reteach before Unit 05** if region/product pairs are still
  swapped, or if profit stories still hide forced labor: repeat
  Lesson 01's region strip and Lesson 02's chain poster with new
  regions/goods.
- **Unit 05 preview:** state and local government builds directly on
  this unit's assemblies and town meetings — keep the Lesson 05
  timeline (1607 → 1619 → 1620) for reuse; do not discard it.

## Source and accuracy notes (adult only)

- Region groupings and products: checked 2026-10-31 against standard
  colonial-region summaries. Cotton is **not** taught as a colonial
  cash crop here: it became dominant later, in the 1800s.
- Money: NPS "Spanish Coins" and Philadelphia Fed "Money in Colonial
  Times," checked 2026-10-31. All lesson prices are practice numbers,
  never historical prices. The 1652 Pine Tree Shilling and the
  "two bits = a quarter" usage are the only dated money facts taught.
- Self-rule: House of Burgesses 1619, Mayflower Compact 1620,
  Jamestown 1607 — gaps 12 years, 1 year, 13 years, all re-subtracted
  for this unit. If a reference the adult consults gives a different
  emphasis (for example, calling the Compact an agreement rather than
  a constitution), accept it and note the wording; do not mark the
  learner wrong for it.
- The Lesson 05 passages are invented teaching passages. Real primary
  sources (Library of Congress, adult-side only) are never replaced by
  them. The generated port illustration is an AI reconstruction with
  generic buildings and clothing. It must never be described as a
  depiction of a specific town, ship, or event.
