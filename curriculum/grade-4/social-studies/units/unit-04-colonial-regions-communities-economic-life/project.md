# Project — Colonial Region Fair

**Product:** a five-panel fair display (poster board, paper panels, or
a slide deck the adult prints) that a visitor can walk through in
order. **Work time:** Week 17, Session 3 (draft) and Week 18, Session
3 (finish and present). **Present to:** the guiding adult, or a small
adult-supervised group. Nothing from the display is published from
this track without the guiding adult's review.

## The five panels

**Panel 1 — Region strip.** The Lesson 01 strip, finished: three
bands, north to south (New England; Middle Colonies; Southern
Colonies), colonies labeled in each band, one environment symbol and
one work symbol per band, all labels in words (never color alone).

**Panel 2 — Production chain.** One chain from Lesson 02 (tobacco,
wheat, fish, or ships) in five ordered steps, each step naming one
resource used (labeled N, H, or P). At the bottom, the honesty
sentence: "Whose work made this chain possible, and were they paid
and free?" — for a plantation chain, the sentence must name forced
labor.

**Panel 3 — Port and trade card.** The generated port illustration
(with its caption: "AI illustration, not a record of a real place")
plus a four-place trade web (New England port, Middle Colony port,
Southern port, Britain) with one labeled good per arrow. Under the
web, one "who benefited / who was harmed" pair for one trade.

**Panel 4 — Coin-evidence card.** The currency-history work from
Lesson 04 for one educator-provided coin image: denomination, figures
or symbols, design, one verified mint-source note, one evidence
sentence, and one limit sentence ("does not prove …"). No real coin
is used, bought, or removed from any collection.

**Panel 5 — Decision and perspective card.** One colonial decision
(a road, a port warehouse, a crop choice, a town vote) shown from two
perspectives — settler view and Indigenous or enslaved person's
view — each labeled "invented teaching sentences — not a historical
document," plus one "fair for whom?" sentence underneath.

## Materials

Poster board or large paper, markers, the region strip from Lesson 01,
chain cards from Lesson 02, the port illustration printout, and the
educator's coin images. No materials beyond ordinary classroom
supplies; no sharp tools beyond adult-supervised scissors.

## Steps

1. **Draft (Week 17, Session 3).** Sketch all five panels on scrap
   paper. The adult checks Panel 2's honesty sentence, Panel 3's
   illustration caption, and Panel 5's "invented" labels before any
   final copy.
2. **Build (Week 18, Session 3).** Copy the draft onto the display
   panels in order. Check every colony against the Lesson 01 table
   and every chain step against Lesson 02.
3. **Present.** Walk a visitor through the panels in order, in about
   5 minutes: region → chain → port → coin → decision. End by
   answering one visitor question with evidence ("Which panel proves
   that?").
4. **Reflect (one paragraph or spoken answer).** Answer: "Which
   region would you have wanted to live in, and why — and who would
   have had a harder time there than you?" The second half of the
   question is required: a region answer without its harder side is
   incomplete.

## Deliverables

- The five-panel display (all five panels present, in order).
- The reflection paragraph (written or adult-scribed).
- A source note on the back of Panel 1 listing the lesson tables used
  (Lesson 01 region table, Lesson 02 chains, Lesson 03 trade webs)
  and the sentence: "Passage-style panels are invented teaching
  sentences, not historical documents. The port picture is an AI
  illustration, not a record of a real place."

## Rubric (12 points total; 4 criteria × 0–3)

| Criterion | 3 — Strong | 2 — Good | 1 — Beginning | 0 — Missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Region accuracy | All three regions in order, colonies correct, environment and work symbols matched | One colony or symbol wrong | Two or more errors, or regions out of order | No region panel |
| Chain and trade | Chain in correct order with resources labeled; web arrows match origin regions; benefit/harm pair present and honest | One chain step out of order or one arrow mismatched; pair present but thin | Chain unordered, or web missing, or harm omitted | No chain or trade panel |
| Coin evidence | Denomination, symbols, verified source note, one evidence sentence, and one limit sentence all present | One element missing | Evidence claimed without a limit, or no source note | No coin panel |
| Decision, perspectives, reflection | Both perspectives present and labeled invented; "fair for whom?" answered; reflection names a harder side | One element missing (label, question, or harder side) | Only one perspective, or perspectives presented as real documents | No decision panel |

**Scoring guide for the adult:** 10–12 = display ready to present as
is; 7–9 = present after fixing the one weakest panel; 4–6 = rework
two panels with the adult before presenting; 0–3 = rebuild with
guided support — the learner keeps the draft and tries again, never a
failing grade without a second attempt.

## Accessibility and safety

- Panels may be spoken instead of written; the adult scribes.
- The port illustration may be used only with its caption ("AI
  illustration, not a record of a real place").
- Enslavement, land-taking, and forced-labor content stays at the
  unit's plain, non-graphic level; the adult stops the presentation
  for discussion at any point.
