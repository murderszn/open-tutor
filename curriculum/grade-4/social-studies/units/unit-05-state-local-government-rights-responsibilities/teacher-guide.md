# Teacher Guide — Unit 05

**Audience:** the guiding adult only. Keep this guide and the
[answer key](answer-key.md) away from learner pages. This unit's
sensitive content (slavery, exclusion from voting) is adult-led at
a plain, non-graphic level; pre-read Lesson 02 before teaching it,
and pause for discussion whenever the learner needs it. Keep all
examples nonpartisan: no current office-holders, parties, or
candidates are named in lesson content — the adult supplies local
officials' names at use time.

## Pacing and session notes

- **Week 20:** Lesson 01 (levels and branches). The town-scene
  investigation (Session 3) must use the illustration's caption and
  text alternative: it is an AI imagining of a generic town, not a
  record of a real place, and the two columned buildings are not
  labeled in the picture — the adult names them with the learner
  (town hall, library, or another public building) rather than
  letting the picture decide.
- **Week 21:** Lesson 02 (rights, responsibilities, federalism).
  Lead the timeline discussion yourself: rights were not given to
  everyone at once, and the unit says so plainly. The deliberation
  (Session 3) is the learner writing one fair classroom rule with a
  reason — check that the rule names who it helps and who it limits.
- **Week 22:** Lesson 03 (bill to law; veto and override numbers)
  then Lesson 04 (taxes → services; the kept agencies quiz as
  guided practice in Session 3), then the Week 22 quiz. All tax
  numbers are practice numbers; never quote them as real rates or
  budgets. If the learner asks about a real local tax, look it up
  together from an official source.
- **Week 23:** Lesson 05 (participation, decision procedures, the
  kept `civic-voting-research.md` assignment), the policy task
  (Session 3), project finish and presentation, then the unit
  assessment. The Lesson 05 passages are invented teaching passages
  — remind the learner at the start that they are invented, not real
  documents or records of real meetings.

## Misconceptions and reteaching

| Misconception | What to say / reteach |
|---|---|
| "The president makes all the laws" | Congress (legislative branch) writes laws; the president carries them out. Re-teach Lesson 01's branch jobs. |
| Levels confused (mayor = national) | Match the official to the size of the place: whole country, one state, one town. Re-teach Lesson 01's table, one row at a time. |
| "The Bill of Rights gave everyone rights in 1791" | Rights were promised in 1791 and expanded by later amendments — 1865, 1920, 1971 — and not everyone enjoyed them at first. Re-teach Lesson 02's timeline and G2. |
| Bill vs. law | A bill is a *proposed* law; it becomes a law only after both chambers pass it in the same form and the president signs it (or Congress overrides a veto). Re-teach Lesson 03's seven steps. |
| Veto override numbers | Two-thirds of 435 is 290; two-thirds of 100 is 66⅔, so 67 votes. Re-teach Lesson 03's worked example 2. |
| "Taxes disappear" | Follow the chain: paid → collected → spent → used. Re-teach Lesson 04's tax-dollar table and G2. |
| Department vs. agency swapped | Departments are the big cabinet offices (State, Defense, Justice); agencies have one specific job (FEMA, NASA, EPA, FDA) and some are independent. Re-teach Lesson 04's table. |
| "Voting is the only participation" | Petitioning, speaking at meetings, volunteering, and serving on a jury are participation too — and a 10-year-old can do most of them. Re-teach Lesson 05's table and G2. |
| Consensus vs. vote | Consensus means everyone can agree; a vote means the most votes win and some people lose. Re-teach Lesson 05's procedure table. |
| An invented passage treated as fact | Passages A and B are invented teaching passages. Re-teach Lesson 05's labeling and G3. |
| Tax-split addition errors | Check that the four parts add to 100. Re-teach Lesson 04's worked example 1. |

## Scoring the assessments

**Week 22 quiz (10 points):** Q1–Q5 are 1 point each (key in the
answer key). Q6: 1 point only if the addition is shown and the
answer is 0¢ left. Q7: 1 point only if both FEMA and the Department
of Homeland Security are named. Q8: 1 point for one real tax and one
real service it pays for. Q9: 1 point only if the sentence names the
19th Amendment (1920) and that the vote cannot be denied because of
sex. Q10: 1 point total only if both a left-out level (with a job)
and a left-out branch (with a job) are given.

**Unit assessment (12 tasks):** score each task as full (1), partial
(0.5), or missing (0) using the answer key's acceptable answers.
11–12 = ready for Unit 06; 8–10.5 = review the weakest objective with
its practice set, then move on; below 8 = re-teach the two weakest
objectives (use the lesson reteach rows above) and re-assess only
those tasks. Never report a single letter grade without the task
breakdown.

**Project (12 points):** use the rubric in [project.md](project.md).
10–12 = present as is; 7–9 = fix the weakest panel first; 4–6 = rework
two panels with adult support; 0–3 = rebuild with guided support and
a second attempt.

## Source and accuracy notes (adult only)

- Government structure: Ben's Guide (U.S. Government Publishing
  Office) and Congress.gov, checked 2026-11-06. House 435, Senate
  100, Congress 535 — re-added for this unit (435 + 100 = 535).
  Override thresholds: two-thirds of 435 = 290 exactly; two-thirds
  of 100 = 66⅔, so 67 votes. Both re-computed for this unit.
- Rights: Ben's Guide "Bill of Rights: 1789-91" (full amendment
  texts) and the National Constitution Center, checked 2026-11-06.
  Ratification December 15, 1791. Amendment years: 13th, 1865;
  15th, 1870; 19th, 1920; 26th, 1971. Timeline gaps re-subtracted:
  1865 − 1791 = 74; 1920 − 1865 = 55; 1971 − 1920 = 51.
- States and territories: 2020 U.S. Census — California 39,538,223
  (largest), Wyoming 576,851 (smallest). Five inhabited territories
  named in Lesson 02; Washington, D.C. is a federal district with a
  non-voting House delegate and no senators. Territory citizenship
  varies (American Samoa residents are U.S. nationals, not
  citizens); the unit states the shared facts only.
- The 13th Amendment's full text includes an exception for
  punishment for a crime; the unit teaches the grade-4 summary
  ("ended slavery in the United States") and the adult can add the
  exception if the learner asks what the exact words say.
- Taxes: all lesson numbers are practice numbers. The 10% sales-tax
  examples ($20 → $2 tax, $22 total; $10 → $1 tax, $11 total) are
  re-multiplied in the answer key. The 100-cent split (40/25/20/15)
  is an invented teaching split, never a real town's budget.
- The Lesson 05 passages are invented teaching passages. Real
  primary sources (Library of Congress, adult-side only) are never
  replaced by them. The generated town illustration is an AI
  imagining of a generic town with generic buildings. It must never
  be described as a depiction of a specific town, office, or event.
