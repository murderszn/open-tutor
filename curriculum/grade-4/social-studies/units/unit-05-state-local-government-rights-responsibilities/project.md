# Project — Our Town Government Fair

**Product:** a five-panel fair display (poster board, paper panels, or
a slide deck the adult prints) that a visitor can walk through in
order. **Work time:** Week 20, Session 3 (draft the levels chart and
the town-scene sort) and Week 23, Session 3 (finish) with Session 4
presenting. **Present to:** the guiding adult, or a small
adult-supervised group. Nothing from the display is published from
this track without the guiding adult's review.

## The five panels

**Panel 1 — Levels-and-branches chart.** The Lesson 01 chart,
finished: three rows (national, state, local) and three columns
(legislative, executive, judicial), officials filled in words for
every box, one real job per row from the learner's own town or
state (the adult supplies local names). Color is never the only
label — every box carries words.

**Panel 2 — Rights card.** The Lesson 02 rights card, finished: one
right from the Bill of Rights and one from the later amendments,
each in the learner's own words with its year and one paired
responsibility. Under both, the sentence: "This right was not
enjoyed by everyone at first, because …" — finished honestly
(enslaved people, women, and others were excluded; amendments and
movements changed that over time).

**Panel 3 — Bill-to-law path.** The Lesson 03 path, finished: seven
boxes in order from "Idea" to "Law," with the "signs → law" and
"vetoes → back to Congress" arrows and the two override numbers
(House 290, Senate 67). Under the path, one checks-and-balances
sentence: one branch, the check it has, and the branch it limits.

**Panel 4 — Tax-dollar trail.** The Lesson 04 trail, finished: one
coin labeled "1 tax dollar" and an arrow chain — "paid → collected
→ spent → used" — with a 100-cent split (the lesson's 40/25/20/15
split or the learner's own split from Practice I1, with the addition
shown) and one service from the town illustration named at the
"used" stop, with who uses it.

**Panel 5 — Policy proposal card.** The Lesson 05 policy proposal,
finished: one real classroom or school problem, the proposed rule in
one sentence, its reason in one sentence, the decision procedure
used (consensus or vote) and why it fit, and one sentence naming who
the rule helps and who it limits. The two deliberation passages from
Lesson 05 are **not** quoted as real documents; any viewpoint
sentence on this panel is labeled "invented teaching sentence."

## Materials

Poster board or large paper, markers, the levels chart from Lesson
01, the rights card from Lesson 02, the bill path from Lesson 03,
the tax trail from Lesson 04, and the generated town illustration
printout. No materials beyond ordinary classroom supplies; no sharp
tools beyond adult-supervised scissors.

## Steps

1. **Draft (Week 20, Session 3).** Sketch Panels 1 and 2 on scrap
   paper. The adult checks the levels chart's officials and the
   rights card's "not enjoyed by everyone at first" sentence before
   any final copy.
2. **Build (Week 23, Session 3).** Copy the draft onto the display
   panels in order. Check every official against the Lesson 01
   table, every amendment year against Lesson 02, and the override
   numbers (290, 67) against Lesson 03.
3. **Present.** Walk a visitor through the panels in order, in about
   5 minutes: levels → rights → bill → tax dollar → policy. End by
   answering one visitor question with evidence ("Which panel proves
   that?").
4. **Reflect (one paragraph or spoken answer).** Answer: "Which
   level of government touches your daily life the most, and what is
   one responsibility you owe back for a right you use?" The second
   half of the question is required: a level answer without its
   responsibility is incomplete.

## Deliverables

- The five-panel display (all five panels present, in order).
- The reflection paragraph (written or adult-scribed).
- A source note on the back of Panel 1 listing the lesson tables
  used (Lesson 01 levels table, Lesson 02 rights timeline, Lesson 03
  bill steps, Lesson 04 tax split) and the sentence: "Viewpoint
  sentences are invented teaching sentences, not real documents.
  The town picture is an AI illustration of a generic town, not a
  record of a real place."

## Rubric (12 points total; 4 criteria × 0–3)

| Criterion | 3 — Strong | 2 — Good | 1 — Beginning | 0 — Missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Levels and branches | All three levels and all three branches correct, officials matched, one real job per row | One official or job wrong | Two or more errors, or a level or branch missing | No levels panel |
| Rights and history | Both rights in own words with correct years and paired responsibilities; the "not at first" sentence honest and complete | One element missing (year, responsibility, or honesty sentence) | Rights copied without own words, or years wrong, or exclusion not named | No rights panel |
| Bill path and tax trail | Seven steps in order with both arrows and both override numbers; tax split totals 100 with addition shown; service named at the "used" stop | One step out of order, one override number wrong, or addition missing | Path unordered, or split does not total 100, or no service named | No bill or tax panel |
| Policy, deliberation, reflection | Rule, reason, procedure with fit, and helps/limits sentence all present; viewpoint sentences labeled invented; reflection names a right and its responsibility | One element missing (label, question, or responsibility half) | Rule without a reason, or procedure without a fit, or invented sentences presented as real | No policy panel |

**Scoring guide for the adult:** 10–12 = display ready to present as
is; 7–9 = present after fixing the one weakest panel; 4–6 = rework
two panels with the adult before presenting; 0–3 = rebuild with
guided support — the learner keeps the draft and tries again, never a
failing grade without a second attempt.

## Accessibility and safety

- Panels may be spoken instead of written; the adult scribes.
- The town illustration may be used only with its caption ("AI
  illustration of a generic town, not a specific place").
- Rights-history content (slavery, exclusion from voting) stays at
  the unit's plain, non-graphic level; the adult stops the
  presentation for discussion at any point.
- No current office-holders, parties, or candidates appear on the
  display; local officials' names are supplied by the adult at use
  time. The policy proposal is addressed to the classroom or school
  and is not sent to any real official.
