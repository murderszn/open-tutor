# Unit Assessment — Unit 02: Indigenous Nations, Geography, and Perspectives

**When:** Week 10, Session 4 · **Coverage:** all five unit objectives ·
**Format:** 12 items; the learner may use the unit illustration and a
culture-area map. Answers in `answer-key.md` — never beside these
questions. This assessment is a draft teaching check, not a standardized
or validated test.

**Name:** ____________________ **Date:** __________

## Part 1 — Culture areas and environment (Objectives 1–2)

**1.** Name all eight culture areas. (1 point)

**2.** For **three** areas of your choice, write one environment feature
and one way of life it shaped (food, shelter, or travel). (3 points)

**3.** Sort into **ADAPT** or **MODIFY** and give the evidence for each:
a. Kayaks built for icy seas
b. Irrigation channels dug to cornfields
c. Fish weirs staked across a salmon stream
(3 points — half per correct sort, half per evidence)

**4.** "The Plateau nations fished for salmon and gathered roots." Is
this more like the Northwest Coast way of life or the Southwest way of
life? Explain with one environment reason. (1 point)

## Part 2 — Evidence from the illustration (Objectives 1, 3)

*Use [`culture-areas-environments.png`](../assets/culture-areas-environments.png)
(an AI-generated teaching picture — not a real place).*

**5.** The bottom-left panel shows red mesas, stacked earth-colored
dwellings, and crop rows with water running between them.
a. Which culture area is it? (½ point)
b. Name two visible clues that prove it. (1 point)
c. Is the watering an adapt or a modify? What is your evidence? (½ point)

**Text-only alternative for item 5:** "A panel shows flat-topped red
rock hills, multi-level houses of sun-dried earth, and neat rows of
small green plants with a thin stream of water between the rows."
Answer a–c from the description.

**6.** The bottom-right panel shows a bark-covered longhouse and a
garden of corn, climbing vines, and round squash.
a. Which culture area is it? (½ point)
b. What are the three plants called together, and how do they help one
another? (1 point)

**Text-only alternative for item 6:** "A panel shows a long
bark-covered house in autumn woods, with tall corn stalks, vines
climbing the stalks, and large round squash on the ground." Answer a–b
from the description.

**7.** Fill one full comparison-chart row (**Food**) for the Arctic
(Inuit) and the Eastern Woodlands (Haudenosaunee), with an evidence
note in each cell. (2 points)

**8.** Write one "because" comparison sentence explaining a **difference**
between the two nations in item 7. (1 point)

## Part 3 — Perspectives and change over time (Objectives 4–5)

**9.** An English colonist's letter about 1621 stresses the harvest and
thankfulness. A Wampanoag account stresses the homeland, the sickness
brought by Europeans, and diplomacy. Name **one reason from each
side's situation** that explains the difference. (2 points)

**10.** Draw and label the three boxes of a context → perspective
chain. Then fill it for the Cherokee removal: one context fact, one
situation fact, one perspective/action fact. (2 points — ½ for the
boxes, ½ per correct fact)

**11.** Put in order: the Indian Removal Act (1830), the Supreme Court
victory (1832), the forced removal (1838). For the **middle** event,
write one sentence saying what changed. (2 points)

**12.** Write 3–4 sentences: how does the Cherokee Nation exist **today**?
Name at least one present-day fact with where it comes from (map,
lesson, or source). (2 points)
