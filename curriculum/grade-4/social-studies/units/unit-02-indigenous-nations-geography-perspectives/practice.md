# Practice Sets — Unit 02: Indigenous Nations, Geography, and Perspectives

Four weekly sets (A–D), one per week of the unit. The adult assigns 6–8
items per week and reviews them the same day. Hints are printed here for
the learner; answers live only in `answer-key.md`.

---

## Week 7 — Set A: culture areas and environment matching

*Use the unit illustration
([`culture-areas-environments.png`](assets/culture-areas-environments.png),
an AI-generated teaching picture — not a real place).*

**A1.** Write the eight culture-area names. *(Hint: they are listed in
Lesson 01 — cover the page and try, then check.)*

**A2.** Match each environment description to its culture area. Write
the area name beside each number.
1. Hot, dry desert with flat-topped mesas and little rain
2. Rainy evergreen coast with salmon rivers
3. Freezing coast with sea ice and tundra
4. Forests, rivers, and lakes east of the Mississippi
*(Hint: picture each illustration panel as you read.)*

**A3.** Look at the top-left illustration panel (snow-block dome, sea
ice, kayak on a rack). Which culture area is it? Name **two** visible
clues that prove it. *(Hint: ask "what is cold here, and what is made
for water?")*

**A4.** Look at the bottom-right panel (bark longhouse, corn with
climbing vines, round squash). Which culture area is it? Name **two**
visible clues. *(Hint: what three plants grow together? Lesson 02
named them.)*

**A5.** A nation lives where tall oak trees drop acorns and the ocean
is nearby. Predict two foods they might rely on, and explain each
prediction from the environment. *(Hint: California area — what does
an oak woodland plus a coast give?)*

**A6.** **Text-only alternative** (answer without the picture): "Panel X
shows red mesas, stacked earth-colored houses, and rows of small green
plants with water running between the rows." Which culture area is
Panel X, and what do the water rows tell you? *(Hint: which area is hot
and dry? What must people do to farm without rain?)*

**A7.** Error-analysis: "Everyone in one culture area lived exactly the
same way." Explain the mistake in two sentences. *(Hint: Lesson 01,
guided practice 6 — area vs. nation.)*

## Week 8 — Set B: adapt, modify, and compare

**B1.** Draw a T-chart with columns **ADAPT (people changed)** and
**MODIFY (land changed)**. Sort: adobe houses, irrigation ditches,
kayaks, fish weirs, cleared cornfields, fur clothing. Add a short
evidence note to each. *(Hint: ask "who changed — the people or the
land?" for each one.)*

**B2.** Write one "because" sentence for an **adapt** example and one
for a **modify** example, naming the evidence in each. *(Hint: use the
sentence frames in Lesson 02's supports.)*

**B3.** Complete this comparison-chart row for **Environment**:
Inuit (Arctic) vs. Haudenosaunee (Eastern Woodlands). Write both cells
with evidence from the illustration. *(Hint: top-left panel vs.
bottom-right panel — name what you see.)*

**B4.** Complete the **Shelter** row for the same pair, with evidence.
*(Hint: what is each shelter made of, and why those materials?)*

**B5.** Write one comparison sentence with "because" explaining a
**similarity** between the Tlingit and the Hopi. *(Hint: Lesson 03's
model compared what both built with — what did each environment give?)*

**B6.** Your chart says: "The Hopi dried salmon on racks." Prove it or
fix it. *(Hint: which panel shows drying salmon? Which area is it?)*

**B7.** **Text-only alternative:** "Two nations are compared. Nation 1
lives on a rainy evergreen coast and dries salmon. Nation 2 lives in a
hot desert and irrigates corn." Name one similarity and one difference
in how their environments shaped their food, each with a "because."
*(Hint: both used what the land gave — but the land gave different
things.)*

## Week 9 — Set C: perspectives

**C1.** What is a perspective? Write the definition in your own words.
*(Hint: Lesson 04 — it starts with "a point of view shaped by…")*

**C2.** List the five questions historians ask about an account.
*(Hint: who, when, for whom, why, and…?)*

**C3.** Account A (colonist) remembers the 1621 gathering as "we invited
them to our harvest feast." Account B (Wampanoag) remembers "our leader
brought ninety men — a show of strength and diplomacy." Give **one
reason** from each side's **situation** that explains its viewpoint.
*(Hint: newcomers fearing starvation vs. a nation protecting its
homeland after sickness.)*

**C4.** A textbook says: "The Pilgrims invited the Indians to the first
Thanksgiving." What is incomplete about that sentence? Write two
sentences. *(Hint: whose perspective is missing? What four things does
Account B notice that the textbook skips?)*

**C5.** "Two accounts disagree, so one side must be lying." Do you
agree? Explain in two sentences. *(Hint: Lesson 04 exit check 3 —
different situations, different true details.)*

**C6.** Fill a perspective card for Account B: creator / audience /
purpose / two things noticed / one reason the viewpoint makes sense.
*(Hint: purpose is about remembering homeland and decisions; the
reason must come from the Wampanoag situation.)*

**C7.** Error-analysis: "Since the Wampanoag had good reasons for their
viewpoint, everything in Account B must be true." Explain the mistake.
*(Hint: Lesson 04, guided practice 6 — reasons explain viewpoints;
what judges claims?)*

## Week 10 — Set D: context, change over time, unit review

**D1.** Draw the three boxes of a context → perspective chain and label
them. *(Hint: Lesson 05 — context, situation, perspective/action.)*

**D2.** Put these four events in order and write what changed at each
step: Sequoyah's syllabary (~1821), the Indian Removal Act (1830), the
Supreme Court victory (1832), the forced removal (1838). *(Hint: make
a timeline strip — what did each event do to the Cherokee situation?)*

**D3.** "Because settlers wanted Cherokee land and Congress passed the
Removal Act, the Cherokee Nation ___ , so its leaders ___ ." Finish the
chain with the situation and the perspective/action. *(Hint: situation =
what the Nation had; action = what leaders did.)*

**D4.** Point to Georgia and Oklahoma on a U.S. map. What caused people
to move between them in 1838, and what was one effect? Name your map
evidence. *(Hint: the cause is a law plus soldiers; the effect shows in
where the Nation's capital is today.)*

**D5.** Name one way the Cherokee Nation exists **today**. *(Hint:
three recognized tribes; the newspaper; language classes — pick one.)*

**D6.** **Text-only alternative:** "A homeland map shows Cherokee towns
across Georgia, Tennessee, North Carolina, and Alabama in 1800, and an
arrow to Oklahoma labeled 1838." What changed, and what does the
"Oklahoma" end of the arrow tell you about today? *(Hint: then = towns
in the Southeast; now = ?)*

**D7.** Unit review: without looking, name the eight culture areas and
write one environment word for each. Then check against Lesson 01.
*(Hint: Arctic, Northwest Coast, Plateau, Great Plains, Eastern
Woodlands, Southeast, Southwest, California.)*

**D8.** Unit review: write one adapt and one modify from the unit, each
with its evidence, and one perspective "because" sentence from Lesson
04 or 05. *(Hint: three quick wins — pick your strongest examples.)*
