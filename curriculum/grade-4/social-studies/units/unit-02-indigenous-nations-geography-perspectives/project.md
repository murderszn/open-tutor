# Project — One Nation, One Environment: A Homeland Study

**Weeks:** 8–10 (work sessions: Week 8 S3, Week 9 S3, Week 10 S3; about
35 minutes each) · **Deliverable due:** Week 10 S3

## What you will make

A **homeland study** of one Indigenous nation — a labeled homeland map,
an environment → way-of-life chart, and a "then and now" write-up or
poster — proving that you can read the land, sort adapt from modify,
compare with evidence, and connect past to present.

## Step 1 — Choose your nation (Week 8 S3, with the adult)

Pick **one** nation from the adult-approved list below. The adult
confirms that at least two reputable sources exist (tribal website or
museum page preferred — see [resource-pack](resource-pack.md)).

| Culture area | Example nations (pick one) |
|---|---|
| Arctic | Inuit, Iñupiat |
| Northwest Coast | Tlingit, Haida |
| Plateau | Nez Perce (Nimiipuu), Yakama |
| Great Plains | Lakota, Cheyenne, Mandan |
| Eastern Woodlands | Haudenosaunee (choose one nation, e.g., Mohawk), Lenape |
| Southeast | Cherokee, Muscogee (Creek), Seminole |
| Southwest | Hopi, Diné (Navajo), Zuni |
| California | Chumash, Pomo |

**Rule:** the nation must come from a *different* culture area than the
ones you compared in Lesson 03's guided practice (Arctic/Eastern
Woodlands), so you build a fresh chart.

## Step 2 — Map the homeland (Week 8 S3)

On a printed or drawn North America map:
1. Outline and label your nation's homeland (the adult helps with
   boundaries — use the tribal or museum source).
2. Label at least **three** environment features (a mountain range,
   river, ocean, desert, or forest — real labels from the map).
3. Add a map key, a compass rose check, and a title (Unit 01 habits).
4. Write one sentence under the map: "The most important environment
   feature for this nation was ___ because ___."

## Step 3 — Build the evidence chart (Week 9 S3)

Fill the four-row chart from Lesson 03 — **Environment, Food, Shelter,
Travel** — for your nation, with an **Evidence** note in every cell
(map evidence or source evidence; "no evidence found" is allowed once).

Then add two labeled rows:
- **ADAPT:** one way the nation changed its ways to fit the land
  (with evidence).
- **MODIFY:** one way the nation changed the land to fit its ways
  (with evidence; "no evidence found" is allowed with a reason).

## Step 4 — Then and now (Week 10 S3)

Write 5–8 sentences (or build a poster with the same content):
1–2 sentences on the homeland **then** (one map fact, one way-of-life
fact); 1–2 sentences on a **change** the homeland went through (with
cause); 2–3 sentences on the nation **today** — tribal government,
language program, school, business, or news item, with the source named
(the tribal website or museum page). Close with one sentence: what this
nation's story teaches about environment and endurance.

## Step 5 — Share and reflect

Present the map, chart, and write-up/poster to the adult (3–5 minutes).
Then answer: "What was the hardest evidence to find, and what did you
do?" The adult scores with the rubric below.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (3) | Developing (2) | Starting (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Map & environment** | Homeland outlined and labeled; 3+ real environment features labeled; key + title present; the "most important feature" sentence names evidence | Homeland labeled; 2 environment features; key or title missing; sentence names a feature without evidence | Homeland misplaced or unlabeled; fewer than 2 features; no key/title; sentence missing |
| **Evidence chart** | All four rows filled with specific evidence in every cell; adapt and modify rows correct with evidence | Chart complete but 1–2 cells lack specific evidence, or one adapt/modify label is swapped | Multiple cells empty or evidence-free; adapt/modify confused |
| **Then and now** | Then/change/today all covered; today's fact comes from a named tribal or museum source; closing sentence connects environment and endurance | All three parts present but today's source unnamed, or the change lacks a cause | A part missing; today treated as "they disappeared"; no source named |
| **Accuracy & respect** | Nations named as living communities; no stereotypes; present tense used for today; claims match the sources | Living-community language mostly used; one stereotype or past-tense slip, corrected when asked | Nations described only in past tense; stereotypes uncorrected |

**Scoring:** 12–10 = strong; 9–7 = developing (revise the weakest
criterion); 6–4 = starting (re-teach with the teacher guide, then
revise). The adult records the score privately — never in this repo.

## Teacher notes (summary; full guidance in `teacher-guide.md`)

- Pre-approve the nation **and** its two sources before Step 1; steer
  toward nations with strong tribal websites (Cherokee, Navajo, Hopi,
  and many others maintain excellent ones).
- If sources are thin for a small nation, allow a switch in Week 8 —
  better a well-sourced study than a frustrated one.
- The "today" section is non-negotiable: a study ending in the past is
  incomplete by this unit's standards.
