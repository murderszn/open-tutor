# Resource Pack — Unit 02: Indigenous Nations, Geography, and Perspectives

**Goal (one sentence):** Give the guiding adult short, grade-4-appropriate
videos, museum references, and search routes that reinforce the eight
culture areas, adapt/modify thinking, evidence-based comparison, two
perspectives on 1621, and the Cherokee removal — always with Indigenous
authorship or museum/tribal provenance where available.
**Age fit:** grade 4 (ages 9–10); the adult previews every video and
co-views with the learner. Captions or transcripts required where
available; the adult checks for ads and age suitability.

**Fact-check record (2026-10-18):** eight culture areas (Arctic,
Northwest Coast, Plateau, Great Plains, Eastern Woodlands, Southeast,
Southwest, California) per the track scope-and-sequence; Three Sisters
(corn, beans, squash) companion planting; Haudenosaunee Confederacy's
five original nations (Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, Seneca);
Indian Removal Act (1830), *Worcester v. Georgia* (1832), Cherokee
forced removal (1838) with ~4,000 deaths on the Trail of Tears —
standard U.S. history facts corroborated across the museum sources
below. **Caveat for every video below:** most children's videos treat
Indigenous peoples in the past tense; this unit teaches nations as
living communities — the adult names the present tense out loud
whenever a video does not.

## Web references (reputable; check dates: 2026-10-18)

1. **Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian — Native
   Knowledge 360° Essential Understandings guide** (PDF): the ten-part
   framework for teaching Native history — "no single American Indian
   culture," "not a people of the past," contemporary identity and
   revitalization.
   https://americanindian.si.edu/nk360/pdf/NMAI-Essential-Understandings-Guide.pdf
   *Use for:* the adult's framing for the whole unit; Lessons 01, 04.
   Free, no account.
2. **Plimoth Patuxet Museums — "You Are the Historian: Investigating
   the First Thanksgiving" (Unit 4):** primary-source units with
   essential questions, discussion prompts, and activities at multiple
   grade levels; built with an Indigenous Advisory Committee; covers
   the 1621 harvest feast, Wampanoag and English gratitude practices,
   and how the holiday's meaning changed.
   https://plimoth.org/yath/unit-4
   *Use for:* Lesson 04's adult-selected accounts; the perspective
   task. Free, no account.
3. **Plimoth Patuxet Museums — Teacher Tools:** field-trip prep,
   classroom activities, and the five primary-source units matching the
   *You Are the Historian* game.
   https://plimoth.org/for-teachers/teacher-tools
   *Use for:* Lesson 04 preparation; extension research. Free, no
   account.
4. **IMLS spotlight — "The First Thanksgiving: How Plimoth Patuxet
   Museums and the Wampanoag People Tell Their Stories Together":**
   background on the museum's 50-year collaboration with Wampanoag
   educators and the making of the *You Are the Historian* game.
   https://www.imls.gov/grant-spotlights/first-thanksgiving-how-plimoth-patuxet-museums-and-wampanoag-people-tell-their
   *Use for:* adult background on source provenance. Free, no account.
5. **Smithsonian Magazine — "Inside a New Effort to Change What Schools
   Teach About Native American History":** how and why the NMAI built
   Native Knowledge 360°; the Essential Understandings in plain
   language.
   https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smithsonian-institution/inside-new-effort-change-what-schools-teach-about-native-american-history-180973166/
   *Use for:* adult background; the "past-tense myth" discussion.
   Free, no account.
