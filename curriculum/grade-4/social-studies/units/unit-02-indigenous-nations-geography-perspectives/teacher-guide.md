# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Indigenous Nations, Geography, and Perspectives

**For the guiding adult.** This unit teaches living nations, not a
vanished past. Preview every source yourself, keep discussion
adult-guided (especially Lessons 04–05), and close every history lesson
on the present. Answers to every learner question are in
`answer-key.md`.

## Sensitive-history ground rules (read before Week 7)

- Indigenous peoples are **present-tense**. Say "the Cherokee Nation
  *is*" and "Wampanoag people *live*." Correct past-tense slips gently
  every time.
- Name difficult history **plainly, never graphic**: "U.S. soldiers
  forced Cherokee families to march west; thousands died of cold,
  hunger, and sickness." No invented dialogue, no imagined suffering.
- Every encounter gets **at least two perspectives**. Never teach the
  colonist account alone.
- Choose sources with **Indigenous authorship or museum/tribal
  provenance** (see [resource-pack](resource-pack.md)). If a source's
  perspective is unclear, do not use it.
- If the learner asks "was it fair?" — answer honestly ("No. The court
  said the land was theirs, and the ruling was ignored anyway."), then
  return to what the nation *did*: resisted, endured, exists today.

## Session-by-session guidance (16 sessions, ~35 min each)

### Week 7 — culture areas and environment (Lesson 01)

- **S1 (Lesson 01, part 1):** Read the framing paragraph aloud — it
  matters. Teach the eight areas with the culture-area map; have the
  learner point to each area as you name it. End with the
  environment → way of life rule in the learner's own words.
- **S2 (Lesson 01, part 2 + image activity):** Open the illustration;
  label it "AI teaching picture" out loud. Do the two worked examples
  exactly as written (think-aloud modeling), then guided practice
  1–4. Success: the learner names two clues per match.
- **S3 (investigation):** The environment-detective application. Let the
  learner pick the mystery area for your turn — engagement beats your
  plan. Success: predictions tied to named clues.
- **S4 (practice set A + review):** Assign 6–8 items; review same day.
  Re-teach any area the learner cannot place on the map.

### Week 8 — adapt/modify and comparison (Lessons 02–03)

- **S1 (Lesson 02):** Teach adapt vs. modify with the worked examples;
  drill the "who changed?" test until it is automatic. Begin the
  project: choose the nation and approve two sources (tribal or museum).
- **S2 (Lesson 02 practice + project Step 2):** T-chart sort; then map
  the homeland with key, compass check, and title (Unit 01 habits).
  Success: every T-chart entry has an evidence note.
- **S3 (Lesson 03, part 1):** Build the modeled Tlingit/Hopi chart
  *together* — the learner supplies the evidence phrases, you hold the
  pen. Then the project chart's Environment row. Success: identical rows
  on both sides before any sentences.
- **S4 (practice set B + review):** Review same day. If adapt/modify
  is still shaky, re-teach with the learner's own-life examples
  (coat vs. garden bed) before Week 9.

### Week 9 — perspectives (Lessons 04 + start 05)

- **S1 (Lesson 04, part 1):** Read the adult-selected accounts FIRST
  (from the Resource Pack — Plimoth's *You Are the Historian* or NMAI
  materials), then teach the five questions. Keep it curious, not
  courtroom. Success: the learner can name who/for whom/why for both
  accounts.
- **S2 (Lesson 04, part 2):** The reasons test (worked example 2) and
  guided practice. Guardrail: no invented thoughts for real people —
  only situation-based reasons. Then project Step 3 (evidence chart).
- **S3 (Lesson 05, part 1):** Teach the three-box chain with the
  Cherokee worked example. Read it slowly; let the learner place fact
  cards into boxes. Name removal plainly. Success: the learner can read
  the chain backward (perspective → situation → context).
- **S4 (formative quiz + review):** Score same day. Below 5/8: re-teach
  the weak lessons' worked examples before Week 10; do not advance on a
  shaky perspectives foundation.

### Week 10 — change over time, then/now, assessment (Lesson 05)

- **S1 (Lesson 05, part 2):** Then/now map work — trace Georgia →
  Oklahoma; place Tahlequah; note the Eastern Band in North Carolina.
  The application task (second nation, adult-selected sources).
- **S2 (practice set D + project Step 4):** Timeline sequencing, then
  the then-and-now write-up/poster. Insist on a named source for the
  "today" facts.
- **S3 (project Step 5):** Presentations (3–5 min) + the reflection
  question. Score with the project rubric; record privately.
- **S4 (unit assessment + unit close):** Assess; close by asking the
  learner to name one nation from the unit that exists today and one
  thing it is doing now. The unit ends in the present — always.

## Likely misconceptions (all lessons)

- "Culture area = one tribe." → Each area holds many nations; the area
  is the geographer's grouping.
- "The illustration is a photograph." → AI teaching picture; say so at
  every use. Real photos come from museum links.
- "Indigenous peoples are all in the past." → Present-tense language,
  present-day connections in every lesson, and the project's "today"
  section exist for this.
- "Adapt and modify are the same." → The "who changed?" test, drilled.
- "Two perspectives = pick your favorite truth." → Perspectives explain
  viewpoints; evidence judges claims.
- "The Cherokee gave up and moved." → The chain proves resistance
  (petition, newspaper, lawsuit, court victory) came first.

## Reteaching guidance

| If the learner struggles with… | Re-teach with… |
|---|---|
| Naming the eight areas | The illustration + map pointing drill; reduce to 4 pictured areas first, then add 4 |
| Adapt vs. modify | Own-life examples (coat/garden bed); the "who changed?" test on 10 rapid examples |
| Comparison charts | Pre-drawn identical rows; the learner only fills evidence cells, then sentences |
| Perspectives (who/for whom/why) | The soccer-game analogy from Lesson 04's readiness check; perspective cards with faces |
| Context → perspective chains | Fact cards sorted into three boxes physically; read chains backward |
| Timeline sequencing | A floor timeline the learner walks; one sentence per stop |

## Readiness signals

- **Ready for Unit 03:** the learner matches environments to ways of
  life with two clues, sorts adapt/modify with evidence, builds a
  two-perspective card, and reads a three-box chain — and names at
  least two nations that exist today.
- **Not yet:** cannot attach evidence to claims, or describes nations
  only in past tense. Re-teach the weak objective's lesson before
  Unit 03's encounter sources arrive.
