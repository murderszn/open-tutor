# Unit 03 — Waves, Patterns, and Information

**Grade:** 4 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#23](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/23)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 11–14 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 35 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

This unit answers a question learners can feel with their own hands: how can
something travel across a room — a ripple, a sound, a flash of light — without
the stuff itself traveling with it? Learners make waves in a water tray and
watch a floating cork bob in place instead of sailing across; they stretch
and pluck, drum and listen, and compare tall, closely spaced waves with low,
widely spaced ones. Then they turn to light: a flashlight, a mirror, and a
dark box show that we see an object only when light reflects off it and
enters the eye. Finally they become message-senders: drum rhythms, grids of
1's and 0's that draw a picture, and tap codes let them send a message
across the room without speaking — and they test which of their own
solutions actually works best against criteria they stated first.

The unit stays inside the grade-4 assessment boundaries throughout:
amplitude and wavelength are described qualitatively (taller/shorter,
closer/farther apart), never with numbers or formulas; interference effects,
electromagnetic waves, and non-periodic waves are not assessed; and color
vision, retinal cells, and other vision mechanisms are not taught. What is
taught is the pattern language itself — a wave is a repeating pattern that
carries energy and information from place to place.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Model waves** with labeled amplitude (wave height) and wavelength
   (spacing between peaks) and explain, with the cork observation, that a
   wave can move an object up and down without carrying it across
   (4-PS4-1).
2. **Model seeing** with a source → object → eye diagram: light reflects
   off an object and enters the eye, and without light reaching the eye
   the object cannot be seen (4-PS4-2).
3. **Generate and compare multiple pattern solutions** for sending
   information — drum rhythms, a 1/0 picture grid, and tap codes — and
   judge them against stated criteria and constraints (4-PS4-3;
   3-5-ETS1-2).
4. **Fair-test a message solution**: change one thing at a time, repeat
   trials, record decoded-message results honestly, and name a failure
   point with its prevention (3-5-ETS1-3 thread from Unit 01).
5. **Explain in words or drawings** that it is the *pattern*, not the
   object, that carries information — and give one example each from
   sound, light, and a picture grid.

## Essential question

*How can a pattern travel across a room and carry a message, even when nothing itself travels with it?*

## Vocabulary

**Wave** — a repeating pattern of motion that carries energy from place to
place. · **Pattern** — something that repeats in a regular way. ·
**Crest (peak)** — the top of a wave. · **Trough** — the bottom of a wave. ·
**Amplitude** — how tall a wave is, from its middle to a crest; described
here as taller or shorter, never measured numerically. · **Wavelength** —
the spacing between one crest and the next; described as closer together or
farther apart. · **Vibration** — a fast back-and-forth movement that makes
sound. · **Pitch** — how high or low a sound seems. · **Volume
(loudness)** — how loud or soft a sound seems. · **Light source** —
something that gives off light, such as the sun or a lamp. · **Reflect** —
light bouncing off a surface. · **Reflection** — the bouncing itself. ·
**Code** — an agreed pattern that stands for a message. · **Signal** — a
pattern sent to carry information. · **Receiver** — the person (or device)
that reads the signal. · **Criteria** — what a successful solution must
do. · **Constraints** — limits on materials, time, or cost. · **Fair test**
(from Unit 01) — a test that changes only one thing while everything else
stays the same. · **Trial** — one try of a test.

## Standards and source notes

- Reference framework: **Next Generation Science Standards**, grade-4
  performance expectations (from the track audit's crosswalk). No state
  adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification is claimed.
- **4-PS4-1** — *Develop a model of waves to describe patterns in terms
  of amplitude and wavelength and that waves can cause objects to move.*
  Clarification (NGSS/NSTA): models may be diagrams, analogies, or
  physical models using wire to show wavelength and amplitude.
  Assessment boundary, kept in this unit: assessment does not include
  interference effects, electromagnetic waves, non-periodic waves, or
  quantitative models of amplitude and wavelength. (Verified 2026-10-24
  against the NSTA NGSS topic page for 4-PS4.)
- **4-PS4-2** — *Develop a model to describe that light reflecting from
  objects and entering the eye allows objects to be seen.* Core idea
  (PS4.B): an object can be seen when light reflected from its surface
  enters the eyes. This unit does not teach which colors are reflected,
  how eye cells work, or how the retina functions.
- **4-PS4-3** — *Generate and compare multiple solutions that use
  patterns to transfer information.* Clarification examples used in this
  unit, exactly as the standard gives them: drums sending coded
  information through sound waves, a grid of 1's and 0's representing
  black and white to send information about a picture, and Morse code to
  send text. (Verified 2026-10-24 against the NSTA NGSS topic page.)
- **3-5-ETS1-2** — *Generate and compare multiple possible solutions to
  a problem based on how well each is likely to meet the criteria and
  constraints of the problem.* Applied in Lesson 05 and the project.
- **3-5-ETS1-3** — *Plan and carry out fair tests in which variables are
  controlled and failure points are considered.* The Unit 01 routine,
  reused for message-solution testing.
- Science and engineering practices: **SEP-2** (developing and using
  models — wave diagrams, light-path diagrams, code grids), **SEP-3**
  (planning and carrying out investigations — water tray, mirror, code
  tests), **SEP-6** (constructing explanations — the cork claim, the
  dark-room claim). Crosscutting concepts: **patterns** (waves and codes
  are repeating patterns), **cause and effect** (a bigger vibration makes
  a louder sound; light reaching the eye causes seeing).

## Materials (for the whole unit)

Shallow clear tray or baking pan; water; small pebble; small cork or bottle
cap; long rope or skipping rope; a wire bent into a wave shape (adult-made)
or a spring toy; drum or upturned pot with a wooden spoon; rubber bands of
different thickness; empty tissue box or small box (guitar box); flashlight
(battery only, adult-handled); small mirror; cardboard box with a peephole
(adult-cut); a small toy or brightly colored ball; science journal; pencils
and colored pencils; grid paper or printed 5-column grids; index cards for
code cards. **Adult reads the safety rules aloud before each
investigation:** never look at the sun or into a flashlight beam; water
trays shallow, adult nearby, spills wiped at once; sound levels kept
comfortable (drumming is short bursts, not shouting over it); no small
loose parts near the mouth; the adult cuts all cardboard. All
investigations are adult-supervised.

Session rotation per the scope-and-sequence: model-building lesson →
wave-and-light station practice → cause-and-effect talk → review game.

## Differentiation and supports

- **Reading/language access:** every lesson goal is read aloud; key words
  are shown on word cards with a picture (wave, mirror, drum); learners
  may answer orally, by pointing, or by drawing, and the adult records
  what they say.
- **Alternate response modes:** wave models may be built with rope or
  wire instead of drawn; codes may be tapped or shown on cards instead of
  written; the assessment allows drawn models in place of sentences.
- **Extensions:** design a code that can send a whole word, not just one
  of four messages; test whether a message still works through a closed
  door (sound) versus around a corner with mirrors (light); research, with
  the adult, how a real lighthouse or signal flag uses patterns — then
  explain it in the unit's pattern language.
- **Reteach triggers:** see [teacher-guide.md](teacher-guide.md). A
  learner who still says the cork "travels with the wave" after Session
  4 re-runs the tray test with a marked starting line for the cork.
