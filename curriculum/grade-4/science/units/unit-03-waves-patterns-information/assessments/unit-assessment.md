# Unit Assessment — Waves, Patterns, and Information

**Adult administers in Session 16, after the week-14 quiz.** 12 tasks
covering all five unit objectives, including a wave model, a seeing
model, a code, and a fair comparison. Oral, drawn, or manipulative
responses are acceptable; the adult records them. Keys and scoring
guidance in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) (adult only).
**This is a practice check, not a standardized or validated test.**

## Part 1 — Wave models (Objective 1)

1. Draw a wave with at least three crests. Label one crest, one
   trough, the amplitude, and one wavelength. Your drawing is judged
   on correct labels, not on artistic skill.
2. Wave A is tall with crests close together. Wave B is low with
   crests far apart. (a) Which has the bigger amplitude? (b) Which has
   the longer wavelength? (c) If both waves reached a floating cork,
   which would bob the cork higher, and why?
3. A friend watches ripples in a tray and says, "The wave is carrying
   the cork to the other side." Explain, with the cork observation,
   why this is wrong. Your answer must say what the cork actually
   does.

## Part 2 — Sound and light (Objectives 1–2)

4. A drum is hit softly, then hard. A rubber band is plucked loose,
   then tight. For each test, say what changes (pitch, volume, or
   both) and name the vibration cause.
5. Draw and label the seeing-chain model for sunlight, a red ball, and
   a person looking at it. Use arrows in the correct order. Then
   explain what happens to seeing if a thick cloud blocks the sun.
6. A learner shines a flashlight at a mirror and the bright spot
   lands on the wall. The learner tilts the mirror and the spot moves
   to the ceiling. Explain what happened to the light, using the word
   *reflect*.
7. In a sealed dark box, no light reaches the toy inside. Explain why
   a person looking through the peephole sees nothing, even though
   the toy is still in the box. Name the missing link of the seeing
   chain.

## Part 3 — Codes and comparisons (Objectives 3–5)

8. Decode this grid. What picture does it make, and what does each
   number mean?
   Row 1: 1 0 0 0 1; Row 2: 1 0 0 0 1; Row 3: 1 1 1 1 1; Row 4: 1 0 0 0 1.
9. Encode the letter T on a 4-row, 5-column grid of 1's and 0's, then
   explain in one sentence why changing one number in row 1 would
   change the message.
10. Plan a fair comparison of a drum code and a flashlight code for
    sending four messages across a room. Name: the one change, three
    things kept the same, the number of trials, the measurement (with
    its counting unit), and one failure point with its prevention.
11. Practice data (fictional): drum code, 3 trials of 4 messages:
    4, 4, 3 right. Flashlight code: 3, 3, 4 right. (a) What is each
    total out of 12? (b) Which solution is more accurate, and by how
    many messages? (c) Write a one-sentence recommendation supported
    by those totals.
12. A team sends its messages by shouting and gets every message
    right, but the constraints say "no speaking." Does the shouting
    solution win? Explain the difference between meeting a criterion
    (accuracy) and breaking a constraint — and say what the team
    should do instead.

**Scoring guide:** 11–12 = strong; 8–10 = developing (revisit the
missed objectives with the matching practice set); ≤7 = reteach the
missed lessons and re-assess those objectives only, before Unit 04.
