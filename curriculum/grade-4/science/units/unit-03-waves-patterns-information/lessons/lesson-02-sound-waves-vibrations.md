# Lesson 02 — Sound Waves: Vibrations You Can Hear

**Time:** 30–35 minutes · **Materials:** drum or upturned pot with a
wooden spoon, rubber bands of different thickness, empty tissue box or
small box, two paper cups and string (string telephone, adult-made),
science journal.

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will find out that sound
starts with a *vibration* — a fast back-and-forth movement — and that
changing the vibration changes the sound you hear.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "What do you feel in your throat when
you hum?" Feeling buzzing or movement shows readiness.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

Sound is a wave too, but you cannot see its pattern the way you see
water ripples. Sound starts when something **vibrates** — moves back
and forth very fast. The vibration pushes the air around it, and that
pushing pattern travels to your ears. That is why sound is a wave: a
pattern that carries energy from place to place.

Two things about a sound change when the vibration changes, and both
are described in plain words in grade 4 — never with numbers:

- **Volume (loudness)** — how loud or soft a sound seems. A bigger
  vibration makes a louder sound. Hit a drum gently: small vibration,
  soft sound. Hit it hard: bigger vibration, louder sound.
- **Pitch** — how high or low a sound seems. A faster back-and-forth
  vibration makes a higher sound. A slower back-and-forth makes a
  lower sound. Stretch a rubber band tight and pluck it: it vibrates
  fast, and the sound is high. Stretch it loosely and pluck it: it
  vibrates slowly, and the sound is low.

Keep the two words apart: loudness is about *how big* the vibration
is; pitch is about *how fast* it moves back and forth. A sound can be
loud and low (a big drum) or soft and high (a quiet whistle).

**Safety:** drumming is in short bursts. If a sound hurts your ears or
feels uncomfortable, stop and tell the adult at once.

**Worked example 1 — drum, soft and hard**

> Adult thinks aloud, drumming: *"Tap… tap… That was gentle. The drum
> skin barely moves, the vibration is small, and the sound is soft.
> Now one firm hit — BANG. The skin jumps more, the vibration is
> bigger, and the sound is louder. I changed only how hard I hit.
> Everything else — same drum, same stick — stayed the same. So the
> cause of the louder sound is the bigger vibration. That is a fair
> comparison, the way Unit 01 taught us: one change, the rest kept the
> same."*

**Worked example 2 — rubber band, tight and loose**

> Adult models with two bands over the box: *"This thick band is
> stretched loose. Pluck — low, slow sound. I can almost see it
> wobbling back and forth slowly. Now the thin band, stretched tight.
> Pluck — high sound, and the band is a blur because it moves back
> and forth fast. Two changes happened here at once — thickness AND
> tightness — so this is NOT a fair test of tightness alone. To test
> tightness fairly, I would use the same band, first loose, then
> tight. Remember: a test that changes two things cannot tell you
> which one caused the difference."*

Key idea to say plainly: **Sound is a vibration traveling as a wave.
Bigger vibration = louder sound. Faster vibration = higher sound.
Loudness and pitch are different things.**

## Guided practice (adult leads, learner answers and does)

1. Hum a low note, then a high note, with your fingers lightly on your
   throat. What do you feel changing?
2. Drum test (fair): hit the drum softly three times, then firmly three
   times. Describe the volume change and name its cause.
3. Band test (fair): pluck the same rubber band loose, then stretched
   tight. Describe the pitch change and name its cause.
4. The string telephone: one person talks softly into a cup while the
   string is pulled tight, then again while the string hangs slack.
   When is the message clearer? Why do you think so?
5. Sort these into "louder/softer" or "higher/lower": a whisper versus a
   shout; a baby's voice versus a grown-up's deep voice; a drum tapped
   gently versus struck hard.

## Independent practice

1. List two sounds from home or school. For each, say whether it is
   loud or soft, and high or low.
2. A guitar string is plucked gently, then plucked hard. What changes
   — pitch, volume, or both? Explain in one sentence.
3. A rubber band is stretched tighter and tighter. What happens to its
   pitch as it gets tighter? What would happen if it were loosened?
4. Explain in your own words why a drum hit and a whistle sound
   different, using the words *vibration*, *loud*, and *high* or *low*.
5. Error analysis: a friend says, "The loud drum must be the
   high-pitched one, because loud and high mean the same thing."
   Explain the friend's mistake with one example.

## Applied task

Build a "two-sound instrument" from the box and two rubber bands of
different thickness, stretched to the same tightness. Play a short
pattern: low–high–low–high. Then change one thing — stretch one band
tighter — and play again. Record what changed and what stayed the
same, and say whether your second test was fair.

## Exit check

Adult plucks a band (learner not watching the band): is the sound high
or low, loud or soft? Learner answers both words correctly and names
the vibration cause for one of them.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** feel the vibration instead of only hearing it — hold a
  hand near the drum skin, or rest fingertips on the box while the band
  is plucked. The adult records oral answers.
- **Extension:** invent a three-beat sound pattern (for example,
  soft–loud–soft) and teach it to the adult by drumming only. The adult
  must repeat the pattern back correctly — the pattern itself carries
  the message, a preview of Lesson 04.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: loud = high. The drum/whistle contrast in the
guided practice exists to break it. Also watch for "sound travels
because air moves across the room like wind" — correct gently: the
pattern of pushing travels; the air itself does not blow from the drum
to the ear. Do not introduce frequency numbers (hertz) or wave-speed
numbers. Answer-key reference: [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md),
Lesson 02.
