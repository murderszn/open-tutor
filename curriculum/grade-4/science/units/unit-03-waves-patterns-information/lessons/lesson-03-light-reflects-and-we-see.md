# Lesson 03 — Light Reflects, Enters the Eye, and We See

**Time:** 30–35 minutes · **Materials:** flashlight (battery only,
adult-handled), small mirror, cardboard box with a peephole (adult-cut)
containing a small bright toy, science journal, colored pencils.

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will find out why you can see
things at all: light bounces off them and travels into your eyes.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "In a room with no windows and the
light off, can you see your hand?" Any answer about darkness shows
readiness for the test.

**Safety (adult reads aloud first):** Never look directly at the sun.
Never shine a flashlight into anyone's eyes, and never stare into the
flashlight beam. The adult holds the flashlight for mirror work.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

Seeing is a chain with three links, and every link must be present:

1. A **light source** gives off light — the sun, a lamp, a flashlight.
2. The light travels to an object and **reflects** — bounces off its
   surface.
3. The reflected light travels on and **enters the eye**.

Break any link and seeing stops. No light source, no light to bounce.
No object, nothing to bounce off. Light that never reaches the eye is
light you never see.

A mirror shows reflection clearly: light hits the smooth surface and
bounces away in a new direction. A rough surface, like paper, scatters
light in many directions instead — which is why paper does not give a
clear mirror picture, even though it still reflects light well enough
for you to see the paper itself.

**Worked example 1 — the dark box**

> Adult thinks aloud with the peephole box: *"The box is sealed tight.
> I look through the peephole. Black. Nothing. Is the toy gone? No —
> I put it in there myself. What is missing? Light. No light source
> inside the box means no light can bounce off the toy, so no light
> reaches my eye. Now the adult opens a small flap that lets light in
> — I look again, and there is the toy, same toy, same peephole, same
> eye. The only thing that changed was letting light in. So the cause
> of seeing is light reaching the eye, not the toy appearing or my eye
> changing."*

**Worked example 2 — moving the mirror spot**

> Adult models with flashlight and mirror: *"The adult shines the
> flashlight at the mirror, and a bright spot lands on the wall. Now
> the adult tilts the mirror a little — and the spot jumps to the
> ceiling. The light did not stop at the mirror. It bounced off and
> kept going somewhere new. If I draw this, my arrows must go:
> flashlight → mirror → wall. An arrow that stops at the mirror is
> unfinished, because reflected light keeps traveling — all the way
> to an eye, when an eye is in its path."*

Key idea to say plainly: **We see when light from a source reflects off
an object and enters the eye. No light reaching the eye means no
seeing — even if the object is right there.**

## Guided practice (adult leads, learner answers and does)

1. Draw the seeing chain for a lamp, a book, and your eye. Draw arrows
   in the correct order and label each link.
2. Peephole box, round 1: look with the box sealed. What do you see?
   Round 2: look with the flap open. What changed, and what does that
   prove?
3. Mirror spot: predict where the spot will go if the adult tilts the
   mirror the other way. Test and describe what happened.
4. Name the three links of the seeing chain for this scene: sunlight,
   a red ball on the grass, and you looking at it. Which link is the
   light source? Which is the object? Where does the light end up?
5. A friend says, "My eyes send out light to find things, like a
   flashlight." Use the dark-box test to explain why that is wrong.

## Independent practice

1. Draw and label a model of seeing a cat in a sunny garden. Include
   the sun, the cat, and an eye, with arrows in the correct order.
2. A room's lamp is switched off. Explain, using the three links, why
   you can no longer see the chair that is still in the room.
3. Light hits a mirror and then a wall. Draw the path with arrows. Does
   the light stop at the mirror? Explain.
4. Which of these is a light source: the moon, a lamp, a mirror, a book?
   Explain each choice in one phrase. (Think carefully about the moon.)
5. A learner looks into the sealed dark box and says, "The toy
   disappeared." Write a better explanation of what happened, using the
   word *reflect*.

## Applied task

With the adult, test three surfaces with the flashlight in a dim room:
a mirror, white paper, and a dark cloth. For each, record: does it make
a bright spot somewhere else (strong, clear reflection), a soft glow
(scattered reflection), or almost nothing? Then answer: which surface
would be best for sending a light signal across a room, and why?

## Exit check

Learner names the three links of the seeing chain in order, without
looking at the journal, and says what happens when the first link is
removed.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** build the chain with three cards on the table — "light
  source," "object," "eye" — and move a toy arrow along them while
  retelling the dark-box story, instead of writing sentences.
- **Extension:** predict and test what happens to the mirror spot when
  the flashlight moves but the mirror stays still. Draw both arrow
  paths and compare them.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: (a) eyes send out light — the dark-box test is
the cure, because an eye that sent light would see in the dark box;
(b) the moon as a light source — the moon reflects sunlight; it does
not make its own light, so in the chain it is an object, not a source.
Do not teach retinal cells, color-vision mechanisms, or refraction —
all outside the grade-4 boundary. Answer-key reference:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md), Lesson 03.
