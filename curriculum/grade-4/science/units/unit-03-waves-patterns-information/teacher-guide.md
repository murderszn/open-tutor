## Readiness evidence and reteach triggers

- **After Session 1:** if the learner cannot label amplitude vs.
  wavelength, return to the rope wave (big shakes = tall; spacing of
  peaks = wavelength) before Session 2. Do not proceed to sound until
  the two words are stable — every later lesson reuses them.
- **After Session 4:** if the cork claim is still "the wave carries
  it," re-run the applied task with the rim mark and ask only: "Did
  the cork end far from its mark, or close to it?" Evidence first,
  vocabulary second.
- **After Session 8:** if the seeing chain is drawn eye → object
  (reversed), re-run the sealed dark box: an eye that sent light
  would see in the dark. That single test corrects the direction.
- **After Session 10:** if grid decoding fails, drop to 3-column grids
  (letter I: 1 / 1 / 1 / 1 in one column) and rebuild to 5 columns.
- **After the week-14 quiz:** score ≤6 on any objective's items →
  reteach that lesson with its practice set before Session 16; do not
  re-administer the same quiz items as the reteach.

## Misconceptions to watch (by lesson)

- **Lesson 01:** waves carry objects across (cork test); amplitude and
  wavelength swapped (height vs. spacing chant: "amplitude = how tall,
  wavelength = how far apart"); waves need numbers (they do not, in
  grade 4 — keep all comparisons qualitative).
- **Lesson 02:** loud = high (drum vs. whistle contrast); sound travels
  as wind blowing across the room (the pattern of pushing travels; the
  air does not blow from drum to ear); pitch and volume as one thing
  (two separate questions in every practice item).
- **Lesson 03:** eyes send out light (dark-box cure); the moon is a
  light source (it reflects sunlight — an object in the chain, not a
  source); light stops at a mirror (mirror-spot movement shows it
  keeps going).
- **Lesson 04:** the drum/paper/light "is" the message (same carrier,
  different pattern, different message); codes may change mid-stream
  (a code changed without telling the receiver is a broken code).
- **Lesson 05:** fastest/loudest = best regardless of accuracy
  (criteria order: constraints first, then accuracy, then time);
  failed trials may be deleted (honest recording is a rubric
  criterion, not a punishable mistake).

## Differentiation

- **Reading/language access:** read every goal aloud; word cards with
  pictures for wave, crest, mirror, drum, code; oral, pointing, and
  drawn answers always accepted and recorded by the adult.
- **Alternate response modes:** rope/wire wave models instead of
  drawings; tapped or card-shown codes instead of written ones; drawn
  seeing-chain models instead of sentences.
- **Extensions:** a code that sends a whole word (Lesson 04); a
  through-a-door sound test vs. an around-a-corner mirror test
  (Lesson 05 follow-up); an adult-guided look at how a lighthouse or
  signal flag uses patterns, explained back in the unit's pattern
  language.
- **Support:** pre-drawn criteria tables with picture symbols for the
  project; 3-column grids before 5-column grids; the adult reads all
  grid rows aloud during decoding.

## Original read-aloud passages

These two short passages are original to this unit. Read them aloud
during Session 3 (talk) and Session 9 (code introduction); they are
discussion starters, not texts to memorize.

**The Ripple Race (Session 3)**

> Sam dropped two pebbles into the tray, one after the other. The
> first pebble made small, low ripples. The second, dropped harder,
> made tall ripples that rose higher and fell lower. A cork floated
> near the edge. When the small ripples reached it, the cork rocked
> gently, up and down, up and down. When the tall ripples reached it,
> the cork bobbed much higher — but after both sets of ripples had
> passed, the cork was still floating in about the same place. "The
> ripples came to the cork," Sam said, "but the cork never went to
> the ripples." Sam's older sister nodded. "The pattern traveled,"
> she said. "The cork just danced."

**The Message in the Dark (Session 9)**

> At camp, Maya and Jon slept in different tents, and talking after
> lights-out was against the rules. So they agreed on a code before
> bed: one flash of the flashlight meant "good night," two flashes
> meant "are you awake," and three flashes meant "come to my tent
> in the morning." That night, Jon flashed twice. Maya counted,
> checked her card, and flashed once back: good night. No one spoke.
> No one walked over. But a message had crossed the dark all the
> same — not as a flashlight traveling, but as a pattern both of
> them understood.

## Safety checklist (read aloud before each investigation)

- Never look at the sun or into a flashlight beam; the adult holds
  the flashlight for mirror and signal work.
- Water trays stay shallow; the adult stays nearby; spills wiped at
  once; water kept away from outlets.
- Drumming in short bursts at a comfortable level; stop at once if
  any sound feels uncomfortable.
- No small loose parts near the mouth; the adult cuts all cardboard
  (peephole box) and bends all wire (wave model).
- Nothing is tasted; nothing from the investigations goes near faces.
