student materials. Do not show this file to the learner.

## Lesson 04 — guided practice (fictional practice dataset)

Brands: A 30/32, B 44/46, C 28/29 mL.

1. Bar graph: A ≈ 31, B ≈ 45, C ≈ 28.5 mL; B tallest. All four display
   requirements present.
2. B held about 45 − 28.5 ≈ **16–17 mL more** than C (accept any answer
   with the subtraction shown).
3. Line plot: X's above 30 and 32.
4. The X's sit close together — the trials agree; the result is
   trustworthy.
5. Pictograph (1 drop = 10 mL): B ≈ 45 mL → 4 full drops + about a half.
6. Without a unit the reader cannot tell *how much*; without a title the
   reader cannot tell *what the graph is about*.

## Practice Set D (fictional practice dataset)

Plants: P1 ≈ 9.3, P2 ≈ 14.3, P3 ≈ 11.3 cm.

1. Bar graph with title, labels, unit; P2 tallest.
2. About **5–6 cm** (14.3 − 9.3 ≈ 5); accept with subtraction shown.
3. Line plot: X's above 11, 12, 11.
4. The X's cluster — the measurements agree.
5. Pictograph (1 leaf = 2 cm): P1 ≈ 4½ leaves, P2 ≈ 7, P3 ≈ 5½–6.
6. Without labels you cannot tell which bar is which plant (or the unit).
7. The 25 cm reading is a likely error — check the notes, re-measure
   honestly, do not hide it.
8. A line plot keeps every single trial visible, so agreement and outliers
   both show.

## Unit assessment

**Part 1**

1. a. T — count paper clips picked up. b. N — "scarier" is an opinion. c.
   T — measure plant height in cm. (6 pts: 1 per sort, 1 per measurement)
2. Value varies; 1 pt for the measurement, 1 for the unit, 1 for a correct
   mm→cm conversion (÷10). (3 pts)
3. a. 700 cm. b. 9 m. c. 4,000 g. d. 3.5 L. (4 pts)
4. Two-column table: 1|1000, 2|2000, 5|5000 (kg|g). (3 pts)

**Part 2**

5. Sample: change = amount of light (sunny windowsill vs. dim corner);
   controls = same bean kind, same soil and pot, same water amount;
   trials = 3 seeds per condition; measure = days until sprout appears
   (or sprout height in cm after 7 days); failure point = soil dries out
   → check and water on a set schedule. (6 pts)
6. a. Unfair — the ball AND the push change; fix: release both the same
   way on the same ramp. b. Fair. (4 pts)
7. a. Bar graph with title, labels, mL scale; P ≈ 61, Q = 45, R ≈ 55.7
   (2 pts). b. Sponge P held the most — e.g., "P's trials were 60, 62,
   61 mL, all above Q's 44–46 mL" (2 pts). c. R's 30 mL trial is far from
   its other two (58, 59) — a likely error; check notes and re-run that
   trial honestly rather than hiding it (2 pts).
8. Line plot: X's above 60, 62, 61; pattern = they cluster — the trials
   agree. (2 pts)

**Part 3**

9. Sample: need — carry lunch without bruising the apple; criteria —
   "the apple is unbruised after the walk to school," "the box closes
   fully"; constraints — "only materials at home," "made in one evening."
   (3 pts)
10. Question, plan, data, display, claim, design connection (2 pts).
    Sample claim sentence: "Sponge P held the most water — about 61 mL per
    trial, about 16 mL more than sponge Q. Limitation: sponge R's 30 mL
    trial looks like an error and needs a re-test." (1 pt)

**Part 4**

11. Labeled diagram of any unit tool with 3 use/care labels (2 pts).
12. Sample: "A model is a drawing, object, or description that represents
    and helps explain something real. Two models I built: my labeled
    observation diagram and my bar graph (or line plot)." (2 pts)

**Total: 44 points.** 38–44 = strong (ready for Unit 02); 28–37 =
developing (reteach missed objectives with the matching practice set);
below 28 = reteach the unit's core lessons before moving on.
