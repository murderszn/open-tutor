# Unit Assessment — Evidence, Measurement, and Scientific Models

**Adult administers in Session 16, after the week-06 quiz.** 12 tasks
covering all five unit objectives, including a fair-test plan and a
data-based claim. Oral, drawn, or manipulative responses are acceptable;
the adult records them. Keys and scoring guidance in
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) (adult only).
**This is a practice check, not a standardized or validated test.**

## Part 1 — Questions and measurement (Objectives 1–2)

1. Sort each as testable (T) or not testable (N), and for each testable one
   name the measurement:
   a. "Which magnet picks up more paper clips?"
   b. "Is thunder scarier than lightning?"
   c. "Does a plant in sunlight grow taller than one in shade?"
2. Measure the length of a crayon or pencil to the nearest millimeter.
   Record the value with its unit, then convert it to centimeters.
3. Convert and show your work:
   a. 7 m = ___ cm
   b. 900 cm = ___ m
   c. 4 kg = ___ g
   d. 3,500 mL = ___ L
4. Fill the two-column table (kg | g) for 1, 2, 5 kg.

## Part 2 — Fair tests and data (Objectives 3–4)

5. Plan a fair test for: "Does the amount of light change how fast a bean
   seed sprouts?" Name the one change, three controls, the number of
   trials, the measurement with its unit, and one failure point with its
   prevention.
6. Judge fair or unfair, and fix each unfair one:
   a. "Which ball rolls farther? A marble released gently and a tennis
      ball thrown hard, same ramp."
   b. "Does water temperature change how fast salt dissolves? Same salt,
      same water amount, same stirring, warm vs. cold water, 3 tries each."
7. Practice data (fictional — labeled "practice data"): three sponges
   tested for water held — Sponge P: 60, 62, 61 mL; Sponge Q: 45, 44, 46
   mL; Sponge R: 58, 30, 59 mL.
   a. Draw a bar graph comparing the sponges (title, labels, mL scale).
   b. Which sponge held the most water? Support your answer with two data
      values.
   c. Something is odd about Sponge R's data. What is it, and what should
      the scientist do?
8. Draw a line plot for Sponge P's three trials. What pattern do you see?

## Part 3 — Design and reporting (Objective 5)

9. Write a design problem for "a lunchbox that keeps an apple from
   bruising": state the need, two measurable criteria, and two constraints.
10. Name the six parts of an investigation report and write one sentence
    for the **Claim** part about the sponge data in task 7 (cite two data
    values and one limitation).

## Part 4 — Models (Objectives 1, 5)

11. Draw a labeled diagram (a model) of one tool you used this unit. Add
    three labels explaining what it measures or how to use it carefully.
12. In one or two sentences, explain what a **model** is and name two
    models you built in this unit. *(e.g., a model is a drawing, object,
    or description that represents and helps explain something real; the
    labeled tool/observation diagram and the bar graph/line plot)*

**Scoring:** see answer-key.md. 38–44 points = strong (ready for Unit 02);
28–37 = developing (reteach missed objectives with the matching practice
set); below 28 = reteach the unit's core lessons before moving on.
