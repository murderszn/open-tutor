# Practice Sets — Unit 01

Short written work (8–12 tasks per set) that the adult reviews the same day.
Oral, drawn, or manipulative answers are always acceptable alternatives; the
adult records what the learner says. Keys are in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md)
(adult only).

## Set A — Testable questions and observations (after Lesson 01)

1. Sort: "How many legs does a spider have?" — testable or not? Name the
   measurement or observation. *(testable — count legs by observing)*
2. Sort: "Are spiders scary?" — testable or not? Why? *(not testable —
   opinion)*
3. Write one testable question about the weather and name what you would
   measure. *(e.g., "How much rain fell today?" — measure mL in a rain
   gauge)*
4. Which tool measures how heavy an apple is: hand lens, ruler, or scale?
   *(scale)*
5. Which tool shows the tiny hairs on a leaf? *(hand lens)*
6. You pour water into a cylinder up to the 80-mL line. How much water is
   there? What unit? *(80 mL)*
7. Draw a labeled diagram (a model) of one tool from the tool tray with
   three labels.
8. Fix this question so it is testable: "What is the coolest rock?"
   *(e.g., "Which rock is heaviest?" — measure grams; or "Which rock is
   longest?" — measure cm)*
9. A friend says "the plant grew a lot." Why is that not useful evidence?
   *(no measurement — "a lot" is vague; needs cm)*
10. Name one safety rule for the tool tray. *(e.g., tools back in the tray;
    nothing near the mouth; adult supervises)*

## Set B — Measuring and converting (after Lesson 02)

1. A pencil measures 14 cm. How many millimeters is that? *(140 mm)*
2. 3 m = ___ cm *(300)*
3. 500 cm = ___ m *(5)*
4. 2 km = ___ m *(2,000)*
5. 6 kg = ___ g *(6,000)*
6. 2,500 mL = ___ L *(2.5)*
7. Look at the [measurement picture](lessons/lesson-02-measuring-and-converting.md):
   name the two mistakes in the wrong station. *(the zero end is not
   aligned with the pencil's tip; the ruler sits crooked instead of flat)*
   **Text-only alternative:** The left station shows a ruler laid flat
   alongside a yellow pencil with the ruler's zero end exactly at the
   pencil's tip. The right station shows a ruler tilted at an angle next to
   an identical yellow pencil, with its zero end lifted away from the
   pencil's tip instead of aligned with it.
8. Maya measured 60 cm but started at the 2-cm mark. Real length? *(58 cm)*
9. Fill the two-column table: 1 L = ___ mL; 2 L = ___ mL; 3 L = ___ mL.
   *(1,000; 2,000; 3,000)*
10. Which is longer: 1 m 50 cm, or 140 cm? Show the conversion. *(1 m 50 cm
    = 150 cm, so it is longer)*
11. Explain in one sentence why the number gets bigger when you convert
    meters to centimeters. *(centimeters are smaller, so you need more of
    them)*
12. Estimate then measure: how many millimeters wide is your thumb? Record
    both numbers with units. *(answers vary; the adult checks the ritual)*

## Set C — Fair tests (after Lesson 03)

Judge fair or unfair; name the problem and the fix for unfair ones.

1. "Does the ball's size change bounce height? Same drop height, same
   surface, 3 drops each, two ball sizes." *(fair)*
2. "Which soap makes more bubbles? Big scoop of soap A, tiny scoop of
   soap B." *(unfair — soap amount changes; same scoop of each)*
3. "Do seeds need water? One seed watered, one seed dry, same light, same
   soil, same seed kind." *(fair — one change: water)*
4. "Which car is fastest? Car A rolls on a smooth floor, car B rolls on
   carpet." *(unfair for that question — the car AND the floor change;
   fix: race both cars on the same floor so only the car changes)*
5. "Does stirring speed change how fast sugar dissolves? Fast stir vs. slow
   stir, same sugar, same water amount and temperature." *(fair)*
6. "Which paper towel is strongest? Brand A pulled gently, brand B yanked
   hard." *(unfair — pulling force changes; pull the same way)*
7. Write the one change, two controls, and trials for: "Does the amount of
   water change how tall a bean plant grows?" *(change: water amount;
   controls: same seed kind, same light, same soil; 3 plants per amount)*
8. Name one failure point in the bean-plant test and how to prevent it.
   *(e.g., a plant gets knocked over — place pots in a tray away from the
   edge)*
9. True or false: "A fair test can change the variable halfway through if
   the first results look wrong." *(false — changing mid-test breaks the
   comparison; finish, then plan a new test)*
10. Why do scientists repeat trials instead of trusting one try? Use the
    word "pattern." *(one try could be a fluke; repeats reveal the pattern)*

## Set D — Data displays (after Lesson 04)

Practice dataset (fictional — labeled "practice data"): plant heights after
two weeks — Plant 1: 9, 9, 10 cm; Plant 2: 14, 15, 14 cm; Plant 3: 11, 12,
11 cm. (Each plant measured 3 times.)

1. Draw a bar graph comparing the three plants (use each plant's middle
   value). Include title, labels, unit. *(~9, ~14.5, ~11.5 cm)*
2. How many more centimeters did Plant 2 grow than Plant 1? *(about 5–6 cm;
   accept with subtraction shown)*
3. Draw the line plot for Plant 3's three measurements. *(X's above 11, 12,
   11)*
4. What pattern do the X's show? *(they cluster — the measurements agree)*
5. Make a pictograph of the three plants' middle values where one leaf
   symbol = 2 cm. *(≈4.5, 7, 6 leaves)*
6. A classmate's graph has bars but no labels. What can you NOT tell from
   it? *(which bar is which plant; possibly the unit)*
7. The three measurements of Plant 1 are 9, 9, 10 cm. The next measurement
   is 25 cm. What do you do? *(treat 25 as a likely error; check notes;
   re-measure honestly)*
8. Explain why a line plot is useful for repeated trials. *(every single
   measurement stays visible, so you can see agreement or outliers)*

## Set E — Review week (Sessions 13–14)

Mixed review for the measurement challenge and the fair-test redesign game.

1. Convert: 4 m 25 cm = ___ cm *(425)*; 3,000 g = ___ kg *(3)*; 1.5 L =
   ___ mL *(1,500)*.
2. Build the two-column table for m → cm for 1–4 m. *(100, 200, 300, 400)*
3. Redesign to fair: "Which sponge holds more? Sponge A soaked 1 minute,
   sponge B soaked 5 minutes, different water amounts." *(same soak time,
   same water amount — only the sponge changes; 3 trials each)*
4. Redesign to fair: "Does ice melt faster in sun or shade? Sun cup is big,
   shade cup is small." *(same-size cups — only the location changes)*
5. Read: a bar graph shows water held — A: 40 mL, B: 55 mL, C: 38 mL.
   Which claim is supported? "B held the most" or "A and C are equal"?
   *(B held the most; A and C are close but not equal — 40 vs 38)*
6. Name the six parts of the investigation report. *(question, plan, data,
   display, claim, design connection)*
7. Write one criterion and one constraint for: "a box that keeps a cookie
   from breaking in a backpack." *(criterion: cookie unbroken after a
   drop test; constraint: box fits in the backpack / only recycled
   materials)*
8. One limitation of the paper-towel project: write it in your own words.
   *(e.g., we tested one package of each brand; results might differ with
   another package)*
