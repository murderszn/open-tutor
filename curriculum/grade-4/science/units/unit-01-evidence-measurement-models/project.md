# Project — The Paper-Towel Investigation

**Question:** *Which paper-towel brand holds the most water?*

The unit's one-variable investigation. Runs across Sessions 10 (planning +
first trials) and 13 (finish trials, display, report). Adult-supervised
throughout.

## Materials

Three brands or types of paper towel (labeled A, B, C); a measuring cup or
graduated cylinder marked in milliliters; a spoon or small cup for pouring;
three identical shallow trays or plates; a timer or clock; science journal;
the investigation-report skeleton (Lesson 05).

**Safety:** Adult pours any water the learner cannot handle safely; spills
wiped at once; water kept away from outlets; nothing near the mouth; towels
go in the compost or trash after — no reusing wet towels for faces.

## Steps

1. **Question** (Session 10): Write the testable question. Name the
   measurement hidden inside it (milliliters of water held).
2. **Plan** (Session 10): Fill the fair-test plan —
   - **Change (variable):** the paper-towel brand (A, B, C).
   - **Keep the same (controls):** the same amount of water poured on each
     (e.g., exactly 100 mL), the same waiting time (e.g., 1 minute), the
     same tray, the same way of lifting the towel.
   - **Trials:** 3 per brand.
   - **Measurement:** milliliters of water the towel holds. Decide together
     how to find it: *pour 100 mL onto the towel in the tray, wait 1
     minute, lift the towel, measure the water left in the tray, subtract
     from 100.* (Adult checks the subtraction plan.)
   - **Failure point:** name one thing that could go wrong and its
     prevention (e.g., "the towel tears when I lift it" → "lift it with
     two hands over the tray"; "water spills off the tray" → "work inside
     a larger baking pan").
3. **Data** (Sessions 10 and 13): Run all 9 trials. Record every trial in
   a labeled table with the unit (mL).
4. **Display** (Session 13): Build a bar graph of the three brands (title,
   labels, mL scale) and a line plot of one brand's three trials.
5. **Claim** (Session 13): Write the claim supported by at least two data
   values, plus one limitation (e.g., "we tested one package of each
   brand; a new package might differ").
6. **Report** (Session 16): Complete the six-part investigation report
   (Lesson 05 format). The adult reviews it the same day.

## Deliverables

1. The completed fair-test plan (question, variable, ≥3 controls, trials,
   unit, failure point).
2. The labeled data table with all 9 trials in mL.
3. The bar graph and the line plot (title, labels, unit on each).
4. The six-part investigation report with a claim citing ≥2 data values
   and one limitation.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (3) | Developing (2) | Starting (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fair plan** | One variable named; ≥3 controls listed; 3 trials per brand; failure point with prevention | Variable named; 1–2 controls; trials planned but uneven | Variable missing or >1 change; no controls or trials |
| **Measurement** | Every value recorded with mL; ruler/cylinder ritual followed (eye level, correct units) | Most values have units; minor ritual slips | Units missing; measurements inconsistent |
| **Data display** | Bar graph + line plot, each with title, labels, unit, and correct data | Both displays present; one requirement missing | Displays missing or data mismatched |
| **Claim and limitation** | Claim cites ≥2 data values; one honest limitation named | Claim cites 1 value; limitation vague | Claim without data; no limitation |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = strong; 7–9 = developing (reteach the missed part);
4–6 = starting (redo the investigation with adult support).
