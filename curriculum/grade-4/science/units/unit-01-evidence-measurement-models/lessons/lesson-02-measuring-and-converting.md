# Lesson 02 — Measuring and Converting Within a System

**Time:** 30–35 minutes · **Materials:** science journal, pencil, metric
ruler (mm/cm), kitchen scale (grams), graduated cylinder or marked measuring
cup (mL), a pencil, an eraser, a small book, the AI-generated measurement
picture below (displayed or printed).

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will measure length, mass, and
volume the careful way — and learn how to rewrite a measurement in a
different unit of the same system.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "Name one tool from yesterday and one thing
it measures." Hand lens → close-up looks; ruler → length; scale → mass;
cylinder → volume.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

Good scientists measure the **careful way**: estimate first, start the ruler
at **zero**, read at **eye level**, and always **write the unit**.

The metric system fits together in tens, hundreds, and thousands. Learn the
ladders:

- **Length:** 10 millimeters = 1 centimeter · 100 centimeters = 1 meter ·
  1,000 meters = 1 kilometer. (mm → cm → m → km)
- **Mass:** 1,000 grams = 1 kilogram.
- **Volume:** 1,000 milliliters = 1 liter.

To **convert** is to rewrite a measurement in a different unit of the same
system. Going from a *larger* unit to a *smaller* one, the number gets
**bigger** (multiply): there are more centimeters than meters in the same
length. Going from a *smaller* unit to a *larger* one, the number gets
**smaller** (divide).

**Worked example 1 — measuring the careful way**

> Adult models: *"Estimate: the pencil looks about 15 cm. Now measure: I
> lay the ruler flat, line the pencil's tip up with the zero mark — not the
> edge of the ruler — and read the other end: 16 cm, and the tip reaches 3
> little marks past 16, so 16.3 cm. I write '16.3 cm' with the unit."*
> Then the adult converts: *"16.3 cm to millimeters: 1 cm = 10 mm, so
> 16.3 × 10 = 163 mm. The number got bigger because millimeters are
> smaller."*

**Worked example 2 — the conversion ladder and a two-column table**

> Adult: *"Question: which is longer, 2 m or 210 cm? Convert to the same
> unit. 2 m = 200 cm (2 × 100). So 2 m is shorter than 210 cm."* The adult
> records pairs in a **two-column table** — a table that pairs equivalent
> measurements:

| Meters (m) | Centimeters (cm) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 100 |
| 2 | 200 |
| 3 | 300 |

> *"Each row says the same length two ways. Two-column tables are how we
> keep conversion records honest — both columns must stay equivalent."*

## Guided practice (adult leads, learner measures and converts)

1. Estimate, then measure the eraser's length in cm and mm. Record both.
   *(≈3 cm / 30 mm; exact value depends on the eraser — the adult checks the
   10× relationship)*
2. Measure the book's width in cm; convert to mm. *(width × 10)*
3. Convert 5 m to cm. *(500 cm)*
4. Convert 3 km to m. *(3,000 m)*
5. Convert 250 cm to m. *(2.5 m — smaller number for the larger unit)*
6. Convert 4 L to mL. *(4,000 mL)*
7. Convert 7 kg to g. *(7,000 g)*
8. Which is heavier: 2 kg or 1,900 g? *(2 kg = 2,000 g, so 2 kg is heavier)*
9. Build a two-column table pairing kg and g for 1, 2, and 3 kg.
10. **Error analysis:** "Maya measured her desk as 60 cm but started the
    ruler at the 2-cm mark." What is the real length? *(58 cm — subtract
    the 2 cm head start)*

## Independent practice

The learner measures five classroom objects (one length, one mass, one
volume, plus two free choices), records each with its unit, converts each
to one other unit of the same system, and stores the conversions in
two-column tables. Sentence frame: "___ measured ___; that equals ___."

## Application

A recipe needs 500 mL of water, but the measuring cup broke and only a
250-mL cup remains. How many fills? *(2 fills.)* Then: the learner finds
something at home labeled in grams and converts it to kilograms.

## Exit check

Prompt: "You measure a table as 120 cm. Your friend says 'that's 1.2 m.'
Who is right?" Accept: both — 120 cm = 1.2 m; same length, different unit
(smaller unit → bigger number).

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** Keep the conversion ladder card visible (10/100/1,000).
  Let the learner convert only one direction (large → small) first.
- **Extension:** The learner converts 1,500 mL to L and explains the
  general rule — "multiply going to a smaller unit, divide going to a
  larger one" — using the ladder, then checks a partner's measurement for
  the zero-start and flat-ruler ritual.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconception:** bigger number = bigger amount ("2,000 g must be
more than 2 kg"). Re-anchor with the table: both columns describe the same
thing; the number grows because the unit shrinks. **Answer-key reference:**
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) (Lesson 02).
