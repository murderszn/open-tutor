# Lesson 01 — Testable Questions and Careful Observations

**Time:** 30–35 minutes · **Materials:** science journal, pencil, 8 question
cards (adult writes them; see Guided practice), hand lens, metric ruler,
kitchen scale or balance, graduated cylinder or marked measuring cup, a leaf
or bark chip, a small rock, a cup of water.

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will learn the difference between a
*testable* question and one science cannot test, and you will practice
observing with four real science tools.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "In grade 3, what is one thing a scientist
does with a ruler?" Any answer about measuring or finding how long something
is shows readiness.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

Science starts with a **question** — but not every question can be answered
with science. A **testable question** is a question we can answer by
**observing**, **measuring**, or **testing**. An observation is something you
notice with your senses or a tool.

- *"Does the classroom plant grow taller in the sun or in the shade?"* →
  **testable.** We can put one plant in the sun, one in the shade, measure
  their heights, and compare.
- *"Is the moon prettier than the stars?"* → **not testable.** "Prettier"
  is an opinion — there is nothing to measure.

**Worked example 1 — testable or not?**

> Adult thinks aloud: *"Which paper towel soaks up more water — brand A or
> brand B?"* Can I answer this by testing? Yes — I can pour the same amount
> of water on each, then measure how much each towel holds. So it's testable.
> The measurement I would make is **milliliters of water held**. Every
> testable question hides a measurement inside it. Find the measurement, and
> you have found the test.

**Worked example 2 — observations with tools**

> Adult models at the tool tray: *"Look at this leaf with just your eyes:
> green, smooth edges. Now through the hand lens: I see tiny lines — the
> veins — and the edge is actually bumpy. The tool shows me things my eyes
> miss."* The adult measures the leaf with the ruler: *"11 centimeters from
> the stem end to the tip. I started the ruler at zero."* Then the adult
> weighs the rock on the scale (*"47 grams"*) and measures 100 mL of water in
> the cylinder (*"I read the level at eye level, at the bottom of the curve"*).
> Each tool answers a different question: lens = what does it look like
> close up, ruler = how long, scale = how heavy, cylinder = how much liquid.

Key idea to say plainly: **A testable question hides a measurement. Ask it,
name the measurement, then pick the tool that makes it.**

## Guided practice (adult leads, learner answers and does)

Sort these question cards (adult reads each aloud; learner sorts into
"testable" and "not testable" piles, naming the measurement for testable
ones):

1. "Which ball bounces higher?" *(testable — measure bounce height in cm)*
2. "Do cats dream about fish?" *(not testable — cannot observe a dream)*
3. "Which ramp surface lets the car roll farther?" *(testable — measure roll
   distance in cm)*
4. "Is blue the best color?" *(not testable — opinion)*
5. "Does warm water or cold water dissolve sugar faster?" *(testable —
   measure time in seconds)*
6. "How heavy is this rock?" *(testable — measure mass in grams)*
7. "What is the nicest smell?" *(not testable — opinion)*
8. "Which brand of paper towel holds the most water?" *(testable — measure
   water held in mL)*

Then the learner rotates through the four tool stations, one observation per
station: describe the rock through the hand lens, measure the leaf's length,
weigh the leaf, measure 50 mL of water. The adult checks the measuring
ritual (start at 0, read at eye level, record the unit).

## Independent practice

The learner writes or dictates two testable questions of their own about
things in the room, naming the measurement each one hides, and draws one
**labeled diagram** of the rock or leaf with at least three labels — a first
**model**. (A model is a drawing, object, or description that represents
and helps explain something real.)

## Application

Look around the kitchen or yard. Find one thing and write one testable
question about it plus the tool you would use. Example: "Does the big spoon
or the small spoon hold more water?" — measure with the graduated cylinder
in milliliters.

## Exit check

Prompt: "Your friend asks, 'Which ice cream flavor is the best?' Is that
testable? What would you change to make it testable?" Accept: not testable
(opinion); testable version could be "Which flavor melts fastest?" measured
with a timer, or "Which flavor do more people pick?" counted with a tally.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** Adult reads and sorts with the learner; the learner only
  names the measurement. Sentence frame: "This is testable because I can
  measure ___."
- **Extension:** The learner explains *why* "nicest smell" cannot become
  testable the way "best flavor" could (there is no countable, observable
  version everyone would accept) — and writes one more testable question
  whose answer needs **two** measurements.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconception:** testable = "has an answer." Re-anchor: a question
can have an answer ("blue is best — I said so") and still be untestable;
testable means *answerable by observing or measuring*. **Answer-key
reference:** [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) (Lesson 01).
