# Lesson 03 — Fair Tests: Change One Thing

**Time:** 30–35 minutes · **Materials:** science journal, pencil, fair-test
step cards (question → one change → keep-same list → 3 trials → table →
claim), toy car, book ramp, two surface cloths, paper towels of two brands
(for framing the project question).

**Goal (read to the learner):** Today you will learn the rule that makes a
test *fair* — change only **one thing** — and you will plan a real fair test
of your own.

**Readiness check:** Adult asks, "Yesterday's pencil: why did starting the
ruler at 1 instead of 0 ruin the measurement?" Any answer about the extra
unit / unfair start shows readiness.

## Teaching (adult reads and models)

A test is **fair** when it changes only **one thing** and keeps everything
else the same. Scientists call the one changed thing the **variable** and
the things kept the same the **controls**. Then, whatever changes in the
result must be because of the one thing you changed. Repeat at least **3
trials**, because one try might be a fluke and patterns only show up in
repeats.

Take the question: *"Does the surface change how far the toy car rolls?"*

- **Change:** the surface — bare wood, then a cloth.
- **Keep the same:** the same car, the same ramp, the same ramp height, the
  same release point, no pushing — released the same way every time.
- **Repeat:** 3 rolls on each surface (**trials**).
- **Record:** every distance in a table, in centimeters, ruler starting at 0.

If you rolled on the cloth from a *low* ramp and on bare wood from a *high*
ramp, you changed **two** things. If the car rolls farther, you cannot tell
whether it was the surface or the height. That test is **unfair** — and an
unfair test cannot answer its question.

**Worked example 1 — a fair plan, built out loud**

> Adult: *"Question: Does ramp height change how far the car rolls? The
> **one thing I change** is ramp height: low, then high. What stays the
> same? Same car, same ramp, same surface, same release point, no pushing.
> I roll **3 trials** at each height and write every distance in a table.
> Fair — one change, everything else the same."*

**Worked example 2 — an unfair test, caught and fixed**

> Adult: *"Question: Which paper towel holds more water? First I tried it
> wrong: I poured half a cup on brand A and a full cup on brand B, and I
> measured A's water in milliliters but B's in spoonfuls. Two problems —
> different water amounts AND different units. Unfair! Fixed version: pour
> exactly 100 mL on each towel, measure what each holds in mL, 3 trials
> each. Now only the brand changes. Fair."*

Key idea to say plainly: **One change, everything else the same, repeated
trials, one set of units. If you changed two things, you learned nothing.**

## Guided practice (adult leads, learner judges and fixes)

For each, the learner says fair or unfair, names the problem, and says how
to fix it.

1. "Does car color change roll distance? I roll a red car and a blue car
   from the same height on the same ramp." *(fair — one change: color)*
2. "Which towel absorbs more? I pour one spoon of water on towel A and three
   spoons on towel B." *(unfair — water amount changes too; fix: same
   spoons on each)*
3. "Does ramp height matter? Same car, low ramp vs. high ramp, same
   surface, no push, 3 trials each." *(fair)*
4. "Do plants need light? One plant by the window, one in the closet, same
   water, same kind of plant." *(fair — one change: light)*
5. "Which brand holds more water? Brand A tested 3 times; brand B tested
   once." *(unfair — trials must be repeated on both; fix: 3 trials each)*
6. "Does warm or cold water dissolve sugar faster? Same spoon of sugar,
   same stirring, different water temperatures, timed in seconds."
   *(fair — one change: temperature)*
7. "Which ramp surface is fastest? Wood surface vs. cloth surface, but the
   cloth test used a heavier car." *(unfair — car also changes; fix: same
   car on both)*
8. **Failure-point check:** "Name one thing that could go wrong in the
   paper-towel test and how you would prevent it." *(e.g., towel tears when
   lifted — support it from below / use a tray; water spills off the table
   — work over a tray) — 3-5-ETS1-3's "consider failure points")*

## Independent practice

The learner picks a testable question from Lesson 01 and writes a full
fair-test plan: the question, the **one change**, **three things to keep the
same**, the number of trials, the unit of measurement, and one failure
point with its prevention. Sentence frame: "My test is fair because I only
change ___, and I keep ___, ___, and ___ the same."

## Application

Frame the unit project question with the adult: *"Which paper-towel brand
holds the most water?"* The learner drafts the variable, controls, trials,
and measurement unit in their journal. (The full investigation runs in
Sessions 10 and 13; see [project.md](../project.md).)

## Exit check

Prompt: "Your friend tested brand A with 50 mL of water and brand B with
100 mL. She says brand B won. Do you trust it? Why not?" Accept: no — two
things changed (brand AND water amount), so the test cannot show which
caused the result; redo with the same water on each.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** Use the fair-test step cards as a checklist for every item.
  Let the learner physically sort "change" vs. "keep the same" word cards.
- **Extension:** The learner explains *why* 3 trials beat 1 trial using the
  word "pattern," then redesigns one unfair item above in two different
  fair ways.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconception:** "fair" meaning "nice" or "equal turns." Re-anchor:
in science, fair means *only one thing changes*. Also watch for learners who
name a control and then change it mid-test — the step cards prevent this.
