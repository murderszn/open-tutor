# Answer Key — Unit 04: Plant and Animal Structures and Functions

**Adult only.** Every lesson item, practice item, quiz item,
assessment task, and project criterion is solved or scored here.
Accept reasonable wording that states the same structure, job, or
reasoning; the adult judges meaning, not exact phrasing.

## Lesson 01 — External plant structures

**Guided practice.** 1. Roots (below soil), stem (tall green part),
leaves (flat green parts), petals (yellow top). 2. Diagram labels:
roots at the bottom in the soil, stem as the central vertical stalk,
leaf as a flat green side part, flower at the top. 3. The leaves'
job — catching sunlight and helping make food — is hardest; growth
slows. 4. The thorn did its job: protection, discouraging the goat
from eating. 5. Order: roots, stem, leaves, flower. Jobs: roots take
in water and hold steady; stem supports and carries; leaves catch
sunlight and help make food; flower petals attract pollinators.
**Independent practice.** 1. Drawing check: four parts present and
labeled. 2. One correct job sentence per part (see Teaching). 3. The
bee carries pollen to another flower; the flower can then make seeds,
and seeds grow into new plants — reproduction. 4. No. The stem carries
water from roots to leaves and supports the plant; with a broken stem,
water cannot reach the leaves, so the plant wilts even with healthy
roots and leaves.
**Applied task.** The flower shows petals and stem best; the carrot
shows a root best — it is a root, grown underground, tapered, with
no leaves or flower attached.
**Exit check.** Roots — take in water, hold steady; stem — support
and carry; leaves — catch sunlight, help make food; petals — attract
pollinators.

## Lesson 02 — External animal structures

**Guided practice.** 1. Rabbit: fur (warmth and protection) and legs
(moving — running and hopping). Accept ears only if paired with a
job the adult confirms (hearing is a Unit 05 topic; do not assess
it). 2. Scales protect; fins move (swim). 3. The short, strong beak
cracks seeds; shape fits food — a beak that cannot crack a seed
cannot eat it. 4. Rabbit: legs, moving (running away). Turtle: shell,
protection (pulling inside). 5. False. Webbed feet are for swimming
and paddling, not flying.
**Independent practice.** 1. Drawing check: three labeled outside
structures. 2. One correct job sentence per structure. 3. Feathers:
they keep the bird warm and shed rain (and wing feathers enable
flight). 4. A meat-eater: a hooked beak tears meat and sharp claws
catch and hold prey — shape predicts food.
**Applied task.** Table: covering — duck feathers vs. turtle shell;
movement — duck webbed feet vs. turtle legs. Sentence: the turtle is
better protected (shell), the duck moves faster in water (webbed
feet).
**Exit check.** Any three correct pairs, e.g., feathers–protect,
beak–get food, wings–fly; each labeled survive, move, or eat.

## Lesson 03 — Internal animal structures

**Guided practice.** 1. Heart — pumps blood; stomach — breaks down
food; lungs — take air in and out; brain — controls the body; skin —
covers and protects. 2. Running: heart beats faster, lungs breathe
faster; resting: both slow down. They change together because a
working body needs more of what blood and air deliver. 3. Stomach is
most directly about food (breaks it down); lungs most directly about
air (take it in and out). 4. The bandage helps the skin's job:
covering and protecting what is inside, keeping the inside in and
germs and dirt out. 5. Any directed action: telling legs to run,
eyes to look, arms to catch, body to stop — the brain directs, the
parts do.
**Independent practice.** 1. Drawing check: five labels, roughly
correct positions. 2. One correct job sentence per structure.
3. Two sentences: the heart pumps blood and the lungs move air, and
both speed up together when the body works hard — neither alone keeps
a running body supplied, so they are a system. 4. Wrong. The heart
pumps blood; the stomach breaks down food.
**Applied task.** Table: Rest — heart slower, lungs slower. Working
hard — heart faster, lungs faster, because both must supply the
working body together.
**Exit check.** Heart — pumps blood; stomach — breaks down food;
lungs — take air in and out; brain — controls the body; skin —
covers and protects.

## Lesson 04 — Arguments with evidence

**Guided practice.** 1. (a) Claim. (b) Evidence. (c) Claim.
(d) Evidence. 2. Evidence: the covered plant grew less than the
uncovered plant. Reason: leaves catch sunlight and help make food, so
blocking sunlight slows growth — which is why leaves help plants
grow. 3. Flaw: "beautiful" is an opinion, not evidence. Fix with an
observation, e.g., "I watched rain roll off a duck's feathers while
its skin stayed dry" or "I saw a bird fly using its wing feathers."
4. Duck: webbed feet, swimming/paddling. Fish: fins, swimming. One
sentence each stating structure and job. 5. Five days of counting is
stronger: repeated observations are more reliable than a single one.
**Independent practice.** 1. Claim: a stem helps a plant survive.
Evidence: a plant with a broken stem wilted even though its roots had
water. Reason: the stem carries water to the leaves, so without it
the leaves cannot get what they need to live. 2. Claim: a turtle's
shell helps it survive. Evidence: when danger came, the turtle pulled
inside and its body was unharmed. Reason: the shell takes the hit
instead of the soft body, so protection keeps the animal alive.
3. Sunflower: petals attract pollinators — reproduction. Rabbit: fur
keeps it warm and protects its skin — survival (legs for movement —
survival/behavior also accepted with a reason). 4. Flaw: "I like
hearts best" is an opinion, not evidence. Rewrite: Claim: the heart
pumps blood around the body. Evidence: the heartbeat felt faster
after running. Reason: a working body needs more blood moving, so the
heart pumps faster.
**Applied task.** Adult verifies all three labeled parts: claim
states structure + job + survival/growth/behavior/reproduction;
evidence reports something actually observed; reason connects the
evidence to the claim.
**Exit check.** Claim, evidence, reason. "I think so" is an opinion
or guess, not something observed.

## Lesson 05 — Structures working together

**Guided practice.** 1. Roots cannot take in water from dry soil, so
the stem has nothing to carry and the leaves cannot help make food —
the system breaks at the first link. 2. Error: lungs do not break
down food. The stomach breaks down food; the lungs take air in and
out. 3. Error: "best" is an opinion. Replacement: "Claim: feathers
help birds survive. Evidence: I saw rain roll off a duck's feathers
and its skin stayed dry. Reason: staying dry and warm keeps the bird
alive." (Any observed-evidence version accepted.) 4. Self-report;
adult checks structure, job, and evidence and initials the revision.
5. Systems answer: the heart and lungs supply the body together —
remove either and the body is not supplied, so ranking them misses
how systems work.
**Independent practice.** 1. Plant system diagram: roots (take in
water) → stem (carries water up) → leaves (catch sunlight, help make
food) → flower/petals (attract pollinators). 2. Animal system
diagram: skin (protect), heart (pump blood), lungs (breathe), stomach
(break down food), brain (control). 3. Paragraph: without roots the
plant takes in no water; the stem then carries nothing upward; the
leaves, however sunny, cannot help make food without water; so the
plant wilts and cannot survive on sunlight alone. 4. Self-revision;
adult checks old claim, named error (if any), and revised claim with
evidence.
**Applied task.** Strongest entry gains one more piece of evidence
or a second organism; weakest entry is fully rewritten (claim,
evidence, reason). Adult initials both.
**Exit check.** Two sentences on systems (parts depend on each other;
one broken link weakens the whole), plus the three checks: right
structure, right job, real evidence.

## Practice sets

**Set A.** 1. (a) takes in water and holds steady; (b) holds up and
carries water; (c) catches sunlight and helps make food; (d) attracts
pollinators; (e) protects by discouraging eaters. 2. Diagram: roots
bottom, stem center, leaf side, flower top. 3. The leaves wilt and dry
over two days: the stem's carrying job is broken, so water cannot
reach them. 4. Petals: their bright color attracts pollinators, which
move pollen so the plant can make seeds — reproduction. 5. False:
each part has its own job. 6. Spines protect (discourage eaters);
small leaves lose less water, which fits a dry desert (adult confirms
the reasoning). 7. Drawing + sentence, e.g., a plant with no roots
cannot take in water or hold steady.
**Set B.** 1. Fur — rabbit; feathers — duck; scales — fish; shell —
turtle. 2. Duck: flat beak scoops food from water. Eagle: hooked beak
tears meat. Shape fits food. 3. Fins — swimming/moving; scales —
protecting (and slipping through water). 4. Fur also protects skin;
a bird's feathers do a similar protecting job. 5. False: claws also
grip, dig, and catch food. 6. Turtle better protected (shell);
rabbit moves faster (legs). 7. A water bird, like a duck: webbed feet
are for paddling in water, and a flat beak is for scooping food from
water.
**Set C.** 1. Heart — pumps blood; stomach — breaks down food; lungs
— take air in and out; brain — controls the body; skin — covers and
protects. 2. Running: heart faster, lungs faster; resting: both
slower; they change together to supply the working body. 3. Stomach
— food; lungs — air. 4. Correction: the brain controls the body; the
heart pumps blood. 5. Drawing check. 6. Two sentences: heart and
lungs speed up together when the body works hard; neither alone
supplies a running body, so they are a system. 7. No: the body uses
broken-down food for energy and growth, so without breakdown the
energy supply fails.
**Set D.** 1. (a) Evidence. (b) Claim. (c) Evidence. (d) Claim.
2. Claim: roots help a plant survive. Evidence: a gentle pull left the
plant in the soil, and a plant with cut roots wilted even with water
on its leaves. Reason: roots anchor the plant and take in water, both
needed for survival. 3. Flaw: "prettiest" is an opinion and says
nothing about seeds. Needed evidence: bee-visit counts comparing
bright and dull flowers over repeated observations. 4. Sunflower:
petals attract pollinators — reproduction. Cactus: spines/thorns
protect — survival. 5. Conclusion: bees visited bright flowers more
often (12 visits vs. 2 in one hour). What it does not prove: that
color alone caused the difference (scent, position, and one hour of
counting are limits). 6. Argument Y is stronger: it gives observed,
repeated evidence (two days, both kinds of stems), while X restates
the claim ("sharp" is a property, not an observation of protection).
**Set E.** 1. Two errors fixed: leaves (not roots) catch sunlight and
help make food; the heart (not the stem) pumps blood — and plants do
not have blood, the stem carries water and food; thorns (not petals)
discourage eaters. 2. Protecting: plant thorns, dog skin (or fur).
Moving/carrying: plant stem, dog legs. 3. No: color alone is not
evidence of attraction. Also needed: observations of pollinators
visiting red flowers compared with other colors, repeated over time.
4. Order: roots take in water → stem carries water up → leaves catch
sunlight → petals attract pollinators. Order matters because each
step supplies the next; break one link and the later steps fail.
5. Systems answer: a flower alone cannot keep a plant alive — roots,
stem, and leaves are all needed; and "prettiest" is an opinion, not
evidence. 6. Adult checks Claim / Evidence / Reason labels and that
the evidence is an observation. 7. Self-report; adult notes the
structure named.

## Week-18 quiz (10 items)

1. Roots — take in water, hold steady; stem — support and carry;
   leaves — catch sunlight, help make food; petals — attract
   pollinators. 2. Roots are below the soil; their two jobs are taking
   in water and holding the plant steady. 3. Duck: webbed feet —
   swimming, or flat beak — scooping food. Turtle: shell — protection,
   or legs — walking. One structure + job per animal. 4. Feathers keep
   the bird warm and shed rain (wing feathers also enable flight).
5. Heart — pumps blood; stomach — breaks down food; lungs — take air
   in and out; brain — controls the body; skin — covers and protects.
6. Running: heart faster, breathing faster, to supply the working
   body; resting: both slower. They change together because blood and
   air supply the same working body. 7. (a) Claim. (b) Evidence.
8. Flaw: "pretty" is an opinion. Fix with an observation, e.g., rain
   rolling off feathers, or a bird flying with wing feathers.
9. Sunflower: petals attract pollinators — reproduction. Rabbit: fur
   keeps it warm and protects skin — survival (legs for movement —
   survival/behavior accepted). 10. Incorrect. Roots take in water and
   anchor; leaves catch sunlight and help make food.

## Unit assessment (44 points)

1. **4 pts** — 1 pt per part correctly drawn, labeled, and given its
   correct job (roots, stem, leaves, petals).
2. **4 pts** — (a) No, 1 pt. (b) The stem's two jobs: supports the
   plant and carries water/food, 2 pts (1 each). (c) Roots' water has
   nowhere to go (or leaves' sunlight-catching is wasted without
   water), 1 pt with a reason.
3. **4 pts** — Bright/colored petals attract pollinators (bees),
   which move pollen between flowers so seeds can form — reproduction.
   Deduct 1 pt each if pollinators or seeds are missing.
4. **4 pts** — (a) Duck: feathers; turtle: shell; fish: scales, 1 pt
   for all three. (b) Each covering protects the body, 1 pt.
   (c) Duck: webbed feet, paddling/swimming, 1 pt. Fish: fins,
   swimming, 1 pt.
5. **3 pts** — Matches: heart — pumps blood; stomach — breaks down
   food; lungs — take air in and out, 2 pts (1 pt if two of three
   correct). Sentence: heart and lungs change together to supply the
   working body, 1 pt.
6. **3 pts** — The brain controls the body, 1 pt; the heart pumps
   blood, 1 pt; stated as a clear correction in two sentences, 1 pt.
7. **4 pts** — Labels roughly placed: 2 pts (1 pt if 3–4 labeled).
   Jobs correct: 2 pts (1 pt if 3–4 correct).
8. **3 pts** — (a) Claim, (b) Evidence, (c) Evidence, 2 pts (1 pt if
   two of three correct). Reason sentence: a reason explains why the
   evidence supports the claim, 1 pt.
9. **4 pts** — Claim (thorns protect the plant) 1 pt; evidence uses
   the goat data (ate thornless, avoided thorny, both days) 1 pt;
   reason (avoidance shows the thorns discouraged eating) 1 pt; three
   parts labeled 1 pt.
10. **4 pts** — Sunflower complete (structure + job + category) 1 pt;
    turtle complete 1 pt; comparison sentence stating that different
    structures do different jobs that help each organism 2 pts (1 pt
    for the sentence, 1 pt for the general principle).
11. **4 pts** — Sentence 1 fixed: leaves (not roots) catch sunlight
    and help make food, 1 pt. Sentence 2 fixed: the heart (not the
    stomach) pumps blood; the stomach breaks down food, 1 pt.
    Sentence 3 replaced with an evidence-based claim, e.g., feathers
    protect because rain rolls off them (observed), 2 pts (1 pt for
    dropping the opinion, 1 pt for observed evidence).
12. **3 pts** — The broken link is the roots/water supply, 1 pt; the
    stem carries nothing upward, 1 pt; leaves cannot help make food
    without water, so the system stalls, 1 pt.
