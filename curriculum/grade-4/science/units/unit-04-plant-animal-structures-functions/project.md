# Project — Structure–Function Field Guide

**Time:** Sessions 12–13 (planning and observation) plus Session 15
(arguments). **Adult supervises all observation; the adult handles
all cutting.**

## The task

Make a field guide with three entries: two plants and one animal.
For each organism, draw and label at least two structures, state each
structure's job, and write one argument (claim, evidence, reason)
that the structure supports survival, growth, behavior, or
reproduction. Present the guide as three pages (one per organism)
plus a cover line naming the guide and its author ("Field guide by
___" — first name only, or no name, per the adult's privacy choice;
no full names, photos of the learner, or personal details go in the
guide).

## Organism choices (adult approves)

- **Plants:** a store-bought flower; a store-bought vegetable with a
  visible root or stem (carrot, celery, bok choy); or a garden/park
  plant observed without picking (adult confirms it is safe to
  approach and that nothing is picked).
- **Animal:** a pet observed gently at home; a bird, duck, or other
  animal observed from a distance outside; or an animal in
  adult-chosen photos or video. No wild animals are handled, chased,
  or fed for this project.

## Materials

Science journal (or three sheets of paper); pencils and colored
pencils; a hand lens (optional); the adult's camera only if the adult
chooses to photograph specimens for reference — learner photos are
not required and no photos of people are used.

## Steps

1. **Plan (Session 12).** Choose your three organisms with the adult.
   For each, predict two structures you expect to see and the job of
   each. Write the plan as a table: organism | structure 1 + job |
   structure 2 + job.
2. **Observe and draw (Session 13).** For each organism, look closely
   and draw what you actually see — not what you remember from a
   textbook. Label at least two structures per drawing. Record one
   observation sentence per structure ("I saw…"). The adult handles
   any cutting (for example, halving a carrot to show the root).
3. **Match jobs (Session 13).** Beside each labeled structure, write
   its job in your own words. If what you saw differs from your
   prediction, note the difference — that is a finding, not a mistake.
4. **Argue (Session 15).** For each organism, write one complete
   argument: Claim (this structure does this job, helping survival /
   growth / behavior / reproduction) + Evidence (what you observed) +
   Reason (why the evidence supports the claim). Label the three
   parts.
5. **Present (Session 15).** Read one argument aloud to the adult.
   The adult asks one question ("What did you actually see?"), and
   you answer from your observation notes.

## Safety

- The adult handles all cutting and confirms every plant is safe to
  handle; check for pollen or plant allergies first.
- Living things are observed gently and never harmed; nothing is
  picked from a garden or park without the adult's and the owner's
  permission.
- Hands washed after soil and plant work. Nothing tasted during
  observations.
- Animals are observed, not chased, grabbed, or fed. If an animal
  moves away, that is data too — record "the animal moved away when
  approached" rather than pursuing it.

## Deliverables

1. Three field-guide pages (one per organism), each with a labeled
   drawing of at least two structures, a job for each structure, and
   one observation sentence per structure.
2. Three complete arguments (one per organism), each labeled Claim /
   Evidence / Reason.
3. A one-paragraph cover note: what the guide shows about how
   structures help living things (2–3 sentences).

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | 10 points | 7 points | 4 points | 0 points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Observations and drawings | All three pages have labeled drawings of ≥2 structures each, drawn from actual observation | Two pages complete, or drawings partly from memory | One page complete, or labels mostly missing | No usable drawings or labels |
| Structure–job matches | Every labeled structure matched to its correct job | Most matches correct; one or two errors | Several matches wrong or missing | Jobs mostly wrong or absent |
| Arguments with evidence | All three arguments complete (claim, evidence, reason), evidence observed, reason connects | Two complete, or one reason weak | One complete, or evidence is opinion | No complete argument |
| Presentation and accuracy | Cover note present; work is legible and organized; no unsafe or false claims | Cover note thin, or minor disorganization | Cover note missing, or work hard to follow | Not presented |

**Bands:** 36–40 = strong, ready for the unit assessment; 28–35 =
solid, fix the weakest criterion before Session 16; ≤27 = revisit
the named lessons and revise the guide before Session 16 (see
teacher-guide).
