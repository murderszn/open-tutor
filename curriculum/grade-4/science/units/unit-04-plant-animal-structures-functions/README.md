# Unit 04 — Plant and Animal Structures and Functions

**Grade:** 4 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#23](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/23)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 15–18 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 35 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

Every living thing is built from parts, and every part has a job. A
sunflower's roots drink, its stem holds it up, its leaves catch
sunlight, and its bright petals invite bees — and the seeds those bees
help make are the next generation. A turtle's shell, a duck's webbed
feet, a dog's heart, lungs, stomach, brain, and skin each do one job
well, and together they keep the animal alive. This unit teaches
learners to name those structures, match each one to its function, and
then do the real scientific work: build an argument, with evidence,
that a structure supports survival, growth, behavior, or reproduction.

The unit stays inside the grade-4 assessment boundary throughout: only
macroscopic structures — parts you can see or feel without a
microscope. Cells, cell parts, and the chemical details of how plants
make food are not taught here. The brain is taught as the body's
control center, not through how it stores memories; sense receptors and
their mechanisms belong to Unit 05, not this one. Week 18 doubles as
the midyear review: learners checkpoint their structure-function
journal, revise one claim with better evidence, and revisit the
highest-need objectives from Units 01–04.

## Prerequisites (from Units 01–03; re-taught here as needed)

- The fair-observation routine from Unit 01: look closely, record what
  you actually see in a labeled drawing or table, and separate what you
  observed from what you guessed. (Units 01–03 are on main; this unit
  depends on no single Unit 02 or 03 file, only on routines those units
  established.)
- The investigation-report format from Unit 01, reused for the field
  guide project.
- Science safety practices: the adult supervises every investigation;
  the adult handles all cutting; living things are observed gently and
  never harmed; hands are washed after soil and plant work; nothing is
  tasted during an observation; no animal dissection; no microscope is
  required.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Name external plant structures and match each to its function:**
   roots take in water and hold the plant steady; stems support the
   plant and carry water and food; leaves catch sunlight and help make
   food for growth; colored petals attract pollinators; thorns protect
   the plant (4-LS1-1).
2. **Name external animal structures and match each to its function:**
   skin, fur, feathers, and scales cover and protect; beaks and claws
   help get food; shells protect; legs, wings, and fins help the animal
   move (4-LS1-1).
3. **Name internal animal structures and state each one's job within
   its system:** the heart pumps blood around the body; the stomach
   breaks down food; the lungs take air in and let it out; the brain
   controls the body; the skin covers and protects what is inside
   (4-LS1-1).
4. **Construct an argument with evidence** that a structure supports
   survival, growth, behavior, or reproduction — stating a claim,
   giving observed evidence, and explaining why the evidence supports
   the claim — and **compare structures across two organisms**
   (4-LS1-1; SEP-6).
5. **Use the structure-function journal:** record observations in
   labeled drawings and tables, and revise a claim when new evidence
   is stronger (Units 01–04 review thread; SEP-2, SEP-6).

## Essential question

*How does each part of a plant or an animal help the whole living thing survive, grow, behave, and reproduce?*

## Vocabulary

**Structure** — a part of a living thing, such as a root, a leaf, or a
heart. · **Function** — the job a structure does. · **External** —
on the outside of the body. · **Internal** — inside the body. ·
**Root** — the part of a plant that grows underground, takes in water,
and holds the plant steady. · **Stem** — the part that holds a plant
up and carries water and food between roots and leaves. · **Leaf** —
the flat, usually green part that catches sunlight and helps make food
for growth. · **Petal** — a colored part of a flower that attracts
pollinators. · **Pollinator** — an animal, such as a bee, that moves
pollen between flowers and helps the plant make seeds. · **Thorn** —
a sharp structure on a stem that discourages animals from eating the
plant. · **Skin** — the covering over an animal's body that protects
what is inside. · **Fur** — soft hair covering a mammal. ·
**Feather** — a light, flat covering on a bird. · **Scale** — a small,
flat, hard plate covering a fish or reptile. · **Beak** — the hard
mouthpart of a bird, shaped for the food it eats. · **Claw** — a
sharp, curved nail for gripping, scratching, or catching food. ·
**Shell** — a hard outer covering that protects an animal. · **Heart**
— the structure that pumps blood around the body. · **Stomach** — the
structure that breaks down food. · **Lung** — the structure that
takes air into the body and lets it out. · **Brain** — the structure
that controls the body. · **System** — structures working together
for one larger job. · **Survival** — staying alive. · **Growth** —
getting bigger or more developed. · **Behavior** — something an
animal does. · **Reproduction** — making new living things of the
same kind. · **Claim** — a statement you say is true. · **Evidence**
— something you observed that supports a claim. · **Argument** — a
claim supported by evidence and reasoning.

## Standards and source notes

- Reference framework: **Next Generation Science Standards**, grade-4
  performance expectations (from the track audit's crosswalk). No state
  adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification is claimed.
- **4-LS1-1** — *Construct an argument that plants and animals have
  internal and external structures that function to support survival,
  growth, behavior, and reproduction.* Clarification (NGSS):
  examples of structures include thorns, stems, roots, colored petals,
  heart, stomach, lung, brain, and skin; each structure has specific
  functions within its associated system. Assessment boundary, kept in
  this unit: assessment is limited to macroscopic structures within
  plant and animal systems — no cells. (Wording, examples, and boundary
  re-verified 2026-10-30 against the NGSS 4-LS1-1 text; see
  [resource-pack.md](resource-pack.md).) 4-LS1-2 (senses and information
  processing) is **not** taught here; it is Unit 05.
- Science and engineering practices: **SEP-2** (developing and using
  models — labeled structure diagrams, the field guide), **SEP-6**
  (constructing explanations and designing solutions — arguments with
  evidence in Lessons 04–05 and the project). Crosscutting concepts:
  **structure and function** (each structure's shape fits its job),
  **systems and system models** (structures work together as a system).
- All practice scenarios that look like data are labeled fictional
  practice data, never presented as the learner's own observations.

## Materials (for the whole unit)

Store-bought flowers (sunflower or daisy if available) and one
store-bought vegetable with a visible root or stem (carrot, celery, or
bok choy); a clear cup of water; a hand lens (optional, macroscopic
only — no microscope); science journal; pencils and colored pencils;
word cards for the vocabulary; printed or screen photos of animals
(duck, turtle, rabbit, dog, fish, bird — adult-chosen); index cards
for claim/evidence sorting. **Adult reads the safety rules aloud
before each investigation:** the adult does all cutting; living things
are observed gently and never harmed; hands washed after soil and
plant work; nothing tasted during an observation; check for pollen or
plant allergies before handling flowers; no wild animals are handled.
All investigations are adult-supervised.

## Differentiation and supports

- **Reading/language access:** every lesson goal is read aloud; key
  words are shown on word cards with a picture (root, stem, leaf,
  petal, thorn, heart, lung, brain, skin); learners may answer orally,
  by pointing, or by drawing, and the adult records what they say.
- **Alternate response modes:** structures may be modeled with
  classroom objects (a straw for a stem, a sponge for a root) instead
  of drawn; arguments may be spoken and adult-scribed; the assessment
  allows drawn models in place of sentences.
- **Extensions:** compare the same structure in three organisms (for
  example, beaks of a duck, an eagle, and a hummingbird — different
  shapes for different foods); research, with the adult, how a real
  cactus survives with very small leaves, then explain it in the
  unit's structure-function language; add a fourth argument to the
  field guide for a structure observed at home.
- **Reteach triggers:** see [teacher-guide.md](teacher-guide.md). A
  learner who still says "roots make food" after Session 4 re-runs
  Lesson 01's observation with the adult's question: "Where does the
  plant catch sunlight — underground or on its leaves?"
