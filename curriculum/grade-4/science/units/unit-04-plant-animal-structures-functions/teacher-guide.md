# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Plant and Animal Structures and Functions

**Adult only.** Student materials never link here except through the
unit README's labeled link. All solutions live in

| Week | Sessions | Focus | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | 1–4 | External plant structures; sunflower picture; thorns and petals | "Roots make food" error — re-run the sunlight question |
| 16 | 5–8 | External animal structures; internal structures and systems | Covering jobs blurred; brain-doing-everything error |

## Readiness evidence and reteach triggers

- **After Session 1:** if the learner cannot name roots, stem, leaves,
  and petals with one job each, return to the sunflower picture and
  the real flower before Session 2. Do not proceed to animals until
  the four plant jobs are stable — every later lesson reuses them.
- **After Session 4:** if "roots make food" persists, ask only:
  "Where does the plant catch sunlight — underground or on its
  leaves?" Then re-run Lesson 01's applied task with the real flower.
- **After Session 8:** if internal jobs migrate (brain pumps blood,
  stomach breathes), re-run the one-word job-card match (pump, break
  down, breathe, control, protect) before Session 9.
- **After Session 10:** if claim and evidence still swap, drop to the
  sentence frames ("I claim…", "I observed…", "This supports my claim
  because…") and rebuild one argument frame by frame.
- **After the week-18 quiz:** score ≤6 on any objective's items →
  reteach that lesson with its practice set before Session 16; do not
  re-administer the same quiz items as the reteach.

## Misconceptions to watch (by lesson)

- **Lesson 01:** roots make food (sunlight question cures it); stems
  are just sticks (celery/water-carrying example); petals are only
  decoration (bee-visit reasoning).
- **Lesson 02:** all coverings do the same job (press for the
  specific job per animal); webbed feet are for flying; claws are only
  for fighting.
- **Lesson 03:** the brain does every job because it controls;
  stomach stores food like a bag (breaking down is the function);
  heart and lungs as unrelated parts (running-dog example).
- **Lesson 04:** a vivid single observation counts as proof (ask for
  a second observation); the claim restated counts as evidence
  ("thorns protect because they are protective"); "I think so" or
  "it is pretty" counts as evidence.
- **Lesson 05:** ranking structures by importance instead of seeing
  systems; copying lesson text into the journal instead of recording
  observations.

## Original read-aloud passages (adult reads; learner discusses)

**Passage 1 — The sunflower's day (Lesson 01).** "In the morning the
sunflower's leaves turn toward the light. All day its roots drink
from the soil, its stem carries the drink upward, and its leaves catch
the sun. In the afternoon a bee lands on the yellow petals, dusted
with pollen, and flies to another flower. By autumn the flower head
holds seeds — and each seed can become a sunflower of its own."
Discussion: which sentence shows a job for roots? For petals? What
would break first if the stem snapped?

**Passage 2 — The turtle and the rabbit (Lesson 02).** "A rabbit
hears a fox and runs — long legs, fast feet, gone in a breath. A
turtle hears the same fox and does the opposite: it pulls its head
and legs inside its shell and waits. The rabbit survives by moving;
the turtle survives by staying. Two animals, two structures, one
goal: stay alive until the danger passes." Discussion: which animal
is better protected? Which moves faster? Which structure explains
each answer?

## Safety checklist (read aloud before each investigation)

- Adult handles all cutting; no learner cuts stems, flowers, or
  vegetables.
- Check for pollen or plant allergies before handling flowers.
- Living things observed gently, never harmed; no wild animals
  handled, chased, or fed; no animal dissection.
- Hands washed after soil and plant work. Nothing tasted during an
  observation.
- No microscope required; a hand lens, if used, is for looking at
  surfaces only.

## What readiness looks like

By Session 16 the learner names the four plant structures and five
internal structures with correct jobs, compares two organisms
structure-by-structure, and writes at least one complete argument
whose evidence is an actual observation. The field guide shows three
organisms drawn from observation, not copied from lesson text. If the
assessment lands in the 32–39 band, review only the missed objectives
with the matching practice sets; a score ≤31 means reteaching, not
re-testing, before Unit 05.
