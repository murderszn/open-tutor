# Unit Assessment — Plant and Animal Structures and Functions

**Adult administers in Session 16, after the week-18 quiz.** 12 tasks
covering all five unit objectives, including a labeled plant model, a
body-systems explanation, an argument, and a cross-organism comparison.
Total: **44 points** (points per task shown). Oral, drawn, or
manipulative responses are acceptable; the adult records them. Keys
and scoring guidance in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) (adult only).
**This is a practice check, not a standardized or validated test.**

## Part 1 — Plant structures (Objective 1)

1. **(4 pts)** Draw a flowering plant with roots, stem, leaves, and
   petals. Label all four parts and write one job beside each label.
   (1 pt per correctly labeled part with a correct job.)
2. **(4 pts)** A plant's stem is broken, but its roots sit in wet soil
   and its leaves are in bright sunlight. (a) Will the plant thrive?
   (b) Explain why, naming the stem's two jobs. (c) Name one more
   structure whose job is now wasted, and why.
3. **(4 pts)** Explain, in two or three sentences, how colored petals
   help a plant reproduce. Your answer must mention pollinators and
   seeds.

## Part 2 — Animal structures (Objectives 2–3)

4. **(4 pts)** A duck, a turtle, and a fish are shown in photos.
   (a) Name the covering of each (feathers, shell, scales).
   (b) Give the protecting job each covering does. (c) Name one
   movement structure for the duck and one for the fish, with its job.
5. **(3 pts)** Match each structure to its job: heart, stomach,
   lungs — with: pumps blood; breaks down food; takes air in and out.
   Then state, in one sentence, why the heart and lungs change
   together when an animal runs.
6. **(3 pts)** A friend says, "The brain pumps blood because it
   controls everything." Correct the statement in two sentences:
   what the brain does, and what pumps blood.
7. **(4 pts)** Draw a simple animal outline. Mark and label the heart,
   stomach, lungs, brain, and skin (rough positions only). Beside each
   label, write its job in a few words. (Judged on labels and jobs,
   not artistic skill.)

## Part 3 — Arguments, comparisons, systems (Objectives 4–5)

8. **(3 pts)** Sort into Claim or Evidence: (a) "Roots hold plants
   steady." (b) "I pulled a plant gently and it stayed in the soil."
   (c) "A goat avoided thorny stems." Then say, in one sentence, what
   a reason adds to a claim and its evidence.
9. **(4 pts)** Write a complete argument (claim, evidence, reason)
   that thorns help a plant survive. You may use this fictional
   practice data as your evidence: a goat ate thornless stems on two
   days and avoided thorny stems on both days. Label the three parts.
10. **(4 pts)** Compare two organisms — a sunflower and a turtle.
    For each, name one structure, its job, and whether it supports
    survival, growth, behavior, or reproduction. Then write one
    sentence saying what the comparison shows about structures in
    general.
11. **(4 pts)** Find and fix the errors in this paragraph: "Roots
    catch sunlight to make food. The heart breaks down food. Feathers
    are the best covering because they are beautiful." Your answer
    must correct all three sentences (or explain why each cannot
    stand) with correct structure-job statements and, for the third,
    an evidence-based replacement.
12. **(3 pts)** A plant has healthy leaves in bright sunlight but its
    roots are in dry, cracked soil. Explain, in two or three
    sentences, why the plant will struggle even though its leaves are
    fine. Use systems language: name the broken link and what stops
    moving through the system.

**Scoring bands:** 40–44 = strong, unit objectives met; 32–39 =
ready with review of missed objectives (see teacher-guide); ≤31 =
reteach the missed lessons with the matching practice sets before
moving to Unit 05.
