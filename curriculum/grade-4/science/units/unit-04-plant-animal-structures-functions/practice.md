# Practice Sets — Unit 04: Plant and Animal Structures and Functions

**Adult note:** Sets A–E cover all five unit objectives. Oral, drawn,
or written answers are acceptable; the adult records oral answers.
Keys in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) (adult only). Scenarios marked
"fictional practice data" are made up for practice, not observations
the learner made.

## Set A — Plant structures (Objective 1)

1. Match each structure to its job: (a) roots, (b) stem, (c) leaves,
   (d) petals, (e) thorns — with: holds the plant up and carries
   water; takes in water and holds steady; attracts pollinators;
   protects by discouraging eaters; catches sunlight and helps make
   food.
2. Label the diagram in [assets/plant-structure-diagram.svg](assets/plant-structure-diagram.svg):
   roots, stem, leaf, flower.
3. A plant's stem is cut halfway through, but its roots and leaves
   look healthy. Predict what happens to the leaves over the next two
   days, and name the stem job that explains it.
4. Which structure helps a plant reproduce, and how? Answer in two
   sentences, using the words *petals* and *pollinators*.
5. True or false, with a reason: "All plant structures do the same
   job."
6. A cactus has very small leaves and sharp spines (a kind of thorn).
   Name one job its spines do, and one reason small leaves still make
   sense for a desert plant. (Accept: spines protect; small leaves
   lose less water — the adult confirms the reasoning, not a
   memorized fact.)
7. Draw a plant with one structure missing (your choice). Write one
   sentence explaining what the plant can no longer do well.

## Set B — Animal structures (Objective 2)

1. Match each covering to its animal: fur, feathers, scales, shell —
   with: fish, rabbit, turtle, duck.
2. A duck and an eagle both have beaks, but the shapes differ. Give
   one food each beak shape fits, and explain why shape matters.
3. Name two structures a fish uses to live in water, and give the job
   of each.
4. A rabbit's fur keeps it warm in winter. Name one more job fur does
   (protecting skin), and one structure a bird has that does a
   similar protecting job.
5. True or false, with a reason: "Claws are only for fighting."
6. Compare a turtle and a rabbit as in Lesson 02: which is better
   protected, which moves faster, and which structure explains each
   answer?
7. An animal has webbed feet, feathers, and a flat beak. Predict where
   it spends much of its time and what it eats. Give one reason for
   each prediction.

## Set C — Internal structures and systems (Objective 3)

1. Match each structure to its job: heart, stomach, lungs, brain,
   skin — with: controls the body; pumps blood; breaks down food;
   takes air in and out; covers and protects.
2. A dog runs, then rests. Describe what its heart and lungs do in
   each stage, and why they change together.
3. Which structure's job is most directly about food, and which is
   most directly about air? Explain each choice in one sentence.
4. A learner says, "The brain pumps blood because it is in charge."
   Correct the error: name what the brain does and what pumps blood.
5. Draw and label a simple animal outline with heart, stomach, lungs,
   brain, and skin. (Judged on labels and roughly correct positions,
   not artistic skill.)
6. Explain in two sentences why the heart and lungs are a system.
   Use the word *together*.
7. If a stomach could not break down food, would the animal still get
   energy for growth? Explain why or why not in two sentences.

## Set D — Arguments with evidence (Objective 4)

1. Sort into Claim or Evidence: (a) "Bees visit bright flowers."
   (b) "Bright petals help flowers reproduce." (c) "The goat avoided
   thorny stems." (d) "Thorns protect plants."
2. Build a full argument (claim, evidence, reason) for: "Roots help a
   plant survive." Evidence you may use: a plant pulled gently stayed
   in the soil, and a plant with cut roots wilted even with water on
   its leaves.
3. Find the flaw in: "This flower is the prettiest, so it will make
   the most seeds." Rewrite the claim as a testable argument, or
   explain what evidence it would need.
4. Compare a sunflower and a cactus: one structure each, its job, and
   whether it supports survival, growth, behavior, or reproduction.
   (Sunflower: petals → attract pollinators → reproduction. Cactus:
   spines/thorns → protect → survival.)
5. Fictional practice data: a gardener counted bee visits for one
   hour — bright flowers: 12 visits; dull flowers: 2 visits. Write a
   one-sentence conclusion supported by these counts, and name what
   the counts do *not* prove.
6. Which argument is stronger, and why? Argument X: "Thorns protect
   because they are sharp." Argument Y: "Thorns protect: a goat
   avoided thorny stems on two different days, while eating thornless
   stems both days."

## Set E — Mixed review and error analysis (Objectives 1–5)

1. Find and fix two errors in this paragraph: "Roots catch sunlight
   to make food. The stem pumps blood around the plant. Petals scare
   away animals that want to eat the plant."
2. A plant and a dog are both living things with systems. Name one
   structure from each that does a protecting job, and one structure
   from each that does a moving or carrying job.
3. Your journal says: "The flower is red." Is that evidence for the
   claim "Red petals attract pollinators"? Explain what else you
   would need to observe.
4. Order these for a plant system, from first to last: leaves catch
   sunlight; roots take in water; stem carries water up; petals
   attract pollinators. Then write one sentence explaining why the
   order matters.
5. A friend argues: "The most important part of a plant is the flower
   because flowers are the prettiest part." Give the systems answer
   in two sentences.
6. Choose any structure from this unit. Write a complete argument
   (claim, evidence, reason) for its job, using an observation from
   your journal or from a lesson. Label each part: Claim / Evidence /
   Reason.
7. Midyear check: name one thing you learned in Unit 04 that changed
   how you look at a plant or animal outside, and say which structure
   it was about.
