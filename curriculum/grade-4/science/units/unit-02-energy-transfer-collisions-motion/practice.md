# Practice — Unit 02: Energy Transfer, Collisions, and Motion

Work one set per session as shown in the [unit pacing](README.md). Sets are
short-answer, drawing, and explain-your-reasoning tasks — no speed numbers,
no energy calculations (the 4-PS3 assessment boundaries). The adult checks
against the [answer key](answer-key.md) the same day; anything missed becomes
tomorrow's warm-up.

---

## Set A — Faster means more energy (after Lesson 01)

1. Look again at [`collision-energy-ramp.webp`](assets/collision-energy-ramp.webp)
   (deep dent with the red marble on the left; shallow dent with the blue
   marble beside it on the right). Which marble was moving faster? Write your
   answer as claim-evidence-reasoning (one sentence each).
2. Two identical balls roll down identical ramps. Ball P is released from the
   top; Ball Q is released from one-quarter of the way down. Both hit clay
   lumps. Predict: which dent will be deeper? Give your reason *before*
   checking with anyone.
3. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "The faster ball had more energy because it
   went down a longer ramp." Is the ramp length the real reason? Rewrite the
   explanation correctly in one sentence.
4. Draw two clay lumps: one hit by a fast ball, one hit by a slow ball. Label
   the deeper dent and write one sentence under your drawing stating what the
   deeper dent proves.
5. A learner runs the clay test but uses a heavy ball for the fast trials and
   a light ball for the slow trials. Name the variable the question needs,
   and name one thing the learner wrongly changed.
6. Give one everyday example of "faster = more energy" that you could test
   with clay or soft dough (adult-supervised). Write the prediction you
   would test.
7. True or false, with a one-sentence reason: "If two dents are the same
   depth, the two balls had the same speed." (Assume a fair test.)
8. **Challenge (optional):** A ball rolls fast into clay and makes a deep
   dent. The *same* ball rolls at the *same* speed into a pillow and makes
   almost no mark. Does this break the "faster = more energy" rule? Explain
   in two sentences. (Hint: what is different about where the energy goes?)

## Set B — Energy moves from place to place (after Lessons 02–03)

1. Write the frame sentence for the drum-and-rice station, filling every
   blank: "Energy moved from ___ to ___ by ___. I know because ___."
2. Write the frame sentence for the lamp-and-paper station, filling every
   blank.
3. Draw the energy's trip for the battery-and-bulb circuit as a labeled loop
   with three arrows (cell → wire, wire → bulb, bulb → light + heat). Label
   what changed at the bulb.
4. Your palm is warm; the table is cool. After 30 seconds with your palm flat
   on the table, your palm feels cooler. Which way did energy move — from
   hand to table, or table to hand? What is your evidence?
5. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "The bulb makes its own light, so no energy
   traveled to it." Write two sentences proving energy *did* travel to the
   bulb, naming the disconnect-the-wire control.
6. Name the transfer type (sound, light, heat, or electric current) for each:
   (a) a ringing bell making a nearby paper tremble; (b) sunlight warming a
   windowsill; (c) a warm mug warming your hands; (d) a battery making a toy
   car's motor spin.
7. A learner says the rice jumped "because the drum is magic." Write the
   two-sentence scientific explanation instead (where the energy started;
   the evidence it traveled).
8. **Challenge (optional):** The bulb glows *and* gets warm. A learner says
   this means energy was "wasted." Using this unit's language ("moved and
   changed, not vanished"), explain in two sentences where the energy went.

## Set C — Collisions (after Lesson 04)

1. A fast marble hits a still marble. The still marble rolls away and you
   hear a loud *clack*. Name two places the fast marble's energy went, with
   one piece of evidence for each.
2. Write a prediction *with a reason* for this test: a slow marble rolls
   into a still marble. (Do not run it first — predict.)
3. Draw the collision's energy trip as three labeled arrows: hitting marble
   → target marble; hitting marble → air (sound); plus a note saying which
   marble *lost* energy and which *gained* it.
4. In three trials of the same collision, the target marble rolled far,
   then a medium distance, then far again. Is this a problem? Explain what
   three trials are for and what you would conclude.
5. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "After the collision the energy is gone
   because both marbles eventually stop." Write three sentences explaining
   where the energy actually went.
6. A learner predicts: "The still marble will roll away because the hitting
   marble pushes it." Rewrite this as an *energy* prediction (what loses
   energy, what gains it, what you will hear) — the framework's emphasis is
   the energy change, not the forces.
7. Plan a fair test for Marcus's question: "What happens if *two* marbles
   roll into the still marble at once?" Name the variable, two controls, and
   the number of trials.
8. **Challenge (optional):** A marble hits a wall and bounces straight back
   at almost the same speed, with only a soft click. Where did most of the
   energy go this time, and where did only a little go? Explain in two
   sentences.

## Set D — Spiral review + error analysis (Week 10, with the project report)

1. Without looking back: state the unit's evidence rule in your own words
   ("the bigger the change...").
2. Build a full energy story (start → travel → arrivals → evidence) for the
   lamp-and-paper station. End with: "Was any energy 'used up'? How do you
   know?"
3. **Error analysis:** "Lena rolls a big heavy ball from the top of the ramp
   and a small light ball from the bottom of the ramp, one trial each."
   List (a) the two things Lena changed, (b) the one variable her question
   needs, (c) the fix — including how many trials and why.
4. A battery circuit's bulb glows. The adult removes one wire and the bulb
   goes dark. What does this control test prove about how the energy was
   traveling?
5. Two marbles collide. Marble A stops; Marble B rolls away; you hear a
   *clack*. A learner writes: "Marble A's energy moved into Marble B and
   into the air as sound." Is anything missing from this story? (Hint: what
   about heat — and what can we actually observe?)
6. Order these four events into an energy story: (a) rice jumps; (b) hand
   hits drum; (c) sound travels through air; (d) drum surface shakes. Then
   write the frame sentence.
7. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "Heavier objects always have more energy than
   faster objects." Use the Lesson 01 same-ball test to argue against this
   in two sentences.
8. **Challenge (optional):** Design (in words, no equipment) a fair test for
   this question: "Does a steeper ramp give the ball more energy?" Name the
   variable, three controls, trials, and what you would compare.
