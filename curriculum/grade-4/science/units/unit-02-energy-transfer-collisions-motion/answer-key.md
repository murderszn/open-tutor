# Answer Key — Unit 02: Energy Transfer, Collisions, and Motion

**Keep with the adult — never beside a student question.** Every item below
was solved independently and reconciled with its question. Accept any
wording that carries the same scientific content; the sample answers show
the *reasoning* a correct response must contain. Partial credit: award half
marks when the claim is right but the evidence or reasoning is missing.

## Lesson 01

**Guided practice table (sample):** Trials 1–3 (top/fast): "deeper" each
time. Trials 4–6 (halfway/slow): "shallower" each time. Accept "deep" vs.
"shallow" wording; the pattern (fast → deeper) is what matters.

**Independent practice:**
1. Sample CER — *Claim:* The ball released from the top of the ramp had more
   energy. *Evidence:* In all three fast trials the dent was deeper than in
   all three slow trials. *Reasoning:* The ball's energy moved into the clay
   and changed its shape; a deeper dent is a bigger change, so the faster
   ball had more energy to give.
2. Drawing: the "after fast" lump shows a visibly deeper dent, labeled as
   the bigger change; sentence: "The deeper dent proves the fast ball had
   more energy because it made a bigger change in the clay."
3. Jaden's error: the test used the *same* ball for fast and slow trials, so
   weight cannot explain the different dents — the only difference was the
   speed (release height). Heavier vs. lighter was never tested.

**Exit check:**
1. The fast ball had more energy; evidence: it made the deeper dent (bigger
   change in the clay).
2. Using two different balls would change *two* things (ball and speed), so
   no one could tell which caused the deeper dent — the same ball keeps
   everything fair except speed.

## Lesson 02

**Guided practice:**
1. Sample table — soft: "rice trembles"; medium: "rice hops a little";
   hard: "rice jumps high." Most energy: the hard tap; evidence: the rice
   jumped highest (biggest change).
2. Prediction (sample): "The three-minute paper will feel warmer, because
   the light has more time to carry energy to the paper." Accept either
   prediction *if* the reason is about energy carried by light; the
   observation (three-minute paper warmer) is the check.

**Independent practice:**
1. "Energy moved from my hand to the drum to the air to the rice grains by
   sound. I know because the rice jumped when the drum was tapped, and my
   hand never touched the rice."
2. "Energy moved from the lamp to the paper by light. I know because the
   paper under the lamp got warmer than the control sheet, and the lamp
   never touched the paper."
3. Drawing: three labeled arrows (hand → drum; drum → air; air → rice) with
   a change noted under each (drum surface shakes; air carries sound — heard
   as the tap; rice jumps).
4. Accept: "Priya's explanation is not scientific because it names no
   mechanism. Better: 'The energy started in my hand when I hit the drum. It
   traveled through the air as sound to the rice — I know because the rice
   moved although nothing touched it.'"

**Exit check:**
1. Path: hand → drum → air → rice grains; evidence: the rice jumped (and
   jumped higher with harder taps) though nothing touched it.
2. In both, the *change where the energy arrived* is the proof: the dent in
   the clay, the warmth in the paper. Energy is never seen directly — only
   its effects.

## Lesson 03

**Guided practice:**
1. Frame sentence: "Energy moved from my warm hand to the cool table by
   heat. I know because my hand felt cooler and the table spot felt warmer."
   Underlined direction: hand → table (warmer → cooler). The hand lost
   energy; the table gained it.
2. Sample prediction: "When the loop is complete, the bulb will glow,
   because the electric current will be able to travel from the battery to
   the bulb." Trace-aloud: "Energy started at the battery cell, traveled by
   electric current through the wires, arrived at the bulb, and I know
   because the bulb glowed only when the loop was complete — and it got a
   little warm."

**Independent practice:**
1. "Energy moved from the warm water to the spoon by heat. I know because
   the spoon's far end got warmer, and nothing else touched the spoon."
2. Drawing: labeled loop (cell, wire, bulb, wire) with arrows cell → wire
   (current travels), wire → bulb (current arrives), bulb → light + heat
   labeled "bulb glows and warms — the changes that prove energy arrived."
3. Theo's correction: "The wires do not make the light — they are the path
   the electric current travels. The light's energy starts in the battery
   cell; I know because removing one wire (breaking the path) makes the
   bulb go dark."

**Exit check:**
1. Energy moved from hand to table (warmer → cooler). Evidence: the hand
   felt cooler and the table spot felt warmer afterward.
2. The bulb's light depends on the complete loop — so the energy must travel
   *through the loop* as an electric current from the battery to the bulb.

## Lesson 04

**Guided practice (sample records):** Medium-speed hit, three trials —
hitting marble: "slowed" each time; target marble: "moved a little/medium"
each time; sound: "soft clack" each time. Energy trace: hitting marble
(lost energy — slowed) → target marble (gained energy — moved) → air (sound
— the clack).

**Independent practice:**
1. Sample predict-observe-explain: *Predict:* "The first still marble will
   roll into the second, which will roll away; the hitting marble will
   slow. Reason: the hitting marble's energy will move into the first
   marble, then into the second, and some will go to the air as sound."
   *Observe:* (learner's actual trials). *Explain:* must name where the
   energy went in the learner's own test, citing what moved and what was
   heard.
2. Drawing: three arrows — hitting marble → target marble ("target moves:
   gained energy"); hitting marble → air ("clack: energy became sound");
   note: "hitting marble lost energy (slowed/stopped); target gained energy
   (started moving)."
3. Ana's correction (sample): "The energy is not gone. Some moved into the
   second marble, which is why it rolled. Some moved into the air as sound
   — the clack proves energy left the marbles. 'Gone' is wrong because we
   can point to where the energy went."

**Exit check:**
1. Two places: into the still marble (evidence: it rolled away — it gained
   energy and motion) and into the air as sound (evidence: the loud clack).
2. A prediction written after the test is just a description of what
   happened — it cannot be wrong, so it teaches nothing. Predicting first
   with a reason is what makes the test a test of the idea.

## Lesson 05

**Guided practice:**
1. Story (sample, drum station): "Energy started in my hand when I hit the
   drum. It traveled through the air as sound. It arrived at the rice
   grains (they jumped — evidence) and at my ears (I heard the tap —
   evidence). No energy was 'used up' — I can point to the jumping rice and
   the sound I heard."
2. Mia's fix: she changed two things — the loudness AND the tapping tool
   (finger vs. spoon). Fixed plan: tap with the *same* tool (finger) at two
   loudness levels, three taps each; variable = tap loudness (strength);
   controls = same drum, same rice, same tool.

**Independent practice:**
1. Sample story (marbles): "Energy started in my hand as I lifted the
   marble to the ramp top. It traveled down the ramp as the marble sped up.
   At the collision it went three places: into the target marble (it rolled
   — evidence), into the air as sound (the clack — evidence), and a tiny
   bit into heat where they touched (named because the sound proves energy
   left the marbles). No energy was 'used up' — I can point to the rolling
   target marble and the clack I heard."
2. Sam's test: (a) two things changed — the ball (big/heavy vs. small/light)
   and the release height (top vs. bottom); (b) the question needs release
   height (speed) as the one variable; (c) control: the same ball for both
   sets (plus same ramp, same clay); (d) three trials per set, because one
   roll could be a lucky bounce.
3. "A drum stops ringing, so its energy is gone" — **Disagree.** The sound
   itself was energy leaving the drum for the air (Lesson 02: the rice
   jumped). When the ringing fades, the energy has finished moving into the
   air as sound — it moved, it did not vanish.

**Exit check:**
1. Sample: "Energy started in my hand, traveled through the air as sound,
   arrived at the rice grains (they jumped — evidence) and at my ears (I
   heard it — evidence)."
2. Unfair because two things changed (ball size/weight AND speed). Fix: use
   the same ball, release from the top three times and from halfway three
   times — one variable (release height/speed), everything else the same.

## Practice Sets

**Set A:** 1. Claim: the red marble was faster. Evidence: its dent is deeper
(left lump). Reasoning: deeper dent = bigger change = more energy (Lesson
01 rule). 2. Predict: Ball P (top release) makes the deeper dent; reason:
it will be moving faster, and faster = more energy. 3. The ramp length is
not the reason — the reason is the *speed*: the longer ramp only matters
because it lets the ball go faster. Correct: "The faster ball had more
energy, which made the deeper dent." 4. Drawing: fast-hit lump with deeper
dent labeled; sentence: "The deeper dent proves the fast ball had more
energy." 5. Variable needed: speed (release height). Wrongly changed: the
ball (heavy vs. light) — two variables. 6. Any adult-supervised fast/slow
pair with a prediction (e.g., sock ball dropped from shoulder vs. knee onto
a pillow; predict shoulder drop marks deeper). 7. **True** — in a fair test
with the same ball and clay, equal dents mean equal changes, so equal
energy and equal speed. 8. Challenge: No — the rule is about the energy the
ball *had*, shown by the total change it makes. The pillow spreads the stop
over a soft, wide area (energy goes into squashing the pillow slowly and
into the air), so the *mark* is small even though the energy was the same.
The dent measures one kind of change, not all the energy.

**Set B:** 1. "Energy moved from my hand to the drum to the air to the rice
grains by sound. I know because the rice jumped when the drum was tapped,
and my hand never touched the rice." 2. "Energy moved from the lamp to the
paper by light. I know because the paper under the lamp got warmer than the
control sheet, and the lamp never touched the paper." 3. Loop drawing with
three arrows and the bulb labeled "glows + warms — the changes proving
energy arrived." 4. Hand → table; evidence: hand felt cooler, table spot
felt warmer. 5. "The bulb cannot make its own light: it glows only when the
loop is complete. Removing one wire makes it go dark, so the energy must
travel to the bulb through the loop as an electric current from the
battery." 6. (a) sound; (b) light; (c) heat; (d) electric current. 7. "The
energy started in my hand when I hit the drum. It traveled through the air
as sound to the rice — I know because the rice moved although nothing
touched it." 8. Challenge: The energy was not wasted — it *moved and
changed*: most became light (the glow we see), some became heat (the warmth
we feel). "Wasted" is wrong because we can point to both arrivals.

**Set C:** 1. Into the still marble (evidence: it rolled away) and into the
air as sound (evidence: the loud clack). 2. Any prediction with a reason,
e.g.: "I predict the still marble will roll a short distance and the clack
will be soft, because the slower marble has less energy to give." 3. Three
arrows: hitting → target ("target moves: gained energy"); hitting → air
("clack: sound"); note: "hitting marble lost energy (slowed); target gained
energy (moved)." 4. Not a problem — three trials exist to show the pattern
despite wobble. Conclusion: the collision reliably sends the target marble
rolling; the medium trial is normal variation (note it, do not hide it). 5.
"The energy is not gone. Some moved into the second marble — that is why it
rolled. Some moved into the air as sound — the clack proves energy left the
marbles. 'Gone' is wrong because I can point to where the energy went." 6.
Energy prediction: "I predict the hitting marble will slow down (lose
energy), the still marble will start moving (gain energy), and I will hear
a clack (some energy goes to the air as sound)." 7. Variable: number of
hitting marbles (one vs. two). Controls (any two): same release height,
same marbles, same target start position, same lane. Trials: three per
condition. 8. Challenge: Most of the energy stayed with the marble (it
bounced back nearly as fast — it kept its energy); only a little went to the
air as sound (soft click) and a trace into heat at the bounce point.

**Set D:** 1. "The bigger the change a moving object makes, the more energy
it had." 2. Story: start = lamp; travel = light; arrivals = paper (warmer —
evidence by touch vs. control sheet); final: "No energy was 'used up' — I
can point to the warmer paper; the light's energy moved into the paper as
warmth." 3. (a) The two things changed: the ball (big/heavy vs.
small/light) and the release height (top vs. bottom) — plus only one trial
each, so no pattern can show. (b) The question needs release height (speed)
as the one variable. (c) Fix: same ball, same ramp, same clay; release from
the top three times, then from halfway three times; compare dent depth.
Three trials per set because one roll could be a lucky bounce. 4.
It proves the energy reaches the bulb *through the complete loop* as an
electric current — break the loop and the energy cannot travel, so the light
stops. 5. The story is missing the *heat* arrival — but honestly: we cannot
feel it in this test, so the correct completion is "a tiny bit also becomes
heat where they touched, which we name (not observe) because the clack
proves energy left the marbles." Full marks for either naming heat with the
honest caveat or noting that heat is not observable here. 6. Order: (b) hand
hits drum → (d) drum surface shakes → (c) sound travels through air → (a)
rice jumps. Frame: "Energy moved from my hand to the drum to the air to the
rice by sound. I know because the rice jumped though nothing touched it." 7.
"The Lesson 01 test used the *same* ball fast and slow, and the fast trials
made deeper dents. Speed alone changed the result — so a faster light ball
can have more energy than a slower heavy one; 'heavier always wins' is
false." 8. Design: variable = ramp steepness (shallow vs. steep). Controls:
same ball, same release height measured along the ramp, same clay lump,
same ramp surface. Trials: three per steepness. Compare: dent depth
(deeper/shallower).

*Reconciliation note (Set D #3): the question as written changes the ball
and runs one trial each — the key above counts "one trial each" as the
second flaw. If a learner instead names "ball and speed" as the two changed
things, accept it only if they note the release heights were never said to
differ — the question says "fast" and "slowly" without fixing how speed was
set, so the plan is underspecified. Either well-argued answer earns full
marks; the fix (same ball, two release heights, three trials) is required.*

## Unit assessment (30 pts)

**Part 1:** 1. Sample: "Roll the same ball down the same ramp into same-size
clay lumps — release from the top three times, then from halfway three
times. Variable: release height (speed). Controls: same ball, same ramp,
same clay. Compare: which set's dents are deeper." (2 pts: 1 for the plan,
1 for variable + two controls + comparison.) 2. Claim: "The faster ball
(the top release) has more energy." Evidence: "Its three dents were deeper
than the three slow-trial dents." Reasoning: "The ball's energy changed the
clay's shape; a deeper dent is a bigger change, so the faster ball had more
energy." (2 pts: all three present and matched.) 3. Unfair: two variables
(ball size/weight AND release height). Fix: same ball, top vs. halfway
releases, three trials each. (2 pts.) 4. Any two sensible explanations:
the clay lumps were not the same softness/size; the "fast" and "slow"
releases were actually similar speeds; the ramp moved between sets; the
dents were compared by memory instead of side by side. (2 pts, 1 each.)

**Part 2:** 5. Frame: "Energy moved from my hand to the drum to the air to
the rice grains by sound. I know because the rice jumped when the drum was

**Part 3:** 9. (a) Yes — good prediction: written *before* testing, with an

**Part 4:** 12. Score 1 pt per element: start named; travel named; every

## Project rubric — sample "meeting" responses

- **Question & prediction (3/4):** "Does releasing the hitting marble from
  higher make the target roll farther? I predict yes, because the higher
  release makes the marble faster, and faster = more energy to give."
- **Fair-test plan (3/4):** variable = release height; controls = same
  marbles, same lane, same target start; 3 trials per height; table columns:
  trial / release height / target roll distance (farther-nearer-same).
- **Data table (3/4):** columns labeled, all 6+ trials recorded with
  distances in cm, one wobble trial noted and kept.
- **CER (3/4):** claim answers the question; evidence cites the table
  ("target rolled farther in all three high-release trials"); reasoning uses
  faster = more energy.
- **Energy story (3/4):** "The hitting marble slowed (lost energy), the
  target rolled (gained energy), and we heard a clack (some energy went to
  the air as sound)."
