# Unit Assessment — Energy Transfer, Collisions, and Motion (culminating)

**Total: 30 points.** Taken in Session 16, after the energy fair. Covers all
four unit objectives (4-PS3-1, 4-PS3-2, 4-PS3-3, fair investigation). The
adult scores against the [answer key](../answer-key.md). No speed numbers or
energy calculations anywhere — qualitative evidence only.

## Part 1 — Faster means more energy (8 pts)

1. **(2 pts)** Describe a fair test that shows a faster-moving object has
   more energy. Name the variable, two controls, and what you would compare.
2. **(2 pts)** In your test from question 1, the fast trials make deep
   dents and the slow trials make shallow dents. Write the
   claim-evidence-reasoning: one sentence for the claim, one for the
   evidence, one for the reasoning.
3. **(2 pts)** Sam rolls a big heavy ball from the top of a ramp and a small
   light ball from the bottom, then compares the dents. What is unfair
   about Sam's test, and what is the one-variable fix?
4. **(2 pts)** A ball rolls down a ramp twice — once fast, once slow — into
   identical clay lumps, but *both* dents look the same depth. Give two
   possible explanations for what went wrong (not "the rule is wrong").

## Part 2 — Energy moves from place to place (10 pts)

5. **(3 pts)** For the drum-and-rice station, write the complete frame
   sentence ("Energy moved from ___ to ___ by ___. I know because ___."),
   then name the full energy path in three or more steps.
6. **(3 pts)** An adult holds a lamp over white paper (lamp never touches
   the paper) and the paper warms up. (a) What carried energy to the paper?
   (b) What is the evidence energy arrived? (c) Why was a second sheet of
   paper, kept in normal room light, a good control?
7. **(2 pts)** Your warm hand rests on a cool table for 30 seconds. Which
   way does energy move, and what two observations prove it?
8. **(2 pts)** A bulb glows in a complete battery-wire-bulb loop and also
   feels warm. Where did the battery's energy go? Name two arrivals and the
   evidence for each.

## Part 3 — Collisions (8 pts)

9. **(3 pts)** Before running a collision test, a learner writes: "I predict
   the still marble will roll away and I will hear a clack, because the
   hitting marble will give some of its energy to the still marble and some
   to the air as sound." After three trials, exactly that happens. (a) Was
   this a good prediction? Why? (b) Name two places the hitting marble's
   energy went, with evidence.
10. **(3 pts)** A learner says, "After the collision both marbles stop, so
    the energy is gone." Write the three-sentence correction: where the
    energy went, what the *clack* proves, and why "gone" is the wrong word.
11. **(2 pts)** Two collisions: in Collision X the hitting marble is fast
    and the *clack* is loud; in Collision Y the hitting marble is slow and
    the *clack* is soft. The target marble rolls farther in X. Explain both
    differences using "more energy → bigger change."

## Part 4 — Energy story (4 pts)

12. **(4 pts)** Build a complete energy story for **one** station of your
    choice (clay dents, drum + rice, lamp + paper, circuit, or marbles).
    Include: where the energy started, how it traveled, every place it
    arrived, one piece of evidence per arrival, and a final sentence
    answering "Was any energy 'used up'? How do you know?"

**Scoring guide:** 27–30 exceeding · 22–26 meeting · 16–21 approaching
(targeted re-teach of the missed parts, then one re-take of those parts
only) · below 16: re-teach the unit's three core ideas with the stations
re-run before any re-take. This assessment is a draft classroom check, not
a standardized or validated instrument.
