# Lesson 02 — Energy Moves: Sound and Light

**Goal (student-readable):** I can show with observations that energy moves
from place to place by sound and by light, and I can name what moved and what
changed.
**Time:** about 35 minutes. **Standard:** 4-PS3-2.

**Materials (adult gathers):** a small drum, pot with a lid, or sturdy bowl;
a few grains of dry rice; a cool-touch LED desk lamp (the adult holds and
aims it — never look directly into the light); a sheet of white paper; a
hand mirror (adult-handled). The adult supervises both stations.

**Readiness check (2 minutes):** "Yesterday's evidence rule said: the
bigger the change a moving object makes, the more energy it had. Today we
follow energy on a trip. When you hear a drum, where did that sound's energy
*start*, and where did it *go*?" Accept guesses; the stations will test
them. Recall: energy is what lets things move, make sound, give light, or
warm things up — and today we watch it **move from place to place**.

## Teaching

Energy does not stay where it starts. It **transfers** — it moves from one
place to another — and we know it arrived because something **changes**.
Today's transfer routes are **sound** and **light**. There are no energy
numbers in this unit; our evidence is always an observation of a change.

**Station 1 — Sound moves energy.** Stretch no equipment over the drum: just
sprinkle a few grains of dry rice on the drum's surface (or the pot lid).
Tap the drum softly, then firmly. The rice grains jump. Ask: *what made the
rice move?* Your hand hit the drum — but your hand never touched the rice.
The energy traveled: **hand → drum → air → rice grains**. The evidence that
energy arrived at the rice is that the rice **moved**. Tap harder and the
rice jumps higher — more energy at the start, bigger change at the end
(connecting back to Lesson 01's rule: more energy → bigger change).

**Station 2 — Light moves energy.** The adult holds the LED lamp about 15 cm
above the white paper for two minutes. The learner feels the paper afterward:
it is slightly **warmer** than paper that sat in the room's normal light.
The lamp never touched the paper. The energy traveled: **lamp → light →
paper**, and the paper **warmed up** — the change that proves energy
arrived. (Adult note: an LED lamp is used because it stays cool to touch;
the warmth on the paper comes from the light's energy, not from a hot
bulb.)

The sentence frame scientists use for 4-PS3-2 observations is:

> **Energy moved from ___ to ___ by ___. I know because ___.**

Example: "Energy moved from the drum to the rice grains by sound. I know
because the rice jumped when the drum was tapped, and jumped higher when I
tapped harder."

### Worked example 1 — The drum and the rice (modeled)

Lena sprinkles rice on a pot lid and taps it gently: the rice trembles. She
taps it firmly: the rice hops.

- **Observation with the frame:** "Energy moved from Lena's hand to the pot
  lid to the air to the rice by sound. I know because the rice moved when
  the lid was tapped, and my hand never touched the rice — so the energy
  must have traveled through the air as sound."
- **The bigger-change check:** "When Lena tapped harder, the rice jumped
  higher. More energy in → bigger change out, the same rule as the clay
  dents."

### Worked example 2 — The lamp and the paper (modeled)

Marcus's adult holds the LED lamp over white paper for two minutes. Marcus
feels the paper: warm. He feels a second sheet that sat nearby in normal
room light: not warm.

- **Observation with the frame:** "Energy moved from the lamp to the paper
  by light. I know because the paper under the lamp got warmer than the
  paper that was not under the lamp, and the lamp never touched the paper —
  so the light must have carried the energy."
- **The fair comparison:** "Marcus used a second sheet as a control — same
  paper, same room, only the lamp light was different. That is the Unit 01
  fair-test habit working inside an observation."

## Guided practice (with the adult)

1. **Sound station, learner-led:** the learner taps the drum at three
   loudness levels (soft, medium, hard), three taps each, and records what
   the rice does each time in a labeled table (columns: *tap strength, what
   the rice did*). Prompt: "Which tap gave the rice the most energy? What is
   your evidence?"
2. **Light station, learner-led:** the learner predicts — in writing, before
   testing — whether paper under the lamp for *one* minute or *three* minutes
   will feel warmer, with a reason. The adult runs the timing; the learner
   feels and records. Prompt: "Did the evidence match your prediction? What
   would you change about your prediction's reason?"

## Independent practice

1. Complete two frame sentences (one per station) for today's stations,
   filling every blank, including the evidence blank.
2. Draw the energy's trip for the drum station as three labeled arrows:
   hand → drum, drum → air, air → rice. Under each arrow write what changed.
3. **Explain-your-reasoning:** Priya says, "The rice jumped because the drum
   is magic." Using today's observations, write two sentences that give a
   better explanation — one naming where the energy started, one naming the
   evidence it traveled.

## Applied task

Find one sound-energy transfer and one light-energy transfer at home (with
the adult): a ringing phone that makes something tremble, sunlight warming a
windowsill, a flashlight warming your palm (adult holds the light). For each,
write the frame sentence: "Energy moved from ___ to ___ by ___. I know
because ___."

## Exit check

1. The learner taps the drum and the rice jumps. Name the full energy path
   (three or more steps) and the evidence that energy reached the rice.
2. How is the lamp-and-paper observation like the clay-dent test from Lesson
   01? (Hint: in both, what tells you energy arrived?)

## Supports, extensions, teacher note

- **Reading/language access:** the frame sentence is posted on a card;
  "transfer" is defined as "energy moving from one place to another" with
  the arrow drawing as the visual definition.
- **Alternate response mode:** frame sentences may be dictated; the
  energy-trip drawing may replace written answers.
- **Extension:** test whether the rice jumps differently on a *tight* vs.
  *loose* drum surface (a second fair test — surface tightness is the new
  variable); or use the hand mirror to bounce lamplight onto the paper and
  check whether reflected light also warms it (yes — light still carries
  energy after reflecting, a preview of Unit 03's light-and-seeing work).
- **Likely misconception:** learners may say the *rice* has the energy "in
  it" and stop there. Press the path question: "Where was the energy *one
  step before* the rice?" until the chain reaches the hand.
- **Safety:** never look directly into the lamp; the adult aims all lights.
