# Lesson 03 — Energy Moves: Heat and Electric Currents

**Goal (student-readable):** I can show with observations that energy moves
from place to place by heat and by electric currents, and I can trace the
energy's path out loud.
**Time:** about 35 minutes. **Standard:** 4-PS3-2.

**Materials (adult gathers):** a ceramic mug of warm (not hot) water — the
adult prepares and tests it; a cool metal spoon; one AA or C battery cell;
a small flashlight bulb or LED with a pre-wired holder; two insulated wires
with alligator clips (a pre-made "battery-and-bulb kit" is ideal). **Safety:
battery cells only, handled by the adult — no mains power, no wall outlets,
no bare wires to the skin.** The adult assembles and disassembles the
circuit; the learner observes, predicts, and records.

**Readiness check (2 minutes):** "Yesterday energy traveled by sound and by
light. Today: by **heat** and by **electric current**. Put your hands
together and rub them fast for ten seconds — what do you feel? Where did
that warmth come from?" (Rubbing hands: motion energy became heat you can
feel — a transfer the learner makes themselves.) Recall the frame: *Energy
moved from ___ to ___ by ___. I know because ___.*

## Teaching

**Station 1 — Heat moves energy.** The adult places the mug of warm water on
the table and lays the cool metal spoon against the outside of the mug for
one minute. The learner touches the spoon's far end (the adult confirms it
is only warm, never hot): it has warmed up. The spoon was cool; the water
was warm; nothing else touched the spoon. The energy traveled: **warm water
→ mug wall → spoon**, and the evidence is that the spoon's far end **got
warmer**. Heat is energy you feel as warmth, and here it moved from the
warmer thing to the cooler thing — that direction (warm → cool) is the
pattern to notice.

Second observation, no equipment: press your warm palm flat on the cool
table for thirty seconds, then lift it. The table spot feels slightly warm
and your palm feels slightly cooler. Energy moved **from your hand to the
table by heat** — in both directions of the warm→cool pattern, the warmer
thing gives energy to the cooler thing.

**Station 2 — Electric currents move energy.** The adult connects the
circuit: battery cell → wire → bulb → wire → back to the cell. The bulb
**glows** — and after a minute, the bulb feels slightly **warm** too. Then
the adult disconnects one wire: the bulb goes dark. Reconnect: it glows
again.

Trace the path with the learner: energy starts in the **battery cell**,
travels as an **electric current** through the **wires** (a complete loop —
a *circuit*), arrives at the **bulb**, and changes into **light** (it glows)
and a little **heat** (it warms). Break the loop and the current stops, so
the light stops. The evidence that energy moved: the bulb glows only when
the loop is complete, and it warms up while it glows. This is the first time
in the unit the learner sees energy **change from one form to another along
its trip** (electric → light + heat) — worth naming, without any formulas.

The learner's trace-aloud routine (used at every station from now on):
point along the path and say, *"Energy started at ___, traveled by ___,
arrived at ___, and I know because ___."*

### Worked example 1 — The spoon against the mug (modeled)

Aisha's adult holds a cool spoon against a mug of warm water for one minute.
Aisha touches the spoon's far end: warm.

- **Observation with the frame:** "Energy moved from the warm water to the
  spoon by heat. I know because the spoon's far end got warmer, and nothing
  else touched the spoon."
- **The pattern:** "The water was warmer than the spoon, and the energy
  moved from the warmer thing to the cooler thing — the same direction as
  my warm hand on the cool table."

### Worked example 2 — The battery-and-bulb circuit (modeled)

Devon's adult connects the battery, wires, and bulb in a loop. The bulb
glows. The adult disconnects one wire; the bulb goes dark. Reconnect; glow.

- **Observation with the trace-aloud:** "Energy started at the battery cell,
  traveled by electric current through the wires, arrived at the bulb, and I
  know because the bulb glowed only when the loop was complete — and it got
  a little warm, so some energy became heat too."
- **The control:** "The disconnected wire is the control test: same battery,
  same bulb, same wires — only the complete loop changed. No loop, no light.
  So the loop is what lets the energy travel."

## Guided practice (with the adult)

1. **Heat station, learner-led:** the learner runs the palm-on-table test
   themselves (30 seconds), then writes the frame sentence and underlines
   the *warmer → cooler* direction. Prompt: "Which thing lost energy, and
   which gained it? How do you know?"
2. **Circuit station, observed:** before the adult connects the loop, the
   learner **predicts in writing**: "When the loop is complete, the bulb
   will ___ because ___." The adult connects; the learner records the
   result and whether the prediction matched. Then the learner traces the
   full path aloud, pointing at each part.

## Independent practice

1. Write the frame sentence for the spoon-against-the-mug observation,
   including the evidence blank.
2. Draw the circuit as a labeled loop (battery cell, wire, bulb, wire) and
   add three arrows showing the energy's trip: cell → wire, wire → bulb,
   bulb → light + heat. Label what changed at the bulb.
3. **Explain-your-reasoning:** Theo says, "The wires make the light."
   Write two sentences explaining what the wires actually do — and where
   the light's energy really comes from.

## Applied task

With the adult, find two heat transfers at home: a warm laundry pile warming
your hands, a sunny windowsill warming a book, soup warming a spoon. For
each, write the frame sentence and circle the *warmer → cooler* direction.
(Do not touch anything hot — warm only, and the adult checks first.)

## Exit check

1. Your palm is warm and the table is cool. After 30 seconds your palm feels
   cooler and the table spot feels warmer. Which way did energy move, and
   what is your evidence?
2. The bulb glows when the loop is complete and goes dark when one wire is
   removed. What does that tell you about how electric current moves energy?

## Supports, extensions, teacher note

- **Reading/language access:** "circuit" is taught as "a complete loop for
  the current — like a racetrack the energy runs around"; the trace-aloud
  routine is posted as four numbered steps.
- **Alternate response mode:** frame sentences may be dictated; the circuit
  drawing may be built with labeled cut-out shapes instead of drawn.
- **Extension:** predict and test what happens with **two** battery cells in
  the loop (brighter bulb — more energy at the start, bigger change at the
  end; adult handles all cells); or time how long the spoon takes to feel
  warm against warm water vs. cool water (a fair test with water temperature
  as the variable — qualitative warmer/cooler judgments only).
- **Likely misconception:** "electricity is used up by the bulb." Return to
  the observation: the bulb gives off light *and* heat — the energy did not
  vanish; it moved and changed. (The full "energy is never destroyed" idea
  is beyond grade 4; the lesson only establishes *moved and changed*.)
- **Safety (non-negotiable):** cells only, adult-handled; no mains power,
  no wall outlets; the adult confirms the mug water is warm, never hot.
