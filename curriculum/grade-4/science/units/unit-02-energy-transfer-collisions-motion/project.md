# Project — The Collision Challenge

**Type:** fair-test investigation (not a device build — 4-PS3-4's
energy-converting device work belongs to Unit 08; this project practices the
3-5-ETS1-3 investigation routine on a collision question).
**Time:** Sessions 12–14 (plan, collect data, write the report).
**Standards:** 4-PS3-3 (ask questions, predict collision outcomes), 4-PS3-1
(evidence: faster = more energy), science practices (fair test, three trials,
labeled tables, Unit 01 report format).

## The challenge

Choose **one** collision question, plan a fair test, predict the outcome
*with a reason*, run at least three trials per condition, and write the
investigation report in the Unit 01 format. Then present your energy story
at the Session 15 energy fair.

**Question bank** (pick one, or write your own with the adult's approval —
your own must keep exactly one variable):

1. **Release height:** Does releasing the hitting marble from higher up
   make the target marble roll farther? (Variable: release height. Controls:
   same marbles, same lane, same target start position.)
2. **Number of hitters:** Does rolling *two* marbles at once into the target
   move it farther than one marble? (Variable: number of hitting marbles.
   Controls: same release height, same marbles, same target.)
3. **Target distance:** Does starting the target marble closer to / farther
   from the ramp change how far it rolls after the hit? (Variable: target
   start position. Controls: same release height, same marbles.)
4. **Surface:** Does the target marble roll farther on a smooth table or on
   a cloth-covered table after the same hit? (Variable: surface. Controls:
   same release height, same marbles, same target start.)

## Materials (adult gathers and checks)

Ramp and books (table height, adult-built), 6 identical marbles, collision
lane (cardboard strip or grooved ruler), modeling clay target stands, tape
for start/finish marks, ruler or tape measure (for *distances only* — we
compare farther/nearer, never compute speed), the Unit 01 report template,
paper and pencils. Same safety rules as the lessons: adult sets up every
ramp; marbles stay clear of stairs.

## Steps

1. **Choose and ask (Session 12, 15 min).** Pick a question from the bank.
   Write it as a testable question: "Does ___ change ___?"
2. **Predict with a reason (Session 12, 10 min).** Write: "I predict ___ will
   happen, because ___." The reason must use unit language (more energy,
   energy moves, faster = more energy). No testing yet.
3. **Plan the fair test (Session 12, 10 min).** Fill the plan card and get
   the adult's signature *before* touching the equipment:
   - Variable (the one thing I change): ___
   - Controls (at least three things I keep the same): ___
   - Trials (how many per condition, minimum three): ___
   - What I will observe and record (columns for my table): ___
4. **Collect data (Session 13, 30 min + 5 min tidy).** Run the trials.
   Record every trial in the labeled table — including the "weird" ones.
   If something breaks the fair setup (a marble jumps the lane), note it
   and re-run that trial; do not silently drop data.
5. **Write the report (Session 14, 25 min).** Use the Unit 01
   investigation-report format, all seven sections: question, prediction,
   materials, steps, data table, claim-evidence-reasoning, what I would
   change next time.
6. **Tell the energy story (Session 15, energy fair).** Run your collision
   as one fair station: visitors (the adult, family) predict first, then
   watch, then hear your three-part energy story (giver → receiver → sound
   to the air).

## Deliverables

1. Signed plan card (variable, controls, trials, table columns).
2. Labeled data table with all trials recorded.
3. Seven-section investigation report.
4. Energy-fair station: a written visitor prediction sheet plus your spoken
   energy story.

## Rubric — 40 points

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Solid | 2 — Developing | 1 — Starting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Question & prediction** (8 pts) | Testable question; prediction written *before* testing with an energy-based reason | Question testable; prediction before testing; reason is vague | Question unclear or prediction written after testing | No prediction, or prediction is a guess with no reason |
| **Fair-test plan** (8 pts) | One variable named; 3+ controls; ≥3 trials; adult-signed plan card | One variable; 2 controls; ≥3 trials | Two variables, or only 1 control, or fewer than 3 trials | No plan, or plan changed mid-test without note |
| **Data table** (8 pts) | Labeled columns, every trial recorded (including odd ones), units named (cm) | Labeled columns, all trials recorded | Table missing labels or missing trials | No table, or data invented after the fact |
| **Claim-evidence-reasoning** (8 pts) | Claim answers the question; evidence cites the table; reasoning uses "faster = more energy" / energy-moved language | All three present; reasoning partly everyday language | Claim-evidence mismatch, or reasoning missing | No CER, or claim contradicts the data |
| **Energy story & fair station** (8 pts) | Three-part story (giver → receiver → sound/heat); visitors predict first; story matches the data | Story told with two of three parts; visitors predict | Story is "it moved" with no parts; no visitor prediction | No story, or story contradicts observed evidence |

**Scoring:** 36–40 Exceeding · 28–35 Meeting · 20–27 Approaching · below 20
Reteach (revisit Lesson 04's predict-observe-explain and Lesson 05's
error analysis, then re-run two trials with the adult).

**Readiness / reteach signals:** a plan card with two variables → stop and
re-do Lesson 05's Sam's-test fix before collecting data. A report whose
claim contradicts its table → re-teach claim-evidence-reasoning with the
Lesson 01 worked example. A story that says energy "disappeared" → replay
the collision and listen for the clack together.
