# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Energy Transfer, Collisions, and Motion

**Keep with the adult.** Learner-facing pages link here with clear labeling;
the [answer key](answer-key.md) is a separate file. This unit is a
**validated draft** — educator review pending; adapt freely and note what
needs changing.

## What the adult needs to know first

- **The three ideas, in adult words:** (1) A faster-moving object has more
  energy — shown by the *change* it makes (deeper dent, farther roll).
  (2) Energy moves from place to place by sound, light, heat, and electric
  currents — shown by the *change* where it arrives. (3) In collisions,
  energy moves from the hitter to the target and to the air as sound (and a
  trace of heat) — it does not vanish. Everything is **qualitative**: no
  speed numbers, no energy units, no formulas. That is the 4-PS3 assessment
  boundary, not a simplification — hold it.
- **Learner language for "energy":** "what lets things move, make sound,
  give light, or warm things up." The adult's "ability to do work" (EIA)
  stays in the background; do not test the learner on a dictionary
  definition.
- **The two sentence frames** (post both on cards):
  - Evidence rule: *"The bigger the change a moving object makes, the more
    energy it had."*
  - Transfer frame: *"Energy moved from ___ to ___ by ___. I know because
    ___."*

## Materials checklist (whole unit)

Ramp: smooth board ~60 cm, stack of books, tape. Balls: 2 identical marbles
or bouncy balls + 5 identical target marbles. Clay: modeling clay (a few
plum-sized lumps; wash hands after). Drum station: small drum, pot + lid, or
sturdy bowl; dry rice grains. Light station: cool-touch LED desk lamp
(adult-held); white paper; hand mirror (adult-handled). Heat station:
ceramic mug, warm (not hot) water — adult prepares and tests; cool metal
spoon. Circuit station: 1 AA/C cell, small bulb or LED in a holder, two
insulated wires with alligator clips (a pre-made kit is ideal). Lane: flat
cardboard strip or grooved ruler; clay target stands; tape measure or ruler
(for *distances only*). Paper, pencils, the Unit 01 report template.

## Safety (read before every hands-on session)

1. Ramps are built from books and boards **at table height only**; the adult
   builds and checks every ramp before the learner touches it.
2. Rolling objects stay **clear of stairs**; run tests on the floor or a
   large table with a tray catch.
3. **Battery cells only, handled by the adult** — no mains power, no wall
   outlets, no bare wires against skin. The adult assembles and
   disassembles the circuit.
4. Lamp work uses a **cool-touch LED** held by the adult; the learner never
   looks directly into any light.
5. Mug water is **warm, never hot** — the adult tests it on the wrist first.
6. Materials stay away from the mouth; hands washed after clay.

## Lesson-by-lesson adult directions

**Lesson 01 (clay dents):** Build the ramp before the session. Flatten the
two clay lumps to matching starting shapes yourself — learners' "same size"
judgments are unreliable. Release the ball *without pushing* (a push adds
uncontrolled energy). If dents are ambiguous, switch to a softer clay or a
heavier ball; do not switch mid-trial-set. The image activity: confirm the
learner's answer cites the *dent depth*, not the marble colors.

**Lesson 02 (sound/light):** Rice grains: a dozen is plenty; tap *near* the
drum, not on the rice. Three loudness levels × three taps keeps it fair.
Lamp: hold it yourself ~15 cm above the paper for the full two minutes; the
"control sheet" must sit in the same room light. If the paper barely warms,
extend to three minutes — then note the change honestly rather than
claiming a big effect.

**Lesson 03 (heat/current):** Prepare the mug water yourself (comfortable
warm). For the palm test, 30 seconds is enough. Circuit: connect it once
yourself before the session to confirm the bulb works; let the learner
*predict first*, then connect in front of them. Name the loop a "circuit =
complete loop, like a racetrack" and keep that metaphor all unit.

**Lesson 04 (collisions):** Mark the target marble's start with tape. The
lane keeps trials comparable — a marble that jumps the lane is a re-run, not
a data point. Insist the prediction is written *before* the first roll;
"predictions" written after are observations wearing a costume. Three
trials minimum; five is better if attention holds.

**Lesson 05 (stories + error analysis):** Let the learner struggle with the
story skeleton for a minute before prompting — the retrieval effort is the
learning. For Sam's test, make them name *both* changed things before
allowing the fix; the most common miss is spotting only one.

## Misconception notes (what to listen for)

| You hear… | It means… | Respond with… |
|---|---|---|
| "Heavier = more energy" | Speed and mass are fused | "We used the *same* ball — so what was different? Only the speed." |
| "The energy got used up" | Everyday language for depletion | "Point to where it went: the dent? the sound? the warmth? It moved — it didn't vanish." |
| "The wires make the light" | Confusing the path with the source | "What happens when we remove one wire? So where did the light's energy *start*?" |
| "The rice jumped because of magic/wind" | Missing the transfer step | "Your hand never touched the rice. What traveled between your hand and the rice?" |
| "Forces/pushes" talk in Lesson 04 | Drifting off the energy emphasis | "What *lost* energy? What *gained* it? What did you hear?" |

## Pacing and differentiation

- The 16-session table in the [unit README](README.md) is the plan; if a
  session runs long, move the *review game*, never the investigation.
- **If the learner races ahead:** Lesson 01/03/04 extensions (steeper ramp
  as a second fair test; two cells; two-marble question) — each is a *new*
  one-variable test, never a modification of the current one.
- **If the learner struggles:** cut Practice sets to the odd-numbered items
  (they cover every objective); do the even items orally the next day.
  Dictation and drawing are always acceptable substitutes for writing.
- **Reteach triggers:** quiz below 7/12 → re-run the missed lesson's station
  as a demo with the learner narrating (not a worksheet). Project plan with
  two variables → redo Lesson 05's Sam's-test fix together before any data
  collection. "Used up" language persisting past Lesson 05 → replay one
  collision and *listen* for the clack together, then rebuild that story.

## The energy fair (Session 15)

Three stations, learner-run: (1) clay dents (fast vs. slow, visitor predicts
the deeper dent); (2) drum + rice (visitor predicts what louder tapping
does); (3) marble collision (visitor predicts, then watches). Rule: every
visitor **predicts in writing before** the demo, and the learner closes each
station with the three-part energy story. Keep it to 25 minutes; the last 10
are the learner's self-check ("which station's story am I surest of?").

## What "done" looks like

Unit assessment ≥ 22/30 with all four objectives showing at least
"approaching"; project report complete in all seven sections; the learner
can tell one full energy story unprompted. Anything less → targeted re-teach
per the triggers above, not a full-unit repeat.
