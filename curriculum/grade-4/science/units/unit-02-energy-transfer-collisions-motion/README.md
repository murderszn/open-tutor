# Unit 02 — Energy Transfer, Collisions, and Motion

**Grade:** 4 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#23](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/23)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 7–10 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 35 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

This unit answers three questions a fourth-grader can genuinely investigate:
*Does a faster-moving object have more energy? How does energy move from place
to place? What happens to energy when objects collide?* Everything stays
**qualitative** — no speed numbers, no energy calculations — per the 4-PS3
assessment boundaries (see Standards notes below). Evidence comes from what the
learner can see, hear, and feel: dents in clay, rice jumping on a drum, a lamp
warming paper, warm hands on a cool table, a battery lighting a bulb, marbles
knocking into each other.

The unit opens with the **clay-dent fair test** (Lesson 01): the same ball
rolled from the top of a ramp and from halfway down makes dents of different
depths in modeling clay — the faster ball makes the deeper dent. That one
observation, repeated with three trials and a fair setup, carries the unit's
core idea: *a faster-moving object has more energy*. The AI-generated
illustration in `assets/` gives learners a second, independent case to reason
about (deep dent vs. shallow dent, no measurements needed).

Weeks 8 and 9 trace **energy moving from place to place** — by sound (drum →
air → rice), light (lamp → paper warms), heat (warm hands → cool table), and
electric currents (battery → wire → bulb glows and warms) — and then ask
learners to **predict what happens to energy in collisions** (marbles):
the hitting marble slows down, the target speeds up, and some energy spreads
to the air as sound (the *clack*) and a tiny bit of heat. Week 10 is the
**energy fair**: predict-observe-explain stations the learner runs, then the
formative unit assessment.

The **fair-test routine** from Unit 01 is reused throughout (one changed
variable, controls, three trials, labeled tables) and the unit closes with the
**Collision Challenge** investigation report written in the Unit 01 report
format — the same format every later unit will use.

## Prerequisites (from grade-3 science and Unit 01; re-taught here as needed)

- Asking testable questions; planning fair investigations (change one thing,
  keep the rest the same, repeat three trials); recording in labeled tables.
- Measuring length to the nearest centimeter (used for release heights and
  roll distances — comparisons only, no speed calculations).
- Writing investigation reports in the Unit 01 format (question, prediction,
  materials, steps, data table, claim-evidence-reasoning, what I would change).
- Science safety: the adult supervises every investigation; rolling objects
  stay clear of stairs; the adult handles battery cells (cells only — no
  mains-powered equipment); materials stay away from the mouth.

Lesson 01's first ten minutes re-run a two-minute fair-test refresher (name
the variable, the controls, the trials) rather than assuming it is secure.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Use evidence from a fair collision test to explain that a **faster-moving
   object has more energy** (deeper dent, farther roll), with no speed
   numbers (4-PS3-1).
2. Make **observations** showing that **energy can be transferred from place
   to place by sound, light, heat, and electric currents** — naming what
   moved, what changed, and the evidence (4-PS3-2).
3. Ask **questions and predict outcomes** about what happens to **energy when
   objects collide**: the hitting object slows, the struck object moves, and
   some energy spreads to the air as sound and heat (4-PS3-3; emphasis on the
   energy change, not on forces).
4. Plan and carry out a **fair investigation** of a collision question, with
   one changed variable, controls, at least three trials, a labeled data
   table, and a report in the Unit 01 format (3-5-ETS1-3; science practices
   thread).

## Vocabulary

**energy** — what lets things move, make sound, give light, or warm things up
(learner wording; the adult's "ability to do work" definition is background
only). **transfer** — energy moving from one place to another. **speed** —
how fast something moves (compared, never numbered, in this unit). **collision**
— when two objects hit each other. **dent** — a pushed-in mark showing where
energy changed an object's shape. **evidence** — observations that support a
claim. **fair test** — an investigation that changes one thing at a time.
**variable** — the one thing changed on purpose. **control** — everything kept
the same. **trial** — one repeat of a test. **predict** — say what you think
will happen before you test, with a reason. **observe** — watch and notice
carefully. **sound** — energy you hear. **heat** — energy you feel as
warmth. **light** — energy you see. **electric current** — energy moving
through a wire from a battery. **circuit** — a complete loop the current
travels through.

## Standards and source notes

Framework: **Next Generation Science Standards**, grade-4 performance
expectations, checked 2026-10-04 against the California Department of
Education's official reproduction of the NGSS grade-4 release ("NGSS Grade
Four Arranged by DCI," revised March 2015, read in full) and
cross-checked with nextgenscience.org, as recorded in the
[scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) §4. No state adoption,
accreditation, or alignment certification is claimed.

| Code | What it asks (this unit) | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|
| 4-PS3-1 | Use evidence to construct an explanation relating the **speed of an object to the energy of that object**. Clarification: evidence includes change of shape on impact. Assessment boundary: **no quantitative measures of speed, no precise or quantitative definition of energy.** | Lessons 01, 05; Practice A; quiz; assessment |
| 4-PS3-2 | Make **observations** to provide evidence that energy can be **transferred from place to place by sound, light, heat, and electric currents**. Assessment boundary: **no quantitative measurements of energy.** | Lessons 02, 03, 05; Practice B; quiz; assessment |
| 4-PS3-3 | **Ask questions and predict outcomes** about the changes in energy that occur **when objects collide**. Clarification: emphasis is on the **change in energy** due to the change in speed, **not on the forces**. Assessment boundary: no quantitative measurements. | Lesson 04; Practice C; project; quiz; assessment |

4-PS3-4 (design an energy-converting device) is intentionally **not** taught
here — per the scope-and-sequence it lands in Unit 08's capstone design work.
The project in this unit is an *investigation* (3-5-ETS1-3 practices), not a
device-design task, and the lessons say so explicitly so learners do not
confuse "testing what happens" with "building a device."

Internal reuse: the fair-test routine, labeled-table format, and
investigation-report format come from
[Unit 01](../unit-01-evidence-measurement-models/README.md); the
`curriculum/grade-4/stem/assignments/simple-machines-at-home.md` and the legacy
`week-03` (Energy & Heat) / `week-04` (Motion & Forces) quizzes were
reviewed per the audit and their qualitative energy/collision ideas are
rebuilt here in 4-PS3 language rather than copied. `resources/physics_fundamentals.md`
(energy & heat section) and `resources/thermodynamics_laws.md` are
**teacher-side background only** — no laws or formulas reach the learner.

## 16-session pacing (Weeks 7–10, ~35 minutes each)

Sessions rotate investigation → practice → reading-and-talk → review, per the
scope-and-sequence.

| # | Week | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7 | **Lesson 01** — Faster means more energy: the clay-dent fair test (adult-supervised ramp setup) | Learner runs 3 fair trials, records dent depth as deeper/shallower/same, and states the claim "the faster ball has more energy" with the dent as evidence |
| 2 | 7 | Practice Set A + the generated-image reasoning activity ([`collision-energy-ramp.webp`](assets/collision-energy-ramp.webp)) | 6 of 8 items correct; image item answered with evidence ("deeper dent") not guesses |
| 3 | 7 | Reading-and-talk: energy in everyday life — fast/slow pairs (bicycle rolling, wind, falling water); learner brings one example | Learner names one everyday fast/slow pair and predicts which has more energy, with a reason |
| 4 | 7 | Review game: energy sort (more-energy / less-energy / need-evidence cards) + Lesson 01 exit check | Exit check 2/2; can explain why "heavier" alone is not the answer (same ball both times) |
| 5 | 8 | **Lesson 02** — Energy moves: sound (drum + rice) and light (lamp + paper) stations | Learner names what moved and what changed at both stations, using "energy moved from ___ to ___" language |
| 6 | 8 | Practice Set B | 6 of 8 items correct |
| 7 | 8 | **Lesson 03** — Energy moves: heat (warm hands, cool table) and electric currents (adult-handled battery-and-bulb circuit) | Learner traces two energy paths aloud without prompts; follows the battery-safety rules exactly |
| 8 | 8 | Review: "trace the energy" map drawing (4 labeled arrows) + formative mini-check | Energy map has all 4 transfer types labeled with evidence; mini-check 3/4 |
| 9 | 9 | **Lesson 04** — Collisions: ask and predict — marble predict-observe-explain trials | Learner makes a written prediction *with a reason* before each collision, then records what actually happened |
| 10 | 9 | Practice Set C + **week-09 quiz** | Quiz ≥ 9/12; practice 6/8 |
| 11 | 9 | **Lesson 05** — Putting it together: energy story chains; the "used up" misconception; Sam's unfair test (error analysis) | Learner builds one 3-link energy story and fixes Sam's test by naming the variable and one control |
| 12 | 9 | Project launch: Collision Challenge — choose a question, plan the fair test (adult reviews the plan) | Written plan names one variable, controls, 3 trials, and the data table before any testing |
| 13 | 10 | Project data collection (adult-supervised) | 3+ trials recorded in a labeled table; no changed mid-test variables |
| 14 | 10 | Project report writing (Unit 01 format) + Practice Set D (spiral review) | Report has all 7 sections; practice 6/8 |
| 15 | 10 | **Energy fair**: learner runs 3 predict-observe-explain stations for the adult (clay dents, drum + rice, marble collisions) | Each station has a written prediction, an observation, and an energy explanation |
| 16 | 10 | **Unit assessment** + reflection: "one energy idea I can now prove with evidence" | Assessment scored against the answer key; reflection names one objective in the learner's own words |

Safety rules for the unit (adult reads first, every session): ramps built from
books and boards at table height only; the adult sets up and checks every
ramp; rolling objects stay clear of stairs; battery **cells only**, handled by
the adult — no mains-powered equipment, no wall outlets; lamp observations use
a cool-touch LED desk lamp held by the adult; materials stay away from the
mouth; hands washed after clay work.
