# Project — Sense-to-Response Model Poster

**Time:** Session 12 (planning) and Session 13 (building) plus
Session 15 (presenting and revising). **Adult supervises all
observation; scent jars are adult-prepared; nothing is tasted in
class.**

## The task

Make a poster that shows a complete sense-to-response model for one
animal and one signal. The poster shows the whole path — sense →
brain → response — with labels and arrows, the evidence behind each
link, and a text-only alternative so the model can be read without
the picture. Present the poster to the adult (spoken, with the poster
as your visual display), and revise one link if the evidence says so.

## Deliverables (all four are required)

1. **The poster** (one large sheet or two joined sheets): a drawing of
   your animal and its signal, with the three-step path labeled
   (sense + kind of information → brain step → response) and arrows
   in the right order, plus one sentence about how the response helps
   the animal survive.
2. **The observation log** (in the science journal): your own results
   from the three fair observations — the 10-trial sound-direction
   test (trial | side of the sound | your point | match?), the scent
   match (how many of 6, and which were hardest), and the texture
   sort (8 objects, smooth or rough). Every result is recorded
   honestly, including misses.
3. **The text-only alternative** (written on the back of the poster or
   on a second sheet): the whole model in words — animal, signal,
   sense, kind of information, brain step, response, survival
   sentence — so a person who cannot see the poster can still follow
   the model.
4. **The spoken presentation** (Session 15, about 3 minutes): walk
   the adult through the poster, pointing to each link, and name the
   evidence for each step.

## Animal and signal choices (adult approves)

- **Animals:** a deer, rabbit, squirrel, dog, cat, bird, fish, or
  frog — from adult-chosen photos or video, or a pet observed gently
  at home. No wild animals are handled, chased, or fed for this
  project.
- **Signals:** a sound (twig snap, whistle, can opener, bird call), a
  scent (food cooking, a flower), a sight (a shadow overhead, a red
  light, food dropping into water), or a touch (cold rain, a hot
  surface — discussed, never tested on the body).

## Materials

Science journal; one large sheet of paper (or two joined sheets);
pencils and colored pencils; word cards; the
[sense → brain → response diagram](assets/sense-brain-response-diagram.svg)
as the layout model; the observation tables from Lessons 02 and 04.
**Adult reads the safety rules aloud:** scent jars are mild and
adult-prepared; nothing is tasted; sound levels stay comfortable;
animal observations are through media or gentle pet watching only.

## Steps

1. **Plan (Session 12).** Choose your animal and signal with the
   adult. Fill in the plan table: animal | signal | sense + kind of
   information | brain step | response | survival sentence | evidence
   I will use. The adult checks that every link is present before you
   build.
2. **Observe (Sessions 2, 5, 13).** Complete the three fair
   observations and record them in the log: the sound-direction test,
   the scent match, and the texture sort. These are your evidence
   that senses pick up information and that fair tests work — the log
   supports the model even when the model's animal is different from
   you, because the path is the same.
3. **Build (Session 13).** Draw the poster: the animal, the signal,
   and the three labeled boxes with arrows. Write the survival
   sentence on the poster. Write the text-only alternative on the
   back.
4. **Present (Session 15).** Present the poster to the adult. Point
   to each link and name the evidence for it. If the adult's questions
   show a link is weak — for example, the brain step is missing, or
   the response does not match the signal — revise that link on the
   poster and note the change in the journal.

## Rubric (12 points total; adult scores)

| Criterion | 3 — Strong | 2 — Developing | 1 — Starting | 0 — Missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Model completeness (3 pts)** | All four parts present: animal + signal, sense + kind of information, brain step, response — in the right order with arrows | One part thin or unclear, but the path is still in order | Two parts missing or the order is wrong | No model |
| **Evidence (3 pts)** | Observation log complete (all three observations, honest results including misses) and named in the presentation | Log mostly complete; one observation thin or one result missing | Log incomplete or results invented | No log |
| **Accuracy (3 pts)** | Sense matches its kind of information; brain step is about working out meaning; response fits the signal | One mismatch (e.g., sense and kind do not match) | Two or more mismatches | Model does not follow sense → brain → response |
| **Communication (3 pts)** | Labels readable; text-only alternative complete; survival sentence clear; presentation walks through every link | Labels readable but the alternative or the survival sentence is thin | Poster hard to read or the alternative is missing | Poster not presented |

**Scoring guide:** 10–12 = strong, ready for the unit assessment;
7–9 = revise the weakest criterion before Session 16; ≤6 = rebuild
the missing links with the adult using Lesson 05 before Session 16.
