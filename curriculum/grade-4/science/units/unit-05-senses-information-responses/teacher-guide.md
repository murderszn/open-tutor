# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Senses, Information Processing, and Responses

**Adult only.** Student materials never link here except through the
unit README's labeled link. All solutions live in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## Pacing at a glance

| Week | Sessions | Focus | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 19 | 1–4 | Five senses and kinds of information; sound-direction test; sense-kind card sort | Brain treated as a sixth sense; taste/smell mix-up |
| 20 | 5–8 | Fair sense observations (scent, texture, sound-direction data); sense → brain → response model; path-order relay | "More trials made my ears better" error; the sense decides |
| 21 | 9–12 | Responses differ (deer picture); response tally; complete models; project plan | "The right response is always to run" error; brain step skipped in models |
| 22 | 13–16 | Project build, quiz, poster presentation + revision, assessment | Copied tally numbers passed off as observations; poster without a text-only alternative |

## Readiness evidence and reteach triggers

- **After Session 1:** if the learner cannot name the five senses with
  one kind of information each, return to Lesson 01's table and the
  sense walk before Session 2. Do not proceed to fair observations
  until the five sense-kind pairs are stable — every later lesson
  reuses them.
- **After Session 4:** if "ears can tell colors" or "eyes can tell
  smells" persists, re-run Lesson 01's thunderstorm example with the
  adult's question: "Which sense told you that — the one that sees
  light, or the one that hears sound?"
- **After Session 6:** if the learner reads the fictional 8-of-10 data
  as "proof ears are perfect," re-run Lesson 02's Worked example 2
  and ask only: "Which two trials were wrong, and what do two misses
  mean?"
- **After Session 8:** if the sense still "decides" (ears decide to
  run), re-run Lesson 03's ball-catch trace with the note-passing
  image: the sense writes the note, the brain reads it and decides.
- **After Session 10:** if freeze/flee/investigate blur together,
  act each one out with the word cards before Session 11; do not let
  the learner start the project plan until three responses are
  distinct.
- **After the week-22 quiz:** score ≤6 on any objective's items →
  reteach that lesson with its practice set before Session 16; do not
  re-administer the same quiz items as the reteach.

## Misconceptions to watch (by lesson)

- **Lesson 01:** the brain is a sixth sense (correct: the brain is the
  processor, not a sense); taste and smell are one sense (correct
  with the blocked-nose example); "I heard the soup was salty"
  (senses do not do each other's jobs).
- **Lesson 02:** more trials sharpen a sense (correct: more trials
  strengthen the conclusion, not the sense); one correct guess proves
  it (correct: one try is luck); peeking during the scent test
  (correct as a fairness question, not a rule break).
- **Lesson 03:** the sense decides (correct with the note-passing
  image); the response happens before the brain (correct: the brain
  is always the middle step); "how does the brain remember?" (answer
  honestly that grade 4 learns the brain uses what the animal already
  knows, not how it stores it — the mechanism is outside the
  boundary).
- **Lesson 04:** the right response is always to run (correct with the
  deer: freezing can be safer); all animals respond the same way
  (correct with the comparison table); confusing the response with
  the sense ("the response was hearing" — ask what the *body* did
  after the brain decided).
- **Lesson 05:** a model is a drawing (correct: a model can be a
  drawing, a table, or acted out); once written, a model is finished
  (correct: praise revision when evidence says so); the brain step is
  skipped (send the learner back to Lesson 03's ball-catch trace).

## Safety checklist (adult reads aloud before each investigation)

- Scent jars are mild and adult-prepared; check for allergies first;
  nothing is tasted in class; any home tasting is adult-chosen,
  familiar, and allergy-checked — never taste anything unknown.
- Sound levels stay comfortable (soft claps, no loud bangs); no
  looking at the sun or into bright lights.
- Animal observations are through adult-chosen media or gentle
  classroom-pet watching only; no wild animals are handled, chased,
  or fed.
- Hands are washed after texture work; texture objects are checked
  for sharp edges before handling.

## Quiz and assessment scoring

- **Week-22 quiz (10 items):** 9–10 = strong, ready for the unit
  assessment; 7–8 = review the missed objectives with the matching
  practice set, then proceed; ≤6 = reteach the missed lessons before
  Session 16.
- **Unit assessment (12 tasks, 44 points):** 40–44 = strong; 33–39 =
  review the missed objectives with the matching practice set;
  ≤32 = reteach the missed lessons before moving to Unit 06, using
  the project poster as the reteach vehicle (rebuild the weakest
  link with the adult). Accept reasonable wording that states the same
  sense, kind of information, step, or reasoning; judge meaning, not
  exact phrasing. Drawn models are judged on labels, order, and jobs,
  not artistic skill.
- **Project (12 points):** see the rubric in
  [project.md](project.md). A poster without a text-only alternative
  cannot score above 2 on Communication; a log with invented results
  cannot score above 1 on Evidence — honesty about misses is part of
  the science.

## Differentiation notes

- For learners who struggle with writing: all answers may be oral,
  drawn, or acted out; the adult records oral answers and may scribe
  the poster.
- For learners who finish early: the Lesson 04 extension (trace a
  fox, frog, or goose) and the Lesson 05 extension (a second model
  with a different response for the same signal) are ready to use.
- The sound-direction test and scent match can be repeated across
  weeks as retrieval practice; do not re-use the same ten trials as
  new evidence.

## Boundary reminders

- Do not teach how the brain stores and recalls information, or how
  sense receptors work inside — both are outside the grade-4
  assessment boundary. If a learner asks, answer honestly that
  scientists are still learning the details, and return to the
  system: sense → brain → response.
- The deer picture is an AI illustration of a moment, used to practice
  tracing a path — not evidence of a real deer. The fictional datasets
  (sound-direction 8 of 10, scent 5 of 6, response tally 8) are
  labeled practice data; the learner's own test results are recorded
  separately in the project log.
