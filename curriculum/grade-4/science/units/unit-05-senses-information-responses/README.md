# Unit 05 — Senses, Information Processing, and Responses

**Grade:** 4 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#23](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/23)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 19–22 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 35 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

Animals are always taking in information. Eyes pick up light, ears pick
up sound, a nose picks up scents, a tongue picks up flavors, and skin
picks up touch — and each sense is good at its own kind of information
and poor at the others. But a sense by itself decides nothing. The
information travels to the brain, the brain works out what it means and
what to do, and then the body responds: a deer freezes at a snapping
twig, a rabbit flees from the same sound, a squirrel stops to
investigate. This unit teaches learners to trace that whole path —
sense → brain → response — to test it with fair observations of their
own senses, and to build a complete model of one animal responding to
one signal.

The unit stays inside the grade-4 assessment boundary throughout: the
emphasis is on the **system** of information transfer, never on the
mechanism. How the brain stores and recalls information, and how sense
receptors work inside, are not taught here. The brain is taught as the
body's control center — the part that works out what information means
— which Unit 04 introduced and Lesson 03 re-teaches so this unit
stands on its own.

## Prerequisites (from Units 01–04; re-taught here as needed)

- The fair-observation routine from Unit 01: look closely, record what
  you actually see in a labeled drawing or table, and separate what you
  observed from what you guessed. (Units 01–03 are on main; Unit 04 is
  delivered as draft PR #298 and is not yet merged. This unit depends
  on no single Unit 04 file, only on routines those units established.)
- The fair-test routine from Unit 01: change one thing at a time, keep
  the rest the same, repeat trials, and record results in a table.
- The investigation-report format from Unit 01, reused for the project
  observation log.
- From Unit 03: light reflecting off objects and entering the eye lets
  us see (4-PS4-2) — the sight link in Lesson 01; patterns can carry
  information (4-PS4-3) — the message idea behind Lesson 03.
- From Unit 04: the brain controls the body; structures work together
  as a system. Lesson 03 re-teaches the brain-as-control-center idea
  before using it.
- Science safety practices: the adult supervises every investigation;
  scent jars are mild and adult-prepared; nothing is tasted during an
  observation in class; sound levels are kept comfortable; animal
  observations are through adult-chosen media or gentle classroom-pet
  watching only; hands are washed after handling texture objects; no
  wild animals are handled, chased, or fed.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Name the five senses and the kind of information each one picks
   up:** eyes see light — what things look like; ears hear sound;
   nose smells scents in the air; tongue tastes flavors in food; skin
   feels touch — shape, texture, and temperature (4-LS1-2).
2. **Describe, from fair observations, that different senses are
   specialized:** each sense is good at its own kind of information
   and poor at others — ears cannot tell colors, eyes cannot tell a
   smell — and explain what makes a sense observation fair (one sense
   at a time, repeated trials) (4-LS1-2; LS1.D).
3. **Use a model to trace the information path sense → brain →
   response,** and explain that the brain is the part that works out
   what the information means and what to do (4-LS1-2; SEP-2).
4. **Compare how different animals respond to the same kind of
   information in different ways** — freeze, flee, investigate, hide,
   call out — and explain that different responses can each help the
   animal survive (4-LS1-2).
5. **Build and explain a complete sense-to-response model** for one
   animal and one signal, using evidence from observations and
   adult-chosen media, and revise the model when new evidence appears
   (4-LS1-2; SEP-2, SEP-6).

## Essential question

*How does an animal take in information, work out what it means, and decide what to do?*

## Vocabulary

**Sense** — one of the five ways an animal picks up information: sight,
hearing, smell, taste, touch. · **Information** — what a sense picks
up about the world, such as a sound, a scent, or a shape. ·
**Signal** — the message that travels from a sense to the brain. ·
**Receptor** — a part of a sense organ that is built to pick up one
particular kind of information (how receptors work inside is not
taught here). · **Specialized** — built to do one kind of job well. ·
**Brain** — the part of the body that works out what information means
and what to do. · **Process** — to work out what information means. ·
**Response** — what an animal does because of information it received.
· **Freeze** — to stop moving and stay very still. · **Flee** — to
run away quickly. · **Investigate** — to move closer and check
something out carefully. · **Scent** — a smell in the air. ·
**Texture** — how something feels to the touch: smooth, rough, soft,
hard. · **Observe** — to watch, listen, smell, or feel carefully and
notice what happens. · **Evidence** — something you observed that
supports a claim. · **Model** — a drawing, table, or acted-out
version that shows how something works, step by step. · **System** —
parts working together for one larger job. · **Fair test** — a test
that changes one thing at a time while keeping the rest the same.

## Standards and source notes

- Reference framework: **Next Generation Science Standards**, grade-4
  performance expectations (from the track audit's crosswalk). No state
  adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification is claimed.
- **4-LS1-2** — *Use a model to describe that animals receive
  different types of information through their senses, process the
  information in their brain, and respond to the information in
  different ways.* Clarification (NGSS): emphasis is on systems of
  information transfer. Assessment boundary, kept in this unit:
  assessment does not include the mechanisms by which the brain stores
  and recalls information or the mechanisms of how sensory receptors
  function. Disciplinary core idea **LS1.D**: different sense receptors
  are specialized for particular kinds of information, which may be
  then processed by the animal's brain; animals are able to use their
  perceptions and memories to guide their actions. (Wording,
  clarification, boundary, and core idea re-verified 2026-11-06 against
  the NSTA display of the standard; see
  [resource-pack.md](resource-pack.md).)
- Science and engineering practices: **SEP-2** (developing and using
  models — the sense → brain → response diagram, the poster project),
  **SEP-6** (constructing explanations — revising a model with
  evidence in Lesson 05 and the project). Crosscutting concept:
  **systems and system models** (senses, brain, and body work together
  as one information-transfer system; break one link and the response
  does not happen). ELA connection from the standard's own listing:
  **SL.4.5** — the learner adds a visual display (the poster) to a
  spoken explanation when presenting the project.
- 4-PS4-2 (light and seeing, Unit 03) and 4-PS4-3 (patterns carrying
  information, Unit 03) are reused, not re-taught. 4-LS1-1 (structures
  and functions, Unit 04) is background: the brain as control center
  is re-taught in Lesson 03.
- All practice scenarios that look like data are labeled fictional
  practice data, never presented as the learner's own observations.
  The learner's own sound-direction test, scent match, and texture
  sort are real observations recorded in the project log.

## Materials (for the whole unit)

Six small jars with lids, adult-prepared with mild scents (lemon peel,
cinnamon, mint leaves, vanilla extract on cotton, ground coffee,
orange peel — check for allergies first; lids stay on except for a
quick sniff); eight texture objects (glass marble, silk or satin
ribbon, polished stone, plastic spoon, sandpaper square, tree bark,
brick piece, wool cloth); a blindfold or sleep mask for the
sound-direction test; a soft hand-clap or two wooden spoons for
making the test sound (no loud noises); science journal; pencils and
colored pencils; word cards for the vocabulary; the printed or screen
[deer picture](assets/deer-freezes-at-twig-snap.png) and the
[sense → brain → response diagram](assets/sense-brain-response-diagram.svg);
adult-chosen short videos of meadow or forest animals (see
[resource-pack.md](resource-pack.md)); index cards for the
sense/brain/response path-order relay. **Adult reads the safety rules
aloud before each investigation:** scent jars are mild and
adult-prepared — nothing is tasted in class; taste is discussed from
familiar foods only, and any home tasting is adult-chosen, familiar,
and allergy-checked; sound levels stay comfortable; animal
observations are through media or gentle classroom-pet watching only;
hands are washed after texture work. All investigations are
adult-supervised.

## Differentiation and supports

- **Reading/language access:** every lesson goal is read aloud; key
  words are shown on word cards with a picture (eye, ear, nose,
  tongue, skin, brain); learners may answer orally, by pointing, or
  by drawing, and the adult records what they say.
- **Alternate response modes:** the sense → brain → response path may
  be acted out (one learner is the sense, one the brain, one the
  body) instead of drawn; the project poster may be spoken and
  adult-scribed; the assessment allows drawn models in place of
  sentences.
- **Extensions:** compare the same sense in three animals (for
  example, hearing in a dog, a bat, and an owl — different strengths
  for different lives); research, with the adult, how a bat finds
  insects in the dark, then explain it in the unit's
  sense → brain → response language; add a second animal to the
  project poster responding to the same signal.
- **Reteach triggers:** see [teacher-guide.md](teacher-guide.md). A
  learner who still says "the ears decide to run" after Session 8
  re-runs Lesson 03's ball-catch trace with the adult's question:
  "Who works out what the ball means — your eyes, or your brain?"
