# Resource Pack — Unit 05: Senses, Information Processing, and Responses

**Goal (one sentence):** Give the guiding adult short, grade-4-appropriate
videos, references, and search routes that reinforce the five senses,
fair sense observations, the sense → brain → response path, and how
different animals respond to the same information.
**Age fit:** grade 4 (ages 9–10); the adult previews every video and
co-views with the learner.

## Web references

1. **NGSS 4-LS1-2 text (NSTA display of the standard)** —
   https://my.nsta.org/ngss/DisplayStandard.aspx?view=pe&id=71
   *Why good:* the authoritative performance-expectation wording for
   4-LS1-2, its clarification (emphasis on systems of information
   transfer), its assessment boundary (no brain-storage/recall or
   receptor mechanisms), the LS1.D core idea, and the SEP-2/CCS
   connections — the wording, clarification, and boundary used in this
   unit's standards notes. Adult background and verification source.
   *Wording verified 2026-11-06 by direct open; free, no account.*
2. **Generation Genius — "Senses, Brain & Nervous System" lesson page
   (Grades 3–5)** —
   https://www.generationgenius.com/blog/senses-brain-nervous-system-lesson-for-kids/
   *Why good:* states the same path this unit teaches in plain
   language (senses gather information, receptors send signals to the
   brain, the brain analyzes it and tells the body how to respond),
   with animal examples (frogs hearing, chameleons seeing, birds
   sensing seasons) matching Lessons 03–04. *Checked 2026-11-06 via
   search-result description; adult previews and checks access before
   use.*
3. **Missouri Department of Conservation — "Super Senses" (Xplor
   magazine)** —
   https://mdc.mo.gov/magazines/xplor/2022-05/super-senses
   *Why good:* a state-agency children's magazine article giving
   concrete animal-senses examples this unit's Lesson 04 and project
   extensions draw on (butterflies tasting with their feet,
   rattlesnakes sensing heat, bats using echolocation, catfish tasting
   with their skin, moths following scent for miles). Written for
   young readers. *Checked 2026-11-06 via search-result description;
   free, no account.*
4. **K12 LibreTexts — "13.8: Senses"** —
   https://k12.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Science_and_Technology/Biology/13%3A_Human_Biology/13.08%3A_Senses
   *Why good:* a clear adult-background summary of the five senses,
   sensory receptors, and nerves carrying signals to the brain, which
   interprets them to form a response — the system this unit teaches.
   Reading level is above grade 4, so it is adult background only and
   is never assigned to the learner. *Checked 2026-11-06 via
   search-result description; free, no account.*
5. **Siemens Stiftung Media Portal — "My five senses"** —
   https://medienportal.siemens-stiftung.org/en/my-five-senses-101943
   *Why good:* an open educational resource page stating each sense
   organ and its job in simple language (eyes see, nose smells, ears
   hear, tongue tastes, skin touches) and framing senses as reception
   of stimuli and processing of information — matching Lesson 01
   exactly. *Checked 2026-11-06 via search-result description; free,
   no account.*
6. **Minnesota DNR — "Super Senses" (Minnesota Conservation
   Volunteer, Jan–Feb 2024)** —
   https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/sites/default/files/assets/mcv/2024/jan-feb/yn/YN-Senses-story.pdf?v=2024.03.25-20.26.53
   *Why good:* a state-agency magazine story on animals whose senses
   far exceed ours (a wolf's smell, a vulture's nose, a spider's
   touch, a mosquito finding people by smell and heat), for Lesson 04
   enrichment and the project extension. *Checked 2026-11-06 via
   search-result description; free, no account (PDF).*
