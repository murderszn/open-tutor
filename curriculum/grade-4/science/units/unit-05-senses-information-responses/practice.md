# Practice Sets — Unit 05: Senses, Information Processing, and Responses

**Adult note:** Sets A–D cover all five unit objectives. Oral, drawn,
or written answers are acceptable; the adult records oral answers.
Keys in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) (adult only). Scenarios marked
"fictional practice data" are made up for practice, not observations
the learner made.

## Set A — Five senses and kinds of information (Objective 1)

1. Match each sense to the kind of information it picks up: (a) eyes,
   (b) ears, (c) nose, (d) tongue, (e) skin — with: flavors in food;
   scents in the air; light — what things look like; touch — shape,
   texture, temperature; sound.
2. Which sense tells you each of these? (a) A bird is red. (b) A bell
   is ringing. (c) Soup is salty. (d) A stone is smooth. (e) Bread is
   baking and you can smell it from the next room.
3. True or false, with a reason: (a) "Your nose can tell you what
   color a flower is." (b) "Your skin can tell you whether water is
   hot or cold."
4. You hold a lemon. Name one thing your eyes tell you about it, and
   one thing your nose tells you about it.
5. Name the sense organ for each sense: sight, hearing, smell, taste,
   touch.
6. Explain in two sentences why taste and smell are two different
   senses, even though they work together when you eat.
7. Error analysis: a learner says, "I heard that the soup was salty."
   What is wrong with that sentence? Rewrite it correctly.

## Set B — Fair observations (Objective 2)

*The sound-direction data in items 1–2 is fictional practice data.*

1. A learner did the sound-direction test: eyes closed, the adult
   clapped once from the left or the right, and the learner pointed
   where the sound came from. Ten trials. The sound came from: left,
   right, right, left, right, left, left, right, left, right. The
   learner pointed: left, right, right, right, right, left, left,
   right, right, right. (a) How many trials were correct? (b) Which
   two trials were wrong?
2. (a) What fraction of the ten trials were correct? (b) Write one
   sentence about what the data show, and one sentence about what
   they do not show.
3. Fictional practice story: a learner matched 5 of 6 scent jars
   (lemon, cinnamon, mint, vanilla, coffee, orange). The one miss was
   cinnamon — the learner guessed coffee. (a) Why might cinnamon and
   coffee be easy to confuse by smell? (b) What should the learner do
   to check whether the mistake was a one-time guess?
4. Sort these eight texture objects into smooth and rough by touch:
   glass marble, silk ribbon, polished stone, plastic spoon,
   sandpaper square, tree bark, brick piece, wool cloth. List four in
   each group.
5. Design a fair test for this question: "Can I tell two textures
   apart with one finger instead of my whole hand?" State what you
   would change, and two things you would keep the same.
6. True or false, with a reason: "Doing a test once and getting it
   right proves your sense is perfect."
7. A friend tests smell with eyes open and gets all six scents right.
   Is that a fair test of smell? Explain why or why not in two
   sentences.

## Set C — The sense → brain → response path (Objective 3)

1. Put these steps in the right order: "hands catch the ball," "the
   brain works out where the ball will be," "eyes see the ball
   coming."
2. Trace this example through the three steps: you hear a phone ring
   and you answer it. Name the sense, the kind of information, what
   the brain works out, and the response.
3. Your skin feels cold rain on your arm, and you go inside. Fill in
   the middle step in your own words: what does the brain work out?
4. Error analysis: a model says, "The nose smells food, so the dog
   eats." What step is missing, and why does the model need it?
5. A dog hears a can opener and runs to the kitchen. (a) Which sense
   started it? (b) What kind of information did it pick up? (c) What
   did the brain work out? (d) What was the response?
6. Explain in two sentences why the brain belongs in the middle of
   every sense-to-response model.

## Set D — Different responses and complete models (Objectives 4–5)

*The tally in item 1 is fictional practice data.*

1. A class watched a 5-minute meadow video and tallied animal
   responses: deer freeze 3, rabbit flee 2, squirrel investigate 2,
   bird call out 1. (a) How many responses were counted in all? (b)
   Which response happened most often? (c) Write one sentence about
   what the tally shows.
2. The same twig snaps. The deer freezes, the rabbit flees, and the
   squirrel investigates. (a) Which response puts the most distance
   between the animal and the sound? (b) Which response keeps the
   animal hardest to see?
3. Explain in two sentences why freezing and fleeing can both be good
   answers to the same sound.
4. Fix this model: "Rabbit sees a fox → rabbit runs." Add the missing
   brain step, then write the whole corrected path.
5. Build a complete model for a cat hearing a bird. Show all four
   parts: animal + signal, sense + kind of information, brain step,
   response.
6. A learner's model says: "A fish smells food in the water → the
   fish swims toward the food." What is missing? Write the corrected
   model with the missing step added.
7. Explain in two sentences why a scientist would revise a model after
   seeing new evidence.
