# Unit Assessment — Senses, Information Processing, and Responses

**Adult administers in Session 16, after the week-22 quiz.** 12 tasks
covering all five unit objectives, including a labeled model, a
response comparison, a tally reading, and a fair-test design.
Total: **44 points** (points per task shown). Oral, drawn, or
manipulative responses are acceptable; the adult records them. Keys
and scoring guidance in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) (adult only).
**This is a practice check, not a standardized or validated test.**

## Part 1 — Senses and kinds of information (Objectives 1–2)

1. **(5 pts)** Match each sense to the kind of information it picks
   up: eyes, ears, nose, tongue, skin — with: flavors in food; scents
   in the air; light — what things look like; touch — shape, texture,
   temperature; sound. (1 pt per correct match.)
2. **(4 pts)** A learner closes her eyes and smells two jars. (a)
   Which sense is being tested? (b) Why are her eyes closed? (c)
   What kind of information is she picking up? (d) Name one thing
   smell cannot tell her about the jars.
3. **(4 pts)** True or false, with a reason: (a) "Ears can tell what
   color a flower is." (b) "Skin can tell whether water is hot or
   cold." (2 pts each: 1 for true/false, 1 for the reason.)

## Part 2 — The path (Objective 3)

4. **(3 pts)** Order the three steps for catching a ball, and label
   each step (sense, brain, response): "hands catch the ball," "the
   brain works out where the ball will be," "eyes see the ball
   coming." (1 pt per step in the right place.)
5. **(4 pts)** A dog hears a can opener and runs to the kitchen. (a)
   Which sense started it? (b) What kind of information did it pick
   up? (c) What did the brain work out? (d) What was the response?
   (1 pt each.)
6. **(3 pts)** A model says: "The nose smells food, so the dog eats."
   (a) What step is missing? (b) Why does the model need it? (c)
   Write the corrected path in one sentence. (1 pt each.)

## Part 3 — Responses differ, models (Objectives 4–5)

7. **(4 pts)** The same twig snaps. A deer freezes, a rabbit flees,
   and a squirrel investigates. (a) Name the response of each animal
   (3 pts, 1 each). (b) In one sentence, say why different responses
   can each help an animal survive (1 pt).
8. **(4 pts)** Draw a complete sense → brain → response model for an
   animal and a signal of your choice. Label the sense, the brain
   step, and the response, with arrows in the right order. (1 pt per
   element: sense labeled, brain labeled, response labeled, arrows in
   the right order.)
9. **(3 pts)** Read the fictional response tally (made-up practice
   data from a 5-minute meadow video): deer freeze 3, rabbit flee 2,
   squirrel investigate 2, bird call out 1. (a) How many responses
   were counted in all? (b) Which response happened most often? (c)
   Write one sentence about what the tally shows. (1 pt each.)
10. **(3 pts)** A model says: "Rabbit sees a fox → rabbit runs." (a)
    Add the missing middle step. (b) Explain in one sentence why the
    model needed it. (c) Name one piece of evidence that could support
    the model. (1 pt each.)
11. **(4 pts)** Explain, in two or three sentences, why the brain
    belongs in the middle of every sense-to-response model. Your
    answer must include one example. (2 pts for the explanation, 2 pts
    for a correct example traced through all three steps.)
12. **(3 pts)** Design a fair sound-direction test. (a) State the one
    thing you will change (or test) from trial to trial. (b) State two
    things you will keep the same. (c) State how many trials you will
    run, and why more than one trial matters. (1 pt each.)
