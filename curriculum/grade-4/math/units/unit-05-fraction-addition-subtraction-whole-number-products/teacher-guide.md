# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Fraction Addition, Subtraction, and Whole-Number Products

**For the guiding adult.** This unit is a validated draft, not
educator-reviewed. Read the unit [README](README.md) before starting.
Sessions are about 35 minutes, four per week. Every lesson's answers are
in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) — a separate file; do not let the
learner read it while working.

## Pacing notes

- **Week 19 (Lessons 01–02):** keep the strips in the learner's hands.
  The two most common errors — adding the denominators, and subtracting
  them — both come from treating the denominator as a count. The
  sentence "the denominator names the size" should be said aloud in
  every session this week.
- **Week 20 (Lessons 03–04, quiz):** decomposition is the hinge of the
  unit: a learner who can split 5/6 two ways already understands that
  only numerators change. Mixed-number regrouping is the same borrowing
  as Unit 01 whole-number subtraction — name that connection out loud.
  The Week 20 quiz is the unit's checkpoint; score it the same day.
- **Week 21 (Lesson 05, project, practice):** fraction-by-whole-number
  multiplication is new. Contrast it with Unit 04 equivalence every
  time the denominator-multiplication error appears: multiplying the
  numerator AND denominator keeps the amount the same; multiplying ONLY
  the numerator makes more of it.
- **Week 22 (presentations, review, assessment):** review stations are
  diagnostic, not new teaching. If a station falls below 80%, reteach
  that lesson's core model before the assessment — do not push ahead.

## Likely misconceptions and reteaching

| Misconception | What it looks like | Reteach |
|---|---|---|
| Adding/subtracting denominators | 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/16; 7/10 − 3/10 = 4/0 | Back to strips: join 3 eighths and 2 eighths and count the shaded length. Ask: "Did the pieces get smaller?" (Lessons 01–02) |
| Whole as a fraction | 1 − 5/8 = 4/8 (or 1/8) | Rewrite 1 as 8/8 with a fully shaded bar before subtracting (Lesson 02) |
| Parts that do not add back | 5/6 = 2/6 + 2/6 | Make the numerator-add-back check non-negotiable before the model (Lesson 03) |
| Subtracting fractions backwards | 3 1/4 − 1 3/4 = 2 2/4 | Regroup one whole first; compare fraction parts before subtracting (Lesson 04) |
| Unfinished mixed-number answers | 2 3/8 + 1 6/8 = 3 9/8 | Convert the improper fraction part: 9/8 = 1 1/8, so the total is 4 1/8 (Lesson 04) |
| Multiplying the denominator too | 3 × (2/5) = 6/15 | Count unit fractions: 3 groups of 2 fifths is 6 fifths, not 6 fifteenths (Lesson 05) |
| Different wholes joined | Adding fractions from different-size wholes | Re-state the same-whole rule from Unit 04; redraw both bars the same length (all lessons) |

## Scoring guidance

- **Week 20 quiz (10 items):** 8 or more correct with models or equation
  work shown = ready for Week 21. Below 8, reteach the missed item type
  (add/subtract, decompose, mixed number, or fraction × whole) before
  Lesson 05.
- **Culminating assessment (18 marks):** 14 marks or more = ready for
  Unit 06, provided no single objective scores below half marks:
  objective 1 = items 1, 2, 3, 5, 9; objective 2 = items 4, 13;
  objective 3 = items 6, 7, 11, 14; objective 4 = items 9, 10, 14;
  objective 5 = items 8, 10. (Items 9, 10, and 14 count toward two
  objectives; score their arithmetic under the operation objective and
  their modeling/equation under the word-problem objective.)
- **Project rubric:** see [project.md](project.md). A "1 — needs reteach"
  on any criterion means that criterion's lesson is revisited before the
  assessment, not after.
- **Practice set:** score the same day; items 17–20 (legacy frame and
  Unit 03 bridge) are the best evidence of transfer — a learner who
  solves them with models is ready for the project.

## What signals readiness for Unit 06

The learner adds and subtracts like-denominator fractions (including
mixed numbers with regrouping) with a model or an equation, decomposes a
fraction two ways with an add-back check, and multiplies a fraction by a
whole number while naming the whole numbers the product lies between —
all without adding or subtracting denominators. If any of those is
insecure, reteach its lesson before starting Unit 06 (decimals), which
assumes fraction addition with denominators 10 and 100 is fluent.

## Accessibility and safety

- Oral explanation is accepted for every "explain" item, with the adult
  scribing if needed. Folding paper strips is an accepted alternative to
  drawing bars throughout.
- The mixed-number regrouping card and the "denominator names the size"
  prompt card stay on the desk during Lessons 02 and 04 and during
  practice; both are removed only for the culminating assessment.
- Materials are paper, pencils, and scissors with adult supervision.
  No food is eaten as part of any activity; the sandwich-tray picture is
  a drawing, not a snack.
