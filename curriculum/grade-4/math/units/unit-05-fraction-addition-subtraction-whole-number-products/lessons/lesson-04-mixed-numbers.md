# Lesson 04 — Adding and Subtracting Mixed Numbers with Like Denominators

**Session:** 6 · **Time:** 35 minutes · **Goal:** I can add and subtract
mixed numbers with like denominators, regrouping a whole into
same-denominator parts when the fraction I must subtract is larger.

**Materials:** paper strips (four identical strips per learner), colored
pencils, paper and pencil.

## Readiness check (4 min)

Warm-up retrieval from Lesson 03: "Write 2 1/8 as 8/8 + 8/8 + 1/8. How
many eighths is that in all?" (17 eighths — 2 1/8 = 17/8.) Then ask: "If
I add 1 2/8 to 2 1/8, what would you add first — the wholes or the
eighths?" (Either order works; adding wholes to wholes and eighths to
eighths keeps the work organized. That is today's method.)

## Explicit teaching (10 min)

A **mixed number** is a whole number plus a fraction: 2 1/4 means
2 wholes and 1/4. To add or subtract mixed numbers with **like
denominators**, work in two parts — wholes with wholes, fraction parts
with fraction parts — and use Lessons 01–03 for the fraction part.

> Add the wholes, add the fractions. Subtract the wholes, subtract the
> fractions.

> 2 1/4 + 1 2/4 = 3 3/4 — wholes: 2 + 1 = 3; quarters: 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4.

Sometimes the fraction part needs **regrouping**. To subtract
4 1/8 − 2 5/8, the fraction part 1/8 is smaller than 5/8, so you cannot
take 5 eighths from 1 eighth. Borrow 1 whole from the 4, rewrite it as
8/8, and add it to the fraction part: 4 1/8 = 3 9/8. Now subtract:
3 9/8 − 2 5/8 = 1 4/8 = 1 1/2. This is exactly the regrouping used in
whole-number subtraction (Unit 01) — only the "ten" borrowed is now a
whole cut into same-denominator pieces.

Two habits for this lesson:

1. **Check whether the fraction part can subtract.** If the top fraction
   is smaller than the bottom fraction, regroup one whole first.
2. **Simplify the fraction part of the answer** (3/6 = 1/2, 4/8 = 1/2,
   12/8 = 1 4/8 = 1 1/2). A mixed-number answer is finished only when
   its fraction part is in simplest form and is less than 1.

## Worked example 1 — adding: 2 1/4 + 1 2/4

Problem: "A recipe uses 2 1/4 cups of flour in the morning and 1 2/4
cups in the afternoon. How much flour is used in all?"

1. Add the wholes: 2 + 1 = 3.
2. Add the fraction parts (same denominator, Lesson 01): 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4.
3. Combine: **3 3/4 cups**. ✓
4. Check with improper fractions: 2 1/4 = 9/4 and 1 2/4 = 6/4;
   9/4 + 6/4 = 15/4 = 3 3/4. ✓ Both methods agree.

## Worked example 2 — subtracting with regrouping: 4 1/8 − 2 5/8

Problem: "You have 4 1/8 meters of ribbon. You cut off 2 5/8 meters.
How much ribbon is left?"

1. Compare the fraction parts: 1/8 is smaller than 5/8, so regroup.
2. Borrow 1 whole from the 4: 4 1/8 = 3 + 1 + 1/8 = 3 + 8/8 + 1/8 =
   **3 9/8**.
3. Now subtract the wholes and the fractions: 3 − 2 = 1;
   9/8 − 5/8 = 4/8.
4. Combine: 1 4/8. Simplify the fraction part: 4/8 = 1/2. Answer:
   **1 1/2 meters**. ✓
5. Check by adding the answer back: 1 1/2 + 2 5/8 = 1 4/8 + 2 5/8 =
   3 9/8 = 4 1/8. ✓
6. Common error to avoid: subtracting the smaller fraction from the
   larger one and writing 2 4/8 (that is, 4 1/8 − 2 5/8 = 2 4/8) ignores
   which number is being subtracted from which. The wholes subtract
   4 − 2 = 2 only if the fraction part also works — here it does not,
   so regroup first.

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. Compute 1 3/5 + 2 1/5. Add wholes, then fifths.
   *(Hint: 1 + 2 = 3 wholes; 3/5 + 1/5 = 4/5. Answer: 3 4/5.)*
2. Compute 3 2/3 − 1 1/3. No regrouping is needed — why not?
   *(Hint: 2/3 is larger than 1/3, so the fraction part subtracts
   directly. Answer: 2 1/3.)*
3. Compute 2 7/8 + 1 5/8. The fraction parts add to more than a whole.
   *(Hint: 7/8 + 5/8 = 12/8 = 1 4/8 = 1 1/2. Wholes: 2 + 1 + 1 = 4.
   Answer: 4 1/2.)*
4. **Error analysis:** Kim says, "3 1/4 − 1 3/4 = 2 2/4, because 3 − 1
   is 2 and 3/4 − 1/4 is 2/4." Explain why Kim is wrong, and give the
   correct answer.
   *(Hint: Kim subtracted the fractions backwards — 1/4 is smaller than
   3/4, so regroup first: 3 1/4 = 2 5/4. Then 2 5/4 − 1 3/4 = 1 2/4 =
   1 1/2.)*

## Independent practice

5. Compute 2 3/10 + 1 6/10.
6. Compute 4 5/6 − 3 1/6. Simplify the fraction part.
7. Compute 1 5/12 + 2 7/12. What happens to the fraction part?
8. **Error analysis:** Alex says, "2 3/8 + 1 6/8 = 3 9/8." Explain what
   is unfinished about Alex's answer, and finish it.

## Applied task

Make a regrouping strip model from paper: draw two whole bars and a
5/5 bar to show 3 2/5, then show the regroup (one whole bar re-cut into
fifths) that lets you subtract 1 4/5. Label the regrouped amount 2 7/5,
the subtraction 2 7/5 − 1 4/5, and the answer 1 3/5. Show the adult: can
they point to the borrowed whole without reading your labels?

## Exit check

Compute 2 5/6 − 1 1/6, simplify, and check with addition.
(Answer: 1 4/6 = 1 2/3 — the fraction part subtracts directly, since
5/6 is larger than 1/6. Check: 1 2/3 + 1 1/6 = 1 4/6 + 1 1/6 = 2 5/6.
If you regrouped anyway, re-read the readiness check: regroup only when
the fraction part you subtract is larger.)

## Supports, extensions, teacher note

- **Reading/language support:** keep the regrouping card on the desk:
  "1 whole = 8/8, 6/6, 10/10 — pick the denominator you are using."
- **Alternate response mode:** fold paper strips instead of drawing bars;
  point to the borrowed whole and the remaining parts instead of writing
  the explanation, with the adult scribing the sentence.
- **Extension:** add and subtract mixed numbers with denominators 12:
  2 7/12 + 1 9/12 = 4 4/12 = 4 1/3; 3 2/12 − 1 8/12 = 1 6/12 = 1 1/2.
- **Teacher note — likely misconception:** learners who subtract the
  fraction parts backwards (Kim's error) are treating subtraction as
  commutative. The regrouping step is the fix, and it is the same
  borrowing they already know from whole numbers — name that connection
  explicitly. **Answer-key reference:**
  [../answer-key.md](../answer-key.md), Lesson 04.
