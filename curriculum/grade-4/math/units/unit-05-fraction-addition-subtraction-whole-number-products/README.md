# Unit 05 — Fraction Addition, Subtraction, and Whole-Number Products

**Grade:** 4 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 19–22
**Backlog:** [issue #22](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/22), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Joining parts of the same whole adds their counts; separating parts
subtracts them. In this unit the learner adds and subtracts fractions
with like denominators — including sums greater than 1, written as mixed
numbers — decomposes a fraction into a sum of same-denominator fractions
in more than one way, adds and subtracts mixed numbers with like
denominators, and multiplies a fraction by a whole number by counting
unit fractions. Every new operation starts from a visual fraction model
(strips, bars, number lines); the written equation always follows the
model, never replaces it.

## Essential question

When you join or separate equal-size pieces of the same whole, what
changes — the number of pieces, or the size of the pieces?

## Prerequisites

From the grade-3 track and Units 01–04:

- A fraction a/b means a parts of size 1/b; fractions on a number line
  (grade 3)
- Equivalent fractions and the same-whole rule (Unit 04): comparisons
  and operations are valid only when the fractions refer to the same
  whole; denominators 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 100
- Multiplication and division facts within 100; factor pairs (Units 02–03)
- Multistep word problems with a letter for the unknown (Unit 03)

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Add and subtract fractions with like denominators as joining and
   separating parts referring to the same whole, including sums greater
   than 1, written as mixed numbers. (4.NF.B.3.a)
2. Decompose a fraction into a sum of fractions with the same denominator
   in more than one way, recording each decomposition by an equation and
   justifying it with a visual fraction model. (4.NF.B.3.b)
3. Add and subtract mixed numbers with like denominators, including
   regrouping a whole into same-denominator parts when the fraction part
   to subtract is larger. (4.NF.B.3.c)
4. Solve word problems involving addition and subtraction of fractions
   referring to the same whole and having like denominators, using visual
   fraction models and equations to represent the problem. (4.NF.B.3.d)
5. Understand a fraction a/b as a multiple of 1/b and multiply a fraction
   by a whole number (n × (a/b) = (n × a)/b), including word problems and
   naming the two whole numbers a product lies between. (4.NF.B.4.a–c)

## Vocabulary (taught with models first, word second)

like denominators · join · separate · decompose · decomposition · mixed
number · improper fraction · unit fraction · regroup · multiple of a unit
fraction · same whole · visual fraction model · estimate

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**, grade 4,
as published at [thecorestandards.org/Math](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/).
The Grade 4 [Number & Operations — Fractions](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/4/NF/)
domain page was opened and read on **2026-11-06**; it renders 4.NF.B.3.a–d
and 4.NF.B.4.a–c verbatim, including the standard's own examples
(3/8 = 1/8 + 1/8 + 1/8 and 1/8 + 2/8; 2 1/8 = 1 + 1 + 1/8 = 8/8 + 8/8 + 1/8;
5/4 = 5 × (1/4); 3 × (2/5) = 6 × (1/5) = 6/5; the roast-beef problem:
5 people × 3/8 pound). Its footnote limits grade-4 fraction expectations
to denominators 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 100. Every fraction in this
unit uses a denominator from that list. Addition and subtraction with
unlike denominators in general is **not** a grade-4 requirement (same
footnote) and is not taught here. No state adoption or accreditation
is claimed. Descriptions below are paraphrases of the official
expectations, except where quoted.

| Unit objective | Standard | What the standard expects (paraphrase) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4.NF.B.3.a | Understand addition and subtraction of fractions as joining and separating parts referring to the same whole. |
| 2 | 4.NF.B.3.b | Decompose a fraction into a sum of fractions with the same denominator in more than one way, recording each decomposition by an equation; justify decompositions with a visual fraction model. |
| 3 | 4.NF.B.3.c | Add and subtract mixed numbers with like denominators, e.g., by replacing each mixed number with an equivalent fraction, and/or by using properties of operations and the relationship between addition and subtraction. |
| 4 | 4.NF.B.3.d | Solve word problems involving addition and subtraction of fractions referring to the same whole and having like denominators, e.g., by using visual fraction models and equations to represent the problem. |
| 5 | 4.NF.B.4.a–c | Understand a fraction a/b as a multiple of 1/b; understand a multiple of a/b as a multiple of 1/b and use this to multiply a fraction by a whole number (n × (a/b) = (n × a)/b); solve word problems involving multiplication of a fraction by a whole number, e.g., by using visual fraction models and equations. |

## Retrieval warm-ups (every week, per the track plan)

- **Week 19:** equivalence retrieval from Unit 04 — name two fractions
  equal to 3/4 before adding thirds and quarters is ever attempted
  (a reminder that only like denominators add directly).
- **Week 20:** quick-fire mixed-number reading — say 2 3/8 as "two and
  three eighths" and as an improper fraction, 19/8.
- **Week 21:** multiplication retrieval from Unit 03 — 4 × 6, 7 × 8,
  and 5 × 9, the whole-number products that become numerators in
  Lesson 05.
- **Week 22:** estimate-first from Unit 01 — before solving, name
  whether the answer will be less than, equal to, or greater than 1.

## Differentiation and accessibility

- **Supports:** paper fraction strips for every lesson (fold and cut before
  any written equation); a "denominator names the size — it never changes
  when you join or separate" prompt card; a mixed-number regrouping card
  ("1 whole = 8/8, 6/6, 10/10 — pick the denominator you are using") on
  the desk during Lessons 02 and 04 (removed only for the unit
  assessment, per the teacher guide); oral explanation accepted for every
  "explain" item, with the adult scribing if needed.
- **Extensions:** sums and differences that regroup across more than one
  whole (for example, 2 7/8 + 1 5/8 = 4 1/2); fraction-by-whole-number
  products whose answers need simplifying (for example, 6 × 2/8 = 12/8
  = 1 1/2); a "denominator detective" set of five solved problems where
  exactly two contain the added-denominators error; writing a word
  problem whose answer is a mixed number between 2 and 3.
- **Text alternatives:** every image-based question states its numbers in
  a text-only alternative, so no learner needs the picture to answer.
