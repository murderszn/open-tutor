# Unit 04 — Fraction Equivalence and Comparison

**Grade:** 4 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #22](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/22), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Two fractions can look completely different and still name exactly the same
amount. In this unit the learner explains why — cutting each piece into
smaller equal pieces changes the number and the size of the pieces, but not
the total amount — generates equivalent fractions by multiplying or dividing
the numerator and denominator by the same number, compares fractions with
different numerators and denominators using benchmarks, common numerators,
and common denominators, and defends every comparison with a visual model.
The unit ends in the track's midyear review week, so Week 18 also revisits
Units 01–03 before the culminating assessment.

## Essential question

When do two different-looking fractions name the same amount, and how can
you prove which of two fractions is greater?

## Prerequisites

From the grade-3 track and Units 01–03:

- Unit fractions and fractions on a number line (grade 3); a fraction
  a/b means a parts of size 1/b
- Multiplication and division facts within 100; factor pairs (Unit 02)
- Multiplying a number by the same number in numerator and denominator
  uses Unit 03's multiplication strategies; simplifying uses Unit 03's
  division reasoning
- The "same whole" habit: a fraction always refers to a stated whole

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Explain why a fraction a/b is equivalent to (n × a)/(n × b) using
   visual fraction models, attending to how the number and size of the
   parts differ even though the two fractions are the same size.
   (4.NF.A.1)
2. Recognize and generate equivalent fractions, including simplifying a
   fraction by dividing its numerator and denominator by the same number.
   (4.NF.A.1)
3. Compare two fractions with different numerators and different
   denominators by comparing to the benchmark 1/2, by creating common
   numerators, or by creating common denominators; record the result with
   `>`, `=`, or `<`; and justify the conclusion with a visual model.
   (4.NF.A.2)
4. Explain why a comparison is valid only when the two fractions refer
   to the same whole, and detect comparisons that break that rule.
   (4.NF.A.2)
5. Order a set of three or more fractions, build equivalence chains, and
   solve comparison word problems, justifying each step with a model or
   an equivalent-fraction equation. (4.NF.A.1, 4.NF.A.2)

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**, grade 4,
as published at [thecorestandards.org/Math](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/).
The Grade 4 [Number & Operations — Fractions](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/4/NF/)
domain page was opened and read on **2026-10-30**; it renders 4.NF.A.1 and
4.NF.A.2 verbatim, and its footnote limits grade-4 fraction expectations to
denominators 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 100. Every fraction in this unit
uses a denominator from that list, and every common denominator used in a
worked comparison is itself in that list. No state adoption or accreditation
is claimed. Descriptions below are paraphrases of the official expectations,
except where quoted.

| Unit objective | Standard | What the standard expects (paraphrase) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4.NF.A.1 | Explain why a/b equals (n × a)/(n × b) with visual fraction models, attending to how the number and size of the parts differ even though the fractions are the same size; use this principle to recognize and generate equivalent fractions. |
| 2 | 4.NF.A.1 | Recognize and generate equivalent fractions with the principle above, including dividing numerator and denominator by the same number. |
| 3 | 4.NF.A.2 | Compare two fractions with different numerators and denominators, e.g., by creating common denominators or numerators, or by comparing to a benchmark such as 1/2; record with `>`, `=`, or `<`; justify with a visual fraction model. |
| 4 | 4.NF.A.2 | Recognize that comparisons are valid only when the two fractions refer to the same whole. |
| 5 | 4.NF.A.1, 4.NF.A.2 | Apply equivalence and comparison to order sets of fractions and solve comparison problems. |

Unlike-denominator addition and subtraction in general is **not** a grade-4
requirement (framework footnote) and is not taught here; Unit 05 handles
like-denominator addition and subtraction.

## Differentiation and accessibility

- **Supports:** paper fraction strips for every lesson (fold and cut before
  any written equation); a "multiply or divide BOTH" prompt card; a
  benchmark card showing 1/2 with its equivalents (2/4, 3/6, 4/8, 5/10,
  6/12, 50/100) on the desk during strategy lessons (removed only for the
  unit assessment, per the teacher guide); oral explanation accepted for
  every "explain" item, with the adult scribing if needed.
- **Extensions:** equivalence chains that reach denominator 100
  (for example, 3/4 = 75/100); ordering sets that mix a whole (4/4) with
  proper fractions; a "same-whole detective" set of five comparisons where
  exactly two are invalid because the wholes differ; writing a comparison
  word problem whose answer requires a common denominator of 12.
- **Text alternatives:** every image-based question states its numbers in
  a text-only alternative, so no learner needs the picture to answer.
