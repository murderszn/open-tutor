# Culminating Assessment — Unit 04: Fraction Equivalence and Comparison

**Session:** 16 · **Time:** 35 minutes · **How to use:** independent
work. Fraction strips may be drawn by hand; the benchmark card and any
pre-made equivalence chart are put away. Show a model or a rewrite for
every comparison item. Total: 18 marks (item 9 has three parts).
Score against [../answer-key.md](../answer-key.md).

1. Write the fraction equivalent to 1/2 with denominator 6.
2. Simplify 8/12. Show the division of both numbers.
3. Fill in the blanks to complete the chain: 1/4 = ___/8 = ___/12.
4. Compare 4/5 and 3/5. Record with `>`, `=`, or `<`.
5. Compare 3/8 and 1/2. Record the symbol and justify with the
   benchmark.
6. Compare 5/6 and 7/12. Record the symbol and show the rewrites.
7. Compare 2/3 and 5/6. Record the symbol and show the rewrites.
8. Order 5/8, 1/2, and 3/4 from least to greatest. Show the rewrites.
9. **Bakery picture question.** A bakery counter holds two identical
   rectangular loaves. The left loaf is whole. The right loaf is cut
   into 5 equal strips. (Text-only alternative: this description is
   all you need — you do not need the picture.)
   - (a) One strip of the right loaf is what fraction of a loaf?
   - (b) Three strips are what fraction of a loaf?
   - (c) Write a fraction with denominator 10 that names the same
     amount as your answer to (b).
10. **Same-whole check:** "Kim drank 3/4 of a small bottle. Lee drank
    1/2 of a large bottle. Kim drank more." Is this conclusion valid
    from the fractions alone? Explain.
11. Word problem: "Two identical ropes are cut for a project. Mia uses
    5/6 of one rope. Noah uses 2/3 of the other rope. Who uses more
    rope? Justify with a rewrite."
12. **Chain check:** Is every link in this chain valid? Explain each
    link: 6/12 = 3/6 = 1/2.
13. Write two fractions equivalent to 5/10: one by simplifying, one by
    multiplying. Name the number used in each step.
14. True or false, with a reason: 4/8 > 3/6.
15. Word problem: "Three identical granola bars are shared. A eats 2/3
    of a bar, B eats 5/6 of a bar, C eats 1/2 of a bar. Order the three
    from least eaten to most eaten. Justify."
16. **Explain in your own words:** why does multiplying the numerator
    and the denominator by the same number NOT change the amount?
    Use the cutting-pieces picture in your explanation.

**Readiness for Unit 05:** 15–18 marks with no comparison item missed
for a same-whole or parts-separately error signals readiness. Below 15
marks, or any same-whole error, reteach Lesson 04 before Unit 05.
