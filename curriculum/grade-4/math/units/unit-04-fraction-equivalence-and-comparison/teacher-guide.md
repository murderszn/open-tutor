# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Fraction Equivalence and Comparison

**Audience:** the guiding adult. Student pages never link here with answers;
this guide is kept separate from all student questions.

## How to run the unit

- **Session length:** 35 minutes, 4 sessions per week, track weeks 15–18.
  The pacing in [README.md](README.md) names every session's activity and
  success criterion; do not compress the unit into its five lessons alone
  — fluency games, the project, and the Week 18 midyear review stations
  are part of the 16 sessions.
- **Materials rule:** paper fraction strips are available for every
  session. The benchmark card (1/2 and its equivalents) is on the desk
  during Lessons 01–05, practice, and the project, and is put away only
  for the culminating assessment, which says so on its face. The point
  of this unit is reasoning with models, not recall under pressure.
- **Show-the-work rule:** every equivalence item must name its
  multiplier or divisor; every comparison item must name its strategy
  (benchmark, same numerator, same denominator, common denominator) or
  show a model. A bare symbol scores half credit on the quiz and the
  assessment.
- **Denominator discipline:** grade-4 fractions use denominators 2, 3,
  4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 100 (framework footnote, verified 2026-10-30).
  Not every pair has a tidy in-list common denominator (3/8 has no
  in-list equivalent by multiplying; 2/3 vs. 3/5 has no in-list common
  denominator). When that happens, the strip model is the valid
  justification — do not let learners force an out-of-list denominator
  or guess. Lesson 04 names this honestly, and the answer key marks
  model justifications as full credit.
- **Midyear review (Week 18, Sessions 14–15):** Station 1 revisits
  Units 01–03 (one problem each: round 4,762 to the nearest 100; factor
  pairs of 48; 36 × 14 by area model; 847 ÷ 6 with remainder
  interpretation). Station 2 revisits Unit 04 and reteaches the
  highest-need objective from the Week 16 quiz. Do not start Unit 05
  until the assessment's readiness bar is met (see the assessment).

## Lesson-by-lesson misconceptions and reteach moves

### Lesson 01 — Same size, different pieces

- **Signature error:** judging fractions by the size of the numbers
  (4/8 "bigger" than 1/2). Reteach: strips in hand, lined up at the
  left edge — no rule talk until the shaded lengths are seen to match.
- **"Different pieces cannot be equal" (Alex's error):** the learner is
  confusing the pieces with the amount. Reteach: count shaded length,
  not pieces — the 2/4 strip's shaded part is one continuous length,
  exactly the 1/2 strip's.
- **Reteach target:** if the exit check (2/3 = 4/6) fails, repeat
  Worked example 2 with 1/3 → 2/6 before Lesson 02.

### Lesson 02 — Generating equivalent fractions

- **Signature error:** adding/subtracting the same number from top and
  bottom (Dana's error). Reteach: the cutting picture — cutting
  multiplies counts; addition is not a cut. Test any proposed rule on
  3/4: adding 1 gives 4/5, which a strip model shows is a different
  amount.
- **Simplifying past the simplest form:** learners sometimes divide by
  a number that does not divide both (for example, 9/12 ÷ 2). Reteach:
  the divisor must be a common factor — check both divisions.
- **Reteach target:** if the exit check fails, drill "multiply or
  divide BOTH, by the SAME number" with 1/4 → 2/8 → 3/12 before
  Lesson 03.

### Lesson 03 — Benchmark and same numerator/denominator

- **Signature error:** "bigger denominator means bigger fraction"
  (Mia's error). Reteach: draw 5/6 and 5/8 bars — the sixths are visibly
  bigger pieces, so 5 sixths cover more.
- **Benchmark overreach:** learners apply the benchmark even when both
  fractions sit on the same side of 1/2 and then guess. Reteach: the
  benchmark decides only when the fractions straddle 1/2; otherwise
  move to Lesson 04.
- **Reteach target:** if the exit check fails, repeat the benchmark
  checks with halves of 10 (5) and 12 (6) as anchors before Lesson 04.

### Lesson 04 — Common denominators and the same whole

- **Signature error:** comparing numerators and denominators separately
  (Lee's error: 4/5 < 7/10 because 4 < 7 and 5 < 10). Reteach: until a
  numerator or denominator matches, no comparison has been made —
  rewrite first, then compare.
- **Same-whole blindness:** learners compare fractions from different
  wholes without noticing. Reteach: Worked example 2, then require the
  sentence "the whole is the same" as the first line of every
  word-problem answer.
- **Reteach target:** if the Week 16 quiz shows same-whole errors,
  spend Session 15's Station 2 on same-whole sorting (five comparisons,
  exactly two invalid) before the assessment.

### Lesson 05 — Ordering and chains

- **Signature error:** ordering by numerator alone (Sam's error) or by
  denominator alone. Reteach: the ordering frame — rewrite, order by
  numerator, answer with the original fractions.
- **Broken chains:** a chain with one unchecked link. Reteach: check
  each link separately; a chain is only as strong as its weakest link.
- **Reteach target:** if the exit check fails, order 1/4, 1/2, 3/4 with
  denominator 4 as a worked redo before the project launch.

## Scoring guidance

- **Quiz (12 marks):** 10–12 with all strategies named signals
  readiness for Week 17. Below 10, or any item type below 80%,
  reteach that lesson before the project.
- **Practice (20 items):** items 17–20 are the legacy-frame set; item 20
  is the midyear bridge (36 × 14 = 504). A wrong bridge answer means
  Unit 03's area model needs a station in Week 18, not a Unit 04
  reteach.
- **Project:** see the rubric in [project.md](project.md). 9–12 points
  with no criterion at 1 signals readiness for Unit 05.
- **Assessment (18 marks):** 15–18 with no same-whole or
  parts-separately error signals readiness for Unit 05 (fraction
  addition/subtraction with like denominators). Below 15, reteach
  Lesson 04; any same-whole error, reteach Lesson 04's Worked
  example 2 even if the total passes.
