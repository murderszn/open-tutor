# Project — Fraction Bakery Menu

**Sessions:** 10, 12, 13 · **Time:** three 35-minute sessions (draft,
finish, present) · **Goal:** design a bakery menu that proves, with
models, that different-looking portions can be the same amount — and
that compares two customers' portions honestly.

## The job

You run a small bakery. Every loaf is baked in the **same-size pan**,
so every loaf is the same whole. Your menu must let a customer see, at
a glance, which portions are equal and which portion is bigger.

## Deliverables

1. **Three loaf models.** Draw three identical-length bars (the loaves).
   Cut the first into halves, the second into quarters, the third into
   eighths — or choose your own three cuttings from denominators 2, 3,
   4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12. Shade one portion on each loaf and label it.
   At least one portion must be a fraction greater than 1/2 (so the
   menu is not all small pieces).
2. **Equivalence chain poster.** Show one amount with at least three
   names (for example, 1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8), with a strip model for each
   name and a checked link between each pair of names (name the
   multiplier or divisor).
3. **Comparison table.** Two customers buy portions of identical loaves.
   Customer A buys 3/4 of a loaf; Customer B buys 5/8 of a loaf. Who
   bought more? Show the common-denominator rewrite, the symbol, and a
   strip-model sketch. Then add a second row: a customer who buys 2/3
   of a loaf vs. one who buys 5/6 — same treatment.
4. **Same-whole warning.** Write a three-sentence warning for the menu
   explaining why portion comparisons only work when the loaves are the
   same size, with one example of a comparison that would be invalid
   if the loaves differed.

## Materials

Paper strips, grid paper, colored pencils, ruler, the unit's
[`assets/fraction-bakery-trays.png`](assets/fraction-bakery-trays.png)
as a story prompt (the picture's loaves are a bakery scene, not your
measuring tool — draw your own equal strips for exact work).

## Steps

1. **Session 10 — Draft.** Choose your three cuttings and shade your
   three portions. Check: are all three bars exactly the same length?
   If not, redraw — different-length bars break every comparison.
2. **Session 12 — Finish.** Build the chain poster and the comparison
   table. Self-check every equivalence with a strip model and every
   comparison with a rewrite or a benchmark.
3. **Session 13 — Present.** Present one equivalence pair and one
   comparison to the adult (or a partner). Then check a peer's menu for
   a same-whole error: are all their bars the same length? Did they
   compare portions from different-sized loaves anywhere?

## Rubric (adult scores; 3 = strong, 2 = developing, 1 = needs reteach)

| Criterion | 3 — Strong | 2 — Developing | 1 — Needs reteach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equivalence models (deliverable 1 + 2) | Three same-length bars, correctly cut and labeled; chain of 3+ names with every link checked by multiplier/divisor | Bars mostly same length or one link unchecked | Bars differ in length, or links missing/wrong |
| Comparison reasoning (deliverable 3) | Both rows correct (3/4 > 5/8 via 6/8 vs. 5/8; 2/3 < 5/6 via 4/6 vs. 5/6) with rewrites and sketches | One row correct, or symbols right without rewrites | Symbols wrong, or comparisons made without rewrites/models |
| Same-whole understanding (deliverable 4) | Warning states the rule and gives a correct invalid example | Rule stated but example unclear or missing | No rule, or claims fractions alone decide different-sized wholes |
| Presentation and checking | Presents one pair and one comparison clearly; finds or correctly clears a peer's same-whole check | Presents but cannot explain a step, or peer check skipped | Cannot present the work, or peer check not attempted |

**Readiness:** 9–12 points with no criterion at 1 signals readiness for
Unit 05. A 1 in comparison reasoning → reteach Lesson 04 before Unit 05.
A 1 in same-whole understanding → reteach Lesson 04's Worked example 2.
