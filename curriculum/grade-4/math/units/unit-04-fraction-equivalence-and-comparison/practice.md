# Practice — Unit 04: Fraction Equivalence and Comparison

**Session:** 11 · **Time:** 35 minutes · **How to use:** complete in
order. Draw a strip model for every equivalence and comparison item
until the adult says models are optional. Show every multiply-or-divide-
both step. Score against [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) — the key is a
separate file; do not read it while working.

## A. Equivalence (Lessons 01–02)

1. Draw two identical bars. Shade 1/2 of one and 2/4 of the other. Are
   the amounts equal? Explain with the number and size of the pieces.
2. Write two fractions equivalent to 3/8 by multiplying — or explain why
   no in-list equivalent exists by multiplying. (Grade-4 denominators:
   2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 100.)
3. Generate the family of 3/4: write two equivalent fractions and name
   the multiplier for each.
4. Simplify 12/12, 6/10, and 9/12. Show the division of both numbers for
   each.
5. True or false, with a reason: 4/6 = 6/10.
6. **Error analysis:** "5/8 = 2/5 because I subtracted 3 from both
   numbers." Explain the error and give the correct way to test
   equivalence.

## B. Comparison (Lessons 03–04)

7. Compare 3/4 and 5/8. Record with `>`, `=`, or `<`, name your strategy,
   and justify.
8. Compare 7/12 and 5/6. Record the symbol and show the rewrites.
9. Compare 3/10 and 3/4. Record the symbol and name your strategy.
10. Compare 9/12 and 3/4. Are they equal? Justify with a rewrite.
11. **Same-whole check:** "Zoe ate 5/6 of her pizza. Max ate 5/6 of his
    pizza. They ate the same amount." Is this conclusion valid from the
    fractions alone? Explain.
12. **Error analysis:** "8/10 > 4/5 because 8 > 4 and 10 > 5." Show this
    is wrong, with a rewrite.

## C. Ordering, chains, word problems (Lesson 05)

13. Order 1/3, 1/2, and 5/6 from least to greatest. Show the rewrites.
14. Order 3/8, 1/4, and 3/4 from least to greatest. Show the rewrites.
15. Write an equivalence chain of three names for 4/5, and check each
    link.
16. Word problem: "Two identical water jugs start full. By evening, one
    is 7/10 full and the other is 3/5 full. Which jug has more water?
    Justify." Then answer: would the comparison still be valid if the
    jugs had started at different sizes? Explain.

## D. Legacy-frame set (adapted from `assignments/fractions-and-equivalent-models.md`)

The legacy assignment asked for 6 models, 3 equivalent pairs, an
ordering, and one story problem — but gave no teaching and no key.
Now that Lessons 01–05 have taught the skills, complete its frame
properly:

17. Draw at least 6 fraction models (bars or strips, same-length wholes)
    showing at least 3 equivalent fraction pairs. Label each model with
    its fraction and write the equation for each pair.
18. Put these 5 fractions in order from least to greatest, showing your
    rewrites: 2/3, 1/2, 5/6, 1/4, 3/4.
19. Write one real-life fraction story problem in which two people use
    different fractions of identical wholes, solve it, and justify the
    comparison with a model or a rewrite.
20. **Midyear bridge:** solve 36 × 14 with an area model (Unit 03) and
    explain in one sentence why the distributive property you used there
    (splitting 36 into 30 + 6) is like splitting a fraction bar into
    smaller pieces (splitting without changing the total).
