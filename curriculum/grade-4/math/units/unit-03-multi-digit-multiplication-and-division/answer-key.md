names the operation difference. The multiplicative answer must be the
larger one (for factors above 1).

## Lesson 03 — Division with partial quotients

**Guided practice**

1. 68 ÷ 4: 10 × 4 = 40 (28 left); 7 × 4 = 28 (0 left). Quotient **17**,
   remainder 0. Check: 17 × 4 = 68. ✓
2. 285 ÷ 3: 90 × 3 = 270 (15 left); 5 × 3 = 15 (0 left). Quotient **95**,
   remainder 0. Check: 95 × 3 = 285. ✓
3. 492 ÷ 6: 80 × 6 = 480 (12 left); 2 × 6 = 12 (0 left). Quotient **82**,
   remainder 0. Check: 82 × 6 = 492. ✓
4. 2,573 ÷ 4: 600 × 4 = 2,400 (173 left); 40 × 4 = 160 (13 left); 3 × 4 =
   12 (1 left). Quotient **643**, remainder **1**. Check: 643 × 4 + 1
   = 2,572 + 1 = 2,573. ✓

**Independent practice**

5. 918 ÷ 9: 100 × 9 = 900 (18 left); 2 × 9 = 18 (0 left). Quotient **102**,
   remainder 0. Check: 102 × 9 = 918. ✓
6. 1,764 ÷ 6: 200 × 6 = 1,200 (564 left); 90 × 6 = 540 (24 left); 4 × 6 =
   24 (0 left). Quotient **294**, remainder 0. Check: 294 × 6 = 1,764. ✓
7. 5,083 ÷ 5: 1,000 × 5 = 5,000 (83 left); 16 × 5 = 80 (3 left). Quotient
   **1,016**, remainder **3**. Check: 1,016 × 5 + 3 = 5,080 + 3 = 5,083. ✓
8. 7,256 ÷ 8: 900 × 8 = 7,200 (56 left); 7 × 8 = 56 (0 left). Quotient
   **907**, remainder 0. Check: 907 × 8 = 7,256. ✓
9. 2,914 ÷ 3: 900 × 3 = 2,700 (214 left); 70 × 3 = 210 (4 left); 1 × 3 = 3
   (1 left). Quotient **971**, remainder **1**. Check: 971 × 3 + 1 =
   2,913 + 1 = 2,914. ✓
10. Kim is wrong: a remainder must be **smaller than the divisor**, and
    her own check fails (141 × 6 + 7 = 853, not 847). After 140 groups
    (100 + 40) the leftover is 7, which holds one more group of 6,
    leaving 1. Correct: **141 remainder 1**; check 141 × 6 + 1 = 847. ✓

**Applied task:** 1,000 ÷ 8: 100 × 8 = 800 (200 left); 20 × 8 = 160 (40
left); 5 × 8 = 40 (0 left). Each group gets **125 stickers**, **0 left
over**. Check: 125 × 8 = 1,000. ✓ Any sensible leftover plan earns the
sentence credit (here there is no leftover).

**Exit check:** 3,962 ÷ 7: 500 × 7 = 3,500 (462 left); 60 × 7 = 420 (42
left); 6 × 7 = 42 (0 left). Quotient **566**, remainder 0. Check: 566 × 7
= 3,962. ✓

**Extension:** 9,999 ÷ 9 = **1,111** remainder 0 (9 × 1,111 = 9,999);
8,888 ÷ 8 = **1,111** remainder 0. The 1s appear because each place of
the dividend is exactly one group of the divisor: 9,000 ÷ 9 = 1,000,
900 ÷ 9 = 100, 90 ÷ 9 = 10, 9 ÷ 9 = 1.

## Lesson 04 — Multistep problems and remainders

**Guided practice**

1. 258 ÷ 9 = 28 remainder 6 (28 × 9 = 252). (a) **28 full trays.** (b)
   **29 trays** in all — the 6 leftover cupcakes need one more tray
   (round up).
2. Step 1: 3 × 48 = **144 muffins**. Step 2: 144 ÷ 8 = **18 boxes**.
   Check: 18 × 8 = 144. ✓
3. 1,250 ÷ 7 = 178 remainder 4 (178 × 7 = 1,246). Each class gets **178
   stickers**; **4 left** for the teacher. Estimate check: 1,400 ÷ 7 =
   200, and 178 is below it as expected (1,250 < 1,400). ✓
4. Equation: 24 × n + 18 = 234. Subtract: 24 × n = 216. n = 216 ÷ 24 =
   **9**. Check: 9 × 24 + 18 = 216 + 18 = 234. ✓

**Independent practice**

5. 436 ÷ 9 = 48 remainder 4 (48 × 9 = 432). **48 full rows**; **4 seats**
   in the last, partly full row.
6. Step 1: 23 × 15 = **345 pencils**. Step 2: 345 − 128 = **217 pencils
   left**.
7. Equation: 32 × b + 17 = 305. Subtract: 32 × b = 288. b = 288 ÷ 32 =
   **9 bags**. Check: 9 × 32 + 17 = 288 + 17 = 305. ✓
8. Sam forgot the 3 leftover students: 43 vans carry 344 students, and 3
   students would have no seat. Correct: **44 vans** (round up, because
   every student must travel).

**Applied task (open):** credit any classroom remainder problem where
the remainder must round up, with a correct division, a correct rounded
answer, and a situation-based reason. If the partner's reading differs
(drop vs. leftover), credit the discussion when it names what the
question actually asks.

**Exit check:** 200 ÷ 7 = 28 remainder 4 (28 × 7 = 196). (a) **28 full
bags.** (b) **4 snacks left over.** (c) **29 bags** if every snack must
be packed.

**Extension (sample):** 100 ÷ 7 = 14 remainder 2. Five readings: 15 vans
(round up — 2 people still need seats); 14 full boxes (drop — only full
boxes counted); 2 left over (leftover — the remainder is the answer);
14 full groups and a group of 2 (last partial group); 14 remainder 2 as
the bare division answer (before any interpretation — unfinished on its
own).

## Lesson 05 — Choosing a strategy

**Guided practice**

1. 198 × 4 by adjusting: 200 × 4 − 2 × 4 = 800 − 8 = **792**. Verify by
   splitting: 100 × 4 + 90 × 4 + 8 × 4 = 400 + 360 + 32 = 792. ✓
2. 52 × 8: split: 50 × 8 + 2 × 8 = 400 + 16 = **416**; adjust from 50
   gives the same split here. Either justification earns credit if it
   names something real (fewer steps, easier numbers, cleaner check).
3. 1,200 ÷ 6: 12 hundreds ÷ 6 = **200**. Verify: 200 × 6 = 1,200. ✓
4. 84 × 6 = **504**. Acceptable strategies: split (80 × 6 + 4 × 6 = 480
   + 24) or doubling (84 × 3 × 2 = 252 × 2) or adjusting from 90 (90 × 6
   − 6 × 6 = 540 − 36). Verify by any second method; the key point is
   the justification sentence, not which strategy was picked.

**Independent practice**

5. 203 × 7 by adjusting: 200 × 7 + 3 × 7 = 1,400 + 21 = **1,421**.
   Verify by splitting: same parts, or 203 × 7 = 200 × 7 + 3 × 7. (For a
   genuinely different check: 1,421 ÷ 7 = 203.) ✓
6. 47 × 9 = **423**. Split: 40 × 9 + 7 × 9 = 360 + 63. Adjust: 50 × 9 −
   3 × 9 = 450 − 27. Either preference earns credit with a justification.
7. 2,408 ÷ 8: 300 × 8 = 2,400 (8 left); 1 × 8 = 8 (0 left). Quotient
   **301**, remainder 0. Estimate: about 2,400 ÷ 8 = 300. ✓ Verify: 301
   × 8 = 2,408. ✓
8. Dana missed the adjustment step: 197 is 3 *less* than 200, so 3 × 5 =
   15 must be subtracted: 1,000 − 15 = **985**. Verify by splitting:
   100 × 5 + 90 × 5 + 7 × 5 = 500 + 450 + 35 = 985. ✓

**Applied task:** 48 × 7 = **336** (split: 40 × 7 + 8 × 7 = 280 + 56;
adjust: 50 × 7 − 2 × 7 = 350 − 14). Credit any two-sentence explanation
that connects second-method checking to catching ordering errors.

**Exit check:** 67 × 8: adjust: 70 × 8 − 3 × 8 = 560 − 24 = **536**;
split check: 60 × 8 + 7 × 8 = 480 + 56 = 536. ✓ Either preference is
acceptable with a real justification.

**Extension:** 999 × 8 = 1,000 × 8 − 1 × 8 = 8,000 − 8 = **7,992**.
1,005 × 6 = 1,000 × 6 + 5 × 6 = 6,000 + 30 = **6,030**. Rule: adjust
*down* (subtract) when the factor is just below a round number, adjust
*up* (add) when it is just above; the adjustment is always the
difference times the other factor.
