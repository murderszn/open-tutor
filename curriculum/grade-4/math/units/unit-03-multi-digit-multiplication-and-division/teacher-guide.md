# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Multi-Digit Multiplication and Division

**Audience:** the guiding adult. Student pages never link here with answers;
this guide is kept separate from all student questions.

## How to run the unit

- **Session length:** 35 minutes, 4 sessions per week, track weeks 11–14.
  The pacing in [README.md](README.md) names every session's activity and
  success criterion; do not compress the unit into its five lessons alone
  — fluency games, the project, and review stations are part of the
  16 sessions.
- **Fact chart:** available during Lessons 01–05, practice, and the
  project. Removed only for the culminating assessment, which says so on
  its face. The point of this unit is strategy and reasoning, not fact
  recall under pressure; Unit 02 already built the facts.
- **Estimate-first rule:** every computation item in this unit asks for an
  estimate first. If a learner skips it, send the work back before
  scoring — the habit is an objective (objective 5), not decoration.
- **The check rule:** every division answer must pass quotient × divisor
  + remainder = dividend, and every project total must be verified a
  second way. An unchecked answer is scored as incomplete, even if the
  number happens to be right.

## Lesson-by-lesson misconceptions and reteach moves

### Lesson 01 — Multiplication as comparison

- **Signature error:** answering "times as many" by adding. Reteach: the
  two sentence frames on cards; the learner must speak the frame and
  underline the comparison words before computing.
- **Unknown-factor confusion:** learners who can compute 8 × 12 = 96 but
  cannot set up 8 × b = 96 need the "smaller amount is unknown" story
  re-told with tiles: 96 tiles in 8 equal rows — how many per row?
- **Reteach target:** if the exit check fails, repeat Worked example 2
  with new numbers (for example, 7 × b = 84 → b = 12) before Lesson 02.

### Lesson 02 — Area models and partial products

- **Signature error:** the "two-piece" area model (tens × tens plus ones
  × ones only). Reteach: draw the rectangle every time; count the cells
  aloud ("four pieces, four partial products") until the learner produces
  all four unprompted. Do not accept a correct final answer with missing
  cross parts — the missing parts are the lesson.
- **Place-value slips:** partial products like 30 × 4 = 12 (dropped zero).
  Reteach: say the product in words ("thirty times four is one hundred
  twenty") before writing digits.
- **Reteach target:** if the exit check misses a cross part, assign the
  Lesson 02 extension (missing partial product, 58 × 34) as a diagnostic
  before Week 12.

### Lesson 03 — Division with partial quotients

- **Signature error:** stopping with a remainder larger than the divisor,
  or skipping the check. Reteach: the two-question test after every
  division — "Is the remainder smaller than the divisor?" and "Does
  quotient × divisor + remainder equal the dividend?"
- **Chunk-size anxiety:** learners who only take 1-group chunks (long,
  error-prone) need permission to take big friendly chunks: start with
  100s, then 10s. Model one division twice — once with small chunks,
  once with big ones — and compare the step counts.
- **Reteach target:** if the Week 12 quiz falls below 11/14, reteach
  partial quotients with dividends under 1,000 before Week 13; do not
  push into multistep problems on a shaky division base.

### Lesson 04 — Multistep problems and remainders

- **Signature error:** a single remainder rule ("always round up" or
  "always drop"). Reteach: the four-choice remainder card; require the
  sentence "The remainder means ___ because ___" on every remainder item.
- **Step-order errors:** learners who compute with the first two numbers
  they see, in order. Reteach: the plan-in-words step — no computation
  until the steps are listed ("first find the total, then share it").
- **Answering the wrong question:** Worked example 3's 744 trap (adding
  apples and juice boxes when nobody asked). Reteach: re-read the question
  sentence after computing, and check the answer's unit against it.

### Lesson 05 — Choosing a strategy

- **Signature error:** adjusting a division dividend and adjusting the
  quotient by the same amount. This is a place-value misunderstanding, not
  carelessness: reteach the dividend split (1,200 ÷ 6 = 12 hundreds ÷ 6),
  not more adjusting practice.
- **Speed without proof:** a fast answer with no check scores as
  incomplete in this unit. If a learner resists checking, make the check
  the graded part for one session (score the verification log, not the
  answer).

## Project scoring notes

Score with the rubric in [project.md](project.md). Notes:

- The sample stall in the project (504 apples, 240 juice boxes, 345
  pencils) is a starter, not an answer: learners who copy it exactly have
  not met the "choose your own numbers" requirement — score the
  multiplication criterion at partial credit at most, and ask for one
  original product.
- A poster with an unverified total cannot score full credit on
  verification, even if the total is correct.
- The peer error-hunt (Step 3) is graded on participation and honesty,
  not on whether an error was found: a draft with no errors still earns
  the credit if the partner's check is shown.

## Assessment guidance

- **Week 12 quiz (14 points):** below 11 → reteach partial quotients and
  remainder interpretation before Week 13. The quiz is formative: allow a
  retake of missed item types, not a retake of the whole quiz.
- **Unit assessment (28 points):** 24–28 = ready for Unit 04 (fraction
  equivalence — the distributive/area-model thinking from this unit
  reprises there); 19–23 = targeted reteach on missed objectives;
  below 19 = rework the Week 14 review stations with the adult before
  moving on.
- **Readiness evidence for Unit 04:** the learner multiplies two-digit
  numbers with area models, divides with a check, and interprets at least
  two remainder types correctly. If any of these is missing, Unit 04's
  fraction models will be built on sand — reteach first.

## Accessibility and safety notes

- All image-based questions carry a text-only alternative; the market
  stall picture is illustrative (only a few crates are drawn) and no
  question requires counting crates in the picture.
- Materials are household or dollar-store items; no sharp tools. Small
  parts (tiles, counters) need adult supervision for younger siblings in
  the room.
- No real learner information, names, photos, schedules, or grades go in
  this repository; completed learner work stays in private storage.
