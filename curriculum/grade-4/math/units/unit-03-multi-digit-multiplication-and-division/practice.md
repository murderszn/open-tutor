# Unit 03 — Mixed Practice (Session 12)

**Time:** 35 minutes · **Instructions:** Estimate first on every item,
show your strategy (area model, partial products, partial quotients, or an
adjustment), and verify each answer by a second method or the inverse
operation. Items 19–20 are the legacy
`assignments/multi-digit-multiplication-practice.md` set, re-audited in the
track scope-and-sequence: estimate, then calculate, show partial products,
and verify two products using a different method.

## Multiplication as comparison (objectives 1, 5)

1. A red box holds 8 marbles. A blue box holds 6 times as many marbles as
   the red box. How many marbles are in the blue box? Write the equation
   first. Then answer the additive version: how many would the blue box
   hold if it held 6 *more* than the red box?
2. Solve for the unknown: 7 × m = 119. Write a one-sentence comparison
   story that fits this equation.
3. A school garden has 4 times as many tomato plants as pepper plants.
   There are 26 pepper plants. How many tomato plants are there?

## Area models and partial products (objective 2)

4. Compute 56 × 7 with partial products. (Estimate first.)
5. Compute 39 × 26 with an area model. (Estimate first.)
6. Compute 4,096 × 3 with partial products. (Estimate first.)
7. Compute 83 × 19 with an area model. (Estimate first.)
8. Compute 2,507 × 8 with partial products. (Estimate first.)

## Division with partial quotients (objective 3)

9. Divide 340 ÷ 4 with partial quotients, then check.
10. Divide 567 ÷ 7 with partial quotients, then check.
11. Divide 623 ÷ 7 with partial quotients, then check.
12. Divide 472 ÷ 8 with partial quotients, then check.
13. Divide 574 ÷ 7 with partial quotients, then check.

## Multistep problems and remainders (objective 4)

14. A library has 864 books to pack into boxes of 9. (a) How many *full*
    boxes? (b) How many books are left over? (c) If every book must be
    packed, how many boxes are needed?
15. A class makes 5 rows of 36 chairs for an assembly, then removes 47
    chairs to make an aisle. How many chairs are left?
16. Let p stand for the number of pages. "A book has p pages in each of 4
    chapters, plus 29 pages of pictures, for 321 pages in all." Write the
    equation, solve for p, and check.
17. A farmer collects 1,152 eggs and packs them in trays of 8. How many
    trays does she fill? Show partial quotients and the check.
18. **Error analysis:** A learner computed 46 × 32 as (40 × 30) + (6 × 2)
    = 1,212. Show the full area model, give the correct product, and
    explain which partial products were missed.

## Legacy practice set (objective 2, 5)

19. Estimate, then calculate with partial products: 36 × 14, 72 × 8,
    48 × 23, and 125 × 6. Show every partial product.
20. Verify two of the products from item 19 using a different method
    (area model, adjustment, or the inverse operation), and write one
    sentence for each saying whether the check agreed.

**Scoring:** the adult scores against
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md). Items 1–3 test comparison language; items
4–8 test area models and partial products; items 9–13 test partial
quotients and the check; items 14–18 test remainder interpretation and
multistep reasoning; items 19–20 test the legacy set with verification.
Reteach any objective below 80% before the Week 14 review stations.
