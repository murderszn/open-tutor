# Project — Market Day Plan

**Sessions:** 11 (launch), 13 (work), 14 (presentations) · **Time:** about
35 minutes per session, plus poster-making time

## The task

You are planning a class market stall for Market Day. Your stall sells
three products. For each product you must decide a pack size and a number
of packs, compute the total with an area model or partial products, plan
what happens to leftovers when the stock is shared, and present your plan
on a poster with a budget table. Every computation must be verified by a
second method or the inverse operation — an unverified number does not go
on the poster.

## Rules (keep the numbers in grade 4)

- Exactly three products. Each product's pack size and pack count must
  keep the total below 1,000,000 (the grade-4 number range) — in practice,
  choose totals in the tens to low thousands so your area models stay
  readable.
- At least one product must use a two-digit × two-digit multiplication
  (for example, 24 packs of 36 items).
- At least one product must be shared equally among a number of classes
  or groups, with a remainder you interpret (round up, drop, or leftover —
  your poster must say which, and why).
- At least one product must include a comparison: either "times as many"
  (multiplicative) or a clearly labeled "more than" (additive) statement,
  with its equation.
- Prices, if you include them, are fictional classroom numbers in whole
  dollars — this is a computation project, not a real sale. No real money
  changes hands, and no real learner information appears on the poster.

## Worked starter (use it, then choose your own numbers)

A sample stall (do not copy it — choose your own products and numbers):

- Apple crates: 14 crates × 36 apples = 504 apples (area model:
  300 + 120 + 60 + 24; verified by 36 × 10 + 36 × 4 = 360 + 144 = 504).
- Juice packs: 5 packs × 48 juice boxes = 240 juice boxes, shared among 6
  classes → 40 per class, no remainder (check: 6 × 40 = 240).
- Pencil bundles: 23 bundles × 15 pencils = 345 pencils; 128 given away
  for a poster contest leaves 217 (345 − 128 = 217).

## Steps

1. **Choose (Session 11).** Pick three products, pack sizes, and pack
   counts. Write your comparison statement and its equation. Estimate
   every total before computing.
2. **Compute (Sessions 11–13).** For each product, show an area model or
   partial products; for each sharing plan, show partial quotients and
   the check (quotient × divisor + remainder = total). Interpret every
   remainder in a sentence.
3. **Check (Session 13).** Verify every number a second way. Trade posters
   with a partner and hunt for one computation error in each other's
   draft — finding it earns both of you credit (see the rubric).
4. **Present (Session 14).** Present one computation and one remainder
   decision to the class. Explain your strategy choice: why did you split,
   adjust, or use partial quotients here?

## Deliverables

1. A **poster** showing the three products, their pack sizes and counts,
   and the total for each, with at least one area model drawn on it.
2. A **budget table** (paper or grid paper) with one row per product:
   product, pack size, number of packs, total, sharing plan, remainder
   interpretation.
3. A **comparison statement** with its equation, on the poster or the
   table.
4. A **verification log**: for each total, the second method or inverse
   check, written out (not just "checked").

## Rubric

| Criterion | Full credit (3) | Partial credit (2) | Needs work (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multiplication (area models / partial products) | All three totals computed with shown splits or area models; all correct | Most totals shown and correct; one split or product error | Totals given without work, or more than one product error |
| Division and remainder interpretation | Sharing shown with partial quotients and check; remainder interpreted from the situation | Sharing shown; remainder named but not justified from the situation | Remainder ignored, or a remainder larger than the divisor left standing |
| Comparison statement | Correct multiplicative (or correctly labeled additive) statement with equation | Statement present with a small equation error | Comparison confused (for example, "times as many" computed by adding) |
| Verification and presentation | Every total verified a second way in the log; presentation explains one strategy choice | Most totals verified; presentation names a strategy without explaining it | Unverified totals on the poster, or no presentation |

**Total:** 12 points. 10–12 = ready for Unit 04; 7–9 = one reteach session
on the weakest criterion; below 7 = rework the poster with the adult
before the unit assessment. The rubric scores the mathematics, not the
artwork: a plain, correct poster beats a decorated, wrong one.
