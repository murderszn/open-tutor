# Unit 03 Culminating Assessment — Multi-Digit Multiplication and Division

**Session:** 16 · **Time:** 35 minutes · **Instructions:** Work
independently. The fact chart is **not** available for this assessment;
estimation and verification are still required where the item asks for
them. Show your strategy on every computation item. Scored against
[../answer-key.md](../answer-key.md). Do not claim this assessment is
standardized or validated — it is a classroom check of this unit's
objectives.

## Part A — Comparison (objectives 1, 5)

1. Write both comparison readings of 56 = 7 × 8.
2. A blue bin holds 9 balls. A red bin holds 5 times as many balls as the
   blue bin. How many balls are in the red bin? Write the equation first.
   Then answer: how many would the red bin hold if it held 5 *more* than
   the blue bin?
3. Solve for the unknown: 6 × t = 144. Write a one-sentence comparison
   story that fits this equation.

## Part B — Multiplication (objective 2)

4. Compute 78 × 9 with partial products. Estimate first.
5. Compute 43 × 27 with an area model. Estimate first.
6. Compute 2,836 × 4 with partial products. Estimate first, and verify by
   a second method.

## Part C — Division (objective 3)

7. Divide 936 ÷ 9 with partial quotients, then check.
8. Divide 2,573 ÷ 4 with partial quotients, then check.
9. Divide 5,083 ÷ 5 with partial quotients, then check.

## Part D — Multistep problems and remainders (objective 4)

10. A hall has 436 seats in rows of 9. How many *full* rows are there,
    and how many seats are in the last, partly full row?
11. Maya buys 23 packs with 15 pencils in each pack, then gives away 128
    pencils. How many pencils does she have left? Show both steps.
12. Let b stand for the number of bags. "A class collects cans in bags of
    32. After adding 17 loose cans, the class has 305 cans in all." Write
    the equation, solve for b, and check.
13. Explain, in your own words, why the same remainder can lead to
    different answers in different situations. Give two examples from
    this unit (for instance, vans vs. full boxes) with their numbers.

**Scoring:** items 1–3: 2 points each (equation/readings, answer,
story/check as applicable); items 4–9: 2 points each (strategy shown,
correct answer, estimate or check as asked); items 10–12: 3 points each
(steps shown, correct answer, interpretation or check); item 13: 3 points
(two correct examples with numbers, and a general explanation). Total:
28 points. 24–28 = ready for Unit 04; 19–23 = targeted reteach on missed
objectives; below 19 = rework the unit's review stations with the adult
before moving on.
