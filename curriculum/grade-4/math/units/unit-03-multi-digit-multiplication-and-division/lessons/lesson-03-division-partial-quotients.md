# Lesson 03 — Division with Partial Quotients

**Session:** 5 · **Time:** 35 minutes · **Goal:** I can divide a number of
up to four digits by a one-digit divisor with partial quotients, name the
quotient and remainder, and check my answer with multiplication.

**Materials:** paper and pencil, multiplication/division fact chart
(allowed for strategy lessons), base-ten blocks or grid paper (optional).

## Readiness check (4 min)

"Estimate 1,365 ÷ 5." (About 1,400 ÷ 5 = 280, or 1,500 ÷ 5 = 300 — either
estimate says the quotient is in the high 200s.) Then: "What multiplication
fact helps with 847 ÷ 6?" (6 × 100 = 600 and 6 × 140 = 840 — friendly
chunks, not a single fact. Division in grade 4 is built from chunks, not
from one memorized step.)

## Explicit teaching (10 min)

In a division, the **dividend** is the amount being shared or grouped, the
**divisor** is how many groups (or how many in each group), the **quotient**
is the answer, and the **remainder** is what is left over. For example, in
847 ÷ 6 = 141 remainder 1, the dividend is 847, the divisor is 6, the
quotient is 141, and the remainder is 1.

**Partial quotients** means taking the dividend apart in friendly chunks:

1. **Estimate first** (round the dividend to a friendly number).
2. **Take a big chunk:** pick a friendly number of groups (100, then 10s,
   then 1s), multiply, and subtract.
3. **Repeat** with what is left until the leftover is smaller than the
   divisor. That leftover is the remainder.
4. **Add the partial quotients** for the quotient.
5. **Check always:** quotient × divisor + remainder must equal the
   dividend. If it does not, the division is wrong — no exceptions.

A remainder must always be **smaller than the divisor**. If your remainder
is 6 or more when dividing by 6, you can take another group — your quotient
is too small.

Division also has an area-model picture: a rectangle of area 847 with one
side 6 breaks into strips of area 600 (side 100), 240 (side 40), and 6 (side
1), with 1 square left over — the same chunks as the partial quotients.

## Worked example 1 — No remainder: 96 ÷ 4

1. Estimate: 100 ÷ 4 = 25, so the quotient is near 25.
2. Take a chunk: 20 × 4 = 80. Subtract: 96 − 80 = 16 left.
3. Take another chunk: 4 × 4 = 16. Subtract: 16 − 16 = 0 left.
4. Add the partial quotients: 20 + 4 = **24**, remainder **0**.
5. Check: 24 × 4 + 0 = 96. ✓

## Worked example 2 — Four digits: 1,365 ÷ 5

1. Estimate: about 1,400 ÷ 5 = 280.
2. Chunk 1: 200 × 5 = 1,000. Left: 1,365 − 1,000 = 365.
3. Chunk 2: 70 × 5 = 350. Left: 365 − 350 = 15.
4. Chunk 3: 3 × 5 = 15. Left: 15 − 15 = 0.
5. Quotient: 200 + 70 + 3 = **273**, remainder **0**.
6. Check: 273 × 5 = 1,365. ✓ (273 × 5 = 200 × 5 + 70 × 5 + 3 × 5 = 1,000 +
   350 + 15 = 1,365.)

## Worked example 3 — With a remainder: 847 ÷ 6

1. Estimate: about 840 ÷ 6 = 140.
2. Chunk 1: 100 × 6 = 600. Left: 847 − 600 = 247.
3. Chunk 2: 40 × 6 = 240. Left: 247 − 240 = 7.
4. Chunk 3: 1 × 6 = 6. Left: 7 − 6 = 1. The leftover 1 is smaller than the
   divisor 6, so it is the remainder.
5. Quotient: 100 + 40 + 1 = **141**, remainder **1**.
6. Check: 141 × 6 + 1 = 846 + 1 = 847. ✓

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. Divide **68 ÷ 4** with partial quotients, then check.
   *(Hint: start with 10 × 4 = 40.)*
2. Divide **285 ÷ 3** with partial quotients, then check.
   *(Hint: 90 × 3 = 270 leaves 15.)*
3. Divide **492 ÷ 6** with partial quotients, then check.
   *(Hint: 80 × 6 = 480 leaves 12.)*
4. Divide **2,573 ÷ 4** with partial quotients, then check.
   *(Hint: start with 600 × 4 = 2,400; your remainder must be smaller
   than 4.)*

## Independent practice

5. Divide **918 ÷ 9** with partial quotients, then check.
6. Divide **1,764 ÷ 6** with partial quotients, then check.
7. Divide **5,083 ÷ 5** with partial quotients, then check. (Watch the
   middle chunk: after 1,000 × 5, what is left?)
8. Divide **7,256 ÷ 8** with partial quotients, then check.
9. Divide **2,914 ÷ 3** with partial quotients, then check.
10. **Error analysis:** Kim computed 847 ÷ 6 = 141 remainder 7 and stopped.
    Explain why a remainder of 7 is impossible when dividing by 6, and give
    the correct answer with its check.

## Applied task

A teacher has 1,000 stickers to share equally among 8 table groups, as far
as possible. How many stickers does each group get, and how many are left
over? Show partial quotients and the check, then write one sentence saying
what the teacher could do with the leftovers. (This is a "leftover" remainder
— Lesson 04 shows remainders that mean something different.)

## Exit check

"Divide 3,962 ÷ 7 with partial quotients and check your answer." (Answer:
566 remainder 0; check 566 × 7 = 3,962. If your remainder was not 0, check
whether you stopped one chunk early.)

## Supports, extensions, teacher note

- **Reading/language support:** label every chunk ("100 groups of 6 =
  600") and every leftover ("247 left") before the next chunk.
- **Alternate response mode:** build the dividend with base-ten blocks and
  physically remove divisor-sized groups in chunks, recording each chunk.
- **Extension:** divide 9,999 ÷ 9 with partial quotients (answer 1,111
  remainder 0) and explain why the pattern of 1s appears; then try 8,888 ÷
  8.
- **Teacher note — likely misconception:** learners who stop with a
  remainder larger than the divisor, or who skip the check, carry both
  errors into multistep problems. Make the check (quotient × divisor +
  remainder = dividend) non-negotiable on every item in this lesson.
