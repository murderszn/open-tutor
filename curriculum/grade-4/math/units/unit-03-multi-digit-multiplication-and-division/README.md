# Unit 03 — Multi-Digit Multiplication and Division

**Grade:** 4 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 11–14
**Backlog:** [issue #22](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/22), section U03
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Big multiplication and division stop being guessing games when you break
numbers apart, use what you already know, and check your answer a second
way. In this unit the learner reads multiplication as comparison ("times as
many," not "more than"), multiplies a number of up to four digits by a
one-digit number and two two-digit numbers with area models and partial
products, divides dividends of up to four digits by a one-digit divisor with
partial quotients, interprets remainders from the situation (not from a
rule), and solves multistep word problems with a letter for the unknown,
always estimating first and judging whether the exact answer is reasonable.

## Essential question

How does breaking numbers into friendlier parts — and checking the answer a
second way — let us multiply and divide big numbers with confidence?

## Prerequisites

From Unit 01 (track weeks 3–6) and Unit 02 (track weeks 7–10), and the
grade-3 track:

- Multiplication and division facts within 100; arrays as pictures of
  multiplication; factor pairs and multiples for numbers 1–100
- Place value to 1,000,000; rounding to any place and estimating with
  rounded numbers (the "estimate first" habit from Unit 01)
- Two-step word problems with a letter for the unknown; area models from
  Unit 02's bridge session (a rectangle of 24 × 13 as an area)

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Interpret a multiplication equation as a comparison, represent verbal
   multiplicative comparisons as equations, and solve multiplicative
   comparison word problems, distinguishing them from additive comparison.
   (4.OA.A.1, 4.OA.A.2)
2. Multiply a whole number of up to four digits by a one-digit whole number,
   and multiply two two-digit numbers, using strategies based on place value
   and the properties of operations, and illustrate and explain the
   calculation with equations, rectangular arrays, and/or area models.
   (4.NBT.B.5)
3. Find whole-number quotients and remainders with up to four-digit
   dividends and one-digit divisors, using strategies based on place value,
   the properties of operations, and/or the relationship between
   multiplication and division, and illustrate and explain with equations,
   rectangular arrays, and/or area models. (4.NBT.B.6)
4. Solve multistep word problems posed with whole numbers and having
   whole-number answers using the four operations, including problems in
   which remainders must be interpreted; represent them with equations using
   a letter for the unknown; assess reasonableness with mental computation
   and estimation strategies including rounding. (4.OA.A.3)
5. Choose and justify an efficient strategy for a given problem, estimate
   before computing, and verify an answer by a second method or by the
   inverse operation. (4.NBT.B.5, 4.NBT.B.6, 4.OA.A.3)

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**, grade 4,
as published at [thecorestandards.org/Math](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/).
The Grade 4 [Operations & Algebraic Thinking](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/4/OA/)
and [Number & Operations in Base Ten](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/4/NBT/)
domain pages were opened and read on **2026-10-23**; they render 4.OA.A.1,
4.OA.A.2, 4.OA.A.3, 4.NBT.B.5, and 4.NBT.B.6 verbatim. No state adoption or
accreditation is claimed. Descriptions below are paraphrases of the official
expectations, except where quoted.

| Unit objective | Standard | What the standard expects (paraphrase) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4.OA.A.1 | Interpret a multiplication equation as a comparison (for example, 35 = 5 × 7 as 35 being 5 times as many as 7 and 7 times as many as 5); represent verbal statements of multiplicative comparisons as multiplication equations. |
| 1 | 4.OA.A.2 | Multiply or divide to solve word problems involving multiplicative comparison, using drawings and equations with a symbol for the unknown, distinguishing multiplicative comparison from additive comparison. |
| 2 | 4.NBT.B.5 | Multiply a whole number of up to four digits by a one-digit whole number, and multiply two two-digit numbers, using strategies based on place value and properties of operations; illustrate and explain with equations, rectangular arrays, and/or area models. |
| 3 | 4.NBT.B.6 | Find whole-number quotients and remainders with up to four-digit dividends and one-digit divisors, using strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the multiplication/division relationship; illustrate and explain with equations, rectangular arrays, and/or area models. |
| 4 | 4.OA.A.3 | Solve multistep whole-number word problems with whole-number answers using the four operations, including problems in which remainders must be interpreted; represent with equations using a letter for the unknown; assess reasonableness with mental computation and estimation including rounding. |
| 5 | 4.NBT.B.5, 4.NBT.B.6, 4.OA.A.3 | Strategy choice, estimation-first, and verification by a second method or inverse operation, as the application of the standards above. |

Grade-4 expectations in the Number & Operations in Base Ten domain are limited
to whole numbers less than or equal to 1,000,000 (framework footnote). The
standard algorithm for multi-digit multiplication is **not** a grade-4
expectation and is not taught here; strategies, area models, and partial
products are the grade-4 route.

## Retrieval warm-ups (every week, per the track plan)

Each week opens with a 5-minute estimation retrieval warm-up from Unit 01 —
the habit "estimate first" never lapses:

- **Week 11:** estimate 36 × 14 (40 × 10 = 400) and 2,438 × 5 (2,400 × 5 =
  12,000) before the first area-model session.
- **Week 12:** estimate 1,365 ÷ 5 (about 1,400 ÷ 5 = 280) and 847 ÷ 6 (about
  840 ÷ 6 = 140) before the division sessions.
- **Week 13:** estimate a two-step total (23 × 15 ≈ 20 × 15 = 300) before the
  multistep sessions; predict whether a remainder rounds up, drops, or
  stays as a leftover.
- **Week 14:** quick-fire — estimate 64 × 18 (60 × 20 = 1,200) and 2,914 ÷ 3
  (about 3,000 ÷ 3 = 1,000) before the review stations.

## 16-session pacing (4 weeks × 4 sessions, 35 minutes each)

Sessions blend core lessons, guided practice, fluency games, the project,
and assessment. Each session names its activity and success criterion.

**Week 11 — Multiplication as comparison; area models and partial products**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lesson 01** — Multiplication as comparison | Learner writes comparison equations for "times as many" problems, solves for an unknown factor, and explains why "4 times as many" is not "4 more." |
| 2 | **Lesson 02** — Area models and partial products (market-stall image activity) | Learner reads the stall signs (14 crates, 36 apples in each crate), builds the 36 × 14 area model, and finds 504 four ways (partial products). |
| 3 | Session task: guided practice set — area models for 2-digit × 2-digit and 4-digit × 1-digit products | Learner completes the set with area-model sketches and estimate-first checks; errors are re-solved by re-splitting a factor. |
| 4 | Fluency game: Partial-Product Race (split, multiply, add — beat the adult's estimate) | Learner splits factors correctly on every turn and names the largest partial product before adding. |

**Week 12 — Division with partial quotients; remainders**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | **Lesson 03** — Division with partial quotients | Learner divides up to four-digit dividends by one-digit divisors with partial quotients and checks every answer with quotient × divisor + remainder. |
| 6 | Division stations: sharing, grouping, and area-model division (remainder sorting) | Learner sorts division situations by what the remainder means before computing, and computes each station correctly. |
| 7 | Formative quiz: [`assessments/week-12_quiz.md`](assessments/week-12_quiz.md) (legacy division-concepts items, deduplicated and revised, plus remainder interpretation) | Learner completes the quiz showing partial-quotient work; adult scores against [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) and reteaches any item type below 80%. |
| 8 | Fluency game: Quotient Bingo (estimate the quotient range, then compute) | Learner estimates each quotient's range before computing and wins a row only with verified answers. |

**Week 13 — Multistep problems; choosing a strategy**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | **Lesson 04** — Multistep problems and interpreting remainders | Learner solves multistep problems with a letter for the unknown, and for each remainder states whether it rounds up, drops, or stays as a leftover — with a reason from the situation. |
| 10 | **Lesson 05** — Choosing a strategy: split, adjust, or estimate? | Learner picks a strategy for each problem, justifies the choice, and verifies with a second method. |
| 11 | Project launch: [`project.md`](project.md) — Market Day Plan (choose products, compute pack totals, plan sharing) | Learner drafts a stall plan with at least three products, area-model or partial-product work, and a remainder plan for leftovers. |

## Differentiation and accessibility

- **Supports:** base-ten blocks or grid paper for every area model; a
  "split-first" prompt card (write both splits before multiplying); partial
  quotients with friendly numbers (100s, then 10s, then 1s) before any
  shortcut; multiplication/division fact charts on the desk during strategy
  lessons (removed only for the unit assessment, per the teacher guide);
  oral explanation accepted for every "explain" item, with the adult scribing
  if needed.
- **Extensions:** dividends just above the grade band for estimation only
  (never as required computation); two two-digit products with a missing
  partial product to find; multistep problems the learner writes for the
  class, with a key; a "remainder museum" — five situations where the same
  remainder 3 means five different answers.
- **Text alternatives:** every image-based question states its numbers in a
  text-only alternative, so no learner needs the picture to answer.
