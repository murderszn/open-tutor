# Unit 02 — Factors, Multiples, and Multiplication Strategies

**Grade:** 4 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #22](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/22), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Every whole number has a structure: it can be broken into factor pairs,
counted up in multiples, classified by how many factors it has, and grown by a
rule into a pattern. In this unit the learner finds all factor pairs of whole
numbers 1–100 using tile arrays, names the factor↔multiple relationship,
decides prime vs. composite from factor lists (never from memorized lists),
generates number and shape patterns from a given rule and explains features
the rule did not state, and turns factor pairs into multiplication strategies
(doubling/halving, breaking into known facts) that lay the groundwork for
Unit 03's area models.

## Essential question

How do factor pairs, multiples, and patterns reveal the hidden structure of
whole numbers — and how can we use that structure to multiply smarter?

## Prerequisites

From Unit 01 (track weeks 3–6) and the grade-3 track:

- Multiplication and division facts within 100; arrays as pictures of
  multiplication; skip counting by 2s, 3s, 4s, 5s, and 10s
- Rounding and estimation from Unit 01 (used here only for the retrieval
  warm-ups and reasonableness checks — never as a crutch for factor work)
- Two-step word problems with a letter for the unknown; reading a 100 chart

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. List **all** factor pairs of a whole number 1–100 using arrays or
   skip-counting reasoning. (4.OA.B.4)
2. Explain that a whole number is a **multiple of each of its factors**, and
   determine whether a number 1–100 is a multiple of a given one-digit number.
   (4.OA.B.4)
3. Determine whether a whole number 1–100 is **prime** or **composite** by
   finding its factors, and justify the decision in words. (4.OA.B.4)
4. Generate number and shape patterns from a given rule; identify and informally
   explain a feature not stated in the rule. (4.OA.C.5)
5. Use factor pairs and rectangular arrays as multiplication **strategies**
   (doubling/halving, breaking into known facts) and connect arrays to area
   models. (early 4.NBT.B.5 groundwork)

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**, grade 4,
as published at [thecorestandards.org/Math](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/).
The [Grade 4 Operations & Algebraic Thinking](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/4/OA/)
domain page was opened and read on **2026-10-17**; it renders 4.OA.B.4 and
4.OA.C.5 verbatim, including the official "Add 3" odd/even-alternation example
for 4.OA.C.5. No state adoption or accreditation is claimed. Descriptions below
are paraphrases of the official expectations.

| Unit objective | Standard | What the standard expects (paraphrase) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4.OA.B.4 | Find all factor pairs for a whole number in the range 1–100. |
| 2 | 4.OA.B.4 | Recognize that a whole number is a multiple of each of its factors; determine whether a given whole number in the range 1–100 is a multiple of a given one-digit number. |
| 3 | 4.OA.B.4 | Determine whether a given whole number in the range 1–100 is prime or composite. |
| 4 | 4.OA.C.5 | Generate a number or shape pattern that follows a given rule; identify apparent features of the pattern not explicit in the rule itself. |
| 5 | 4.NBT.B.5 (early groundwork) | Strategy groundwork for multiplying whole numbers using place value and properties of operations — this unit covers only the factor-pair/array reasoning, not the standard itself. |

## Retrieval warm-ups (every week, per the track plan)

Each week opens with a 5-minute rounding/estimation retrieval warm-up from
Unit 01 — the habit "estimate first" never lapses:

- **Week 7:** round 4,832 and 51,906 to the nearest hundred and thousand; then
  estimate 23 × 4 (20 × 4 = 80) before the first tile-array session.
- **Week 8:** estimate 3,847 + 2,965 (to the nearest thousand) before the
  prime/composite sessions; verify exact work from memory.
- **Week 9:** estimate 7,248 − 3,911; then predict whether the difference is
  odd or even (parity link to the pattern week).
- **Week 10:** quick-fire — round 99,750 to the nearest thousand (the halfway
  case), estimate 25 × 6 and 24 × 13 before the area-model bridge session.

## 16-session pacing (4 weeks × 4 sessions, 35 minutes each)

Sessions blend core lessons, fluency games, the project, and assessment. Each
session names its activity and success criterion.

**Week 7 — Factor pairs and multiples**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lesson 01** — Factor pairs with arrays (Factor Tile Garden image activity) | Learner reads the four sign amounts (36, 24, 48, 30), lists **every** factor pair of each, and sketches two different arrays per number. |
| 2 | Session task: legacy `assignments/factors-and-multiples.md` — decision: **keep** (factors of 18, 24, 35, 47 + first-six multiples of 6 and 9; all in standard range; no revision needed) | Learner completes the organizer; adult scores the factor lists, prime/composite calls, and multiples against this unit's [answer-key.md](answer-key.md). |
| 3 | **Lesson 02** — Multiples and the factor↔multiple relationship | Learner states "a number is a multiple of each of its factors" with an example, and decides correctly whether given numbers are multiples of given one-digit numbers. |
| 4 | Fluency game: Factor-Pair War (tile arrays beat face value) + 100-chart multiples coloring relay | Learner names all factor pairs of a drawn number faster than the adult; the colored chart shows clean skip-counting columns. |

**Week 8 — Prime vs. composite**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | **Lesson 03** — Prime and composite (factor-list justification; the sieve on 1–100) | Learner classifies numbers 1–100 as prime, composite, or neither, justifying each with its factor list; runs the sieve with no memorized list. |
| 6 | Prime detective stations: build factor lists with tiles for 12, 17, 21, 29, 36, 51 (kept vs. revise station labels from Lesson 03) | Learner sorts every station card correctly and explains why 1 is neither. |
| 7 | Mixed practice retrieval: factor pairs + multiples + Week 7 vocabulary (written set, no new content) | Learner works the set with ≤1 error; errors are re-solved with the tile-array routine. |
| 8 | Pattern preview game: "predict the 10th term" with a growing dot row (bridge to patterns) | Learner predicts correctly and names the rule used. |

**Week 9 — Patterns and multiplication strategies**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | **Lesson 04** — Number patterns from a rule ("Add 3", "multiply by 2") | Learner generates both patterns, identifies the hidden feature (odd/even alternation; always-even), and explains informally *why* it continues. |
| 10 | **Lesson 05** — Shape patterns and multiplication strategies (doubling/halving, breaking into known facts; 24 × 13 area preview) | Learner solves 12 × 6 two ways and builds the 24 × 13 area model with labeled dimensions. |
| 11 | Pattern stations + error-analysis task (from [practice.md](practice.md) section F) | Learner finds the planted factor-list error and writes the corrected list with a justification. |
| 12 | Project day 1: Factor Garden Exhibition — arrays poster + factor lists + prime/composite classification | Learner completes the poster draft with all arrays drawn to scale (relative, not decorative) and every factor pair listed. |

**Week 10 — Review, project, assessment**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | Project day 2: exhibition finishing — rule-pattern journal entry, peer teach | Learner presents the poster, teaches one factor-pair find to the adult, and completes the pattern journal entry. |
| 14 | Mixed practice ([practice.md](practice.md)) + formative quiz (`assessments/week-10_quiz.md`) | Learner completes both; adult scores with [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) and reteaches any objective below criterion. |
| 15 | Culminating assessment (`assessments/unit-assessment.md`) + reflection | Learner completes the assessment; adult and learner review what is solid and what needs revisiting before Unit 03. |
| 16 | Re-teach flex + area-model bridge: 24 × 13 revisited with partial products (direct lead-in to Unit 03) | Learner breaks 24 × 13 into four partial products and names the strategy used; gaps from the assessment get one targeted reteach. |
