## Lesson 01

(1, 24), (2, 12), (3, 8), (4, 6). 48 → (1, 48), (2, 24), (3, 16), (4, 12),

**Guided:** 1. (1, 36), (2, 18), (3, 12), (4, 9), (6, 6) — 5 fails, and 6 × 6
is the square pair that ends the hunt. 2. (1, 48), (2, 24), (3, 16), (4, 12),

listed twice and (4, 3) is the turn-around of (3, 4); the distinct pairs are
(1, 12), (2, 6), (3, 4). 4. 36 (5 pairs) has more than 30 (4 pairs); the
square pair (6, 6) is the extra one.

**Independent:** 5. (1, 40), (2, 20), (4, 10), (5, 8). 6. (1, 25), (5, 5) —
the hunt ends at the square pair. 7. Agree: hunt gives (1, 18), (2, 9),
(3, 6); 4 and 5 fail; 6's partner is 3 < 6, so the pairs repeat from here.
8. 28: (1, 28), (2, 14), (4, 7) — three pairs.

**Application:** rectangles 1 × 28, 2 × 14, 4 × 7. The hunt ends when the
trial row's partner becomes smaller than the row (at 7, the partner is 4 —
the turn-around of (4, 7)).

**Exit:** (1, 16), (2, 8), (4, 4). Stop at the square pair: any further rows
only repeat turned-around pairs.

## Lesson 02

**Guided:** 1. 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42 (4th term 28 = 7 × 4). 2. 72 = 8 × 9 —
yes to both (they are the same partner pair). 3. 36 is a multiple of 1, 2, 3,
**4, 6, 9, 12, 18**, 36. 4. True — 9 × 5 = 45, so 9 is a factor of 45 and 45
is a multiple of 9.

**Independent:** 5. 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40. Also multiples of 2:
10, 20, 30, 40 (the even ones — 2 is a factor of each). 6. 84 = 6 × 14 →
yes; 5 × 16 = 80, 5 × 17 = 85 → no partner → no. 7. 12 and 24 (the common
multiples of 3 and 4 below 30: 12 = 3 × 4 = 4 × 3; 24 = 3 × 8 = 4 × 6).
8. Pairs of 20: (1, 20), (2, 10), (4, 5). One-digit multiples-of check: 20 is
a multiple of 1, 2, 4, 5.

**Application:** multiples of 5 form the 5s column pattern (…, 5, 10, 15, …
all ending in 5 or 0). Every multiple of 10 is 10 × k = 5 × (2k), so it is
automatically a multiple of 5 — 5 is a factor of 10.

**Exit:** "A whole number is a multiple of each of its factors." 96 = 12 × 8
→ yes, 96 is a multiple of 12.

## Lesson 04

**Guided:** 1. 2, 7, 12, 17, 22, 27, 32, 37. 2. Hidden feature: strict
even/odd alternation (E, O, E, O, …). Why: 5 is odd; adding an odd number
flips parity every step, so alternation continues forever. 3. 4, 14, 24, 34,
44, 54, 64. Hidden feature: ones digit always 4; every term even. Unlike
"Add 3," adding an even number never flips parity. 4. Even — from Worked
Example 2, every term from the 2nd on is a multiple of 2.

**Independent:** 5. 1, 5, 9, 13, 17, 21, 25, 29. Hidden feature: all odd.
Explanation: 4 is even; odd + even = odd at every step, so parity never flips
(unlike "Add 3"). 6. 2, 6, 18, 54, 162, 486. Hidden feature: every term after
the first is even (multiplying by 3 keeps an even number even: 3 × even =
even). 7. Agree: 5 is odd, 6 is even, odd + even = odd forever — the terms
must all be odd. 8. Odd. Defense: adding 2 (even) to 7 (odd) gives odd at
every step, so all 50 terms are odd.

**Exit:** 2, 11, 20, 29, 38, 47. Hidden feature: even/odd alternation.
Explanation: 9 is odd, so each step flips parity — the alternation must
continue.

## Lesson 05

**Guided:** 1. Next counts 9, 11. Rule: figure n has 2n − 1 dots (each new
figure adds one dot to each arm). 2. Breaking: (6 × 6) + (6 × 6) = 72.
Doubling/halving: 12 × 6 = 24 × 3 = 72 (double 12, halve 6). 3. 8 × 15 =
4 × 30 = 120 — restack 8 rows of 15 into 4 rows of 30; no tile gained or
lost. 4. Sketch: top rectangle 24 wide × 10 tall (240), bottom 24 × 3 (72);
dimensions labeled.

**Independent:** 5. Figure 7 = 49 dots (25 + 11 + 13 = 49; the gaps are the
odd numbers 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13). 6. (9 × 10) + (9 × 6) = 90 + 54 = 144; and
(9 × 8) + (9 × 8) = 72 + 72 = 144. 7. 14 × 5 = 7 × 10 = 70 (halve 14, double
5); check 14 × 5 = 70 ✓. 8. Figure 5 = 15 dots, figure 6 = 21. Hidden feature:
the gaps grow by 1 each time (2, 3, 4, 5, 6) because figure n adds n dots —
or: the counts alternate in odd/even pairs (1, 3 odd; 6, 10 even; …).

**Application:** (a) (16 × 10) + (16 × 2) = 160 + 32 = 192; (b) 16 × 12 =
8 × 24 = 192; (c) skip-counting 12 sixteen times = 192. All agree at 192;
fastest is usually (b), one halving away from 8 × 24.

**Exit:** 7 × 18 = (7 × 10) + (7 × 8) = 70 + 56 = 126. 12 × 15 = 6 × 30 = 180
(halve 12, double 15).
