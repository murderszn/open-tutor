# Unit Assessment — Unit 02 (culminating)

**Session:** 15 · **Time:** 30 minutes · **Coverage:** objectives 1–5
**Scoring:** see [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment). 10/13
meets; below 10, use the teacher guide's reteach plan before Unit 03.

*Show your work for every item: the hunt, the partner check, the factor list,
or the strategy steps.*

## Factor pairs and multiples

1. List **all** factor pairs of **66**, in order. State the rule that tells
   you when to stop.
2. List **all** factor pairs of **81**. How many factors does 81 have in
   total?
3. True or false, with reasoning: "Every factor of 36 is a number that 36 is
   a multiple of." (Use the Lesson 02 rule sentence.)
4. Determine whether **96** is a multiple of 4, of 5, and of 12. Show each
   partner check.
5. A gardener has 54 tiles and wants exactly 3 equal rows. How many tiles per
   row? Then: list **all** the rectangular patios she could build with 54
   tiles (rows × columns for each).

## Prime and composite

6. Classify each with a one-sentence factor-list justification:
   **41, 57, 62, 73, 100**.
7. **Explain your reasoning:** a classmate says "97 must be composite because
   it's a big number." Use factor evidence to agree or disagree.
8. Run the sieve's first two steps on paper for 1–30 (cross out 1, circle 2,
   cross its multiples, circle 3, cross its multiples). Which numbers below 30
   are still undecided — and which of those are prime?

## Patterns

9. Rule "Add 8", start 5: generate 8 terms. Name a hidden feature and explain
   informally why it continues.
10. The triangle-dot counts are 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, …. Draw figure 6, give its
    count, and name one hidden feature of the counts with an informal
    explanation.

## Multiplication strategies

11. Compute **18 × 4** by doubling/halving. Compute **13 × 6** by breaking
    into known facts. Show both.
12. Sketch the **24 × 13** area model as four small rectangles (split 24 into
    20 + 4 and 13 into 10 + 3). Label every dimension and write the four
    partial products — but do not add them yet. (You will finish this in
    Unit 03.)

## Retrieval

13. Round **48,506** to the nearest thousand, and estimate **19 × 5**.
