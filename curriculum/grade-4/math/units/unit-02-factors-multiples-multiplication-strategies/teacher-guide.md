# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Factors, Multiples, and Multiplication Strategies

**For the guiding adult.** Student pages never include answers; this guide and
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md) stay adult-side. Every solution below was solved
independently and reconciled with the key.

## Session-by-session notes

- **S1 (L01):** the hunt routine is the unit's engine — do not rush it. Have
  the learner *say* "start at 1, stop at the square or the turn-around" before
  every hunt. Display the tile-garden image large; insist the learner reads
  the signs, not the decorative tiles. The SVG
  [`assets/factor-pairs-arrays.svg`](assets/factor-pairs-arrays.svg) shows the
  four exact arrays of 24 (1×24, 2×12, 3×8, 4×6) — print it for the desk.
- **S2 (legacy task):** `assignments/factors-and-multiples.md` — audit
  decision: **keep as-is** (factors of 18, 24, 35, 47 and first-six multiples
  of 6 and 9, all inside the 1–100 standard range; no revision needed). Score
  against [answer-key.md](answer-key.md#legacy-factors-and-multiples).
- **S3 (L02):** drill the rule sentence until it is automatic: "a number is a
  multiple of each of its factors." The 100-chart coloring is the visual
  anchor — keep the chart on the wall all unit.
- **S4 (games):** Factor-Pair War — draw a number card, first to list all
  pairs wins; ties broken by naming the stopping rule. Multiples relay: color
  a row of the 100 chart by skip counting while the adult times.
- **S5 (L03):** run the sieve slowly and ceremonially — crossing out is the
  lesson. After the chart, every classification still needs a factor list as
  evidence; the chart is a tool, not an answer book. Teach the shortcut: test
  only prime factors 2, 3, 5, 7 for numbers ≤ 100.
- **S6 (detective stations):** 12, 17, 21, 29, 36, 51 — build factor lists
  with tiles at each station; sort cards into prime / composite / neither
  bins. 51 is the trap (looks prime).
- **S7 (mixed retrieval):** written set, no new content; every error returns
  to the tile-array hunt.
- **S8 (pattern preview):** "predict the 10th term" with a growing dot row —
  light, no formal write-up; it primes Session 9.
- **S9 (L04):** the hidden feature must be something the rule never stated.
  Accept "it keeps alternating because adding 3 flips odd/even every time" —
  informal causal language is exactly the standard.
- **S10 (L05):** build the 24 × 13 area model with tiles or on graph paper;
  label dimensions only — do NOT compute the total (that is Session 16's
  payoff and Unit 03's launch).
- **S11 (stations + error analysis):** [practice.md](practice.md#e-multiplication-strategies-objective-5)
  item 20 (Mia's 48) is the station centerpiece — plant the same mistake type
  (dropped pair) in one more list for the learner to catch.
- **S12–S13 (project):** the docent talk is the real assessment of objectives
  1–3; the pattern journal entry is the assessment of objective 4. Push past
  "it was surprising" to the evidence.
- **S14:** [practice.md](practice.md) then the formative quiz. Score the
  same day; reteach before S15.
- **S15:** unit assessment + reflection. File the reflection privately —
  never in the repo.
- **S16:** break 24 × 13 into 200 + 60 + 40 + 12 = 312 — the first time the
  learner *finishes* an area model. Name the strategies used. This is the
  direct bridge to Unit 03.

## Misconceptions to watch

| Signal | Likely cause | Reteach move |
|---|---|---|
| Lists factors of 24 as "2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12" (drops 1 and 24) | The 1 × n pair feels "too easy" to count | The hunt *starts* at 1, every time — lay the 1-row tile strip first |
| Counts (2, 12) and (12, 2) as different pairs | Treats order as meaningful | Turn the tile rectangle 90° — same rectangle; count distinct pairs once |
| "Multiples are smaller than the number" | Confuses factors with multiples | Factors are ingredients (bounded); multiples are products (unbounded). Chart it |
| "91 is prime — it ends in 1" / "51 looks prime" | Judges by appearance or memory | Only the factor list decides; hunt partners for 2, 3, 5, 7 |
| Calls 1 prime | "Its factors are 1 and itself" | Stress *exactly two different* factors; 1 has one |
| Sieve memorized as "the prime list" | Chart treated as answer book | After the chart, every classification still cites a factor list |
| States the rule back as the "hidden feature" | Doesn't separate rule from observation | Hidden feature = visible only *after* generating (parity, digits) |
| Explanation describes but doesn't cause ("it alternates because it alternates") | Stops at the observation | Ask "what in the rule *makes* it happen?" until the causal step appears |
| 6 × 14 = (6 × 10) × (6 × 4) | Add/multiply confusion on splits | Tile cut: pieces sit side by side → add. "Sum-split → add; product-split → multiply" |
| Doubling/halving "changes the answer" | Doesn't trust the shortcut | Count all tiles before and after one restack, then trust it |

## Reteach moves

- **Objective 1 (factor pairs):** back down to numbers ≤ 20 with real tiles;
  narrate the hunt aloud every time; reintroduce the stopping rule only when
  turn-around pairs start appearing naturally.
- **Objective 2 (multiples):** skip-counting only, no factor talk, for a full
  session; then show that every factor pair is a "multiple certificate."
- **Objective 3 (prime/composite):** shrink to numbers ≤ 30; always write the
  factor list before the verdict; the 2-3-5-7 partner check is the whole test.
- **Objective 4 (patterns):** generate-only sessions first (just follow rules);
  then add "color the odds and evens" as the inspection step; explanations
  come last.
- **Objective 5 (strategies):** stay with breaking into known facts until the
  tile cut is obvious; doubling/halving only after the restack is trusted.

## Readiness signals

Ready for Unit 03 (multi-digit multiplication and division) when the learner:
lists all factor pairs of any number ≤ 100 with the stopping rule; states the
factor↔multiple rule with an example; classifies prime/composite from factor
lists; generates a pattern, names a hidden feature, and gives a causal
explanation; and breaks a product like 14 × 6 into two known facts with the
tile cut. The project rubric score of 7+ and quiz 8+ together are the formal
bar.
