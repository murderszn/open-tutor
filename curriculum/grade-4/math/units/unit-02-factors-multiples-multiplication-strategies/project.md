# Project — Factor Garden Exhibition

**Sessions:** 12–13 · **Time:** two 35-minute sessions + optional finishing time
**Type:** investigation with a real deliverable

You are the curator of the Factor Garden Exhibition. Your job: take the four
sign numbers from the Factor Tile Garden —
[`assets/factor-tile-garden.png`](assets/factor-tile-garden.png) (or the
text-only list below) — and turn them into an exhibition poster that teaches
visitors everything the garden knows: every array, every factor pair, which
numbers are prime or composite, and one pattern the garden grows by.

> **All sign amounts are fictional practice data** created for this project —
> not counts from a real garden. Label them "practice data" on your poster.

## Materials

Poster paper or two taped sheets, pencil, crayons or markers, ruler, tiles
for building (optional), the tile-garden illustration (or its text-only
alternative: **36, 24, 48, 30**).

## Steps

**Day 1 — Build the exhibits (Session 12)**

1. For each of the four sign numbers (36, 24, 48, 30), draw **every**
   rectangular array you can build — one rectangle per factor pair, labeled
   with rows × columns. Arrange them so a visitor can see that (2, 12) and
   (12, 2) are the same rectangle turned, not two different arrays.
2. Under each number, write its **complete factor list** in order, then
   classify it: prime, composite, or neither — with a one-sentence
   justification quoting the definition ("exactly two factors" / "more than
   two factors").
3. Add a **"How to hunt" panel**: write the Lesson 01 hunt routine in your own
   words (start at 1; stop at the square or the turn-around) so a visitor can
   check your work.
4. Label the whole poster "practice data — fictional sign amounts."

**Day 2 — The pattern journal and the opening (Session 13)**

5. **Pattern journal entry:** the garden grows by the rule "Add 4, start 2"
   (2, 6, 10, 14, … — new beds each season). Generate 8 terms, identify one
   hidden feature, and write the informal explanation. Glue or tape this entry
   to the poster as the fifth exhibit.
6. **Docent talk:** teach one factor-pair hunt to the adult — pick the number
   you found trickiest (36 or 48), build it with tiles while you talk, and
   answer one "why" question from your visitor.
7. **Curator's note** (3–4 sentences): which number surprised you most and
   why? What does the exhibition prove about how factors and multiples are
   related?

## Deliverables

1. Exhibition poster: four array exhibits (all factor pairs drawn and
   labeled), complete ordered factor lists, prime/composite verdicts with
   justifications, the "How to hunt" panel, and the "practice data" label.
2. The pattern journal entry (rule "Add 4, start 2": 8 terms, hidden feature,
   informal explanation).
3. The curator's note (3–4 sentences).

## Rubric

| Criterion | Getting started (1) | On track (2) | Strong (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Array completeness** | One or more factor pairs missing or mislabeled (rows × columns wrong) | All pairs present but two turned-around duplicates counted as different | Every factor pair of all four numbers drawn once, labeled rows × columns, duplicates recognized as the same rectangle |
| **Factor lists & classification** | Lists missing factors or missing the number itself; verdict without justification | All four lists complete and ordered; verdicts correct but justifications vague | All four lists complete and ordered; every verdict justified with the "exactly two / more than two factors" definition quoted |
| **Pattern journal entry** | Terms generated with errors or no hidden feature named | 8 correct terms and a hidden feature, but the explanation only describes | 8 correct terms; hidden feature named AND informally explained (cause, not description) |
| **Curator's note & docent talk** | Note is a verdict without evidence; talk skips the "why" | Note cites evidence; talk covers the hunt but not the stopping rule | Note names the surprise with evidence and states the factor↔multiple relationship; talk includes the square/turn-around stopping rule |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = exceeds; 7–9 = meets (ready for Unit 03); 4–6 = revise
one criterion with the adult and resubmit; below 4 = reteach factor-pair
hunting first (see [teacher-guide.md](teacher-guide.md#reteach-moves)).

## Accessibility and safety

No special materials; the text-only alternative (36, 24, 48, 30) carries every
number in the illustration. The learner may dictate the curator's note while
the adult scribes, and may build arrays with tiles instead of drawing them.
All data is fictional — no real counts, names, or places.
