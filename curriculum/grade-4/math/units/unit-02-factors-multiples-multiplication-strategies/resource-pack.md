# Resource Pack — Unit 02: Factors, Multiples, and Multiplication Strategies

**Goal:** support an adult teaching a grade-4 learner to find all factor pairs
1–100 with arrays, name the factor↔multiple relationship, classify prime vs.
composite from factor lists, generate and explain patterns from rules, and use
factor pairs as multiplication strategies. **Age fit:** grade 4 (ages 9–10);
adult-led, 35-minute sessions.

All external links were checked on **2026-10-17**. Direct pages were opened
and read; video links are clearly labeled preferred-channel **search links**
(the adult previews before showing). The Khan Academy unit page loaded a
client-side challenge in the text reader, so it is listed with an honest
caveat. Every core objective has a free, no-account route.

## Web references (verified 2026-10-17)

1. **Common Core State Standards — Grade 4, Operations & Algebraic Thinking**
   `https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/4/OA/`
   Official source for the 4.OA.B.4 and 4.OA.C.5 codes used in this unit.
   Opened and read 2026-10-17: the page renders 4.OA.B.4 (factor pairs,
   factor↔multiple relationship, prime/composite for 1–100) and 4.OA.C.5
   (patterns from a rule, including the official "Add 3" odd/even-alternation
   example). Adult reference; no account needed.
2. **Math Is Fun — Factors and Multiples**
   `https://MATHSISFUN.COM/numbers/factors-multiples.html`
   Kid-friendly definitions with worked examples (factors of 12; multiples of
   3 and 5 with number lines; factors finite vs. multiples infinite). Opened
   and read 2026-10-17; free, no account. Suitable for the adult to read
   aloud with the learner.
3. **The Math Learning Center — free math apps**
   `https://www.mathlearningcenter.org/apps`
   Free web apps, no account: **Partial Product Finder** ("multiplication
   combinations represented as a rectangle, or array, with dimensions that
   match") for Lessons 01/05 and the 24 × 13 area preview; **Number Frames**
   and **Number Line** for the 100-chart multiples work and pattern
   sequences. Opened 2026-10-17; confirmed free and browser-based.
4. **Cuemath — How to tell if a number is prime or composite**
   `https://www.cuemath.com/questions/how-to-tell-if-a-number-is-prime-or-composite/`
   Short worked explanation: prime = exactly two factors (1 and itself),
   composite = more than two, with the 6-vs-7 example. Opened and read
   2026-10-17; free, no account. Learner-readable with adult support.
5. **Khan Academy — Factors, multiples and patterns (4th grade unit)**
   `https://en.khanacademy.org/math/cc-fourth-grade-math/imp-factors-multiples-and-patterns/imp-factors-and-multiples`
   Khan Academy's own 4th-grade unit on factor pairs, prime/composite, and
   number/shape patterns — the same arc as this unit. Opened 2026-10-17, but
   the page rendered a client-side loading challenge in the text reader, so
   content could not be read directly; the adult should open it in a real
   browser to confirm fit before assigning. Free with optional account.
6. **Repository: `resources/math_fundamentals.md`** — adult/teacher-side
   reference only (its band is grades 4–7, in-band for this unit per the track
   audit): consult its Place Value section when writing reteach plans. Never
   assigned to the learner whole.
7. **Repository: generated illustration
   `assets/factor-tile-garden.png`** — the Session 1 / Lesson 01 tile-garden
   activity image (AI illustration; sign amounts are fictional practice data;
   drawn tiles are decorative).
8. **Repository: unit SVG `assets/factor-pairs-arrays.svg`** — the exact
   20px-cell arrays of 24 (1×24, 2×12, 3×8, 4×6); print it for the learner's
   desk.
