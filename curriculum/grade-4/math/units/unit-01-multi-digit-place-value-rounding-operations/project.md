# Project — Market Day Newspaper

**Sessions:** 13–14 · **Time:** two 35-minute sessions + optional finishing time
**Type:** investigation with a real deliverable

You are the lead reporter for the Market Day Newspaper. The market stall in
[`assets/estimation-market-scene.png`](assets/estimation-market-scene.png) had a
big day, and your job is to turn its sign numbers into a front page: a headline
with friendly rounded numbers, a data table with exact numbers, and a short
piece explaining how you checked your math.

> **All stall amounts are fictional practice data** created for this project —
> not counts from a real market. Label them "practice data" on your newspaper.

## Materials

Paper or a folded "newspaper" sheet, pencil, crayons or markers, the market
illustration (or its text-only alternative: 4,786 APPLES, 2,395 PEARS, 9,965
VISITORS, 12,438 SEEDS), a ruler for the table.

## Steps

**Day 1 — Report the numbers (Session 13)**

1. Copy the four stall amounts into a data table with two columns: *Stall* and
   *Exact count (practice data)*.
2. Round each amount to the nearest **thousand** and add a third column:
   *Headline number*. Check each rounding against the illustration's panel —
   do you agree with all four?
3. Write your **headline**: one big line using the rounded numbers and their
   estimated total (e.g., "About 29,000 at Market Day!"). Headlines use
   rounded numbers because they are easy to read fast.
4. Invent a **fifth stall** of your own (a fictional amount under 100,000 —
   name the goods and the count). Add it to the table, round it, and update
   your headline total.

**Day 2 — Check the math (Session 14)**

5. **Estimate first:** add your five rounded headline numbers for an estimated
   grand total.
6. **Compute exactly:** add the five exact counts with the standard algorithm.
   Show the column work (attach a second sheet if needed).
7. Build an **"Estimate vs. Exact" box**: show the estimate, the exact total,
   and the difference between them.
8. **Differences desk:** pick two stalls and compute exactly how many more one
   had than the other (estimate first, subtract, check with addition).
9. Write a 3–4 sentence **reporter's note** answering: *Was my estimate
   reasonable? How do I know? Which rounding place would give a closer
   estimate, and when would the rougher one be fine?*

## Deliverables

1. A one-page newspaper front: headline, data table (exact + rounded columns,
   "practice data" label), Estimate-vs.-Exact box, and your fifth stall.
2. Shown computation: the exact addition column work and the exact subtraction
   with its addition check.
3. The reporter's note (3–4 sentences).

## Rubric

| Criterion | Getting started (1) | On track (2) | Strong (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rounding accuracy** | Two or more rounding errors in the headline column | One rounding slip, or a correct column with no checking shown | All five amounts rounded correctly to the nearest thousand; illustration's four verified |
| **Exact computation** | Addition/subtraction has column or regrouping errors; no estimate shown | Correct totals with estimate-first shown for one operation | Both exact totals correct with estimate-first, column work, and the addition check visible |
| **Newspaper completeness** | Missing headline, table, or estimate box | All parts present but the "practice data" label or fifth stall is missing | Headline, labeled table, Estimate-vs.-Exact box, and fifth stall all present and readable |
| **Reporter's note** | Verdict without evidence ("it was reasonable") | Verdict with the estimate–exact difference cited | Verdict with the difference cited AND a sensible comment on rounding-place choice |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = exceeds; 7–9 = meets (ready for Unit 02); 4–6 = revise one
criterion with the adult and resubmit; below 4 = reteach rounding or regrouping
first (see [teacher-guide.md](teacher-guide.md#reteach-moves)).

## Accessibility and safety

No special materials; the text-only alternative above carries every number in
the illustration. The learner may dictate the reporter's note while the adult
scribes. All data is fictional — no real counts, names, or places.
