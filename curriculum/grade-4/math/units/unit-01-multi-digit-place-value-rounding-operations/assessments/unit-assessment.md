# Unit Assessment — Unit 01: Multi-Digit Place Value, Rounding, and Operations

**Session:** 16 · **Time:** 30 minutes · **Coverage:** objectives 1–5
**Scoring:** 2 points per item (1 for a correct answer, 1 for shown reasoning
or work) = 24 points. 19+ meets; 15–18 approaching (reteach missed objectives);
below 15, reteach before Unit 02. Full solutions in
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment).

*For computations: estimate first, compute exactly, then a one-word verdict.*

1. In 66,000 there are two 6s. What is each one worth? Explain how they are
   related using the ten-times rule.
2. Write "seven hundred twenty thousand, three hundred nine" as a numeral and
   in expanded form.
3. Order from greatest to least: 505,505; 550,055; 505,055. Show the
   place-by-place reasoning.
4. Round 847,362 to the nearest **ten thousand** and to the nearest **hundred
   thousand**. Show the deciding digit for each.
5. Round 99,750 to the nearest thousand. What is special about this rounding?
6. 234,567 + 185,439 = ? Estimate first, then compute with the standard
   algorithm.
7. 500,200 − 275,864 = ? Estimate first, compute, and check with addition.
8. A bookstore orders 12,438 notebooks and 9,965 pens. Estimate the total by
   rounding to the nearest thousand, then find the exact total. Was your
   estimate reasonable? How do you know?
9. A marathon has 46,207 registered runners; 38,865 finish the race. How many
   did not finish? Estimate first, then compute exactly.
10. **Error analysis:** "Priya computed 4,762 + 3,485 = 7,247." Find her
    regrouping mistake, explain it, and give the correct sum.
11. **Explain your reasoning:** when is rounding to the nearest ten thousand a
    better choice than rounding to the nearest hundred? Give an example with a
    number over 100,000 and defend your choice.
12. **Reflection:** which strategy helps you most when subtracting across
    zeros? Describe it in your own words and show where you use it in your
    answer to item 7.
