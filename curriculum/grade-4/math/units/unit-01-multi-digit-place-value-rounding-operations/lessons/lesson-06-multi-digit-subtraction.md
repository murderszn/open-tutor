# Lesson 06 — Multi-Digit Subtraction with the Standard Algorithm

**Session:** 11 · **Time:** 35 minutes · **Goal:** I can subtract multi-digit
whole numbers fluently with the standard algorithm — including ungrouping
across zeros — and check my answer by adding.

**Materials:** paper and pencil, place-value chart for reference.

## Readiness check (4 min)

"7,205 − 3,468 — estimate first." (7,000 − 3,000 = 4,000.) "Which column will
need ungrouping first?" (The ones: 5 < 8.) If ungrouping within 10,000 is
shaky, re-anchor with one small subtraction before the zero-chain work.

## Explicit teaching (10 min)

The subtraction standard algorithm mirrors addition: line up by place, work
right to left. When the top digit is smaller than the bottom digit,
**ungroup** (borrow): take 1 from the next place left (worth ten of the
current place), add those ten to the current digit, then subtract.

Across zeros, the ungrouping chains left until it finds a nonzero digit:

> **The zero-chain trick:** 600,000 − 47,385. Borrow from the 6: the zeros
> between become 9s, and the ones column gets 10. Think "5 9 9 9 9 10" across
> the top, then subtract each column.

Always **check a subtraction with addition**: difference + subtracted number
must equal the starting number. This is the inverse-operation check, and it
catches nearly every ungrouping slip.

## Worked example 1 — 7,205 − 3,468

1. Estimate: 7,000 − 3,000 = 4,000.
2. Ones: 5 < 8 → ungroup. Tens digit is 0, so chain to the hundreds: hundreds
   2 → 1, tens → 10; then tens 10 → 9, ones → 15. Now 15 − 8 = 7.
3. Tens: 9 − 6 = 3. Hundreds: 1 < 4 → ungroup from thousands: thousands 7 → 6,
   hundreds → 11. 11 − 4 = 7. Thousands: 6 − 3 = 3.
4. Result: 3,737. Check with addition: 3,737 + 3,468 = 7,205. ✓ Reasonable
   against the estimate. ✓

## Worked example 2 — 600,000 − 47,385 (across zeros)

1. Estimate to the nearest ten thousand: 600,000 − 50,000 = 550,000.
2. Zero-chain: borrow from the 6 → top row becomes 5 9 9 9 9 10.
3. Subtract column by column: ones 10 − 5 = 5; tens 9 − 8 = 1; hundreds 9 − 3 =
   6; thousands 9 − 7 = 2; ten-thousands 9 − 4 = 5; hundred-thousands 5 − 0 = 5.
4. Result: 552,615. Check: 552,615 + 47,385 = 600,000. ✓ Near the estimate. ✓
5. Common slip: writing 553,615 — forgetting to reduce one of the chained 9s
   (usually the thousands). The addition check exposes it instantly.

## Guided practice (with hints)

Estimate first for each, then compute and check with addition.

1. **52,010 − 28,476** *(Hint: the ones need a borrow and the tens are 0 —
   chain from the hundreds.)*
2. **81,436 − 57,289** *(Hint: four of the five columns need ungrouping. Slow
   is smooth; smooth is fast.)*
3. **Check this:** is 8,005 − 3,468 = 4,537 correct? Add to verify, and show
   your work. *(Hint: the check is the assignment.)*

## Independent practice

4. **400,206 − 158,977** — estimate, compute, add-to-check.
5. **93,105 − 74,856** — estimate, compute, add-to-check.
6. Word problem: The school library holds 52,010 books; 28,476 are checked
   out. How many remain on the shelves? Estimate first, then compute exactly.
7. **Find the error:** "600,000 − 47,385 = 553,615." Explain the zero-chain
   mistake and give the correct difference.

## Application

Change-maker: the adult names a fictional starting amount (e.g., 400,206
stickers in a warehouse) and a shipment sent out (158,977). The learner plays
inventory manager: estimate the remaining stock for the morning report, compute
exactly for the ledger, and write one sentence explaining why the estimate was
good enough for the report but the exact count matters for the ledger.

## Exit check

Compute 9,320 − 5,147. Show the estimate, the column work, and the
addition check.

## Supports

- **Reading/language access:** narrate borrows aloud ("hundreds 2 becomes 1,
  tens become 10"); let the learner echo each chain.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may mark the "9 9 9 10" chain above
  the problem in color before subtracting.
- **Extension:** 1,000,000 − 1 = ? Then 1,000,000 − 999,999 = ? Explain the
  pattern (ungrouping across a full zero chain).

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: subtracting the smaller digit from the larger regardless
of top/bottom (e.g., 5 − 8 answered as 3 without ungrouping). Reteach: "we can
only subtract down a column when the top can give." Also watch learners who
borrow but forget to reduce the lending digit. Answer-key reference:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-06).
