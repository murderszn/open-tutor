# Lesson 05 — Multi-Digit Addition with the Standard Algorithm

**Session:** 9 · **Time:** 35 minutes · **Goal:** I can add multi-digit whole
numbers fluently with the standard algorithm, regrouping as needed, and check
my answer with an estimate.

**Materials:** paper and pencil, place-value chart for reference.

## Readiness check (4 min)

"2,486 + 3,759 — estimate first, then compute." (Estimate 2,000 + 4,000 =
6,000; exact 6,245.) If regrouping within 10,000 is shaky, do one more small
addition before the six-digit work.

## Explicit teaching (10 min)

The **standard algorithm** for addition lines numbers up by place and works one
column at a time, right to left:

1. Line up the ones under the ones (the right edge is the anchor).
2. Add each column. When a column totals 10 or more, write the ones digit and
   **regroup** the ten to the next column left (the small carried 1).
3. Keep the carried digits small and written above the next column — never in
   your head only.
4. **Estimate first, compute second:** round both addends, estimate the sum,
   then compare. If the exact sum is far from the estimate, recheck each
   column.

Regrouping is just the ten-times rule in action: 10 ones become 1 ten, 10 tens
become 1 hundred, and so on.

## Worked example 1 — 4,762 + 3,485

1. Estimate: 5,000 + 3,000 = 8,000.
2. Line up right edges:
   ```
       4,762
     + 3,485
   ```
3. Ones: 2 + 5 = 7. Tens: 6 + 8 = 14 → write 4, carry 1. Hundreds: 7 + 4 + 1 =
   12 → write 2, carry 1. Thousands: 4 + 3 + 1 = 8.
4. Result: 8,247. Compare with the estimate 8,000 — reasonable. ✓

## Worked example 2 — 356,209 + 187,644 (a carry chain)

1. Estimate to the nearest thousand: 356,000 + 188,000 = 544,000.
2. Column by column, right to left:
   - Ones: 9 + 4 = 13 → 3, carry 1
   - Tens: 0 + 4 + 1 = 5
   - Hundreds: 2 + 6 = 8
   - Thousands: 6 + 7 = 13 → 3, carry 1
   - Ten-thousands: 5 + 8 + 1 = 14 → 4, carry 1
   - Hundred-thousands: 3 + 1 + 1 = 5
3. Result: 543,853. Near the estimate 544,000 — reasonable. ✓
4. Common slip: forgetting a carried 1 mid-chain (especially the thousands
   column here). Writing every carry defeats this.

## Guided practice (with hints)

Estimate first for each, then compute.

1. **28,416 + 35,902** *(Hint: estimate 28,000 + 36,000; watch the hundreds
   column — it regroups.)*
2. **46,207 + 38,865** *(Hint: the zero in the tens place still takes the
   carry.)*
3. **905,318 + 76,295** *(Hint: line up the right edges — the numbers have
   different digit counts.)*

## Independent practice

4. **712,546 + 288,354** — estimate, compute, verdict.
5. **59,847 + 23,168** — estimate, compute, verdict.
6. Word problem: A library has 12,438 picture books and 9,965 chapter books.
   Estimate the total, then find it exactly. Reasonable?
7. **Find the error:** "7,205 + 3,468 = 9,673." Is this right? If not, explain
   the regrouping mistake and give the correct sum.

## Application

Receipt check: the adult writes a "store receipt" with three fictional
multi-digit amounts (e.g., 4,786 + 2,395 + 9,965). The learner estimates the
total two ways (nearest thousand, nearest hundred), computes exactly, and
decides which estimate was closer and whether the extra precision was worth
the effort.

## Exit check

Compute 6,318 + 2,047. Show the estimate, the column work with carries, and a
one-word verdict (reasonable / not reasonable).

## Supports

- **Reading/language access:** narrate columns aloud ("ones: 9 plus 4 is 13,
  write 3, carry 1 ten"); let the learner echo.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may use a place-value chart with
  digit cards to regroup physically before writing.
- **Extension:** add three addends: 46,207 + 38,865 + 12,438. What changes
  about carrying? (A column can total 20+; carry the tens digit.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: misaligned columns when addends have different digit
counts (lining up left edges instead of right). Reteach: "the ones column is
the anchor — always line up the right edge." Also watch carried 1s written
inside the answer row. Answer-key reference:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-05).
