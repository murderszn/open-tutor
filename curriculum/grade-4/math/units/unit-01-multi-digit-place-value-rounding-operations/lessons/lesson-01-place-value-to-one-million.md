# Lesson 01 — Place Value to One Million: The Ten-Times Rule

**Session:** 1 · **Time:** 35 minutes · **Goal:** I can explain that each place
in a multi-digit number is worth ten times the place to its right, and use that
idea to read the value of any digit up to the millions place.

**Materials:** place-value chart (ones through millions), digit cards 0–9,
paper and pencil.

## Readiness check (4 min)

Ask aloud: "In 345, what is the 4 worth?" (40 — four tens.) "What is the 3
worth?" (300.) "How many times bigger is 300 than 30?" (Ten times.) If the
learner hesitates on three-digit values, re-anchor with base-ten blocks for a
few minutes before the new millions content.

## Explicit teaching (10 min)

A **digit** is one of 0–9. A digit's **value** depends on its **place** — where
it sits in the number. Here is the rule that builds every place:

> **Each place is worth ten times the place to its right.**

Ones → tens → hundreds → thousands → ten-thousands → hundred-thousands →
millions. Moving one step left multiplies the value by 10; moving one step
right divides it by 10.

Places come in **periods** of three: the ones period (ones, tens, hundreds), the
thousands period (thousands, ten-thousands, hundred-thousands), and the
millions period. We say the period name once when we read: 246,531 is "two
hundred forty-six **thousand**, five hundred thirty-one."

The standard's own example: **700 ÷ 70 = 10**. Why? 700 is the same as 70 tens,
and 70 is 7 tens. So 700 ÷ 70 asks "how many 7-tens fit in 70 tens?" — ten of
them. The 7 in the hundreds place is worth ten times the 7 in the tens place.

## Worked example 1 — The two 5s in 55,000

1. Write 55,000 in the place-value chart: 5 in the ten-thousands place, 5 in
   the thousands place.
2. The left 5 is worth 50,000. The right 5 is worth 5,000.
3. 50,000 = 10 × 5,000. The ten-thousands place is ten times the thousands
   place — exactly the rule.
4. Check by division: 50,000 ÷ 5,000 = 10. ✓

## Worked example 2 — Build 731,208 in the chart

1. Read the periods: "seven hundred thirty-one **thousand**, two hundred eight."
2. Place each digit: 7 → hundred-thousands, 3 → ten-thousands, 1 → thousands,
   2 → hundreds, 0 → tens, 8 → ones.
3. The 0 in the tens place holds its spot — without it, the 2 and 8 would slide
   into the wrong places.
4. Value check: 7 is worth 700,000, which is ten times what a 7 in the
   ten-thousands place would be worth (70,000). ✓

Common slip: reading 731,208 as "seven hundred thirty-one million." The comma
groups periods — only one comma here, so the left group is thousands, not
millions.

## Guided practice (with hints)

Work through these together; the adult gives the hint, the learner does the
writing.

1. In 68,420, the digit 8 is in the thousands place and is worth 8,000. How
   many times greater is that than what an 8 in the hundreds place would be
   worth (800)? *(Hint: divide 8,000 by 800 — or just use the rule.)*
2. What is 10 × 30,000? *(Hint: move the 3 one place left in your head.)*
3. Explain 700 ÷ 70 = 10 using tens. *(Hint: rewrite both numbers as "how many
   tens.")*
4. In 45,500, compare the two 5s: what is each worth, and how are they related?
   *(Hint: name the place of each 5 first.)*

## Independent practice

5. In 507,300, the digit 7 is in the thousands place. What is it worth? What
   would a 7 in the hundreds place be worth? How many times greater is 7,000
   than 700?
6. Compute 100 × 6,000. Explain with the ten-times rule.
7. Which is greater: the value of the 9 in 19,402 or the value of the 9 in
   91,240? By how many times?
8. Build 604,017 in a place-value chart. List each digit with its place name
   and value.

## Application

Digit-card challenge: draw six digit cards and arrange them to make the largest
possible number, then the smallest possible number (a leading zero is not
allowed for the smallest — why?). Write both in a place-value chart and
explain, using the ten-times rule, why the largest arrangement wins.

## Exit check

In one or two sentences, explain why 40,000 is ten times 4,000.

## Supports

- **Reading/language access:** read all numbers aloud together before writing;
  point to each period as you say its name.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may answer by pointing at chart
  columns and moving digit cards instead of writing.
- **Extension:** how many times greater is the millions place than the
  thousands place? (1,000 times — ten, three steps: 10 × 10 × 10.) Prove it
  with a chain of multiplications.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: "a bigger digit always means a bigger value" (e.g.,
thinking the 9 in 19,402 beats the 4 in 46,207 — place beats face value).
Reteach: cover all but one column and compare column-by-column from the left.
