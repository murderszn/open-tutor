# Practice — Unit 01 Mixed Practice Set

**Use:** Session 15 mixed review (and any reteach session). Work the estimate
first, then the exact answer, then a one-word verdict. All solutions are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md#practice); do not copy them here.

## A. Place value (objectives 1–2)

1. In 246,531, what is the 6 worth? The 4 is worth 40,000 — how many times the
   value of the digit to its right is that?
2. Write 720,309 in words and in expanded form.
3. Compare with `>`, `=`, or `<`: 408,615 ○ 406,815. Explain with places.
4. True or false: in 55,000, the left 5 is worth ten times the right 5.
   Explain.

## B. Rounding (objective 3)

5. Round 56,789 to the nearest thousand. Show the digit that decides.
6. Round 123,456 to the nearest ten thousand. Show the benchmarks.
7. Round 99,750 to the nearest thousand. What is special about this one?
8. A number rounded to the nearest ten thousand is 850,000. Name two different
   numbers that could round to it, and explain how you know.

## C. Addition and subtraction (objective 4)

9. 45,678 + 27,345 — estimate, compute, add-to-… (no check needed for
   addition; compare with the estimate).
10. 80,000 − 36,254 — estimate, compute, check with addition.
11. 234,567 + 185,439 — estimate, compute, verdict.
12. 500,200 − 275,864 — estimate, compute, check with addition.

## D. Estimation and reasonableness (objective 5)

13. A classmate says 62,410 + 38,295 = 100,705. Estimate to the nearest
    thousand and give a verdict — then confirm by computing exactly.
14. **Error analysis:** "9,965 rounded to the nearest hundred is 9,900." Find
    the mistake, explain it, and give the correct rounding.
15. Which estimate is closer to the exact sum 4,786 + 2,395: rounding each
    addend to the nearest thousand, or to the nearest hundred? Show both and
    compare the errors.
16. **Explain your reasoning:** when is rounding to the nearest ten thousand a
    better choice than rounding to the nearest hundred? Give an example with a
    number over 100,000.
