# Unit 01 — Multi-Digit Place Value, Rounding, and Operations

**Grade:** 4 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #22](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/22), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Big numbers stop being scary when you know how they are built, when they are
"close enough," and how to combine them without losing track. In this unit the
learner generalizes place value to 1,000,000 — each place worth ten times the
place to its right — reads, writes, and compares multi-digit numbers in numeral,
number-name, and expanded form, rounds to any place using number-line
benchmarks, adds and subtracts multi-digit whole numbers fluently with the
standard algorithm, and uses rounding to estimate and judge whether an exact
answer is reasonable.

## Essential question

How do place value, rounding, and estimation let us work confidently — and
check our work — with numbers up to one million?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the grade-3 track:

- Three-digit place value to 1000 (hundreds/tens/ones, expanded form);
  rounding to the nearest 10 or 100; fluent addition/subtraction within 1000
- Multiplication and division facts within 100; two-step word problems with a
  letter for the unknown
- Fractions as numbers on number lines (used only as a familiar "between"
  idea when rounding)

The diagnostic re-teaches any insecure grade-3 skill in this unit's first two
sessions; Lesson 01 re-anchors place-value grouping before new instruction.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Explain the ten-times relationship: in a multi-digit whole number, a digit
   in one place represents ten times what it represents in the place to its
   right (e.g., explain 700 ÷ 70 = 10 with place-value reasoning). (4.NBT.A.1)
2. Read, write, and compare whole numbers to 1,000,000 using base-ten numerals,
   number names, and expanded form; record comparisons with `>`, `=`, `<`.
   (4.NBT.A.2)
3. Round multi-digit whole numbers to any place, using number-line benchmarks
   and the halfway rule, and explain each rounding decision. (4.NBT.A.3)
4. Fluently add and subtract multi-digit whole numbers using the standard
   algorithm, regrouping and ungrouping across places (including across zeros);
   check a subtraction with addition. (4.NBT.B.4)
5. Round to estimate sums and differences, and use the estimate to judge
   whether an exact answer is reasonable.

## Standards and source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**, grade 4,
as published at [thecorestandards.org/Math](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/).
The [Grade 4 Number & Operations in Base Ten](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/4/NBT/)
domain page was opened 2026-10-10; it renders 4.NBT.A.1 (with the 700 ÷ 70 = 10
example), 4.NBT.A.2, 4.NBT.B.5, and 4.NBT.B.6, and carries the footnote that
grade-4 expectations in this domain are limited to whole numbers ≤ 1,000,000.
The 4.NBT.A.3 (rounding) and 4.NBT.B.4 (fluency) official wordings were
corroborated during the track A00 audit (2026-10-03) against a verbatim
standards reproduction. No state adoption or accreditation is claimed.
Descriptions below are paraphrases of the official expectations.

| Unit objective | Standard | What the standard expects (paraphrase) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4.NBT.A.1 | A digit in one place represents ten times what it represents in the place to its right. |
| 2 | 4.NBT.A.2 | Read and write multi-digit whole numbers with numerals, number names, and expanded form; compare two multi-digit numbers by the meanings of the digits in each place, using `>`, `=`, `<`. |
| 3 | 4.NBT.A.3 | Use place-value understanding to round multi-digit whole numbers to any place. |
| 4 | 4.NBT.B.4 | Fluently add and subtract multi-digit whole numbers using the standard algorithm. |
| 5 | — | Estimation/reasonableness thread supporting 4.NBT.B.4 and the track's problem-solving objectives. |

**Rounding convention used in this unit:** when a number is exactly halfway
between two round numbers, it rounds **up** (round half up: 67,500 → 68,000 to
the nearest thousand; 45,000 → 50,000 to the nearest ten thousand). This matches
the common U.S. elementary convention; halfway cases are taught explicitly.

## 16-session pacing (4 weeks × 4 sessions, 35 minutes each)

Sessions blend core lessons, guided practice, fluency games, the project, and
assessment. Each session names its activity and success criterion.

**Week 3 — Place value to one million**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lesson 01** — Place value to one million: the ten-times rule | Learner explains, with a place-value chart, why each place is ten times the place to its right, and shows 700 ÷ 70 = 10 with tens. |
| 2 | Fluency game: Place-Value War + "build the largest number" with digit cards | Learner names each digit's place and value without hesitation; justifies which of two builds is larger. |
| 3 | **Lesson 02** — Reading, writing, comparing to 1,000,000 | Learner writes a dictated six-digit number three ways (numeral, name, expanded form) and compares two numbers place-by-place from the left. |
| 4 | Guided practice: ordering stations (task cards: order four numbers; spot the zero-holder) | Learner orders sets correctly and explains what a 0 holds in place. |

**Week 4 — Rounding to any place; estimation**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | **Lesson 03** — Rounding to any place (benchmarks, halfway, digit rule) | Learner rounds to a named place and points to the benchmark number line to justify the decision. |
| 6 | Session task: legacy `assignments/rounding-place-value.md` (re-audited; all items inside the 1,000,000 range) | Learner completes the organizer; adult checks against the unit answer key. |
| 7 | **Lesson 04** — Estimating with rounding (market image activity) | Learner verifies the market illustration's estimate, computes the exact total, and judges reasonableness. |
| 8 | Estimation challenges: estimate-then-reveal pairs; number-talk discussion | Learner chooses a sensible rounding place and defends it ("nearest thousand was enough because…"). |

**Week 5 — Multi-digit addition and subtraction**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | **Lesson 05** — Multi-digit addition, standard algorithm | Learner adds two 5–6-digit numbers with regrouping, narrating each column, and checks with an estimate. |
| 10 | Practice: legacy `assignments/multi-digit-math-workshop.md` addition items (key separated in this unit) | Learner completes the addition set; every error is re-solved with the estimate-first routine. |
| 11 | **Lesson 06** — Multi-digit subtraction, standard algorithm (incl. across zeros) | Learner subtracts across zeros using the borrow-chain, narrating, and checks with addition. |
| 12 | Practice: legacy workshop subtraction items; error-analysis pairs | Learner finds a planted regrouping error and explains the fix. |

**Week 6 — Project, review, assessment**

| # | Session | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | Project day 1: Market Day Newspaper — data table, headline rounding, new stall | Learner records the four stall amounts (labeled fictional) and drafts a rounded headline. |
| 14 | Project day 2: exact totals, estimate-vs-exact box, written reflection | Learner completes the newspaper front; reflection answers "was my estimate reasonable, and how do I know?" |
| 15 | Mixed practice ([practice.md](practice.md)) + formative quiz (`assessments/week-04_quiz.md`) | Learner completes both; adult scores with [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) and reteaches any objective below criterion. |
| 16 | Culminating assessment (`assessments/unit-assessment.md`) + reflection | Learner completes the assessment; adult and learner review what is solid and what needs revisiting before Unit 02. |
