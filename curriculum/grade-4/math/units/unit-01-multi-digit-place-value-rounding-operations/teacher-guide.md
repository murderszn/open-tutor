# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Multi-Digit Place Value, Rounding, and Operations

**For the guiding adult.** Student pages never include answers; this guide and
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md) stay adult-side. Every solution below was solved
independently and reconciled with the key.

## Session-by-session notes

- **S1 (L01):** the ten-times rule is the unit's engine — do not rush it. Have
  the learner *say* "ten times" for each step up the chart. If 700 ÷ 70 = 10
  does not land, build it with 70 ten-rods grouped into 7 tens-of-tens.
- **S2 (games):** Place-Value War — each player draws 6 digit cards, builds a
  number, higher value wins the round; the winner must name every digit's
  place. Watch for face-value thinking (9 in the ones beating 4 in the
  hundred-thousands).
- **S3 (L02):** dictation is the skill — say numbers once, at natural speed.
  The comma is the period boundary; insist the learner pause at it.
- **S4 (ordering stations):** include at least one pair that differs only in a
  zero-held place (e.g., 507,300 vs 57,300 — different digit counts, then
  570,300 vs 507,300).
- **S5 (L03):** print the SVG number line. The routine to drill: *circle the
  rounding digit → look one step right → 5+ goes up.*
- **S6 (legacy rounding task):** `assignments/rounding-place-value.md` rounds
  3,847; 6,152; 9,965; 12,438 to ten/hundred/thousand — all inside the
  1,000,000 range. Score against [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).
- **S7 (L04):** display the market illustration large. Let the learner be the
  fact-checker: "the illustrator claims 29,000 — prove it or correct it."
- **S8 (estimation games):** pairs of estimate-then-reveal cards; the learner
  must name the rounding place *before* computing. Celebrate close estimates,
  not exact ones.
- **S9 (L05):** narrate every column aloud the first time. Carries get written
  small above the next column — never held in the head.
- **S10:** legacy `assignments/multi-digit-math-workshop.md` addition items
  (2,486+3,759; 6,318+2,047; 4,972+1,685 — verified in the audit).
- **S11 (L06):** teach the zero-chain as a chant: "find a nonzero digit, turn
  the zeros between into 9s, give the ones column 10." Color the chain.
- **S12:** legacy workshop subtraction items (5,204−1,876; 8,005−3,468;
  9,320−5,147 — verified in the audit). Plant one error for the learner to
  find (see L06 I4).
- **S13–S14 (project):** the fifth stall is where creativity meets constraint —
  enforce "under 100,000" and "fictional." The reporter's note is the real
  assessment of objective 5; push past "it was reasonable" to *how they know*.
- **S15:** [practice.md](practice.md) then the formative quiz. Score the same
  day; reteach before S16.
- **S16:** unit assessment + reflection. File the reflection privately — never
  in the repo.

## Misconceptions to watch

| Signal | Likely cause | Reteach move |
|---|---|---|
| Compares numbers from the rightmost digit | "Bigger digit = bigger number" habit | Cover all but the leftmost differing column; compare left to right only |
| Rounds 46,207 to 46,000 "to the nearest ten thousand" | Applies the digit rule at the wrong place | Circle the rounding-place digit first, then look exactly one step right |
| Drops a carried 1 mid-chain (7,247 for 8,247) | Carries held mentally | Require every carry written small above its column; add a "carry check" pass |
| Lines up addends by left edge | Treats numbers as digit strings | "The ones column is the anchor — right edges touch" |
| 600,000 − 47,385 = 553,615 | Forgot to reduce a chained 9 | Re-chant the zero-chain; then the addition check, which exposes it |
| Subtracts smaller-from-larger per column regardless of top/bottom | Ungrouping not triggered | "We can only subtract down when the top can give"; model with blocks |
| Estimate treated as the answer | Estimation taught as shortcut, not guardrail | Always require both: estimate box AND exact work, with a verdict sentence |

## Rounding scaffolds

Rehoused from the legacy `quizzes/hints-for-hints.md` (which now points here):

- "Which two hundreds is this number between?" → then "which thousand?"
  → then "which ten thousand?" (scale the question to the rounding place)
- "Find the halfway mark first — is your number past it?"
- "Cover every digit to the right of the decider. What do you see now?"

## Reteach moves

- **Objective 1 (ten-times):** back down to hundreds/tens/ones with blocks;
  rebuild upward one period at a time. The 700 ÷ 70 = 10 example is the anchor.
- **Objective 2 (read/write/compare):** daily 2-minute dictations; zero-holder
  hunts (Lesson 02 application).
- **Objective 3 (rounding):** number-line work only, no digit rule, for a full
  session; reintroduce the rule as "the shortcut for what the number line
  shows."
- **Objective 4 (algorithms):** shrink to four-digit numbers until columns are
  clean; then scale back up. For subtraction, color-code the borrow chain.
- **Objective 5 (estimation):** "estimate first" becomes a non-negotiable
  ritual — no exact computation starts without an estimate box.

## Readiness signals

Ready for Unit 02 (factors, multiples, multiplication strategies) when the
learner: explains the ten-times rule in their own words; rounds to any place
with a justification; adds/subtracts five-digit numbers with clean columns and
an addition check; and habitually estimates before computing. The project
rubric score of 7+ and quiz 8+ together are the formal bar.
