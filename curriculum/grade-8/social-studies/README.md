# Grade 8 Social Studies

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are
delivered; the eight units are planned, not yet written. See
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained
for one grade-8 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 8 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-8 **U.S. history through 1877** course built
on the C3 Framework's inquiry arc: colonial societies and the Atlantic
exchange; the Revolution and its contested ideals; the Constitution, ratification,
and rights; the early republic — party politics, expansion, and displacement;
industry, reform, and abolition; sectionalism, slavery, secession, and the Civil
War; Reconstruction — amendments, citizenship, and resistance; and a
primary-source capstone covering the whole span to 1877.

Each of the eight units runs four weeks at about five 45-minute sessions per
week: a concept launch, a close-reading and skills session, a source
investigation or application session, a writing/deliberation session, and a
review-and-check session. Learners work with original public-domain primary
sources (founding documents, speeches, ordinances, amendments), maps and
timelines, structured debates and simulations, and evidence-based argument
writing. Existing draft quizzes and assignments are kept as formative banks and
will be revised into the units; separate teacher guides and answer keys are
still to be written.

This is a proposed pathway, not a universal requirement: schools vary in
emphasis and unit order. A guiding adult checks placement before starting and
previews every primary source and topic for mature themes — slavery, war, and
forced displacement are central to this course and are handled with
age-appropriate, adult-supervised inquiry (see the audit's source and safety
notes).

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. **Analyze colonial societies and the Atlantic exchange:** explain labor
   systems (including enslaved labor), cultural contact, and resistance in
   colonial North America using maps and primary accounts. (C3 D2.His.1, 2, 4,
   14.6-8; D2.Geo.10, 11.6-8)
2. **Explain the Revolution and its contested ideals:** classify change and
   continuity from colonial rule to independence; analyze whose perspectives
   the "liberty" ideal served and whose it excluded. (C3 D2.His.2–4, 6.6-8;
   D2.Civ.8, 14.6-8)
3. **Analyze the Constitution and ratification:** explain the Articles'
   failures, the ratification debate, the Bill of Rights, and the powers and
   limits of the three branches. (C3 D2.Civ.3–5, 8.6-8)
4. **Explain the early republic:** connect party politics, westward expansion,
   and Native displacement; evaluate causes of the War of 1812 and the market
   revolution's effects. (C3 D2.His.1, 2, 14, 15.6-8; D2.Geo.8, 11.6-8)
5. **Analyze industry, reform, and abolition:** explain how industrial change,
   reform movements, and abolitionist arguments reshaped American society.
   (C3 D2.Eco.7, 9.6-8; D2.His.5, 6.6-8; D2.Civ.6, 14.6-8)
6. **Explain sectionalism, slavery, secession, and the Civil War:** evaluate
   the relative influence of legislative compromises, court decisions, and
   moral conflict; organize evidence into a coherent argument about causes.
   (C3 D2.His.14–16.6-8)
7. **Evaluate Reconstruction:** explain the 13th, 14th, and 15th Amendments,
   compare presidential and congressional Reconstruction, and analyze Black
   political leadership and the era's contested legacy. (C3 D2.Civ.3, 8, 13,
   14.6-8; D2.His.5, 17.6-8)
8. **Work with primary sources:** classify source kinds; detect limitations;
   infer maker, date, place of origin, and intended audience; evaluate a
   source's relevancy and utility for a specific inquiry. (C3 D2.His.9–13.6-8;
   D3.1, 3.2.6-8)
9. **Argue from evidence:** develop claims and counterclaims from multiple
   sources, noting strengths and limitations; critique arguments for
   credibility and structure. (C3 D3.3, 3.4.6-8; D4.1, 4.2, 4.4, 4.5.6-8)
10. **Inquire and take informed action:** frame compelling and supporting
    questions; determine helpful sources; present conclusions to real
    audiences; and use disciplinary lenses to analyze a public issue and plan
    constructive action. (C3 D1.1–1.5.6-8; D4.3, 4.6–4.8.6-8)

Standards references are to the College, Career, and Civic Life (C3) Framework
for Social Studies State Standards (National Council for the Social Studies),
grades 6–8 band. Codes and descriptions were verified during the audit (see
§4 of the scope-and-sequence); **no state adoption, accreditation, or alignment
certification is claimed.**

## Existing materials and their status

The audit inventoried the 21 existing files in this folder (re-audited
2026-10-06 against `main`). Summary of decisions — full table in
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md) §1:

- **Keep (as formative banks, with revision):** 15 ten-question quizzes, each
  with a primary-source excerpt and a collapsible parent answer key, covering
  the Articles of Confederation, ratification, the three branches, the Bill of
  Rights, the early republic, the market revolution, manifest destiny,
  abolition, the deepening sectional divide, the election of 1860, emancipation,
  Appomattox, and Reconstruction.
- **Keep (with revision):** 4 assignments — Bill of Rights modern-legal
  scenarios, the Constitutional Convention debate simulation, a Constitutional
  Convention evidence review, and the Reconstruction Amendments legacy research
  report.
- **Revise/replace:** the civil-rights argument-brief template is a useful
  format but is topic-mismatched (1960s content in a through-1877 course); it
  will be re-templated for the U08 capstone.
- **Gaps to build:** no written lessons, no separate teacher guides or answer
  keys (existing keys are brief and collocated with student questions), no
  colonial or Revolution instruction, no Native-displacement treatment, no
  diagnostic/review weeks, no resource packs, no generated images, no capstone.
