## 🏛️ Historical Context

Following the devastation of the Civil War (1861–1865), the United States faced an existential question: how to rebuild the Union while integrating four million newly emancipated African Americans into full political, economic, and civic life.

Historian Eric Foner terms Reconstruction America’s **"Second Founding"** because the three constitutional amendments passed during this era fundamentally remade the Constitution:
- **13th Amendment (1865)**: Formally and permanently abolished slavery and involuntary servitude throughout the entire United States, except as punishment for a crime.
- **14th Amendment (1868)**: Redefined American citizenship to include all persons born or naturalized in the U.S. (reversing the infamous 1857 *Dred Scott* decision); established national guarantees of **Due Process** and **Equal Protection under the law**.
- **15th Amendment (1870)**: Prohibited federal and state governments from denying a citizen the right to vote based on "race, color, or previous condition of servitude."

During this vibrant, revolutionary window, formerly enslaved people established schools, built churches, owned land, and elected thousands of Black officials to local, state, and national office.

### Part 2: Pioneer Profiles in Black Excellence
Select and research **two** pioneering Black political leaders who served during Reconstruction:
- **Hiram Rhodes Revels (Mississippi)**: First African American to serve in the United States Senate (1870), taking the very seat once held by Confederate President Jefferson Davis.
- **Blanche Kelso Bruce (Mississippi)**: First African American to serve a full six-year term in the United States Senate (1875–1881).
- **Joseph Rainey (South Carolina)**: Born into slavery, became the first African American to serve in the United States House of Representatives (1870–1879).
- **Robert Smalls (South Carolina)**: Civil war hero who commandeered the Confederate steamboat *Planter* to freedom, helped found the South Carolina Republican Party, and served multiple terms in the U.S. House of Representatives.

**For each leader, provide:**
1. **Biographical Background**: Early life, how they gained freedom/education, and their entry into public service.
2. **Major Legislative or Civic Achievement**: What specific policy, institution, or initiative did they champion?
3. **Historical Significance**: Why is their breakthrough remarkable given the racial climate of the 1870s?
