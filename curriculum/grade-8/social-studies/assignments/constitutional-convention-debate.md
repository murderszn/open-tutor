## 🏛️ Background & Scenario

It is the sweltering summer of **1787** in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. Fifty-five delegates have gathered behind closed doors and locked windows at the Pennsylvania State House (Independence Hall). Originally tasked with revising the weak Articles of Confederation, the delegates quickly realize an entirely new framework of government is needed.

Deep divisions threaten to fracture the young nation:
1. **Large States vs. Small States**: Should legislative representation be based strictly on population (The Virginia Plan proposed by James Madison) or should every state have an equal vote regardless of size (The New Jersey Plan proposed by William Paterson)?
2. **The Question of Enslaved Persons (Representation & Taxation)**: Southern delegates insist that enslaved persons be counted towards population representation to maximize their congressional power, while Northern delegates argue that if enslaved people have no civil rights, Southern states cannot hypocritically count them for representation.
3. **The Power of the Chief Executive**: Should the new republic have a single President with veto power, an executive council, or should the legislature hold supreme authority?

- **Roger Sherman (Connecticut)**: Shrewd negotiator seeking compromise between large and small states.
- **George Mason (Virginia)**: Deeply concerned about individual liberties and suspicious of an overly powerful federal executive without a bill of rights.
