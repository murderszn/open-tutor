## 🏛️ Background & Purpose

When the United States Constitution was signed in September 1787, Anti-Federalists like George Mason and Patrick Henry refused to sign or support it without a explicit guarantee of individual liberties. In 1789, James Madison drafted twelve amendments, ten of which were ratified by the states in December 1791. These first ten amendments are known as the **Bill of Rights**.

The Bill of Rights is not merely a historical artifact; it is a living framework that federal and state courts interpret every day. In this assignment, Learner will act as a constitutional jurist, applying the Bill of Rights to modern scenarios to determine whether a citizen's rights have been violated.

## 📜 Reference: The First Ten Amendments Summary

- **Amendment I**: Freedom of religion (establishment and free exercise clauses), speech, press, peaceful assembly, and petitioning the government.
- **Amendment II**: The right of the people to keep and bear arms in the context of a well-regulated militia and personal defense.
- **Amendment III**: Freedom from the forced quartering of soldiers in private homes during peacetime.
- **Amendment IV**: Protection against unreasonable searches and seizures; requirement of warrants based on probable cause.
- **Amendment V**: Rights of the accused: grand jury indictment, protection against double jeopardy, right against self-incrimination ("pleading the fifth"), and due process of law.
- **Amendment VI**: Right to a speedy and public trial by an impartial jury, right to be informed of charges, right to confront witnesses, and right to legal counsel.
- **Amendment VII**: Right to a jury trial in civil lawsuits involving controversies over $20.
- **Amendment VIII**: Protection against excessive bail, excessive fines, and cruel and unusual punishments.
- **Amendment IX**: Clarification that rights not enumerated in the Constitution are retained by the people (unenumerated rights).
- **Amendment X**: Powers not delegated to the United States federal government by the Constitution, nor prohibited by it to the states, are reserved to the states respectively, or to the people (federalism).
