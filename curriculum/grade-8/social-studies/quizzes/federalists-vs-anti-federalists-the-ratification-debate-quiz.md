## 📜 Historical Context & Primary Source
> *"The necessity of a Bill of Rights appears to me to be greater in this government than in any other... A government possessing such powers ought to have its boundaries accurately circumscribed."*  
> — **Brutus II (Anti-Federalist Essay, 1787)**

---

### Questions

**1.** What were the two opposing political factions that emerged during the debate over whether the states should ratify the 1787 Constitution?

**2.** Who were the three primary authors of *The Federalist Papers*, written under the joint pseudonym "Publius" to persuade New York voters?

**3.** What was the core argument made by the **Federalists** regarding the necessity of a strong national government?

**4.** Why were the **Anti-Federalists** deeply suspicious of the Constitution's "Necessary and Proper Clause" (Elastic Clause) in Article I, Section 8?

**5.** What did Anti-Federalists like Patrick Henry and George Mason fear about the newly created office of the presidency?

**6.** Read the excerpt from *Brutus II* above. Why did Anti-Federalists consider the absence of a Bill of Rights a fatal flaw in the proposed Constitution?

**7.** How did Alexander Hamilton defend the *omission* of a Bill of Rights in *Federalist No. 84*?

**8.** In *Federalist No. 10*, what did James Madison argue regarding "factions," and why did he maintain that a large republic was superior to a small democracy at controlling factional violence?

**9.** What critical political compromise secured the ratification of the Constitution in key contested states like Massachusetts, Virginia, and New York?

**10.** How many states out of the thirteen were required by Article VII to ratify the Constitution for it to take effect, and which state became the ninth to ratify in June 1788?

<summary><strong>Parent Answer Key (Click to Expand)</strong></summary>

1. **Federalists** (supporters of ratification and strong central government) and **Anti-Federalists** (opponents of ratification who favored state sovereignty and individual rights).
2. **Alexander Hamilton, James Madison, and John Jay.**
3. **A strong federal government was vital to maintain national defense, manage foreign trade, stabilize the currency, prevent internal insurrections, and pay national debts.**
4. **They feared it granted Congress unlimited, open-ended power to pass any law it chose, obliterating the reserved powers of the individual states.**
5. **They feared the presidency could easily transform into an elected monarchy or tyranny, commanding a standing army and ruling through patronage.**
6. **Without explicit constitutional boundaries protecting free speech, religious liberty, jury trials, and privacy, a powerful federal government could trample individual liberties.**
7. **Hamilton argued the Constitution already limited federal powers to those specifically listed; adding a list of protected rights might imply that any unlisted right was unprotected.**
8. **Factions (special interest groups/parties) are inevitable in a free society.** In a large, diverse republic, no single faction could easily gain a tyrannical majority over the whole nation because diverse interests would balance one another.
9. **The Federalists pledged to immediately draft and attach a comprehensive Bill of Rights as the first order of business in the new Congress.**
10. **Nine (9) states were required; New Hampshire became the crucial ninth state on June 21, 1788.**
