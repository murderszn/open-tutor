## 📜 Historical Context & Primary Source
> *"Congress shall make no law respecting an establishment of religion, or prohibiting the free exercise thereof; or abridging the freedom of speech, or of the press; or the right of the people peaceably to assemble, and to petition the Government for a redress of grievances."*  
> — **First Amendment to the United States Constitution (Ratified December 15, 1791)**

---

### Questions

**1.** Name the five fundamental freedoms guaranteed by the **First Amendment**.

**2.** What is the crucial legal distinction between the **Establishment Clause** and the **Free Exercise Clause** in the First Amendment?

**3.** How does the **Fourth Amendment** protect citizens from arbitrary police intrusion, and what standard must law enforcement meet to obtain a search warrant signed by a judge?

**4.** Explain the protection against **"Double Jeopardy"** established in the Fifth Amendment.

**5.** What does it mean when a criminal suspect invokes their Fifth Amendment privilege against **"self-incrimination"**?

**6.** Which amendment guarantees the right to a speedy and public trial, an impartial jury of peers, the right to confront witnesses, and the right to legal counsel?

**7.** What does the **Eighth Amendment** forbid regarding bail, fines, and punishment?

**8.** What was the historical grievance from British colonial rule that led directly to the creation of the **Third Amendment**?

**9.** Explain the vital legal purpose of the **Ninth Amendment**: how does it address the fear that listing certain rights might mean other rights are not protected?

**10.** What principle does the **Tenth Amendment** establish regarding federalism and powers not explicitly assigned to the national government?

---

<details>
<summary><strong>Parent Answer Key (Click to Expand)</strong></summary>

1. **Freedom of Religion, Freedom of Speech, Freedom of the Press, Right to Peaceably Assemble, and Right to Petition the Government.**
2. **Establishment Clause** prohibits the federal government from creating an official state religion or favoring one religion over another; **Free Exercise Clause** protects an individual's right to practice their religious beliefs without government interference.
3. **It protects against unreasonable searches and seizures.** Police must demonstrate **probable cause** supported by oath or affirmation describing the specific place to be searched and persons/things to be seized.
4. **A person cannot be put on trial twice for the exact same crime** once acquitted or convicted by a court of competent jurisdiction.
5. **A suspect cannot be compelled to testify against themselves or provide testimony that would incriminate them** in a criminal case (the right to remain silent).
6. **The Sixth Amendment.**
7. **It prohibits excessive bail, excessive fines, and cruel and unusual punishments.**
8. **The British Quartering Acts of 1765 and 1774**, which forced American colonists to house and feed British regular soldiers in private dwellings and barns.
9. **It affirms unenumerated rights:** Just because a specific right is not explicitly enumerated in the Constitution does not mean the people do not possess it (the enumeration of certain rights shall not be construed to deny or disparage others retained by the people).
10. **Federalism / Reserved Powers:** Any power not delegated to the federal government by the Constitution, nor prohibited to the states, is reserved to the states respectively, or to the people.
