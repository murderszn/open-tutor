## 📜 Historical Context & Primary Source
> *"They had for more than a century before been regarded as beings of an inferior order, and altogether unfit to associate with the white race, either in social or political relations; and so far inferior, that they had no rights which the white man was bound to respect..."*  
> — **Chief Justice Roger B. Taney, Dred Scott v. Sandford (March 6, 1857)**

---

### Questions

**1.** What was the **Kansas-Nebraska Act of 1854**, authored by Stephen A. Douglas, and how did it effectively repeal the long-standing Missouri Compromise of 1820?

**2.** Why did the territory of Kansas become known as **"Bleeding Kansas"** between 1854 and 1859?

**3.** What major new political party emerged in 1854 in direct response to the passage of the Kansas-Nebraska Act, with the explicit platform of halting the westward expansion of slavery?

**4.** What shocking act of violence occurred inside the United States Senate chamber in May 1856 involving Senator Charles Sumner and Representative Preston Brooks?

**5.** Who was **Dred Scott**, and on what legal grounds did he sue for his and his family's freedom in Missouri courts?

**6.** Read the excerpt from Chief Justice Roger B. Taney’s majority opinion above. What did the Supreme Court rule regarding whether African Americans (enslaved or free) could be citizens of the United States?

**7.** How did the Supreme Court use the **Fifth Amendment Due Process Clause** in the *Dred Scott* decision to rule that the federal government could never prohibit slavery in any federal territory?

**8.** What was the reaction across the Northern free states to the *Dred Scott* ruling, and how did it affect the credibility of the Supreme Court?

**9.** During the 1858 Illinois Senate race, what famous metaphorical phrase did Abraham Lincoln use to describe the impossibility of the nation remaining half-slave and half-free?

**10.** What was John Brown’s objective when he led an armed raid on the federal arsenal at **Harpers Ferry, Virginia**, in October 1859, and how did his execution polarize the North and South?

<summary><strong>Parent Answer Key (Click to Expand)</strong></summary>

1. **It organized Kansas and Nebraska territories and instituted popular sovereignty**, allowing settlers to vote on slavery, which overturned the 1820 Missouri Compromise ban north of the 36°30′ parallel.
2. **A violent guerrilla civil war erupted** between pro-slavery "border ruffians" from Missouri and anti-slavery "free-staters" who rushed into the territory to control the vote, resulting in voter fraud, arson, and massacres.
3. **The Republican Party.**
4. **Representative Preston Brooks of South Carolina brutally beat abolitionist Senator Charles Sumner unconscious with a heavy gold-topped cane** on the Senate floor after Sumner delivered a speech ("The Crime Against Kansas") attacking Southern slaveholders.
5. **An enslaved man taken by his enslaver (Dr. John Emerson) to live for years in the free state of Illinois and the free Wisconsin Territory;** Scott argued that extended residence on free soil made him legally free.
6. **Taney ruled that Black people were not citizens under the Constitution** and possessed "no rights which the white man was bound to respect," and therefore had no standing to sue in federal court.
7. **The Court ruled that enslaved people were legal property**, and the Fifth Amendment prevents the government from depriving citizens of property without due process; therefore, any congressional ban on slavery in territories was unconstitutional.
8. **Outrage and defiance across the North;** it convinced millions that a "Slave Power conspiracy" controlled the federal government and destroyed trust in the judicial branch.
9. **"A house divided against itself cannot stand.** I believe this government cannot endure, permanently, half slave and half free. I do not expect the Union to be dissolved—I do not expect the house to fall—but I do expect it will cease to be divided. It will become all one thing or all the other."
10. **Brown intended to seize federal weapons, arm enslaved people, and ignite a massive liberation uprising across the South.** Southerners saw him as proof the North wanted to massacre them; many Northerners viewed him as a righteous martyr who gave his life for human freedom.
