## 📜 Historical Context & Primary Source
> *"What, to the American slave, is your 4th of July? I answer; a day that reveals to him, more than all other days in the year, the gross injustice and cruelty to which he is the constant victim. To him, your celebration is a sham; your boasted liberty, an unholy license; your national greatness, swelling vanity..."*  
> — **Frederick Douglass, Address at Rochester, New York (July 5, 1852)**

---

### Questions

**1.** What was the core goal of the **Abolitionist Movement**, and how did radical abolitionists differ from gradual emancipationists?

**2.** Who was the radical white abolitionist who published the anti-slavery newspaper *The Liberator* in Boston beginning in 1831, famously declaring: *"I will not equivocate—I will not excuse—I will not retreat a single inch—AND I WILL BE HEARD"*?

**3.** Read the excerpt above from Frederick Douglass's 1852 Rochester address. What biting paradox was Douglass exposing between the ideals of the American Revolution and the reality of American chattel slavery?

**4.** Describe the extraordinary life and achievements of **Frederick Douglass**: how did he gain his freedom, and what major roles did he play as an author, orator, and advisor to President Lincoln?

**5.** Why was **Harriet Tubman** known as the "Moses of her people"? Approximately how many trips did she make into the South along the Underground Railroad, and what was her record regarding losing passengers?

**6.** Explain how the secret network known as the **Underground Railroad** functioned, including terms such as "conductors," "stations," and "tracks."

**7.** What famous speech did formerly enslaved orator and women’s rights activist **Sojourner Truth** deliver at the 1851 Women’s Rights Convention in Akron, Ohio?

**8.** How did David Walker’s fiery 1829 publication, *Appeal to the Coloured Citizens of the World*, frighten Southern slaveholders and challenge Northern moderate abolitionists?

**9.** What groundbreaking anti-slavery novel published by Harriet Beecher Stowe in 1852 humanized the horrors of slavery for millions of Northern readers and became an international bestseller?

**10.** How did Southern state legislatures and Congress respond to the surge of abolitionist literature in the 1830s (e.g., the congressional "Gag Rule")?

<details>
<summary><strong>Parent Answer Key (Click to Expand)</strong></summary>

1. **The total and immediate eradication of chattel slavery.** Gradualists favored slow phase-outs or recolonization schemes (like the American Colonization Society in Liberia), whereas radical abolitionists demanded immediate, uncompensated emancipation and full civil rights.
2. **William Lloyd Garrison.**
3. **The hypocrisy of celebrating liberty, independence, and natural rights on the Fourth of July while holding millions of Black human beings in brutal, unconstitutional bondage.**
4. **Douglass escaped slavery in Maryland by disguising himself as a sailor;** he wrote three influential autobiographies, published the newspaper *The North Star*, delivered master oratorical tours worldwide, and pressured Lincoln to authorize Black Union troops and make emancipation a primary war aim.
5. **She repeatedly risked her life returning to Maryland to guide dozens of enslaved people to freedom;** she made roughly 13 to 19 missions and famously never lost a passenger nor let anyone turn back.
6. **A clandestine resistance network of free Black people and white allies.** "Conductors" guided runaways; "stations" were safe homes, churches, or barns; "tracks" were secret escape routes toward Northern free states and Canada.
7. **"Ain't I a Woman?"** (demanding both the abolition of slavery and equal rights for women).
8. **Walker advocated immediate armed resistance and self-defense against slaveholders**, asserting that Black people had an inherent God-given right to fight for their liberty.
9. **_Uncle Tom's Cabin_**; it inflamed Northern moral outrage and was blamed by Southern politicians for fanning sectional flames.
10. **Southern states banned abolitionist mail and placed bounties on activists;** the U.S. House of Representatives instituted the **"Gag Rule" (1836–1844)**, automatically tabling and refusing to read any anti-slavery petitions presented by citizens.
