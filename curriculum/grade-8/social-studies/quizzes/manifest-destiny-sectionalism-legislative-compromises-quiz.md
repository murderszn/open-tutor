## 📜 Historical Context & Primary Source
> *"Our manifest destiny is to overspread the continent allotted by Providence for the free development of our yearly multiplying millions."*  
> — **John L. O'Sullivan, Democratic Review (1845)**

---

### Questions

**1.** Define the term **"Manifest Destiny"** coined by journalist John L. O'Sullivan in 1845. What underlying political and cultural beliefs did it express?

**2.** Explain the core terms of the **Missouri Compromise of 1820**: which state entered the Union as a slave state, which entered as a free state, and what boundary line was established in the Louisiana Territory?

**3.** What was the **Mexican-American War (1846–1848)**, and what vast territory (the Mexican Cession) did the United States acquire under the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo?

**4.** What was the controversial **Wilmot Proviso (1846)**, and why did it trigger intense, irreconcilable fury among Southern senators even though it never became law?

**5.** The California Gold Rush of 1849 caused California’s population to explode, leading to its rapid application for statehood. Why did California's request to enter as a free state threaten the balance of power in the U.S. Senate?

**6.** Name the legendary statesman known as the "Great Compromiser" who crafted both the Missouri Compromise of 1820 and the Compromise of 1850.

**7.** Outline the four major provisions of the **Compromise of 1850**.

**8.** What was the most hated provision of the Compromise of 1850 in the Northern free states, and how did it compel ordinary Northern citizens to become complicit in capturing alleged runaway slaves?

**9.** How did several Northern states resist the Fugitive Slave Act of 1850 using constitutional arguments of states' rights (such as passing "Personal Liberty Laws")?

**10.** Explain the political concept of **"Popular Sovereignty"** championed by Senator Stephen A. Douglas of Illinois.

<summary><strong>Parent Answer Key (Click to Expand)</strong></summary>

1. **The belief that the United States was divinely ordained by God to expand its democratic institutions and territory across North America from the Atlantic to the Pacific Ocean.**
2. **Missouri** entered as a slave state; **Maine** entered as a free state (maintaining Senate balance at 12–12); slavery was prohibited in remaining Louisiana territory north of the **36°30′ parallel**.
3. **A war sparked by the U.S. annexation of Texas and border skirmishes along the Rio Grande;** the U.S. acquired present-day California, Nevada, Utah, Arizona, and parts of New Mexico, Colorado, and Wyoming.
4. **A proposed amendment that would ban slavery in any territory acquired from Mexico;** Southerners viewed it as an existential threat and an insult to Southern rights and property.
5. **There were exactly 15 free states and 15 slave states.** California's admission as a free state would permanently tip the balance in favor of the North, ending Southern parity in the Senate.
6. **Henry Clay (of Kentucky).**
7. **1) California admitted as a free state; 2) Popular sovereignty in Utah and New Mexico territories; 3) Slave trade abolished in Washington, D.C.; 4) A harsher federal Fugitive Slave Law enacted.**
8. **The Fugitive Slave Act of 1850.** It denied accused runaways a jury trial or the right to testify, paid commissioners more money ($10 vs $5) for returning captives to slavery, and imposed heavy fines or jail on any citizen who refused to assist slave catchers.
9. **They passed Personal Liberty Laws** that barred state officials from assisting federal slave catchers, prohibited using local jails to hold runaways, and guaranteed accused persons the right to a jury trial.
10. **The doctrine that the residents of a federal territory should vote directly to decide for themselves whether to permit or prohibit slavery**, rather than having Congress decide.
