## 📜 Historical Context & Primary Source
> *"The American continents, by the free and independent condition which they have assumed and maintain, are henceforth not to be considered as subjects for future colonization by any European powers... We should consider any attempt on their part to extend their system to any portion of this hemisphere as dangerous to our peace and safety."*  
> — **President James Monroe, Seventh Annual Message to Congress (December 2, 1823)**

---

### Questions

**1.** What British maritime practice of stopping American merchant ships at sea and forcibly drafting American sailors into the Royal Navy was a primary cause of the War of 1812?

**2.** Who were the "War Hawks" in the U.S. Congress, and name two prominent young political leaders associated with this faction?

**3.** What major national disaster occurred in August 1814 when British forces invaded Washington, D.C., and what heroic action did First Lady Dolley Madison take?

**4.** During the British bombardment of Fort McHenry in Baltimore in September 1814, what famous poem (later set to music as the U.S. national anthem) was written by Francis Scott Key?

**5.** What peace treaty formally ended the War of 1812 in December 1814, and what was the status of territorial boundaries before and after the treaty?

**6.** Why was the Battle of New Orleans in January 1815 fought after the peace treaty had already been signed, and which future U.S. president became an overnight national hero because of this victory?

**7.** Why is the period following the War of 1812 during James Monroe’s presidency commonly referred to as the **"Era of Good Feelings"**?

**8.** Read the excerpt from President James Monroe’s 1823 address above. What two core principles did the **Monroe Doctrine** declare regarding European colonization in the Americas?

**9.** How did the Monroe Doctrine promise that the United States would treat existing European colonies and wars in Europe?

**10.** Which European naval power tacitly supported the enforcement of the Monroe Doctrine in the early 19th century because it favored free trade in newly independent Latin American republics?

<details>
<summary><strong>Parent Answer Key (Click to Expand)</strong></summary>

1. **Impressment.** The British claimed British-born naturalized American citizens were still subject to the Crown.
2. **Young nationalist congressmen (mostly from the South and West) who aggressively pressured for war against Britain; Henry Clay (Kentucky) and John C. Calhoun (South Carolina).**
3. **The British burned the White House, Capitol building, and other public buildings; Dolley Madison saved historic government papers and the famous Lansdowne portrait of George Washington.**
4. **"The Defence of Fort M'Henry" (which became "The Star-Spangled Banner").**
5. **The Treaty of Ghent; *status quo ante bellum* (all boundaries and borders returned exactly to what they were before the war started, with no territorial gains).**
6. **Slow communications across the Atlantic (ships took 4–6 weeks to cross the ocean); General Andrew Jackson.**
7. **There was a surge of national pride, economic growth, and the collapse of the Federalist Party left a single dominant national party (the Democratic-Republicans), creating an apparent absence of fierce partisan division.**
8. **1) The Western Hemisphere was closed to future European colonization. 2) Any European attempt to subjugate or interfere with independent nations in the Americas would be viewed as an act of hostility against the United States.**
9. **The U.S. pledged not to interfere with existing European colonies in the Americas, nor take sides in internal European wars.**
10. **Great Britain (and the British Royal Navy), because an open Latin America offered lucrative merchant trade opportunities that Britain wanted to protect from rival empires.**
