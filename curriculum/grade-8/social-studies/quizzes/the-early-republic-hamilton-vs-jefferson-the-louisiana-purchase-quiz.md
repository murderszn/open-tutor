## 📜 Historical Context & Primary Source
> *"The Incorporation of a Bank can never be considered as among the powers implied in the Constitution... To take a single step beyond the boundaries thus specially drawn around the powers of Congress, is to take possession of a boundless field of power, no longer susceptible of any definition."*  
> — **Thomas Jefferson, Opinion on the Constitutionality of a National Bank (1791)**

### Questions

**1.** Contrast the economic visions of Treasury Secretary Alexander Hamilton and Secretary of State Thomas Jefferson during George Washington's presidency.

**2.** Explain the philosophical difference between **"strict construction"** and **"loose construction"** of the United States Constitution.

**3.** What constitutional clause did Alexander Hamilton cite to justify the creation of the First Bank of the United States?

**4.** In his 1796 Farewell Address, what two major warnings did President George Washington give to the young nation regarding domestic politics and foreign alliances?

**5.** In 1803, President Thomas Jefferson completed the **Louisiana Purchase** from France. What was the purchase price, and approximately how much land did it add to the United States?

**6.** Why was the port city of **New Orleans** and access to the Mississippi River so economically vital to American farmers living west of the Appalachian Mountains?

**7.** What philosophical and constitutional dilemma did Thomas Jefferson face when deciding whether to authorize the purchase of the Louisiana Territory from Napoleon Bonaparte?

**8.** How did the successful revolution led by Toussaint Louverture and enslaved Africans in Saint-Domingue (Haiti) influence Napoleon's decision to sell Louisiana to the United States?

**9.** What was the primary mission assigned to Meriwether Lewis and William Clark’s Corps of Discovery (1804–1806), and who was the Shoshone woman who served as their invaluable guide and translator?

**10.** What significant long-term consequence did the Louisiana Purchase have on the national debate over the geographic expansion of slavery?

<details>
<summary><strong>Parent Answer Key (Click to Expand)</strong></summary>

1. **Hamilton** envisioned an urban, commercial, manufacturing powerhouse with a national bank, strong federal credit, and global trade; **Jefferson** envisioned an agrarian republic of independent yeoman farmers with decentralized state power.
2. **Strict construction** (Jefferson) holds that the federal government possesses ONLY the powers explicitly listed in the Constitution; **loose construction** (Hamilton) holds that the federal government can exercise broad implied powers necessary to fulfill its constitutional duties.
3. **The "Necessary and Proper Clause"** (Article I, Section 8, Clause 18, also known as the Elastic Clause).
4. **Warning against political party factionalism (hyper-partisanship)** and **warning against permanent foreign military alliances (entangling alliances).**
5. **$15 million; it added approximately 828,000 square miles**, effectively doubling the geographic size of the United States.
6. **New Orleans controlled the mouth of the Mississippi River.** Western farmers floated their crops downriver on flatboats to New Orleans for shipment to Eastern and European markets; whoever controlled New Orleans held the economic lifeline of the American interior.
7. **Jefferson was a strict constructionist.** The Constitution nowhere explicitly granted the President the power to purchase foreign territory. Jefferson had to embrace a loose interpretation (treating it as a treaty power) to finalize the purchase.
8. **The Haitian Revolution defeated Napoleon's army and ended French ambitions in the Americas.** Having lost France's most profitable colony in Saint-Domingue, Napoleon no longer needed Louisiana to supply the Caribbean sugar islands and urgently needed cash for his European wars.
9. **To map the newly acquired territory, find a viable water route to the Pacific Ocean (the Northwest Passage), document flora/fauna, and establish diplomatic contact with Native nations; Sacagawea.**
10. **It reignited bitter sectional conflict between North and South** over whether new states carved out of the western territories would be admitted as slave states or free states.

</details>
