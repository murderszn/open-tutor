## 📜 Historical Context & Primary Source
> *"That on the first day of January, in the year of our Lord one thousand eight hundred and sixty-three, all persons held as slaves within any State or designated part of a State, the people whereof shall then be in rebellion against the United States, shall be then, thenceforward, and forever free..."*  
> — **Abraham Lincoln, The Emancipation Proclamation (January 1, 1863)**

---

### Questions

**1.** Fought on September 17, 1862, in Maryland, what battle remains the single bloodiest day in American military history, and why was it strategically vital for President Lincoln?

**2.** Read the excerpt from the **Emancipation Proclamation** above. Did the proclamation immediately free all enslaved people in the United States, including those in the Union Border States? Explain why or why not.

**3.** How did the Emancipation Proclamation transform the moral and diplomatic nature of the Civil War, particularly regarding Great Britain and France?

**4.** What authorization in the Emancipation Proclamation allowed African American men to enlist in the United States military, and approximately how many Black soldiers and sailors served the Union by war's end?

**5.** Who was the commander of the celebrated **54th Massachusetts Volunteer Infantry**, and what historic assault on Fort Wagner, South Carolina, in July 1863 proved the extraordinary courage of Black soldiers?

**6.** Who was William H. Carney, and what heroic action during the assault on Fort Wagner earned him the distinction of being the first African American to be awarded the Congressional Medal of Honor?

**7.** Fought across July 1–3, 1863, in Pennsylvania, why is the **Battle of Gettysburg** considered the major military turning point of the Civil War in the Eastern Theater?

**8.** On July 4, 1863, Confederate General John C. Pemberton surrendered the fortress city of **Vicksburg, Mississippi**, to Union General Ulysses S. Grant. Why was this victory decisive for Union strategy?

**9.** Delivered on November 19, 1863, what was the central message of President Lincoln's two-minute **Gettysburg Address**, and how did it redefine the war as a "new birth of freedom"?

**10.** How did thousands of enslaved African Americans participate in what W.E.B. Du Bois later termed the "general strike" during the war by escaping plantations and aiding advancing Union armies?

<summary><strong>Parent Answer Key (Click to Expand)</strong></summary>

1. **The Battle of Antietam (Sharpsburg);** it halted General Robert E. Lee's first invasion of the North and gave Lincoln the necessary political victory to publicly issue the preliminary Emancipation Proclamation without seeming desperate.
2. **No.** Lincoln used his constitutional war powers as Commander-in-Chief. It applied **only** to states currently in active rebellion against the Union. It deliberately exempted loyal Border States (e.g., Maryland, Kentucky, Missouri, Delaware) and areas already under Union control, where Lincoln had no emergency war powers to seize property.
3. **It transformed the war from a narrow constitutional conflict to preserve the Union into a moral crusade to destroy slavery.** This made it politically impossible for Britain or France, where the public fiercely opposed slavery, to officially recognize or ally with the Confederacy.
4. **It authorized the formal enlistment of Black troops in the Union Army and Navy;** approximately 180,000 to 200,000 Black men served (about 10% of the Union Army).
5. **Colonel Robert Gould Shaw;** the valiant assault on Fort Wagner demonstrated unmatched bravery under ferocious fire, disproving racist doubts about the fighting capabilities of Black soldiers.
6. **Sergeant William H. Carney seized the American flag after the color sergeant fell**, carried it through murderous crossfire to the parapet, took multiple severe bullet wounds, and brought the flag back safely, famously saying: *"Boys, I only did my duty; the old flag never touched the ground!"*
7. **It permanently crushed Robert E. Lee’s offensive capabilities.** The staggering casualties (over 50,000 combined casualties) and the disastrous failure of Pickett's Charge forced Lee into a permanent strategic retreat back into Virginia; the Confederacy never again invaded Northern soil.
8. **It gave the Union complete control of the Mississippi River**, cutting the Confederacy in two (severing Texas, Arkansas, and Louisiana from the eastern states) and fulfilling a primary objective of the Anaconda Plan.
9. **Lincoln connected the Civil War to the 1776 Declaration of Independence ("four score and seven years ago")**, declaring that the nation was dedicated to the proposition that all men are created equal, and honoring the fallen so that government "of the people, by the people, for the people, shall not perish from the earth."
10. **As Union armies approached, hundreds of thousands of enslaved people stopped working, fled to Union lines (treated as "contraband of war"), and worked as laborers, scouts, spies, nurses, and armed soldiers**, collapsing the South’s agrarian economy from within.
