## 📜 Historical Context & Primary Source
> *"Ambition must be made to counteract ambition. The interest of the man must be connected with the constitutional rights of the place... If men were angels, no government would be necessary."*  
> — **James Madison, Federalist No. 51 (1788)**

---

### Questions

**1.** Name the three branches of the United States federal government established by Articles I, II, and III of the Constitution, and state the primary constitutional function of each branch.

**2.** Explain the core difference between the principle of **Separation of Powers** and the principle of **Checks and Balances**.

**3.** Give one specific example of how the **Legislative Branch** can check the power of the **Executive Branch**.

**4.** How can the **President (Executive Branch)** check the legislative authority of **Congress** when a bill passes both chambers?

**5.** How can Congress override a presidential veto, and what fractional vote is required in both the House and the Senate?

**6.** Which branch has the authority to declare federal laws or executive actions unconstitutional through judicial review, and which landmark 1803 Supreme Court case formally established this power?

**7.** Read the excerpt from *Federalist No. 51* above. What did Madison mean when he asserted that *"Ambition must be made to counteract ambition"*?

**8.** What role does the Senate play in checking presidential appointments to federal courts, ambassador posts, and cabinet positions?

**9.** Explain the constitutional process of **impeachment**: which chamber has the sole power to impeach (charge) a federal official, and which chamber conducts the trial?

**10.** Why did the Framers establish an independent judiciary with lifetime tenure for federal judges during "good behavior"?

<details>
<summary><strong>Parent Answer Key (Click to Expand)</strong></summary>

1. **Legislative (Article I):** Makes laws. **Executive (Article II):** Enforces/executes laws. **Judicial (Article III):** Interprets laws and administers justice.
2. **Separation of Powers** divides government authority among three distinct branches so no single entity holds total control; **Checks and Balances** empowers each branch to limit, inspect, or block the actions of the other two.
3. **Impeachment, power of the purse (funding), Senate confirmation of appointments/treaties, or overriding a presidential veto.**
4. **The President can veto legislation passed by Congress.**
5. **A two-thirds (2/3) supermajority vote in both the House of Representatives and the Senate.**
6. **The Judicial Branch (Supreme Court); established in *Marbury v. Madison* (1803).**
7. **Human nature is prone to seeking power.** By giving each branch constitutional tools and institutional self-interest to resist encroachment by the other branches, power checks power naturally.
8. **Advice and Consent.** The Senate must confirm presidential nominations by a majority vote before the officials can take office.
9. **The House of Representatives has the sole power to impeach (bring formal charges); the Senate holds the trial, requiring a two-thirds vote to convict and remove.**
10. **To insulate judges from political pressure, public passion, and retaliation by the President or Congress**, ensuring impartial rulings based solely on the law and the Constitution.
