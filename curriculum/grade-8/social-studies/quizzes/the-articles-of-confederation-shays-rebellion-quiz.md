## 📜 Historical Context & Primary Source
> *"Each state retains its sovereignty, freedom, and independence, and every power, jurisdiction, and right, which is not by this Confederation expressly delegated to the United States, in Congress assembled."*  
> — **Articles of Confederation, Article II (1781)**

---

### Questions

**1.** Under the Articles of Confederation, how many votes did each state receive in the unicameral Confederation Congress, regardless of its population or geographical size?

**2.** Identify two major structural weaknesses of the national government under the Articles of Confederation regarding taxation and interstate commerce.

**3.** Why did the authors of the Articles of Confederation deliberately create an exceptionally weak national executive and avoid creating a national court system?

**4.** In 1786, Daniel Shays led an armed uprising of indebted farmers in western Massachusetts. What specific economic grievances drove Shays and his followers to shut down county courthouses?

**5.** How did the Confederation Congress respond to Shays' Rebellion, and what did this response reveal about the federal government's military capability under the Articles?

**6.** Read the excerpt from Article II above. How did the concept of "state sovereignty" prevent the Confederation Congress from establishing a stable national currency and paying off Revolutionary War debts?

**7.** What was the Northwest Ordinance of 1787, and what historic precedent did it establish regarding the expansion of slavery north of the Ohio River?

**8.** Following Shays' Rebellion, George Washington wrote to Henry Knox: *"There are combustibles in every State, which a spark might set fire to."* What did Washington mean by this metaphor?

**9.** Why was the requirement of unanimous consent (all 13 states) to amend the Articles of Confederation such a fatal flaw for the young republic?

**10.** How did the failure of the Annapolis Convention in 1786 directly lead to the historic call for the Philadelphia Convention in May 1787?

<details>
<summary><strong>Parent Answer Key (Click to Expand)</strong></summary>

1. **One vote per state.** Each state had equal voting power regardless of population size or wealth.
2. **No power to tax and no power to regulate interstate commerce.** Congress could only request funds from states (requisitions), which states routinely ignored, and states placed tariffs on each other's goods.
3. **Fear of tyranny and monarchy.** Having just fought a revolution against King George III and Parliament, Americans feared a centralized ruler would infringe upon their hard-won liberties.
4. **Farm foreclosures, heavy state property taxes, and imprisonment for debt.** Massachusetts farmers faced seizure of their land and livestock due to debt and lack of hard currency (specie).
5. **Congress was powerless to send military aid.** The federal government had no standing army and could not compel states to send militia troops, proving the national government could not maintain public order.
6. **States acted as independent nations.** Because each state retained sovereignty, individual states printed their own depreciating currencies, refused federal debt quotas, and engaged in economic trade wars.
7. **Organized the Northwest Territory and banned slavery.** It created a pathway for territories to enter the Union as equal states with a bill of rights and explicitly prohibited slavery in the territory.
8. **Widespread unrest threatened civil war.** Washington warned that economic instability, class resentment, and weak government existed throughout all states, which could ignite nationwide rebellion.
9. **Gridlock.** Any single state could veto vital reforms, tax proposals, or structural changes, making it impossible to fix defects in the Articles.
10. **Alexander Hamilton and James Madison used Annapolis to petition Congress.** When only five states attended Annapolis, Hamilton drafted a report urging all thirteen states to send delegates to Philadelphia in May 1787 to thoroughly revise the national government.
