# Unit Assessment — Unit 03: Constitution, Ratification, Rights, Institutions

**Week 14 S5 · ~45 minutes.** The adult scores with [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md).
Answer in complete sentences unless a question says otherwise. The essay from
practice Task H is assessed separately as the portfolio capstone; this assessment
covers knowledge, source analysis, and application.

## §1 — Knowledge (8 questions, 2 points each)

1. List three powers Congress lacked under the Articles of Confederation.
2. What was the Great Compromise? Name both chambers it created and how each is
   apportioned.
3. What did the Three-Fifths Compromise count, and for what two purposes?
4. Name the first, ninth, and last states to ratify the Constitution, with dates.
5. What is the difference between a Federalist and an Anti-Federalist? Name one
   author from each side.
6. What fraction of each house is needed to override a presidential veto?
7. Which amendment reserves powers to the states or the people, and what does it
   reserve?
8. On what date did the Bill of Rights take effect (ratification of Amendments
   1–10), and how many amendments had Congress originally proposed?

## §2 — Source analysis (10 points)

Read the two paraphrased arguments (originals are public domain; the adult may
substitute short original excerpts):

- **Source A (Madison, *Federalist No. 10*):** "Liberty is to faction what air is
  to fire. Factions cannot be removed without destroying liberty, so a well-constructed
  union must control their effects. In an extensive republic, many interests compete,
  so no single faction can easily oppress the rest."
- **Source B (Brutus, *Brutus No. 1*):** "A republic this large cannot represent its
  people. The necessary-and-proper and supremacy clauses will let the national
  government expand until the states are empty shells. Without a bill of rights,
  liberty has no written protection."

Answer:

1. What do both authors agree is a danger? (2 points)
2. How do their remedies differ? (3 points)
3. Which author's worry was answered by the Bill of Rights, and how completely?
   Defend your answer with one fact. (3 points)
4. For the question "What did ordinary Americans think of the Constitution?",
   explain why Source A alone is weak evidence. (2 points)

## §3 — Trace the check (6 points)

Congress passes a bill creating a new federal court and funding it. The president
believes the court is unnecessary and vetoes the bill. The House overrides the veto
with 290 of 435 votes; the Senate overrides with 68 of 100 votes.

1. Does the bill become law? Show the fractions and explain. (3 points)
2. Once the court exists, who nominates its judges and who confirms them?
   (2 points)
3. Name one further check Congress holds over the courts after they are created.
   (1 point)

## §4 — Bill of Rights scenarios (10 points, 5 each)

Decide each case: name the amendment, state its rule, give your decision, and name
one fact that would change your decision.

1. Federal officers, with a valid warrant describing a specific house and specific
   stolen goods, search that house and seize the listed goods.
2. A federal law makes it a crime to publish criticism of a federal agency's
   director.

## §5 — Short argument (10 points)

In about 200–300 words, answer: **Did the Constitution settle the question of how
much power the national government should have?** Use at least two pieces of
evidence from the unit (a clause, a compromise, or a ratification fact) and state
one limit of your argument. This is a shorter companion to the Task H essay, not
a replacement for it.
