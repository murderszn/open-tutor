# Formative Quiz — Unit 03: Constitution, Ratification, Rights, Institutions

Given in three parts (Weeks 11, 12, 13, session S5). The adult scores with
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md). This quiz is formative: it finds gaps, it does
not grade the learner.

## Part A — The Articles fail (Week 11 S5)

1. Under the Articles of Confederation, how many votes did each state get in
   Congress, and how many states had to agree to pass an important law?
2. True or false: Congress under the Articles could tax citizens directly to pay
   war debts. Explain your answer in one sentence.
3. Name two things Congress could **not** do under the Articles that the
   Constitution later allowed it to do.
4. Amending the Articles required the agreement of how many states? What happened
   if one state refused?
5. In one or two sentences, explain how Shays' Rebellion (1786–1787) exposed a
   weakness of the Articles. Name the weakness.
6. Short answer: Why did the Philadelphia convention meet in 1787 — what was it
   officially called to do, and what did it actually do?

## Part B — Compromises and ratification (Week 12 S5)

1. Complete the table:

   | Plan | Chambers | Representation |
   |---|---|---|
   | Virginia Plan | ? | ? |
   | New Jersey Plan | ? | ? |
   | Great Compromise | ? | ? |

2. Under the Three-Fifths Compromise, a state with 100,000 free people and 50,000
   enslaved people counted how many people for representation? Show your arithmetic.
3. Which state ratified first, on what date, and by what vote?
4. Which state was ninth, on what date, and why did the ninth ratification matter?
5. Paraphrase Madison's argument in *Federalist No. 10* in two sentences: what is a
   faction, and how does a large republic control its effects?
6. Paraphrase Brutus's central fear in *Brutus No. 1* in two sentences. What did
   Brutus demand?

## Part C — Structure and rights (Week 13 S5)

1. Name the three branches, the article that creates each, and one core power of
   each.
2. A bill passes both houses; the president vetoes it. What must Congress do to
   make it law anyway? Give the fraction for each house.
3. The president nominates a judge. Who must confirm, and by what fraction?
4. Which amendment protects against unreasonable searches, and what does it require
   for a warrant?
5. A defendant is acquitted in federal court and then charged again for the same
   act. Which amendment is violated, and what is the rule called?
6. Short answer: The Bill of Rights was proposed in 1789 as twelve amendments.
   How many became Amendments 1–10, and on what date were they ratified? What
   happened to the other two proposed articles?
