## Task A — Close read: the Articles' own words (Week 11 S2)

Read these public-domain excerpts from the Articles of Confederation (1781):

- **Article II:** "Each state retains its sovereignty, freedom, and independence,
  and every power, jurisdiction, and right, which is not by this confederation
  expressly delegated to the United States, in Congress assembled."
- **Article V:** each state has one vote in Congress; delegates are appointed by
  state legislatures.

Answer:

1. Paraphrase Article II in your own words (two sentences maximum).
2. Under Article V, how many votes does the largest state get? The smallest?
3. A problem needs Congress to act. Nine states must agree. Suppose five states
   support action, four oppose, and four are undecided. Can Congress act? Explain.
4. Why would Article II make it hard for Congress to claim a power not listed in
   the Articles? (Hint: "expressly delegated.")

## Task D — Deliberative comparison (Week 12 S4, with project §2)

Answer both questions in a short paragraph each:

1. The Philadelphia convention met in secret; the ratification debate happened in
   public print. Give one advantage of secrecy for honest bargaining and one
   advantage of publicity for legitimate decision-making.
2. Which process — the convention or the ratification conventions — produced the
   better decision about the Constitution? Defend your answer with one argument
   from Lesson 2 or 3 and one fact about a ratification vote.

## Task E — Trace the checks (Week 13 S2)

Use the branches diagram (`../assets/branches-checks-diagram.svg`) and Lesson 4.

## Task F — Bill of Rights scenarios (Week 13 S4, with project §3)

Decide each case. For each: name the amendment, state its rule in your own words,
give your decision, and name one fact that would change your decision.

1. Federal officers search a farmer's barn without a warrant, claiming they smelled
   something suspicious, and seize papers.
2. A state legislature (not Congress) passes a law banning a newspaper in 1795.
   Does the 1st Amendment as ratified in 1791 decide this case? Why or why not?
   (Hint: whom did the amendments bind in 1791?)
3. A defendant is acquitted of a federal charge; a federal prosecutor charges him
   again for the same act, hoping for a different jury.
4. A judge sets bail so high that no defendant charged with a minor offense could
   pay it, saying high bail "keeps order."
5. Congress passes a law quartering soldiers in private homes during peacetime
   over the owners' objections.

## Task G — Source relevancy evaluation (Week 14 S2)

The adult selects one public-domain ratification-era document (a *Federalist* or
Anti-Federalist essay, a convention speech, or a state ratification record).
Evaluate it for the inquiry question **"Was ratification democratic?"**:

1. Maker: who wrote it, and what side were they on?
2. Date and place: when and where was it produced?
3. Audience and purpose: whom was it trying to persuade, and of what?
4. Relevancy: what does it prove for this question, specifically?
5. Limit: what can it not prove? Name one gap and one other source that would fill it.

## Task H — The unit argument (Week 14 S4; also project §5)

Write an argument essay (about 500–700 words) answering: **Was ratification
democratic?** Your essay must contain:

- a claim stated in the first paragraph;
- a genuine counterclaim, answered (not dismissed) in its own paragraph;
- at least three sources cited by name, each with what it proves;
- an analysis of who could and could not vote in the ratifying conventions;
- at least one stated limit — something the evidence cannot establish;
- a conclusion that says what would strengthen or weaken your claim.
