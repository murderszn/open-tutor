# Resource Pack — Unit 03: Constitution, Ratification, Rights, Institutions

**Goal (one sentence):** Give grade-8 learners reputable, free, no-account sources
that support explaining the Articles' failure, comparing the Convention's
compromises, analyzing the ratification debate, tracing checks and balances,
applying the Bill of Rights, and arguing about ratification's democratic character
— all checked 2026-10-27.

**Age fit:** Grade 8 (ages 13–14). Videos are supplements to the lessons, not
replacements; primary-source excerpts in the lessons are adult-selected and brief.
Enslavement, armed rebellion, and exclusion from voting are handled with adult
preview (see teacher guide).

## Focused queries

1. `site:archives.gov Constitution of the United States transcript preamble Article I`
2. `Articles of Confederation text Article II one vote nine states amendments unanimous`
3. `Federalist No. 10 Madison factions large republic text`

5. `ratification dates votes Delaware December 7 1787 New Hampshire June 21 1788 Rhode Island May 29 1790`
6. `Bill of Rights proposed September 25 1789 ratified December 15 1791 National Archives`

## Web references (checked 2026-10-27)

1. **National Archives — The Constitution of the United States**
   (archives.gov) — the official Constitution page with transcript link; the
   preamble wording quoted in Lesson 4 was verified here. Free, no account.
   Relevance: Lessons 2, 4; project §4.
   https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution
2. **National Archives — Bill of Rights (1791)** (archives.gov) — confirms 12
   amendments proposed September 25, 1789, and Articles 3–12 ratified December 15,
   1791, as Amendments 1–10. Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 5; Task F.
   https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/bill-of-rights
3. **National Constitution Center — "On this day, the Articles of Confederation
   are approved"** (constitutioncenter.org) — approved November 15, 1777; in force
   March 1, 1781; one vote per state; nine of thirteen to pass laws; no executive
   or judiciary. Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 1; Task A.
   https://constitutioncenter.org/blog/on-this-day-our-first-flawed-constitution-went-into-effect
4. **Ben's Guide (U.S. Government Publishing Office) — States and Dates of
   Ratification** (bensguide.gpo.gov) — the full ratification order with dates and
   votes used in Lessons 3 and 6 (Delaware 30–0 through Rhode Island 34–32).
   Free, no account. Relevance: Lessons 3, 6; project §2; assessment.
   https://bensguide.gpo.gov/j-states-ratification
5. **Constitution Annotated (Congress.gov / Library of Congress)**
   (constitution.congress.gov) — the Constitution explained article by article,
   with Supreme Court interpretation history; use the adult-selected sections on
   Articles I–III and the Bill of Rights. Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 4
   extensions; Lesson 5 reading rules. https://constitution.congress.gov/
6. **Library of Congress — Creating the United States Constitution / The
   Federalist Papers guide** (loc.gov) — primary documents from the Continental
   Congress and the Constitutional Convention, 1774–1789, and the Federalist
   Papers research guide. Free, no account. Relevance: Lessons 2–3; project §4
   source selection. (Linked from the verified Constitution Annotated home page,
   2026-10-27; use the LOC guides index to reach the current guide pages.)
   https://www.loc.gov/
7. **Massachusetts state government — "The Ratification of the United States
   Constitution"** (mass.gov) — the Federal Pillars prints, Massachusetts's
   February 6, 1788 vote, New Hampshire as the ninth pillar, Rhode Island last
   (May 29, 1790). Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 3; Lesson 6 Rhode Island
   discussion. https://www.mass.gov/news/the-ratification-of-the-united-states-constitution
