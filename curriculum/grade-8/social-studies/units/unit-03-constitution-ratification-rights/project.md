# Project — The Ratification Portfolio

**What you make:** a portfolio in five parts, assembled across Weeks 11–14 and
presented in Week 14 S4–S5. Keep each part in your private workspace; only the
portfolio itself is assessed, never your identity or personal information.

## §1 — The failure chain (Week 11 S3)

Build a four-link cause-and-effect chain from the Articles to Shays' Rebellion:

1. A rule of the Articles (quote or paraphrase it).
2. A breakdown that rule caused (with a date and a fact).
3. An attempted response that failed (and why it failed).
4. The consequence: Shays' Rebellion and the call for a convention.

Each link needs one cause and one effect, stated as a sentence pair. Draw the chain
as boxes and arrows or write it as numbered paragraphs. Your Lesson 1 proposal
(the one-rule fix) may be attached as a fifth box: "What I would have changed."

## §2 — Delegate brief and convention debate (Week 12 S3)

**Part A — Delegate brief (one page).** Choose a delegate position: a large-state
Federalist-leaning delegate, a small-state delegate, or George Mason's position
(no signature without a bill of rights). Your brief must state: your position on
representation, your position on the Three-Fifths Compromise, your red line (the
compromise you will not accept), and one quote or paraphrase from Lesson 2 or 3
that supports you.

**Part B — Deliberation record.** Hold the debate (with an adult or partner, or as
two written speeches). Record: each side's strongest argument, the compromise
reached (if any), and the argument that was hardest to answer. Then write the
deliberative comparison from practice Task D. The record is assessed on accuracy
and fairness to both sides, not on which side "won."

## §3 — Bill of Rights scenario briefs (Week 13 S3–S4)

Revise and extend the track's legacy Bill of Rights scenarios assignment into a
brief set: take the five scenarios in practice Task F and write a formal brief for
each (amendment, rule, decision, counterargument, fact that would change your mind).
Then add one scenario of your own, in the same format, based on a right you think
is most likely to be tested today. The adult previews your scenario for
appropriateness before you write it.

## §4 — Evidence-review organizer (Week 14 S3)

Revise the track's legacy Constitutional Convention evidence-review organizer into
a worked review of one Convention debate (representation, Three-Fifths, or the
executive). Your organizer must contain:

- the question;
- two sources (title, maker, date) with one quoted or paraphrased piece of
  evidence from each;
- a diagram, table, or timeline showing how the debate moved;
- your explanation of the outcome and what it postponed;
- one open question and the source you would consult next.

## §5 — Argument assembly (Week 14 S4)

Assemble practice Task H (the "Was ratification democratic?" essay) into the
portfolio with your Lesson 6 skeleton attached, so the adult can see how the
argument was built. The essay is the portfolio's capstone.

## §6 — Rubric

| Criterion | Strong (3) | Adequate (2) | Needs work (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | Dates, votes, and rules are correct; fictional practice numbers are labeled as such | One or two small errors; no invented quotations | Repeated factual errors or an invented quotation presented as real |
| Evidence use | Every claim cites a source by name; limits are stated | Most claims cited; limits mentioned | Claims without sources; no limits stated |
| Reasoning | Chains and comparisons show causes, thresholds, and trade-offs | Chains are present but miss a step or a trade-off | Assertions without steps or comparisons |
| Fairness | Counterclaims are genuine and answered; both debate sides get their strongest case | Counterclaim present but weak or dismissed | One-sided; opposing view caricatured |
| Completeness | All five parts present; presentation is organized | Four parts present, or one part thin | Two or more parts missing |
| Communication | Clear sentences; vocabulary used correctly; sources distinguished from interpretation | Mostly clear; occasional confusion of quotation and paraphrase | Unclear; quotations, paraphrase, and opinion mixed without labels |

**Scoring:** 15–18 = exceeds; 11–14 = meets; 6–10 = approaching; below 6 = revisit
with the adult. The portfolio is a learning record, not a standardized test; the
adult uses the rubric to plan reteaching, not to rank learners.
