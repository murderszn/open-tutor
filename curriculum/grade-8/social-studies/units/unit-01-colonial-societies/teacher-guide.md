# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Colonial Societies, Atlantic Exchange, Labor, Resistance

**Grade 8 Social Studies · Weeks 3–6 · for the adult/educator**
Student materials: [README](README.md) · [lessons](lessons/) · [practice](practice.md) · [project](project.md) · [assessments](assessments/) · [resource pack](resource-pack.md). Solutions: [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## Unit at a glance

The unit builds in four arcs: **map the system** (Week 3) → **compare the regions** (Week 4) → **confront enslavement and resistance** (Week 5) → **argue about the whole** (Week 6). Each week follows S1 launch → S2 reading/practice → S3 investigation → S4 writing/deliberation → S5 review + check. The [Evidence Board project](project.md) assembles one panel per week.

## Mature-content preview notes

- **Lesson 4 (Middle Passage, plantation labor):** The Equiano excerpt describes lethal overcrowding and suffering in restrained 18th-century language. Read it aloud yourself first; decide whether to read it to the learner or have them read it. No learner role-plays an enslaved person, a trader, or an overseer — ever.
- **Lesson 5 (resistance, revolt):** Covers the Stono Rebellion and the 1712 New York revolt, including executions and reprisals. Present facts plainly without graphic elaboration. The perspective-writing task (Task G) asks for a planter's and an enslaved person's viewpoints as *writing* exercises with clear labels — any learner may instead write the historian's paragraph only.
- **General:** If the learner becomes distressed, pause the lesson, acknowledge the weight of the material, and resume later. The observer/recorder role is always available in discussions.

## Lesson-by-lesson guidance

### Lesson 1 — Atlantic world map
- **Watch for:** learners drawing straight-line routes (correct with the wind-loop logic, not memorization); confusing the trade winds' direction (east→west near the equator).
- **If the exit check fails:** return to the globe — physically trace Europe → south → west → north → east while narrating the wind at each turn. The loop is one idea, not four facts.
- **Key line to land:** *Winds, not wishes, set the routes.*

### Lesson 2 — Columbian exchange
- **Watch for:** one-direction answers (only "Europe got potatoes" or only "disease killed Natives"). Insist on the T-chart's *both* columns every time.
- **Depth move:** ask "who *decided* what moved?" — crops spread by choice and accident; disease spread with no one's consent; enslaved people were moved by force. The exchange was not one kind of movement.
- **If the paragraph is thin:** require the sentence frame "___ moved ___ because ___, which caused ___."

### Lesson 3 — Three regions
- **Watch for:** memorizing the chart without the causal chain. Always ask "why *this* labor *here*?" until the environment → crop → labor link is fluent.
- **Common error:** "the South was just racist, that's why" — redirect: racism was real and load-bearing, but the *economic* mechanism was staple-crop labor demand + planter choices + laws. Both are true; the lesson teaches the mechanism.
- **Extension readiness:** learners who finish early take the Bacon's Rebellion prompt (Lesson 3 extension).

### Lesson 4 — Enslavement as a system
- **Core move:** keep the four requirements (demand / supply / law / violence) visible all week — on a wall chart or the notebook's inside cover. Every later fact should be sortable into one of the four.
- **The illustration activity** is the unit's D2.His.10 anchor: praise answers that name what the image *cannot* show more than answers that describe what it shows.
- **Arithmetic check:** 12.5 − 10.7 = 1.8 million died on the crossing. Let the learner do the subtraction; the number lands harder from their own hand.

### Lesson 5 — Resistance
- **Core move:** the spectrum (everyday → escape → revolt → protest) prevents the "why didn't they all revolt?" misconception — each form had different costs and risks.
- **The 1740-law worked example** is the unit's best "read against the grain" moment. If it clicks, name the skill explicitly so learners reuse it.
- **Task G sensitivity:** read both paragraphs yourself before any sharing. Redirect caricature ("evil planter" / "noble victim") toward interests and reasoning.

### Lesson 6 — Synthesis
- **Core move:** the profit-tracing worked example (the sugar barrel) is the moral and economic center of the unit. Take time with "whose labor created the value, and who was paid for it?"
- **Assessment rehearsal:** Task J is a low-stakes draft of the assessment essay — give feedback on *evidence use* (not position) so the assessment measures argument skill.

## Reteaching moves

- **Map skills weak (Quiz Part A < 15/19):** one extra session with the globe and the wind-loop narration; re-take Part A only.
- **Region confusion (Part B < 10/14):** rebuild the three-column chart from scratch with the learner teaching *you* each cell; require the environment → crop → labor sentence for each region.
- **System requirements shaky (Part C Q1 < 6/8):** four-box diagram drill — deal fact cards, learner sorts into demand/supply/law/violence and justifies each.
- **Source analysis weak (Assessment §2 < 8/12):** re-do Task I (ledger vs. petition) as a guided think-aloud, then re-attempt §2 with fresh eyes.
- **Week 6 recovery plan (assessment < 35/50):** two sessions — Session 1: re-teach the two lowest sections using the moves above; Session 2: learner revises the essay with the rubric beside it. Do not advance to Unit 02 until the learner can state the four system requirements and one resistance form with evidence.

## What success looks like

By Week 6 S5 the learner can: trace the Atlantic loop and explain the winds; name exchange items both directions with effects; explain the three regions' labor differences causally; list the four system requirements with examples; place resistance on the spectrum with evidence; and argue the compelling question with a claim, counterclaim, cited evidence, and a stated limit. The Evidence Board should show all four panels with limit notes — the limit notes are the clearest signal the historical-thinking objectives landed.
