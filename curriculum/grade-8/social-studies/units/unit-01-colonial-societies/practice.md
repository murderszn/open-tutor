## Task A — Exchange cause/effect paragraph (Week 3 S4)

Write a 4–6 sentence paragraph with this structure:

1. Name **one item** that moved because of the Columbian exchange and state its **direction** (Americas → Europe/Africa, or Europe/Africa → Americas).
2. Explain **why** it spread (one cause — e.g., it grew where other crops failed; people wanted it; it traveled with livestock).
3. Explain **one effect** on the receiving continent (who was affected and how).
4. End with one sentence on what your paragraph does **not** show (a limit of a single-item story).

## Task F — Resistance evidence cards (Week 5 S3)

Read each card, place it on the spectrum (**everyday / escape / revolt / protest**), and for **three** cards cite the specific evidence the card gives:

1. **Card 1 — Runaway advertisement (1740s newspaper, paraphrased from typical ads):** "Ran away, a young man, speaks good English, took a blue coat; whoever returns him shall have forty shillings." *What it proves: someone fled; the reward shows the owner wanted him back badly enough to pay.*
2. **Card 2 — Stono Rebellion (South Carolina, September 1739):** Dozens of enslaved people seized weapons, marched south toward Florida (where Spain promised freedom to escapees), and were stopped by militia; dozens died. *What it proves: organized, armed, goal-directed revolt — the largest in the mainland British colonies.*
3. **Card 3 — Broken tools:** Archaeologists at plantation sites find hoes and plow parts deliberately damaged. *What it proves: slowing or sabotaging work without open confrontation.*
4. **Card 4 — The 1688 Germantown petition:** Four Quakers formally protest the slave trade to their meeting. *What it proves: organized written protest — though by free colonists, not the enslaved themselves.*
5. **Card 5 — Secret gatherings:** Colonial laws banning enslaved people from assembling, drumming, or learning to read. *What it proves (read against the grain): people were assembling, drumming, and learning — the bans are evidence of the acts.*
6. **Card 6 — New York revolt (1712):** Enslaved people set fires and attacked responders; executions and harsher laws followed. *What it proves: urban revolt, met with terror — and resistance continuing anyway.*

## Task G — Perspective writing (Week 5 S4)

The same event: **the Stono Rebellion, September 1739.**

- Paragraph 1: a South Carolina planter writes to a friend about the rebellion. Show his interests (property, safety, order) — do not make him a cartoon villain; make his reasoning visible.
- Paragraph 2: an enslaved person who chose not to join writes privately about the same week. Show *their* interests (family safety, fear of reprisal, judgment of the risk) — not a single "enslaved viewpoint."
- Paragraph 3 (historian's voice): what do the two perspectives together reveal that neither shows alone?
