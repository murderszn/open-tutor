Learners may use their notes, maps, charts, and the unit vocabulary list. No new sources are introduced. Score with [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md).

2. Give **two** Columbian exchange items moving in **each** direction, and for **each direction** state one significant effect. (6 points)

## Section 2 — Source analysis (12 points)

Read the two excerpts (also studied in Lessons 4–5):

**Source A — Olaudah Equiano (1789):** *"The stench of the hold while we were on the coast was so intolerably loathsome, that it was dangerous to remain there for any time... the whole ship's cargo were confined together... The shrieks of the women, and the groans of the dying, rendered the whole a scene of horror almost inconceivable."*

**Source B — 1688 Germantown petition:** *"There is a saying, that we shall do to all men like as we will be done ourselves; making no difference of what generation, descent or colour they are... But to bring men hither, or to rob and sell them against their will, we stand against."*

7. For **each** source, identify the maker, the approximate date, and the purpose. (6 points — 3 per source)
8. A learner claims: "These two sources together prove exactly what the Middle Passage was like for everyone." Evaluate this claim: what **can** the two sources together establish, and what is one important **limit** of using them this way? (6 points)

## Section 3 — Argument essay (14 points)

**Compelling question:** *Was the Atlantic system primarily economic or political?*

Write a 3-paragraph essay:

- **Paragraph 1 — Claim:** Take a position (economic or political) and support it with **two** pieces of evidence from the unit.
- **Paragraph 2 — Counterclaim:** Present the strongest case for the other side with **one** piece of evidence.
- **Paragraph 3 — Limits and judgment:** State **one limit** of your own evidence (something it cannot prove), and give your final judgment in one sentence: which mattered more, and why?

**Scoring (14 points):** clear claim (2) · two relevant evidence citations (4) · genuine counterclaim with evidence (3) · stated limit (2) · reasoned judgment (2) · organization and use of unit vocabulary (1).
