# Unit 01 — Colonial Societies, Atlantic Exchange, Labor, and Resistance

**Grade 8 Social Studies · Weeks 3–6 · 20 sessions (~45 minutes each)**
**Track:** [Grade 8 Social Studies](../../README.md) · [Scope and sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md) · Issue #41

## Essential question

Who did the Atlantic system connect — and who did it benefit, exploit, or leave out?

## Overview

Between roughly 1500 and 1800, the Atlantic Ocean became a single connected system. European ships carried people, crops, animals, diseases, and ideas between the Americas, Africa, and Europe. English colonies in North America grew into three distinct regions — New England, the Middle colonies, and the Southern colonies — shaped by different environments and built on different kinds of labor. The most brutal labor system, racialized enslavement, expanded through the transatlantic slave trade: more than 12 million Africans were forced across the Atlantic (Voyages: The Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database), and resistance to that system never stopped.

Learners in this unit:

- read maps of the Atlantic world and trace two-direction exchange effects;
- compare the three colonial regions' environments, economies, and labor systems with evidence;
- analyze enslavement as a designed economic system and study resistance from everyday acts to revolt;
- close-read adult-selected public-domain primary sources (Equiano's 1789 narrative; the 1688 Germantown Quaker petition);
- argue with claims, counterclaims, and noted limitations about who benefited from the Atlantic system.

This is the track's second pass over material first surveyed in grade 5: here the work is primary sources, multiple perspectives, and historiographic argument, not narrative re-teaching.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, learners will be able to:

1. **Map and explain the Columbian exchange's two-direction effects** — trace at least three items moving each direction between the hemispheres and explain one cause and one effect per direction with evidence (D2.Geo.10.6-8; D2.Geo.11.6-8; D2.His.14.6-8).
2. **Compare the three colonial regions' labor systems** — sort evidence about New England, Middle, and Southern colonies by region and explain how environment and crop shaped the choice between family, indentured, and enslaved labor (D2.His.4.6-8; D2.Geo.10.6-8; D2.Eco.15.6-8).
3. **Analyze enslavement as a system and resistance to it** — describe what the plantation system required (supply, demand, law, violence) and identify at least four forms of resistance, citing primary-source evidence (D2.His.14.6-8; D2.His.9.6-8; D2.His.11.6-8).
4. **Argue from evidence with acknowledged limits** — write an argument about who benefited from the Atlantic system that states a claim, a counterclaim, cites at least two sources, and names what the evidence cannot show (D3.3.6-8; D3.4.6-8; D4.1.6-8; D1.1.6-8).

## Prerequisites

From the grade-7 track: constructing and interpreting maps with scale, key, and compass rose (D2.Geo.1–3.6-8); classifying source kinds and inferring maker/audience/purpose (D3.1–3.2.6-8; D2.His.9–13.6-8); writing a claim with evidence and noting a counterclaim (D3.3–3.4.6-8). From the grade-8 diagnostic (Weeks 1–2): placing 1500–1800 events on a timeline; reading a scaled map; basic supply-and-demand vocabulary.

## Vocabulary

**Atlantic world** — the connected system of Europe, Africa, and the Americas created by regular ocean trade after 1500. **Columbian exchange** — the two-direction transfer of crops, animals, diseases, people, and ideas between the hemispheres after 1492. **Mercantilism** — the economic idea that a nation's power comes from accumulating wealth through favorable trade; colonies existed to enrich the mother country. **Navigation Acts** — English laws restricting colonial trade to English ships and ports. **Indentured servitude** — labor under a contract (typically 4–7 years) exchanged for passage; temporary, unlike lifelong enslavement. **Enslavement** — the system of treating people as legal property, hereditary and lifelong in the English colonies. **Middle Passage** — the forced Atlantic crossing of enslaved Africans. **Triangular trade** — the three-legged shipping pattern linking Europe, Africa, and the Americas. **Resistance** — actions opposing enslavement, from work slowdowns and escape to revolt and protest writing. **Staple crop** — a crop grown in bulk for sale (tobacco, rice, indigo, wheat). **Charter colony / proprietary colony / royal colony** — three forms of colonial government. **Primary source** — evidence created at the time by someone with direct experience. **Reconstruction (AI)** — a modern illustration imagining a past scene; not evidence.
