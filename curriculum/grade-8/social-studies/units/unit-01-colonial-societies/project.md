# Project — The Atlantic World Evidence Board

**Unit 01 investigation · built across Weeks 3–6 · presented Week 6 S4–S5**

## Driving question

*Who did the Atlantic system connect — and who did it benefit, exploit, or leave out?*

## Product

A physical or digital **evidence board** (poster, slide deck, or notebook spread) with four panels — **Map**, **Labor**, **Resistance**, **Argument** — plus a 3-minute presentation. Every claim on the board cites its evidence; every panel names one limit of its evidence.

## Materials

- Poster board or slide deck; markers or digital shapes
- Atlantic outline map (Week 3), region chart (Week 4), evidence cards (Week 5)
- Access to the [resource pack](resource-pack.md) references
- Notebook for drafts

## Steps

### §1 Map panel (Week 3 S3)

1. Draw the Atlantic basin: three continents, the ocean, prevailing wind arrows, and the clockwise sailing loop (from Lesson 1).
2. Add **six labeled exchange items** with directional arrows: at least three Americas → Europe/Africa and three Europe/Africa → Americas (from Lesson 2).
3. Annotate **two effects** on the map itself (e.g., near Europe: "potatoes → population growth"; near the Americas: "smallpox → demographic collapse").
4. Write the panel's **limit note**: one thing the map cannot show (e.g., the human cost of the crossings, or who profited).

### §2 Labor panel (Week 4, assembled across S2–S4)

1. Build the three-region mini-chart: environment → product → labor for New England, Middle, and Southern colonies (from Lesson 3).
2. Attach **three evidence slips** (one per region): a quoted or paraphrased fact from the lessons or practice tasks that supports the labor claim (e.g., the planter's ledger entry for the South; the wheat-harvest barn-raising for the Middle colonies).
3. Write the panel's **limit note**: what the chart simplifies (e.g., "enslaved labor existed in all three regions; the chart shows where it dominated, not where it existed").

### §3 Resistance panel (Week 5, assembled across S2–S4)

1. Place **four resistance examples** on the everyday → escape → revolt → protest spectrum (from Task F), each with a one-line evidence citation.
2. Include **one primary-source quotation** (brief, attributed): either the Equiano excerpt or the Germantown petition.
3. Write the panel's **limit note**: whose voices are missing from the panel's sources, and why that matters (D2.His.10.6-8).

### §4 Argument panel (Week 6 S3–S4)

1. State your **claim** answering the driving question in one sentence.
2. Support it with **three evidence citations** drawn from at least two different panels.
3. State the strongest **counterclaim** in one sentence, with one evidence citation.
4. Write the panel's **limit note**: one thing your evidence cannot prove, and what source *would* be needed.

### §5 Presentation (Week 6 S4–S5)

Present the board in 3 minutes: one minute per panel for Map/Labor/Resistance, one minute for the Argument (claim, evidence, counterclaim, limit). Classmates (or the adult) ask one evidence question; you answer by pointing to the board.

## Deliverables

1. The four-panel evidence board (physical or digital).
2. A 3-minute presentation with a Q&A response.
3. A half-page reflection: *What was the hardest evidence problem you faced (a gap, a contradiction, a source limit), and how did you handle it?*

## Rubric

| Criterion | Exceeds (4) | Meets (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Map accuracy** | All continents, winds, loop, 6+ items, 2 effects correct and labeled | Continents, loop, 6 items, 1–2 effects correct | Missing wind arrows or loop; fewer than 6 items | Map incomplete or largely mislabeled |
| **Labor analysis** | Three correct environment→product→labor chains + 3 evidence slips with citations | Three chains correct + 2–3 evidence slips | Chains present but one link wrong or unsupported | Regions confused or labor claims uncited |
| **Resistance evidence** | 4 examples correctly placed on spectrum, each with evidence citation; quotation attributed | 4 examples placed, most cited; quotation attributed | Examples listed but spectrum placement or citations weak | Fewer than 3 examples; no citations |
| **Argument quality** | Clear claim; 3 citations from 2+ panels; strong counterclaim; honest limit note | Claim + 2–3 citations; counterclaim present; limit noted | Claim vague or evidence thin; counterclaim missing | No arguable claim |
| **Source limits** | Every panel names a specific, accurate limit | 3 panels name limits | 1–2 panels name limits | Limits missing |
| **Presentation** | Clear 3-minute tour; answers evidence question by citing the board | Covers all panels; answers the question | Rushed or skips a panel | Cannot explain board choices |

**Scoring:** 20–24 = exceeds; 15–19 = meets; 10–14 = developing; below 10 = revise and re-present. See [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) for scoring guidance and sample responses.
