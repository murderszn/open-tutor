# Project — The Expansion Portfolio (Unit 04)

**Due:** Week 18 S4–S5, presented with the unit assessment. **Format:** a
folder or binder with four sections, each labeled. Work may be handwritten or
typed. The adult scores with the rubric below and
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md) §Project. Keep this project separate from the
answer key: do not copy key wording into your portfolio.

## §1 — Party-platform brief (Week 15)

Write a one-page brief as if you advise a voter in 1800. Present the
Federalist platform and the Democratic-Republican platform on three
policies: the national bank, the strength of the national government, and
Britain vs. France. For each policy, give each party's position in one
sentence and name one economic interest behind it. End with a two-sentence
recommendation for your voter and one sentence stating what your voter would
lose if the other party won. Label the brief as a modern reconstruction.

## §2 — Expansion map and statehood timeline (Week 16)

**Map (learner-drawn schematic, not a traced atlas map):** Sketch the
eastern United States, the Mississippi River, New Orleans, and the Louisiana
Purchase territory as a labeled region. Mark: the Mississippi River, New
Orleans, the Appalachian Mountains (as a rough western edge of early
settlement), and three states admitted 1791–1821 with their admission years
from `resources/us_states.csv`. Title the sketch "Schematic — not to scale"
and write one sentence under it stating what a schematic cannot show
(exact boundaries, Native nations' territories, terrain).

**Timeline:** List the eleven admissions from practice Task C2 in order
(Vermont 1791 through Missouri 1821), marking the two from the purchase
itself. Add three more dated events from the unit: the purchase (1803), the
War of 1812 (1812–1815), and the Missouri Compromise (1820). Write two
sentences under the timeline: what the quickening pace after 1812 suggests
about expansion, and what it does not prove.

## §3 — Removal evidence organizer (Weeks 17–18)

Build a five-row table — one row per chain link: **1830 Act; 1831
*Cherokee Nation*; 1832 *Worcester*; 1835 New Echota; 1838 forced removal**.
Each row has four columns: (a) what happened (one sentence), (b) the
Cherokee action at that step, (c) the American action at that step, (d) one
source for the row (lesson, resource pack, or dataset — name it). No row may
have an empty Cherokee-action column. End with a two-sentence note: what the
organizer shows about consent, and what question it leaves open.

Sensitive-content rule: describe the forced removal factually (dates,
numbers as estimates, causes of death in general terms). Do not write
graphic scenes, and do not role-play a march or a stockade. If any part of
this section feels heavy, the learner may pause and return with the adult.

## §4 — Argument: was expansion progress? (Week 18)

Write a 400–600-word argument answering the essential question: *Expansion
made the United States bigger, richer, and more powerful — for whom, and at
whose expense?* Requirements:

- A claim and a genuine, fairly stated counterclaim.
- At least three cited sources from this unit (lesson, practice task, or
  resource-pack reference — name each).
- At least two perspectives with different interests in the same event.
- One explicitly stated limit of the evidence.
- A weighed judgment, not a slogan: concede what the other side gets right
  before concluding.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | Full credit | Partial credit | No credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| §1 Platform accuracy (8) | Three policies, both parties correct, one interest each | One policy wrong or interests missing | Platforms swapped or invented |
| §1 Recommendation (4) | Two-sentence recommendation + one loss sentence, labeled reconstruction | Recommendation without the loss sentence | No recommendation |
| §2 Map + timeline (10) | Schematic labeled not-to-scale; river, New Orleans, three dated states; eleven admissions in order, two purchase states marked; two-sentence reflection | Minor dating errors or missing reflection | No schematic label, or admissions out of order |
| §3 Organizer completeness (8) | Five rows; every row has Cherokee + American actions and a source | One row incomplete | Empty Cherokee column anywhere |
| §3 Consent note (4) | Two sentences on consent + one open question | Consent note only | Missing |
| §4 Argument (6) | Claim, fair counterclaim, 3+ sources, two perspectives, stated limit, weighed judgment | One element missing | Claim only, or counterclaim as caricature |

**Scoring:** 36–40 = exemplary; 30–35 = proficient; 22–29 = developing
(revise one section with the adult); below 22 = re-teach with the teacher
guide's Week 18 notes and resubmit §3 or §4.
