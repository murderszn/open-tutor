# Unit Assessment — Unit 04: Early Republic, Expansion, and Displacement

Given in Week 18 S5. The adult scores with [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md).
This assessment is cumulative for the unit: it tests objectives 1–6. The
Expansion Portfolio (project.md) is due the same session and is scored
separately on its 40-point rubric.

## Part 1 — Timeline and sequence (20 points, 2 each)

Put these ten events in chronological order (write the correct sequence;
one point for each event in its correct position relative to its neighbors
is not awarded — score 2 points per event placed in exactly the right slot):

Washington's Neutrality Proclamation; Bank of the United States chartered;
election of 1800; Louisiana Purchase treaty signed; War of 1812 declared;
Treaty of Ghent signed; Monroe Doctrine announced; Indian Removal Act
signed; *Worcester v. Georgia* decided; forced Cherokee removal begins.

## Part 2 — Short answers (30 points, 5 each)

1. A new national road is proposed in 1795. Write the strict-construction
   objection and the loose-construction reply, one sentence each, then state
   which side Washington's bank decision supports and why.
2. Explain why the election of 1800 mattered more as a system test than as a
   change of personnel. (Three sentences.)
3. Work the Louisiana Purchase per-acre arithmetic from memory of the inputs
   ($15 million; 828,000 square miles; 640 acres per square mile). Show each
   step and state the result in cents per acre.
4. Rank impressment, trade restrictions, and western grievances as causes of
   the War of 1812, with one reason per rank.
5. Explain the difference between the Indian Removal Act's letter
   (negotiated treaties) and its practice (compulsion) in three sentences.
6. State what *Worcester v. Georgia* decided and why the decision did not
   prevent Cherokee removal, in three sentences. Do not use the apocryphal
   Jackson quotation.

## Part 3 — Source and map work (20 points, 10 each)

1. **Source task (paraphrases, adult-selected):** Source A paraphrases
   Jackson's 1830 removal message (removal protects Native peoples and lets
   them govern themselves in the West). Source B paraphrases Cherokee
   memorials (the Cherokee are a sovereign nation; treaties guarantee their
   land). Answer: (a) maker, audience, and purpose of each (one line each);
   (b) which source is more relevant to "Did the Cherokee consent to
   removal?" and why (two sentences); (c) one limit of using paraphrases
   instead of full texts (one sentence).
2. **Map task (schematic):** Sketch the Mississippi River, New Orleans, and
   the Louisiana Purchase region, labeled "Schematic — not to scale." Mark
   three states admitted 1791–1821 with years from `us_states.csv`. Write
   two sentences under the sketch: one thing the sketch shows about
   expansion, and one thing it cannot show.

## Part 4 — Argument (30 points)

Write a 400–600-word argument answering: *Expansion made the United States
bigger, richer, and more powerful — for whom, and at whose expense?*

Requirements (6 points each): a clear claim; a fairly stated counterclaim;
at least three cited unit sources; at least two perspectives on the same
event; one explicitly stated limit of the evidence. A claim without a
counterclaim, or a counterclaim stated as caricature, cannot score above 18
on this part. See the project §4 rubric for the same standard.

**Total: 100 points.** 90–100 = exemplary; 75–89 = proficient; 60–74 =
developing (re-teach the lowest part with the teacher guide and reassess
that part); below 60 = re-teach the unit's weakest week and reassess.
