# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Early Republic, Expansion, and Displacement

This guide is for the guiding adult. It is not student material. Keep it separate
from student pages when assigning work.

## Week-by-week notes

### Week 15 — Two visions, first parties

- **S1 (Lesson 1):** Teach strict vs. loose as two disciplined readings, not
  "careful" vs. "sloppy." The worked examples are the lesson's core; do not
  skip the "necessary" wordplay — learners who miss it will confuse the two
  positions for the rest of the unit.
- **S2 (Task A):** Read the paraphrased opinions aloud. The sorting is easy
  to game by keyword; require the one-line reason per item, which is where
  understanding shows.
- **S3 (comparison table):** The "interest behind each position" column is
  the point. If a learner writes only policies, send them back for interests.
- **S4 (Lesson 2):** Stress the 1800 point twice: the losers accepted losing.
  Learners often retell 1800 as "Jefferson won," missing the system test.
- **S5 (quiz Part A):** Q6 (parties vs. modern parties) is the most-missed
  item; re-teach the name-reuse point if missed.

### Week 16 — The Louisiana Purchase

- **S1 (Lesson 3):** Do the per-acre arithmetic on paper or calculator with
  the learner, step by step. Name the rounding issue out loud: "roughly 4
  cents" (Archives) vs. ~2.8 cents (straight division) — both are "a few
  cents," and false precision is the error to avoid.
- **S2 (Task C):** Check the CSV column: `statehood_year`, not population or
  area. Learners sometimes sort by the wrong column; verify the first two
  entries before they continue.
- **S3 (image + map):** Show the flatboat illustration with its caption and
  the "not a primary source" label every time. If the learner asks what the
  image proves, answer: it shows what river trade looked like in general; it
  proves nothing about any particular boat, town, or day, and it hides the
  land question entirely — that absence is the activity's third question.
- **S4 (argument):** Accept any winner/loser pair with evidence; score the
  evidence, not the choice.
- **S5 (quiz Part B):** Re-teach Jefferson's dilemma if Q3 is missed; it
  returns in the assessment Part 2 Q1 (the road problem).

### Week 17 — War and doctrine

- **S1 (Lesson 4):** Rank causes aloud as a class before assigning Task D;
  learners who have never ranked causes will otherwise list them
  unordered and call it a ranking.
- **S2 (Tasks D–E):** The Monroe table's third row (no U.S. interference in
  Europe) is often left blank; it is the reciprocal promise, and it matters
  for understanding the doctrine as a bargain, not a boast.
- **S3 (Tecumseh investigation):** Name the point explicitly: Tecumseh's
  confederacy pursued Native interests; alliance with Britain was a means,
  not obedience. Do not let the war be retold as a two-player game.
- **S4 (cause/effect chain):** The chain must have four links; the most
  common gap is the Ghent link (treaty settles boundaries, not causes).
- **S5 (quiz Part C):** Give the Week 18 sensitive-content preview here (see
  below) so the learner is not surprised by Lesson 5.

### Week 18 — Displacement and synthesis

- **S1 (Lesson 5):** Preview before reading (see sensitive-content note).
  The chain must have five links with both sides present; a link with only
  American actors is incomplete by the lesson's own rule.
- **S2 (Tasks F–G):** Task G's paraphrase labels matter: learners must not
  quote paraphrases as if they were Jackson's or the memorials' words.
  Check the "paraphrase, not quotation" label on every answer.
- **S3 (Lesson 6):** Model the strong counterclaim aloud. Learners' first
  counterclaims are nearly always straw men; the worked example exists to
  prevent that.
- **S4 (argument assembly):** Check Task H fairness before the essay is
  written; fixing a caricature after drafting costs twice the time.
- **S5 (assessment + portfolio):** Score the assessment and portfolio
  separately. A strong portfolio does not rescue a weak Part 2, and vice
  versa; report both scores.

## Sensitive-content preview (Week 17 S5, before Week 18)

This unit covers enslavement, forced removal, stockades, and a death toll of
about 4,000 Cherokee. Teach it plainly and without graphic detail: dates,
numbers labeled as estimates, general causes of death (disease, exposure,
starvation), and Cherokee resistance and reconstitution alongside the
catastrophe. Do not assign role-play of a forced march, a stockade, or an
enslaved person's experience. The learner may pause, ask questions, or skip
to the organizer table and return to the narrative with the adult. If the
learner is distressed, the facts-and-agency framing in Lesson 5 (resistance
at every link; government reconstituted in 1839) is the right place to land.

## Misconception alerts (whole unit)

1. **"Strict = good, loose = bad" (or reverse).** Both are readings with
   rules. Score definitions, not attitudes.
2. **First parties = modern parties.** Names were reused by different
   coalitions. Correct on sight.
3. **"The land was empty."** The purchase transferred a European claim over
   inhabited territory. Correct every time it appears.
4. **"Ghent banned impressment."** It did not mention it; impressment faded
   when the Napoleonic Wars ended.
5. **"New Orleans won the war."** It was fought after Ghent; it shaped memory
   more than the treaty.
6. **"The Act ordered removal" / "The Cherokee agreed at New Echota."**
   Letter vs. practice; unauthorized signers vs. the elected government.
7. **The apocryphal Jackson quotation.** Never present it as Jackson's words;
   the accurate evidence is non-enforcement, not a quip.

## Reteaching moves

- **Strict/loose confusion:** return to Lesson 1 Worked Example 1 and
  re-define "necessary" both ways before any new material.
- **Arithmetic errors:** redo Task C1 with the adult doing step 1 (acres)
  and the learner doing step 2 (division); swap roles on the second try.
- **Ranking without reasons:** require one reason per rank in writing before
  any discussion; discussion without written reasons drifts into voting.
- **One-sided arguments:** require the perspective table (Lesson 6
  independent practice 1) before the essay draft; no table, no draft.
- **Timeline slips:** rebuild the Part 1 sequence with dated cards on a
  table; the 1803/1812/1814/1823 cluster is the usual tangle.
