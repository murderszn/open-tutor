# Resource Pack — Unit 04: Early Republic, Expansion, and Displacement

**Goal (one sentence):** Give grade-8 learners reputable, free, no-account
sources that support comparing the first parties, analyzing the Louisiana
Purchase, evaluating the War of 1812 and the Monroe Doctrine, explaining
Indian removal, and arguing about expansion — all checked 2026-11-02.

**Age fit:** Grade 8 (ages 13–14). Videos are supplements to the lessons, not
replacements; primary-source excerpts in the lessons are adult-selected,
brief, and mostly paraphrased to avoid fabricated quotations. Forced removal,
enslavement, and the Trail of Tears death toll are handled with adult preview
(see teacher guide). At least one free, no-account route supports every core
objective below; no objective depends on a video alone.

## Web references (checked 2026-11-02)

1. **National Archives — Louisiana Purchase Treaty (1803)**
   (archives.gov) — signed April 30, 1803; 828,000 square miles for $15
   million; "roughly 4 cents an acre"; Livingston and Monroe authorized for
   $10 million for New Orleans and the Floridas. Free, no account.
   Relevance: Lesson 3; Tasks C1–C2; project §2.
   https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/louisiana-purchase-treaty
2. **Gilder Lehrman, Hamilton Education Program — Hamilton on the
   Constitutionality of a National Bank, 1791** — the bank bill passed both
   houses by February 1791; Jefferson and Randolph advised unconstitutionality;
   Hamilton's February 23, 1791 opinion argued liberal construction of the
   necessary-and-proper clause. Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 1;
   Task A.
   https://hamilton.gilderlehrman.org/supporting-document/hamilton-constitutionality-national-bank-1791
3. **Annenberg Classroom — The National Bank and Federalism** — Jefferson's
   strict construction vs. Hamilton's loose construction; *McCulloch v.
   Maryland* (1819) as the later confirmation of Hamilton's reading. Free,
   no account. Relevance: Lesson 1; extension.
   https://www.annenbergclassroom.org/resource/the-pursuit-of-justice/pursuit-justice-chapter-2-national-bank-federalism/
4. **USS Constitution Museum — War of 1812 Overview** — war declared June 18,
   1812; impressment and Orders in Council as leading causes; Treaty of
   Ghent signed December 24, 1814. Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 4;
   Task D.
   https://ussconstitutionmuseum.org/major-events/war-of-1812-overview/?gad_source=1&gclid=CjwKCAiAhP67BhAVEiwA2E_9g68T785ZxXgWoiuayMJ8boHTMTr1y4GBzBm2JvnL4AuYr00PPblwDhoCAYoQAvD_BwE
5. **Britannica — War of 1812** — June 18, 1812–February 17, 1815; causes in
   maritime rights; Ghent's status quo; New Orleans fought after the treaty.
   Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 4; Task D; assessment.
   https://www.britannica.com/event/War-of-1812
6. **TeachingHistory.org — A Trail of 4,000 Tears** — about 16,000 Cherokee
   removed 1836–1839, about 4,000 perished; Georgia's 1828 annexation;
   *Worcester* (1832) unenforced; Treaty of New Echota (1835); May 1838
   roundups and stockades. Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 5; Task F;
   project §3.
   https://teachinghistory.org/history-content/ask-a-historian/a-trail-of-4000-tears/
7. **NVCC U.S. History, ch. 10.4 — Indian Removal** (pressbooks.nvcc.edu) —
   the 1830 Act and the five Southeast nations; *Worcester*'s "distinct
   political communities"; about 15,000–16,000 Cherokee relocated and as many
   as 4,000 deaths; NPS interactive Trail of Tears map pointer. Free, no
   account. Relevance: Lesson 5; Task G; project §3.
   https://pressbooks.nvcc.edu/ushistory/chapter/10-4/
