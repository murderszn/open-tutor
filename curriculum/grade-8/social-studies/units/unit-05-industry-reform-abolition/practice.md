## Task A — Canal arithmetic and supply/demand (Lesson 1; Week 20 S2)

**A1.** Show your work: a merchant ships 40 tons of wheat from Buffalo to
New York City. At the pre-canal rate of about $100 a ton, what is the cost?
At the post-canal rate of about $10 a ton, what is the cost? How much is
saved, and what percentage cut is that?

**A2.** The canal is 363 miles long and cost $7,143,000 to build. Divide to
find the cost per mile (round to the nearest dollar). Then write one
sentence: was the canal cheap or expensive for its builders, and what made
it worth building anyway?

**A3.** **Labeled fictional practice dataset — not historical data.** A
town's cloth market, in one week:

| Week | Yards available | Price per yard |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100 | $4.00 |
| 2 | 160 | $3.00 |
| 3 | 160 | $3.00 |
| 4 | 90 | $4.50 |

For each change, say whether supply or demand moved and whether the price
rose or fell: (a) Week 1 → Week 2; (b) Week 3 → Week 4. Then write two
sentences: which change matches the canal's effect on flour in the East,
and why?

**A4.** In two sentences, explain how the cotton gin could make cloth
cheaper for buyers *and* increase the demand for enslaved labor at the
same time.

## Task D — Reading the Declaration of Sentiments (Lesson 4; Week 21 S3)

**D1.** Paraphrase each borrowed line in your own words (one sentence each):

## Task G — Source evaluation (Lessons 5–6; Week 22 S4)

You are given two sources. **Source 1** is a verified quotation (public
domain, 1852); **Source 2** is a paraphrase, not a quotation:

- **Source 1 (quotation):** Frederick Douglass, Rochester, July 5, 1852:
  "What, to the American slave, is your 4th of July? I answer; a day that
  reveals to him, more than all other days in the year, the gross injustice
  and cruelty to which he is the constant victim. To him, your celebration
  is a sham; your boasted liberty, an unholy license; your national
  greatness, swelling vanity."
- **Source 2 (paraphrase):** William Lloyd Garrison's *Liberator* argues
  that slavery must end immediately and without payment to owners, and
  that the Constitution, as written, protects slavery — so moral argument,
  not politics, is the way to end it.

For each source, name: maker, intended audience, purpose, and one limit.
Then answer: which source is more useful for the question "Why did
abolitionists disagree with each other about method?" — and why? (Three
sentences.) Finally, write one sentence explaining why Source 2 must be
labeled a paraphrase and never quoted as Garrison's words.

## Task H — Argument outline (Lesson 6; Week 23 S4)

Draft the outline for your unit argument (project §4):

1. **Claim** (one sentence): which lever — law, persuasion, organization,
   or direct action — changes a society most?
2. **Counterclaim** (one fair sentence): the best version of the other side.
3. **Evidence** (three sources from this unit, one line each, with the fact
   each contributes).
4. **Perspectives** (two groups with different interests in the same event).
5. **Limit** (one sentence): what this unit's evidence does not show.
6. **Judgment** (two sentences): your weighed conclusion.

The adult checks only fairness of the counterclaim and reality of the limit
before you write the full essay.
