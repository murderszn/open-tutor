# Resource Pack — Unit 05: Industry, Reform, Abolition, and Social Change

**Goal (one sentence):** Give grade-8 learners reputable, free, no-account
sources that support explaining the market revolution, analyzing Lowell
labor, comparing reform methods, reading the Declaration of Sentiments,
and explaining abolitionism — all checked 2026-11-09.

**Age fit:** Grade 8 (ages 13–14). Videos are supplements to the lessons, not
replacements; primary-source excerpts in the lessons are adult-selected,
brief, and mostly paraphrased to avoid fabricated quotations. Enslavement,
asylum conditions, and the dangers of escape are handled with adult
preview (see teacher guide). At least one free, no-account route supports every
core objective below; no objective depends on a video alone.

## Web references (checked 2026-11-09)

1. **National Park Service — The Mill Girls of Lowell**
   (nps.gov) — first factory 1823; mill girls generally 15–30, recruited
   from New England farms; 12–14 hour days plus a half day Saturday;
   boarding houses of 20–40 women; turnouts in 1834 and 1836; Sarah Bagley
   and the ten-hour campaign; workday cut to 11 hours. Free, no account.
   Relevance: Lesson 2; Tasks B1–B4; project §2.
   https://www.nps.gov/lowe/learn/historyculture/the-mill-girls-of-lowell.htm
2. **National Park Service — Women's Rights in Seneca Falls**
   (nps.gov) — convention July 19–20, 1848, Wesleyan Chapel; five
   organizers; Declaration of Sentiments modeled on the Declaration of
   Independence; about 300 attendees; signed by 68 women and 32 men.
   Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 4; Task D; project §3.
   https://www.nps.gov/places/womensrights.htm
3. **National Park Service — Harriet Tubman and the Underground Railroad**
   (nps.gov) — escaped 1849; about 13 trips leading about 70 people, plus
   instructions to 50–60 more; "never lost a passenger" (her 1896 words);
   Fugitive Slave Act of 1850 pushed routes to Canada; met John Brown
   1858. Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 6; Task G; project §3.
   https://home.nps.gov/articles/harriet-tubman-and-the-underground-railroad.htm
4. **Britannica — William Lloyd Garrison** — *The Liberator* first issue
   January 1, 1831 ("I will not equivocate…"); New England Anti-Slavery
   Society 1832; American Anti-Slavery Society 1833; paper ran to 1865.
   Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 5; Task E; assessment.
   https://www.britannica.com/biography/William-Lloyd-Garrison
5. **Britannica — Horace Mann** — first secretary of the Massachusetts
   Board of Education, 1837; *Common School Journal* 1838; first state
   normal schools; served until 1848. Free, no account. Relevance:
   Lesson 3; Task C; project §3.
   https://www.britannica.com/biography/Horace-Mann
6. **Disability History Museum — Dorothea Dix, Memorial to the
   Legislature of Massachusetts (1843, full text)** — Dix's own words,
   including "I come to present the strong claims of suffering humanity";
   cages, closets, cellars, stalls, pens. Free, no account. Relevance:
   Lesson 3; Task C2; project §3.
   https://www.disabilitymuseum.org/dhm/lib/detail.html?id=737&print=1
7. **U.S. Senate Historical Office — "What Hath God Wrought": Morse's
   Telegraph in the Capitol** (senate.gov) — first message May 24, 1844,
   Washington to Baltimore, about 38 miles; "What hath God wrought"
   (Numbers 23:23), chosen by Annie Ellsworth; Congress appropriated
   $30,000 in 1843. Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 1; Task A;
   project §1.
   https://www.senate.gov/artandhistory/senate-stories/morses-telegraph-in-the-capitol.htm
