# Project — The Reform Portfolio (Unit 05)

**Due:** Week 23 S3–S5, presented with the unit assessment. **Format:** a
folder or binder with four sections, each labeled. Work may be handwritten or
typed. The adult scores with the rubric below and
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md) §Project. Keep this project separate from the
answer key: do not copy key wording into your portfolio.

## §1 — Innovation case: the telegraph (Week 20)

Write a one-page case study of the telegraph as innovation and
entrepreneurship (or, with the adult's agreement, the sewing machine —
see Lesson 1's extension). Cover: the problem Morse set out to solve;
the invention and its first public message (date, route, words, who chose
them); who paid for it and how; one group that gained and one group that
paid a cost; and one sentence on what the case shows about innovation in
a market economy (D2.Eco.7.6-8). Label the case study as a modern
reconstruction. Include the Erie Canal schematic from Week 20 S3 as an
appendix: your own labeled copy of
[assets/erie-canal-schematic.svg](assets/erie-canal-schematic.svg) with
Albany, Buffalo, the Hudson River, Lake Erie, 363 miles, and 1825 marked,
titled "Schematic — not to scale."

## §2 — Lowell evidence organizer (Weeks 20–21)

Build a six-row table — one row per fact — with four columns: (a) the
fact, (b) the figure, (c) what it meant for a worker (one sentence), (d)
one source for the row (lesson, resource pack, or dataset — name it).
Rows: hours per week; wages per week; board per week; boarding-house
rules; 1834 turnout; 1836 turnout. No row may have an empty source
column. End with a two-sentence note: what the organizer shows about who
held power in Lowell, and what question it leaves open. Then add a
one-paragraph take-home-pay calculation for a mill girl of your choosing
(wage and board from Lesson 2), showing the subtraction step by step.

## §3 — Reform-case presentation (Weeks 22–23)

Choose one reform movement from this unit (temperance, common schools,
asylums, women's rights, or abolition). Present its case to an audience
outside the classroom — a poster, a speech, or a letter to a real
community group or family gathering (adult-reviewed before any external
sharing; do not mail or post anything without the adult). The
presentation must: state the problem in two sentences; trace the reform
from problem to action to result (see practice Task F); name the method
used and one limit of that method; cite at least two sources from this
unit by name; and end with one sentence stating what your audience could
do next. Keep the presentation to one poster page or about 300 spoken
words. After presenting, collect one question from the audience and
answer it in two sentences in the portfolio.

Sensitive-content rule: describe enslavement, asylum conditions, and
forced labor factually (dates, numbers as estimates, conditions in
general terms). Do not write graphic scenes, and do not role-play an
enslaved person, an escape, or a patient. If any part of this section
feels heavy, the learner may pause and return with the adult.

## §4 — Argument: which lever changes a society? (Week 23)

Write a 400–600-word argument answering the essential question's second
half: *who got to decide what changed next?* — framed as: which lever
changes a society most: law, persuasion, organization, or direct action?
Requirements:

- A claim and a genuine, fairly stated counterclaim.
- At least three cited sources from this unit (lesson, practice task, or
  resource-pack reference — name each).
- At least two perspectives with different interests in the same event
  (for example, a mill owner and a mill girl; a temperance seller and a
  temperance reformer; a slaveholder and an abolitionist).
- One explicitly stated limit of the evidence.
- A weighed judgment, not a slogan: concede what the other side gets right
  before concluding.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | Full credit | Partial credit | No credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| §1 Innovation case (8) | Problem, invention, first-message facts, funder, one winner and one loser, one market-economy sentence; schematic appendix labeled and complete | One element wrong or the schematic unlabeled | Case missing, or facts invented |
| §2 Organizer completeness (10) | Six rows; every row has figure, meaning, and source; take-home calculation shown step by step; two-sentence power note + open question | One row incomplete or calculation missing steps | Empty source column anywhere, or no calculation |
| §3 Presentation (12) | Problem in two sentences; trace complete; method + limit named; 2+ sources cited; audience next step; audience question answered in two sentences | One element missing | No presentation, or no sources cited |
| §4 Argument (10) | Claim, fair counterclaim, 3+ sources, two perspectives, stated limit, weighed judgment | One element missing | Claim only, or counterclaim as caricature |

**Scoring:** 36–40 = exemplary; 30–35 = proficient; 22–29 = developing
(revise one section with the adult); below 22 = re-teach with the teacher
guide's Week 23 notes and resubmit §2 or §4.
