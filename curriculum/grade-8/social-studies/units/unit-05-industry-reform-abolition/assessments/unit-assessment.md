# Unit Assessment — Unit 05: Industry, Reform, Abolition, and Social Change

Given in Week 23 S5. The adult scores with [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md).
This assessment is culminating, not standardized or validated. It is scored
out of 40 points; the project (Reform Portfolio) is scored separately out of
40 (see [project.md](../project.md)).

## Part 1 — Market revolution and labor (10 points)

1. (2 points) A merchant ships 25 tons of wheat from Buffalo to New York.
   Show the cost before the canal (about $100 a ton) and after the canal
   (about $10 a ton), and state the saving.
2. (2 points) The telegraph's first public message was sent on May 24,
   1844. Name the message, the two cities, and the person who chose the
   words.
3. (3 points) Using the labeled practice dataset in Task A (fictional
   numbers), explain what happened to the cloth price when supply fell
   from 160 yards to 90 yards, and why. Then name one real historical
   change from Lesson 1 that moved a price, and say whether it was a
   supply change or a demand change.
4. (3 points) A mill girl earns $2.00 a week and pays $1.50 for board.
   Show her take-home pay, then write two sentences: one stating what
   she could do with it, and one stating what the wage-and-board system
   let the corporation control.

## Part 2 — Reform and Seneca Falls (10 points)

5. (3 points) Match each reformer to the correct movement and method:
   (a) Horace Mann, (b) Dorothea Dix, (c) Neal Dow, (d) Elizabeth Cady
   Stanton. One line each: movement, method, one result.
6. (3 points) In three sentences, explain the temperance movement's
   method ladder: what persuasion achieved, where it stopped working, and
   what law added.
7. (4 points) Quote or paraphrase the opening of the Declaration of
   Sentiments, then write three sentences: (a) what Stanton borrowed from
   the Declaration of Independence, (b) what she changed, (c) why the
   suffrage resolution was the hardest fight at the convention.

## Part 3 — Source task: Douglass, July 5, 1852 (10 points)

Read this verified quotation (public domain, 1852) from Frederick
Douglass's Rochester address, "What to the Slave Is the Fourth of July?":

> "What, to the American slave, is your 4th of July? I answer; a day that
> reveals to him, more than all other days in the year, the gross injustice
> and cruelty to which he is the constant victim. To him, your celebration
> is a sham; your boasted liberty, an unholy license; your national
> greatness, swelling vanity."

8. (3 points) Name the maker, the date and place, the intended audience,
   and the purpose. (One line each.)
9. (3 points) In three sentences, explain the paradox Douglass is
   exposing: what the Fourth of July celebrates, what it meant for an
   enslaved person in 1852, and why Douglass calls the celebration "a
   sham."
10. (2 points) Name one limit of this source for learning about slavery
    in 1852, and one further source that would help answer the question
    "How did enslaved people themselves experience the Fourth of July?"
11. (2 points) Douglass's speech is an example of moral suasion. In two
    sentences, explain what moral suasion is and name one thing it can do
    that law cannot — and one thing law can do that it cannot.

## Part 4 — Argument: who decides what changes? (10 points)

12. (10 points) Write a 200–300-word argument answering this question:
    *Which lever — law, persuasion, organization, or direct action —
    changes a society most?* Use at least two movements from this unit as
    evidence. Requirements: a claim; a genuine, fairly stated
    counterclaim; at least two cited sources from this unit (name each);
    at least two perspectives with different interests in the same event;
    one explicitly stated limit of the evidence. Score with the
    [answer key](../answer-key.md) §Assessment.
