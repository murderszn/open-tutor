# Practice Tasks — Unit 02: Revolution, Independence, Contested Ideals

Referenced by the [session plan](README.md). Solutions and scoring notes are in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) — adult only.

## Task A — The colonial case in one paragraph (Week 7 S4)

Write one paragraph (5–7 sentences) making the colonial case against Britain as a patriot pamphleteer in 1775 might. Include: one specific grievance (with date), one colonial action already taken (boycott, congress, protest), and one British response. End with one sentence stating what the colonists want.

## Task B — Battles in broader context (Week 8 S2)

For each of Lexington/Concord (1775), Saratoga (1777), and Yorktown (1781), write two sentences: one connecting the battle to an earlier cause (D2.His.1) and one connecting it to a later consequence. Then answer: which of the three best fits the label "turning point," and why?

## Task C — One turning point, fully explained (Week 8 S4)

Choose Saratoga or the French alliance. Write a half-page explanation with **two causes** (why it happened) and **two effects** (what it changed), each cause/effect tied to a specific fact from the lessons. Underline your strongest cause-effect link and write one sentence defending why it is the strongest.

## Task E — Structured deliberation prep (Week 9 S4)

Resolution: **"The American Revolution fulfilled its ideals."** Prepare both sides:

- **For:** two evidence cards (source + one-sentence use).
- **Against:** two evidence cards (source + one-sentence use).
- **Your judgment:** one paragraph stating your verdict *with a qualifier* (for whom / in what respect), plus one sentence naming the strongest evidence against your own verdict.

Bring the cards to the deliberation; the adult records the strongest limit named by either side.

## Task F — Whose voices are missing? (Week 10 S2)

List **three** groups or individuals whose perspective is absent or thin in this unit's sources (e.g., loyalist farmers, British soldiers, Native women, indentured servants, the French). For each: (1) name what kind of source they *might* have left (letter, petition, oral account, ledger); (2) write two sentences on what that source might have recorded that our sources do not; (3) state one reason the source may not exist (illiteracy, destruction, never created). (D2.His.10)

## Task G — Comparing two secondary accounts (Week 10 S3)

Using the two account summaries from Lesson 6 (political narrative vs. social history):

1. State each account's main claim in one sentence.
2. Name one piece of evidence Account A uses that Account B downplays, and vice versa.
3. Write 3–4 sentences: do the accounts disagree about *facts* or about *which question matters*? Give one example supporting your answer. (D1.4)

## Task H — The turning-point argument (Week 10 S4)

Write an essay (4–6 paragraphs) answering: **Was the American Revolution a turning point?**

Requirements: a qualified thesis (for whom / in what respect); a genuine counterclaim with evidence; at least **three** unit sources cited (one must be a primary-source excerpt); one paragraph or closing section explicitly naming what your evidence **cannot** establish (a limit); no new uncited factual claims in the conclusion. See the rubric in [project.md](project.md) §4 and the scoring notes in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).
