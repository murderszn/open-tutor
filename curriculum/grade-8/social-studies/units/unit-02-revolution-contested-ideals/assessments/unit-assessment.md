# Unit Assessment — Unit 02: Revolution, Independence, Contested Ideals

**Week 10 S5 · ~45 minutes.** Keys and scoring in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) — adult only.

## §1 — Sequence and explain (24)

1. Build a mini-timeline: place **six** of these eight events in order with correct years — Stamp Act, Boston Massacre, Boston Tea Party, Lexington/Concord, Declaration of Independence, Saratoga, French alliance, Yorktown. (6)
2. Choose **two** timeline events and code each as change, continuity, or both, with a one-sentence reason per event. (6)
3. Explain in 3–4 sentences why the colonies won the war despite Britain's greater resources. Name two structural causes. (6)
4. Paraphrase the Declaration's preamble argument ("We hold these truths...") in 2–3 sentences of your own. (6)

## §2 — Source analysis (14)

Read the two excerpts (from Lesson 4):

- **Source A:** Wheatley to Occom (1774) — "for in every human Breast, God has implanted a Principle, which we call Love of Freedom..."
- **Source B:** Dunmore's Proclamation (1775) — "...declare all indentured Servants, Negroes, or others, (appertaining to Rebels,) free, that are able and willing to bear Arms..."

5. For **each** source, identify the maker, the audience, and the purpose (one sentence each — 3 per source). (6)
6. A historian wants to prove that enslaved people actively pursued freedom during the Revolution. Evaluate: what can this pair of sources establish, and what is their limit? Write 4–6 sentences with at least one "can establish" claim and one explicit limit. (8)

## §3 — Turning-point essay (14)

7. Answer in 4–6 paragraphs: **Was the American Revolution a turning point?** Requirements: a qualified thesis (for whom / in what respect); a genuine counterclaim with evidence; at least **three** sources cited from the unit (at least one primary excerpt); a closing passage naming what your evidence **cannot** establish. Scored with the project rubric's Argument row ([project.md](../project.md) §4) plus the notes in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md).
