# Formative Quiz — Unit 02: Revolution, Independence, Contested Ideals

Given in three parts at the Week 7, 8, and 9 S5 review sessions. Keys and scoring in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) — adult only.

## Part A — The road to independence (Week 7 S5)

1. Place these four events in chronological order: Boston Tea Party, Stamp Act, Lexington and Concord, Boston Massacre. (4)
2. Explain "no taxation without representation" in 2–3 sentences, using one specific tax as your example. (4)
3. The Stamp Act was repealed in 1766. Was the repeal an example of change, continuity, or both? Defend your coding in 2–3 sentences. (4)
4. Name one British action and the colonial response it produced, for **two** different events between 1767 and 1774. (4)

**Part A total: 16**

## Part B — War, turning points, alliances (Week 8 S5)

1. Name two British advantages and two colonial advantages at the start of the war. (4)
2. Complete the causal chain with one linking sentence per arrow: Saratoga (1777) → ______ → French alliance (1778) → ______ → Yorktown (1781). (4)
3. A classmate says, "Yorktown was the battle where America won the war single-handedly." Write 3–4 sentences correcting this claim with two facts. (5)

**Part B total: 13**

## Part C — Contested ideals and sources (Week 9 S5)

1. Quote or closely paraphrase one sentence from Wheatley's 1774 letter and state what it argues. (3)
2. State Dunmore's purpose in issuing his 1775 proclamation in one sentence, and explain why that purpose matters for reading the document. (4)
3. Which source — Wheatley's letter or Dunmore's Proclamation — better shows what enslaved people themselves wanted? Give two reasons. (4)
4. Name one group whose perspective is missing from this unit's sources so far, and state one thing their missing source might have recorded. (3)

**Part C total: 14**

**Quiz total: 43**
