# Resource Pack — Unit 02: Revolution, Independence, Contested Ideals

**Goal (one sentence):** Give grade-8 learners reputable, free, no-account sources that support sequencing the road to independence, close-reading the Declaration, analyzing contested perspectives, and evaluating how images and records shape historical memory — all checked 2026-10-20.

**Age fit:** Grade 8 (ages 13–14). Videos are supplements to the lessons, not replacements; primary-source excerpts in the lessons are adult-selected and brief. War, enslavement, and propaganda are handled with adult preview (see teacher guide).

## Web references (checked 2026-10-20)

1. **National Archives — Declaration of Independence** (archives.gov) — the official transcription of the Declaration used to verify this unit's preamble and grievance wording; also hosts the drafting history. Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 2 close read; Task D. (Wording verified against the Archives transcription via the unit's source check, 2026-10-20.)
2. **Gilder Lehrman Institute — "Paul Revere's 'The Bloody Massacre,' 1770"** (hamilton.gilderlehrman.org) — the engraving with context: produced three weeks after the event, based on Henry Pelham's drawing, "probably the most effective piece of war propaganda in American history." Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 5. https://hamilton.gilderlehrman.org/supporting-document/paul-reveres-bloody-massacre-1770
3. **Smithsonian National Museum of American History — "Comparing coffins, remembering the Boston Massacre"** (americanhistory.si.edu) — how Revere's engraving shaped memory of March 5, 1770; notes the print was "not an accurate depiction." Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 5. https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/stories/remembering-boston-massacre
4. **American Battlefield Trust — "Lord Dunmore's Proclamation of 1775"** (battlefields.org) — full public-domain text with context (drafted Nov 7, published Nov 14, 1775; the Ethiopian Regiment; Great Bridge). Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 4; Task D. https://www.battlefields.org/learn/primary-sources/lord-dunmores-proclamation-1775?ms=tworg%23%21
5. **Bill of Rights Institute — "Lord Dunmore's Proclamation, 1775"** (billofrightsinstitute.org) — the proclamation with student activities on Black participation and natural rights. Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 4 extension. https://billofrightsinstitute.org/activities/lord-dunmores-proclamation-1775/
6. **National Constitution Center — "Forgotten Founders: Phillis Wheatley"** (constitutioncenter.org) — Wheatley's 1774 Occom letter in context, her 1773 *Poems*, and Washington's 1776 reply. Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 4; Lesson 6 significance activity. https://constitutioncenter.org/amp/blog/forgotten-founders-phillis-wheatley-african-american-poet-of-the-revolution
7. **American Literature I open anthology — Wheatley's letter to Rev. Samson Occom** (pressbooks.pub) — public-domain full text of the March 11, 1774 letter; wording matches this unit's excerpt. Free, no account. Relevance: Lesson 4 close read verification. https://pressbooks.pub/amlit1to1865/chapter/letter-to-rev-samson-occom-1774-phillis-wheatley/
