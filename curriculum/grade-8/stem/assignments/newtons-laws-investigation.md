## 🎯 Investigation Objective & Background

Sir Isaac Newton formulated three foundational laws that govern classical mechanics. In this investigation, you will act as the lead experimental physicist to design, execute, and analyze a quantitative physics experiment testing the relationship between **Force, Mass, and Acceleration** ($F = ma$) as well as the **Conservation of Momentum** ($p = mv$) during inelastic and elastic collisions.

### Core Physical Laws Under Investigation:
1.  **Newton's 1st Law (Inertia)**: An object at rest remains at rest, and an object in motion remains in motion at a constant velocity unless acted upon by an unbalanced external force ($\Sigma F \neq 0$).
2.  **Newton's 2nd Law ($F = ma$)**: The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass ($a = \frac{F_{net}}{m}$).
3.  **Newton's 3rd Law (Action-Reaction)**: For every action force exerted on Object B by Object A, Object B exerts an equal and opposite reaction force on Object A ($F_{A \to B} = -F_{B \to A}$).
4.  **Momentum & Collisions**: Linear momentum is defined as $p = mv$. In a closed system with no external forces, total initial momentum equals total final momentum ($\Sigma p_{initial} = \Sigma p_{final}$).

## 📋 Experimental Procedures

### Part A: Investigating Newton's 2nd Law (Varying Mass with Constant Incline)
1.  Set up the ramp at a fixed incline angle (e.g., $15^\circ$ or raised 15 cm at one end). Keep ramp angle constant for all trials in Part A.
2.  Measure the total track distance ($d$) from the release line to the bottom.
3.  **Trial Set 1 (Base Mass)**: Measure the mass of the empty cart ($m_1$). Release from rest ($v_0 = 0$). Record the time ($t$) to reach the bottom. Repeat 3 times and calculate average time $t_{avg}$.
4.  Calculate average acceleration using kinematics: $d = v_0 t + \frac{1}{2}at^2 \implies a = \frac{2d}{t_{avg}^2}$.
5.  **Trial Set 2 & 3 (Increased Mass)**: Add mass to the cart ($m_2 = m_1 + 100g$, $m_3 = m_1 + 200g$) and repeat Step 3 & 4.
6.  Compare net accelerating force: $F_{gravity,\parallel} = m \cdot g \cdot \sin(\theta)$ versus observed acceleration.

### Part B: Newton's 3rd Law & Momentum Transfer (Collision Impact)
1.  Position the target block at the base of the ramp on a flat, uniform surface (e.g., hardwood floor or table).
2.  Release the cart from a fixed height ($h_1$).
3.  Upon collision at the bottom, measure the slide distance of the target block ($d_{slide}$) in centimeters.
4.  Double the cart mass ($2m$) and release from the exact same height.
5.  Measure the new slide distance. Analyze how doubling the momentum ($p = mv$) alters the kinetic energy and work done against friction ($W = F_{friction} \cdot d_{slide} = \Delta KE$).

## Interpretation Note
On an ideal fixed-angle ramp, both gravitational force and mass increase together, so acceleration need not decrease as mass increases. Wheel rotation and friction complicate real results. A target’s slide distance alone does not directly measure impact force or demonstrate momentum conservation; use measured velocities or an educator-provided simulation.
