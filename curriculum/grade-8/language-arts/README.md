# Grade 8 Language Arts

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are
delivered; the eight units are planned, not yet written. See
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained
for one grade-8 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials, and
supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in
private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 8 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-8 **reading and writing** course: close
reading and evidence-based inference, Shakespearean drama (character foils,
dramatic irony, adaptation), informational rhetoric and conflicting accounts,
grammar (voice, verbals, punctuation) taught through revision, narrative craft
(perspective, pacing, scenes), argument writing with counterclaims and
rebuttals, short research projects with attribution, and comparative poetry
analysis ending in a presentation and writing portfolio. Each of the eight units
runs four weeks at about five 45-minute sessions per week, blending direct
instruction, modeled reasoning, close-reading practice, writing workshops,
peer/adult discussion, and revision.

This is a proposed pathway, not a universal requirement: schools vary in text
choices and unit order. A guiding adult checks placement before starting and
previews every literary selection for mature themes (see the audit's text-rights
notes).

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Cite the strongest textual evidence for explicit and inferential claims
   about literature and informational text.
2. Determine a theme or central idea and analyze how it develops across a
   text (including through characters, setting, and plot); write an
   objective summary.
3. Analyze how particular lines of dialogue or incidents propel action,
   reveal character, or provoke decisions.
4. Analyze word choice — including figurative and connotative meanings —
   for its effect on meaning and tone; explain allusions to other texts.
5. Compare the structures of two or more texts and analyze how differences
   in structure and point of view (including dramatic irony) create effects
   such as suspense or humor.
6. Analyze how a filmed or staged adaptation stays faithful to or departs
   from a text or script, evaluating the director's or actors' choices.
7. Delineate and evaluate an argument's claims, assessing whether the
   reasoning is sound and the evidence relevant and sufficient; recognize
   irrelevant evidence.
8. Analyze conflicting accounts of the same topic; describe how an author
   acknowledges and responds to conflicting evidence or viewpoints.
9. Write arguments that introduce and distinguish claims from counterclaims,
   support them with logical reasoning and credible evidence, use cohesive
   transitions, and end with a supported conclusion.
10. Write narratives with effective technique — dialogue, pacing,
    description, reflection, sensory language — in a well-structured event
    sequence with a reflective conclusion.
11. Conduct short research projects: gather information from multiple
    sources, assess credibility, quote or paraphrase without plagiarism, and
    follow a standard citation format.
12. Demonstrate grade-8 grammar conventions: the function of verbals
    (gerunds, participles, infinitives); verbs in the active and passive
    voice and in the conditional and subjunctive mood; standard punctuation
    including semicolons, colons, and conjunctive adverbs.
13. Engage in collaborative discussion with preparation and evidence;
    present claims and findings with multimedia support in formal English.
14. Determine the meaning of unknown words using context, Greek/Latin
    affixes and roots, and reference materials; use grade-appropriate
    academic and domain-specific vocabulary accurately.

## Standards summary

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for English Language Arts,
grade 8** (strands RL.8, RI.8, W.8, SL.8, L.8). Codes and descriptions were
verified 2026-10-06 on thecorestandards.org; the scope-and-sequence paraphrases
them and maps each unit to its standards. No state adoption, accreditation, or
alignment certification is claimed.

## Planned units (prose until built)

- U01 — Close reading: inference, theme, and evidence synthesis
- U02 — Literature: drama, character foils, and adaptation
- U03 — Informational rhetoric: argument and conflicting accounts
- U04 — Grammar: voice, verbals, punctuation, and style
- U05 — Narrative: perspective, pacing, and crafted scenes
- U06 — Argument writing: counterclaims, rebuttals, and reasoning
- U07 — Research synthesis, attribution, and explanatory essays
- U08 — Poetry: comparative analysis, presentations, and portfolio

## Existing material and its status

The track currently holds 22 legacy draft files: 3 assignments, 16 quizzes,
and 2 templates (see the audit for the keep/revise map). They contain usable
question stems and project seeds but **no answer keys outside embedded
collapsible blocks, no lessons, no teacher guides, no diagnostics, and no
units** — the eight units will be authored from scratch, re-verifying and
re-separating legacy content at build time. Full details, source checks, and
the 36-week pacing plan are in
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).
