## 📋 Assessment Questions

**1.** What is the central existential question posed in the opening line: *"To be, or not to be, that is the question"*?

**2.** Analyze the mixed metaphor in lines 57–59: *"Or to take arms against a sea of troubles, and by opposing end them."* Why is fighting a 'sea' an intentional logical paradox?

**3.** In lines 60–64, how does Hamlet describe death as sleep, and what makes this sleep initially seem desirable?

**4.** What sudden complication shifts Hamlet's thinking in line 65 (*"To sleep, perchance to dream: ay, there's the rub"*)?

**5.** What does the archaic noun **"the rub"** mean in this context?

**6.** Hamlet refers to the afterlife as *"The undiscover'd country from whose bourn no traveller returns."* What does this fear of the unknown cause humans to do?

**7.** Explain the irony of Hamlet claiming *"no traveller returns"* from the afterlife when he spent Act I speaking directly with his dead father's ghost.

**8.** Analyze the imagery in lines 83–85: *"And thus the native hue of resolution is sicklied o'er with the pale cast of thought."* What do the contrasting colors represent?

**9.** What does Hamlet mean when he concludes that *"enterprises of great pith and moment... lose the name of action"*?

**10.** How does this entire soliloquy encapsulate Hamlet's primary psychological struggle throughout the play?

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
Whether it is nobler to endure the agonies of living existence ('to be') or to choose death and end one's consciousness ('not to be').

**Question 2:**  
One cannot physically put on armor and fight an ocean; a sea will inevitably drown anyone who battles it. The metaphor highlights the futility and overwhelming magnitude of human suffering against cosmic fate.

**Question 3:**  
He equates death to an eternal sleep that ends the heartache and the 'thousand natural shocks' that mortal flesh is subject to.

**Question 4:**  
He realizes that if death is like sleep, death may also contain dreams; the terrifying uncertainty of what nightmarish visions might haunt an eternal afterlife stops him.

**Question 5:**  
A bowling term from lawn bowls referring to an obstacle or uneven bump on the turf that deflects the ball; here it means the crucial catch, obstacle, or dilemma.

**Question 6:**  
It makes humans choose to endure the miserable, familiar burdens of life rather than escape into an unknown, potentially more terrifying afterlife.

**Question 7:**  
The ghost of King Hamlet returned from purgatory/the afterlife to command Hamlet to avenge his murder; Hamlet's statement reveals his deep philosophical skepticism and doubt about whether the ghost was genuine or a demonic delusion.

**Question 8:**  
The 'native hue of resolution' is a ruddy, vibrant, healthy flush of immediate action; the 'pale cast of thought' is the sickly, drained pallor of over-intellectualizing and second-guessing.

**Question 9:**  
Grand, crucial plans of monumental importance become paralyzed and abandoned because of endless philosophical rumination.

**Question 10:**  
It captures his tragic paralysis: his hyper-analytical intellect and moral hesitation continually prevent him from executing the swift, decisive action needed to avenge his father.
