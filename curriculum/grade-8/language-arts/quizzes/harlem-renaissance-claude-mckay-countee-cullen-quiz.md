# Grade 8 Language Arts — Harlem Renaissance: Claude McKay & Countee Cullen Quiz

**Topic:** Harlem Renaissance: Claude McKay & Countee Cullen  

This quiz analyzes Claude McKay's militant Elizabethan sonnets and Countee Cullen's classical lyricism, examining how Harlem Renaissance writers repurposed traditional European poetic forms.

## 📋 Assessment Questions

**1.** What traditional 14-line poetic structure did Jamaican-born poet Claude McKay utilize for his explosive protest poem *"If We Must Die"*?

**2.** What historical period of racial violence in the summer of 1919 inspired McKay to write *"If We Must Die"*?

**3.** Analyze the opening lines of *"If We Must Die"*: *"If we must die, let it not be like hogs / Hunted and penned in an inglorious spot..."* What animal comparison does McKay reject?

**4.** What values does McKay advocate for those facing overwhelming violent oppression?

**5.** Why was Winston Churchill later moved to recite McKay's *"If We Must Die"* to the British Parliament during the darkest days of World War II?

**6.** How did Countee Cullen's poetic philosophy differ from that of Langston Hughes regarding European literary traditions?

**7.** In Cullen's short poem *"Incident"*, the speaker recalls a childhood visit to Baltimore. What single racist slur shattered his joyful memory of the eight-month trip?

**8.** Analyze the concluding couplet of *"Incident"*: *"Of all the things that happened there / That's all that I remember."* What psychological truth about racial trauma does this convey?

**9.** In Cullen's famous sonnet *"Yet Do I Marvel"*, what is the final, miraculous paradox he ponders: *"Yet do I marvel at this curious thing: / To make a poet black, and bid him sing!"*?

**10.** How does the mastery of classical poetic forms (sonnets, iambic meter, rhyming couplets) by McKay and Cullen challenge white supremacist assumptions of the early 20th century?

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
The Shakespearean (English) Sonnet (14 lines in iambic pentameter with an ABAB CDCD EFEF GG rhyme scheme).

**Question 2:**  
The 'Red Summer' of 1919, during which white supremacist mobs attacked Black communities in dozens of cities across the United States.

**Question 3:**  
He rejects dying like trapped, slaughtered hogs who squeal helplessly in pens, insisting that human beings must face death with warrior dignity and defiance.

**Question 4:**  
Courage, noble resistance, self-defense, mutual solidarity, and fighting back even against insurmountable odds (*'noble death'*, *'defying monsters'*).

**Question 5:**  
The poem's universal call for fearless, outnumbered defiance resonated universally with Britain standing alone against Nazi totalitarianism.

**Question 6:**  
Cullen believed Black poets should master classical English poetic forms (like Keats and Shelley) to prove their equality within the highest traditions of world literature, whereas Hughes prioritized authentic Black musical forms and vernacular.

**Question 7:**  
A white boy roughly his own age stuck out his tongue and called him the N-word.

**Question 8:**  
It shows how a single moment of visceral racial malice can completely overwhelm and erase months of positive memories, leaving an indelible scar on a child's consciousness.

**Question 9:**  
He marveled that God, who created inexplicable mysteries like blind moles and Greek mythological suffering, would bestow upon a Black person the divine gift of poetic song in a society designed to silence and degrade him.

**Question 10:**  
It demonstrated intellectual and artistic equality at the highest technical level, wielding the very poetic instruments of European tradition to articulate Black dignity and demand social justice.
