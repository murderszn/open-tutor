## 📋 Assessment Questions

**1.** In what city and along what river was Shakespeare's famous Globe Theatre constructed in 1599?

**2.** Who were the "groundlings" in Elizabethan theater, and where did they stand during performances?

**3.** Why were Elizabethan plays performed in open daylight without modern theatrical stage lighting?

**4.** Explain the difference between **prose** and **verse** in Shakespeare's plays, and how Shakespeare used this distinction to signal social class or psychological states.

**5.** What is the definition of **iambic pentameter**?

**6.** How many syllables are in a standard line of iambic pentameter, and what is the stress pattern of an **iamb**?

**7.** Mark the stressed (/) and unstressed (u) syllables in this iconic line: *"Two households, both alike in dignity"*.

**8.** What is **blank verse**?

**9.** What is **dramatic irony**? Provide an example from any play or story.

**10.** Why did Elizabethan theater companies feature all-male casts, with young boys playing the roles of women like Juliet and Lady Macbeth?

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
London, England; along the south bank of the River Thames (Southwark).

**Question 2:**  
Groundlings were lower-class spectators who paid a single penny to stand in the open-air yard ('the pit') directly in front of the stage.

**Question 3:**  
The theater had an open-air, thatched-roof design and relied entirely on natural sunlight; artificial indoor stage lighting did not yet exist.

**Question 4:**  
Verse is structured in rhythmic poetic meter (iambic pentameter), spoken by nobility, royalty, and tragic heroes during moments of high emotion. Prose is unmetered, everyday speech, spoken by commoners, servants, clowns, or nobles when descending into madness or colloquial banter.

**Question 5:**  
A poetic meter consisting of five metrical feet (iambs) per line, where each iamb consists of one unstressed syllable followed by one stressed syllable.

**Question 6:**  
Ten syllables per line (5 feet $\times$ 2 syllables). An iamb has an unstressed-stressed pattern: `da-DUM`.

**Question 7:**  
`u  /   | u   /   |  u   /  | u   / | u  / ` (Two HOUSE | holds, BOTH | a-LIKE | in DIG | ni-TY).

**Question 8:**  
Unrhymed iambic pentameter.

**Question 9:**  
Dramatic irony occurs when the audience or reader knows a critical piece of information or secret that a character on stage does not yet know (e.g., the audience knows Juliet is only asleep from a potion, but Romeo genuinely believes she is dead).

**Question 10:**  
Elizabethan social customs and legal statutes strictly barred women from the public acting profession, considering it improper; thus, pre-pubescent boys with high vocal registers played female roles.
