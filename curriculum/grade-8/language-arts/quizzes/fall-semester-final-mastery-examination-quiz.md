## 📋 Assessment Questions

**1.** Formulate the five structural components of a comprehensive argumentative essay.

**2.** Identify the logical fallacy: *"Either you support our candidate's defense budget completely, or you hate our country and want our military to fail."*

**3.** Define the Aristotelian appeals of **Ethos, Pathos, and Logos**, and provide a brief example of each.

**4.** What was Frederick Douglass's core rhetorical argument in *"What to the Slave Is the Fourth of July?"*?

**5.** What mathematical and historical milestone did Lincoln commemorate with *"Four score and seven years ago"*?

**6.** Define **blank verse** and scan the meter of standard Shakespearean dialogue.

**7.** What is a **character foil**? Name one pair of foils from *Romeo and Juliet* and explain how their contrast functions.

**8.** In *Hamlet*, what is the central philosophical dilemma explored in the *"To be, or not to be"* soliloquy?

**9.** Explain the extended metaphor in Langston Hughes's *"Mother to Son"* and Claude McKay's sonnet structure in *"If We Must Die"*.

**10.** Correct all mechanical errors in this compound-complex sentence: *"The book was read by Learner in two days however he did not write his reflection until Friday morning"* (Ensure active voice, proper semicolon, and comma placement).

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
1) Debatable Claim/Thesis, 2) Supporting Sub-claims with Evidence, 3) Analytical Commentary/Warrants, 4) Counterclaim with Rebuttal, 5) Concluding Synthesis with Broader Significance.

**Question 2:**  
False Dilemma (Either/Or fallacy).

**Question 3:**  
Ethos = moral credibility/authority (e.g., 'As a licensed physician...'); Pathos = emotional/empathetic resonance (e.g., 'Picture a child weeping in fear...'); Logos = logical reasoning/data (e.g., 'Clinical trials showed an 80% recovery rate...').

**Question 4:**  
That celebrating national freedom and liberty while holding millions of Black people in chattel slavery is a gross, hypocritical mockery of American founding principles.

**Question 5:**  
87 years ($4 \times 20 + 7$), commemorating the 1776 Declaration of Independence and the founding proposition that all men are created equal.

**Question 6:**  
Unrhymed iambic pentameter: lines of 10 syllables following an unstressed/stressed pattern (`da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM`).

**Question 7:**  
A foil is a character whose traits contrast with another's to accentuate specific qualities. Tybalt (aggressive firebrand) vs. Benvolio (peacemaker) or Mercutio (cynical realist) vs. Romeo (sentimental romantic).

**Question 8:**  
The agony of living versus the peaceful release of death, complicated by the terrifying uncertainty of what dreams may come in the unknown afterlife, paralyzing the will to take action.

**Question 9:**  
Hughes compares life to an arduous, uncarpeted staircase that requires continual climbing; McKay uses the 14-line Elizabethan sonnet to channel militant racial self-defense and dying with human dignity.

**Question 10:**  
Corrected Active & Punctuated: *"Learner read the book in two days; however, he did not write his reflection until Friday morning."*
