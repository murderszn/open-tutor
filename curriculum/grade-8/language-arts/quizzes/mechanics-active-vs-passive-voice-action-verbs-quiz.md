# Grade 8 Language Arts — Mechanics: Active vs. Passive Voice & Action Verbs Quiz

**Topic:** Mechanics: Active vs. Passive Voice & Action Verbs  

This quiz tests your mastery of grammatical voice, teaching you how to diagnose and transform passive constructions into forceful, concise active voice.

## 📋 Assessment Questions

**1.** What is the grammatical definition of **active voice**?

**2.** What is the grammatical definition of **passive voice**?

**3.** How can you identify a passive verb phrase by its auxiliary (helping) verb and past participle?

**4.** Why do professional editors and essay readers generally advise against overusing passive voice in argumentative writing?

**5.** Identify whether the following sentence is Active or Passive: *"The intricate chess combination was calculated by Learner in less than forty seconds."*

**6.** Identify whether the following sentence is Active or Passive: *"The Supreme Court ruled that the municipal surveillance ordinance violated the Fourth Amendment."*

**7.** Transform this weak passive sentence into vigorous active voice: *"A brilliant touchdown pass was thrown by the quarterback into double coverage."*

**8.** Transform this passive sentence into active voice: *"Mistakes were made during the laboratory synthesis by the chemistry students."*

**9.** In what rare scientific or journalistic scenarios is using passive voice actually justified or preferred?

**10.** Revise this wordy, passive sentence to be concise, active, and punchy: *"It was decided by the school board committee that an increase in student technology funding would be approved."*

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
In active voice, the subject of the sentence performs the action expressed by the verb (e.g., *'The chef prepared the meal'*).

**Question 2:**  
In passive voice, the subject of the sentence receives the action, and the agent performing the action is either placed in a prepositional phrase (*'by...'* ) or omitted entirely (e.g., *'The meal was prepared by the chef'*).

**Question 3:**  
Passive voice almost always uses a conjugated form of the verb 'to be' (*is, was, were, been, being*) followed immediately by a past participle (e.g., *was conducted, were broken, has been observed*).

**Question 4:**  
Passive voice often creates unnecessary wordiness, obscures who is responsible for an action, and drains vitality and momentum from the prose.

**Question 5:**  
Passive (the subject 'combination' is being acted upon by Learner).

**Question 6:**  
Active (the subject 'Supreme Court' performed the action 'ruled').

**Question 7:**  
Active revision: *"The quarterback drilled a brilliant touchdown pass into double coverage."*

**Question 8:**  
Active revision: *"The chemistry students made mistakes during the laboratory synthesis."*

**Question 9:**  
When the performer of the action is unknown, irrelevant, or when the focus should deliberately remain on the result or object (e.g., in scientific lab reports: *'The liquid was heated to 100°C'* or *'The bank was robbed early Tuesday'*).

**Question 10:**  
Active revision: *"The school board approved increased student technology funding."*
