## 📋 Assessment Questions

**1.** Compare how Frederick Douglass in 1852 and Claude McKay in 1919 both used language to confront systemic racial violence. What common values of dignity do both authors champion?

**2.** In both *Romeo and Juliet* and *Hamlet*, characters make fateful decisions driven by passion vs. intellect. Contrast Romeo's fatal flaw with Hamlet's fatal flaw.

**3.** Identify which rhetorical appeal (Ethos, Pathos, or Logos) is most dominant in Abraham Lincoln's *Gettysburg Address*, and justify your selection.

**4.** Explain how Shakespeare uses character foils in *Hamlet* (such as Fortinbras and Laertes) to illuminate Hamlet's character, just as he used Benvolio to illuminate Tybalt in *Romeo and Juliet*.

**5.** How does Langston Hughes's use of vernacular free verse in *"Mother to Son"* achieve a different rhetorical effect than Claude McKay's strict iambic sonnet in *"If We Must Die"*?

**6.** Identify the literary device shared by both *"Mother to Son"* (the staircase) and Hamlet's soliloquy (the *"slings and arrows"* / *"undiscovered country"*).

**7.** Rewrite this passive sentence into a vigorous active sentence suitable for an analytical essay: *"The theme of political corruption in Denmark is revealed by Shakespeare through imagery of poison."*

**8.** Punctuate this sentence correctly using a semicolon and a conjunctive adverb: *"Hamlet possessed conclusive proof of the king's guilt nevertheless he delayed taking physical action."*

**9.** Explain how the concept of **dramatic irony** heightens emotional tension in the final scenes of *Romeo and Juliet*.

**10.** Reflect on how rhetoric and literature have functioned throughout history not merely as entertainment, but as vital instruments for moral courage and social transformation.

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
Both reject passive submission to brutalization; Douglass uses fierce prophetic oratory to shatter national hypocrisy, while McKay uses the dignified sonnet form to demand unyielding, heroic resistance against mob violence.

**Question 2:**  
Romeo's flaw is violent impulsivity and lack of emotional restraint (acting before thinking), whereas Hamlet's flaw is paralyzing over-contemplation and philosophical hesitation (thinking without acting).

**Question 3:**  
Ethos and Pathos woven through solemn biblical cadence. Lincoln builds Ethos by honoring fallen martyrs and framing the nation under God, while invoking Pathos to inspire listeners to dedicate themselves to the 'unfinished work' of preserving democratic liberty.

**Question 4:**  
Both Laertes and Fortinbras are men of swift, violent action who instantly mobilize to avenge their slain fathers, spotlighting Hamlet's chronic delay and philosophical brooding by contrast.

**Question 5:**  
Hughes's vernacular free verse creates an intimate, tender, conversational mother-child voice that feels immediate and warm, whereas McKay's sonnet has a formal, rigid martial cadence that sounds like a heroic battle cry.

**Question 6:**  
Extended metaphor.

**Question 7:**  
Active revision: *"Shakespeare exposes Denmark's political corruption through visceral imagery of poison."*

**Question 8:**  
*"Hamlet possessed conclusive proof of the king's guilt; nevertheless, he delayed taking physical action."*

**Question 9:**  
The audience knows Juliet is merely drugged and will awaken within minutes; watching Romeo despair and swallow poison while standing feet away from his breathing wife creates unbearable tragic tension.

**Question 10:**  
Open student synthesis response. Look for deep reflection on how orators like Douglass and Lincoln and writers like Hughes, McKay, and Shakespeare challenge injustice, preserve national memory, cultivate empathy, and hold mirrors up to human nature.
