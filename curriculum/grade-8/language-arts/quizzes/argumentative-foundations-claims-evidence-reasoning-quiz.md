## 📋 Assessment Questions

**1.** What distinguishes an argumentative **claim** from a statement of fact or personal preference?

**2.** Evaluate this thesis statement: *"Social media platforms cause problems for teenagers."* Why is this weak, and how can it be revised into a defensible, complex 8th-grade claim?

**3.** What is the difference between empirical evidence and anecdotal evidence? Which carries greater weight in academic argumentation?

**4.** Define the term **warrant** (or commentary) in the context of the Claim-Evidence-Reasoning model.

**5.** Read the excerpt: *"According to a 2024 Stanford Cyber Initiative report, high school students who received structured algorithmic literacy training demonstrated a 43% increase in identifying online disinformation."* If you are arguing for mandatory digital literacy curricula, what specific warrant connects this statistic to your claim?

**6.** Why must direct quotations be framed with introductory context (signal phrases) rather than dropped abruptly as isolated sentences into a paragraph?

**7.** Which of the following is an example of a properly integrated and cited quotation in MLA format?
   A) "Reading fiction increases empathy." (Smith 42).
   B) According to cognitive scientist Elena Smith, "reading immersive fiction systematically strengthens neural pathways associated with social empathy" (42).
   C) Reading fiction makes empathy better Elena Smith 42.

**8.** Explain the concept of **source credibility**. What criteria should a researcher use to determine if an online source is trustworthy?

**9.** Identify the flaw in this reasoning: *"Over 80% of our student council voted for longer lunch periods; therefore, longer lunch periods are guaranteed to improve test scores across all academic subjects."*

**10.** Draft an original, two-pronged argumentative thesis statement on whether school systems should adopt year-round academic calendars.

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
A claim is a defensible, debatable position on an issue about which reasonable people can disagree; it requires evidence and logic to substantiate, unlike a verifiable fact (which can be proven true immediately) or a personal preference (which is subjective taste).

**Question 2:**  
It is weak because it is overly broad, vague ('problems'), and does not specify causes or mechanisms. A stronger revision: *"Unregulated algorithmic feeds on social media platforms harm adolescent psychological development by amplifying sleep disruption and encouraging compulsive social comparison."*

**Question 3:**  
Empirical evidence is derived from systematic observation, scientific experimentation, or measurable data. Anecdotal evidence is based on isolated personal stories. Empirical evidence carries far greater authority in academic argumentation.

**Question 4:**  
A warrant (commentary) is the analytical bridge that explains *how* and *why* the cited evidence directly supports and proves the stated claim.

**Question 5:**  
Acceptable model: *"This 43% increase demonstrates that misinformation is not an inevitable byproduct of internet usage, but a vulnerability that can be systematically mitigated through direct instruction, proving that state educational boards must integrate algorithmic literacy into standard graduation requirements."*

**Question 6:**  
Abruptly dropped quotes ('quote bombs') disrupt syntactical flow, disorient the reader, and fail to establish the speaker's authority or situational context.

**Question 7:**  
B (It smoothly embeds the quotation within a complete sentence and uses proper MLA parenthetical attribution).

**Question 8:**  
Credibility depends on: author expertise/credentials, institutional reputation, publication date (recency), peer-review or editorial oversight, absence of commercial/political bias, and clear corroborating citations.

**Question 9:**  
This is a faulty causal connection / false cause (and an appeal to popularity); student preference for longer lunches does not prove or guarantee academic performance improvement.

**Question 10:**  
Acceptable model: *"School districts should adopt year-round academic calendars because continuous instruction prevents summer learning regression and enables strategically distributed breaks that mitigate student and teacher burnout."*
