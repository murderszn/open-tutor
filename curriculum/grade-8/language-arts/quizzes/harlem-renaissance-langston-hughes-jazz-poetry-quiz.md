# Grade 8 Language Arts — Harlem Renaissance: Langston Hughes & Jazz Poetry Quiz

**Topic:** Harlem Renaissance: Langston Hughes & Jazz Poetry  

This quiz analyzes the literary contributions of Langston Hughes, examining musicality, vernacular voice, extended metaphors, and the social philosophy of the Harlem Renaissance.

## 📋 Assessment Questions

**1.** What historical movement between 1910 and 1970 saw millions of African Americans move from the rural South to Northern and Western cities like Harlem, New York?

**2.** Why is Langston Hughes often celebrated as the pioneer of **jazz poetry**?

**3.** In Hughes's poem *"Mother to Son"*, what is the central **extended metaphor**?

**4.** Analyze the mother's description of her stairs: *"It's had tacks in it, / And splinters, / And boards torn up, / And places with no carpet on the floor— / Bare."* What do these physical details symbolize about her life journey?

**5.** In *"Harlem"* (also known as *"A Dream Deferred"*), Hughes opens with the line: *"What happens to a dream deferred?"* What does the word **deferred** mean?

**6.** Analyze the sensory similes Hughes uses in *"Harlem"* (e.g., drying up like a raisin in the sun, festering like a sore, stinking like rotten meat).

**7.** What is the ominous implication of the italicized final line of *"Harlem"*: *"Or does it explode?"*

**8.** In *"Theme for English B"*, what is the setting and who is the speaker addressing?

**9.** How does Hughes challenge his white college instructor's worldview in *"Theme for English B"* when he says, *"You are white—yet a part of me, as I am a part of you. That's American."*?

**10.** What was the core cultural message of Hughes's landmark 1926 essay *"The Negro Artist and the Racial Mountain"*?

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
The Great Migration.

**Question 2:**  
Hughes incorporated the syncopated rhythms, improvisational structures, call-and-response patterns, and blue notes of African American jazz and blues music into written verse.

**Question 3:**  
Life is compared to climbing a staircase; the mother's life is not a smooth, luxurious 'crystal stair,' but a splintered, ragged, continuous climb.

**Question 4:**  
They symbolize intense poverty, systemic racial hardship, physical labor, and sudden obstacles; yet despite the bareness, she continuously climbed upward without quitting.

**Question 5:**  
Postponed, delayed, put off, or denied.

**Question 6:**  
He uses decay imagery (raisin shriveling, wounds festering, meat rotting, crusting over like syrup) to demonstrate that denied human dreams do not simply disappear; they rot, sicken, and poison the soul.

**Question 7:**  
It warns that systemic suppression of human rights and aspirations cannot be contained forever; eventually, it will result in explosive social rebellion and revolution.

**Question 8:**  
The speaker is a twenty-two-year-old Black college student at Columbia University in Harlem writing a mandatory personal essay for his white English professor.

**Question 9:**  
He asserts that despite racial segregation and disparate social standing, their lives, cultures, and identities are inextricably linked together as part of the shared American tapestry.

**Question 10:**  
He declared that Black artists must express their authentic, individual Black identity boldly and without shame or fear, refusing to conform to white standards or pander to white approval.
