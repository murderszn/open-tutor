## 📋 Assessment Questions

**1.** What is the definition of a **character foil** in literary analysis?

**2.** In Act I, Scene 1, how does the violent brawl immediately establish **Tybalt** and **Benvolio** as character foils?

**3.** Analyze Tybalt's famous line: *"What, drawn, and talk of peace? I hate the word, as I hate hell, all Montagues, and thee."* What does this reveal about his worldview?

**4.** How does **Mercutio** serve as a character foil to **Romeo** in their conversations about love in Act I, Scene 4?

**5.** What is Mercutio's Queen Mab speech about, and what is its underlying satirical purpose?

**6.** Explain the dramatic irony present in Act II, Scene 2 (the Balcony Scene).

**7.** In Act III, Scene 1, why does Romeo refuse to draw his sword against Tybalt when Tybalt insults him?

**8.** How does Mercutio interpret Romeo's refusal to duel Tybalt?

**9.** Explain the catastrophic turn of events in Act III, Scene 1 that leads to Mercutio's death and Romeo's banishment.

**10.** Analyze Mercutio's dying curse: *"A plague o' both your houses!"* What does this curse foreshadow for the remainder of the play?

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
A character foil is a secondary character whose contrasting attributes, attitudes, and decisions highlight and accentuate the qualities of another character.

**Question 2:**  
Benvolio attempts to part the fighting servants and maintain peace ('Part, fools! Put up your swords'), whereas Tybalt instantly escalates the fight into lethal violence, demanding blood.

**Question 3:**  
It reveals his fiery, fanatical adherence to family honor and tribal hatred; for Tybalt, peace is not merely undesirable, but an offensive insult on par with hell itself.

**Question 4:**  
Romeo is a melancholy, hyper-idealistic Petrarchan lover who views love as sacred, agonizing, and serious. Mercutio is a cynical, witty realist who views romantic infatuation as a ridiculous illusion and treats love bawdily.

**Question 5:**  
Mercutio describes a miniature fairy midwife who delivers dreams tailored to people's selfish desires (lawyers dream of fees, soldiers of war); he argues that dreams are childish fantasies born of idle, deceptive brains.

**Question 6:**  
Romeo overhears Juliet confessing her deep love for him while she believes she is speaking into the private darkness alone on her balcony.

**Question 7:**  
Romeo has secretly married Tybalt's cousin, Juliet, just hours earlier; because of their secret familial bond, Romeo now considers the Capulet name as dear as his own.

**Question 8:**  
Mercutio sees Romeo's refusal as a 'calm, dishonourable, vile submission'—a coward's humiliation that stains Montague family honor.

**Question 9:**  
Mercutio steps in to fight Tybalt on Romeo's behalf; Romeo steps between them to stop the duel; Tybalt stabs Mercutio under Romeo's outstretched arm; an enraged Romeo then pursues and kills Tybalt in vengeance, leading Prince Escalus to banish Romeo.

**Question 10:**  
Mercutio's curse condemns the pointless Capulet-Montague feud and directly foreshadows the tragic deaths of the two lovers, showing that innocent youth bear the fatal cost of generational hatred.
