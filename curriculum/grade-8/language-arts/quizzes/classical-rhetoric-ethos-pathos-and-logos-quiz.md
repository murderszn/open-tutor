# Grade 8 Language Arts — Classical Rhetoric: Ethos, Pathos, and Logos Quiz

**Topic:** Classical Rhetoric: Ethos, Pathos, and Logos  

This assessment covers the classical Aristotelian rhetorical triangle, testing your ability to define, identify, and deploy ethos, pathos, and logos in persuasive oratory and analytical prose.

## 📋 Assessment Questions

**1.** Who first formulated the rhetorical appeals of Ethos, Pathos, and Logos in classical antiquity?

**2.** Define **Ethos** and list two specific strategies a speaker can use to establish it.

**3.** Define **Logos** and identify what forms of evidence constitute strong logos.

**4.** Define **Pathos** and explain how an author can invoke it responsibly without manipulating the audience.

**5.** Read this passage and identify the primary appeal: *"As a board-certified trauma surgeon who has treated severe concussion injuries in youth athletics for twenty-two years, I urge the league to implement mandatory baseline neurological testing."*

**6.** Read this passage and identify the primary appeal: *"Clinical trial data indicates that patients receiving Treatment A experienced a 68% reduction in arterial inflammation over six months compared to a 12% reduction in the control group (p < 0.01)."*

**7.** Read this passage and identify the primary appeal: *"Picture the desolate face of a child waking up on a freezing winter morning in an unheated apartment, wondering whether dinner tonight will be half a slice of stale bread or nothing at all."*

**8.** Why is an argument that relies *exclusively* on pathos vulnerable to criticism?

**9.** How does an orator's **Kairos** (sense of opportune timing and cultural moment) influence the effectiveness of their rhetorical appeals?

**10.** Write a short 3-sentence speech urging peers to participate in a community food drive, intentionally incorporating at least one appeal to Ethos and one appeal to Logos.

### Model Answers & Rubric (10 Points Total: 1 point per question)

**Question 1:**  
Aristotle (in his seminal work *Rhetoric*).

**Question 2:**  
Ethos is the appeal to the speaker's moral authority, credibility, competence, and trustworthiness. Strategies include: citing relevant professional experience/qualifications, demonstrating fairness toward opposing views, and using respectful, precise language.

**Question 3:**  
Logos is the appeal to logic, rationality, and deductive/inductive reasoning. It utilizes statistics, verified empirical facts, expert studies, historical precedents, and coherent cause-and-effect structures.

**Question 4:**  
Pathos is the appeal to the audience's emotions, values, moral imagination, and empathy. Responsible pathos uses authentic human narratives and evocative sensory imagery to illuminate real human stakes rather than cynically exploiting fear or prejudice.

**Question 5:**  
Ethos (establishing medical credentials, board certification, and 22 years of surgical experience).

**Question 6:**  
Logos (presenting clinical trial statistics, comparison percentages, and statistical significance).

**Question 7:**  
Pathos (invoking visceral emotional imagery of a freezing, starving child to arouse protective empathy).

**Question 8:**  
Arguments relying solely on emotional appeals lack factual verification and structural reasoning; once the emotional intensity subsides, the audience realizes no logical proof was provided.

**Question 9:**  
Kairos refers to the opportune, critical moment to speak; delivering the right argument at the exact psychological or historical moment magnifies its urgency and receptive impact.

**Question 10:**  
Acceptable model: *"Having volunteered at our regional food bank every Saturday this semester, I have seen our community's quiet strength firsthand (Ethos). Yet local county data shows that one out of every five families in our school district faces food insecurity every month (Logos). By contributing just two non-perishable cans today, our collective effort can stock 500 emergency meal kits for our neighbors."*
