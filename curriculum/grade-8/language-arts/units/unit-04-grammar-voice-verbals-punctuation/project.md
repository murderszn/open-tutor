# Project — The Editor's Portfolio

**Time:** Sessions 14–15 (two ~45-minute workshops), plus the
editor's note as homework or a third short session if needed.
**Standards:** L.8.1.a, L.8.1.b, L.8.1.c, L.8.2, L.8.3.a, W.8.5.

## The task

You are the editor of a short sports report. The draft below was
written quickly and it shows: it contains **12 faults** from this
unit — capitalization, comma splices, subjunctive errors,
confused words, a dangling phrase, a faulty list, a conditional
mismatch, and a voice choice to defend or change. Your portfolio
has three parts.

### Part 1 — Diagnose (workshop 1)

Copy the draft. Mark every fault by type (capitalization, comma
splice, subjunctive, confused word, dangling phrase, faulty
parallelism, conditional mismatch, voice choice), number the
faults 1–12, and write one phrase of reason for each.

> "swimming is fun. Mara loves to swim, she practices daily. The
> pool was cleaned by the caretaker, the water was clear. If Mara
> was taller, she would reach the high shelf. The coach demands
> that Mara practices every morning. She needs three things:
> goggles, a cap, and she needs a towel. Their going to the meet
> on Friday. Its going to be exciting. The race started, however
> Mara was ready. Tired from coaching, the team practiced hard.
> If it will rain, the meet would move inside. Mara won and
> smiled."

### Part 2 — Revise (workshop 2)

Rewrite the draft as a clean paragraph. You may reorder clauses
and change sentence boundaries, but you must not change the
facts: the same people do the same things, the meet is still on
Friday, and Mara still wins. Keep at least one passive sentence
only if you can defend it in Part 3 — otherwise use active voice.

### Part 3 — Editor's note

Write a note of 150–200 words explaining **six** of your revision
choices, one for each tool in this unit:

1. a verbal choice (gerund, participle, or infinitive),
2. a voice choice (active or passive, with its effect),
3. a mood choice (conditional or subjunctive),
4. a punctuation choice (semicolon, colon, or conjunctive adverb),
5. a capitalization or confused-word choice,
6. a sentence-combining choice (subordination or parallelism).

Each explanation must name the tool, quote the before and after
versions, and state the effect on the reader. A note that only
lists corrections without reasons earns no more than half credit
on this part (see the rubric).

## Deliverables

1. The marked-up draft with 12 numbered faults and reasons.
2. The revised paragraph.
3. The editor's note (150–200 words, six choices).

## Alternative when no earlier draft is on hand

This project is self-contained: it uses only the supplied draft
above. If the learner also has a Units 01–03 draft, the adult may
substitute one paragraph of it for Part 2's revision target, but
the supplied draft must still be diagnosed in Part 1, so the
12-fault count stays comparable.

## Rubric

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Solid | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Diagnosis accuracy (Part 1) | All 12 faults found, correctly typed, with a correct reason each | 10–11 faults found, correctly typed | 7–9 faults found, or several mistyped | Fewer than 7 faults found, or reasons mostly missing |
| Revision correctness (Part 2) | Every fault repaired; no new fault introduced; facts unchanged | 10–11 faults repaired; at most one new fault | 7–9 faults repaired, or facts changed | Fewer than 7 faults repaired |
| Explanation quality (Part 3) | All 6 tools explained with before/after quotes and an effect on the reader | 5 tools explained, or one explanation missing its effect | 3–4 tools explained, or explanations are lists without reasons | Fewer than 3 tools explained |
| Style and purpose fit (whole portfolio) | Every kept choice (including any kept passive) is defended for purpose and audience | Most kept choices defended | Defenses are generic ("it sounds better") | No defense of choices |

**Scoring:** 14–16 = exceeds; 11–13 = meets; 8–10 = approaching;
below 8 = reteach with Lesson 06, then revise one part. The
[answer key](answer-key.md) gives the 12-fault diagnosis and one
correct revised paragraph; other clean revisions that repair the
same faults are acceptable.
