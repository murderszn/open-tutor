## Practice A — Verbal sort and entry check (Session 2)

Label each *-ing* word **G** (gerund) or **P** (participle) and
name its job in one phrase. State the replacement test for at
least four items.

1. "Swimming is good exercise."
2. "The swimming ducks crossed the pond."
3. "Mara likes swimming."
4. "Swimming across the lake, Mara reached the dock."
5. "Her hobby is swimming."
6. "I saw the swimming fish."
7. "Swimming takes practice."
8. "Tired from swimming, Mara rested."
9. "The boy swimming in the pool is my brother."
10. "Mara's favorite activity is swimming laps."
11. "Running water flooded the path."
12. "Running builds stamina."

## Practice B — Sentence combining (Session 4)

Combine each pair by subordination, without changing the facts.
Name the subordinating word you used and state which idea you
made main.

1. "The rain stopped. We planted seeds."
2. "Jon was tired. He finished the chapter."
3. "The dog barked. The mail carrier left."
4. "We will leave. The sun rises."
5. "Mara practiced daily. She made the team."
6. "The soil was dry. The plants survived."

## Practice R1 — Verbals in prose (Session 5)

Read this original passage. Find and label six verbals (at least
one gerund, one participle, and one infinitive), and explain two
of the writer's choices — why that verbal, in that job, serves
the sentence.

> "Building the shed took a month. Jon, hammering nails in the
> heat, worked every afternoon. His goal was to finish before the
> rain returned. Carrying lumber across the yard, he learned to
> pace himself. Finishing the roof felt like a victory."

## Practice C — Voice revision (Session 7)

**C1.** Identify the voice of each sentence, and name the doer
or note that no doer is named:

1. "Scientists measured the rainfall."
2. "The rainfall was measured every morning."
3. "The principal announced the winners."
4. "Mistakes were made."
5. "The librarian shelved the books."
6. "The books were shelved before lunch."
7. "Mara painted the fence."
8. "The fence was painted by Mara."

**C2.** Rewrite sentences 2, 3, 6, and 8 in the opposite voice.
For each rewrite, write one sentence stating what the new version
emphasizes. For sentence 4, write two sentences: who might the
hidden actor be, and why might the writer have hidden them?

## Practice D — Mood identification and repair (Session 9)

**D1.** Label the mood of each sentence's main verb:

1. "Water the plants before noon."
2. "Have you watered the plants?"
3. "The plants need water."
4. "If we watered daily, the plants would thrive."
5. "She wishes the plants were taller."
6. "The gardener insists that the soil remain damp."
7. "If it rains, we will move inside."
8. "If it rained, we would move inside."

**D2.** Repair each faulty sentence, and name the rule used:

1. "I wish I was at the garden today."
2. "The rule requires that every visitor signs the log."
3. "If Mara practices more, she would win."
4. "The teacher suggested that Jon practices his speech."

## Practice R2 — Voice and mood in argument (Session 10)

Discuss with the adult, then write a short paragraph (4–6
sentences) answering both questions with one example each:

1. A school announcement says, "The decision was made to cancel
   the trip." What information does the passive voice hide, and
   why might a reader want it? Rewrite the sentence in active
   voice once the actor is named (choose a plausible actor and
   say that you supplied it).
2. A classmate writes, "If the school was fair, it would listen."
   Is *was* correct here? What does the sentence claim about
   whether the school is fair, and which verb form marks that
   claim?
