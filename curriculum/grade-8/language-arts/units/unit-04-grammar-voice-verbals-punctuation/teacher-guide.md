# Teacher Guide — Unit 04 (Adult / Educator Only)

This guide supports the adult guiding a single grade-8 learner.
It contains teaching notes and scoring guidance. Full solutions
are in the [answer key](answer-key.md), not here, so this file
can be read alongside the learner's work without giving answers
away in the lesson flow.

## Unit at a glance

- **Question:** how do small sentence choices change what a
  reader understands and believes?
- **Core move:** grammar as revision. Every tool in this unit is
  taught as a decision with an effect, and every revision task
  requires a stated reason. Grade the reason, not just the
  correction.
- **Midyear role:** Week 18 reviews Units 01–03 (evidence and
  theme; argument and conflicting accounts) and the formative
  quiz doubles as the cumulative U01–U04 check. Keep the review
  sessions to retrieval and discussion — do not introduce new
  U05 material.

## Session-by-session notes

**Sessions 1–5 (Week 15, verbals).** The replacement test is the
unit's most important habit; insist learners say it aloud for
the first dozen items. The dangling trap (Lesson 01) and the
*to*-verb-or-noun test (Lesson 02) are the two errors that will
recur all unit — name them every time they appear, even in later
weeks. Session 5's passage (Practice R1) is original; accept any
labeling that names six verbals with at least one of each kind.

**Sessions 6–10 (Week 16, voice and mood).** Expect the
"passive is always bad" rule from prior schooling. Do not fight
it with a counter-rule; fight it with the emphasis question:
"what does this version spotlight?" Session 10's discussion
(Practice R2) deliberately connects voice to Unit 03's argument
work — a hidden actor is an evidence problem, not just a style
problem. For moods, drill only the two subjunctive triggers
(*wish / if-only* → *were*; *demand / require / suggest /
recommend + that* → base form) and the conditional matching
rule; the other moods are mostly labeling.

**Sessions 11–15 (Week 17, punctuation and project).** The two
tests (stand-alone sides for semicolons; complete thought before
colons) should be written on a card and kept visible. The
project's 12 faults are counted exactly in the answer key — if
a learner finds a 13th candidate, judge it on its merits: a
genuine additional fault earns credit in Part 1 even though it
is not in the key, and a stylistic preference does not.

**Sessions 16–20 (Week 18, review and assessment).** Session 16
retrieves U01–U02: strongest evidence, theme vs. topic,
objective summary. Session 17 retrieves U03: relevant vs.
irrelevant evidence, fact disagreement vs. interpretation
disagreement. Session 19 is reteach, not a second quiz: rework
each missed item with the learner, then assign the matching
lesson section as follow-up. Session 20's portfolio discussion
asks the learner to name one strength and one next step — record
the next step privately; it becomes the U05 entry note.

## Misconceptions to expect

| Misconception | Correction |
|---|---|
| Every *-ing* word is a gerund | The replacement test: noun job = gerund, adjective job = participle |
| *To* + anything is an infinitive | *To* + verb = infinitive; *to* + noun = prepositional phrase |
| Passive voice is always bad | Voice is a choice; grade the emphasis and the defense |
| *Was painting* is passive | The "who did it?" test: Mara is still the doer, so it is active |
| Subjunctive is fancy past tense | Subjunctive marks unreality (wish, demand, contrary to fact), not past time |
| A semicolon is a fancy comma | A semicolon joins only independent clauses; run the stand-alone test |
| A colon can follow any introduction | A colon must follow a complete thought |

## Differentiation and reteaching

- **Reading access:** read any sentence aloud on request;
  labeling, sorting, and charting are full answers where a
  paragraph is not the skill tested.
- **If verbals are still swapped after Lesson 01:** return to
  the replacement test with noun/adjective word cards before
  Lesson 02.
- **If voice rewrites only shuffle word order:** return to
  Lesson 03's "who is doing the action?" question and the
  three-step rewrite procedure.
- **If semicolons appear before dependent clauses:** return to
  Lesson 05's stand-alone test with five fresh sentence pairs
  before Practice E.
- **If mood errors persist:** reduce to the two subjunctive
  triggers on a card and drill ten demand/wish sentences before
  the assessment.
- **Extensions:** Lesson 02's three-version garden paragraph
  and Lesson 04's subjunctive rule set are the unit's extension
  tasks; both are optional and ungraded, or graded only on the
  explanation sentences.

## Scoring guidance

- **Formative quiz:** score, review the same day, and reteach in
  Session 19. Do not average it into the unit assessment; its
  purpose is diagnosis.
- **Unit assessment (36 points):** 32–36 exceeds; 26–31 meets;
  19–25 approaching (reteach the missed tool, then revise);
  below 19 reteach with Lessons 01–06 and use a parallel form
  (build it by changing the assessment's nouns, verbs, and
  sentence topics while keeping the same fault types — do not
  reuse the same sentences for a retake).
- **Project:** score with the rubric in
  [project.md](project.md). Part 3's editor's note earns full
  credit only when each explanation names its tool, quotes
  before and after, and states an effect on the reader.
- **What "meets" looks like:** the learner can label verbals
  by job, choose voice and mood deliberately, punctuate
  semicolons/colons/conjunctive adverbs correctly, and explain
  most revisions. Occasional slips on borderline cases (such as
  *swimming pool*) do not block "meets" when the reasoning is
  stated.
