# Lesson 04 — Verb Moods: Fact, Command, Question, Possibility, Wish

**Goal:** you will label the five verb moods, form the conditional
and subjunctive correctly, and repair mood errors.
**Time:** ~45 minutes. **Materials:** notebook.
**Readiness check:** "Mara painted the fence" states a fact. What
mood does a fact-stating verb have? (Indicative — defined below.)

## What mood is

**Mood** is the form a verb takes to show how the speaker views
the action: as fact, as a command, as a question, as a
possibility, or as a wish or demand. English has five moods.
Tense tells you *when*; mood tells you *how real* the speaker
treats the action.

| Mood | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Indicative** | States a fact or an opinion | "The garden is dry." |
| **Imperative** | Gives a command or request; the subject *you* is understood | "Water the tomatoes." |
| **Interrogative** | Asks a question | "Did you water the tomatoes?" |
| **Conditional** | Shows an action that depends on a condition, often with *would*, *could*, or *might* | "If it rained, we would stay inside." |
| **Subjunctive** | Expresses a wish, a demand, a recommendation, or a state **contrary to fact** | "I wish I were taller." / "The rule requires that she be on time." |

Two subjunctive forms cause most errors, so learn them exactly:

1. **Contrary-to-fact *were*.** For a present situation that is
   *not* true, use *were* for every subject: "If I **were** king,
   I would…" (not "was"), "I wish she **were** here" (not "was").
   Reserve *was* for situations that might actually have happened:
   "If Mara was at the garden yesterday, she saw the new beds"
   states a real possibility about the past, not a contrary-to-fact
   wish.
2. **Base form after demand and recommendation verbs.** After
   *demand, require, suggest, recommend, insist* + *that*, use the
   base form of the verb, with no *-s* ending, for every subject:
   "The coach demanded that Mara **run** laps" (not "runs");
   "She suggested that he **practice** daily" (not "practices").

Conditional sentences pair their parts: a present contrary-to-fact
*if* clause (*if it rained*, *if I were*) pairs with *would /
could / might* in the main clause. A real future condition pairs
present tense with *will*: "If it rains, we **will** move inside"
is correct as a real possibility; "If it rains, we **would** move
inside" mixes the two patterns and is the error to catch.

## Worked example 1 — labeling moods

> "If Mara were taller, she would reach the shelf."

*Were* marks a state contrary to fact (Mara is not taller), and
*would reach* marks the dependent result. So the sentence mixes
**subjunctive** (*were*) and **conditional** (*would reach*) —
and that mixture is exactly correct for a contrary-to-fact
condition.

> "The teacher requires that every student submit the form."

*Requires* is indicative (a fact about the rule), but *submit* is
**subjunctive**: after *requires that*, the base form *submit*
appears with no *-s*, even though the subject is "every student."

## Worked example 2 — repairing mood errors

> Faulty: "I wish I was a bird."
> Repair: "I wish I were a bird."
> Reason: a wish about a state contrary to fact takes *were*.

> Faulty: "Mara suggested that Jon practices daily."
> Repair: "Mara suggested that Jon practice daily."
> Reason: after *suggested that*, the subjunctive uses the base
> form *practice*.

> Faulty: "If it rains, we would stay inside."
> Repair (two correct options, different meanings): "If it rains,
> we will stay inside" (real possibility) or "If it rained, we
> would stay inside" (contrary-to-fact condition). The repair must
> pick one pattern and match its parts — that choice is the skill.

## Guided practice

Label the mood of the underlined verb, and repair any error:

1. "Close the gate." (verb: *close*)
2. "Did you close the gate?" (verb: *did close*)
3. "If it rained, we would stay inside." (verb: *would stay*)
4. "I wish I was taller." (verb: *was*)
5. "The coach demanded that Mara run laps." (verb: *run*)
6. "If it rains, we would move inside." (verbs: *rains / would move*)

## Independent practice

1. Label the mood of each sentence's main verb:
   a. "Water the plants before noon."
   b. "Have you watered the plants?"
   c. "The plants need water."
   d. "If we watered daily, the plants would thrive."
   e. "She wishes the plants were taller."
   f. "The gardener insists that the soil remain damp."
2. Repair each faulty sentence, and name the rule the repair uses:
   a. "I wish I was at the garden today."
   b. "The rule requires that every visitor signs the log."
   c. "If Mara practices more, she would win." (Hint: the *if*
      clause and the main clause belong to different patterns —
      pick one pattern and match both parts.)
3. Error analysis: a classmate writes, "If I was a bird, I would
   fly south." The classmate argues, "I used *was* because the
   sentence is in the past." Explain why the classmate is wrong,
   using the contrary-to-fact rule.

## Applied task — mood in a set of rules

Write five rules for an imaginary club, using at least two
subjunctive forms (a wish or a demand/recommendation) and one
conditional sentence. Example starter (do not copy it as your
own): "It is required that every member bring a notebook." Then
underline each mood-marked verb and label its mood.

## Exit check

Write one sentence in the subjunctive expressing a wish, and one
sentence in the conditional expressing a dependent possibility.
Underline the verb that marks each mood.

## Supports and extension

- **Support:** memorize two triggers: *wish / if-only* → *were*;
  *demand / require / suggest / recommend + that* → base verb.
  Most subjunctive errors are one of these two.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may label moods by
  sorting sentence cards into five mood piles and defending two
  placements aloud.
- **Extension:** write a short set of rules for an imaginary club
  entirely in the subjunctive ("It is required that every member…;
  it is suggested that…"), then write two sentences explaining why
  the base verb form is correct in each rule.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: "subjunctive is just fancy past tense."
Stress the meaning test: subjunctive marks something *not real*
(a wish, a demand, a contrary-to-fact state), not something past.
Also watch for overcorrection: learners who learn *were* sometimes
apply it to real past possibilities ("If Mara was at the garden
yesterday…" is correct indicative). The Lesson 04 independent
practice item 3 and the error-analysis task both target that
boundary. See the [teacher guide](../teacher-guide.md) and
[answer key](../answer-key.md).
