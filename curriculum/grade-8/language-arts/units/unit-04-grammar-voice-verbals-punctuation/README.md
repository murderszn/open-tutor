# Unit 04 — Grammar: Voice, Verbals, Punctuation, and Style

**Grade:** 8 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #40](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/40), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Units 01–03 taught the learner to read other writers critically: to
weigh evidence, to track a theme, to test an argument and to compare
conflicting accounts. This unit turns that critical eye on the
learner's own sentences. Grammar here is not a list of labels to
memorize for a test. It is a set of revision decisions: a verbal that
packs an action into a noun or a description, a voice that puts the
actor or the action in the spotlight, a mood that marks a wish or a
demand as different from a fact, a semicolon that shows two ideas
standing as equals. By the end of the unit the learner can take a
draft — including drafts written in Units 01–03 — diagnose its
sentence-level faults, fix them, and explain why each fix serves the
writer's purpose and audience.

## Essential question

How do small sentence choices — a verbal, a voice, a mood, a mark of
punctuation — change what a reader understands and believes?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks and Units 01–03:

- Parts of speech (noun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition,
  conjunction) and subject/predicate/object identification — the
  legacy parts-of-speech and subject/predicate quizzes are the entry
  check (see Practice A).
- Citing textual evidence and writing objective summaries (U01) —
  now applied to the learner's own sentences: every revision needs a
  reason stated in words.
- Word choice and tone analysis (U02) — now applied to voice and
  mood: the same facts sound different in active and passive voice,
  in a wish and in a demand.
- Argument evaluation (U03) — now applied to voice: a passive
  sentence can hide who acted, and a careful reader notices when an
  actor disappears.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. **Explain the function of verbals** — gerunds, participles, and
   infinitives — in general and in particular sentences, and
   distinguish a gerund from a participle when both end in *-ing*
   by testing the job the word does (L.8.1.a).
2. **Form and use verbs in the active and passive voice**, and
   choose between them to achieve a particular effect — emphasizing
   the actor or the action (L.8.1.b, L.8.3.a).
3. **Form and use verbs in the indicative, imperative,
   interrogative, conditional, and subjunctive moods**, especially
   the conditional and subjunctive for uncertainty and for states
   contrary to fact (L.8.1.c, L.8.3.a).
4. **Use the conventions of capitalization, punctuation, and
   spelling when writing**, with the unit's core punctuation load
   of semicolons, colons, and conjunctive adverbs (L.8.2).
5. **Develop and strengthen writing through planning, revising,
   editing, and rewriting**, applying verbal, voice, mood, and
   punctuation choices deliberately and explaining each choice for
   purpose and audience (W.8.5); use grade-appropriate academic
   vocabulary accurately (L.8.6).

## Differentiation, supports, and extensions

- **Reading access:** every lesson prints its sentences in full;
  the adult reads any sentence aloud on request, and the learner
  may answer orally, by dictation, or in writing.
- **Response modes:** labeling, sorting, and charting count as full
  answers wherever a paragraph is not the skill being tested;
  speech-to-text is always allowed for longer writing.
- **Scaffolds:** every lesson gives sentence starters ("This
  *-ing* word is a ___ because it does the job of ___") and a
  worked repair before the learner repairs alone; Practice C gives
  a partially rewritten sentence for learners who need it.
- **Extensions:** Lesson 02's extension rewrites one garden-scene
  paragraph three ways — all gerunds, all participles, all
  infinitives — and compares their effects; Lesson 04's extension
  writes a short set of rules for an imaginary club in the
  subjunctive ("It is required that every member…") and explains
  why the base verb form is correct.
- **Reteaching signals:** if gerund/participle labels are still
  swapped after Lesson 01, return to its replacement test before
  Lesson 02 (infinitives build on the same "what job does it do?"
  question); if voice rewrites only swap word order at random,
  return to Lesson 03's "who is doing the action?" question before
  Practice C; if semicolons appear before dependent clauses, return
  to Lesson 05's independent-clause test before Practice E.
