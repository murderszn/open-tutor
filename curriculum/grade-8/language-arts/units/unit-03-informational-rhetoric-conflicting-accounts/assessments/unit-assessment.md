# Unit Assessment — Informational Rhetoric and Conflicting Accounts

**Time:** about 45 minutes. Answer in your notebook. All passages are
original to this unit unless marked as public-domain quotations.
Answers and scoring guidance are in the
[answer key](../answer-key.md) — do not open it until you finish.

## Part 1 — Delineate an argument (Lessons 1–3)

Read this original argument (written for this unit):

> "Our school should start a composting program. Food waste makes up
> nearly a third of cafeteria trash, and the science club's audit
> counted 62 kilograms of food waste in one week. Composting would
> teach students real environmental responsibility, and our science
> teacher once grew prize-winning tomatoes. If we compost, we cut
> trash-hauling trips, because the hauler charges by the bin."

**A1.** State the claim. Then list each reason with its evidence, if
any. Name the irrelevant evidence and the reason that has no evidence.
(6 points: claim 1, reasons 2, evidence 2, irrelevant 1 — see the key
for partial credit.)

**A2.** Judge the argument: is the reasoning sound, the evidence
relevant, and the evidence sufficient? Answer each test in one
sentence, and give an overall verdict in one sentence. (4 points.)

## Part 2 — Conflicting accounts (Lesson 5)

Two original accounts (written for this unit) of one fictional school
fundraiser. All names and numbers are invented practice data.

**Account C — Priya, student council treasurer:** "The spring fair
raised $1,200, our best total ever. We counted every ticket stub and
every cash-box dollar twice."

**Account D — Mr. Hale, faculty advisor:** "The spring fair raised
$900. The $1,200 figure counts $300 of ticket money that was refunded
when the rain canceled the evening show."

**A3.** (a) Is the $1,200-vs.-$900 disagreement about fact or
interpretation? Explain in one sentence. (b) What single piece of
evidence would settle it? (c) Write one sentence on which account
handles the other side's evidence better, quoting one phrase.
(5 points.)

**A4.** Do the two accounts disagree about whether the fair was a
success? Answer in two sentences, explaining whether that is a fact
or an interpretation disagreement. (3 points.)

## Part 3 — Appeals and structure (Lessons 1–2)

**A5.** Read this original paragraph (written for this unit):

> "Critics call the new library rules strict. They are not strict;
> they are clear. Before the rules, students wandered the stacks
> unsure whether talking was allowed. Now every table carries the same
> short list, and the librarian enforces it the same way for everyone."

(a) Name the structure move used in the first two sentences (deny-
and-replace, concession, or praise-and-turn). (b) Identify the key
sentence and explain what would be lost without it, in two sentences.
(c) Name one appeal at work in the paragraph and quote the proving
words. (5 points.)

## Part 4 — Objective summary and argument paragraph (Lessons 4–6)

**A6.** Write an objective summary (three sentences) of the
composting argument in Part 1. No praise, no blame, no new claims.
(3 points.)

**A7.** Write a short argument paragraph (5–7 sentences) on this
claim: "Our school lunch period should be extended by ten minutes."
Your paragraph must: state the claim, give at least one reason with
evidence (you may use a clearly labeled invented practice fact),
fairly acknowledge a counterclaim, distinguish your view from it, and
answer it. (7 points — see the rubric in the answer key.)

**Total: 33 points.** Scoring bands: 29–33 strong; 22–28 solid, with
reteaching on any part below half credit; below 22, reteach Lessons 3
and 5 before U06.
