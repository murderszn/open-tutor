# Week-14 Quiz — Informational Rhetoric and Conflicting Accounts

**Time:** about 30 minutes. Answer in your notebook. Each question is
worth the points shown; total 20 points. Answers are in the
[answer key](../answer-key.md) — do not open it until you finish.

**1. (2 points)** Name the three rhetorical appeals and, in one
phrase each, say what each one appeals to.

**2. (2 points)** Read this original sentence: "I have taught middle
school for fourteen years, and every year the students who read
twenty minutes a night finish the year ahead of those who do not."
Name the leading appeal and quote the words that prove it.

**3. (2 points)** An argument claims: "The school needs a later start
because teenagers are sleepy, and our principal once ran a marathon."
Which part is irrelevant evidence, and why, in one sentence?

**4. (2 points)** In Lincoln's Gettysburg Address, what does the
sentence "we can not dedicate — we can not consecrate — we can not
hallow — this ground" do for the paragraph that contains it? Answer
with a verb (denies, replaces, concedes, concludes) and one sentence
of explanation.

**5. (2 points)** Douglass writes: "This Fourth of July is yours, not
mine. You may rejoice, I must mourn." In one sentence each, state his
point of view and his purpose in these lines.

**6. (2 points)** Two accounts of one town meeting disagree: one says
40 people attended, the other says 90 people attended. Is this a fact
disagreement or an interpretation disagreement? What single piece of
evidence would settle it?

**7. (2 points)** Two accounts agree that a survey found 55 percent of
respondents favor a new park. One calls this "a clear mandate"; the
other calls it "barely half, from a small sample." Is this a fact
disagreement or an interpretation disagreement? Explain in one
sentence.

**8. (2 points)** A writer argues for a skate park and writes: "Some
neighbors worry about noise. They are right that skateboards are loud
— which is why the park should be built away from houses, with posted
evening hours, rather than not built at all." Name the move the writer
makes with the neighbors' worry (acknowledge-and-answer, ignore, or
insult), and say in one sentence why it strengthens the argument.

**9. (2 points)** Write a one-sentence objective summary of this
original paragraph: "The Cedar Falls lot has sat empty for years. Two
groups disagree about its future: one wants a garden, the other wants
parking. Both cite the same town survey and read it differently."

**10. (2 points)** Error analysis: a classmate says, "Account B must
be right because it uses more numbers than Account A." In two
sentences, explain why this reasoning fails.
