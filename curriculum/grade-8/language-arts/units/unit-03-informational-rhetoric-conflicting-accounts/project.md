# Project — Conflicting Accounts Brief

**Deliverable:** a short brief (about 2–3 pages, handwritten or typed)
on the Cedar Falls lot decision from Lesson 5, plus a completed
comparison chart. You may argue for either side; your grade depends on
fairness and evidence, not on which side you pick.

## Materials

Lesson 5's two accounts, your fact/interpretation table, your Lesson 6
counterclaim paragraph, notebook or paper, and the image activity notes
from Lesson 5.

## Steps

1. **Re-read both accounts** and finish your fact/interpretation
   table if it is incomplete. You need at least two fact disagreements
   and two interpretation disagreements, each labeled with its kind.
2. **Build the comparison chart** (one page): columns for Account A,
   Account B, and "What would settle or weigh it." Every disagreement
   gets a row; every fact disagreement names its settling evidence;
   every interpretation disagreement names the shared fact.
3. **Write the brief** in four sections:
   - *Summary (one paragraph):* an objective summary of the decision
     and what is at stake, with no praise or blame.
   - *Where the accounts disagree (one paragraph or a table):* the
     fact disagreements and the interpretation disagreements, each
     labeled.
   - *Argument (one paragraph):* your recommendation, written as a
     counterclaim paragraph — claim, fair acknowledgment, distinction,
     answer, conclusion.
   - *What would change my mind (two or three sentences):* the
     evidence that would make you switch sides.
4. **Review pass:** read your brief aloud. Cut any sentence that
   praises or insults a writer instead of evaluating their argument.
   Check every quotation against Lesson 5's accounts.
5. **Adult review:** an adult reads the brief for fairness — could a
   supporter of the other side agree your summary of their case is
   fair? Revise once from that feedback.

## Deliverables

- Comparison chart (all disagreements labeled, settling/weighing
  evidence named).
- Brief (summary, disagreements, counterclaim argument, what-would-
  change-my-mind section).
- One revised page showing at least one change made after adult review
  (a note in the margin is enough: "changed after review: …").

## Rubric

Scored by an adult; each criterion is 0–3 (0 missing, 1 attempted,
2 solid, 3 strong). Total out of 12.

| Criterion | 3 (strong) looks like |
|---|---|
| **Accurate comparison** | Every disagreement is correctly labeled fact or interpretation; quotations match the accounts; no disagreement is invented. |
| **Evidence judgment** | Every fact disagreement names realistic settling evidence; interpretation disagreements are weighed with Lesson 3 tests (soundness, relevance, sufficiency), not by confidence or word count. |
| **Fair argument** | The counterclaim paragraph states the other side's best point fairly, distinguishes agreement from disagreement, and answers with reasoning and evidence. |
| **Clear communication** | Objective summary has no praise or blame; organization follows the four sections; quotations are accurate and attributed to Account A or B. |

**Readiness signals:** a brief that scores 9 or above and survives the
adult fairness check is ready for U06's argument writing. A brief that
mislabels fact and interpretation, or that caricatures the other side,
needs reteaching from Lessons 5 and 6 before U06.

**Safety and privacy:** this project uses only the fictional Cedar
Falls accounts. Do not publish the brief, and do not attach real
people's names, school records, or personal details to it.
