# Practice — Unit 03: Informational Rhetoric and Conflicting Accounts

Extra practice for Weeks 12 and 14. All passages here are original to
this unit unless marked as public-domain quotations. Answers are in
[`answer-key.md`](answer-key.md); do not read them before attempting
an item.

## P1 — Appeals sort (Lesson 1)

Read each original sentence and name the leading appeal (ethos,
pathos, or logos), quoting the proving words.

1. "As a nurse who has worked night shifts for nine years, I know what
   exhaustion does to judgment."
2. "The river rose six feet in one night, and the Johnsons' porch
   floated away with their dog still barking on it."
3. "If the bridge carries 4,000 cars a day and the inspection found
   three cracked supports, the bridge must close until it is repaired."

## P2 — Key sentences (Lesson 2)

Lincoln's sentence: "The world will little note, nor long remember
what we say here, but it can never forget what they did here."

1. What does each half of the antithesis do (saying vs. doing)?
2. Why is this sentence a key sentence for the speech's purpose, in
   two sentences?

## P3 — Delineation drill (Lesson 3)

Original argument (written for this unit): "The library should buy
more graphic novels. Students read more when the books look inviting,
and circulation of graphic novels rose 40 percent last year after the
library added a display. The librarian also has a degree in
literature."

1. State the claim, the reason, and the evidence.
2. Which sentence is irrelevant evidence, and why?
3. Is the 40 percent figure sufficient on its own? What one
   additional fact would help most?

## P4 — Point of view (Lesson 4)

Original announcement (written for this unit): "From the Principal's
Office: Beginning Monday, hallway passes will be required after the
first bell. This keeps classrooms calm and students safe."

1. State the writer's point of view and purpose, one sentence each.
2. What conflicting view does the announcement fail to acknowledge?
   Write the one-sentence acknowledgment it should have included.

## P5 — Conflicting accounts (Lesson 5)

Two original one-sentence accounts of the same fictional event:

- Account X: "The festival drew a huge crowd, with families filling
  every seat by noon."
- Account Y: "The festival drew a modest crowd, with many seats still
  empty at noon."

1. Is this a fact disagreement, an interpretation disagreement, or
   could it be either? Explain in two sentences.
2. What single piece of evidence would settle it, and why?

## P6 — Mixed appeals (Lessons 1–2)

Douglass, public-domain quotation: "Your high independence only reveals
the immeasurable distance between us."

1. Name the leading appeal and quote the proving words.
2. Explain in two sentences how the word "distance" works as both
   image and argument here.

## P7 — Sufficiency judgment (Lesson 3)

Original claim: "Video games rot your brain, because my brother plays
for hours and his grades dropped."

1. Delineate: claim, reason, evidence.
2. Name two specific flaws (one about relevance or sufficiency, one
   about reasoning) in one sentence each.

## P8 — Counterclaim repair (Lesson 6)

Original weak paragraph (written for this unit): "School should start
later. Some people disagree, but they just like mornings. Later starts
help everyone."

1. Name the two faults in the counterclaim handling.
2. Rewrite the paragraph in 4–5 sentences with a fair counterclaim,
   a distinction, and an answer.

## P9 — Objective summaries (Lessons 4–5)

Write a two-sentence objective summary of Account B (Cedar Falls,
Lesson 5). Then, in one sentence, say what your summary deliberately
left out (opinion words, praise, or blame) and why.

## P10 — Full delineation (Lessons 3–5)

Original argument (written for this unit): "The town should fund a
free summer lunch program. One in five local children faces food
insecurity in summer, according to the county health survey, and
children who eat regularly return to school ready to learn. Opponents
worry about cost, but the program would use the school kitchen that
already stands empty in July, and volunteer staffing kept a nearby
town's program under budget for three years."

1. Map it: claim, each reason with its evidence.
2. Name the acknowledgment move and judge whether the response to the
   cost objection is sound, in two sentences.
3. Is the evidence sufficient? What would you still want to know
   before voting for it?
