# Resource Pack — Unit 02: Drama, Foils, Adaptation

**Goal:** give a grade-8 learner free, no-account routes into reading
Shakespeare scenes, understanding character foils, and comparing
page-to-stage adaptations. **Age fit:** 13–14; the two Shakespeare
excerpts are trimmed in-unit, and the adult preview note in the unit
README applies.

All links checked **2026-10-20**. Videos supplement the lessons; they do
not replace them. Items marked *(search link)* were not individually
previewed — the adult should skim before assigning.

## Videos

1. **RSC Shakespeare Learning Zone: "Staging the Balcony Scene"** —
   Royal Shakespeare Company — the 2010 *Romeo and Juliet* cast discusses
   staging choices for Act 2, Scene 2. Via
   [rsc.org.uk](https://www.rsc.org.uk/shakespeare-learning-zone/romeo-and-juliet/language/other-scenes)
   (page verified 2026-10-20; hosts the RSC's own video). *Why good:*
   actors explaining *choices* models L5's critic question directly.
2. **RSC: "The Balcony Scene" (2010 production excerpt)** — Royal
   Shakespeare Company — a staged version of 2.2 to compare against the
   text. Same RSC page as above. *Why good:* a professional staging the
   learner can pause and track against the script.
3. **Folger Shakespeare Unlimited: "Olivia Hussey: The Girl on the
   Balcony"** — Folger Shakespeare Library (podcast with transcript) —
   on Zeffirelli's 1968 film casting and look. Via
   [folger.edu](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/romeo-and-juliet/)
   (verified 2026-10-20; linked from the play's Folger page). *Why good:*
   reputable source on the most-taught film adaptation; transcript means
   no account and skimmable text.
4. **Shakespeare's Globe — YouTube channel** *(search link)* —
   [youtube.com/results?search_query=Shakespeare's+Globe+Romeo+and+Juliet](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Shakespeare%27s+Globe+Romeo+and+Juliet)
   — the Globe's channel has posted full productions and scene clips
   (their 2009 *Romeo and Juliet* streamed on the channel). *Why good:*
   the closest to Shakespeare's own stage conditions; adult previews the
   chosen clip.
5. **"Why Shakespeare loved iambic pentameter" — TED-Ed** *(search
   link)* —
   [youtube.com/results?search_query=TED-Ed+Shakespeare+iambic+pentameter](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=TED-Ed+Shakespeare+iambic+pentameter)
   — TED-Ed's Shakespeare entries are classroom staples with captions.
   *Why good:* rhythm explains why the verse *sounds* the way it does;
   captions available.
6. **"Dramatic irony" explainer** *(search link)* —
   [youtube.com/results?search_query=dramatic+irony+explained+literature](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=dramatic+irony+explained+literature)
   — prefer TED-Ed, CrashCourse Literature, or Khan Academy results.
   *Why good:* a second voice on L3's irony work; learner picks the
   clearest 5-minute option.

*No video is required for any core objective: every lesson's analysis can
be completed from the in-unit texts alone.*
