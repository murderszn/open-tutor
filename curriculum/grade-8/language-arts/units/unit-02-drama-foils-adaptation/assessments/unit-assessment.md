# Unit 02 Assessment — Drama: Foils and Adaptation

**~50 minutes · the scene below is new; read it cold.** Answer in
complete sentences. The [answer key](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment)
is separate.

## Cold-read scene: "The Lighthouse Log" (original)

*A lighthouse gallery at dusk. Keeper ALBA, 60s, polishes the great lens
with a cloth. Her apprentice NOA, 17, leans on the railing, watching
gulls.*

ALBA: *[without looking up]* Logbook. Wind: northwest, rising. Lamp:
trimmed at six. Your initials, not mine, on every line.

NOA: The lines are the same every night. Wind, lamp, gulls. If I write
"same as yesterday" fourteen times, is that dishonest or efficient?

ALBA: It's unwritten. *[She holds out the pen]* The log is the only
witness we get. Ships don't thank you. The book remembers.

NOA: *[takes the pen, then sets it down]* What if I told you I trimmed
the lamp at six-fifteen, not six? The book would remember a lie
perfectly.

ALBA: *[stops polishing]* Then the book would be wrong, and the next
keeper would trust it. *[A long pause]* Why are you telling me this?

NOA: *[aside]* Because she's the only person who'd be angry at the
right thing. *[to ALBA]* Because if I don't say it now, I'll write it
down clean tomorrow and it'll be true forever.

ALBA: *[She picks up the pen herself and corrects the line]* Six-fifteen.
Initials: N.V. *[She hands him the pen]* Now it's true. The book
forgives. People are slower.

NOA: *[signing]* You make it sound like the log is alive.

ALBA: Everything we tend is alive. That's why we write it down.

*[The lamp begins its rotation. Light sweeps the gallery, then the sea.]*

---

**Part A — Script reading (Objectives 1, 3)**

1. Identify one stage direction that changes how a line should be read.
   Quote the direction and the line, and explain the change in two
   sentences.
2. What does Noa's aside reveal that his dialogue to Alba does not?
   Answer in 2–3 sentences.

**Part B — Foils (Objective 2)**

3. Name the foil pair and prove it against the three foil rules (one
   sentence per rule, with at least one quoted line total).
4. Write a paragraph (6–8 sentences): how does the contrast between Alba
   and Noa sharpen the scene's theme about truth and records? Quote at
   least one line from each character and use literary present tense.

**Part C — Dialogue as action (Objective 3)**

5. Choose the single line that most changes what happens next in the
   scene. Quote it and analyze it with the three-job frame (propel /
   reveal / provoke) in 3–4 sentences.

**Part D — Dramatic irony (Objective 4)**

6. Is there dramatic irony in this scene — something the audience knows
   that a character does not? If yes, name it and its effect (suspense
   or humor) in two sentences. If no, explain in two sentences what the
   scene would need for irony to exist.

**Part E — Adaptation judgment (Objective 6)**

7. A student director stages this scene with Alba and Noa *arguing
   loudly* through the whole exchange, and cuts the final two lines
   ("Everything we tend is alive…"). Evaluate both choices — the loud
   delivery and the cut — with the critic's question: for each, state
   faithful or departed, and argue serve or cost with one quoted line
   from the scene. (5–7 sentences total.)

**Part F — Short adaptation (Objective 7)**

8. Write a 4-line continuation of the scene: Noa alone in the gallery
   after Alba leaves — a brief soliloquy. Then, in two sentences,
   explain how the soliloquy structure lets you show something the
   dialogue could not.
