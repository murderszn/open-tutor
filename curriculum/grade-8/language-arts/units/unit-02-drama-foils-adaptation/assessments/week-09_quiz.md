# Week-9 Formative Quiz — Unit 02

**12 items · ~25 minutes · closed lesson pages, open scene excerpts.**
Scored in the [answer key](../answer-key.md#week-9-quiz). The key is
separate; do not write answers on this page.

**1.** In one sentence each, define *dialogue* and *stage direction*.

**2.** Label each as **D** (dialogue), **SD** (stage direction),
**AS** (aside), or **SO** (soliloquy):
   - a. *[She crosses to the window without speaking.]*
   - b. "I have been waiting here since six."
   - c. *[to the audience]* "He has no idea I heard everything."
   - d. A 30-line speech by a character alone on stage about a hard choice.

**3.** Which two of these are *required* for a true character foil?
   (Choose two.)
   - a. The characters look different.
   - b. The contrast follows a deliberate pattern.
   - c. The characters share a situation or pressure.
   - d. One character is good and the other is evil.

**4.** Maya says, "There is a list. The list has boxes." Jay says,
   "Pure basketball jazz." In two sentences, explain how Jay's line
   sharpens our understanding of Maya (not just of Jay).

**5.** Quote one line from the R&J 3.1 excerpt that **provokes a
   decision**, name who must decide, and state the decision.

**6.** In the 3.1 excerpt, Romeo says he has "reason to love" Tybalt.
   In two sentences, explain the dramatic irony: what does the audience
   know that Tybalt does not?

**7.** A soliloquy and a dialogue scene handle a character's secret
   differently. In two sentences, explain the difference.

**8.** From the Hamlet excerpt, quote one phrase where death sounds
   peaceful and one where it sounds frightening.

**9.** "Take arms against a sea of troubles": in two sentences, explain
   what makes the *sea* image work (consider whether a sea can be
   fought).

**10.** Define *adaptation* in one sentence. Then name the critic's
    question for judging one (from L5).

**11.** A film moves the balcony scene from night to sunrise but keeps
    every word. Is the time change faithful or a departure? In two
    sentences, argue whether it serves or costs the story, citing one
    line of the text.

**12.** Your friend says, "Laertes is Hamlet's foil because they are
    both princes." Using the three foil rules, explain in 3–4 sentences
    what is missing from this claim and supply the missing piece.
