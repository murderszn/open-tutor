# Unit 02 — Drama: Character Foils and Adaptation

**Grade:** 8 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #40](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/40), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A drama is a story written to be performed, and everything on the page —
who speaks, who stays silent, what a character does while talking —
carries meaning the playwright never says outright. In this unit the
learner moves from reading scripts aloud, to naming **character foils**
(characters built to sharpen each other through contrast), to tracing how
dialogue and incidents propel action and reveal character, to comparing a
Shakespeare scene on the page with that same scene on stage or screen. The
unit closes with the learner's own **adaptation**: restaging a scene with
deliberate, defensible choices.

## Essential question

What does a character's contrast — and a director's choice — reveal that
the script alone does not say?

## Prerequisites

From U01 and the grade-7 track:

- Distinguish explicit statements from inferences and cite textual
  evidence (U01)
- Read grade-8-level fiction independently and write a multi-paragraph
  response with quoted or clearly referenced evidence
- Basic literary terms (theme, plot, dialogue, tone); U01's word-choice
  work (connotation, figurative language) is reused, not re-taught

**Adult note (read first):** two scenes in this unit carry mature
content. *Romeo and Juliet* Act 3, Scene 1 depicts a sword fight in which
Mercutio and Tybalt are killed; the Hamlet soliloquy contemplates suicide.
Preview both, use the trimmed excerpts provided here, and substitute the
original scenes offered in the teacher guide if the adult judges either
unsuitable. Biblical devotional study is not part of this unit.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Read a dramatic scene aloud with attention to dialogue, stage
   directions, asides, and soliloquies, explaining what each element
   contributes (RL.8.10).
2. Identify a character foil and explain, with quoted dialogue, how the
   contrast sharpens both characters and advances a theme (RL.8.1, RL.8.3).
3. Analyze how particular lines of dialogue or incidents propel the
   action, reveal aspects of a character, or provoke a decision (RL.8.3).
4. Explain how differences between what characters know and what the
   audience knows (dramatic irony) create suspense or humor (RL.8.6).
5. Compare the structure of a dialogue scene with a soliloquy and analyze
   how the differing structures shape meaning and style (RL.8.5); analyze
   diction and figurative language in dramatic verse (RL.8.4).
6. Evaluate how a filmed or staged production stays faithful to or departs
   from a script, judging the director's or actors' choices (RL.8.7,
   RI.8.7).
7. Write an original adaptation of a scene — dialogue, stage directions,
   and a brief director's rationale — using dialogue and pacing to develop
   character (W.8.3), and present a 60–90-second pitch of it (SL.8.1).

## Materials

Notebook or paper, pen/pencil, the five lesson pages, two highlighters
(one for dialogue, one for stage directions), the generated illustration,
a print or digital dictionary, and index cards for the foil matrix. For
the project: anything that records a short presentation (phone, webcam,
or a written script for a live reading). No purchases required; no
hazardous materials.

## Assessment summary

- **Exit checks** (each lesson): 2–3 quick items; scored in the
  [answer key](answer-key.md).
- **Formative quiz** (Session 18): 12 items — drama structure, foils,
  dialogue-as-evidence, dramatic irony, adaptation vocabulary.
- **Project** (Sessions 13–17): **Stage It: Adaptation Portfolio** — an
  adapted scene with stage directions, a director's rationale citing
  textual evidence, and a 60–90-second pitch, scored with the
  [project rubric](project.md#rubric).
- **Unit assessment** (Session 20): a new original dramatic scene read
  cold, with foil identification, dialogue analysis, and an
  adaptation-evaluation task.

All scoring guidance, misconception notes, and reteaching moves live in
the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md); every item is solved in the
[answer key](answer-key.md). Teacher materials are labeled and kept
separate from learner pages.

## Where this unit leads

U03's informational rhetoric leans on the close-reading moves built here:
quoting with lead-ins, diction analysis, and the literary present tense.
U04's grammar-and-style work revises the adaptation's stage directions
for verb voice and punctuation precision. U05 returns to foils and
dramatic irony in longer narrative fiction; U08's portfolio may include
the Stage It adaptation with its pitch.
