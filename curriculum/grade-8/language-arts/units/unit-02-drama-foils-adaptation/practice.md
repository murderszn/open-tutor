# Unit 02 Practice Sets

Each set matches a pacing session. Do the set, then check the
[answer key](answer-key.md). Starred (★) items are the error-analysis /
explain-your-reasoning tasks.

## Practice A (Session 2) — Reading the script {#practice-a-session-2}

Use "The Stairwell" ([L1](lessons/lesson-01-reading-a-script.md)).

1. Copy out two stage directions: one that tells an actor *where to go*
   and one that tells an actor *how to feel or sound*.
2. The scene's first line is a stage direction, not dialogue. In two
   sentences, explain what information the scene would lose if that
   direction were deleted.
3. ★ A classmate claims the aside is unnecessary — "Jay could just think
   it." Write 3–4 sentences defending the aside as a structural choice:
   what does the audience gain that a silent thought would not give?
4. Mark each as **D** (dialogue) or **SD** (stage direction) and explain
   what each contributes:
   - "Stay close. I know every stair in this building."
   - *[hands it over without a word, then]*
   - *[The lights go out completely. A beat of silence.]*

## Practice B (Session 4) — Foil matrix, second pass {#practice-b-session-4}

1. Complete the Maya/Jay foil matrix from L2 if unfinished. Add a fifth
   row: **What each character fears** (cite one line or direction each).
2. ★ Test this foil claim against the three rules: "Maya and the
   basketball are foils — she is organized and it keeps rolling away."
   Identify exactly which rules it passes and which it fails.
3. Choose one dimension from your matrix. Write 4–5 sentences proving
   the contrast with two quoted lines (one per character) and one
   sentence on what the contrast reveals about the theme.
4. Predict: in Lesson 3's Shakespeare scene, which character do you
   expect to be the "planner" and which the "improviser"? Write one
   sentence. (You will check this prediction after L3.)

## Vocabulary (Session 5) — Drama terms {#vocabulary-session-5}

1. Define in your own words: *dialogue, stage direction, act, scene,
   aside, soliloquy, monologue, foil*.
2. ★ Explain the difference between an *aside* and a *soliloquy* in two
   sentences, giving the "who can hear it" rule for each.
3. Use three of the terms in original sentences about "The Stairwell."
4. Dictionary routine: look up *foil* — find the jewelry/gemstone sense
   and copy the definition. In one sentence, connect it to the literary
   term.

## Practice C (Session 7) — Turning points in R&J 3.1 {#practice-c-session-7}

Use the Act 3, Scene 1 excerpt ([L3](lessons/lesson-03-dialogue-propels-action.md)).

1. List three lines where a character tries to *prevent* the fight.
   Quote each (short quotes are fine) and name the speaker.
2. ★ A classmate says the turning point is "when Tybalt arrives." Argue
   for a *later* line as the true point of no return, using the
   three-job frame (propel / reveal / provoke). 4–5 sentences.
3. Find one line where Mercutio's *word choice* (not just his meaning)
   reveals his character. Quote it and analyze two specific words.
4. Dramatic irony, second angle: Benvolio warns "we shall not 'scape a
   brawl" in the scene's first lines. The audience has watched two
   earlier street fights in the play. In two sentences, explain how that
   foreknowledge changes the suspense of this scene.

## Practice D (Session 9) — Diction in the soliloquy {#practice-d-session-9}

Use the Hamlet excerpt ([L4](lessons/lesson-04-soliloquy-vs-scene.md)).

1. Paraphrase these two lines into plain modern English (one sentence
   each):
   - "Whether 'tis nobler in the mind to suffer / The slings and arrows
     of outrageous fortune"
   - "To sleep: perchance to dream: ay, there's the rub"
2. ★ Explain why "a sea of troubles" is a *stronger* image than "a pile
   of troubles" would be. Discuss connotation and impossibility in 3–4
   sentences.
3. Find the line where Hamlet shifts from welcoming death to fearing it.
   Quote it and explain the shift in two sentences.
4. **Foil drill:** Laertes learns of his father's death in Act 4 and
   storms the castle with followers. In 3–4 sentences, contrast his
   response with Hamlet's using one quoted phrase from the soliloquy.

## Practice E (Session 12) — Adaptation tracker, new scene {#practice-e-session-12}

1. Reread the Act 2, Scene 2 excerpt ([L5](lessons/lesson-05-adaptation-text-to-stage.md)).
   List two *faithful* choices any stage production must make to keep
   the scene recognizable, and two choices a director is *free* to
   change. One sentence of justification each.
2. ★ A director cuts Juliet's "What's in a name?" speech to four lines
   to save time. Evaluate the cut: what does the scene gain, and what
   does it lose? Cite one specific image from the speech. 4–5 sentences.
3. Stage Juliet's entrance two ways: (a) she appears *before* Romeo
   speaks, (b) she appears *after* his first five lines. For each, state
   in one sentence how the timing changes the dramatic irony.
4. Begin project brainstorming: which scene will you adapt for
   [Stage It](project.md), and what is the *one* choice you are most
   excited to defend? Write 2–3 sentences.

## Vocabulary (Session 15) — Citation and tense {#vocabulary-session-15}

1. Write the citation format for drama quotes taught in this unit:
   *Act.Scene* (e.g., *Rom.* 3.1). Then cite one line from L3 and one
   from L4 correctly.
2. ★ Correct these to literary present tense, then explain the rule in
   one sentence:
   - "Shakespeare showed that Mercutio was brave."
   - "Juliet didn't know Romeo was listening."
3. Define: *adaptation, faithful, depart, director, pacing,
   characterization*. Use *pacing* and *characterization* in sentences
   about the Zeffirelli balcony scene.
4. Proofread your Practice C answers: circle every past-tense verb
   describing the play's action and rewrite it in literary present
   tense.

## Synthesis block (Session 16) — Cold foil analysis {#synthesis-block-session-16}

Read this new original excerpt once, then answer without rereading the
lessons.

*Two lifeguards, end of shift. REN folds towels in sharp creases. KAI
spins a rescue buoy on one finger.*

REN: *[not looking up]* Tower log. Incidents: zero. Sunscreen
applications reminded: fourteen. You signed none of the forms.

KAI: The forms signed themselves in spirit. Nobody drowned, nobody
cried, nobody even got stung. That's the real log.

REN: The real log is the log. *[She holds out the pen]* Sign.

KAI: *[takes the pen, signs with a flourish, draws a small wave under
his name]* There. Now it's art *and* administration.

REN: *[studies the wave for a long moment]* …It's crooked.

KAI: It's *alive*.

1. Name the foil pair and test it against the three rules (one sentence
   per rule).
2. Quote one Ren line and one Kai line; in 4–5 sentences explain what
   their contrast reveals about the scene's theme.
3. Identify one line that propels the action (causes the next thing to
   happen). Explain the cause and effect in two sentences.
