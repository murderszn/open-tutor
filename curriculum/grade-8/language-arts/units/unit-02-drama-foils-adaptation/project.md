# Project — Stage It: Adaptation Portfolio

**Sessions 13–14, 17** (plus pitch prep) · **Type:** investigation /
creative production · **Standards:** W.8.3, SL.8.1, RL.8.7

You are the director. Choose **one** scene — "The Stairwell" (this
unit), the *Romeo and Juliet* 3.1 excerpt, or the *Romeo and Juliet*
2.2 excerpt — and adapt it for a new stage production. Your production
makes deliberate choices; your portfolio *defends* them with evidence.

## Deliverables

1. **The adapted scene (1–2 pages):** dialogue plus original stage
   directions. You may trim lines, reorder within the scene, or add up
   to 6 lines of new dialogue — every addition or cut must be marked
   *(ADDED)* or *(CUT)* in the margin.
2. **Director's rationale (300–450 words):** at least three deliberate
   choices (one per tracker row: setting & design, pacing & cuts, body
   & voice). For each: what you kept or changed, whether it is faithful
   or a departure, and what the choice *serves*, proved with at least
   one quoted line from the original text.
3. **Foil note (1 paragraph):** if your scene has a foil pair, explain
   how your staging sharpens their contrast (lighting, costume,
   blocking, or delivery). If it has none, explain which character your
   staging spotlights and how.
4. **Pitch (60–90 seconds):** a spoken trailer for your production —
   your interpretive angle, one piece of textual evidence, and a
   spoiler-aware invitation to the audience (adapted from
   `templates/audiobook-trailers.md`). Record it or perform it live.

## Steps

1. **Choose and reread (Session 13, 20 min).** Read your scene aloud
   twice. Mark the lines you cannot cut — the scene's load-bearing
   walls.
2. **Choice tracker (Session 13, 25 min).** Fill a three-row tracker
   (setting & design / pacing & cuts / body & voice). Draft each choice
   as *choice → faithful or departed → what it serves*.
3. **Draft the scene (Session 14).** Write dialogue and stage
   directions. Read it aloud; cut anything that sounds wrong spoken.
4. **Rationale (Session 14–17).** Turn tracker rows into paragraphs.
   Every choice needs a quoted line as evidence — "I cut X because…"
   must finish with "…as shown when [character] says '[quote].'"
5. **Foil note + pitch (Session 17).** Rehearse the pitch twice with a
   timer. 60–90 seconds means roughly 130–180 spoken words.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Deliberate choices** | 3+ choices, each labeled faithful/departed with a clear served purpose | 3 choices labeled; purposes stated but one is vague | Fewer than 3 choices, or labels missing | Choices undescribed; changes look accidental |
| **Textual evidence** | Every choice proved with a quoted line; citations in Act.Scene form | Most choices have quoted evidence; minor citation slips | Evidence is paraphrase-only or mismatched to the choice | No textual evidence |
| **Foil / spotlight note** | Contrast sharpened through a specific staging tool, explained | Contrast identified; staging link general | Foil misidentified or staging undescribed | Missing |
| **Scene craft** | Dialogue sounds speakable; stage directions precise and actable; cuts/additions marked | Speakable with minor awkwardness; directions clear | Dialogue reads as prose; directions vague | Incomplete or unperformable |
| **Pitch** | 60–90 sec; angle + evidence + invitation; confident delivery | Meets time; all three parts present | Over/under time or a part missing | Missing or read verbatim without rehearsal |
| **Conventions** | Literary present tense; dialogue punctuation correct | 1–2 slips | Frequent slips | Interferes with meaning |

**Scoring:** 21–24 = exceeds · 16–20 = meets · 11–15 = approaching ·
6–10 = revisit with the teacher guide's reteaching moves. The rationale's
evidence column counts double in the adult's holistic judgment.

## Academic integrity

Adaptations of Shakespeare are everywhere — that is the point of the
unit. Your *choices* and *rationale* must be your own. Watching a film
version for ideas is allowed; copying its staging without credit is not.
Name any production you borrowed from in the rationale ("inspired by the
RSC's 2010 staging choice to…").
