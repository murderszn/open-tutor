# Practice — Unit 05: Narrative Perspective, Pacing, and Crafted Scenes

Work these in the sessions named in the unit
[README](README.md) pacing table. Check your work against the
[answer key](answer-key.md) after finishing each section — the
key explains the reasoning, and for open responses it gives a
sample response, not the only correct one.

## Practice A — Point-of-view identification (Session 2)

Label each passage **1P** (first person), **3L** (third-person
limited), or **3O** (third-person omniscient). For each, write
one sentence naming whose thoughts you hear — and, for 3O, one
thing the narrator knows that no character knows.

1. "I had packed my suitcase twice and unpacked it once, and I
   still was not sure I was doing the right thing."
2. "Mara had packed her suitcase twice and unpacked it once,
   and she still was not sure she was doing the right thing."
3. "Mara had packed her suitcase twice and unpacked it once.
   Jon, watching from the doorway, was sure she was doing the
   right thing — he had packed his own bag the night before,
   though she did not know that."
4. "We stood on the platform until the train came into view."
5. "The keeper lifted his lantern and looked down the track. He
   had seen a hundred travelers wait on that platform, and he
   knew which ones would board and which ones would turn back —
   though he never said so aloud."
6. "I lifted my lantern and looked down the track. I had seen
   a hundred travelers wait on that platform."
7. "Mara did not know that Jon had already bought her ticket.
   She only knew that he was smiling in a way she could not
   read."
8. "She smiled in a way he could not read, and he wondered what
   she was hiding."

Then rewrite passage 2 in first person (two or three sentences)
and passage 3 in third-person limited to Mara (two or three
sentences). Keep the facts the same; change only what the
narrator can know.

## Practice B — Pacing rewrite (Session 7)

**Expand (turn a summary into a scene).** Each item gives a
summary and a focus. Write four or five sentences. Keep the
facts; add dialogue, action, or sensory detail — and write one
sentence after each expansion saying why this moment deserves a
scene.

1. Summary: "Mara said goodbye to her mother at the door."
   Focus: the goodbye matters — it is the last one before a
   long journey.
2. Summary: "Jon ran to catch the bus."
   Focus: the run matters — he will miss the train if he misses
   the bus.
3. Summary: "The keeper lit the lamps at dusk."
   Focus: the lighting matters — it is the keeper's nightly
   ritual, and the traveler is watching.

**Compress (turn a scene into a summary).** Each item gives a
scene. Write one sentence that keeps every fact.

4. "Mara woke at six. She dressed in the dark. She ate bread
   standing in the kitchen. She put on her coat. She walked to
   the station. She bought her ticket."
5. "Jon checked his pocket. His ticket was there. He checked
   his other pocket. His money was there. He checked his bag.
   His book was there."
6. "The rain fell. Mara pulled her coat tight. The rain fell
   harder. She moved under the roof. The rain kept falling."

## Practice C — Scene craft (Session 10)

1. Write a scene of six to eight sentences set at the market.
   It must contain: two lines of dialogue in which the second
   line changes the first speaker's plan; one sensory detail
   for smell and one for sound; and one reflection sentence
   (underline it).
2. Rewrite this "telling" paragraph as "showing" (four to five
   sentences). Keep the facts: "Jon was embarrassed. He had
   forgotten the tickets. Mara was not angry, but Jon felt
   terrible anyway." Do not use the words *embarrassed*,
   *angry*, or *terrible*.
3. Repair this flat dialogue exchange by adding one beat and
   changing one line so that it propels the action. Keep the
   facts (Mara asks about the train; Jon answers that it is
   late):
   > "'Is the train coming?' Mara asked. 'It is late,' Jon
   > said."
4. Write one reflection sentence for this moment: Jon finds the
   timetable Mara circled for him. The reflection must say what
   Jon understands now, in his own situation — not a general
   moral.
5. Write a dialogue of four lines (two speakers) in which the
   second speaker reveals a fact the first speaker did not
   know, and the first speaker's reply shows they have changed
   their mind. No adverbs in the tags; use *said* at least once.
6. Explain in two or three sentences why a writer would choose
   to *tell* the reader that a character walked home but *show*
   the reader the moment the character decided to leave. Use the
   words *matters*, *scene*, and *summary* in your answer.

## Practice R1 — Reading: "The Gift of the Magi" (Session 4)

Read O. Henry's "The Gift of the Magi" (1905) in full at the
link in the [Resource Pack](resource-pack.md) (Project
Gutenberg). Then answer:

1. What is the story's point of view — first person,
   third-person limited, or third-person omniscient? Quote or
   describe two details that prove it (one showing the narrator
   knows Della's thoughts, one showing the narrator knows Jim's
   thoughts or a fact neither character knows).
2. What does the reader know, before the final scene, that
   Della does not know? What does the reader know that Jim does
   not know? Answer each in one sentence.
3. The story opens with a precise number: "One dollar and
   eighty-seven cents. That was all." What does that exact detail
   do for the reader that a vague opening ("Della was poor")
   would not do? Answer in two sentences.
4. Name one passage the story tells as a **scene** (with dialogue
   or moment-by-moment action) and one passage it tells as a
   **summary** (time compressed). For each, say in one sentence
   why O. Henry gave that moment its speed.

## Practice R2 — Discussion: "The Gift of the Magi" (Session 5)

Discuss with the adult, with evidence. Prepare two quotations
before the discussion.

1. The narrator ends the story by calling Della and Jim "the
   magi" — the wisest of all who give gifts. Do you agree with
   the narrator's judgment? Defend your answer with two details
   from the story.
2. In Unit 02 you studied dramatic irony in Shakespeare: the
   audience knows something a character does not. How is the
   irony in "The Gift of the Magi" the same as dramatic irony,
   and how is it different? (Hint: who knows what, and when?)
3. If the story were told in first person by Della, what would
   the reader lose? Name two things the first-person version
   could not show, and one thing it might gain.

## Practice R3 — Reading: "An Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge" (Session 9)

Read Ambrose Bierce's "An Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge"
(1890) in full at the link in the [Resource Pack](resource-pack.md)
(Project Gutenberg). **Adult preview first** — the story centers
on a hanging and an imagined escape. Then answer:

1. Map the story's time handling in three parts: (a) the opening
   on the bridge — scene or summary, and how much story time
   does it cover? (b) the middle part about how Farquhar came
   to the bridge — what is it (flashback or chronology), and what
   question does it answer? (c) the escape through the river and
   woods — scene or summary, and how does its speed compare with
   the opening?
2. The story's last line reveals that the escape was imagined:
   Farquhar was dead all along. What is the one fact the narrator
   held back from the reader until that line? Answer in one
   sentence.
3. Find one passage in which time seems to expand — a few
   seconds of story time told in many words (for example, the
   falling, the water, or the running). Describe it in two
   sentences and say what the expansion makes the reader feel.
4. The story is told in third person and stays close to
   Farquhar's senses (what he sees, hears, and feels). Find one
   detail the narrator reports that Farquhar himself could not
   know or could not have noticed, and explain in one sentence
   why that detail matters for the ending.

## Practice R4 — Reading: "The Story of an Hour" (Session 15)

Read Kate Chopin's "The Story of an Hour" (1894) in full at the
link in the [Resource Pack](resource-pack.md) (Project Gutenberg
collection). **Adult preview first** — the story centers on a
reported death, a marriage, and a sudden death at the end. Then
answer:

1. What is the story's point of view? Name the character whose
   thoughts the narrator follows, and quote or describe one
   detail that proves the narrator stays with that character.
2. The whole story takes about one hour. How does Chopin use
   that single hour — what part of the hour is expanded into a
   scene, and what part is compressed? Answer in two or three
   sentences.
3. What does the reader know about Louise's feelings that her
   sister Josephine does not know? Answer in one sentence, with
   one detail from the story.
4. The doctors say Louise died of "the joy that kills." What
   does the reader know that the doctors do not know? Answer in
   one sentence, and say in a second sentence how that gap is
   dramatic irony.
