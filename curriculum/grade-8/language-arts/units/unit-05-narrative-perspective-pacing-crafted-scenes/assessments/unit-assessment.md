# Unit Assessment — Unit 05: Narrative Perspective, Pacing, and Crafted Scenes

**Time:** ~45 minutes. **Points:** 24. Write your answers on
separate paper. Do not open the answer key — your adult has it
separately.

## Part 1 — Reading a narrative passage (8 points)

Read the passage below. It is original, written for this unit.
Then answer questions 1–4 (2 points each).

> **The Last Train**
>
> Mara had waited at the station for two hours, and the lamps
> had come on one by one. She checked the clock: ten minutes
> to six. She moved her suitcase out of the wind and sat on the
> bench, where the wood was still warm from the afternoon sun.
>
> That morning, Jon had said, "Leaving is a mistake," and she
> had answered, "Staying is worse." She had packed anyway, and
> bought her ticket at noon. Now the lamps were coming on, and
> she was still deciding whether he would come.
>
> The station keeper lifted his lantern and looked down the
> track, then looked back at her. "The last train is running
> late tonight," he said. "It will come, but not before seven."
>
> Mara stood up. If the train came at seven, she would miss her
> connection in the city — and the whole journey would have to
> wait until morning. She looked at the keeper and said, "Then
> I will wait here with you." It was the first time all day she
> had been sure of anything.

**1.** What is the passage's point of view — first person,
third-person limited, or third-person omniscient? Quote or
describe **two** details that prove it.

**2.** Name one passage in the story that is told as a
**scene** and one passage told as a **summary**. For each, say
in one sentence why the writer gave that moment its speed.

**3.** What does the keeper's line "The last train is running
late tonight" reveal, and what does it change for Mara? Answer
in two sentences.

**4.** The passage contains a flashback. Quote the transition
into it and the transition back to the present, and say in one
sentence what the flashback explains about the present moment.

## Part 2 — Pacing rewrite (6 points)

**5.** (3 points) Expand this summary into a scene of four to
six sentences. Keep the facts (Jon runs; he misses the bus; he
is late for the train). Include one line of dialogue and one
sensory detail.

> "Jon ran to catch the bus and missed it, so he was late for
> the train."

**6.** (3 points) Compress this scene into **one** sentence.
Keep every fact (Jon woke at six; dressed in the dark; ate
bread standing in the kitchen; walked to the station in the
cold; bought his ticket; waited).

> "Jon woke at six. He dressed in the dark so he would not wake
> Mara. He ate bread standing up in the kitchen. He walked to
> the station in the cold and bought his ticket from the sleepy
> keeper. Then he waited."

## Part 3 — Write a narrative scene (10 points)

**7.** (10 points) Write a scene of **200–300 words** set at a
place you know well (a kitchen, a park, a bus stop, a gym).
Your scene must contain:

- a **named point of view** (write it at the top: first person,
  third-person limited to one character, or third-person
  omniscient);
- **dialogue that propels action** — at least two lines, in
  which the second line changes the first speaker's plan or
  reveals a new fact;
- **one sensory detail** (sound, touch, smell, or taste) and
  **one reflection sentence** (underline it);
- **one time shift** — a flashback or a foreshadowing detail —
  with a clear transition into it.

**Scoring for Part 3** (2 points each): point of view named and
used consistently · dialogue propels action · sensory detail
present and precise · reflection sentence underlined and
specific to this scene · time shift with a clear transition.
