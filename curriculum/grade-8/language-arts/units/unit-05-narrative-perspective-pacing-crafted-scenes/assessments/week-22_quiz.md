# Formative Quiz — Unit 05, Week 22 (Track Week 22)

**Time:** ~25 minutes. **Points:** 14. Answer in the spaces or
on separate paper. Do not open the answer key — your adult has
it separately.

**Questions 1–8:** circle the best answer (1 point each).

**1.** Which passage is written in **third-person omniscient**
point of view?

a. "I checked the clock for the third time. Jon was late
again, and I was sure he had forgotten me."
b. "Mara checked the clock for the third time. Jon was late
again, and she was sure he had forgotten her."
c. "Mara checked the clock for the third time, sure Jon had
forgotten her. Across town, Jon was running for the bus,
furious at himself for losing track of the time."

**2.** The knowledge test asks two questions. Which pair is
correct?

a. "Is the narrator a character? Does the narrator use *I*?"
b. "Whose thoughts am I hearing? Whose thoughts am I not
hearing?"
c. "Is the story true? Is the narrator honest?"

**3.** Which narrator shows the clearest sign of being
**unreliable**?

a. "I waited an hour. The clock showed six when I arrived and
seven when the train came."
b. "I am always on time. I arrived at seven for a six o'clock
train, because I am never late."
c. "I did not know whether the train would come, so I watched
the track and waited."

**4.** Which situation is **dramatic irony**?

a. The reader learns at the last line that the whole escape
was a dream.
b. The reader knows the traveler is leaving for good, while
the station keeper believes the traveler is only visiting for
the weekend.
c. The narrator tells the reader that the story is about a
train station.

**5.** Which passage is a **summary** rather than a scene?

a. "'It'll come,' the keeper said, lifting his lantern. Mara
nodded, though she did not believe him."
b. "Mara waited at the station for two hours. The train did
not come, and the lamps came on one by one."
c. "Mara checked the clock. Ten minutes to six. She moved her
suitcase out of the wind and sat on the bench."

**6.** Which sentence is **foreshadowing** rather than a
spoiler?

a. "Mara would never return to this town."
b. "Mara looked at her house for the last time, and left the
key on the table."
c. "This story is about Mara leaving home forever."

**7.** Which line of dialogue **propels the action**?

a. "'Nice evening,' Jon said. 'Yes, nice,' Mara said."
b. "'I sold the watch,' Jim said."
c. "'Hello,' the keeper said. 'Hello,' Mara said."

**8.** Which line uses a **beat** instead of an adverb to show
how the line was said?

a. "'It'll come,' the keeper said hopefully."
b. "'It'll come,' the keeper said, lifting his lantern."
c. "'It'll come!' the keeper said loudly."

**Questions 9–12:** write your answers (points as shown).

**9.** (2 points) Read this passage. Name its point of view
(first person, third-person limited, or third-person
omniscient) and name one thing the narrator knows that no
single character in the passage knows.

> "Jon stared at the timetable. He had never missed a train in
> his life, and he was not about to start tonight. Mara,
> watching from the bench, was sure he would miss it — she had
> seen him miss three buses that month."

**10.** (2 points) Expand this summary into a scene of two or
three sentences. Keep the facts (Mara waits; it is raining; the
train is late). Include one line of dialogue and one sensory
detail.

> "Mara waited an hour for the bus in the rain."

**11.** (1 point) Write a transition (one clause or sentence)
that moves the reader from the station platform at dusk to
Mara's kitchen the next morning.

**12.** (1 point) In one sentence, explain what makes a
conclusion **reflective** rather than a moral.
