# Project — Two Ways to Tell It

**Time:** Sessions 12–14 (three ~45-minute workshops) and
Session 17 (final revision + craft note); the craft note may be
homework or a short extra session.
**Standards:** W.8.3, W.8.3.a–e, W.8.4, W.8.5, W.8.10, RL.8.6,
RL.8.3, L.8.6.

## The task

You will write a complete original narrative — and then prove
you understand point of view by telling its opening a second
way. The project has three parts.

### Part 1 — The narrative (workshops 1–2)

Write an original narrative of **600–900 words** about one
evening event. Choose one of these topics, or propose your own
to the adult (it must be one evening, two characters, and a
decision):

1. **The station wait** — a traveler waits on a platform at
   dusk for a train (use the unit's illustration as your
   setting if you like); the station keeper is the second
   character.
2. **The farewell** — one character leaves on a bus at night;
   the other came to say goodbye but is hiding a gift, a
   secret, or a change of plan.
3. **The night before** — the evening before a first day at a
   new school; two characters (the learner and a parent,
   sibling, or friend) disagree about whether to go.
4. **The returned thing** — a character finds something lost
   and returns it in the evening; the owner is not who they
   expected.

Your narrative must contain:

- a **chosen point of view** (first person, third-person
  limited, or third-person omniscient) used consistently;
- an **event sequence that unfolds naturally** — the reader can
  answer "why did this happen?" at every step;
- at least **two crafted scenes** with dialogue that propels
  action, sensory description, and a reflection sentence;
- **pacing on purpose**: at least one passage of summary that
  compresses unimportant time and at least one scene that
  expands an important moment — and the reader can tell which
  is which;
- at least one **time shift** — a flashback or a foreshadowing
  detail — with a clear transition into it and back out;
- a **reflective conclusion** that follows from the events and
  says what the narrator (or a character) understands now.

### Part 2 — The perspective shift (workshop 3)

Rewrite your narrative's **opening scene** (150–250 words) from
the **second character's point of view**. If Part 1 was first
person as character A, Part 2 is first person as character B, or
third-person limited to character B. If Part 1 was third-person
limited to character A, Part 2 is third-person limited to
character B (or first person as B). The facts must stay the
same — the same evening, the same place, the same things said
and done — and only the narrator's knowledge may change. Add
one sentence at the end of Part 2 naming one thing this second
narrator knows that the first narrator did not know.

### Part 3 — Craft note (final revision session)

Write a note of **150–200 words** explaining **four** of your
craft choices, one for each:

1. a **point-of-view** choice (why this narrator, and what the
   narrator can and cannot know);
2. a **pacing** choice (one moment you expanded into a scene
   and one stretch you compressed into summary — and why each
   deserved its speed);
3. a **scene** choice (one dialogue exchange or sensory detail,
   and what it does in the story);
4. a **conclusion** choice (what your reflective conclusion says
   that a summary ending would not say).

Each explanation must name the choice, quote the words from your
narrative, and state the effect on the reader. A note that only
lists choices without reasons earns no more than half credit on
this part (see the rubric).

## Deliverables

1. The narrative (600–900 words), revised, with its point of
   view named at the top.
2. The perspective-shift opening (150–250 words) from the
   second character's point of view, with its point of view
   named at the top and the one-sentence knowledge note.
3. The craft note (150–200 words, four choices).

## Alternative when no earlier draft is on hand

This project is self-contained: it uses only the topic list
above. If the learner has a narrative draft from an earlier
unit or from school, the adult may approve it as the Part 1
base — but the draft must still be revised to meet every
requirement above (two crafted scenes, pacing on purpose, one
time shift, reflective conclusion), and Parts 2 and 3 must be
written fresh for this project.

## Rubric

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Solid | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Point of view (Part 1 + 2) | POV is consistent throughout; Part 2 changes only the narrator's knowledge, not the facts; the knowledge note names a real difference | POV is mostly consistent (one small slip); Part 2 keeps the facts with one small change | POV slips more than once, or Part 2 changes facts, not only knowledge | POV is unclear or changes without purpose; Part 2 retells a different event |
| Pacing and structure (Part 1) | Scenes expand the moments that matter; summary compresses the rest; the time shift has clear transitions both ways; the sequence unfolds naturally | Pacing is mostly purposeful, with one scene or summary misjudged; transitions are present | Pacing is flat (everything one speed) or the time shift confuses the reader | No clear pacing choices; events do not follow from each other |
| Scene craft (Part 1) | Both scenes have dialogue that propels action, sensory description, and reflection; language is precise | Scenes have most elements; one scene is thin on dialogue, description, or reflection | Scenes report events with little dialogue or description; reflection is generic | Scenes are summaries; no working dialogue or sensory detail |
| Craft note and conclusion (Parts 1 + 3) | Conclusion reflects on the events and belongs only to this story; all 4 choices explained with quotes and effects | Conclusion reflects but could fit another story; 3 choices explained, or one explanation missing its effect | Conclusion only summarizes or moralizes; 2 choices explained | No reflective conclusion; fewer than 2 choices explained |

**Scoring:** 14–16 = exceeds; 11–13 = meets; 8–10 = approaching;
below 8 = reteach with Lessons 01, 03, and 06, then revise one
part. The [answer key](answer-key.md) gives a model opening in
two points of view and a model craft-note paragraph; other
narratives that meet the same requirements are acceptable.
