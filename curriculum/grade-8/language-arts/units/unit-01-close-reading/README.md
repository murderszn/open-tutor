# Unit 01 — Close Reading: Inference, Theme, and Evidence Synthesis

**Grade:** 8 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #40](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/40), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A close reader does three things: notices exactly what the text says,
figures out what it suggests but does not say, and proves it with the
strongest available evidence. In this unit the learner builds those three
moves across four weeks — first on short original stories, then on two
public-domain poems (Langston Hughes's "Mother to Son," 1922, and Claude
McKay's "If We Must Die," 1919), then on an original informational passage
about the Tacoma Narrows Bridge. By the end, the learner can state a theme,
trace how it develops, write an objective summary, explain how word choice
shapes tone, and synthesize evidence into a short supported analysis.

## Essential question

How do readers prove that what a text *suggests* is really there?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the grade-7 track:

- Read grade-7-level fiction and nonfiction independently and write a
  multi-paragraph response stating an opinion with at least one reason
- Identify basic literary terms (simile, metaphor, theme, plot, character)
- Write complete sentences with mostly correct capitalization and end
  punctuation

The unit re-teaches inference, quotation/paraphrase mechanics, and summary
from the ground up — no prior mastery is assumed.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Distinguish explicit statements from inferences in literary and
   informational texts, and cite the strongest textual evidence supporting
   each claim (RL.8.1, RI.8.1).
2. Determine a theme or central idea, analyze how it develops through
   characters, setting, and plot, and write an objective summary of a text
   (RL.8.2).
3. Integrate evidence using quotation, paraphrase, and lead-ins with correct
   punctuation and attribution.
4. Analyze how word choice — figurative language, connotation, and allusion —
   shapes meaning and tone (RL.8.4).
5. Determine the meaning of unknown words using context clues, Greek/Latin
   affixes and roots, and reference materials (L.8.4, L.8.5).

## Materials

Notebook or paper, pen/pencil, the five lesson pages, highlighters in two
colors (one for explicit statements, one for inference-supporting details),
a print or digital dictionary/thesaurus, sticky notes or index cards for the
evidence chart, and the generated illustration. No purchases required; no
hazardous materials.

## Assessment summary

- **Exit checks** (each lesson): 2–3 quick items; scored in the
  [answer key](answer-key.md).
- **Formative quiz** (Session 18): 12 items — inference, theme, vocabulary
  in context, summary, word choice.
- **Project** (Sessions 13–17): the **Evidence Portfolio** — an evidence
  chart plus a short supported theme analysis, scored with the
  [project rubric](project.md#rubric).
- **Unit assessment** (Session 20): a new original passage read cold, with
  evidence, theme, summary, and word-choice tasks.

All scoring guidance, misconception notes, and reteaching moves live in
the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md); every item is solved in the
[answer key](answer-key.md). Teacher materials are labeled and kept
separate from learner pages.
