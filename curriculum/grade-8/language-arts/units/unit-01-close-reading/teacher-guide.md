# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Close Reading, Inference, Theme, Evidence Synthesis

**For the guiding adult.** Learner-facing pages never link here directly
except where labeled; keep this guide and the [answer
key](answer-key.md) separate from the learner's materials. All timing
assumes ~45-minute sessions, five per week; adapt freely.

## How to run this unit

1. **Read the lesson yourself first** (10 min). Note the worked
   examples — they model the exact reasoning you want to hear back.
2. **Teach the worked examples aloud**, thinking visibly: "Watch how I
   test this inference…"
3. **Release gradually:** guided practice together → independent
   practice solo → application as the day's proof of learning.
4. **Score exit checks the same day** with the answer key. A missed exit
   check re-teaches tomorrow's first 10 minutes; do not let gaps
   compound across weeks.
5. **Save everything.** First drafts from this unit feed U04's revision
   work; the evidence-chart habit feeds every later unit.

## Lesson-by-lesson guidance

### Lesson 01 {#lesson-01}

*Goal:* separate explicit statements from inferences; demand named
evidence.

- **Discussion move:** after the readiness check, ask "What did you
  *see* vs. what did you *decide*?" Keep pressing until the learner
  distinguishes the two without prompting.
- **Image activity:** accept any reasonable observation list. The key
  teaching moment is the *weak* inference (the book): model saying
  "probably" and asking what would strengthen it. Uncertainty stated
  honestly is a skill, not a failure.
- **Watch for:** learners listing inferences as observations ("the kid
  is waiting for the bus" stated as fact). Redirect: "Can you point to
  it? Then it's an inference — which is fine, as long as it's labeled."
- **Exit check bar:** #1 must name (b) *and* explain that no sentence
  states worry outright. If the learner picks (b) but cannot explain,
  the concept is not secure.

### Lesson 02 {#lesson-02}

*Goal:* rank evidence; integrate quotations, paraphrases, lead-ins.

- **The ranking table is the lesson's heart.** Do not accept "they're
  all good evidence." Force the 1–2–3 and the *why* — the "if this were
  my only detail" test.
- **Punctuation:** teach the two rules (comma/colon before full-sentence
  quotes; no comma for blended fragments; periods inside quotes) as
  *reader courtesy*, not arbitrary law: the reader must always know
  where the author's words start and stop.
- **Watch for:** quote-bombing (a quotation with no lead-in) and
  paraphrases presented as the learner's own idea. Every paraphrase
  needs an attribution signal ("Mara keeps checking…," "the narrator
  notes…").

### Lesson 03 {#lesson-03}

*Goal:* theme as a full-sentence, whole-text claim; tracking across
beginning/middle/end.

- **The repair exercise (Guided A) does the heavy lifting.** Let the
  learner struggle with #3 ("life is unfair and nobody can change it")
  — the contradiction with "I'se still climbin'" is the discovery.
- **Accept theme-statement variation.** The answer key lists the
  acceptable range; any full sentence the *whole poem* supports earns
  credit. Reject topic-phrases and morals detached from the text.
- **"The Empty Chair" note:** grief is the topic; the theme is about
  what the family *does* with absence. If a learner writes "death is
  sad," push: "What does the *plate* tell us that 'sad' doesn't?"

### Lesson 04 {#lesson-04}

*Goal:* objective summary; central idea in informational text.

- **The opinion purge is physical:** have the learner literally cross
  out judgment words with a pen. The visual of a summary surviving
  without them teaches objectivity better than any definition.
- **Watch for:** summaries that are *accurate but empty* ("It was about
  a bridge that fell"). Demand the dates, numbers, and names — precise
  *and* neutral.
- **Bridge to Lesson 3:** explicitly name that central idea is theme's
  informational sibling; the tracking method (repeated idea, opening and
  closing frame) transfers.

### Lesson 05 {#lesson-05}

*Goal:* connotation, tone, allusion; three vocabulary strategies.

- **Read the poem aloud twice** (flat, then defiant). Ask what changed —
  the answer is always word stress, which leads naturally to tone
  builders.
- **Connotation sort:** expect disagreement on *willful* vs. *firm*.
  That disagreement is the lesson; do not resolve it by fiat. Ask what
  context would flip each word's charge.
- **McKay context:** give the two-sentence Red Summer note yourself;
  do not ask the learner to research it. Preview for mature themes per
  the track's adult guidance.
- **Allusion:** keep it at the pattern level (heroic last-stand shape).
  Do not require naming a specific epic.

## Practice sessions

- **A (S2), B (S4):** run as designed; B's proofreads are quick wins —
  celebrate them.
- **Vocabulary (S5, S15):** the dictionary/thesaurus routines are
  *skills*, not filler. Time them; learners who cannot verify a word
  in under two minutes need the routine re-taught, not more words.
- **C (S7), D (S9), E (S12):** C's stress test and D's opinion hunt are
  the week's formative data — read them before planning Sessions 10–11.
- **Synthesis block (S16):** run under mild time pressure (40 min for
  all four tasks). It rehearses the assessment; note which objective
  wobbles and re-teach it in Session 19.

## Project facilitation

- **Text choice:** steer struggling learners to "The Lantern" or "The
  Empty Chair" (narrative is easier to track than poetry); steer strong
  learners to McKay (tone analysis has more to chew on).
- **The chart before the paragraph.** Do not let drafting start until
  the six rows exist — the paragraph is then assembly, not invention.
- **The challenging question (Session 17):** prepare one in advance,
  e.g. "What is the *weakest* row on your chart, and why did you keep
  it?" or "What would change your theme statement?"
- **Scoring:** use the rubric as written; the "revise one criterion"
  band (9–12) is the expected outcome for a first attempt, not a
  failure.

## Quiz and assessment administration

- **Week-6 quiz (S18):** closed-book for the passage (it is printed on
  the quiz), open-notebook for vocabulary definitions. 30 minutes is
  firm; unfinished items become Session 19's review set.
- **Unit assessment (S20):** cold read, closed everything except a
  dictionary for Task 8. Read the passage aloud once for accessibility
  if needed, then let the learner work.
- **After scoring:** the pattern of misses across the class-of-one
  matters more than the total. All misses on Objective 4 → re-teach
  Lesson 5's connotation sort before U02. All misses on Objective 2 →
  revisit Lesson 3's tracking chart with a new text.

## Reteaching moves by objective

| If this wobbles… | Reteach with… |
|---|---|
| Obj 1: inference without evidence | Lesson 1 image chart, new picture; "point to it" drill |
| Obj 2: topic instead of theme | Lesson 3 repair set, new short text; the "could you say that about any story?" test |
| Obj 3: quote-bombing / bad punctuation | Lesson 2 lead-in ladder with a fresh sentence; read integrations aloud |
| Obj 4: denotation-only answers | Lesson 5 connotation sort with new word sets; tone-contrast rewrite |
| Obj 5: skipping straight to the dictionary | Cover the definition; require context guess + word parts first |

## Extensions (for learners who finish early or want more)

- Apply the full chain (chart → track → paragraph) to a self-chosen
  short story or news article; present findings.
- Memorize and recite "Mother to Son" or "If We Must Die"; write 200
  words on how performance changed the tone reading.
- Rewrite "The Lantern"'s last paragraph from Tomas's point of view;
  analyze what the perspective change does to the inferable theme
  (previews U05).

## Accessibility notes

- All activities work orally or with adult scribing; the image activity
  has a full text-only alternative.
- Session length is a suggestion: split any 45-minute session into two
  25-minute halves with movement between.
- The McKay and Hughes poems deal with hardship, dignity, and (McKay)
  violent death — the adult previews and may substitute "The Lantern,"
  "The Empty Chair," or "Salt" for any activity without breaking the
  unit's skill sequence.
