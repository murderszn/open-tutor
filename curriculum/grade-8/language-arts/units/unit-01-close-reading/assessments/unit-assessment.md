# Unit Assessment — Unit 01: Close Reading, Inference, Theme, Evidence Synthesis

**Session:** 20 · **Time:** ~45 minutes · **Scoring:**
[answer key](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment) · 30 points

Read the passage **cold** — you have not seen it before. That is the
point: this assessment measures what you can do with a new text, not
what you memorized. Answer in complete sentences unless told otherwise.

## The Map Drawer (original passage for this assessment)

Every summer, Jonah's family drove the same route to his grandmother's
lake house: six hours, two gas stations, one diner with pie. Jonah,
sixteen now, had started driving the middle stretch. This year, at the
second gas station, his father tossed him the keys and said, "Your turn
to navigate too." Jonah unfolded the paper map his grandmother had drawn
decades ago — lake roads in blue ink, a red X for the pie diner, and in
the margin, in her looping handwriting: "Don't trust the shortcut. — G."

The GPS said the shortcut would save twenty minutes. Jonah looked at the
note, then at his father, who shrugged: "Her map, her rules." They took
the long way. The shortcut, they learned later from the diner waitress,
had washed out in the spring rains. Jonah refolded the map carefully,
the way his grandmother had taught him — thirds, then in half — and
slipped it into the glove box. Some things, he thought, you don't
update.

## Tasks

**A. Evidence and inference (8 pts)**

1. (2 pts) Write **one inference about Jonah** and **one inference
   about the grandmother** (she never appears in the story — infer from
   what others do with her things). Each inference needs **one quoted
   or precisely referenced detail** as evidence.
2. (3 pts) Three details are listed below. **Rank** them 1 (strongest) to
   3 (weakest) as evidence for the claim *"The family respects the
   grandmother's judgment,"* and justify the ranking in one sentence
   per detail.
   - (a) "Her map, her rules."
   - (b) The family drives the same route every summer.
   - (c) Jonah refolded the map "the way his grandmother had taught
     him — thirds, then in half."
3. (3 pts) Write a **claim about the father's attitude** toward the
   grandmother in one sentence, then support it with a **blended
   quotation** (fragment integrated into your sentence, punctuated
   correctly).

**B. Theme and summary (9 pts)**

4. (3 pts) State the passage's **theme** as a full sentence. Then name
   **two moments** (beginning/middle/end) that develop it, with one
   cited detail for each.
5. (4 pts) Write an **objective summary** of the passage in 4–5
   sentences. It must contain zero opinion words and no facts from
   outside the passage.
6. (2 pts) A classmate says the theme is "GPS technology is
   unreliable." Explain in two sentences why this fails as a theme of
   *this* passage.

**C. Word choice and vocabulary (8 pts)**

7. (3 pts) The grandmother's note says "Don't trust the shortcut."
   Explain the **two levels** of this line: what it means literally
   about the road, and what it suggests about the story's larger idea.
   Quote one other phrase from the last paragraph that echoes the
   larger idea.
8. (3 pts) The word **"looping"** describes the grandmother's
   handwriting. You may not know this use of *looping*.
   - (a) What does the **context** suggest it means? (1 pt)
   - (b) Break it into parts or connect it to a word you know.
     (1 pt)
   - (c) Write your final definition in your own words. (1 pt)
9. (2 pts) Describe the passage's **tone** in one word (e.g., warm,
   ironic, solemn) and name **two word choices** that create it.

**D. Synthesis paragraph (5 pts)**

10. (5 pts) Write one paragraph (6–8 sentences): *What does the map
    represent to Jonah by the end of the story?* State a claim,
    integrate **two** pieces of evidence (one quotation, one
    paraphrase — each with a lead-in), and close with one sentence
    connecting your claim to the theme. Score yourself against the
    [project rubric](../project.md#rubric) before handing in.

---

**After finishing:** check your work against the unit's core moves —
every inference has named evidence, the theme is a full sentence the
whole text supports, the summary has no opinions, and every quotation is
punctuated. Then close the booklet.
