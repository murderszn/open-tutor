# Practice Sets — Unit 01

Each set names its session, goal, tasks, and success criterion. Full
solutions are in the [answer key](answer-key.md#practice-sets); teaching
notes in the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md#practice-sessions).

## Practice A (Session 2) — Evidence sorting {#practice-a-session-2}

**Goal:** Sort explicit statements from inferences quickly and accurately.

**Tasks:**

1. Re-read "The Lantern" ([Lesson 1](lessons/lesson-01-explicit-vs-inferred-evidence.md#the-lantern-original-story-for-this-lesson)).
   Highlight **explicit facts** in one color and **inference-supporting
   details** in another. Aim for at least 6 highlights of each.
2. Complete the **image evidence chart** for the bus-stop illustration:
   three columns — *Observation* | *Inference it supports* |
   *How strong (1–3) and why*. Fill at least 4 rows.
3. Trade charts with the guiding adult. For each of their inferences,
   ask: "Which observation proves it?" If they cannot answer, the
   inference goes back for more evidence.

**Success criterion:** Every inference in your chart names at least one
observation, and you can explain one ranking choice aloud.

## Practice B (Session 4) — Evidence-integration drills {#practice-b-session-4}

**Goal:** Build fluent sentences that carry evidence.

**Tasks:**

1. **Lead-in ladder.** Take these three bare quotations from "The
   Lantern" and write a lead-in sentence for each that states a claim
   first:
   - "she had reheated it twice already"
   - "no messages, which was strange"
   - "she pretended she hadn't been watching the window at all"
2. **Paraphrase swap.** Rewrite each of your three sentences with a
   paraphrase instead of a quotation. Keep the lead-in.
3. **Punctuation proofread.** Correct these (each has exactly one
   error):
   - Mara "stood at the kitchen window," long after dark.
   - The narrator explains: Tomas "texted about everything".
   - "She checked her phone for the fourth time" the narrator reports.

**Success criterion:** All six integration sentences read smoothly aloud,
and all three proofreads are corrected with the rule named.

## Vocabulary (Session 5) — Evidence vocabulary + reference routines {#vocabulary-session-5}

**Goal:** Own the unit's evidence words and use references well.

**Tasks:**

1. Define in your own words (no copying): *inference, explicit,
   evidence, citation, claim, strongest evidence*.
2. **Dictionary drill:** look up *cite*. Write its verb forms and the
   definition that fits "cite textual evidence." Note one *wrong*
   definition for this context (e.g., "cite for a traffic violation")
   and explain why it does not fit.
3. **Thesaurus check:** find two synonyms for *infer*. For each, write
   one sentence where the synonym works and one where it fails
   ("deduce" works for evidence; "guess" fails the evidence test).

**Success criterion:** Definitions are in your own words, and you can
explain why *infer* ≠ *guess* using the dictionary.

## Practice C (Session 7) — Theme tracking in "The Empty Chair" {#practice-c-session-7}

**Goal:** Track a theme across a full story independently.

**Tasks:**

1. Re-read "The Empty Chair" ([Lesson 3](lessons/lesson-03-theme-vs-topic-tracking-theme.md#the-empty-chair-original-story-for-this-lesson)).
2. Complete the three-column tracking chart (beginning / middle / end)
   from Lesson 3's independent practice — this time without the guided
   first row.
3. Write your theme statement. Then **stress-test** it: find one detail
   in the story that does *not* obviously fit your theme, and either
   revise the theme or explain the detail's fit in two sentences.

**Success criterion:** The chart has at least two cited details per
section, and the theme survives the stress test.

## Practice D (Session 9) — Summary workshop: purging opinion {#practice-d-session-9}

**Goal:** Write summaries a disagreeing reader would accept.

**Tasks:**

1. Write a 3-sentence objective summary of "The Empty Chair."
2. **Opinion hunt:** trade with the guiding adult. Circle every word in
   each other's summary that carries judgment (*sad, sweet, unfair,
   should, unfortunately, heartwarming…*). Rewrite the summary without
   them — keeping all the facts.
3. **Length check:** is your summary under 60 words? If not, cut the
   least essential sentence. A summary is a reduction, not a retelling.

**Success criterion:** The final summary has zero opinion words and
still contains the story's main arc.

## Practice E (Session 12) — Connotation sorts and allusion hunt {#practice-e-session-12}

**Goal:** Make connotation judgments deliberately.

**Tasks:**

1. Sort these into negative / neutral / positive connotation and defend
   any close calls: *shrewd, cunning, clever · cheap, thrifty,
   economical · proud, arrogant, confident*.
2. **Allusion hunt:** re-read "Mother to Son." The "crystal stair" has
   been read as an echo of the biblical stairway-to-heaven image
   (Jacob's ladder). Write three sentences: what the echo adds, which
   words create it, and one reason a reader might *reject* the echo.
3. Return to your Lesson 5 application paragraph. Upgrade two word
   choices using a thesaurus — then write one sentence each on whether
   the upgrade actually improved the tone or just sounded fancier.

**Success criterion:** Sorts are defended with connotation reasoning,
and the allusion answer names both the echo and its evidence.

## Vocabulary (Session 15) — Affixes, roots, and reference skills {#vocabulary-session-15}

**Goal:** Attack unknown words systematically.

**Tasks:**

1. Break and define using affixes/roots: *predictable, transport,
   reunion, disagreeable, circumnavigate*. For each, name the
   prefix/root/suffix and the assembled meaning.
2. **Multiple-meaning check:** the word *track* appears in this unit as
   "track a theme." Look it up: list three other meanings with one
   example sentence each. Explain how context tells you which meaning
   applies in "track a theme."
3. Build a personal **word bank**: 6 words from the unit's poems you can
   now define and use. Write an original sentence for each.

**Success criterion:** Every affix breakdown is labeled, and the word
bank sentences use the words correctly.

## Synthesis block (Session 16) — Full evidence chart + paragraph {#synthesis-block-session-16}

**Goal:** Run the whole unit skill chain on a new text, cold.

Read this original passage once, then complete the tasks without
re-reading the lessons.

### Salt (original passage for synthesis practice)

When the power went out on the hottest night of August, Mrs. Abernathy
did what she always did: she opened her front door, dragged two kitchen
chairs onto the porch, and set a pitcher of lemonade between them.
Within an hour, half the block had drifted over — the Nguyens with their
twins, old Mr. Bell with his radio, the teenagers from the corner house
who usually kept their headphones in. Nobody said much. The ice melted,
the lemonade went warm, and still nobody left. When the lights finally
buzzed back on at midnight, people stood up slowly, the way you leave a
good movie, and Mrs. Abernathy said, "Same time next blackout," and
everybody laughed.

**Tasks:**

1. **Evidence chart:** two inferences about Mrs. Abernathy or the
   neighbors, each with two supporting details ranked by strength.
2. **Theme statement** (full sentence) + a three-moment track
   (beginning / middle / end).
3. **Word choice:** name two words or phrases whose connotation shapes
   the passage's warm tone, and explain each.
4. **Synthesis paragraph** (6–8 sentences): state the theme, track it
   through two moments with integrated evidence (one quotation, one
   paraphrase, both with lead-ins), and close with one sentence on the
   word choice.

**Success criterion:** The paragraph would earn full marks on the
[project rubric](project.md#rubric) — it is a rehearsal for the
portfolio.
