# ⚔️ Dramatic Foil Analysis: Shakespeare’s Romeo & Juliet — Learner

In classical drama, a **character foil** is a character whose traits, values, and actions sharply contrast with those of another character (usually the protagonist or another central figure). By placing these two characters side-by-side, the playwright highlights, illuminates, and magnifies the specific flaws, virtues, and motivations of both.

In William Shakespeare's *The Tragedy of Romeo and Juliet*, the clash between opposing personalities drives the tragedy forward just as much as the ancient feud between the Capulets and Montagues.

## ✅ Learner’s Close-Reading Checklist
- [ ] Direct quotes integrated with proper lead-ins (no "quote bombs").
- [ ] Quotes cited with Act, Scene, and Line numbers (e.g., `Rom. 1.1.68-70`).
- [ ] Analyzed specific words (diction) rather than merely summarizing the plot.
- [ ] Used active literary present tense (e.g., *"Shakespeare portrays"*, *"Tybalt demands"*, not *"portrayed"* or *"demanded"*).
- [ ] Reviewed for semicolons and sentence variety.

## Text Selection
Educators should preview mature themes, violence, racism, grief, and suicide in literary selections, use age-appropriate adaptations where needed, and offer another text addressing the same skill. Biblical devotional study is optional; historical analysis of religious allusions does not require endorsing a belief.
