# Lesson 05 — Rational Coefficients

**Goal (student-readable):** I can solve equations with fraction and
decimal coefficients and choose an efficient first move.
**Time:** ~45 minutes · **Materials:** notebook, pencil.

## Readiness check

Solve `(2/3)x = 6` and `0.4x = 2.4`.
(Answers: `x = 9`; `x = 6`.) Dividing by a fraction means multiplying
by its reciprocal; dividing by a decimal is easier after scaling.

## Teaching

"Rational coefficient" just means the number in front of `x` is a
fraction or a decimal. The solving moves do not change — but the
first move can save a lot of messy arithmetic:

- **Divide first when the equation is already distributed.** In
  `(1/2)(x − 6) = 8`, dividing both sides by `1/2` (multiplying by 2)
  at once gives `x − 6 = 16`, so `x = 22`. Distributing first also
  works but creates fractions to carry.
- **Clear denominators when fractions appear on both sides.** In
  `(3/4)x − 1/2 = (1/4)x + 3`, multiplying every term by 4 gives
  `3x − 2 = x + 12`, so `x = 7`. Clearing works only if you multiply
  *every* term — a missed term breaks equality.
- **With decimals, divide or multiply to reach whole numbers** when
  that is cleaner: `0.4x + 1.2 = 3.6` gives `0.4x = 2.4`, so `x = 6`.

No matter which first move you choose, the substitution check at the
end must use the *original* equation, not the cleared one — otherwise
a clearing error hides.

### Worked example 1

**Solve `(2/3)x + 4 = 10`.**

Subtract 4: `(2/3)x = 6`.
Multiply both sides by the reciprocal `3/2`: `x = 6 × (3/2) = 9`.
Check: `(2/3)(9) + 4 = 6 + 4 = 10`. ✓

### Worked example 2

**Solve `(3/4)x − (1/2) = (1/4)x + 3` by clearing denominators.**

Multiply every term by 4: `3x − 2 = x + 12`.
Subtract `x`: `2x − 2 = 12`. Add 2: `2x = 14`. Divide: `x = 7`.
Check in the original: left `(3/4)(7) − 1/2 = 21/4 − 2/4 = 19/4`;
right `(1/4)(7) + 3 = 7/4 + 12/4 = 19/4`. ✓

## Guided practice

Solve each equation. State your first move and why you chose it,
then check in the original equation.

1. `(1/2)x + 5 = 11`
2. `0.5x − 3 = 9`
3. `(1/3)(x + 9) = 10`
4. `(2/5)x − 1 = (1/5)x + 4`
5. Explain why clearing denominators requires multiplying every term,
   using item 4 as your example: what goes wrong if the `−1` is not
   multiplied?

## Independent practice

Solve and check each equation in the original. Give exact answers.

1. `(3/4)x + 2 = 11`
2. `0.25x + 1.5 = 4`
3. `(2/3)x − 4 = 8`
4. `1.2x − 0.6 = 5.4`
5. `(1/2)(2x − 8) = 12`
6. `(5/6)x + (1/3) = (1/6)x + 2`
7. `0.8x + 1.4 = 0.2x + 5`
8. **Explain your reasoning:** Two learners solved `(1/4)x = 5`.
   Ana multiplied both sides by 4 and got `x = 20`. Ben divided
   both sides by `1/4` and got `x = 20`. Are both methods valid?
   What does dividing by `1/4` mean as multiplication?

## Application

A recipe calls for `(3/4)` cup of sugar per batch plus a fixed
`(1/2)` cup for a glaze. In total, a baker uses `3(1/2)` cups of
sugar. How many batches did the baker make? Write the equation with
fractions, solve it, and check.

## Exit check

Solve `(4/5)x − 3 = 9` and check your answer in the original equation.

## Supports, alternate modes, extension

- **Language access:** "Rational" here means "a fraction or decimal
  number" — it does not mean "sensible." Keep a fraction-reciprocal
  table (`1/2 ↔ 2`, `2/3 ↔ 3/2`, `3/4 ↔ 4/3`) visible.
- **Alternate response mode:** Solve with a calculator for the
  arithmetic only, after writing each algebraic move by hand; or
  dictate moves while the adult scribes.
- **Extension:** Solve `(2/3)(x − 6) + (1/2)x = 11`. (Distribute or
  clear denominators — either path must reach the same answer.)

## Teacher note

Two traps dominate this lesson: (1) clearing denominators but
missing the constant term, and (2) checking the answer in the cleared
equation instead of the original. Require the check line to copy the
original equation. If fraction multiplication is the barrier rather
than the algebra, pause for a fraction-skills session; do not let
arithmetic hide the equation reasoning. Answer-key reference:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-05).
