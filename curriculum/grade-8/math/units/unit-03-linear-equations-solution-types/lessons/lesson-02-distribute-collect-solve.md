# Lesson 02 — Distribute, Collect, Solve

**Goal (student-readable):** I can solve multi-step equations by
distributing and collecting like terms, then checking my answer.
**Time:** ~45 minutes · **Materials:** notebook, pencil.

## Readiness check

Expand `3(x + 4)` and combine like terms in `5x + 2x − 7`.
(Answers: `3x + 12`; `7x − 7`.)

## Teaching

Most equations are not ready to solve on sight. Two moves get them
ready:

1. **Distribute** — remove parentheses by multiplying every term
   inside by the factor outside. `3(x + 4)` becomes `3x + 12`, not
   `3x + 4`. Forgetting to multiply the second term is the most common
   error in this unit.
2. **Collect like terms** — combine terms with the same variable part
   on each side. `5x + 2x − 7` becomes `7x − 7`.

After those moves, the equation is usually a two-step equation:
undo the addition/subtraction, then undo the multiplication/division.
Every move must keep the scale balanced: do the same thing to both
sides. Finish with a substitution check — it catches distribution
slips immediately.

A useful order of work: **distribute → collect → move variable terms
together → move constants → divide**. You do not have to follow it
rigidly, but if you are stuck, start at the top.

### Worked example 1

**Solve `3(x + 4) = 21`.**

Distribute: `3x + 12 = 21`.
Subtract 12 from both sides: `3x = 9`.
Divide both sides by 3: `x = 3`.
Check: `3(3 + 4) = 3(7) = 21`. ✓

### Worked example 2

**Solve `2(3x − 1) + 4x = 26`.**

Distribute: `6x − 2 + 4x = 26`. (Note: `2 × (−1) = −2`.)
Collect like terms on the left: `10x − 2 = 26`.
Add 2 to both sides: `10x = 28`.
Divide by 10: `x = 28/10 = 14/5 = 2.8`.
Check: `2(3(2.8) − 1) + 4(2.8) = 2(7.4) + 11.2 = 14.8 + 11.2 = 26`. ✓
A fractional or decimal answer is fine — the check is what proves it.

## Guided practice

Solve each equation. Show every move, then check by substitution.

1. `4(x − 2) = 20`
2. `5x + 3x − 4 = 28`
3. `2(5x + 3) = 34`
4. `7x − 2x + 9 = 34`
5. Explain why `3(x + 2) = 3x + 6` is not a finished solution step
   but a simplification step. What still needs to happen before you
   can read off `x`?

## Independent practice

Solve and check each equation. Give exact answers (fractions in
lowest terms where needed).

1. `6(x + 3) = 42`
2. `3x + 5x − 6 = 26`
3. `5(2x − 3) = 35`
4. `4x + 9 − x = 30`
5. `2(x + 7) + 3x = 47`
6. `8 − 2(x − 1) = 2` (Watch the sign when distributing `−2`.)
7. `3(4x − 5) − 2x = 43`
8. **Explain your reasoning:** Two learners solved `4(x + 3) = 28`.
   Ana divided both sides by 4 first: `x + 3 = 7`, so `x = 4`.
   Ben distributed first: `4x + 12 = 28`, so `4x = 16`, so `x = 4`.
   Who is correct? Is one method better? Explain.

## Application

A rectangular garden has length `2x + 3` meters and width `x` meters.
Its perimeter is 54 meters. The perimeter formula gives
`2(x + (2x + 3)) = 54`. Simplify inside the parentheses first, then
solve for `x`, and give the garden's length and width in meters.

## Exit check

Solve `5(x − 4) = 25` and check your answer by substitution.

## Supports, alternate modes, extension

- **Language access:** Keep a "distribute means multiply EVERY term
  inside" poster visible. Draw arrows from the outside factor to each
  inside term on the first two problems.
- **Alternate response mode:** Use color: one color for the
  distributed terms, another for collected terms. Oral step-naming
  ("distribute, collect, subtract, divide") counts as showing work
  when the adult scribes.
- **Extension:** Solve `3(2x + 1) − 4(x − 2) = 23`. (Distribute both
  groups, collect, and solve.)

## Teacher note

The `−2(x − 1)` item in independent practice is the deliberate
sign trap: `−2x + 2`, not `−2x − 2`. If the learner distributes
negatives unreliably, spend a session on "multiply every term,
including its sign" before moving on. Answer-key reference:
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#lesson-02).
