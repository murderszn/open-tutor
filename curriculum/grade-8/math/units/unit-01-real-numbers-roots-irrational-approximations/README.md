# Unit 01 — Real Numbers, Roots, and Irrational Approximations

**Grade:** 8 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #38](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/38), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Every point on the number line is a real number — but not every real number
can be written as a fraction. In this unit the learner discovers the two
families of real numbers (rational and irrational), learns to convert
repeating decimals to fractions, evaluates square and cube roots, solves
root equations, and pins irrationals down between rational approximations.

## Essential question

How can we describe every point on the number line — and what do we do when
a number's decimal expansion never ends and never repeats?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the grade-7 track:

- Fraction ↔ terminating-decimal conversion (1/4 = 0.25, 3/5 = 0.6)
- Integer operations with all sign combinations; order of operations
- Evaluating whole-number powers (5² = 25, 2³ = 8, 10³ = 1,000)
- Area of a square and volume of a cube from side/edge length
- Plotting rational numbers on a number line; long division to 4–6 places
- Using a calculator for arithmetic checks (not as a substitute for reasoning)

No new equation-solving instruction is required before this unit; root
equations are taught here.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Classify a real number as rational or irrational from its decimal
   expansion; explain that every number has a decimal expansion and that a
   rational number's expansion terminates or eventually repeats.
2. Convert an eventually-repeating decimal (one- and two-digit repetends,
   including delayed repetends like 0.8333…) to a fraction in simplest form.
3. Evaluate square roots of perfect squares to 225 and cube roots of perfect
   cubes to 1000; solve equations of the form x² = p (listing all real
   solutions) and x³ = p.
4. State that √2 is irrational and outline the even/odd contradiction
   argument for why it cannot be written as a fraction.
5. Bracket an irrational (√2, √3, √5, π) between consecutive tenths by
   squaring; place mixed rational/irrational values on a number line; estimate
   expression values such as π² or 2√3.

## Materials

Graph paper, ruler, pencil, calculator (for checking, not for first
reasoning), scissors and string (project), tape measure, three round
household objects (plate, jar lid, can), three square objects or cardboard
squares, the deterministic number-line diagram
[`assets/number-line-real-numbers.svg`](assets/number-line-real-numbers.svg),
the generated illustration
[`assets/investigation-station.png`](assets/investigation-station.png)
(project launch). No sharp tools beyond scissors; adult supervises measuring.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts written, oral, or drawn responses; the adult scribes
when writing stamina is the barrier rather than the math. Number lines are
also built with string and clothespins as a tactile alternative to the SVG.
Each lesson includes a language-access support, an alternate response mode,
and an extension. The generated illustration ships with a text-only
alternative naming every item the project needs.
