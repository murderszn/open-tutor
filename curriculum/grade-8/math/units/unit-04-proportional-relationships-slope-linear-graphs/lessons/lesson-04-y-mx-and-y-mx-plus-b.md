# Lesson 04 — `y = mx` and `y = mx + b`

**Goal (student-readable):** I can write the equation of a line as
`y = mx` or `y = mx + b` from a graph, a table, or a description,
and name its slope and intercept.
**Time:** ~45 minutes · **Materials:** notebook, pencil, ruler,
graph paper.

## Readiness check

A line has slope 3 and passes through (0, 4). What is `y` when
`x = 2`? (Answer: start at 4, climb 3 per step for 2 steps:
4 + 6 = 10.) If that feels shaky, practise "start value plus rate
times steps" with slope 2 from a start of 5.

## Teaching

Every non-vertical line has an equation in one of two forms:

- Through the origin: `y = mx` — slope `m`, no starting value
  (or, if you like, starting value 0). This is exactly the
  proportional equation `y = kx` from Lessons 01–02, with the unit
  rate renamed `m` for "slope."
- Anywhere else: `y = mx + b` — slope `m`, and `b` is the
  **y-intercept**: the value of `y` when `x = 0`, the point where
  the line crosses the vertical axis. In a story, `b` is the
  starting value: what you already have before the steady change
  begins.

Deriving the equation is two steps, always in this order:

1. **Find `m`.** From two points, slope = rise/run (Lesson 03).
   From a table, `m` is the change in `y` for a change of 1 in `x`.
   From a description, `m` is the "per" number — per hour, per
   mile, per month.
2. **Find `b`.** Read `y` when `x = 0` — from the graph's axis
   crossing, from the table's `x = 0` row, or from the story's
   starting amount. If no `x = 0` row is given, use a known point:
   put its `x` and `y` into `y = mx + b` and solve for `b`
   (Unit 03 equation solving, one step).

Then check: put a second known point into your equation. If it
fails, `m` or `b` is wrong — find which, don't just move on.

Proportional or not? Look at `b`. If `b = 0`, the line passes
through the origin and the relationship is proportional. If
`b ≠ 0`, it is linear but **not** proportional — the zero test
from Lesson 01, now readable straight from the equation.

### Worked example 1

**A graph shows a line through (0, 4) and (3, 13). Write its
equation.**

Step 1, slope: rise = 13 − 4 = 9, run = 3 − 0 = 3, so
`m = 9/3 = 3`.

Step 2, intercept: the point (0, 4) *is* the axis crossing, so
`b = 4`.

Equation: `y = 3x + 4`. Check with the other point: `3 × 3 + 4 =
13` ✓.

Through the origin for contrast: a line through (0, 0) and (2, 10)
has slope 10/2 = 5 and intercept 0, so `y = 5x` — proportional, as
expected.

### Worked example 2

**A table shows `x`: 0, 1, 2, 3 and `y`: 7, 10, 13, 16. Write the
equation and predict `y` when `x = 6`.**

Step 1, slope: each step of 1 in `x` raises `y` by 3, so `m = 3`.
(Check with two points: (16 − 7)/(3 − 0) = 9/3 = 3 ✓.)

Step 2, intercept: the `x = 0` row gives `y = 7`, so `b = 7`.

Equation: `y = 3x + 7`. Prediction: `y = 3 × 6 + 7 = 25`.
Reasonableness check: the table climbs 3 per step, so from
`x = 3` (16) three more steps add 9 → 25 ✓. Two routes, same
answer.

**From a description.** A gym charges a $10 joining fee plus $15
per month. The "per" number is the slope: `m = 15`. The starting
amount is the intercept: `b = 10`. Equation: `y = 15x + 10`, where
`x` is months and `y` is total dollars. For 6 months:
`15 × 6 + 10 = $100`. Note the order in the story (fee first) is
the reverse of the order in the equation (slope first) — the
equation always leads with the rate.

## Guided practice

Work these with the adult; always check with a second point.

1. Line through (0, 2) and (4, 14). Find `m`, `b`, and the
   equation. Is the relationship proportional? Why?
2. Line through (0, 0) and (3, 18). Find the equation. What does
   `m` mean here?
3. Table: `x`: 0, 1, 2, 4; `y`: 5, 8, 11, 17. Find `m` and `b`,
   write the equation, and predict `y` when `x = 10`.
4. Table: `x`: 1, 2, 3; `y`: 9, 13, 17. No `x = 0` row is given.
   Find `m`, then use the point (1, 9) to find `b`, and write
   the equation.
5. A taxi charges $2 pickup plus $3 per mile. Write the equation
   (total cost in terms of miles) and find the cost of a 9-mile
   ride. Proportional or not? Which number in the equation tells
   you?
6. Explain why `y = 4x + 0` and `y = 4x` are the same line, and why
   that means every proportional relationship is also a
   `y = mx + b` line with `b = 0`.

## Independent practice

1. Line through (0, 6) and (2, 16). Write the equation and check
   it with a third point you invent on the line.
2. Line through (0, 0) and (5, 45). Write the equation. What is
   the unit rate?
3. Table: `x`: 0, 2, 4; `y`: 3, 9, 15. Write the equation and find
   `y` when `x = 7`.
4. A car rental charges $25 per day plus a $40 one-time fee. Write
   the equation (total cost in terms of days) and find the cost of
   a 4-day rental.
5. A line has slope −2 and intercept 9. Write its equation and
   find `y` when `x = 4`. Does the line rise or fall? How do you
   know before graphing?
6. **Error analysis.** A learner writes the equation of the line
   through (0, 5) and (3, 14) as `y = 14x + 5`, "because 14 is the
   other `y`-value." Find `m` correctly, write the right equation,
   and explain the mistake in one sentence.

## Application

A streaming service offers two plans. Plan S: $8 per month, no
setup fee. Plan T: $5 per month plus a $18 setup fee. Write both
equations (total cost in terms of months). For 3 months, which
costs more, and by how much? For 12 months? Use tables, not
algebraic system solving — Unit 05 will solve crossings that way.
Say in one sentence what each plan's `b` means for a new customer.

## Exit check

Write the equation of the line with slope 6 and `y`-intercept −2,
and state whether the relationship is proportional, with the reason
(look at `b`).

## Supports and extension

- **Language access:** "intercept" = "where it intercepts (meets)
  the vertical axis"; "starting value" is the same idea in a story.
- **Alternate response mode:** give the equation as a sentence
  ("start at 7, add 3 each step") before writing symbols; the
  sentence and the symbols must match.
- **Extension:** a line passes through (2, 11) and (5, 23) and no
  `x = 0` point is given. Find `m`, then find `b` by working
  backwards from (2, 11), and write the equation.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: taking the second point's `y` as the slope
(Independent practice item 6), swapping `m` and `b`, and reading a
table's first row as `b` when `x` does not start at 0 (Guided
practice item 4). Insist on the two-step order — slope first,
intercept second — and on the second-point check. Answer-key
reference: [answer key, Lesson 04](../answer-key.md#lesson-04).
