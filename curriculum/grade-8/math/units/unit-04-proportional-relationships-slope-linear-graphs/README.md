# Unit 04 — Proportional Relationships, Slope, and Linear Graphs

**Grade:** 8 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #38](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/38), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A straight line on a graph is a story about a steady rate. Sometimes
the story starts at zero — every hour adds the same distance, every
cup adds the same price — and the line passes through the origin.
Sometimes it starts somewhere else: a joining fee, a tank already
partly full, a rider with a head start. In this unit the learner
learns to read both kinds of story from tables, equations, and
graphs; to compute slope and explain, with similar triangles, why it
never changes along a straight line; to write `y = mx` and
`y = mx + b`; and to say what the slope and the intercept mean in
the real situation — not just what numbers they are. The unit ends
with the midyear review, pulling Units 01–04 together: real numbers,
exponents, one-variable equations, and now lines.

## Essential question

How can one number — the slope — describe a whole steady relationship,
and what changes when the line does not start at zero?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks, grade 7, and Units 01–03:

- Ratios and unit rates; deciding whether two quantities stay in the
  same ratio (grade-7 proportional reasoning, re-taught in Lesson 01)
- Plotting ordered pairs; reading tables and graphs
- Solving one-variable linear equations and checking by substitution
  (Unit 03); the distributive property and collecting like terms
- Integer, fraction, and decimal arithmetic; simplifying fractions
- Similar triangles and the idea that corresponding sides of similar
  triangles are in proportion (used in Lesson 03; the unit proves
  only what it needs, with a picture)

No function language is required before this unit; `y = mx + b` is
introduced here as an equation of a line, and Unit 06 will develop
the function view. Solving two-equation systems algebraically is
**not** part of this unit: where two lines cross, the learner reads
the crossing point from a table or a graph (Unit 05 will solve it
algebraically).

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Decide whether a relationship is proportional from a table,
   equation, or graph; find its unit rate (constant of
   proportionality) and write its equation as `y = kx`.
2. Graph proportional relationships; interpret the unit rate as the
   slope of the graph; compare two proportional relationships given
   in different representations (table vs. equation vs. graph) and
   say which is greater, with numbers.
3. Compute the slope of a non-vertical line from any two of its
   points, and explain with similar triangles why the slope is the
   same between any two distinct points on the line.
4. Derive and write `y = mx` for a line through the origin and
   `y = mx + b` for a line intercepting the vertical axis at `b`,
   from a graph, a table, or a description; identify the slope and
   the intercept in each form.
5. Interpret the slope (rate of change) and the `y`-intercept
   (starting value) in context — distance-time, draining-tank, and
   pricing-plan situations — and use an equation, table, or graph to
   predict values and judge whether an answer is reasonable.
6. Audit linear models: distinguish proportional from
   non-proportional linear relationships, explain what a
   graph/equation leaves out, and use Units 01–04 skills together in
   the midyear review (objectives 1–7 of the track).

## Accessibility

Every check accepts written, oral, or drawn responses; the adult
scribes when writing stamina is the barrier rather than the math.
Each lesson includes a language-access support, an alternate response
mode, and an extension. Both visuals ship with text-only alternatives
carrying every number the questions need. Slope may be found by
counting grid steps, by a table of differences, or by the formula —
the unit accepts any correct route and asks the learner to show a
second route as a check. The "steeper means faster/more" chant
("rise over run, same all along the line") is taught for oral
rehearsal.
