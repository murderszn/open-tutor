# Unit 05 — Systems of Linear Equations

**Grade:** 8 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 19–22
**Backlog:** [issue #38](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/38), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

One equation asks one question. Two equations, asked together, ask
a harder one: *is there a pair of numbers that makes both true at
the same time?* In this unit the learner tests ordered pairs, graphs
pairs of lines and reads their intersection, solves systems by
substitution and by elimination, recognizes the simple cases by
inspection, and uses systems to settle real questions — when two
plans cost the same, how many tickets of each kind were sold, when
one cyclist catches another. The three solution cases from Unit 03
(one, none, infinitely many) return here with a picture: one
intersection, parallel lines, or a single shared line.

## Essential question

When two conditions must hold at once, how do we find the pair that
satisfies both — and what does it mean when there is no such pair,
or when every pair works?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks, grade 7, and Units 01–04:

- Adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing integers, fractions,
  and decimals; simplifying fractions (Units 01–02)
- Solving multi-step linear equations in one variable, including
  variables on both sides and rational coefficients (Unit 03)
- Classifying a one-variable equation as one / none / infinitely
  many solutions by its final form `x = a`, `a = a`, or `a = b`
  (Unit 03)
- Graphing linear equations in slope-intercept form, reading slope
  and intercept from a graph and an equation, and interpreting slope
  as a rate of change (Unit 04)
- Writing a linear equation from a table, a graph, or a description
  (Unit 04)

No function language is used here; that arrives in Unit 06.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Test whether an ordered pair is a solution of a system of two
   linear equations by substituting it into both equations, and
   explain why both checks are required.
2. Solve a system by graphing, read the intersection as the
   solution, and estimate a solution from a graph, then verify the
   estimate by substitution.
3. Solve systems of two linear equations in two variables
   algebraically by substitution, showing every step and checking
   the result in both original equations.
4. Solve systems algebraically by elimination (adding, subtracting,
   or multiplying an equation first), showing every step and
   checking the result in both original equations.
5. Classify a system as having one solution, no solution, or
   infinitely many solutions — by inspection of simple cases and by
   graph or algebra — and explain what each case means for the
   lines and for the situation.
6. Write a system of two linear equations from a real-world
   description, solve it by an efficient method, and interpret the
   solution (including a no-solution or infinitely-many-solutions
   result) in the context, with units.

## Accessibility

Every check accepts written, oral, or drawn responses; the adult
scribes when writing stamina is the barrier rather than the math.
Each lesson includes a language-access support, an alternate response
mode, and an extension. The system-graph diagram and the
two-food-trucks illustration both ship with text-only alternatives
carrying every number the questions need. Graphing may be done on
graph paper, with a straightedge, or with Desmos as a checking tool;
the classification rules (parallel lines → no solution; same line →
infinitely many solutions; crossing lines → one solution) are also
taught as a chantable three-line check for oral rehearsal.
