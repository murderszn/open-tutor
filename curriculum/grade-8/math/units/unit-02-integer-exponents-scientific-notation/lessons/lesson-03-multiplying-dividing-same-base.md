# Lesson 03 — Multiplying and Dividing with the Same Base

**Student goal:** I can multiply and divide powers with the same base by
adding or subtracting exponents, and I can show why the rule works by
expansion.
**Time:** ~45 minutes · **Unit:** [U02](../README.md) · **Session 5 of 16**

## Materials

- Notebook, pencil, calculator (for checking only)

## Readiness check (5 minutes)

Evaluate 10³ × 10² two ways: by writing both as decimals and multiplying,
and by counting zeros. (Expected: 1,000 × 100 = 100,000 = 10⁵.) If the
zero-count shortcut is shaky, re-chant the Lesson 01 ladder.

## Teaching

**The product rule.** Multiply 2³ × 2⁴. Expand both:

2³ × 2⁴ = (2 × 2 × 2) × (2 × 2 × 2 × 2) = 2⁷

Seven 2s are being multiplied. The exponents *added*: 3 + 4 = 7.

> **Product rule:** aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ (same base a).

The rule works because multiplication is just "put the factor chains
end to end and count the total." It holds for negative exponents too:
2³ × 2⁻⁵ = 2⁻² = 1/4. Check by expansion if you doubt it: 2³ = 8,
2⁻⁵ = 1/32, and 8/32 = 1/4. ✓

**The quotient rule.** Divide 5⁴ ÷ 5². Expand:

5⁴ ÷ 5² = (5 × 5 × 5 × 5) ÷ (5 × 5) = 5 × 5 = 5²

Two pairs canceled, two 5s survived. The exponents *subtracted*: 4 − 2 = 2.

> **Quotient rule:** aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ (same base a, a ≠ 0).

Try a case where the answer has a negative exponent: 7² ÷ 7⁵.
Subtracting: 7²⁻⁵ = 7⁻³. Expanding: (7×7) ÷ (7×7×7×7×7) = 1/7³ = 1/343.
And 7⁻³ = 1/7³. Both paths agree — the rules and the negative-exponent
definition are consistent.

**The same-base guard:** these rules need the *same base*. 2³ × 3²
cannot combine into one power — the chains have different links.

**Key vocabulary:** *product rule* (add exponents), *quotient rule*
(subtract exponents), *expansion* (writing out the factor chain to verify).

## Worked example 1 — product rule with a negative exponent

Simplify 3² × 3⁻⁵.

Same base, multiplication → add exponents: 2 + (−5) = −3. Result: 3⁻³.
Write with a positive exponent: 1/3³ = 1/27. (This is the exact example
in the 8.EE.A.1 standard description.)

**Reasoning to name aloud:** "Adding a negative exponent is the same as
subtracting its positive version: 2 − 5 = −3. The final flip to a
positive exponent is presentation, not a new rule."

## Worked example 2 — quotient rule crossing zero

Simplify 10⁷ ÷ 10⁷, then simplify 10⁷ ÷ 10¹⁰.

First: 7 − 7 = 0 → 10⁰ = 1. (A number divided by itself is 1 — the rule
agrees with arithmetic.) Second: 7 − 10 = −3 → 10⁻³ = 1/1,000 = 0.001.

**Reasoning to name aloud:** "The quotient rule re-derives the zero and
negative exponent rules. All three rules are one family, not three
separate facts."

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

1. 4² × 4³. *(Hint: "Same base, multiplying — add the exponents.")*
2. 9⁵ ÷ 9³. *(Hint: "Same base, dividing — subtract.")*
3. 6⁴ × 6⁻⁶. *(Hint: "Add: 4 + (−6). Then flip to a positive exponent.")*
4. Error analysis: "Leo simplifies 2³ × 5³ as 10⁶." What is wrong?
   *(Hint: "What did the expansion of 2³ × 5³ actually look like? Are
   the bases the same?")*
5. Simplify 10⁻² × 10⁻⁴, then write as a decimal.
6. Simplify (2⁵ × 2⁻³) ÷ 2⁻¹. *(Hint: "Work inside the parentheses
   first, then divide.")*

## Independent practice

1. 7³ × 7 × 7² — simplify. (Note the lone 7 = 7¹.)
2. x⁵ ÷ x⁸ — write two equivalent forms.
3. (3⁻² × 3⁴) ÷ 3⁻⁵ — simplify to a whole number.
4. Error analysis: "Ana simplifies 5³ ÷ 5³ as 5⁰ = 0." Fix both steps.
5. Write 10⁶ ÷ 10⁻³ two ways (as a power of ten, then as a decimal), and
   verify by expansion.

*Alternate response mode:* narrate each step aloud ("same base, times,
add the twos and threes…") while the adult writes the symbols.

## Application

A computer stores about 10¹² bytes. A photo is about 10⁶ bytes.
Write (storage ÷ photo size) as a power of ten and say how many photos
fit. (Expected: 10¹² ÷ 10⁶ = 10⁶ — one million photos.)

## Exit check

"Simplify 4⁻² × 4⁵ two ways — first with the product rule, then by
writing both as fractions and multiplying. Do they agree?" Expected:
4³ = 64; (1/16) × 1,024 = 64. Record: secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** anchor phrases — "multiply: add the little
  numbers; divide: subtract the little numbers." Always say *which* rule
  fires and why (same base, operation).
- **Alternate response mode:** oral narration of each transformation
  with the adult scribing the algebra.
- **Calculator boundary:** calculator checks the final decimal only;
  expansions and exponent arithmetic are done by hand.

## Extension

Prove the product rule in full generality: aᵐ × aⁿ has m + n factors of
a. Then investigate: does (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ? Test with numbers and try to
expand one case — bring your evidence to Lesson 04.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) Adding exponents when bases differ.
Expansion is the cure. (2) Multiplying bases and adding exponents
simultaneously (2³ × 2⁴ = 4⁷). (3) Subtracting in the wrong order in the
quotient rule (computing n − m). (4) Thinking 2⁻³ × 2⁻⁴ = 2¹² — sign
errors when adding negatives.
