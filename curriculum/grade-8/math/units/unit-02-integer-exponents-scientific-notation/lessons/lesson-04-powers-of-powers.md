# Lesson 04 — Powers of Powers and Mixed Expressions

**Student goal:** I can raise a power to a power by multiplying
exponents, and simplify expressions that mix all the exponent rules.
**Time:** ~45 minutes · **Unit:** [U02](../README.md) · **Session 7 of 16**

## Materials

- Notebook, pencil, calculator (for checking only)
- Yesterday's extension evidence about (aᵐ)ⁿ

## Readiness check (5 minutes)

Simplify (2³)² two ways: expand it fully, and guess a shortcut from the
pattern. (Expected: 2³ × 2³ = 2⁶ = 64.) If expansion is slow, re-expand
(10²)³ = 10⁶ before continuing.

## Teaching

**(2³)²** means "2³, used as a factor twice": (2 × 2 × 2) × (2 × 2 × 2).
Count the 2s: six. The exponents *multiplied*: 3 × 2 = 6.

> **Power-of-a-power rule:** (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ.

Think of the inner exponent as "how big each bundle is" and the outer
exponent as "how many bundles" — the total is the product. It works with
negative exponents too: (10⁻²)³ = 10⁻⁶ = 1/1,000,000.

**Putting all rules together.** Simplify (3²)³ × 3⁻⁴.

- Step 1 (power of a power): (3²)³ = 3⁶.
- Step 2 (product rule): 3⁶ × 3⁻⁴ = 3⁶⁺⁽⁻⁴⁾ = 3².
- Step 3 (present): 3² = 9.

One more, with a quotient inside: simplify (5⁻¹)² ÷ 5⁻³.

- Step 1: (5⁻¹)² = 5⁻².
- Step 2: 5⁻² ÷ 5⁻³ = 5⁻²⁻⁽⁻³⁾ = 5¹ = 5.

**Order-of-rules strategy** (say it every time): (1) handle powers of
powers first (innermost parentheses), (2) combine products and quotients
of the same base, (3) flip any negative exponent last, for a clean final
form. When subtraction appears inside an exponent, keep the parentheses:
m − (−n) = m + n.

**Key vocabulary:** *power of a power* (multiply exponents), *mixed
expression* (several rules in one simplification).

## Worked example 1 — power of a power with a negative exponent

Simplify (4⁻³)².

Multiply exponents: (−3) × 2 = −6 → 4⁻⁶. Flip to a positive exponent:
1/4⁶. Evaluate: 4⁶ = 4,096, so the value is 1/4,096.

**Reasoning to name aloud:** "The outer exponent multiplies the inner
one — including its sign. Then I flip once, at the end, because an
answer with a negative exponent is unfinished business."

## Worked example 2 — three rules in one expression

Simplify (2⁵ × 2⁻²) ÷ (2⁻⁴)².

Step 1: inside the parentheses — 2⁵ × 2⁻² = 2³.
Step 2: the power of a power — (2⁻⁴)² = 2⁻⁸.
Step 3: the quotient — 2³ ÷ 2⁻⁸ = 2³⁻⁽⁻⁸⁾ = 2¹¹.
Step 4: present — 2¹¹ = 2,048.

**Reasoning to name aloud:** "I named each rule before using it: product
rule, power-of-a-power, quotient rule. Then the arithmetic 3 − (−8) = 11
is just signed-number addition — the exponent rules do the structure,
integer arithmetic does the finishing."

## Guided practice (adult stays close, hints allowed)

1. (10²)⁴. *(Hint: "How many bundles of how many 10s? Multiply the
   exponents.")*
2. (3⁻¹)³. *(Hint: "Multiply −1 by 3 first, then flip.")*
3. Simplify 5⁴ × (5²)⁻¹. *(Hint: "Power of a power first, then the
   product rule.")*
4. Error analysis: "Sam writes (2³)² = 2⁵." What did Sam do, and what
   is 2⁶ really? *(Hint: "Did Sam add or multiply the exponents? What
   does expansion say?")*
5. Simplify (10³)⁻² × 10⁷. *(Hint: "Handle the parenthesis, combine,
   then read the sign.")*
6. Simplify (7²)⁰. *(Hint: "Two rules fire — which order?")*

## Independent practice

1. (6²)³ and 6² × 6³ — simplify each; are they equal? Explain.
2. Simplify (4⁻²)⁻¹ ÷ 4³ to a whole number.
3. Simplify (10⁻⁵)² × (10³)⁴; give the answer as a power of ten and as
   a decimal.
4. Error analysis: "Riya writes (5³)² = 5³ × 5²." Rewrite her work
   correctly and name the error.
5. Construct your own mixed expression that simplifies to 10⁻², using at
   least two different exponent rules. Show every step.

## Application

A folding-paper model: start with a sheet of area 1 m². Each fold halves
the visible area. After n folds the area is (1/2)ⁿ m². Write the area
after 10 folds as a power of 2 with a negative exponent, as a fraction,
and as a decimal; then say whether 10 folds are physically possible and
why. (Expected: 2⁻¹⁰ = 1/1,024 ≈ 0.00098 m²; paper is too thick after
~7 folds — the model outruns the material.)

## Exit check

"Simplify (3⁴)⁻¹ × 3⁶, showing each rule you use." Expected:
3⁻⁴ × 3⁶ = 3² = 9. Record: secure / developing / not yet.

## Supports

- **Language access:** anchor phrase — "power of a power: multiply the
  little numbers; the outer counts the bundles." Require naming the rule
  aloud before each step.
- **Alternate response mode:** step-by-step oral narration with adult
  scribing; the learner supplies only the rule names and exponent
  arithmetic.
- **Calculator boundary:** no calculator until the final decimal of an
  already-simplified expression.

## Extension

Investigate (ab)²: expand (3 × 5)² and compare with 3² × 5². Does the
power distribute over multiplication? Test (2 × 7)³ and try a
counterexample for (a + b)² vs a² + b². Report with expansions.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconceptions:** (1) (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ — adding instead of
multiplying; expansion is the fix. (2) Sign errors: (−2)(−3) = −6.
(3) Applying the power-of-a-power rule to a sum, (a + b)² = a² + b².
(4) Forgetting the last flip — answers left with negative exponents are
correct but unfinished; require the positive form.
