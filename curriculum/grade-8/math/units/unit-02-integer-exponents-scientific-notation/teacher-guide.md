## Misconceptions and reteaching moves

- **"Negative exponent = negative answer."** Anchor phrase: "the minus
  sign moves it downstairs." Cure: evaluate 2⁻³ by repeated halving
  (1 → 1/2 → 1/4 → 1/8) — every step positive.
- **"a⁰ = 0."** Re-run the dividing-down pattern on a new base (powers
  of 5). Never let "I memorized 1" be the reason — the pattern is.
- **Adding exponents with different bases** (2³ × 5³ → 10⁶). Expansion
  cure: write both chains; nothing cancels or merges.
- **(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ.** Expansion cure with (2³)²; then contrast with
  2³ × 2².
- **Adding exponents when *adding* numbers** (5 × 10³ + 5 × 10³ →
  10 × 10⁶). Like-terms language: "match the sizes before adding the
  shapes."
- **Coefficient outside [1, 10)** (62 × 10⁻⁵). Normalize immediately,
  every time — make it a habit, not a fix-up.
- **Poster rungs as data.** If the learner quotes "Earth is 10¹² m"
  from the picture, redirect to the table: the image illustrates scale,
  the table measures it.

## Readiness criteria

Move to Unit 03 when the learner can, unprompted: (1) simplify a mixed
exponent expression naming each rule; (2) convert both directions
between decimal and scientific notation; (3) explain a magnitude
comparison with exponent subtraction. The formative quiz (8+) and the
project rubric (12+) are the evidence; the assessment (20+) confirms.

## Catch-up guidance

If Session 16 shows gaps: one extra session per missed objective —
re-teach the lesson's worked examples with new numbers, then re-assign
the parallel practice items. Do not advance to Unit 03's equation work
while exponent rules are insecure; Unit 03's solution-type reasoning
leans on them.

## Answer-key use

The [answer key](answer-key.md) is adult-only. It contains worked
solutions for every lesson item, practice, quiz, assessment, and project
