# Unit 02 — Practice and Review Sessions

Student-facing practice sessions referenced in the
[unit pacing](README.md#16-session-pacing-4-weeks--4-sessions-45-minutes-each).
Keep this file with the lessons; the [answer key](answer-key.md) holds all
solutions separately.

## Practice A — Power-of-ten detectives (Session 2)

Predict each value as a power of ten, then verify by writing the decimal.

1. 10⁵
2. 10³ × 10²
3. 10⁶ ÷ 10⁴
4. Predict: 10⁰ = ? Explain your prediction to the adult in one sentence.
5. A city has 10⁶ people. Write the population as a decimal and name the
   number in words.
6. Error analysis: "Mia says 10³ + 10² = 10⁵." Show with decimals that
   she is wrong, then explain the right way to add them.

## Review — Exponent card sort (Session 4)

On index cards, write one expression per card: 10⁴, 10³, 10², 10¹, 10⁰,
10⁻¹, 10⁻², 10⁻³, 5⁰, 2⁻¹, 4⁻², 3⁰, 10² × 10⁻¹, 10³ ÷ 10².

Round 1: order the cards from greatest to least value.
Round 2: the adult shuffles; draw a card and say its value two ways
(fraction/decimal).
Round 3: the adult reads an exit re-check aloud — "Is 5⁻² positive or
negative? Why?" — and the learner finds the matching card.
Exit re-check: write 10⁻² × 10³ two ways (power of ten, decimal).

## Practice B — Exponent rules lab (Session 6)

Work each item by expansion first, then by the rule. Both routes must
agree.

1. 2⁴ × 2² (expansion vs. product rule)
2. 5³ ÷ 5⁵ (expansion vs. quotient rule — watch the sign)
3. (3²)³ (expansion vs. power-of-a-power)
4. 7⁻¹ × 7³ — expansion is messy here; explain why the rule is the
   better tool.
5. Error analysis: "Eli writes 3² × 4² = 12⁴." Expand one small case
   (2² × 3²) to find and explain the error.
6. Construct: pick a base and exponents so that the product rule gives a
   *negative* exponent answer; show the work.

## Practice C — Expression puzzles (Session 8)

Simplify each fully; the final answer must use positive exponents.

1. (2³)² × 2⁻⁴
2. (5⁻²)³ ÷ 5⁻⁴
3. (10⁴ × 10⁻⁶) ÷ (10⁻³)²
4. (3⁻¹ × 3⁵) ÷ 3²
5. (x³)⁻² × x⁷ — write two equivalent forms
6. **Puzzle:** fill the blanks so the simplification is correct:
   (2^__ × 2⁴) ÷ 2^__ = 2³, with both blanks negative integers. Find
   *two* different solutions and prove both work.

## Practice D — Planet magnitudes (Session 10)

Use [`resources/solar_system_planets.csv`](../../../../../resources/solar_system_planets.csv).
Columns and units: `distance_from_sun_million_km` (millions of
kilometers), `equatorial_diameter_km` (kilometers),
`orbital_period_days` (days), `surface_gravity_m_s2` (m/s²),
`known_moons` (count). Values are real rounded figures (see the resource
pack for the check date and rounding note).

1. Earth's distance from the Sun is 149.6 million km. Write it in km,
   then in scientific notation (km).
2. Jupiter's diameter is 139,820 km. Write it in scientific notation.
   Earth's diameter is 12,742 km — how many times wider is Jupiter?
3. Neptune is 4,495.1 million km from the Sun; Mercury is 57.9 million
   km. Express the ratio as a power of 10 (order of magnitude), then
   more precisely.
4. Saturn has 146 known moons; Earth has 1. Write the ratio in scientific
   notation two ways (decimal first, then normalized).
5. Which planet's orbital period is closest to 10⁴ days? Show the
   comparison in scientific notation.
6. Error analysis: "A friend writes Mars's distance, 227.9 million km,
   as 2.279 × 10⁸ km." Is this correct? Explain.
