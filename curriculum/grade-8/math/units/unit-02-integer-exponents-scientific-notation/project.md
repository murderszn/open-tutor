# Project — The Powers-of-Ten Journal

**Unit:** [U02](README.md) · **Sessions 12 and 14** (~45 min each, plus
short home measurements between) · **Standards:** 8.EE.A.3, 8.EE.A.4

The learner builds a personal "Powers-of-Ten Journal": a page per power
of ten from 10⁻⁶ to 10¹², each page showing one real or estimated
measurement at that scale, written in scientific notation, with the unit
deliberately chosen to fit. The journal closes with a comparison essay
explaining how exponents make extreme scales thinkable.

## Materials

- Notebook (the journal itself), pencil, ruler, calculator for checking
- Reference: the [powers-of-ten table](practice.md#reference--powers-of-ten-table-session-10)
- Data: [`resources/solar_system_planets.csv`](../../../../../resources/solar_system_planets.csv)
  (columns and units defined in the practice session; real rounded values)
- Adult available for the home measurements

## Steps

### Part 1 — Collect and convert (Session 12)

1. **Measure three small things** at home with the ruler: the width of a
   fingernail (cm), the thickness of 10 stacked pages divided by 10 (mm),
   and the length of a grain of rice (mm). Convert each measurement to
   meters, then to scientific notation.
2. **Look up two large things** in the planets CSV: the diameter of a
   chosen planet (km) and its distance from the Sun (million km).
   Convert both to scientific notation in *meters* (1 km = 10³ m;
   1 million km = 10⁹ m).
3. **Fill the journal**: one page each for 10⁻⁶, 10⁻³, 10⁰, 10³, 10⁶,
   10⁹, 10¹². Each page needs: the power of ten, the measurement in
   scientific notation with its unit, the same value in decimal form,
   and one sentence saying why the chosen unit fits (for example,
   "millimeters keep the rice grain readable; meters would bury it in
   zeros").
4. **Unit-choice challenge**: for one measurement, write it in two
   different units (for example, the planet's diameter in km and in
   millions of km). Circle the better choice and justify it in writing.

### Part 2 — Compare and present (Session 14)

5. **Magnitude comparison**: pick the smallest and largest journal
   entries. Write both in meters in scientific notation, then compute
   how many times larger the big one is — show the exponent subtraction
   and the coefficient division separately.
6. **Mixed-form computation**: add your two middle-scale measurements
   (for example, the rice grain and the planet diameter in meters),
   showing the exponent-matching step. Then multiply your two
   smallest-scale measurements, showing normalization. Which operation
   was harder, and why?
7. **Comparison essay** (one page): answer the unit's essential
   question — how do exponents let us think about numbers too big or
   small to write out? Use at least three journal entries as evidence
   and name the standard's population example (8.EE.A.3) in your own
   words.
8. **Present**: walk the adult through the journal, reading one page
   aloud per power of ten and defending the unit choices.

## Deliverables

1. The journal: 7 pages, one per power of ten from 10⁻⁶ to 10¹², each
   with scientific notation, decimal form, unit, and unit-fit sentence.
2. The two-unit comparison for one measurement (Step 4).
3. The smallest-vs-largest magnitude comparison with shown work (Step 5).
4. The addition and multiplication with shown exponent work (Step 6).
5. The one-page comparison essay (Step 7).
6. The oral walkthrough (Step 8).

## Rubric

| Criterion | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Notation accuracy | All scientific-notation forms correct; coefficients in [1, 10); decimals convert back correctly | Most correct; one or two coefficient/exponent slips | Multiple conversion errors; coefficients outside range |
| Exponent work | Magnitude ratio shows exponent subtraction and coefficient division; addition shows exponent matching; multiplication normalized | Steps present but one rule misapplied | Work missing or rules applied at random |
| Unit choice | Every page names a fitting unit with a reason; the two-unit comparison defends the better choice | Units named; reasons thin or missing on some pages | Units arbitrary or missing |
| Essay reasoning | Answers the essential question with three journal entries as evidence; explains the 8.EE.A.3 population comparison in own words | Answers with one or two entries; population example named but not explained | Off-topic or no evidence cited |
| Presentation | Reads pages accurately; defends at least two unit choices when asked | Reads pages; struggles to defend choices | Cannot explain journal entries |

**Scoring:** 12–15 = ready for the unit assessment; 8–11 = revise the
weak criterion with the adult, then reassess; below 8 = re-teach the
flagged objective ([teacher guide](teacher-guide.md)) before Session 15.

## Safety and accessibility

No hazards: ruler and household objects only; the adult supervises the
rice-grain measuring (choking-hazard awareness for small siblings nearby).
The essay may be dictated to the adult; the journal may be drawn rather
than written where fine-motor stamina is the barrier.
