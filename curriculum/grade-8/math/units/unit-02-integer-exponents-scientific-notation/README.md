# Unit 02 — Integer Exponents and Scientific Notation

**Grade:** 8 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #38](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/38), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Ten raised to a power is a 1 followed by zeros — until it isn't. Extend the
pattern one step past 10⁰ and the exponent goes negative: 10⁻³ = 0.001.
In this unit the learner builds the complete integer-exponent toolkit
(zero and negative exponents, the product, quotient, and power-of-a-power
rules), then uses powers of 10 to write, estimate, and compute with very
large and very small quantities in scientific notation — the language of
planetary distances, cell sizes, and calculator displays.

## Essential question

How do we write, compare, and compute with numbers that are far too big
or far too small to write out — without ever losing track of how big
they are?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks, grade 7, and Unit 01:

- Evaluating whole-number powers (5² = 25, 2³ = 8, 10³ = 1,000)
- Fraction multiplication/division; equivalent fractions and unit fractions
- Decimal place value and shifting the decimal point by powers of 10
- Multiplying/dividing decimals; rounding to a stated place
- Order of operations (PEMDAS); working with signed integers
- Solving one-step equations (what operation undoes another)

No new equation-solving instruction is required before this unit.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Explain why a⁰ = 1 (a ≠ 0) and a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ using the dividing-down
   powers pattern; evaluate expressions with zero and negative integer
   exponents.
2. Multiply and divide powers with the same base by adding or subtracting
   exponents; raise a power to a power by multiplying exponents; justify
   each step by expansion where the pattern is new.
3. Simplify complex numerical expressions that combine all integer-exponent
   properties, producing an equivalent expression with positive exponents.
4. Write a number in scientific notation (single digit 1–9 times an
   integer power of 10) and convert between scientific and decimal forms,
   both directions.
5. Estimate a very large or very small quantity as a power of 10; express
   how many times greater one quantity is than another as a ratio of
   powers of 10 (for example, the U.S. and world populations).
6. Add, subtract, multiply, and divide numbers in scientific notation;
   choose appropriately sized units for very large or very small
   measurements; interpret technology-generated notation (E notation).

## Accessibility

Every check accepts written, oral, or drawn responses; the adult scribes
when writing stamina is the barrier rather than the math. Each lesson
includes a language-access support, an alternate response mode, and an
extension. Exponent rules are also rehearsed by chanting the "pattern
down" rhythm as an oral alternative. The generated poster ships with a
text-only alternative naming every labeled rung and object, and all
measured values are given in text tables — never read off the picture.
