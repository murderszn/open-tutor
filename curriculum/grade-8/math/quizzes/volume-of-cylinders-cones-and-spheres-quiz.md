## 🔑 Parent Answer Key (Educator)

> [!NOTE]
> *This section is for Educator (educator) to verify answers, assess computational precision, and provide targeted feedback.*

**Question 1:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** Cylinder: $V = \pi r^2 h$<br>Cone: $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$<br>Sphere: $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$

**Question 2:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** In terms of $\pi$: $V = \pi (4^2)(10) = 160\pi\text{ cm}^3$. Rounded: $160 \times 3.14159 \approx 502.7\text{ cm}^3$.

**Question 3:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi (6^2)(14) = \frac{1}{3}\pi (36)(14) = 168\pi \approx 527.79\text{ in}^3$.

**Question 4:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi (4.7)^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi (103.823) \approx 138.43\pi \approx 434.9\text{ in}^3$.

**Question 5:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** The volume of the cone is exactly one-third ($\frac{1}{3}$) the volume of the cylinder.

**Question 6:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** $450\pi = \pi r^2 (18) \implies 18r^2 = 450 \implies r^2 = 25 \implies r = 5\text{ cm}$.

**Question 7:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** $100\pi = \frac{1}{3}\pi (5^2) h \implies 100 = \frac{25}{3}h \implies h = 100 \times \frac{3}{25} = 12\text{ feet}$.

**Question 8:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** Radius $r = 12 / 2 = 6\text{ cm}$. $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi (6^3) = \frac{4}{3}\pi (216) = 288\pi\text{ cm}^3$.

**Question 9:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** $V = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{4}{3}\pi (9^3)\right) = \frac{2}{3}\pi (729) = 486\pi\text{ cm}^3 \approx 1{,}526.8\text{ cm}^3$.

**Question 10:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** Volume scales with $r^2$. Since $(2r)^2 = 4r^2$, the volume increases by a factor of 4.

**Question 11:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** Cylinder: $\pi (10^2)(30) = 3000\pi$. Cone: $\frac{1}{3}\pi (10^2)(12) = 400\pi$. Total Volume $= 3000\pi + 400\pi = 3400\pi\text{ ft}^3$.

**Question 12:**  
- **Target Answer & Key Steps:** Both have radius $r = 3\text{ cm}$. Cone volume $= \frac{1}{3}\pi (3^2)(10) = 30\pi\text{ cm}^3$. Sphere volume $= \frac{4}{3}\pi (3^3) = 36\pi\text{ cm}^3$. Since $36\pi > 30\pi$, yes, it will overflow by $6\pi\text{ cm}^3$ ($\\approx 18.8\text{ cm}^3$).
