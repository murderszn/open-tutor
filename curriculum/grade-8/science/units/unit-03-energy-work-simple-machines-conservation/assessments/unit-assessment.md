# Unit Assessment — Unit 03: Energy, Work, Simple Machines, and Conservation

**Unit 03** · 50 minutes · closed notes except the formula card
($W = Fd$, $KE = \tfrac12 mv^2$, $PE = mgh$, $g = 9.8$ m/s², MA =
output ÷ input, efficiency = out ÷ in × 100%) · **Answer key:**
[answer key](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment)

*The adult scores the same day. 28 of 40 = on track; below 28, use
the re-teach plan in the [teacher guide](../teacher-guide.md)
before Unit 04.*

1. (3 points) A worker pushes a crate with 55 N for 8 m in the push
   direction. (a) Work done? (b) The worker then holds the crate
   still for 30 seconds. Work done during the hold? (c) Explain
   the difference in one sentence.
2. (3 points) A 5 kg object moves at 6 m/s. (a) Kinetic energy?
   (b) If its mass doubles at the same speed, kinetic energy?
   (c) If instead its speed doubles at the same mass, kinetic
   energy?
3. (3 points) **Graph interpretation (fictional practice dataset):**
   At 4 m/s, masses of 1, 2, 3, 4 kg have kinetic energies of 8, 16,
   24, 32 J. For a 2 kg object, speeds of 1, 2, 3, 4 m/s give 1, 4, 9,
   16 J. (a) Which relationship is a straight line? (b) Which is
   a curve? (c) A learner predicts a 2 kg object at 8 m/s has
   32 J by doubling the 4 m/s value. Is that right? Give the
   correct value with working.
4. (3 points) A 2 kg ball is held 6 m above the floor, then dropped
   (ignore air). (a) PE at the top? (b) KE just before impact?
   (c) At 3 m above the floor, what are PE and KE?
5. (3 points) Two setups: a 3 kg object at 4 m and a 6 kg object at
   1.5 m, both above the same floor. (a) PE of each? (b) Which
   stores more? (c) What does this show about mass vs. height?
6. (3 points) Write the energy chain for a battery-powered torch
   from battery to light, naming every form in order, and state
   which form most learners forget to include.
7. (3 points) A skateboarder with 300 J of total energy rides a
   half-pipe with friction. At the end of the run the skater's
   mechanical energy is 240 J. (a) How much energy became thermal
   (and sound)? (b) What is the total energy now, and why?
   (c) Why does the skater reach a lower height each run?
8. (3 points) A lever: load 360 N, load arm 0.4 m, effort arm 1.2 m.
   (a) Ideal effort force? (b) Mechanical advantage? (c) If the
   load rises 0.25 m, how far does the effort end move?
9. (3 points) A pulley system with 4 supporting ropes lifts a 480 N
   load 1.5 m. (a) Ideal effort? (b) Length of rope pulled?
   (c) Useful work out?
10. (3 points) A ramp: a 300 N load rises 2 m using a 10 m ramp with
    a measured effort of 90 N. (a) Useful work? (b) Work in?
    (c) Efficiency? (d) Why is the efficiency below 100%? (One
    sentence; note this item is scored out of 3 total, not per
    part.)
11. (4 points) **Machine choice (paragraph):** A 900 N safe must be
    raised 1 m onto a platform. You can push with at most 200 N.
    Propose a machine (lever, pulley, or ramp) that could do the
    job, give its key numbers (forces and distances), and explain
    why your choice works in force–distance terms. A correct
    paragraph names the machine, shows MA ≥ 4.5 in effect (or an
    equivalent ramp/pulley tradeoff), and states the distance cost.
12. (6 points) **Conservation argument:** A closed system's energy
    account reads — Before: KE 50 J, PE 120 J, thermal 30 J.
    After: KE 140 J, thermal 50 J. (a) What is the total before?
    (b) What must PE be after? (c) In 3–4 sentences, argue that
    this account supports conservation and explain what changed
    physically (which transformation happened, and what the
    growing thermal column means).

**Scoring:** Items 1–10: 3 points each (1 per part, with item 10
scored 3 total across its parts). Item 11: 4 points (machine named
1, numbers correct 2, distance-cost explanation 1). Item 12:
6 points (totals 2, PE after 1, argument 3). Total 40 points.
