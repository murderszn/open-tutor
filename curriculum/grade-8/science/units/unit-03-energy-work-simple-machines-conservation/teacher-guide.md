# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Energy, Work, Simple Machines, and Conservation

**Answer key:** [answer key](answer-key.md) (separate file; do not
show it to the learner)
**Unit README:** [README](README.md) (pacing, objectives, standards)

## How this unit is built

The unit's argument runs in three beats. Beat 1 (Lessons 01–03)
gives the learner three formulas and the discipline to use them:
work only counts in the force's direction, kinetic energy squares
speed but only scales with mass, potential energy needs a stated
reference. Beat 2 (Lesson 04, Sessions 8–9) makes the learner track
energy through real trials and name where it goes. Beat 3 (Lessons
05–06, Sessions 14–15, 18) turns that tracking into two verdicts:
machines trade force for distance, and totals never change. If a
learner is weak on Beat 1 arithmetic, do not push them into Beat 3
arguments — re-teach the setup cards first (see below).

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

### L1 — Work

- **Likely misconception:** "Holding/carrying is work." Response:
  acknowledge tired muscles (internal chemical energy), then apply
  the test: did the force move the object in its own direction?
  Holding: no. Level carrying: force up, motion sideways: no.
- **Watch for:** learners multiplying force by *any* distance
  (for example, the height a carried box was held at). Insist on
  "distance in the force's direction" in every setup line.
- **Reteach move:** act out three cases with a toy cart (push
  along motion; hold still; carry levelly) and have the learner
  call out "work / zero" before any numbers.

### L2 — Kinetic energy

- **Likely misconception:** doubling speed doubles KE. Response:
  compute both (same mass at 2 and 4 m/s) and let the factor of 4
  do the arguing.
- **Diagnostic:** answers consistently double the correct value
  mean the $\tfrac12$ was dropped — point at it, do not re-teach
  the whole lesson.
- **Reteach move:** the two-column setup card (square speed, halve
  mass, multiply) plus the deterministic graph: mass = straight
  line, speed = curve. Keep the graph visible during practice.

### L3 — Potential energy

- **Likely misconception:** one "true" potential energy, or
  potential energy as a property of the object alone. Response:
  the same book has different PE above the floor and above the
  table; only *changes* are reference-free.
- **Watch for:** unit slips (grams for kilograms, centimeters for
  meters) — the lesson's error-analysis item targets exactly this.
  Require units in every substitution line.
- **Reteach move:** lift the real book to two heights and compute
  both; then change the reference (floor vs. table) and recompute
  the *change*.

### L4 — Transfers and transformations

- **Likely misconception:** "lost energy." Response (every time):
  "Lost to where?" Accept only a form and a place (thermal energy
  in the brakes/track/air; sound in the air).
- **Watch for:** learners listing only the first and last forms
  and skipping the middle (for example, battery → light, skipping
  electrical). Require every link in a chain.
- **Reteach move:** energy bars with paper strips — the total
  strip is cut and re-divided, never shortened, until thermal and
  sound strips are added.

### L5 — Simple machines

- **Likely misconception:** "Machines create energy / save work."
  Response: compute work in and work out for the learner's own
  example. Work out ≤ work in, always.
- **Watch for:** MA quoted without the distance trade (a lever
  with MA 3 whose effort distance is never computed). Require
  both columns in every machine problem.
- **Reteach move:** one lever, one measurement: lift a known load
  a known height, measure effort and effort distance, and compare
  the two work numbers on the spot.

### L6 — Conservation

- **Likely misconception:** reciting "energy cannot be created or
  destroyed" while still writing "lost" for friction. Response:
  send the learner back to L4's question — the account must name
  the thermal column.
- **Key distinction to protect:** total energy conserved vs.
  useful energy shrinking. Cars need fuel because useful energy
  spreads into thermal energy, not because energy is destroyed.
- **Reteach move:** the closed-system account template (before
  total = after total) applied to the learner's own ramp-lab data
  in words: "What left the mechanical account, and where is it?"

## Session 17 re-teach plan

Score the formative quiz the same day. For each objective below
70% (fewer than 7 of 10 quiz items overall means re-teach the
unit's weakest objective first):

| Weak objective | Re-teach (30 min) | Follow-up check |
|---|---|---|
| Work / zero-work (Q1–2) | L1 acted cases + 3 new W = Fd problems | 3 of 3 correct setups |
| Kinetic energy (Q3–4) | L2 setup card + graph re-read | Mass vs. speed factor questions, 2 of 2 |
| Potential energy (Q5–6) | L3 book-lift demo + reference change | PE pair with different references, correct |
| Transfers / thermal (Q7, Q10) | L4 paper-strip accounts, pendulum demo | One full chain + one "lost to where?" |
| Machines (Q8–9) | L5 one-lever measurement + ramp numbers | MA + efficiency pair, correct |

If the whole quiz is below 50%, re-teach Beat 1 (L1–L3) across
Sessions 17 and 19 and postpone the design presentations to
Session 19's flex time; do not start Unit 04 until the learner can
compute $W$, $KE$, and $PE$ setups correctly.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** setup cards for every formula; data-table
  alternatives to every graph; timer/reader roles in the lab;
  verbal presentation alternative; calculator allowed after a
  handwritten setup.
- **Extension:** angled-force work (L1); car braking-energy
  calculation (L2); roller-coaster budget (L3); PhET friction
  investigation (L4); household machine identification (L5);
  hydroelectric account (L6). Extensions are optional and never
  prerequisites for the assessment.
- **Reading access:** pre-teach *transfer, transformation,
  system, efficiency* with the vocabulary cards before Lessons 04
  and 05; the adult reads lesson text aloud on request without
  penalty.

## Safety and materials notes

- The adult assembles the simple-machine kit and the ramp; the
  learner never climbs, lifts overhead loads, or uses mains
  electricity. Spring/rubber-band launches are aimed only at the
  marked target area.
- The 1 kg load in the design challenge is a bag the adult weighs;
  if no scale is available, the adult states the assumed weight
  (about 9.8 N for 1 kg) and the report records that assumption.
- Effort-force measurement: a spring scale is preferred. Without
  one, the adult helps estimate effort with known weights and the
  report must say so — an estimated effort is honest data; an
  invented precise value is not.

## Grading notes

- Formative quiz: 20 points; 14+ on track. Unit assessment:
  40 points; 28+ on track. Project rubric: 16 points maximum
  (4 criteria × 4); 12+ on track. Open responses are scored on
  reasoning, not on matching a single sentence — the answer key
  gives acceptable variations.
- Do not deduct for arithmetic slips when the setup and reasoning
  are correct; do deduct when a setup is missing, a unit is wrong,
  or a total does not balance without comment.
