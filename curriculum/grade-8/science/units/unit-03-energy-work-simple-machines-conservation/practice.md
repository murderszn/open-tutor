# Practice — Unit 03: Energy, Work, Simple Machines, and Conservation

**Answer key:** [answer key](answer-key.md#practice-sessions)
**Rules for every calculation:** write the formula, substitute with
units, then give the answer with units. Use $g = 9.8$ m/s² for
gravitational potential energy. A calculator is allowed once the
setup is written by hand.

## Session A — Work calculations

1. A learner pushes a cart with 30 N for 10 m in the push direction.
   Work done?
2. A motor lifts a 75 N load 2 m straight up. Work done?
3. A learner holds a 60 N box still at chest height for 20 seconds.
   Physics work on the box? Explain in one sentence.
4. A learner carries a 40 N box across a level room. (a) While
   walking levelly, how much physics work does the carrying force
   do on the box? (b) Why does the learner still get tired?
5. A force of 90 N pushes a crate 0 m (the crate is stuck against a
   wall). Work done?
6. Worker A pushes with 50 N over 6 m; Worker B pushes with 100 N
   over 2 m. Who does more work? Show both calculations.

## Session B — Kinetic-energy graphs

*The tables below are a fictional practice dataset, computed from
$KE = \tfrac12 mv^2$ — not measurements from any experiment.*

Mass table (speed fixed at 4 m/s): 1 kg → 8 J; 2 kg → 16 J;
3 kg → 24 J; 4 kg → 32 J.

Speed table (mass fixed at 2 kg): 1 m/s → 1 J; 2 m/s → 4 J;
3 m/s → 9 J; 4 m/s → 16 J.

1. From the mass table, what is the kinetic energy of a 5 kg object
   at 4 m/s? Explain the pattern you used.
2. From the speed table, what is the kinetic energy of the 2 kg
   object at 6 m/s? Show the calculation (do not just extend the
   table by adding).
3. A 5 kg object moves at 4 m/s and a 2 kg object moves at 6 m/s.
   Which has more kinetic energy? Show both calculations.
4. Sketch or describe both graphs: which is a straight line and
   which is a curve? What does each shape mean in words?
5. A learner says, "The speed table goes 1, 4, 9, 16 — that is just
   adding 3, 5, 7, so speed adds energy evenly." Is that the right
   conclusion? Explain what the squares show instead.
6. **Error analysis:** A learner computed a 4 kg object at 3 m/s as
   $4 \times 9 = 36$ J. What did they forget, and what is the
   correct value?

## Session C — Potential energy and energy bars

1. A 3 kg object is held 4 m above the floor. Gravitational potential
   energy (floor as reference)?
2. A 6 kg object is held 2 m above the floor. Gravitational potential
   energy? Compare with question 1.
3. A 2 kg ball falls from 5 m (ignore air). Fill in the energy
   account: at the top (PE, KE, total), halfway down at 2.5 m
   (PE, KE, total), just before impact (PE, KE, total).
4. A book rests on a table 0.8 m above the floor. Its potential
   energy relative to the table is 0 J. A learner says, "So the book
   has no potential energy." Is that right? Explain using a
   different reference.
5. Two rubber bands: one barely stretched, one stretched far. Which
   stores more elastic potential energy? When each is released,
   what form does that energy become first?
6. A 10 kg object and a 2 kg object are both held 3 m above the
   floor. (a) Which stores more potential energy? (b) By what
   factor? (c) If both fall with no air resistance, which has more
   kinetic energy just before impact, and why?

## Session D — Transfer chains and accounts

1. A swing is pulled back and released. Write the energy chain from
   release to the highest point on the other side, naming each form
   and each change (transfer or transformation).
2. A flashlight is switched on. Write the full energy chain from the
   battery to the light, including the form most learners forget.
3. A car brakes to a stop. Its kinetic energy before braking was
   400 J (model number). After stopping, where is that 400 J?
   Name at least two places/forms.
4. A bouncing ball's mechanical energy drops from 100 units to 80
   units to 64 units across two bounces (model numbers). (a) What
   fraction of the mechanical energy remains after each bounce?
   (b) Where did the rest go? (c) Predict the mechanical energy
   after a third bounce if the same fraction remains each time.
5. A skateboarder rides a half-pipe with friction. At the start of
   each run the skater reaches a slightly lower height. Draw or
   describe the energy account across three peaks, naming the
   column that grows.
6. **Explain your reasoning (3–4 sentences):** A learner watches a
   pendulum slow down and says, "Energy is not conserved — the
   pendulum proves it." Answer them, using the system-boundary idea.

## Session E — Machines: advantage, efficiency, and choice

1. A lever lifts a 400 N load with an effort of 100 N. Mechanical
   advantage? If the load rises 0.3 m, how far does the effort end
   move (ideal case)?
2. A pulley system with 3 supporting ropes lifts a 300 N load.
   Ideal effort? How much rope must be pulled to lift the load
   2 m?
3. A ramp job: a 500 N load rises 1 m (useful work 500 J). The
   effort along a 5 m ramp is 130 N. (a) Work in? (b) Efficiency?
   (c) How many joules went to friction?
4. A machine has an efficiency of 75% and a work input of 200 J.
   Useful work out?
5. **Choice task:** A 600 N crate must reach a truck bed 1.5 m high.
   Option 1: lift straight up (needs 600 N). Option 2: push it up a
   9 m ramp with a measured effort of 120 N. (a) Useful work in
   both cases? (b) Work in for the ramp? (c) Efficiency of the
   ramp? (d) Which option would you choose if you can only push
   with 150 N, and why?
6. **Error analysis:** A learner measures a lever's MA as 4 and
   concludes, "Work out is 4 times work in." Show with a worked
   example (load 200 N, effort 50 N, load rises 0.2 m) why work out
   is not 4 times work in.
