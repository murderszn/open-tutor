# Unit 05 — Electric and Magnetic Interactions and Circuits

**Grade:** 8 · **Subject:** Science (Physical Science pathway) · **Track weeks:** 19–22
**Backlog:** [issue #39](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/39), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Rub a balloon on a sweater and it sticks to a wall without glue.
Hold a magnet near a paper clip and the clip jumps across empty
space. Wrap wire around a nail, connect a battery, and the nail
lifts paper clips — disconnect the battery and they fall. Build a
loop of wire, battery, and bulb, and the bulb lights; add a second
bulb the wrong way and both go dim. This unit teaches learners
that electric and magnetic forces act through fields that exist in
the space around objects, that charge and current follow rules
they can test, and that a circuit is a loop with one path or
several. The center of the unit is a real investigation: the
learner changes one thing at a time — coil turns, batteries, core
— counts the paper clips an electromagnet lifts, and argues from
their own data what makes the magnet stronger.

## Essential question

How can a force act across empty space — and how do we build
devices that control that force to do work?

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–04 and the diagnostic weeks:

- SI units for length and time; reading labeled tables and
  graphs; recording data with units and repeating trials
- Speed as distance ÷ time; slope of a distance–time graph
- Forces as pushes and pulls; energy as something that transfers
  and transforms but is conserved in a closed system; kinetic
  energy grows with mass and with the square of speed
- Waves as patterns that carry energy without carrying matter;
  the field idea is new here, but the habit of modeling
  something invisible (a wave) with a visible model (a rope) is
  not
- Arithmetic with decimals and fractions; proportional reasoning
  ("twice the turns, about twice the clips"); solving a one-step
  formula for an unknown
- Writing a one-sentence claim with one reason
  (claim–evidence–reasoning); designing a fair test with one
  variable changed at a time (from Unit 02)
- Safe habits for adult-supervised household-scale investigations

New content here: electric charge, positive and negative charge,
electron, static electricity, net charge, conductor, insulator,
magnet, north and south poles, magnetic field, compass,
electromagnet, coil (turns), core, circuit, closed and open
circuits, battery, electric current, switch, series and parallel
circuits, filament, volt (as the battery's "push"), and short
circuit. Nothing here assumes Unit 06's atomic models beyond the
one sentence used in Lesson 01 (atoms have a positive nucleus and
negative electrons that can move); the full atomic model is
Unit 06's work.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Explain static electricity with the charge model: rubbing
   moves electrons (negative charges) from one material to
   another; like charges repel and opposite charges attract; a
   charged object can attract a neutral object; and charge is
   conserved — it moves, it is not created.
2. Classify a material as a conductor or an insulator from a
   test (a simple circuit with the material in the loop, or a
   charged-object test), and explain why static charge builds up
   on insulators and drains away through conductors; state and
   follow the battery-only safety rule for every electrical task.
3. Map a magnetic field with a compass and explain, from the
   compass evidence, that the field exists in the space around a
   magnet even though nothing touches the magnet; describe
   qualitatively that the field is stronger near the poles and
   weaker farther away.
4. Ask questions about data and use proportional reasoning to
   determine what affects electromagnet strength — number of
   coil turns, current (number of batteries), and core material
   — and build and explain a working electromagnet that can be
   switched on and off.
5. Build and troubleshoot series and parallel circuits with a
   battery, wires, bulbs, and a switch; predict and explain bulb
   brightness in each arrangement; and trace the energy transfers
   in a circuit (chemical energy in the battery → electrical
   energy → light and thermal energy in the bulb).
6. Evaluate an investigation design — fair test, controlled
   variables, repeated trials — and use its evidence to support
   a claim about fields or forces, naming what the evidence
   shows and what it cannot prove.

- **MS-PS2-3** — ask questions about data to determine the
  factors that affect the strength of electric and magnetic
  forces. Clarification honored: the devices studied are
  electromagnets (and electric motors named only as an example
  of where the same factors matter); the data studied are the
  effect of the number of turns of wire on electromagnet
  strength and the effect of the number of batteries (current)
  on strength. Assessment boundary honored: quantitative items
  stay at proportional reasoning and algebraic thinking — no
  Coulomb's-law calculation, no field-strength formula, no
  Ohm's-law calculation anywhere in this unit.
- **MS-PS2-5** — conduct an investigation and evaluate the
  experimental design to provide evidence that fields exist
  between objects exerting forces on each other even though the
  objects are not in contact. Clarification honored: the
  phenomena are magnets, electrically charged strips of tape,
  and the balloon-and-wall effect; the investigations are
  first-hand experiences and the PhET simulations named in the
  resource pack. Assessment boundary honored: electric and
  magnetic fields only, and qualitative evidence only — no
  graded item asks for a measured field value.

No state adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification is
claimed. All practice datasets are labeled fictional practice
data; the learner's own investigation data are their own
measurements, recorded with units and repeated trials. No
generated image is presented as a measurement.

## Safety, materials, and accessibility

- **Electricity safety (adult reads aloud before Lesson 01
  and again before Lesson 05):** battery voltages only — AA or
  AAA cells (1.5 V each), at most two cells in any build. Never
  use a wall outlet, a mains adapter, a phone charger, or any
  rechargeable pack. Never connect a bare wire directly across
  a battery's two ends (a short circuit): the wire heats up fast
  enough to burn. If a wire or battery feels warm, disconnect it
  at once and tell the adult. The adult strips wire ends and
  checks every circuit before the learner connects the battery.
- **Magnets:** keep magnets away from phones, bank cards, and
  pacemakers; the adult handles any strong magnet. Small parts
  (paper clips, pins) are a choking hazard for younger children
  — the adult stores them between sessions. Iron filings, if
  used at all, stay sealed in a clear bag; never loose on a
  table, and wash hands after handling.
- **Static work:** rubbing balloons and tape is safe; do not rub
  anything near eyes. The balloon-and-water-stream demo uses a
  thin stream from a tap, with the adult present.
- **Materials:** balloons, wool or fleece cloth, small paper
  bits, clear tape, two bar magnets, a compass, a large iron
  nail, insulated copper wire (about 1 m), two AA batteries with
  a holder, two small bulbs with holders, a switch, connecting
  wires with alligator clips, paper clips (about 40), a ruler,
  notebook.
- Accessibility: every image-based question has a text-only
  alternative carrying the same information; the series/parallel
  diagram is an exact SVG with a full text description; learners
  may answer orally, by drawing, or in writing; the PhET
  simulation alternatives are named in the
  [project](project.md) and [resource pack](resource-pack.md)
  for learners who cannot do the hands-on builds.
