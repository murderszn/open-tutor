# Unit Assessment — Unit 05: Electric and Magnetic Interactions and Circuits

**Unit 05** · 50 minutes · closed notes · 40 points ·
**Answer key:** [answer key](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment)

## Section A — Multiple choice (8 points, 1 point each)

1. Two objects with like charges are held near each other. They:
   A) Attract
   B) Repel
   C) Do nothing — charges only act on contact
   D) Become neutral
2. Rubbing a balloon on wool gives the balloon a negative net
   charge. What moved during the rubbing?
   A) Positive charges (protons) from the balloon to the wool
   B) Electrons from the wool to the balloon
   C) Neutrons from the wool to the balloon
   D) New charge that the rubbing created
3. Which of these is an insulator?
   A) A copper wire
   B) A steel paper clip
   C) A rubber band
   D) Aluminum foil
4. A compass needle is placed near a bar magnet. The needle
   turns because:
   A) The magnet touches the needle
   B) The needle lines up with the magnetic field at that spot
   C) The needle is attracted to the table
   D) The magnet heats the needle
5. Which change would NOT make an electromagnet stronger?
   A) Winding more turns of wire on the coil
   B) Adding a second battery
   C) Replacing the iron nail core with a wooden pencil
   D) Using a fresh battery instead of a tired one
6. In a series circuit with two bulbs, the current through bulb A
   compared with the current through bulb B is:
   A) Greater through bulb A
   B) Greater through bulb B
   C) The same through both
   D) Zero through both, because the bulbs use it up
7. In a parallel circuit, each bulb gets:
   A) Half the battery's push
   B) The battery's full push
   C) No push at all — only the first branch gets push
   D) Push only when the other bulb is on
8. A parallel circuit has both bulbs dark. The switch is closed
   and the wires are connected. The most likely fault is:
   A) One bulb burned out
   B) The battery is dead (or the main wire is loose)
   C) One branch wire is loose
   D) The bulbs are wired in series by mistake

## Section B — Short answers (12 points, 3 points each)

9. A balloon rubbed on wool sticks to a wall. Explain, using
   the charge model: what moved during rubbing, what net
   charges resulted, and why the neutral wall attracts the
   balloon.
10. Describe the circuit test that sorts materials into
    conductors and insulators. Then explain why static charge
    builds up on an insulator (like a rubber balloon) but drains
    away from a conductor (like a metal spoon).
11. Name two observations from this unit that prove a magnetic
    field exists in the space around a magnet even though
    nothing touches the magnet. For each observation, say what
    was observed and what it proves.
12. One bulb on one battery is bright. Adding a second identical
    bulb in series makes both dim; adding it in parallel keeps
    both bright. Explain both results using the path and push
    rules.

13. (a) Describe the pattern in the turns table in one sentence.
    (2 points)
    (b) Predict the lift for 40 turns, and state the assumption
    behind the prediction. (2 points)
    (c) What does the batteries table tell you about the link
    between current and electromagnet strength? (2 points)
    (d) A learner tests "more turns = stronger" by rewinding
    with more turns *and* adding a second battery at the same
    time. The lift rises from 5 clips to 14. What can the
    learner conclude, and what can they not conclude? Explain.
    (2 points)
    (e) Which single factor in these tables makes the biggest
    difference to the lift? Support your answer with numbers.
    (2 points)

## Section D — Circuit task (6 points)

14. You have one battery, one switch, and two identical bulbs.
    (a) Describe (in words or a sketch) a series circuit using
    these parts, and a parallel circuit using the same parts.
    (2 points)
    (b) Predict the brightness of each bulb in the series circuit
    compared with one bulb alone, and in the parallel circuit
    compared with one bulb alone. Give the reason for each.
    (2 points)
    (c) In each circuit, one bulb burns out. State what happens
    to the other bulb in each case, and why. (2 points)

## Section E — Extended argument (4 points)

15. The claim is: "Fields exist between objects exerting forces
    on each other even though the objects are not in contact."
    Support the claim with evidence from TWO different
    investigations in this unit — one magnetic and one
    electric. For each, name the observation and explain how it
    supports the claim. Then name one thing this evidence
    cannot prove.
