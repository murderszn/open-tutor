# Project — Electromagnet Crane Challenge

**Unit 05 investigation/project** · Three 50-minute blocks
(Sessions 14–15 and 17) plus the Lessons 01–04 background ·
**Rubric:** 40 points · **Answer key and scoring notes:**
[answer key](answer-key.md#project)

## The challenge

Build an electromagnet, find out — by changing one thing at a
time — what makes it stronger, and then use what you learned to
design a crane that lifts a small load and moves it across a gap.
You will argue, from your own test counts, which factors matter
and by how much, exactly as Lessons 04 and 06 require.

## Materials (adult-supervised, household items only)

- A large iron nail (about 8–10 cm), about 1 m of insulated
  copper wire, two AA batteries with a holder, paper clips
  (about 40, all the same size), a ruler, a notebook
- A wooden pencil and a plastic pen (for the no-iron-core
  comparison), a small cardboard box or tray as the "loading
  dock," a strip of cardboard as the "gap" (about 10 cm wide)
- Optional observation alternative: the PhET "Magnets and
  Electromagnets" simulation (see the
  [resource pack](resource-pack.md)) for learners who cannot do
  the hands-on build — the simulation's electromagnet screen
  lets you change coils and current and watch the field change

## Safety rules (adult reads aloud before starting)

- Battery voltages only: AA cells (1.5 V each), at most two in
  any build. Never use a wall outlet, a mains adapter, or a
  phone charger.
- Connect the battery only while testing, then disconnect it.
  Never leave it connected. If any wire or battery feels warm,
  disconnect it at once and tell the adult.
- The adult strips wire ends and checks every circuit before the
  learner connects the battery. Keep magnets away from phones
  and bank cards. Paper clips are small parts — the adult stores
  them between sessions.

## Steps

### Part A — The controlled investigation (Session 14)

1. **Build the electromagnet.** Wind the wire neatly around the
   nail. Count the turns as you wind: start with 10 turns.
   Leave about 15 cm of wire free at each end. Connect the ends
   to the battery holder (adult checks first).
2. **Test the turns.** With 1 battery and the iron nail core,
   count how many paper clips the electromagnet can lift. Do it
   three times and record all three counts: try to lift the
   clips the same way each time (dip the tip straight down into
   a pile, lift slowly, count what stays on). Then rewind with
   20 turns and repeat (three trials), then 30 turns and repeat
   (three trials). Disconnect the battery between settings.
   Fill in the table:

   | Coil turns | Trial 1 | Trial 2 | Trial 3 | Average |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 10 | | | | |
   | 20 | | | | |
   | 30 | | | | |

3. **Test the batteries.** Keep 20 turns and the iron nail core.
   Test with 1 battery (three trials), then with 2 batteries
   (three trials). Record in the same style:

   | Batteries | Trial 1 | Trial 2 | Trial 3 | Average |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | | | | |
   | 2 | | | | |

4. **Test the core.** Keep 20 turns and 1 battery. Test with
   the iron nail core (three trials), then with no core — the
   same coil held in the air, or wound around the wooden pencil
   (three trials). Record:

   | Core | Trial 1 | Trial 2 | Trial 3 | Average |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Iron nail | | | | |
   | No core (air / pencil) | | | | |

5. **Graph it.** Draw a bar graph of average clips lifted
   against coil turns (10, 20, 30). Label both axes with units
   (turns; paper clips). The graph goes with your conclusion.

### Part B — The crane design (Session 15)

6. **Define the job.** Your crane must lift a load of 10 paper
   clips from the loading dock, carry it across the 10 cm gap,
   and set it down on the far side — without dropping it. The
   crane is your electromagnet, held by hand or hung from a
   string over the gap. Criteria: the load arrives complete
   (10 clips); the crane is switched off to release the load
   (disconnect the battery to drop it — that is the point of an
   electromagnet). Constraint: at most 2 batteries, and you may
   not touch the load with your fingers while carrying it.
7. **Design from your data.** Before building, write down which
   turns, batteries, and core you will use, and why — citing
   your Part A averages. Predict how many clips your crane
   will lift.
8. **Build, test, iterate.** Test the crane. If it drops the
   load, change ONE thing (more turns, a second battery, or a
   better core) and test again. Record each version: what you
   changed, how many clips it lifted, and whether the load
   crossed the gap. At least one deliberate design change must
   be tested and recorded.

### Part C — Conclusion and evaluation (Session 17)

9. **Conclude.** Write a conclusion (6–8 sentences) that answers
   the investigation question: what affects electromagnet
   strength? Cite the averages from all three tables, use the
   proportional language ("doubling the turns about doubled the
   lift"), and name the strongest factor you found.
10. **Evaluate the design.** Answer in writing: Was your test
    fair? Name one thing you kept the same across settings and
    one thing that could have varied by accident (for example,
    how deep the nail dipped into the pile, or a battery getting
    tired across trials). Say how you would improve the test if
    you ran it again. Then evaluate the crane: did it meet the
    criteria? What would you change next?

## Deliverables

1. The three completed data tables (turns, batteries, core),
   each with three trials and averages.
2. The bar graph of average clips against coil turns, with
   labeled axes.
3. The crane design note (settings chosen, prediction, test log
   with at least one deliberate change).
4. The written conclusion with evidence from the tables, plus
   the fair-test evaluation and the crane evaluation.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | 10 points | 6–7 points | 3–4 points | 0–1 points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Data tables (10) | All three tables complete: three trials per setting, averages computed correctly, units and settings labeled | Two tables complete; or averages with small errors | One table complete, or trials missing | No usable tables |
| Bar graph (10) | Graph drawn from the turns table; both axes labeled with units; bars match the averages | Graph present with small labeling or value errors | Graph present but bars do not match the table, or axes unlabeled | No graph |
| Crane design and test log (10) | Crane built from the data (settings justified); load crossed the gap; at least one deliberate change tested and logged | Crane tested; design partly justified or the change log thin | Crane attempted but no test log, or settings chosen without data | No crane test |
| Conclusion and evaluation (10) | Conclusion cites all three tables with proportional language; fair-test evaluation names a control and a possible error with a fix; crane evaluated against criteria | Conclusion cites most tables; evaluation names a control or an error but not both | Conclusion with no data cited, or no evaluation | No conclusion |
