# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Electric and Magnetic Interactions and Circuits

Adult-facing. Learner pages stay free of answer-key content; this
guide explains the teaching moves, the likely misconceptions, and
what to do when scores fall below the bar.

## How the unit hangs together

Lessons 01–02 build the charge model (electrons move; like
repel, opposite attract; induction; conductors vs. insulators).
Lesson 03 moves the same "force across a gap" idea to magnets:
the compass is the test object that maps the field, and the SVG
field diagram is the model the learner's own compass readings
should match. Lesson 04 is the payoff of the first half: current
makes magnetism, and the three strength factors (turns, current,
core) are stated as proportional rules the learner will test.
Lessons 05–06 cash the model out as circuits: series vs.
parallel is one idea (shared push vs. full push, one path vs. two
paths), and troubleshooting is the fair-test discipline applied
to a dead loop. The project is the unit's central experience, not
a garnish — run it for real, with real counts and three trials
per setting, because the assessment's data section (item 13) is
graded on exactly that reasoning.

Week 22 is doing double duty by design (see the track
scope-and-sequence): the Week 22 quiz tests Unit 05, Session 17
finishes the investigation, and Practice Set F mixes Unit 05
troubleshooting with Units 01–03 retrieval. Do not skip Session
17 to "make room" for assessment prep; the battery-count tests
and the graph are the investigation's conclusion.

## Misconceptions to watch for

| Learner says | The confusion | The move |
|---|---|---|
| "Rubbing creates charge." | Charge conservation | Count + and − in the before/after diagram (Lesson 01, independent practice 4): totals are unchanged, only addresses moved |
| "A neutral object has no charges." | Neutral means equal, not empty | The wall: induction shifts charges that are already there; nothing arrives from outside |
| "The field only exists at the poles." | Field vs. object | Compass turns in empty space centimeters from the magnet; the needle is the evidence |
| "The compass needle points at the magnet." | Needle direction vs. pole | The needle's north-seeking end points *toward* a south pole — it is attracted to the opposite pole, along the field line |
| "The battery makes the nail magnetic." | Current vs. battery | Disconnect the wire in front of the learner; the clips fall — the current, not the battery's presence, makes the field |
| "More wire always means stronger." | Proportional range | The pattern holds in the tested range; a tired battery or very long wire breaks it — that is why trials are repeated |
| "The first bulb uses up the current." | Current vs. energy | The bulb takes energy, not charge; the same charge flows around the loop (Lesson 05, worked example 1) |
| "In series the second bulb gets less current." | Current vs. push | One loop, one current — it is the push that is shared; trace the loop with a finger |
| "I swapped the battery and the bulb, so the battery was dead." | Two variables at once | One swap at a time; the swap test only proves what it isolates (Lesson 06) |
| "A short circuit is just a fast circuit." | Short = no load | The bulb limits the current; a bare wire across a battery has almost no limit, so it heats up — that is the safety rule |

## Pacing notes

- Lesson 01 needs the full 50 minutes if the balloon demo is
  new; if the learner has done static tricks before, bank the
  spare time for Practice Set A, item 3 (the proportional
  prediction), which pays off in Lesson 04.
- The investigation splits naturally: Part 1 (static
  observations) after Lesson 02, Part 2 (field mapping) after
  Lesson 04. If time is short, Part 1 can be a demonstration
  with learner predictions instead of hands-on tape work — but
  keep the written charge-transfer log.
- The project needs all three blocks. Block 1 (Session 14)
  almost always runs long on coil winding; cap winding at 15
  minutes and pre-wind a spare 20-turn coil the adult can swap
  in. Session 17 is the conclusion, not a spare period: protect
  it, because the graph and the fair-test evaluation are what
  the assessment grades.
- Lesson 05's two circuits should both be built, not just
  drawn. If only one build is possible, build the parallel
  circuit (it is the one whose behavior surprises learners) and
  use the SVG for the series case.

## Safety reminders

- Batteries only (AA/AAA, at most two cells). The adult strips
  wire ends and checks every circuit before the battery is
  connected. Connect the battery only while testing; if a wire
  or battery feels warm, disconnect at once.
- Magnets away from phones, bank cards, and pacemakers. Iron
  filings, if used, stay sealed in a clear bag. Paper clips are
  small parts — the adult stores them between sessions.
- Static work uses a balloon and tape only; no rubbing near
  eyes. The water-stream demo uses a thin tap stream with the
  adult present.

## Re-teach plan

- **Quiz below 8/10:** re-teach the missed objective with the
  matching lesson's worked examples before the assessment.
  Charge-model misses (items 1–2) → Lesson 01 examples.
  Conductor/insulator misses (item 3) → Lesson 02 test.
  Field misses (item 4) → Lesson 03 compass demo. Electromagnet
  misses (items 5–6) → Lesson 04 worked examples with the real
  coil. Circuit misses (items 7–9) → rebuild both circuits from
  Lesson 05. Energy misses (item 10) → Unit 03 energy tracking.
- **Assessment below 28/40:** do not advance to Unit 06 until
  Section C (data) and Section D (circuits) are re-worked with
  the key. A learner who cannot state the proportional rule from
  a table needs the project data re-graphed with the adult, not
  more reading. A learner who cannot trace a loop needs the
  physical circuit in hand, not the diagram.
- **Project below 28/40:** re-run the weakest table with the
  adult (three fresh trials, battery disconnected between
  settings) and re-write the conclusion from the new averages
  before Unit 06.

## Project scoring notes

- Data tables: accept any consistent trial method (dip-and-lift
  counts) as long as the method is the same across settings.
  Averages should be computed correctly; a one-clip arithmetic
  slip costs the point for that table only, not the whole
  criterion.
- Bar graph: bars must match the learner's own averages, not
  the practice-set numbers. Axes labeled with units ("coil
  turns", "paper clips lifted (average)").
- Crane: the design must cite Part A data (for example, "I used
  30 turns because the 30-turn average was highest"). A crane
  that lifts the load on the first try with no design change
  still needs one deliberate change tested (for example, trying
  20 turns to see if the load still crosses) — the rubric
  requires a tested change, not a successful first try.
- Conclusion: "doubling the turns about doubled the lift" is
  the expected proportional language. Accept "more turns =
  more clips" only at the 6–7 point level; the 10-point level
  needs the proportional comparison and all three factors.

## Model troubleshooting card (Lesson 06 applied task)

1. **Switch closed?** Flip it off and on. Proves: the loop was
   (or was not) open at the switch.
2. **Battery fresh?** Swap in a known-good battery. Proves: the
   old battery was (or was not) dead.
3. **Wires connected?** Wiggle each clip gently. Proves: a
   loose or disconnected wire was (or was not) the break.
4. **Bulb good?** Swap in a known-good bulb. Proves: the old
   bulb's filament was (or was not) broken.
5. **Loop complete?** Trace battery → wire → switch → wire →
   bulb → wire → battery with a finger. Proves: where the gap
   is, if the first four checks passed.
   *Series vs. parallel:* in series, one fault darkens every
   bulb; in parallel, a dark bulb with a lit bulb means the
   fault is in that bulb's branch only.
