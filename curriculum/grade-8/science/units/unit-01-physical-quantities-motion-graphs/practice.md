# Practice Sessions — Unit 01: Physical Quantities, Motion and Graphs

Each session is a 50-minute block: 10 minutes of retrieval warm-up (mixed
review of earlier sessions), 30 minutes on the session tasks below, and
10 minutes of adult feedback the same day. Solutions and scoring notes:
[answer key](answer-key.md#practice-sessions).

## Session A — Prefix conversions

*Retrieval:* state the three SI base units with symbols from memory.

1. Convert: (a) 1.8 m → cm; (b) 2500 m → km; (c) 90 mm → cm; (d) 0.35 kg → g.
2. Which is greater: 1.5 km or 1600 m? Show the conversion.
3. A ribbon is 2 m 40 cm long. Express its length in (a) cm, (b) mm.
4. **Explain your reasoning:** why does 1 kg of feathers have the same
   mass as 1 kg of bricks, even though the piles look different?
5. Order from lightest to heaviest: 800 g, 1.2 kg, 950 g, 0.5 kg.
   Show one conversion that proves the order.

## Session B — Measure, repeat, record

*Retrieval:* name the four good-measurer habits from Lesson 2.

1. Measure the width of a doorway three times with a tape measure.
   Record a labeled table and report your best value with one sentence of
   justification.
2. Time 10 of your own heartbeats with a stopwatch, three trials.
   Report each trial and comment on repeatability.
3. A scale reads 12 g with an empty cup on it, then 145 g with water in
   the cup. What is the water's mass? Explain the zero-check step.
4. List the controlled variables you will keep the same in the Sessions
   8–9 motion lab, and say why each one matters.
5. **Error analysis:** two learners measure the same table: 122 cm and
   1.22 m. "We disagree!" Do they? Explain.

## Session C — Speed calculations

*Retrieval:* write $v = d/t$, $d = v \times t$, $t = d/v$ from memory.

1. A runner covers 400 m in 80 s. Speed?
2. A car travels 150 km in 2 h. Speed in km/h? In m/s? (Show the
   conversion: 150,000 m ÷ 7,200 s.)
3. How far does a cyclist at 8 m/s travel in 45 s?
4. How long does it take to walk 1.2 km at 1.5 m/s? Give the answer in
   minutes.
5. 20-m split times: 4.0, 4.2, 4.1, 6.8, 4.0 s. Average speed over
   100 m? Constant or changing — and what is the most likely story
   behind the 6.8 s split?
6. **Explain your reasoning:** a snail and a jet both travel 1 km. Which
   has the greater speed, and which takes longer? What single number
   distinguishes them?

## Session D — Graph-matching drills

*Retrieval:* sketch a blank d–t axes pair with labeled axes from memory.

Use this fictional practice dataset (a remote-control car; times in
seconds, distances in meters):

| t | 0 | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| d | 0 | 10 | 20 | 20 | 35 | 50 |

1. Plot the six points with labeled axes and a title.
2. Compute the speed on each segment (0–5, 5–10, 10–15, 15–20, 20–25 s).
3. Which segment was fastest? Which segment was the car stopped?
4. Overall average speed for the 25 s? Why is it lower than the fastest
   segment's speed?
5. Write a 2-sentence story that fits this graph (what the driver did).
6. **Error analysis:** a classmate says "the graph's highest point, 50 m,
   is where the car was fastest." Correct the confusion between *where*
   (position) and *how fast* (slope).

## Session E — v–t sketches and the area idea

*Retrieval:* on a v–t graph, what does height mean? What does area mean?

1. Sketch the v–t graph for: 6 m/s for 5 s, then 0 m/s for 5 s. Shade
   the area and write the total distance.
2. Two v–t rectangles: (a) 3 m/s × 12 s; (b) 6 m/s × 6 s. Which area is
   larger? What does that say about the two trips?
3. A bus: 10 m/s for 20 s, 0 m/s for 30 s (a stop), 10 m/s for 10 s.
   Sketch, compute total distance from areas, and check with
   $d = v \times t$ per moving segment.
4. **Explain your reasoning:** a friend says "area under the line is
   just multiplying height by width — it has nothing to do with
   physics." Use the unit multiplication (m/s × s = m) to argue that
   the area *must* be a distance.
5. Convert this d–t description to a v–t sketch: "steady climb at 2 m/s
   for 10 s, flat for 5 s, steady climb at 4 m/s for 5 s." Label heights
   and widths; compute total distance both ways (slope vs. area).
