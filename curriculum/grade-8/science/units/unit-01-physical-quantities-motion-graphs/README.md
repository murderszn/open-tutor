# Unit 01 — Physical Quantities, Units, Motion and Graphs

**Grade:** 8 · **Subject:** Science (Physical Science pathway) · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #39](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/39), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A stopwatch and a meter stick are the first instruments of physics. In this
unit the learner measures length, mass, and time in SI units, converts
confidently between metric prefixes, and builds the habits of careful
measurement: choosing the right instrument, repeating trials, and recording
data with units. Speed becomes a *rate* — distance divided by time — and then
a picture: the learner collects position–time data, plots distance–time
graphs, reads speed as the slope of the line, and compares two movers by
their graphs. The unit closes with velocity–time sketches, including the
idea that the area under the line represents distance traveled.

## Essential question

How can careful measurements and simple graphs turn "how fast?" into
something we can see, compare, and defend?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and prior grades:

- Decimal and fraction arithmetic; one-step formula solving
  (e.g., solve $d = v \times t$ for $t$)
- Reading tables and bar graphs; labeling axes with units
- Writing a one-sentence claim with one reason (claim–evidence–reasoning
  introduced in the diagnostic)
- Safe habits for household-scale investigations (adult supervises all
  data collection)

The diagnostic verifies measurement and graphing fluency; Sessions 2–4
re-teach insecure skills before the unit assumes them.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Measure length, mass, and time using correct SI units (m, kg, s);
   convert between mm, cm, m, km and between g and kg; explain what a
   metric prefix means.
2. Plan and carry out a measurement: choose an instrument with the right
   precision, repeat trials, record data in a labeled table, and identify
   the controlled variable(s).
3. Calculate average speed from distance and time data (including unit
   conversions in the inputs), and distinguish constant from changing
   speed in a data table.
4. Construct a distance–time graph from position–time data; read
   coordinates from a graph; compute speed as the slope of a straight
   segment; explain what steeper, flatter, and horizontal segments mean;
   compare two movers from their graphs.
5. Sketch and interpret velocity–time graphs qualitatively (constant
   speed, faster vs. slower, stopped); explain, using a rectangle example,
   that the area under a constant-speed line equals the distance traveled.

## Vocabulary (taught with objects and models first, word second)

physical quantity · SI unit · meter (m) · kilogram (kg) · second (s) ·
prefix (milli-, centi-, kilo-) · precision · instrument · trial ·
repeatability · variable · controlled variable · average speed ·
constant speed · changing speed · rate · distance–time graph · slope ·
intercept (informal: "starting distance") · segment · velocity · stop
(horizontal segment) · area (informal: "space under the line")

## Materials

Notebook, pencils, ruler or meter stick, flexible tape measure (household
scale), balance or kitchen scale, stopwatch (phone or digital), index cards
for vocabulary, graph paper or printed graph grids, a straight hallway or
sidewalk course at least 15–20 m, masking tape for position markers, a toy
car or ball for one investigation session, printed or on-screen copy of
[`assets/two-movers-story.png`](assets/two-movers-story.png), and the
resource pack's verified references. Adult-supervised data collection
happens in a safe open area — no racing near streets or stairs; see the
project's safety box. Digital option: a spreadsheet app for data tables;
the free PhET "Moving Man" simulation (resource pack) as an observation
alternative.

## Accessibility and supports

All lessons include reading/language supports, an alternate response mode,
and extensions (see each lesson and the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md)).
Graph work can be done on raised-line graph paper, with large-print grids,
or orally by describing coordinates ("at 10 seconds, 20 meters"). The
motion lab has a seated observation role (timer/reader) and an
observation-only alternative via the PhET simulation.
