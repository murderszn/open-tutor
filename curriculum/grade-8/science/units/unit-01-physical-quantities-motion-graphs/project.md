# Project — Two-Mover Motion Investigation

**Type:** adult-supervised household-scale investigation with written
report and presentation · **Sessions:** 8–9 (lab), 14–15 (drafting and
revision), 18 (presentations) · **Rubric:** below · **Teacher scoring
notes:** [teacher guide](teacher-guide.md#project-scoring)

## Driving question

Which of two movers is faster — and how do the graphs prove it?

## Safety box (adult reads aloud before Session 8)

- The course is a straight, flat, open area at least 15–20 m long: a
  hallway, driveway, or park path — **never near streets, stairs, pools,
  or traffic.**
- Movers walk or jog at a comfortable pace. **No racing, no sprinting at
  full speed, no chasing.**
- The adult places and removes tape markers, operates or supervises the
  stopwatch, and stops the session if the area becomes unsafe.
- **Observation alternative:** if outdoor space or mobility is limited,
  the learner takes the timer/reader role while the adult moves, or runs
  the whole investigation as a simulation study with the PhET "Moving
  Man" sim (see resource pack) — same tables, graphs, and report.

## Materials

Tape measure (≥ 10 m), masking tape, stopwatch, notebook, graph paper,
printed data-table blanks (below), the two "movers" (e.g., learner
walking steadily vs. learner walking with a planned rest stop; or two
different walkers — the adult approves the pair).

## Lab sessions 1–2 (Sessions 8–9)

**Session 8 — plan and first data (50 min):**

1. With the adult, mark a straight course: start line at 0 m and tape
   markers every 5 m to at least 20 m. Record the course length.
2. Choose the two movers and write the **plan**: what each mover will do
   (Mover 1: steady walk the whole course; Mover 2: walk, rest 10 s at
   the 10-m mark, continue), which instrument measures what, how many
   trials (3 per mover), and the controlled variables (same course,
   same start signal, same timer, same rest length for Mover 2).
3. Collect Mover 1's data: the adult calls time every 5 s; the learner
   reads and records the mover's position at each call. Run 3 trials.
4. Record in labeled tables (one per mover per trial):

| Trial | Time (s) | Position (m) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 0 |
| | 5 | … |

**Session 9 — repeats and graphing (50 min):**

1. Collect Mover 2's data the same way (3 trials).
2. Check repeatability: do the three trials per mover agree within
   ~10%? Flag and re-run any trial with a timing error.
3. Choose each mover's **best (most repeatable) trial** and plot both
   movers' distance–time graphs on the *same* axes.
4. Compute per-segment speeds for both movers (rise ÷ run) and each
   mover's overall average speed.

## Report drafting sessions (Sessions 14–15)

**Session 14 — draft (50 min):** write the investigation report (1–2
pages, adult reviews the same day):

1. **Question and plan** — what was compared, how, and what stayed
   controlled.
2. **Data** — the two best-trial tables and the combined graph (photo
   or careful copy).
3. **Speed calculations** — per-segment and overall speeds with units.
4. **Claim–evidence–reasoning** — claim ("Mover __ was faster because
   …"), evidence (two numbers from the data), reasoning (how slope
   shows speed).
5. **Limits** — one thing that could have changed the result (wind,
   timing reaction, an uneven course) and how you'd fix it next time.

**Session 15 — peer critique and revision (50 min):** the adult (or a
family member) reads the draft against the rubric below and names one
strength and one fix; the learner revises and resubmits.

## Deliverables

1. Labeled data tables for both movers (best trials starred; repeats
   shown or summarized).
2. One combined distance–time graph with labeled axes, both movers'
   lines, and a legend.
3. Speed calculations with units (per-segment and overall, both movers).
4. A written claim–evidence–reasoning report (see structure above).
5. Session 18: a 5-minute presentation of the graph and the claim.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Solid | 2 — Developing | 1 — Starting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Measurement quality (10) | 3 labeled trials per mover; units everywhere; controlled variables listed; outlier flagged/re-run | Trials and units present; minor gaps in controls | Missing trials or units; controls unclear | Data incomplete or unlabeled |
| Graph (8) | Both movers on shared labeled axes; correct points; legend; title | Graph correct with 1–2 labeling slips | Points misplotted or axes unlabeled | No usable graph |
| Speed calculations (8) | Per-segment and overall speeds, correct, with units | Correct with minor arithmetic slips | Formula misapplied or units missing | No valid calculations |
| CER report (10) | Clear claim; ≥2 data-based evidence statements; reasoning links slope to speed; limits named | All parts present, reasoning thin | Claim without data evidence, or no reasoning | Missing CER structure |
| Presentation (4) | 5-min talk explains graph and claim; answers one question | Covers graph and claim briefly | Reads report aloud without explaining | No presentation |

**Scoring:** 36–40 = exceeds; 30–35 = meets; 24–29 = approaching (target
for re-teach in Session 17); below 24 = revise and resubmit with adult
support.

## Capstone presentations (Session 18)

Each learner presents the combined graph and the CER claim in 5 minutes,
then the adult asks one follow-up ("What would the graph look like if
Mover 2 had rested twice as long?"). The adult scores with the rubric
above; the learner files the final report privately (never in this
repository).
