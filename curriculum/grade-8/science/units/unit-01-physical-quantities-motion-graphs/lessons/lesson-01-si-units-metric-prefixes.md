# Lesson 01 — SI Units and Metric Prefixes

**Goal (learner-facing):** I can name the SI units for length, mass, and
time, explain what milli-, centi-, and kilo- mean, and convert measurements
between metric units.

**Time:** 50 minutes · **Materials:** ruler, meter stick, notebook, index
cards · **Teacher key:** [answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-01)

## Readiness check (5 min)

The adult holds up a ruler and asks: "About how long is this — in inches?
In centimeters?" Any reasonable estimates are fine. The point: measuring
means comparing something to an agreed unit.

## Explanation

A **physical quantity** is something we can measure: how long, how heavy,
how long ago. Every measurement is a number *times a unit*:

> 3.2 **meters** — the number 3.2 says "how many," the unit "meters" says
> "of what."

Scientists worldwide use one shared system so nobody has to guess: the
**International System of Units (SI)**. Its three base units for this
course:

| Quantity | SI unit | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Length (distance) | meter | m |
| Mass | kilogram | kg |
| Time | second | s |

A meter is roughly a long stride. A kilogram is about the mass of a liter
of water. A second is about one "Mississippi" count.

Meters and kilograms are sometimes too big or too small, so SI adds
**prefixes** — fixed multipliers in front of the unit:

| Prefix | Symbol | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| milli- | m | one-thousandth (0.001) | 1 mm = 0.001 m |
| centi- | c | one-hundredth (0.01) | 1 cm = 0.01 m |
| kilo- | k | one thousand (1000) | 1 km = 1000 m |

A millimeter is about the thickness of a dime edge; a centimeter is about
the width of a pinky finger; a kilometer is about ten city blocks.

**Converting** just moves the decimal point by counting place values:

- **Big unit → small unit** (km → m): multiply, decimal moves right.
  $2.5\ \text{km} = 2.5 \times 1000 = 2500\ \text{m}$
- **Small unit → big unit** (cm → m): divide, decimal moves left.
  $350\ \text{cm} = 350 \div 100 = 3.5\ \text{m}$

Convert through the *base unit* when the two units don't share a step:
mm → cm → m, or g → kg.

**Vocabulary:** *physical quantity* (something measurable), *unit*
(the agreed comparison), *SI* (the shared international system),
*prefix* (a fixed multiplier like milli- or kilo-).

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Convert $4.75\ \text{m}$ to centimeters.
The step m → cm multiplies by 100 (centi- = 0.01, so 100 cm fit in 1 m).
$4.75 \times 100 = 475$. **Answer: 475 cm.**

**Example 2.** Convert $1200\ \text{g}$ to kilograms.
The step g → kg divides by 1000. $1200 \div 1000 = 1.2$.
**Answer: 1.2 kg.**

**Example 3.** Convert $45\ \text{mm}$ to centimeters.
1 cm = 10 mm, so mm → cm divides by 10. $45 \div 10 = 4.5$.
**Answer: 4.5 cm.**

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. Convert $0.75\ \text{m}$ to millimeters. *(Hint: go m → cm → mm, or
   multiply by 1000 directly.)*
2. Convert $300\ \text{cm}$ to meters.
3. Convert $3\ \text{kg}\ 250\ \text{g}$ to grams. *(Hint: handle the kg
   part first.)*
4. Which is longer: $900\ \text{mm}$ or $0.9\ \text{m}$? Explain.
5. A track is $1.2\ \text{km}$ long. How many meters is that?

## Independent practice

1. Convert: (a) $2.05\ \text{m} \to \text{cm}$; (b) $780\ \text{mm} \to
   \text{cm}$; (c) $0.6\ \text{km} \to \text{m}$; (d) $4500\ \text{g} \to
   \text{kg}$.
2. Order these from shortest to longest: $15\ \text{mm}$,
   $2\ \text{cm}$, $0.25\ \text{m}$, $300\ \text{mm}$.
3. A recipe uses $500\ \text{g}$ of flour and the bag holds
   $1\ \text{kg}$. After the recipe, how many grams remain?
4. **Error analysis:** Sam says "$150\ \text{cm} = 1.5\ \text{km}$
   because you move the decimal three places." What did Sam mix up?
   Give the correct conversion.

## Application

Measure the length of this lesson's notebook in centimeters, then in
millimeters, then convert your centimeter reading to meters. Do all three
values agree (they should describe the same length)? Record each with its
unit.

## Exit check

Write the three SI base units for length, mass, and time with their
symbols, and convert $2.4\ \text{m}$ to centimeters without a calculator.

## Supports

- **Reading/language:** prefix meanings are printed on index cards with
  examples (milli- = thousandth, like a millimeter of rain).
- **Alternate response mode:** conversions may be shown on a place-value
  chart instead of written equations.
- **Extension:** research why the kilogram is the only SI base unit with a
  prefix built into its name, and state the equivalent mass in grams.

## Teacher note

**Likely misconception:** learners multiply when they should divide
because "kilo is bigger so the number must get bigger." Teach the
intuition first: the *same physical amount* gets a *bigger number* when
measured in *smaller units* — 1 km of road is also 1000 m of road.
Watch for missing unit symbols in answers; insist the unit is part of the
answer. Key: [answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-01).
