## Session-by-session notes

- **S1–S2 (units):** Drill the "same amount, bigger number in smaller
  units" intuition before the decimal-moving mechanics. Accept any
  consistent conversion method.
- **S3–S4 (measuring):** The 10-m rehearsal walk is deliberately
  low-stakes — the adult should model calling time and reading position.
  Insist on the labeled table format; it becomes the lab format.
- **S5–S6 (speed):** The formula triangle ($d$ over $v \cdot t$) is the
  recommended scaffold. Watch for km ÷ minutes reported as m/s.
- **S7 (graphing):** Spend extra time on the "the line is not the path"
  distinction — it is the single most durable misconception in this
  unit. Have the learner narrate one graph aloud: "from 4 to 8 seconds,
  the car stayed at 8 meters."
- **S8–S9 (lab):** The adult is the safety officer and the timer. If the
  learner's three trials disagree by more than ~10%, re-run rather than
  averaging. The observation alternative (adult moves / PhET sim) earns
  full credit when documented.
- **S10–S11 (slope):** The generated illustration is a *story prompt*,
  not evidence — say so aloud. Verify the slope arithmetic together
  (jogger 2 m/s, rest 0 m/s, 2 m/s; cyclist 2 m/s) before independent
  work.
- **S12–S13 (v–t):** Contrast-drill d–t vs. v–t explicitly: "steepness
  asks how fast on a d–t graph; height asks how fast on a v–t graph."
  The area = distance connection should be *discovered* via the
  2 m/s × 10 s rectangle before it is stated.
- **S14–S15 (report):** Peer critique uses the rubric's CER row only —
  one strength, one fix. Revision is mandatory, not extra credit.
- **S16 (quiz):** 30 minutes, closed-note except the formula triangle
  card.
- **S17 (re-teach):** see plan below; do not preview the unit
  assessment.
- **S18 (presentations):** 5 minutes each; the adult's one follow-up
  question must require graph reasoning, not recall.
- **S19 (flex):** catch-up, the Lesson 6 PhET extension, or the
  independent-practice challenge items.
- **S20 (assessment):** 45 minutes; graph paper provided.

## Common misconceptions (watch list)

1. **"Bigger prefix → bigger number."** Reversed: 1 km = 1000 m, so the
   *same road* is "1" in km but "1000" in m. Use the road example.
2. **"The average of three trials is automatically true."** Teach
   spread-checking first; re-run outliers.
3. **"The d–t line is the path / the hill."** Flat = stopped. Up =
   farther away. Narrate graphs aloud until this is automatic.
4. **"Flat segment = went back."** Flat means distance unchanged —
   stopped. "Back" would slope *downward*.
5. **"Steeper = faster" carried onto v–t graphs.** On v–t, faster =
   *higher*. Post both conventions side by side during S12–S13.
6. **Area = height + width.** The unit check kills it: (m/s) × s = m is
   meaningful; (m/s) + s is not.

## Project scoring

Use the [40-point rubric](project.md#rubric-40-points). Score the graph
and calculations from the filed report; score the CER row from the
Session 14 draft *and* the revision (credit improvement). The
presentation row is scored live in Session 18. Learners below 24 points
revise and resubmit with adult support before Unit 02 begins.

## Re-teach plan (Session 17)

Diagnose from the quiz, then pick the matching block (20 min each; do at
most two):

- **Conversions shaky (Q1–Q2):** place-value chart drill with 10 fresh
  conversions; re-quiz with 3 items.
- **Speed setup errors (Q5–Q6):** formula-triangle re-teach with the
  convert-first rule; 4 new problems.
- **Graph reading (Q7–Q8):** narrate-aloud protocol on 3 printed graphs
  (steep, flat, two-mover); learner narrates the 4th.
- **v–t confusion (Q9–Q10):** side-by-side d–t/v–t of the *same* motion
  (use the jogger data from Lesson 5); height-vs-steepness contrast
  drill.

## Extensions (Session 19 or homework)

- PhET "Moving Man": reproduce the jogger's v–t graph in the sim and
  screenshot the matching d–t graph (filed privately).
- Research task: why is the kilogram the only SI base unit whose name
  contains a prefix? (Answer: historical artifact — the gram was deemed
  too small for a base unit; 1 kg = 1000 g exactly.)
- Design task: invent a third mover for the Lesson 5 story (e.g., a
  skater who starts late) and draw its d–t line on the same axes.
