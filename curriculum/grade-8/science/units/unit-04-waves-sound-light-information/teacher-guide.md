# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Waves, Sound, Light, and Information

Adult-facing. Learner pages stay free of answer-key content; this
guide explains the teaching moves, the likely misconceptions, and
what to do when scores fall below the bar.

## How the unit hangs together

Lessons 01–02 build the wave model (what travels, the four
numbers, $v = f\lambda$, amplitude ↔ energy). Lessons 03–04 apply
the model to the two waves learners meet every day: sound (needs
a medium, speeds by medium, pitch vs. loudness, echoes) and light
(reflection, absorption, transmission, color). Lesson 05 widens
the picture to the whole electromagnetic family, descriptively.
Lesson 06 cashes the model out as an information argument: the
noisy-channel game is the unit's central experience, not a garnish
— run it for real, with real counts, because the assessment's
extended argument (item 12) is graded on evidence from it.

Week 18 is doing double duty by design (see the track
scope-and-sequence): the Week 18 quiz tests Unit 04, Session 17
retrieves Units 01–03, and Practice Set F mixes both. Do not skip
Session 17 to "make room" for assessment prep; the midyear review
is the point of the week.

## Misconceptions to watch for

| Learner says | The confusion | The move |
|---|---|---|
| "The wave carries the water to the beach." | Medium vs. pattern | Redo the flag/rope demo slowly; predict the flag's final position first |
| "Amplitude and wavelength are both how big the wave is." | Height vs. length | Point at the SVG: amplitude is vertical (rest to crest), wavelength is horizontal (crest to crest) |
| "Louder means higher frequency." | Pitch vs. loudness | Hum one note softly, then loudly; name what changed (amplitude) and what did not (frequency) |
| "Echo distance is speed × time." | Round trip | Ask: "Did the sound stop at the wall, or come back?" — then divide by 2 |
| "Translucent means no light gets through." | Translucent vs. opaque | The reading test: can you read through it? Wax paper: light yes, image no |
| "A red apple is red in the dark." | Color needs light | Green-only light demo (or the Lesson 04 worked example): no red arriving, nothing red to reflect |
| "Digital means computers / binary." | Signal shape vs. device | The flashlight code in Lesson 06 is digital with no computer and no binary counting |
| "Digital is always better." | Transmission vs. source | A digital copy of a bad recording is faithfully bad; reliability is about the trip, not the talent |

## Pacing notes

- Lesson 02 needs the full 50 minutes if the formula triangle is
  new; if learners are fluent from Unit 01's speed work, bank the
  spare time for Practice Set B, item 4 (the inverse-relationship
  table), which pays off in Lesson 05.
- The investigation splits naturally: Part 1 (rope/slinky) after
  Lesson 02, Part 2 (light materials) after Lesson 04. If time is
  short, Part 2 can be a demonstration with learner predictions
  instead of hands-on sorting — but keep the written R/A/T table.
- The project needs both blocks. Block 1 almost always runs long
  on code-chart arguments; cap chart negotiation at 15 minutes
  and let the test results settle the arguments.

## Scoring and re-teach

- **Quiz (10 points):** 8/10 is on track. Missed items 2 or 5
  (calculations) → re-teach with the formula triangle and one
  fresh worked example each. Missed item 9 (signals) → re-run
  the noisy-channel game once, with the learner as receiver.
- **Assessment (40 points):** 28/40 is on track. Below 28, use
  this plan before Unit 05: (a) re-work every missed Part A item
  from the key, in writing; (b) re-do Practice Set F items 1–5
  for midyear gaps; (c) for a missed item 12, re-run the pulse
  chain with noise and have the learner dictate the paragraph
  orally first, then write it.
- **Project (40 points):** 28/40 is on track. Below 28, re-run
  the noisy round with the adult and re-write the recommendation
  from the new counts. Do not award chart points for a chart the
  adult wrote.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** formula card on every calculation session (not
  just the assessment); labeled-card ordering for the spectrum
  instead of recall writing; oral answers accepted for the exit
  checks and the project recommendation draft.
- **Extension:** the Lesson 02 inverse table at $v = 12$ m/s;
  the Lesson 03 sonar extension (148 m); the Lesson 05 bee-vision
  note; the Lesson 06 repeat-rule (error correction) design.
  None of these is required for the assessment.
- **Accessibility:** every image question has a text-only
  alternative in the lesson; the PhET simulation alternative is
  named in the project and resource pack; learners may answer by
  drawing, pointing at the diagram, or speaking.

## Safety recap (adult reads before each investigation)

Rope/slinky at waist height or below, nothing aimed at anyone.
Flashlights at walls, never eyes; no lasers. Ordinary speaking
and clapping volumes only. Household light-lab items only; no
looking directly at bright sources. The echo activity happens
outdoors or in a large room with the adult present.

## Standards boundaries honored (do not expand in re-teaching)

- MS-PS4-1 graded work uses standard repeating mechanical waves
  only; electromagnetic waves stay descriptive, and light wave
  speed is never a calculation item.
- MS-PS4-2 work stays qualitative for light and mechanical waves.
- MS-PS4-3 work never uses binary counting and never asks how a
  device works inside. If a learner asks about binary, acknowledge
  it as a real coding system for a later course and return to
  pulses and decisions.
