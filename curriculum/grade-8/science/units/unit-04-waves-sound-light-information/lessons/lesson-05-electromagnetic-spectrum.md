# Lesson 05 — The Electromagnetic Spectrum

**Goal (learner-facing):** I can put the electromagnetic spectrum
in order and explain why some kinds of light carry more energy
than others — and why light, unlike sound, needs no medium.

**Time:** 50 minutes · **Materials:** notebook, a prism or a
rainbow photo (adult-provided) · **Teacher key:**
[answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-05)

## Readiness check (5 min)

The adult asks: "Sunlight warms your skin, a radio plays music,
and an X-ray photograph shows bones. Are those three things the
same kind of wave or three different kinds?" Accept all answers;
today's answer is: one family, different frequencies.

## Explanation

Light is one member of a family called **electromagnetic waves**
(or electromagnetic radiation). Every member of the family is a
wave that can travel through empty space — that is the great
difference from sound. Sunlight crosses the vacuum between the
Sun and Earth precisely because light does not need matter to
travel through. (How a wave can travel with no medium is a deeper
question; this unit takes the fact as given and uses it.)

The family is ordered by **frequency** (and therefore by
wavelength: higher frequency means shorter wavelength, since the
speed is the same in a vacuum). From lowest frequency to highest:

**radio → microwave → infrared → visible → ultraviolet →
X-ray → gamma**

A few landmarks make the order memorable:

- **Radio waves** have the lowest frequencies and longest
  wavelengths. They carry radio, television, and many phone
  signals, and they pass through many everyday obstacles more
  easily than visible light does.
- **Microwaves** heat food by making water molecules vibrate;
  they are also used for some phone and satellite links.
- **Infrared** is felt as warmth — a heat lamp, a warm stove,
  your own skin all give off infrared. Remote controls use it.
- **Visible light** is the narrow slice our eyes detect: red has
  the lowest frequency (longest wavelength) in the visible slice,
  violet the highest (shortest). A rainbow shows the visible
  slice in order.
- **Ultraviolet** (UV) comes from the Sun beyond violet; it
  tans and burns skin, which is why sunscreen is a UV shield.
- **X-rays** pass through soft tissue but are absorbed more by
  bone, which is how a medical X-ray image works.
- **Gamma rays** have the highest frequencies and shortest
  wavelengths, and carry the most energy per wave; they come from
  radioactive materials and some cosmic events.

The energy rule is qualitative and simple: **higher frequency
means more energy carried by the wave.** A gamma ray carries far
more energy than a radio wave of the same size. That is also why
the high-frequency end of the spectrum needs more care: UV burns
skin, and X-rays and gamma rays can damage living tissue, so
their uses are controlled by adults and professionals. This unit
treats the spectrum descriptively: no graded item asks for a
wave-speed calculation with light, and no item asks for numbers
beyond the order and the energy comparison. (For reference only,
light in a vacuum travels at about 300,000,000 m/s — far faster
than any sound — but you will not be asked to compute with it.)

Two comparisons pull the unit together. First, sound vs. light:
sound is a mechanical, longitudinal wave that needs a medium and
travels about 343 m/s in air; light is an electromagnetic wave
that needs no medium and travels vastly faster. Second, color vs.
spectrum: the colors you see are just the visible slice, ordered
the same way — red lowest, violet highest — and an object's color
(Lesson 04) is decided by which slice it reflects.

**Vocabulary:** *electromagnetic wave* (a wave that can travel
through empty space), *electromagnetic spectrum* (the family of
such waves ordered by frequency), *infrared* (below red, felt as
heat), *ultraviolet* (beyond violet, tans and burns skin),
*visible light* (the slice human eyes detect).

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Put these in order from lowest to highest
frequency: X-ray, radio, infrared, visible. Lowest first: radio,
infrared, visible, X-ray. Check against the full order: radio
starts the list, infrared comes just before visible, and X-ray
sits near the high end, after ultraviolet.

**Example 2.** Which carries more energy, a microwave or an
ultraviolet wave? Ultraviolet: it sits higher in the spectrum
than microwave, and higher frequency means more energy. That is
also why ultraviolet can burn skin while the microwaves in a
kitchen oven (contained in the oven, and a different use) are
managed as a heating tool rather than a skin hazard — the point
for this lesson is the comparison, not the appliance.

## Guided practice

1. Write the full spectrum order from lowest to highest
   frequency without looking, then check it.
2. Which has the longer wavelength: a radio wave or a gamma ray?
   Which carries more energy? Give the rule you used.
3. A remote control uses infrared. Is infrared visible to human
   eyes? Is it above or below visible light in frequency?
4. Why can astronauts use radios to talk on the Moon but not
   their unaided voices? (One sentence using Lessons 03 and 05.)

## Independent practice

1. An X-ray and a visible-light wave are compared. Which has
   the higher frequency, the shorter wavelength, and the greater
   energy? (All three answers should agree.)
2. Sunscreen blocks ultraviolet. A learner says sunscreen should
   also block radio waves "because they are both invisible
   waves." What is wrong with that reasoning?
3. A rainbow shows red on the outside and violet on the inside.
   Which color in the rainbow has the higher frequency?
4. A learner claims: "Light is just like sound, only faster."
   Give two ways that claim is wrong (medium, and kind of wave).

## Applied task

A museum wants a display that is safe for visitors but shows
that invisible waves exist. Propose two exhibits: one using
infrared (for example, a warmth detector) and one using
ultraviolet (for example, a fluorescent poster that glows under
a UV lamp). For each, state what the visitor observes and what
it proves about that part of the spectrum. Adult supervision and
eye safety rules apply to any UV lamp use.

## Exit check

Which carries more energy: an infrared wave or an ultraviolet
wave? Which has the higher frequency? One sentence.

## Support, extension, teacher note

- **Reading/language access:** teach the order with the first
  letters as a chant (R-M-I-V-U-X-G) before asking for recall;
  the energy rule ("higher = more energy") stays on the board.
- **Alternate response mode:** learners may order seven labeled
  cards instead of writing the list.
- **Extension:** some animals see beyond our slice — bees detect
  ultraviolet patterns on flowers that humans cannot see. Learners
  who finish early can explain why "visible" means "visible to
  humans," not "all there is."
- **Teacher note:** do not let the spectrum become a vocabulary
  list: every item in practice asks for a comparison (order,
  energy, or medium), because comparison is what the assessment
  tests. Key: [answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-05).
