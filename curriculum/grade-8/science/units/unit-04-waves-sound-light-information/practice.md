# Practice — Unit 04: Waves, Sound, Light, and Information

Work in your notebook. Show every calculation with units. The
adult checks with the [answer key](answer-key.md); re-work any
missed item before moving on. Datasets in these sets are fictional
practice data unless a set says otherwise.

## Set A — Wave basics (Lessons 01–02)

1. A wave diagram (described in words): the distance from one
   crest to the next crest is 6 cm, and the height from the rest
   line to a crest is 2 cm. State the wavelength and the
   amplitude.
2. Classify each as transverse or longitudinal: (a) a plucked
   guitar string, (b) sound traveling through air. Give the
   direction test for each.
3. A hand shakes a rope 20 times. Where does a flag tied to the
   middle of the rope end up, and what does it do while the waves
   pass? Explain what traveled and what did not.
4. Two waves travel on identical ropes. Wave A has amplitude
   1 cm; wave B has amplitude 4 cm. Which carries more energy?
   How do you know?
5. Twelve waves pass a fixed point in 4 s. Find the frequency and
   the period.
6. A learner pushes a slinky end in and out, then shakes the same
   slinky up and down. Classify both waves and state the medium's
   motion direction in each.
7. A cork bobs on a pond as waves pass but stays in the same patch
   of water. Does the wave carry the cork? Explain.
8. **Error analysis.** A learner writes: "A wave carries matter
   from one place to another, like a truck carries boxes."
   Correct the sentence and give one observation that proves the
   correction.

## Set B — Wave math (Lesson 02)

1. A wave has frequency 3 Hz and wavelength 2 m. Find its speed.
2. A wave travels at 15 m/s with frequency 5 Hz. Find its
   wavelength.
3. A wave has wavelength 0.5 m and speed 4 m/s. Find its frequency
   and its period.
4. **Table (fictional practice data).** A wave travels at a fixed
   speed of 12 m/s. Complete the wavelengths: $f$ = 2 Hz → ?;
   $f$ = 3 Hz → ?; $f$ = 4 Hz → ?; $f$ = 6 Hz → ?. Then predict
   the wavelength at $f$ = 12 Hz and state the relationship
   between frequency and wavelength at fixed speed.
5. A wave has wavelength 50 cm and frequency 6 Hz. Find its speed
   in m/s. (Hint: convert centimeters to meters first.)
6. A wave's amplitude is doubled while its frequency and
   wavelength stay the same. What happens to its speed, and what
   happens to the energy it carries? Explain both.
7. Two waves travel in the same medium. Wave A: $f$ = 2 Hz,
   λ = 3 m. Wave B: $f$ = 6 Hz, λ = 1 m. Find both speeds. What
   does the result tell you about speed in a given medium?
8. Design a wave with speed 9 m/s and frequency 3 Hz. State its
   wavelength and period.

## Set C — Sound (Lesson 03)

Use 343 m/s for sound in air (at 20 °C), about 1,480 m/s in water,
and about 5,960 m/s in steel.

1. An echo returns 0.5 s after a clap. How far away is the
   reflecting wall?
2. Flash-to-thunder time is 9 s. Estimate the strike distance in
   meters, then in kilometers (rounded to one decimal place).
3. Rank air, water, and steel from slowest to fastest sound
   travel. Give one real-world consequence of this ranking.
4. Two tones: one at 880 Hz played softly, one at 220 Hz played
   loudly. Which has the higher pitch? Which is louder? Explain
   which property (frequency or amplitude) decides each answer.
5. Two astronauts stand on the Moon, face to face, with no radios.
   One shouts. Does the other hear it? Explain using the word
   "medium."
6. A sound travels 1,192 m. How long does it take through steel?
   How long through air? Give the air time to two decimal places.
7. A cup-and-string telephone works when the string is taut but
   barely works when the string is slack. Explain why, using
   vibrations and the medium.
8. A ship's sonar pulse returns 0.2 s after it is sent into water.
   How deep is the seafloor below the ship?

## Set D — Light and materials (Lesson 04)

1. Classify each as transparent, translucent, or opaque, or
   "mostly reflecting": (a) a clear windowpane, (b) frosted
   bathroom glass, (c) a brick wall, (d) a mirror.
2. A black shirt and a white shirt hang in the sun. Which warms
   faster? Explain with absorption and reflection.
3. A beam hits a mirror at 40° from the normal. At what angle
   does it leave, and on which side of the normal?
4. A blue object sits under white light, then under red-only
   light. Describe what it looks like in each case and explain
   both with reflection and absorption.
5. A greenhouse window should let light in to warm the plants.
   Should it be transparent, translucent, or opaque? Justify in
   one sentence.
6. An opaque object casts a sharp shadow; a translucent object
   casts a fuzzy, partial shadow. Explain both with transmission.
7. A straw in a glass of water looks bent at the water's surface.
   Name the effect and explain it in one sentence.
8. **Error analysis.** A learner writes: "Translucent means no
   light gets through." Correct the sentence and give one example
   that proves the correction.

## Set E — Spectrum and signals (Lessons 05–06)

1. Put these in order from lowest to highest frequency: gamma,
   visible, radio, microwave.
2. Which carries more energy: an ultraviolet wave or an infrared
   wave? Which has the higher frequency? Give the rule you used.
3. Astronauts on the Moon talk by radio, not by shouting. Explain
   why the radio works and the shouting does not, using the
   medium rule for sound and for electromagnetic waves.
4. An analog recording is copied three times, copy of a copy of a
   copy. Describe what happens to the hiss and explain why it
   cannot be removed at the end.
5. A pulse message ("short, long, short") is sent through a noisy
   room where someone is clapping softly. Why does the receiver
   usually still read it correctly?
6. A fiber-optic cable sends light pulses. Explain why the signal
   can travel far and be passed on without degrading the way a
   smooth analog signal degrades.
7. A digital photo is emailed from person to person five times.
   Why does the fifth copy look the same as the first?
8. **Error analysis.** A learner writes: "Digital signals are
   more reliable because computers count in binary." Rewrite the
   reason correctly using pulses, noise, and copying — without
   mentioning binary counting.

## Set F — Midyear retrieval and error analysis (Units 01–03, plus waves)

1. (Unit 01) A runner covers 120 m in 20 s at a steady pace. Find
   the speed.
2. (Unit 01) A distance–time graph rises from 0 m to 40 m in 10 s
   along a straight line. What is the slope, and what does it
   mean?
3. (Unit 02) A 10 N net force acts on a 2 kg object. Find its
   acceleration.
4. (Unit 03) A 2 kg object moves at 3 m/s. Find its kinetic energy.
5. (Unit 03) A worker pushes with 20 N for 5 m in the push
   direction, then holds the load still for 10 s. Find the work
   done in each part.
6. (Waves) A learner says amplitude and wavelength are the same
   thing: "both are how big the wave is." Correct the learner
   using the crest, the rest line, and the repeat length.
7. (Waves) A wave travels at 6 m/s with frequency 2 Hz. Find its
   wavelength.
8. (Waves) **Error analysis.** A learner writes: "Light needs air
   to travel, just like sound." Correct the sentence and give
   one piece of evidence (think: Sun and Earth).
