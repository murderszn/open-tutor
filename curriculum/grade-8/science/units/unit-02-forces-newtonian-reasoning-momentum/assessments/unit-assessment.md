# Unit Assessment — Forces, Newtonian Reasoning, and Momentum

**Unit 02** · 50 minutes · closed notes · **Answer key:**
[answer key](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment)

*Matched to the unit's five objectives. Open-response items are scored
with the rubric in the [teacher guide](../teacher-guide.md).*

## Part A — Forces and diagrams (Objectives 1–2)

1. Define **net force** in your own words (one sentence).
2. A dog pulls a sled with 120 N forward; friction pulls 85 N
   backward. (a) Net force and direction? (b) Balanced or unbalanced?
   (c) What happens to the sled's motion?
3. Draw a labeled free-body diagram for a 10 N box sliding to the
   right at a *steady* speed while someone pushes it right with 6 N.
   (Name all four arrows.)
4. A car rounds a curve at a steady 20 m/s. A cup of water sits on the
   dashboard. Using Newton's first law, predict what the water's
   surface does in the curve — and why.

## Part B — The second law (Objective 3)

5. A 5 kg wagon feels a net force of 15 N. Acceleration?
6. What net force accelerates a 0.4 kg ball at 25 m/s²?
7. A 12 N net force acts on an unknown mass and produces an
   acceleration of 3 m/s². What is the mass?
8. **Proportional reasoning:** Cart A (2 kg) and Cart B (6 kg) receive
   the *same* net push. (a) How do their accelerations compare — give
   the ratio. (b) Explain the ratio with the second law in two
   sentences.

## Part C — Investigation and the third law (Objectives 4–5)

9. You plan a ramp investigation with three cart masses. Name **two**
   variables you must control, and explain what goes wrong if you
   change the ramp height halfway through.
10. A skateboarder pushes backward on the ground with her foot. Name
    the action–reaction pair, and explain why the two forces don't
    cancel out.
11. Two balls: X is 0.6 kg moving at 3 m/s; Y is 0.2 kg moving at
    9 m/s. (a) Compute both momenta. (b) Which would shove a foam
    block farther in a head-on hit — or is it a tie? Justify.

## Part D — Gravity and design (Objective 5)

12. Give **one** observation supporting "gravity is attractive" and
    **one** supporting "gravity depends on mass."
13. An astronaut (mass 80 kg) weighs about 784 N on Earth and about
    128 N on the Moon. (a) What is their mass on the Moon? (b) Explain
    the weight difference in mass-vs-weight language (two sentences).
14. **Design argument (open response, half page):** Your crash-test
    data shows the foam-bumper cart left its egg passenger intact in
    all 3 trials, while the rigid-bumper cart cracked its passenger
    twice. (a) State your claim about which design feature works.
    (b) Cite the data. (c) Explain the result using Newton's third law
    *and* the stopping-time idea. (d) Name one improvement you would
    test next and what you would measure.

**Scoring:** Parts A–C: 2 points per item (28 points). Part D item 14:
8 points (2 per subpart). Total 40 points. Grade bands and re-teach
triggers: [teacher guide](../teacher-guide.md#assessment-scoring).
