# Lesson 01 — Forces: Pushes, Pulls, Balanced, Unbalanced

**Goal (learner-facing):** I can name forces as pushes or pulls with a
direction and a size, add up force sets to find the net force, and decide
whether forces are balanced (motion stays) or unbalanced (motion
changes).

**Time:** 50 minutes · **Materials:** notebook, index cards, a book, a
rubber band · **Teacher key:** [answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-01)

## Readiness check (5 min)

The adult points to a book sitting on the table: "Is anything happening
to this book?" (Nothing visible.) "Is anything pushing or pulling on
it?" Accept all answers — the point: forces can act even when nothing
moves.

## Explanation

A **force** is a push or a pull on an object. Every force has two
parts: a **direction** (which way) and a **size** (how strong). We
measure force size in **newtons (N)**. A newton is a smallish force —
about the pull of gravity on a medium apple.

Forces come in pairs of directions: push a door *away from you*, pull
it *toward you*. Gravity always pulls *downward* (toward Earth's
center). Friction pushes *against* sliding. A stretched rubber band
pulls back toward its resting length.

Most objects feel several forces at once. The **net force** is what you
get when you combine them — add forces in the same direction, subtract
forces in opposite directions:

- **Balanced forces** — the forces add to zero net force. Nothing about
  the motion changes: a resting object stays at rest; a moving object
  keeps moving at a steady speed in a straight line.
- **Unbalanced forces** — the forces add to a nonzero net force. The
  motion changes: the object speeds up, slows down, or changes
  direction. The change happens in the direction of the net force.

The book on the table feels Earth's gravity pulling down (about 5 N
for a 0.5 kg book) *and* the table pushing up with exactly 5 N.
Equal and opposite → balanced → the book sits still. Friction is why a
sliding box stops: the push you gave it is gone, and friction's pull
against the motion is now the unbalanced force that slows it down.

**Vocabulary:** *force* (a push or pull, size in newtons), *net force*
(what remains after combining all forces), *balanced forces* (net force
zero; motion unchanged), *unbalanced forces* (net force nonzero; motion
changes).

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Two teams tug a rope. Team A pulls left with 300 N; Team B
pulls right with 300 N. Opposite directions, so subtract:
300 N − 300 N = 0 N. **Net force = 0 → balanced → the rope doesn't
move.** Equal, opposite forces cancel.

**Example 2.** One more person joins Team B and pulls right with 80 N.
Rightward total: 300 + 80 = 380 N. Leftward: 300 N. Net:
380 − 300 = **80 N to the right**. Unbalanced → the rope accelerates
toward Team B. *The change follows the net force's direction.*

**Example 3.** A box sits on the floor. You push it right with 25 N;
friction pushes left with 25 N. Net = 25 − 25 = 0 N → balanced → the
box stays put. You push harder: 40 N right; friction still 25 N left.
Net = 40 − 25 = **15 N to the right** → unbalanced → the box starts
sliding right. *Friction didn't vanish — it was outmatched.*

## Guided practice

1. A dog pulls its leash forward with 60 N; the owner pulls back with
   45 N. Net force? Direction? What happens to the dog's motion?
   *(Hint: forward minus backward.)*
2. A soccer ball flies through the air. Name two forces acting on it
   and their directions.
3. **Spot the misconception:** "The book sits still, so no forces act on
   it." What's wrong with this claim? *(Hint: gravity.)*

## Independent practice

1. Pushes: (a) 12 N east, 7 N east; (b) 20 N north, 20 N south;
   (c) 50 N right, 30 N left. Find each net force with direction, and
   say balanced or unbalanced.
2. A sled weighs 400 N (gravity pulls down 400 N). The snow pushes up
   400 N. A child pulls the sled forward with 90 N; friction pulls back
   60 N. (a) What's the vertical net force? (b) What's the horizontal
   net force? (c) Does the sled speed up, slow down, or hold steady?
3. **Explain your reasoning:** A car cruises at a steady 25 m/s. Is the
   net force on it zero or nonzero? What does that say about the engine
   force vs. air resistance + rolling friction?

## Application

**Hallway push census (15 min):** Walk through one room and list five
pushes/pulls you notice (a door swinging, a backpack strap, your feet on
the floor…). For each, name the direction and guess whether it is
balanced or unbalanced *for the object being pushed*. Share two with
the adult and defend your balanced/unbalanced call with the net-force
idea.

## Exit check

A 10 N wind pushes a kite string north; a 6 N hand pulls the string
south. Net force? Direction? Balanced or unbalanced — and what does
that mean for the kite's motion?

## Supports

- **Reading/language:** keep a running "force word wall" (push, pull,
  gravity, friction, net) with a sketch per word; read each worked
  example aloud with the adult before writing.
- **Alternate response mode:** act out force pairs with a partner — one
  pushes, one resists — and call out "balanced!" or "unbalanced!" from
  how the motion changes.
- **Extension:** find the two forces on a hanging picture frame and
  explain why a second nail changes nothing about the balance (the
  upward force just splits between two hooks).

## Teacher note

The durable misconception is "still = no forces." Keep returning to
the book-on-table pair: gravity down, table up, equal, balanced. Another
common slip: treating friction as "not a force" — it is a real push
against sliding, and it belongs in every net-force sum. See the
[answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-01) for worked solutions.
