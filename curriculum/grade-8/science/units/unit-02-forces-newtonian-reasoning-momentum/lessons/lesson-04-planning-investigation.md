# Lesson 04 — Planning the Force-and-Motion Investigation

**Goal (learner-facing):** I can write a testable question, name the
variables I will control, and plan repeated trials so my data can
actually answer the question.

**Time:** 50 minutes · **Materials:** notebook, the lab materials list
below, index cards · **Teacher key:**
[answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-04)

## Readiness check (5 min)

The adult asks: "I timed one cart rolling down a ramp once — 2.3
seconds. What can I conclude?" The class should push back: one trial
proves nothing; nothing was controlled; there is no comparison. This
lesson fixes all three.

## Explanation

A good investigation answers one clear question with *fair* data. The
plan has four parts:

1. **The question** names the cause you change and the effect you
   measure. "How does the cart's **mass** change how quickly it speeds
   up down the ramp?" — mass is the **changed variable**; "how
   quickly it speeds up" (measured as travel time over a fixed
   distance, then converted to a speed comparison) is the **measured
   variable**. Everything else is a **controlled variable**: same ramp
   height, same release point, same cart, same track surface, same
   timer, same person timing.
2. **Trials and repeats.** Three trials per condition, minimum.
   Outliers get a fourth run. Averages smooth out timing wobbles.
3. **The data table, drawn before touching the ramp.** Columns labeled
   with units; one row per trial; space for the average. If the table
   doesn't exist, the data ends up on scraps and dies there.
4. **The claim plan.** Decide *now* what patterns would count as
   evidence: "If heavier carts take longer, mass slows the speedup; if
   times tie, mass doesn't matter here." Writing this before the data
   keeps the conclusion honest.

**Our unit investigation** (Sessions 8–9; adapted from the track's
verified legacy assignment
[`newtons-laws-investigation.md`](../../../../stem/assignments/newtons-laws-investigation.md),
re-verified for grade-8 use in this unit):

- **Question:** How does the cart's mass (or, in the alternate arm,
  the ramp height — the pushing force) change how quickly it covers a
  fixed 1-meter run?
- **Setup:** smooth board 1–1.5 m as a ramp, toy car/cart, washers or
  coins as added mass, kitchen scale (grams → kilograms), meter
  stick, masking-tape start/finish lines, stopwatch, target area at
  the bottom kept clear.
- **Conditions:** (a) empty cart; (b) cart + 100 g; (c) cart + 200 g —
  OR — ramp low / medium / high with the cart unchanged. The adult
  picks one arm per learner.
- **Measure:** release from rest at the tape line; time from release to
  the finish line; 3 trials per condition; average the times. Compare
  average speeds (distance ÷ time) across conditions.
- **Claim:** write one sentence linking the pattern to the second law
  ("times stayed nearly equal, so the extra mass didn't change the
  speedup much — consistent with $a = F/m$ when the downhill force also
  grows with mass") or to friction where the data points there.

**Safety box (adult reads aloud before Session 8):** the ramp sits on a
clear floor or table — **never aimed at people, pets, stairs, or
breakables**; the adult places the ramp and the target area; carts roll,
they are never thrown; stop the session if anything tips or rolls away.
**Observation alternative:** a learner who cannot run the physical ramp
takes the timer/reader role, or runs the whole investigation as a
simulation study with the PhET "Forces and Motion: Basics" sim
(resource pack) — same question, same table, same claim format.

**Vocabulary:** *investigation* (planned test of a question),
*changed variable*, *measured variable*, *controlled variable*,
*trial*, *outlier*.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Weak question: "Do ramps matter?" Strong question: "How
does raising the ramp from 10 cm to 20 cm change the cart's travel time
over 1 m?" *The strong version names the change, the size of the
change, and the measurement.*

**Example 2.** A learner plans: "I'll time the cart with no washers,
then with 5 washers, then with 10 washers — same ramp, same release
spot, same timer, 3 runs each." Controlled variables named: ramp
height, release point, cart, surface, timer, releaser. Changed: added
mass. Measured: travel time (→ speed). **This plan can answer its
question;** a plan that also swaps the cart mid-way cannot.

**Example 3.** Data so far: no washers — 1.8, 1.9, 1.8 s; 5 washers —
1.8, 1.9, 1.8 s; 10 washers — 1.9, 1.8, 1.9 s. Claim draft: "Adding
mass did not change the travel time much (averages 1.83, 1.83,
1.87 s), so mass alone didn't change how quickly the cart sped up on
this ramp." *Honest claims describe the pattern first, then the
interpretation — never the reverse.*

## Guided practice

1. Improve this question: "Is friction real?" Rewrite it as a testable
   question with a changed and a measured variable.
2. List the controlled variables for the ramp investigation above.
   What goes wrong if the releaser changes between conditions?
3. Draw the blank data table for 3 conditions × 3 trials, with units
   in the headers.

## Independent practice

1. Write the full plan for the *ramp-height arm*: question, changed /
   measured / controlled variables, number of trials, and the blank
   table.
2. A learner reports: "Trial 1: 1.5 s, Trial 2: 1.6 s, Trial 3: 3.1 s."
   What should they do about trial 3, and why? What is the average of
   the kept trials?
3. **Explain your reasoning:** why does the plan forbid changing the
   cart *and* the added mass in the same investigation? Use the word
   "confound" correctly or explain the idea in your own words.

## Application

**Plan review (15 min):** Swap plans with a partner (or read yours to
the adult). The reviewer hunts for three flaws: an uncontrolled
variable, a missing unit, a vague claim plan. Mark them, fix them, and
sign off — a plan nobody else can run is a plan that isn't finished.

## Exit check

In two or three sentences: what is your investigation's question, what
are you changing, what are you measuring, and what will you keep the
same?

## Supports

- **Reading/language:** a plan template with labeled blanks (Question /
  Change / Measure / Keep same / Trials / Table) — fill, don't invent.
- **Alternate response mode:** dictate the plan to the adult, who
  scribes; the learner's job is the *decisions*, not the handwriting.
- **Extension:** add a prediction with numbers: "I predict the 200 g
  condition averages within 0.2 s of the empty cart, because…" —
  Sessions 8–9 will grade the prediction.

## Teacher note

The classic failure is a "plan" that is really a materials list. Insist
on the question first; no ramp gets built in Session 7. Also watch the
temptation to *change the ramp to make the data nicer* mid-run — that
is data fabrication, and naming it plainly now prevents it later. See
the [answer key](../answer-key.md#lesson-04).
