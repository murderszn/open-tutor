# Unit 02 — Forces, Newtonian Reasoning, and Momentum

**Grade:** 8 · **Subject:** Science (Physical Science pathway) · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #39](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/39), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A push or a pull is the beginning of every motion story. In this unit the
learner names forces by direction and size, separates **balanced** forces
(nothing changes) from **unbalanced** forces (motion changes), and draws
free-body diagrams to make the force picture visible. Newton's first law
turns "objects keep doing what they're doing" into a prediction tool;
Newton's second law turns it into arithmetic — $F = ma$ with proportional
reasoning ("double the force, double the acceleration; double the mass,
half the acceleration"). Then the unit puts the learner in charge: they
plan and run a controlled force-and-motion investigation, use Newton's
third law and the idea of momentum to explain collisions, argue from
evidence that gravity is an attractive, mass-dependent force, and finish
by designing, testing, and improving a real crash-protection device with
measured results.

## Essential question

How do pushes and pulls decide how things move — and how do we design for
the crashes we can't avoid?

## Prerequisites

From Unit 01 and the diagnostic weeks:

- SI units for length, mass, time; reading labeled tables; recording data
  with units and repeating trials
- Speed as distance ÷ time; reading distance–time graphs (slope = speed)
- Arithmetic with decimals and fractions; solving a one-step formula for
  an unknown; proportional reasoning at the "twice the mass" level
- Writing a one-sentence claim with one reason (claim–evidence–reasoning)
- Safe habits for adult-supervised household-scale investigations

New content here: force, net force, free-body diagrams, inertia, $F = ma$,
action–reaction pairs, momentum, gravitational interaction, and
engineering test-data analysis. Nothing here assumes Unit 03's energy
ideas — the collision work stays in force and momentum language.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Describe forces as pushes or pulls with direction and size; decide
   whether a force set is balanced or unbalanced and predict whether the
   object's motion will change.
2. Draw and read free-body diagrams with labeled force arrows; apply
   Newton's first law to predict inertia behavior in familiar situations
   (e.g., a passenger leaning at a sudden stop).
3. Calculate force, mass, or acceleration from the other two using
   $F = ma$, and reason proportionally: predict how doubling the net
   force or the mass changes the acceleration, with a written
   justification.
4. Plan and carry out a controlled force-and-motion investigation: name
   the question and the controlled variables, repeat trials, record data
   in labeled tables, and write a claim supported by the data about how
   the sum of forces and the mass affect changes in motion.
5. Apply Newton's third law to a collision-protection design: build, test,
   record results, analyze the data to identify the best design features,
   and improve once; compare momentum ($p = mv$) of movers; argue with
   evidence that gravitational interactions are attractive and depend on
   the masses involved.

## Materials

Notebook, pencils, index cards for vocabulary, graph paper or printed
grids, a stopwatch, a kitchen or postal scale (grams), masking tape, a
meter stick or tape measure, a toy car or low-friction cart, books or
blocks to raise a ramp, a smooth board or cookie sheet (1–1.5 m), a
target block (wooden block or paper cup) for collision tests, washers or
coins for added mass, and the crash-design kit: a raw egg (or a
clay-ball "passenger" as a no-mess alternative), tape, straws, cotton
balls, foam scraps, paper, and rubber bands — the adult assembles the
kit and sets the exact material budget. Printed or on-screen copy of
[`assets/crash-test-bumper-designs.png`](assets/crash-test-bumper-designs.png).
Adult-supervised data collection happens in a safe open area — no racing
toward walls at speed by people, no throwing objects; see the project's
safety box. Digital option: a spreadsheet app for data tables; the free
PhET "Forces and Motion: Basics" simulation (resource pack) as an
observation alternative for the investigation.

## Accessibility and supports

All lessons include reading/language supports, an alternate response
mode, and extensions (see each lesson and the
[teacher guide](teacher-guide.md)). Force arrows can be felt as physical
pushes on a learner's hand (adult-guided) or drawn with raised-line
arrows; free-body diagrams can be built with cut-paper arrows on a board
instead of drawn. The force lab has a timer/reader role and a full
observation-only alternative via the PhET simulation. The crash-design
project offers a clay-ball passenger alternative to the egg and a
verbal-presentation alternative to the written report.
