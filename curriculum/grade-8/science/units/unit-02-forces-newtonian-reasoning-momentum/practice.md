# Practice Sessions — Unit 02: Forces, Newtonian Reasoning, Momentum

Each session is a 50-minute block: 10 minutes of retrieval warm-up
(mixed review of earlier sessions), 30 minutes on the session tasks
below, and 10 minutes of adult feedback the same day. Solutions and
scoring notes: [answer key](answer-key.md#practice-sessions).

## Session A — Net force from force lists

*Retrieval:* define force, net force, balanced, unbalanced — in your
own words, one line each.

1. Find the net force (size + direction) and say balanced or
   unbalanced: (a) 15 N east, 15 N west; (b) 40 N up, 25 N down;
   (c) 100 N south, 60 N south, 30 N north.
2. A box: push 35 N right, friction 20 N left, gravity 50 N down,
   floor 50 N up. (a) Horizontal net? (b) Vertical net? (c) What
   happens to the box's motion — and which net force decides it?
3. Draw two FBDs for a book on a table: one with correct equal-length
   arrows, one with the "up" arrow visibly shorter. Under each, write
   the predicted motion and say which diagram obeys the balanced-force
   rule.
4. A tug-of-war: left team 500 N, right team 620 N. Net force? Which
   way does the center flag move, and what does that tell you about the
   teams' forces?
5. **Explain your reasoning:** a parked car on a flat street. List the
   vertical forces and the horizontal forces, and explain why *both*
   sets must be balanced for it to stay parked.

## Session B — Free-body diagrams and inertia

*Retrieval:* sketch the four FBD rules from Lesson 2 from memory.

1. Draw and label FBDs: (a) a soccer ball rolling to a stop on grass
   (include friction); (b) a book being carried at a steady walking
   pace; (c) a skydiver falling at terminal (steady) velocity.
2. For each FBD in (1), state the net force and the first-law
   prediction for the next second of motion.
3. A bus accelerates forward from a stop. An air freshener hangs from the
   rearview mirror — which way does it swing relative to the bus, and why?
   *(Hint: what does its inertia want it to keep doing?)*
4. **Error analysis:** "The FBD of a book on a table needs three
   arrows: gravity down, table up, and the book's push down on the
   table." Which arrow breaks a diagram rule, and why must it go?
5. Rank by inertia (least → most): a bicycle, a loaded school bus, a
   bowling ball, a feather. Justify the order in one sentence using
   the mass–inertia link.

## Session C — F = ma calculations

*Retrieval:* write $F_{net} = ma$ and its two rearrangements from
memory.

1. Find the missing value (show the formula step):
   (a) $F = 24$ N, $m = 6$ kg → $a$?
   (b) $m = 0.5$ kg, $a = 8$ m/s² → $F$?
   (c) $F = 100$ N, $a = 2$ m/s² → $m$?
   (d) $F = 45$ N, $m = 900$ g → $a$? *(Convert first.)*
2. A 3 kg cart and a 6 kg cart get the same 12 N net push. Find both
   accelerations. What proportional rule does the comparison show?
3. A 50 kg rider on a 10 kg bike (total 60 kg) accelerates at
   1.5 m/s². Net force? If the net force rises to 135 N, what is the
   new acceleration?
4. A 2 kg block: push 30 N forward, friction 12 N backward.
   (a) Net force? (b) Acceleration? (c) If friction were magically
   removed but the push stayed 30 N, what would the acceleration be —
   and what does that say about which force "counts" in $F = ma$?
5. **Explain your reasoning:** two identical cars, one empty (1200 kg)
   and one loaded (1800 kg), brake with the *same* net braking force.
   Which stops from the same speed in a shorter distance? Justify with
   $a = F/m$ — no numbers needed beyond the ratio.

## Session D — Action–reaction and momentum

*Retrieval:* state Newton's third law in one sentence; write $p = mv$.

1. Name the action–reaction pair (both forces, both objects): (a) a
   swimmer's hands stroke backward through water; (b) a car tire grips
   the road during acceleration; (c) a balloon deflates and flies.
2. Compute $p$ with units: (a) 0.2 kg marble at 6 m/s; (b) 80 kg
   runner at 3 m/s; (c) 2000 kg truck at 15 m/s.
3. Cart P: 1 kg at 4 m/s. Cart Q: 4 kg at 1 m/s. (a) Which has more
   momentum? (b) Each hits an identical foam block. Predict which
   block slides farther — or if it's a tie — and why.
4. Two skaters push off each other: 45 kg and 60 kg, same-size push.
   Who ends up moving faster, and which two laws explain it? (Name
   both.)
5. **Explain your reasoning:** in a head-on crash test, engineers
   would rather the car crumple than stay rigid — even though both
   cars lose the same momentum. Explain the preference using stopping
   time and peak force.

## Session E — Gravity arguments

*Retrieval:* state the two gravity patterns (attractive;
mass-dependent) with one observation each.

1. A 5 kg bowling ball and a 0.05 kg tennis ball are dropped together
   in a vacuum chamber. (a) Which lands first? (b) Which feels the
   bigger gravitational force — by what factor? (c) Why don't (a) and
   (b) contradict each other?
2. The Moon's surface gravity is about 1/6 of Earth's. A 72 kg
   astronaut: (a) mass on the Moon? (b) weight on Earth (~705 N given)?
   weight on the Moon? (c) Explain the difference between your answers
   to (a) and (b) in mass-vs-weight language.
3. **Argue from evidence:** your younger cousin says "gravity pushes
   things down like a giant hand." Write a 3–4 sentence correction
   using the attraction idea and one piece of evidence.
4. Spring tides happen when the Sun and Moon line up with Earth; neap
   tides when they form a right angle. Using mass-dependence (and the
   fact that the Sun's pull also reaches Earth), explain why the lineup
   makes higher tides.
5. **Error analysis:** "The space station has no gravity, that's why
   things float." Give the two-sentence correction from Lesson 6.
