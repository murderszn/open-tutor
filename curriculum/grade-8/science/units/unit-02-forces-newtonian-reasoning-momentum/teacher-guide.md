# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Forces, Newtonian Reasoning, and Momentum

**For the guiding adult.** Student pages never link here except where
lessons explicitly say "teacher key." Keep this guide and the
[answer key](answer-key.md) out of the learner's hands.

## How the unit fits

Unit 01 built measurement and graphing; this unit adds the *causes* of
motion changes. Lessons 1–3 establish the force language (net force →
diagrams → $F = ma$). Lesson 4 + Sessions 8–9 run the MS-PS2-2
investigation. Lessons 5–6 add the third law, momentum, and gravity.
Sessions 14–15 + 18 run the MS-PS2-1 / MS-ETS1-3 crash-design
challenge. Nothing here needs energy ideas — if the learner reaches
for "energy" language in the design argument, accept it as enrichment
but score the force/momentum explanation.

## Per-lesson teaching notes

**L1 — Forces.** Demo the book-on-table pair physically: press down on
the book and ask what pushes back (their hand feels the table's push
through the book). Misconception to kill early: "still = no forces."
Keep the newton concrete (an apple's weight) all unit.

**L2 — FBDs and the first law.** Grade the *arrows*, not the art:
right forces, right directions, honest relative lengths, labels. The
two-objects rule ("arrows are forces *on the dot*") needs repeating in
every later lesson — preview that Lesson 5 lives or dies on it.

**L3 — $F = ma$.** Insist on the net-force-first habit: ask "what's
the net?" before any calculation. Drill the gram→kilogram conversion
(500 g = 0.5 kg) — it is the unit's most common arithmetic error.
The ramp prediction (independent practice 3) is the lab hypothesis;
refer back to it in Session 9.

**L4 — Investigation planning.** No building in Session 7 — plans
only, reviewed and signed off. Watch for "changing the ramp to make
data nicer" and name it as fabrication plainly. Confirm the adult's
choice of arm (mass vs. ramp height) before Session 8.

**L5 — Third law and momentum.** Side-by-side FBDs of the two
colliding objects is the cure for "they cancel." Keep momentum
qualitative-comparative; the numbers are simple products, and the
*comparison* is the skill. The crumple-zone idea is qualitative here
(longer stop → smaller peak force); do not introduce impulse
formalism.

**L6 — Gravity.** The hammer–feather story does the heavy lifting;
tell it well. Keep mass (kg) vs. weight (N) units visible on the
board all lesson. The space-station correction is the payoff — let the
learner argue it out before confirming.

## Misconception watchlist

| Misconception | Where it surfaces | Counter |
|---|---|---|
| Still = no forces | L1, practice A | Book on table: gravity down, table up, equal, balanced |
| Motion needs a continuing push | L2, quiz 4 | Steady-speed box: balanced forces, zero net — *change* needs force |
| FBD includes forces the object exerts | L2, practice B4 | "Arrows are forces *on the dot*" |
| Plug a gross push into $F = ma$ | L3, practice C4 | Net-force-first: subtract friction before calculating |
| Grams into a kilogram formula | L3, C1d | Convert first, every time |
| Action–reaction cancel | L5, practice D | Different objects — draw both FBDs side by side |
| Momentum = speed | L5, D independent 4 | Demand the mass in every momentum sentence |
| Heavy falls faster (in general) | L6, practice E | Vacuum evidence; air is the confound |
| No gravity at the space station | L6, E5 | ~90% of Earth's gravity; free-fall orbit |

## Re-teach triggers (Session 17)

- Quiz < 14/20 → re-teach the lowest objective: L1/L2 reteach = more
  FBD drills with physical push/pull demos; L3 = the three-line
  template (Know/Find/Rule) on 4 fresh numbers; L5 = balloon-rocket
  demos + pair-naming drills; L6 = evidence-bank argument rebuild.
- Lab data missing or fabricated → the report is redone as a
  simulation study (PhET) rather than excused; the honesty gate in the
  rubric stands.
- If $F = ma$ rearrangements are the blocker, allow the "triangle
  cover-up" mnemonic as scaffolding, then fade it by the unit
  assessment.

## Project scoring

Rubrics are in [project.md](project.md). Notes:

- **Lab honesty gate:** fabricated or "tidied" data caps the report at
  6/12. Distinguish honest outliers (kept, labeled, re-run) from
  fabrication — praise the former explicitly.
- **Design challenge:** score the *argument from data*, not the
  prettiest bumper. A design that failed all 3 trials can still score
  3/4 on argument if the learner correctly diagnoses why (e.g., "the
  bumper was too stiff — it didn't crush, so the stop was short and
  the peak force cracked the egg").
- **MS-ETS1-3 evidence:** look for the explicit cross-design
  comparison ("Maya's foam crushed 2 cm and survived; ours didn't
  crush and cracked — so crush distance is the feature to copy").
  Without it, cap data analysis at 3/4.

## Assessment scoring

- **Formative quiz (20 pts):** 14+ = on track. 10–13 = targeted
  re-teach in Session 17. Below 10 = re-teach plus a second attempt at
  the missed items before the unit assessment.
- **Unit assessment (40 pts):** 32+ = mastery; 24–31 = proficient
  with the noted gaps retaught in Unit 03's first week; below 24 =
  full re-teach of the weakest objective before Unit 03, using the
  triggers above. Item 14 (design argument): 2 pts claim, 2 pts data
  citation, 2 pts physics explanation (third law + stopping time
  both required for full credit), 2 pts next-test proposal.

## Accessibility and differentiation

- **Reading:** all lessons have a word wall and sentence frames; the
  evidence bank (L6) and plan template (L4) reduce recall load.
- **Response:** FBDs may be built with cut-paper arrows; arguments may
  be spoken; the design report may be a narrated photo sequence.
- **Extension paths:** the air-resistance extension (L3), the
  balloon-rocket demo write-up (L5), the Mars-weight computation (L6),
  and a "design a second iteration with a mass budget" challenge in
  the flex session.

## Answer key use

The [answer key](answer-key.md) solves every practice, quiz, and
assessment item independently. If the learner's valid alternative
differs (e.g., a different-but-correct FBD arrow set), score the
reasoning, not the match. Open responses accept any defensible
claim–evidence–reasoning chain that cites the unit's data or evidence
bank.
