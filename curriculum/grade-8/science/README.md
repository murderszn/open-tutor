## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-8 **physical science** course — motion,
forces, energy, waves, electricity and magnetism, atoms, chemical reactions,
and thermal energy, with an engineering-design capstone. Each of the eight
units runs four weeks at about five 50-minute sessions per week, blending
direct explanation with adult-supervised household-scale investigations, data
graphing and analysis, mathematical reasoning proportional to mass and speed,
and independent written practice that the adult reviews the same day.

This is a proposed pathway, not a universal requirement: some schools place
life or earth science in 8th grade instead. A guiding adult checks placement
before starting.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Measure, record, and graph physical quantities with correct SI units;
   calculate speed and interpret distance–time and velocity–time graphs,
   including what slope and area represent.
2. Plan and carry out an investigation showing that a change in an object's
   motion depends on the sum of the forces acting on it and on its mass;
   distinguish balanced from unbalanced forces and state Newton's three laws
   in their own words.
3. Apply Newton's third law to design a solution to a collision problem;
   define momentum and explain why momentum changes are equal and opposite
   for the two colliding objects.
4. Construct and present an argument, with evidence, that gravitational
   interactions are attractive and depend on the masses of the interacting
   objects.
5. Ask questions about data to determine what affects the strength of
   electric and magnetic forces; design and explain a simple electromagnet
   and an electric circuit.
6. Construct and interpret graphical displays of data to describe how
   kinetic energy relates to mass and to speed separately; compare stored
   (potential) energy for objects in different positions or arrangements.
7. Track energy transfers through a system — including energy lost to
   friction or air as thermal energy — and argue, with evidence, that total
   energy is conserved even as kinetic energy changes.
8. Use mathematical representations to describe a wave model: relate
   amplitude to energy carried, and describe waves in terms of wavelength,
   frequency, and amplitude.
9. Develop and use a model showing that waves are reflected, absorbed, or
   transmitted by different materials, for both light and mechanical waves.
10. Support, with qualitative scientific and technical information, the claim
    that digitized signals are a more reliable way to encode and transmit
    information than analog signals.
11. Develop models describing the atomic composition of simple molecules and
    extended structures using the proton–neutron–electron picture; explain
    how the periodic table's patterns connect to element behavior.
12. Analyze and interpret data on substance properties before and after an
    interaction to decide whether a chemical reaction occurred; classify
    reactions into the five major types; balance simple equations.
13. Develop and use a model showing that the total number of atoms — and
    therefore mass — is conserved in a chemical reaction.
14. Apply scientific principles to design, test, and modify a device that
    minimizes or maximizes thermal energy transfer (or that releases/absorbs
    thermal energy by chemical processes), working within defined criteria
    and constraints.
15. Define an engineering problem's criteria and constraints precisely,
    evaluate competing design solutions systematically, and use test data to
    improve a design.

## Standards reference

Objectives reference the Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS) middle-band
(grades 6–8) performance expectations, verified 2026-10-06 against official
NGSS text transcriptions (see sources in scope-and-sequence). Codes below are
reference points only — **not a claim of state adoption, accreditation, or
alignment certification**. Full crosswalk in
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md#4-standards-crosswalk):

- Matter and Its Interactions — MS-PS1-1 through MS-PS1-6
- Motion and Stability: Forces and Interactions — MS-PS2-1 through MS-PS2-5
- Energy — MS-PS3-1 through MS-PS3-5
- Waves and Their Applications in Technologies for Information Transfer —
  MS-PS4-1 through MS-PS4-3
- Engineering Design (secondary in MS-PS1-6 and the capstone) — MS-ETS1-1
  through MS-ETS1-4
