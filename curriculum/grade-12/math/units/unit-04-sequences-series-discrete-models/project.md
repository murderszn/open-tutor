# Project — Discrete-Model Portfolio

**Deliverable (due Session 20, presented Session 19):** a short
written portfolio (4–6 pages or the equivalent) plus a one-page
summary table. Work individually. All data must be either real
public data with a source, or clearly labeled fictional practice
data. Money contexts are educational models, not financial advice;
medical contexts, if chosen, are mathematical models, not medical
advice.

## Task

Choose one discrete situation that changes step by step. Examples:
a savings plan with growing deposits; a loan balance with interest
and payments; a bouncing ball; a population with a constant
percentage change; a game with repeated rounds; a stack, queue, or
repeating process from everyday life. (Do not choose a situation
that requires private or personal data.)

Your portfolio must contain:

1. **The situation, in words** — what changes, what one step
   means, and what the index `n` counts. State your indexing
   convention (`a_1` is the first value) and every assumption.
2. **Two models of the same sequence** — a recursive rule and an
   explicit formula — with a translation between them and a check
   at `n = 1` and `n = 2`.
3. **A sum** — a finite sum (arithmetic or geometric, with its
   derivation sketched) or, if your situation genuinely runs
   without end, an infinite sum with a convergence argument using
   partial sums. State what the sum means in the situation.
4. **A proof** — one statement about your model proved by
   mathematical induction (for example, a sum formula or a
   divisibility/inequality claim about your sequence), written in
   base-step / inductive-step form.
5. **A binomial connection (short)** — one place where Pascal's
   Triangle or a binomial coefficient counts something in or near
   your situation (for example, counting paths, choices, or round
   outcomes), or a clear explanation of why no binomial connection
   fits and what counting tool fits instead.
6. **Limitations** — at least two ways the model could fail in
   reality, and what data would test it.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | 10 — Strong | 7 — Adequate | 4 — Partial | 0–2 — Missing/unsound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Situation & assumptions (10) | Step, index, and all assumptions stated precisely; units named | Mostly clear; one assumption unstated | Vague step or index; assumptions missing | No usable description |
| Two models + translation (10) | Recursive and explicit forms correct, translated, checked at `n = 1, 2` | Correct forms; translation or check thin | One form wrong or untranslated | Forms missing or wrong |
| Sum with meaning (10) | Correct formula/derivation; convergence argued where needed; meaning stated | Correct value; derivation or meaning thin | Value wrong or meaning absent | No sum |
| Proof + binomial + limitations (10) | Induction proof complete and correct; binomial connection apt; ≥2 real limitations | Proof or connection has a small gap; limitations thin | Proof incomplete; limitations generic | Missing |

A portfolio can earn full marks with fictional practice data if the
data are labeled fictional and the mathematics is correct. A
portfolio that hides its assumptions, or presents an idealized model
as a measured fact, loses marks under "Situation & assumptions"
even if its arithmetic is right.
