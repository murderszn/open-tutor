# Practice Sets A–G — Unit 03

Worked solutions for every item are in `answer-key.md` (adults only).
State every direction angle with its reference (standard position
unless a bearing is asked for). Give exact answers where exact values
exist; otherwise round to two decimal places and say so.

## Set A — Vectors as quantities (Lesson 01)

1. For `v = <8, 15>`, find `|v|`, its direction angle, and its unit vector.
2. A displacement runs from `(3, −1)` to `(−2, 11)`. Find its components,
   magnitude, and direction angle.
3. A vector has magnitude 12 at direction angle 300°. Find its components
   exactly, then approximately.
4. Find magnitude and direction for `<−7, 24>` and for `<−20, −21>`.
5. Explain why `<2, 3>` and the displacement from `(5, 5)` to `(7, 8)`
   are the same vector, in one or two sentences.
6. A force of 50 N acts at 120°. Write it in component form exactly.

## Set B — Vector operations and applications (Lesson 02)

1. For `u = <5, −2>` and `v = <−3, 6>`, compute `u + v`, `u − v`,
   `2u − v`, and `u · v`.
2. Find the angle between `<3, 0>` and `<2, 2>`, and between `<4, 3>`
   and `<−3, 4>`. Interpret the second result.
3. A boat steams at 15 km/h due south through water; the current is
   8 km/h due west. Find the boat's velocity over ground (vector,
   speed, direction with reference). What heading gives a due-south
   course over ground, and what is the southward speed then?
4. Two forces, `<60, 0>` N and `<0, 80>` N, act on an object. Find the
   resultant's magnitude and direction, and the force that would cancel
   it exactly.
5. Show that the vectors `<1, 2>` and `<−2, 1>` are orthogonal, and
   explain what orthogonality predicts about the magnitude of their sum.
6. A hiker's displacement is `<6, 8>` km on day 1 and `<−8, 6>` km on
   day 2. Find the total displacement and its magnitude. Why is the
   total distance walked (20 km) larger than the displacement magnitude?

## Set C — Parametric equations (Lesson 03)

1. Eliminate the parameter for `x(t) = 5t + 2`, `y(t) = 3t − 1`.
   State the rectangular equation and the direction of travel.
2. For `x(t) = 2 cos t`, `y(t) = 2 sin t`, `0 ≤ t ≤ 2π`, state the trace,
   the starting point, and the direction of travel. What changes if
   `y(t) = −2 sin t` instead?
3. A ball follows `x(t) = 12t`, `y(t) = −5t² + 24t` (meters, seconds).
   Find its landing time and distance, its maximum height and when it
   occurs, and its rectangular equation with domain.
4. Parametrize the segment from `(1, 2)` to `(7, 10)` for `0 ≤ t ≤ 1`,
   and give its rectangular equation.
5. For `x(t) = t²`, `y(t) = t` with `t ≥ 0`, eliminate the parameter and
   state precisely which part of `y² = x` is actually traced.
6. Two particles trace `y = x²`: particle P with `(t, t²)`, particle Q
   with `(t², t⁴)`, both for `t ≥ 0`. Same curve? Same motion? Explain.

## Set D — Polar coordinates (Lesson 04)

1. Convert `(5, 2π/3)` and `(−4, π/3)` to rectangular coordinates exactly.
2. Convert `(−6, 6)` and `(3, −3√3)` to polar form with `r > 0` and
   `0 ≤ θ < 2π`.
3. Convert `r = 6 cos θ` and `r = −2 sin θ` to rectangular equations;
   name each curve with center and radius.
4. Convert `x² + y² = 4x` and `y = −x` to polar form.
5. Give three polar names for `(4, π/6)`, one with negative `r` and one
   with `θ > 2π`.
6. A point has polar coordinates `(r, θ)` with `r < 0`. Explain, in
   words and with a sketch description, where it is plotted.

## Set E — Polar graphs (Lesson 05)

1. Classify `r = 5 + 5 cos θ`: family, symmetry, zeros, maxima, orientation.
2. Classify `r = 3 + 5 sin θ`: family, inner loop or dimple, zeros,
   maxima. (Compare with `r = 5 + 3 sin θ`.)
3. How many petals does `r = 6 sin(5θ)` have, and how many does
   `r = 6 sin(4θ)` have? Give one petal-tip angle for each.
4. Find the zeros of `r = 4 cos(2θ)` in `[0, 2π)` and sketch the graph
   from the zeros and maxima before checking with Desmos.
5. A graph is symmetric about the line `θ = π/2`, reaches `r = 4` at
   `θ = π/2`, and has its cusp at the pole. Give a plausible equation
   and justify each feature from it.
6. Explain why `r = 2 + cos θ` never reaches the pole, using its
   minimum value.

## Set F — Complex numbers in polar form (Lesson 06)

1. Write `−3 + 3√3 i` in polar form exactly.
2. Write `4 cis 210°` in rectangular form exactly.
3. Compute `(2 cis 15°)(4 cis 75°)` and `(9 cis 200°)/(3 cis 50°)`
   geometrically; give the first product in rectangular form approximately.
4. Find all cube roots of `−8` (write `−8` in polar form first), and
   plot or describe their positions.
5. Find the distance and midpoint between `2 + 3i` and `−4 + 7i`.
6. Compute `(√3 + i)³` in polar form and verify the rectangular result
   by one rectangular check of your choice (state the check).

## Set G — Mixed review (all lessons)

1. A vector has components `<−9, 40>`. Find its magnitude, direction,
   and unit vector.
2. A boat's velocity through water is 10 knots north; a current of
   10 knots east acts on it. Find the velocity over ground and explain
   why this case is special (equal components).
3. Eliminate the parameter for `x(t) = 1 + 2t`, `y(t) = 3 − t²`, and
   state the domain restriction coming from `t` if `t ≥ 0` is imposed.
4. Convert `(−5, 5)` to polar form and convert `r = 4 sin θ` to a named
   rectangular curve.
5. Classify `r = 3 sin(3θ)` (petals, reach) and give the zeros of
   `r = 3 cos θ` in `[0, 2π)`.
6. Write `1 − i` in polar form, find its fourth power in polar form,
   and give the power in rectangular form.
7. A force `<12, 5>` N and a force `<−12, 5>` N act together. Find the
   resultant and the angle between the two forces.
8. **Explain your reasoning.** A student says: "Polar coordinates are
   just rectangular coordinates with extra steps, and parametric
   equations are just functions with extra steps." Give one situation
   from this unit where each representation is genuinely better, and
   one limitation of each representation.
