# Practice Sets — Unit 02

Work on paper; justify every step (A-REI.1). Use the unit README's
exact-value table. A calculator is for checking decimal approximations
only, never for replacing an exact answer. Solutions are in
`answer-key.md` (adults only).

## Set A — Identity toolkit (Lesson 01)

1. Given `cos θ = −8/17` with `θ` in Quadrant II, find `sin θ` and
   `tan θ`.
2. Given `tan θ = −3/4` with `θ` in Quadrant IV, find `sin θ` and
   `cos θ`. (Hint: draw the triangle in Quadrant IV first.)
3. Simplify `(1 − sin²θ)/cos θ`, stating any restriction.
4. Simplify `sec θ · sin θ`, and name the identity used in the last step.
5. Verify `(1 + cos θ)(1 − cos θ) = sin²θ` by expanding the left side.
6. Evaluate `cos 150°` exactly, and write `sin 150°` as a cofunction of
   a cosine.

## Set B — Sum and difference (Lesson 02)

1. Find `sin 75°` exactly by a sum split, and confirm it against
   `cos 15° = (√6 + √2)/4` with a cofunction argument.
2. Find `cos 15°` by a difference split different from Lesson 02's
   (use 60° − 45°), and show the result equals `(√6 + √2)/4`.
3. Find `tan 105°` exactly. (Hint: 105° = 60° + 45°; expect a negative
   value.)
4. Simplify `sin(x + π/6) − sin(x − π/6)` to a single term.
5. Verify `cos(A − B) − cos(A + B) = 2 sin A sin B` from the cosine
   formulas.
6. Find the exact value of `sin(π/4)cos(π/6) − cos(π/4)sin(π/6)` by
   recognizing it as a difference formula, then name the angle.

## Set C — Double-angle and half-angle (Lesson 03)

1. Given `sin θ = 12/13` with `θ` in Quadrant I, find `sin 2θ` and
   `cos 2θ`.
2. Given `cos θ = −3/5` with `θ` in Quadrant III, find `tan 2θ`.
   (Hint: find `tan θ` first — its sign in Quadrant III matters.)
3. Find `sin 15°` by the half-angle formula with `A = 30°`, and confirm
   it equals `(√6 − √2)/4`.
4. Rewrite `sin²(3x)` with a power-reducing identity.
5. Verify `tan θ = sin 2θ/(1 + cos 2θ)` using double-angle formulas.
6. Find `cos(θ/2)` given `cos θ = 7/25` and `θ ∈ (0, π/2)`, with the
   quadrant reasoning for `θ/2` shown.

## Set D — Inverse trigonometric functions (Lesson 04)

1. Evaluate `arcsin(√3/2)` and `arccos(−1/2)`, stating the principal
   range used for each.
2. Evaluate `sin(arctan(−3/4))`. (Hint: a negative arctangent lands in
   Quadrant IV.)
3. Evaluate `cos(arcsin(5/13))`.
4. Evaluate `arctan(tan(5π/6))` — and explain why the answer is not
   `5π/6`.
5. Evaluate `sin(2 arccos(5/13))`.
6. A ladder leans so that `cos θ = 0.8`, where `θ` is its angle with the
   ground. Write `θ` with an inverse function and approximate it in
   degrees to one decimal place.

## Set E — Solving trigonometric equations (Lesson 05)

Solve each equation on `[0, 2π)` unless stated otherwise. Show the
factoring or substitution step and check every candidate.

1. `3 sin x − √3 = 0`.
2. `2cos²x − cos x − 1 = 0`.
3. `sin 3x = −1/2` on `[0, 2π)`. (Hint: `u = 3x` ranges over three
   periods — expect six solutions.)
4. `tan²x − 3 = 0`.
5. `cos x = sin x` on `[0, 2π)` by the Lesson 05 method, with the
   division justified.
6. `sin x + 1 = cos x` on `[0, 2π)`, checking every candidate.
   (Hint: squaring both sides will produce one candidate that fails the
   original equation — find it by checking, not by guessing.)

## Set F — Periodic applications (Lesson 06)

1. A Ferris wheel has center 30 m up and radius 25 m, turning once every
   16 minutes, capsule at the bottom at `t = 0`. Write `h(t)` and find
   the first time it reaches 40 m.
2. A tide model is `h(t) = 3 + 1.5 sin(πt/6)`, `t` in hours. Find the
   maximum height, the period, and the times in `[0, 12)` when
   `h(t) = 3.75`.
3. Prove `(1 − cos 2θ)/sin 2θ = tan θ` for all `θ` where both sides are
   defined.
4. A lighthouse lamp is 45 m above the water. The angle of depression
   to a boat is 15°. Find the boat's horizontal distance to the nearest
   meter.
5. A spring's displacement is `d(t) = 6 cos(2πt/5)` cm. Find the period
   and the first two positive times the displacement is 3 cm.
6. Write a one-paragraph defense of a model choice: for the tide in
   item 2, why is a sine model with period 12 hours reasonable, and
   what real-world feature would make it fail? (No computation
   required; the reasoning is the answer.)

## Set G — Mixed review

1. Simplify `(sin θ + cos θ)² − 2 sin θ cos θ`.
2. Find `cos 75°` exactly by two different splits, and show the results
   agree.
3. Evaluate `tan(arcsin(8/17))`.
4. Solve `2sin²x + sin x = 0` on `[0, 2π)`.
5. A model is `h(t) = 10 − 8 cos(πt/6)`. State midline, amplitude,
   period; find `h(0)` and the first time `h(t) = 14` after `t = 0`.
6. Prove `csc θ − sin θ = cot θ cos θ`.
