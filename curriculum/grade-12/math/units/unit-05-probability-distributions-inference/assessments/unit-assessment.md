# Culminating Assessment — Unit 05 (Week 22)

**50 points · ~60 minutes · calculator for approximations only.**
Show a reason for every step; open responses are scored on the
reasoning, not on matching a single sentence. Solutions and scoring
guidance are in `answer-key.md` (adults only). All datasets here are
theoretical or clearly labeled fictional practice data.

## Task 1 — A distribution from scratch (8 pts)

Toss a fair coin three times. Let `X` be the number of heads.
(a) Build the probability distribution table for `X`.
(b) Check both distribution rules for your table.
(c) Find `P(X ≥ 2)` and the expected value `E(X)`.

## Task 2 — Binomial in context (8 pts)

A basketball player makes 80% of her free throws, independently.
In a game she attempts 5 free throws. Let `X` be the number she
makes.
(a) Explain why `X` is binomial, and state `n` and `p`.
(b) Find `P(X = 4)` and `P(X ≥ 4)` to three decimal places.
(c) Find `μ` and `σ` for `X` (σ to three decimal places), and say in
one sentence what `μ` means here.

## Task 3 — Expected value and spread (10 pts)

A $2 scratch card (fictional practice data) wins $50 with probability
0.01, $5 with probability 0.09, and $0 with probability 0.90. Let `G`
be the net gain on one card.
(a) Build the distribution of `G`.
(b) Find `E(G)` and interpret it in one sentence.
(c) Find the standard deviation of `G` to two decimal places.
(d) What ticket price would make this a fair game? Show the
calculation.

## Task 4 — The normal model (8 pts)

Adult heights in a population (fictional practice data) are
approximately normal with mean 170 cm and standard deviation 8 cm.
(a) Estimate the percentage of adults between 162 cm and 178 cm.
(b) Estimate the percentage taller than 186 cm.
(c) Between which two heights do about 95% of adults fall?
(d) A person is 150 cm tall. Find the z-score and say in one sentence
whether that height is unusual.

## Task 5 — Normal approximation (8 pts)

A multiple-choice test has 120 questions, each with 4 choices. A
learner guesses at random on every question. Let `X` be the number
correct.
(a) State `n` and `p`, and check the large-counts condition.
(b) State the approximating normal distribution's `μ` and `σ`
(σ to two decimal places).
(c) Estimate `P(X ≥ 40)` with a continuity correction, to three
decimal places.
(d) In one sentence, say whether getting 40 or more right by guessing
is surprising.

## Task 6 — Judging a claim (8 pts)

A school claims 80% of its students finish their homework on time.
A random sample of 50 students finds 32 who do (`p̂ = 0.64`).
(a) Compute the standard error under the claim, to four decimal
places.
(b) Compute the z-score of the sample result under the claim.
(c) Estimate the probability of a sample proportion of 0.64 or lower
if the claim were true.
(d) State the verdict on the claim in one sentence, and name one
assumption the verdict depends on.

**Scoring (adult):** Task 1: 2 + 2 + 4. Task 2: 2 + 3 + 3. Task 3:
2 + 3 + 3 + 2. Task 4: 2 + 2 + 2 + 2. Task 5: 2 + 2 + 3 + 1.
Task 6: 2 + 2 + 2 + 2. Partial credit for a correct method with an
arithmetic slip (1 point off per slip, not per item). Open responses
(Task 2c, 3b, 4d, 5d, 6d) earn full credit for a correct interpretation
in the learner's own words.
