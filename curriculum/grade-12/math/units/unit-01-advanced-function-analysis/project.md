# Project — Model Shootout

**Unit 01 · studios in Week 6 (S13, S15, S16); gallery adjacent to
the unit assessment (S19)**

## Purpose

Two function families enter; one model leaves — with a written
defense. The learner picks one of three original fictional datasets,
fits **two competing function families** to it, builds a residual
table, compares rate-of-change behavior, and argues for the winner in
a one-page written argument. The project uses everything the unit
taught: analyzing functions across families (Lesson 01), choosing
equivalent forms to reveal properties (Lesson 02), composing and
inverting in context (Lesson 03), average rate of change and the
difference quotient (Lesson 04), piecewise and rational rigor
(Lesson 05), and building models from data (Lesson 06). Nothing is
graded on artistry or on how pretty the graphs are; it is graded on
the mathematics being correct, the quantities being defined, and the
argument being honest.

**Privacy note:** the learner's notebook stays with the learner. No
learner work, names, or photos go into any public repository.

## Materials

Notebook, pencil, ruler, graph paper, calculator. Desmos (free, no
account) for fitting and verification graphs. A spreadsheet is
optional for the residual table. The three datasets below (all
values are fictional practice data, invented for this project):

**Dataset A — Cooling cocoa (fictional).** A café tracks a cup of
cocoa cooling toward room temperature, 70 °F. Columns: `t` = minutes
since pouring (min), `T` = temperature (°F). Values rounded to two
decimals:

| t | 0 | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 |
|---|---|---|----|----|-----|
| T | 150 | 130 | 115 | 103.75 | 95.3125 |

**Dataset B — Robotics club fundraiser (fictional).** A club tracks
cumulative dollars raised for a trip. Columns: `w` = weeks since the
campaign started (weeks), `R` = cumulative dollars raised ($):

| w | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R | 2 | 9 | 20 | 35 | 54 | 77 |

**Dataset C — Library card sign-ups (fictional).** A library tracks
weekly new-card sign-ups after a summer campaign. Columns:
`w` = week number (weeks), `S` = sign-ups that week (sign-ups):

| w | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S | 30 | 47 | 61 | 72 | 80 |

## Studio 1 — Choose and frame (S13)

1. Pick one dataset. Copy it into the notebook and **define your
   quantities**: name the independent and dependent variables, their
   units, and the realistic domain (what values could the situation
   actually produce?).
2. Plot the points by hand. Compute first differences; compute
   second differences and/or consecutive ratios — whichever the
   shape calls for. State in two sentences what the differences or
   ratios suggest about the growth pattern.
3. Propose **two competing function families** for your data (for
   example: linear vs. exponential, quadratic vs. linear, or
   exponential vs. power — choose what the data suggests). Write one
   assumption each family makes about the situation (e.g., "a
   linear model assumes the same number of sign-ups joins every
   week").

## Studio 2 — Fit and compare (S15)

4. Fit both families to your data. Write each model's formula with
   units on every quantity, and show how you found the parameters
   (two-point fit, differences, ratios, or Desmos regression — name
   your method and show the key arithmetic).
5. Build a **residual table**: for each data point, actual value,
   each model's prediction, and each model's residual
   (`actual − predicted`). Mark the largest residual for each model.
6. Compare **rate-of-change behavior**: compute the data's average
   rates of change between consecutive points and compare them with
   what each model predicts (constant? growing? shrinking?). Write
   three sentences on which model the rate-of-change evidence
   favors, and why.

## Studio 3 — Defend (S16)

7. Write a **one-page argument** (12–18 sentences) defending your
   model choice. It must:
   - name the chosen family and give its formula with units;
   - cite the residual table (which model had smaller residuals,
     and by roughly how much);
   - cite the rate-of-change comparison from step 6;
   - state the model's realistic domain and one input the model
     must refuse (with what the formula would wrongly predict);
   - end with one honest limitation: a situation where your model
     would break down, and what family might fit better there.
8. Present the one-pager and residual table to the guiding adult,
   who critiques using the rubric below. Address each critique in
   writing, or log why you disagree with mathematical backing.

## Deliverables

1. Dataset choice with defined quantities, units, and realistic
   domain.
2. Hand plot with differences/ratios and the two-sentence pattern
   reading.
3. Two competing families with one stated assumption each.
4. Both fitted formulas with parameter-finding work shown.
5. Complete residual table with largest residuals marked.
6. Rate-of-change comparison (three sentences).
7. One-page written defense (all five required elements).
8. Written response to the adult's critique.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | 10 — Exemplary | 8 — Proficient | 5 — Developing | 2 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Quantities & setup** (8 pts → scaled: 8 / 6 / 4 / 2) | Quantities named with units, realistic domain justified from the situation, plot accurate | Quantities and units named, domain stated, plot accurate | Quantities named but units or domain missing; plot roughly right | Quantities undefined or plot missing |
| **Both models fit** (10 / 8 / 5 / 2) | Both formulas correct with units, method named, all parameter arithmetic shown and verified | Both formulas correct with units; method named; minor arithmetic gap | One formula correct, or both present with errors in parameters or missing units | Formulas missing or do not fit the data |
| **Residual & rate-of-change evidence** (10 → scaled: 8 / 6 / 4 / 2) | Residual table complete and correct, largest residuals marked; rate-of-change comparison names constant/growing/shrinking for data and both models | Table complete with small errors; comparison present but one behavior mislabeled | Table incomplete or residuals computed with wrong sign; comparison generic | No table or no comparison |
| **Written defense** (8 / 6 / 4 / 2) | All five required elements present, cites actual numbers from the table, limitation is mathematically honest | All five elements present; numbers cited loosely | One or two elements missing, or argument ignores the residual evidence | No defense, or choice defended by preference rather than evidence |
| **Communication** (6 / 4 / 2 / 1) | Notation consistent throughout, tables labeled with units, one-page argument organized and readable | Notation consistent, tables labeled, argument readable | Notation inconsistent or tables unlabeled | Work unreadable or unlabeled |

**Scoring note:** 34–40 = mastery (ready for Unit 02); 26–33 =
proficient with one revision task assigned by the adult; below 26 =
revisit Lessons 01, 04, and 06 before the unit assessment. The adult
records the score and the revision plan privately — never in this
repository.
