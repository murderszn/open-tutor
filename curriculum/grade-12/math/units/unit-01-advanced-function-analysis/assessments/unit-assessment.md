# Unit 01 Culminating Assessment (S19) — about 50 minutes

This assessment is matched to the unit's three objectives:

1. **Analyze** functions across families (domain, range, intercepts,
   intervals, extrema, end behavior, asymptotes, symmetry).
2. **Compose, decompose, and invert** functions in context,
   including domain restriction.
3. **Build, compare, and select** models for data with written
   justification; define quantities and units.

Notebook, calculator, and graph paper allowed. Desmos may be used to
check a graph, not to skip written analysis. The adult scores
same-day; solutions are in the adult-only answer key
(`../answer-key.md`).

> **Note to the guiding adult:** this is a curriculum-draft
> assessment written for home instruction. It is not a standardized,
> validated, or normed instrument — treat scores as one piece of
> evidence alongside the project, the practice sets, and the
> learner's notebook.

**Task 1 — Function analysis (Objective 1, 15 pts).**
Analyze `f(x) = (x − 3)²(x + 3)` completely:
a) Expand or factor to list the zeros with their multiplicities,
   and give the y-intercept. (3 pts)
b) State the domain, range, and end behavior. (3 pts)
c) Find the critical points from `f′(x)` (or by completing the
   square reasoning), and give the intervals of increase and
   decrease with the local maximum and minimum values. (6 pts)
d) State the symmetry (even/odd/neither) with algebra, and note
   whether `f` has any asymptotes. (3 pts)

**Task 2 — Across families (Objective 1, 10 pts).**
For `g(x) = 4 · (1/2)^x − 2`:
a) State the domain, range, x- and y-intercepts. (4 pts)
b) Give the horizontal asymptote and the end behavior as
   `x → −∞` and `x → +∞`. (3 pts)
c) State where `g` increases or decreases, and explain in one
   sentence why `g` has no extrema. (3 pts)

**Task 3 — Composition and inverse in context (Objective 2, 12 pts).**
A treatment plant pumps `r(t) = 1200t` liters of water in `t`
hours, and doses chemical at `c(L) = 0.02L + 5` grams for `L`
liters treated.
a) Write chemical used as a function of hours, `C(t)`, and show
   the units cancel through the composition. (4 pts)
b) Compute `C(3)` two ways — by the composite and stage-by-stage —
   and confirm they agree. (4 pts)
c) Find the inverse of `c` (liters as a function of grams), state
   its domain, and interpret it in one sentence with units. (4 pts)

**Task 4 — Decomposition and domain restriction (Objective 2, 8 pts).**
a) Decompose `d(x) = ∛(4x² − 7)` into an inner and an outer
   function, and verify by recomposing. (3 pts)
b) The function `f(x) = (x − 2)² + 1` is restricted to `x ≥ 2`.
   Find `f⁻¹` by swap-and-solve, choosing the correct sign, and
   state the domain of `f⁻¹`. (5 pts)

**Task 5 — Model selection (Objective 3, 20 pts).**
*Fictional practice data.* A community tool library tracks total
member checkouts by month. Columns: `m` = months since the library
opened (months), `C` = total checkouts that month (checkouts):

| m | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C | 40 | 58 | 84 | 121 | 175 | 253 |

a) Name the quantities and their units, and state the realistic
   domain for `m`. (3 pts)
b) Fit an exponential model through the points and a linear model
   through the first and last points. Show how you found each
   model's parameters. (6 pts)
c) Build a residual table (`actual − predicted`) for both models.
   (5 pts)
d) Compare the rate-of-change behavior of the data with each
   model's, and write a short defense (6–10 sentences) of your
   model choice, citing the residual table and the rate-of-change
   evidence. Name one limitation of your chosen model. (6 pts)

**Task 6 — Mixed fluency (10 pts).**
a) For `f(x) = { 5 − x,  x ≤ 2;  x² − 3,  x > 2 }`: evaluate `f(2)`
   and `f(4)`, and decide whether `f` is continuous at `x = 2`
   with evidence. (5 pts)
b) Simplify `[q(4 + h) − q(4)]/h` fully for `q(x) = x² − 5x`,
   and state the value it approaches as `h → 0`. (5 pts)

## Scoring criteria (75 points total)

- **Task 1:** (a) 1 pt per correct zero with multiplicity, 1 pt for
  the y-intercept; (b) 1 pt each; (c) 2 pts for the derivative and
  critical points, 2 pts for correct intervals, 2 pts for the
  max/min values with locations; (d) 2 pts for symmetry with
  algebra, 1 pt for the asymptote note.
- **Task 2:** (a) 1 pt each for domain, range, x-intercept,
  y-intercept; (b) 1 pt for the asymptote, 2 pts for both end
  behaviors; (c) 1 pt for monotonicity, 2 pts for the no-extrema
  sentence (must reference the horizontal asymptote or unbounded
  growth).
- **Task 3:** (a) 2 pts for the correct composite, 2 pts for the
  unit cancellation shown; (b) 2 pts per correct method with
  agreement; (c) 2 pts for the inverse formula, 1 pt for the
  domain, 1 pt for the interpretation with units.
- **Task 4:** (a) 1 pt for the decomposition, 2 pts for the
  verification; (b) 3 pts for the correct inverse with the right
  sign, 2 pts for its domain.
- **Task 5:** (a) 1 pt per quantity with units, 1 pt for a sensible
  realistic domain; (b) 3 pts per model with parameter work shown;
  (c) 3 pts for correct residuals, 2 pts for the table being
  complete and labeled; (d) 2 pts for the rate-of-change comparison,
  3 pts for a defense that cites numbers, 1 pt for an honest
  limitation.
- **Task 6:** (a) 1 pt per evaluation, 3 pts for the continuity
  verdict with two-sided evidence; (b) 3 pts for the simplified
  form, 2 pts for the limit value.

**Reading the score:** 64–75 = mastery (ready for Unit 02); 50–63 =
proficient with one revision task assigned by the adult; below 50 =
revisit the flagged objectives with the adult before Unit 02.
