# Grade 12 Social Studies — Economics and Policy Inquiry

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are
delivered; the eight units are planned, not yet written. See
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained
for one grade-12 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials,
and supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades
in private storage — never in this public repository.

Core social studies in this library stays **inquiry-based and nonpartisan**:
institutions, processes, and arguments are presented without endorsing parties,
candidates, or causes. Contested questions get multiple sourced perspectives.
Financial and market examples are **educational, not investment advice** —
verify current prices, rates, laws, and policy claims before learner-facing use.

See also: [Grade 12 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-12 economics course: how individuals,
firms, and governments make decisions under scarcity; how markets coordinate
production and prices; where markets falter and governments step in; how the
national economy is measured and steered; how trade and finance connect the
U.S. to the world; and how to build personal-finance models and argue policy
from evidence. Sessions are about 50 minutes, five per week, blending explicit
instruction with data investigations, models, structured deliberations, and
evidence-based writing.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Analyze how scarcity, incentives, and opportunity cost shape choices and
   produce policies with different costs and benefits for different groups
   (D2.Eco.1.9-12); use marginal benefits and marginal costs to argue for or
   against an economic approach (D2.Eco.2.9-12).
2. Explain how incentives influence what is produced and distributed in a market
   system; model supply and demand shifts and compute the resulting price and
   quantity changes (D2.Eco.3.9-12).
3. Evaluate the extent of competition among buyers and sellers in specific
   markets and describe the consequences of that competition (D2.Eco.4.9-12;
   D2.Eco.5.9-12).
4. Generate explanations for a government role where market inefficiencies
   exist; evaluate policies to improve market outcomes by benefits and costs,
   including intended and unintended consequences (D2.Eco.6.9-12;
   D2.Eco.7.9-12; D2.Eco.8.9-12).
5. Describe the roles of institutions — clearly defined property rights and the
   rule of law — in a market economy (D2.Eco.9.9-12).
6. Use current, dated data to explain how changes in spending, production, and
   the money supply affect economic conditions (D2.Eco.10.9-12).
7. Use economic indicators (GDP, inflation, unemployment) to analyze the
   current and expected state of the economy (D2.Eco.11.9-12).
8. Evaluate the selection of monetary and fiscal policies under different
   economic conditions; explain the roles of the Federal Reserve and the
   federal budget process (D2.Eco.12.9-12).
9. Explain why technology, capital investment, and human capital raise economic
   growth and standards of living (D2.Eco.13.9-12).
10. Analyze the role of comparative advantage in international trade of goods
    and services (D2.Eco.14.9-12); explain how globalization trends and
    policies affect growth, labor markets, citizens' rights, the environment,
    and income distribution across nations (D2.Eco.15.9-12).
11. Frame compelling and supporting questions, gather and evaluate sources with
    attention to limitations, build evidence-based arguments, present
    conclusions, and assess options for informed civic action within lawful,
    adult-supervised channels (D1.1–D1.5.9-12; D3.1–D3.4.9-12; D4.1–D4.4.9-12;
    D4.6.9-12; D4.7.9-12).
12. Build and interpret a personal-finance model (budget, saving, borrowing,
    compounding) with verified current rates and fees, explaining assumptions,
    risks, and limits — as education, not financial advice.

## Standards reference

Objectives reference the National Council for the Social Studies **C3
Framework** economics indicators for the 9–12 band (codes verified against
published C3 indicator text; descriptions below are brief paraphrases; no
state adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification claimed). Full
crosswalk in [scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md#4-standards-crosswalk):

- Economic decision making: D2.Eco.1.9-12, D2.Eco.2.9-12
- Exchange and markets: D2.Eco.3.9-12 through D2.Eco.9.9-12
- The national economy: D2.Eco.10.9-12 through D2.Eco.13.9-12
- The global economy: D2.Eco.14.9-12, D2.Eco.15.9-12
- Inquiry arc: D1.1–D1.5.9-12, D3.1–D3.4.9-12, D4.1–D4.4.9-12, D4.6.9-12,
  D4.7.9-12

## Structure

- [Scope and sequence](scope-and-sequence.md) — audit, standards crosswalk,
  36-week pacing with weekly goals, session model, materials, accessibility.
- `units/` — eight units, planned (not yet written):
  1. Economic reasoning: scarcity, incentives, and opportunity cost
  2. Supply, demand, markets, and price signals
  3. Firms, labor, competition, and market structures
  4. Market failures, public goods, and policy tradeoffs
  5. Macroeconomic indicators: growth, inflation, and unemployment
  6. Fiscal and monetary policy and economic institutions
  7. International trade, finance, development, and inequality
  8. Personal-finance models, economic evidence, and policy capstone

## For the guiding adult

Read the scope-and-sequence before starting. Every current-data claim (rates,
prices, figures, policy details) must be dated and re-verified at authoring —
economic facts go stale faster than any other subject's. Keep examples
institutional rather than partisan: roles, rules, and evidence, not commentary.
Supervise any field observation or interview; the audit names simulation and
observation alternatives. Each future unit will include its own teacher guide
and answer key.
