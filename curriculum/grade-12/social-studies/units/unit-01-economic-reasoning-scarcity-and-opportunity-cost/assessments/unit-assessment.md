# Unit 01 Assessment — Economic Reasoning (summative)

Take in W6S5 after the unit review. ~50 minutes. You may use your PPF
sketch from Lesson 02 and your vocabulary list. Every task is solved and
scored in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md). All figures are fictional
practice data unless a source and date are named.

## Task 1 — Read and use a PPF (Objectives 2, 3; 20 pts)

A workshop has fixed workers and machines for one week. Its possible
output combinations are:

| Combination | Chairs | Tables |
|---|---|---|
| A | 60 | 0 |
| B | 48 | 12 |
| C | 32 | 20 |
| D | 16 | 26 |
| E | 0 | 30 |

a. Compute the per-unit opportunity cost of *tables* (in chairs) for the
moves A→B and D→E. Show each division. Is opportunity cost increasing?
Explain with your two numbers. (8 pts)

b. The workshop is currently producing 30 chairs and 10 tables. Is this
point efficient, inefficient, or unattainable? Explain, and name one
combination on the frontier the workshop could reach without giving up
either good. (6 pts)

c. A new machine lets the workshop produce 25% more of *both* goods at
every combination. Does its PPF shift or does it move along the curve?
Sketch or describe the change and explain. (6 pts)

## Task 2 — Analyze the two-good farm (Objectives 1, 2, 3; 12 pts)

![An AI-generated illustration: one farm, two possible crops. Left: wheat
fields and a harvester. Right: corn fields. The farmer stands at the
boundary. If the farm expands its wheat half, what must it give
up?](../assets/two-good-farm-ppf.png)

a. In two or three sentences, explain what this AI illustration shows about
scarcity and choice. (4 pts)

b. Suppose the farm's current split (half wheat, half corn) is point C on
its PPF. The farmer considers converting the corn half entirely to wheat
(moving to point A). Using the Greenfield Farm schedule from Lesson 02
(C: 50 wheat/50 corn; A: 100 wheat/0 corn), what is the opportunity cost of
this move? Express it as a total and per bushel of wheat gained. (5 pts)

c. The illustration's fence is described as a visual metaphor. In one or
two sentences, explain what real-world feature of farming the sharp fence
line oversimplifies. (3 pts)

*Text-only alternative for the image:* the left half of the farm grows
wheat; the right half grows corn; a farmer stands at the boundary between
them. All parts can be answered from this description.

## Task 3 — Incentives across groups (Objective 4; 16 pts)

A county introduces a $0.25 fee per plastic grocery bag (fictional policy).
Read the scenario, then answer:

a. Identify the incentive type and the target group, and predict the
behavior change. (4 pts)

b. Name two distinct groups that gain from this policy and two that bear a
cost. For each, say what they gain or lose in one phrase. (8 pts)

c. Name one way shoppers or stores might respond that the county did NOT
intend, and state what evidence would show it happening. (4 pts)

## Task 4 — Decide at the margin (Objective 5; 16 pts)

A community pool considers extending its summer season by extra weeks.
Each extra week costs $2,000 (staff, chemicals). Expected additional
revenue per week: week 1: $3,500; week 2: $2,800; week 3: $2,100;
week 4: $1,400; week 5: $700. (Fictional figures.)

a. Build the marginal table (week, MB, MC) and find the optimal number of
extra weeks. Show each comparison. (8 pts)

b. Write a 5–7 sentence marginal argument for or against extending the
season, using the structure: claim → marginal comparison with numbers →
acknowledged forgone alternative → conclusion. (8 pts)

## Task 5 — Compare systems with evidence (Objective 6; 16 pts)

"Which questions does each economic system answer best?" Write one
paragraph (8–12 sentences) comparing how a market-leaning mixed economy and
a command-leaning economy each answer *what*, *how*, and *for whom* for one
good of your choice (bread, housing, or smartphones). Use mechanism-level
language — who decides, by what process — and close with one acknowledged
tradeoff of each system's approach. No slogans; no partisan commentary.
