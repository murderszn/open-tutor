# Project — One Decision, Three Lenses

**Due:** W6S4 (defended in the economic-systems deliberation)
**Format:** your choice — written report (4–6 pages) or 10-minute
presentation (slides or poster)
**Question:** pick one real decision with a genuine tradeoff, then analyze
it through all three of this unit's lenses.

## The task

With your adult's approval, choose **one** decision from the list below (or
propose your own — it must be real, current or historical, and analyzable
without partisan commentary):

- A city deciding whether to convert two downtown parking lots into a public
  plaza (fictionalized city welcome; use public meeting records or news
  coverage your adult selects).
- A school board weighing a later start time for high school (use published
  sleep research summaries and district budget figures your adult selects).
- A family business deciding whether to open a second location.
- A state debating a bottle-deposit recycling program (use the deposit
  states' published program data your adult selects).
- A historical case: the U.S. interstate highway system's routing choices
  (1950s) — what was built, what was not, and at what cost.

Then produce **three analyses** of the same decision:

### Lens 1 — Opportunity cost and the PPF (Lessons 1–2)

- Name the decision-maker and the scarce resource(s).
- List at least three alternatives; identify the best forgone alternative
  and defend why it — not the others — is the opportunity cost.
- Build a simplified two-good PPF for the decision (fictional numbers
  clearly labeled, or real figures with source and date). Mark the chosen
  point and one point that was given up; compute the per-unit opportunity
  cost of moving between two points on your curve.

### Lens 2 — Incentives (Lesson 3)

- Identify at least two incentives (positive or negative) acting on the
  decision-maker or affected parties.
- For one policy-like incentive, map who gains and who bears the cost —
  name at least two distinct groups on each side where they exist.
- Name one way the incentive could misfire (unintended response) and what
  evidence would tell you it happened.

### Lens 3 — Marginal analysis (Lesson 4)

- Frame the decision as a "how much" question (how many lots, how much
  later, how big a deposit).
- Build a marginal table with at least four steps (fictional estimates
  clearly labeled, or sourced figures with dates). Find the optimal level
  with the MB ≥ MC rule.
- Write the decision as a marginal argument: claim → marginal comparison
  with numbers → acknowledged forgone alternative → conclusion
  (D2.Eco.2.9-12).

### Close

- One paragraph: which lens changed your view of the decision most, and
  what evidence caused the change?
- Source list: every real figure, quotation, or claim cited with source and
  date checked. Fictional modeling numbers labeled as such.

## Deliverables

1. The report or presentation covering all three lenses plus the closing
   paragraph.
2. The PPF sketch and the marginal table (inside the report or as
   appendices).
3. The source list with dates.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | Excellent (9–10) | Proficient (7–8) | Developing (4–6) | Points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lens 1: opportunity cost & PPF | Best forgone alternative correctly identified and defended; PPF built accurately; per-unit cost computed with work shown | Minor error in identification or computation; PPF essentially correct | Confuses total forgone with best forgone; PPF misread | /10 |
| Lens 2: incentives | Two+ incentives correctly typed; gains/costs mapped across distinct groups; plausible misfire with evidence test | Incentives identified; group mapping thin or misfire vague | Incentives misidentified or only intended effects considered | /10 |
| Lens 3: marginal analysis | 4+ step table; optimal level found with MB ≥ MC applied step by step; argument follows claim→evidence→conclusion | Table built; optimum found with minor slip; argument structure present | Totals used instead of margins, or no optimum found | /10 |
| Evidence, sources & close | All real claims sourced and dated; fictional numbers labeled; closing paragraph names the lens that changed the view and the evidence | Most claims sourced; minor labeling gaps | Unsourced real claims or unlabeled fictional data | /10 |

**Total: /40.** 36–40 = exceeds; 30–35 = meets; 24–29 = approaching;
below 24 = revise and resubmit with the adult.

## Adult notes

- Approve the topic before research begins; steer away from live partisan
  contests toward institutional decisions with public records.
- Check every real figure's source and date with the learner; reject
  unsourced "statistics."
- The defense (W6S4) is a 5-minute Q&A after the presentation: ask "what
  would change your conclusion?" — the learner should be able to name the
  evidence that would flip the marginal call or the opportunity-cost ranking.
