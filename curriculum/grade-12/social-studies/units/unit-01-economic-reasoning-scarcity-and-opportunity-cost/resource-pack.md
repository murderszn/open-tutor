# Resource Pack — Unit 01: Economic Reasoning

**Summary (adult planning judgment):** Supports a grade-12 learner's first
four weeks of economics — scarcity, opportunity cost, the PPF model,
incentives, marginal analysis, and economic systems — with free, no-account
readings and videos that supplement (never replace) the unit's explicit
lessons. Checked 2026-10-15.

## Web references (checked 2026-10-15 unless noted)

1. **Econlib — "Opportunity Cost" topic page** (Library of Economics and
   Liberty). Concise Encyclopedia of Economics definition ("the value of the
   next-highest-valued alternative use"), essays, and an EconTalk podcast on
   the concept. Free, no account.
   <https://www.econlib.org/library/Topics/College/opportunitycost.html>
   — supports Lessons 1–2; adult pre-reads for grade fit.
2. **Econlib — "Scarcity" topic page.** Companion reference for Lesson 1's
   scarcity framing.
   <https://www.econlib.org/library/Topics/College/scarcity.html> —
   search-verified topic page on the same reputable site; adult confirms the
   page before assigning.
3. **Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis — Economic Education.**
   Classroom-ready economics resources; the parent site of
   federalreserveeducation.org. <https://www.stlouisfed.org/education> —
   known official Federal Reserve education portal; adult confirms the
   landing page before assigning.
4. **Council for Economic Education — National Standards (reference
   shelf).** Concept-coverage reference for the adult; not cited as an
   adopted standard. <https://www.councilforeconed.org/> — known official
   site of the Council for Economic Education; adult confirms before use.
5. **Khan Academy — Economics.** Practice and articles on microeconomics
   concepts; free. <https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain>

6. **Britannica — "command economy" / "market economy" articles.**
   Encyclopedia background for Lesson 5's system comparisons (adult-side).
   <https://www.britannica.com/> — search the site for the article titles;
   labeled search starting point, adult previews.

## Access notes

Every core objective has a free, no-account route: the unit's own lessons,
the Econlib topic pages, MRU videos, and Federal Reserve Education
resources all require no account. Videos supplement teaching rather than
replace it. The adult previews every video for age fit, ads, comments, and
captions before assigning; search links are never presented as verified
items.

## Review notes

Relevance: all items map to U01's five lessons or the project. Credibility:
Econlib (Liberty Fund), the Federal Reserve System, MRU (George Mason /
Mercatus), Britannica, and the Council for Economic Education are
established educational organizations; Khan Academy is a well-known free
education platform. Caveats: Econlib essays reflect their authors'
perspectives — the adult frames them as one reputable viewpoint, not
neutral fact, in line with the unit's nonpartisanship rule. Khan Academy's
economics page and the Britannica/Council landing pages were not
text-verified on 2026-10-15 and are labeled accordingly. No runtimes or
caption claims are made for videos the adult has not previewed.
