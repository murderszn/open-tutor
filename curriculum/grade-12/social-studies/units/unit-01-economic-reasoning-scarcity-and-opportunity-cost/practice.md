# Unit 01 Practice — Economic Reasoning

Guided and extra practice for Lessons 1–5. Work the guided set with your
adult; the extra set is independent. Every item is solved in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md). All figures are fictional practice data.

## Guided practice (with your adult)

### A. Opportunity cost (Lesson 1)

A1. Lena can spend Saturday (a) babysitting for $70, (b) at a robotics
workshop she values at $55 of learning, or (c) streaming shows she values at
$25 of relaxation. She babysits. State the opportunity cost and explain why
it is not $80.

A2. A town has one vacant lot. Options: a parking lot ($40,000/year revenue),
a community garden ($28,000/year of food and well-being value), or a
playground ($22,000/year of recreation value). The town builds the garden.
What is the opportunity cost? What would it be if the town had built the
parking lot instead?

### B. PPF (Lesson 2)

Use the Greenfield Farm schedule from Lesson 02 (A: 100 wheat/0 corn;
B: 80/30; C: 50/50; D: 20/65; E: 0/75):

B1. Moving B→C→D (two steps), what is the total wheat given up and total
corn gained? What is the average per-unit opportunity cost of corn across
both steps?

B2. A drought destroys irrigation and every combination now yields 20% less
of both goods. Does the PPF shift or does the farm move along it? Sketch or
describe the new curve.

### C. Incentives (Lesson 3)

C1. A library starts charging $1 per day for overdue books (previously
free). Name the incentive type, the target, the predicted behavior change,
and one group that gains and one that bears a cost.

C2. A factory gets a $10,000 grant for each worker it hires from a
job-training program (fictional). The factory hires five such workers but
admits it would have hired three of them anyway. What is the incentive
paying for in those three cases, and what concept from Lesson 01 does this
resemble? (The grant pays for behavior that was already happening — compare
with opportunity cost thinking: the money's best alternative use.)

### D. Marginal analysis (Lesson 4)

D1. MB per unit: 30, 24, 18, 12, 6. MC per unit: 10, 14, 18, 22, 26. Find
the optimal number of units. Then explain why the 3rd unit is still worth
doing even though MB = MC exactly. (At equality the unit breaks even; doing
it is not a loss. Conventions vary — "do it" at MB = MC is the standard
classroom rule; note the adult may also accept "indifferent.")

D2. A student says, "The marginal cost of my 5th practice problem is zero
because I'm already sitting at my desk." Identify the error: what is the
marginal cost actually measuring here?

### E. Economic systems (Lesson 5)

E1. In 2–3 sentences each, explain how a traditional economy and a market
economy would each answer "for whom" for a catch of fish.

E2. A government runs the postal service but private firms deliver packages.
Name the system type and identify one market feature and one command feature.

## Extra practice (independent)

E3. **Opportunity-cost chain.** You choose a part-time job ($200/week) over
an unpaid internship (you value the experience at $150/week of future
earnings) and over extra rest (valued at $60/week of well-being). (a) State
the opportunity cost. (b) Six months later the internship would have led to
a $400/week offer. Does that change the opportunity cost of your *original*
decision? Explain why or why not. (No — opportunity cost is judged on the
information available at decision time; hindsight does not rewrite it.
This is a common trap.)

E4. **PPF with a twist.** A second farm has this schedule (fictional):
A: 60 wheat/0 corn; B: 45/20; C: 30/35; D: 15/45; E: 0/50. Compute the
per-unit opportunity cost of corn for A→B and for D→E. Is opportunity cost
increasing here too? Plot both farms' PPFs on the same axes — which farm has
the absolute advantage in wheat? In corn?

E5. **Incentive design.** Design one incentive (positive or negative) to
reduce food waste in your school cafeteria. State the target, the predicted
response, one group that gains, one that bears a cost, and one way students
might dodge the intended response.

E6. **Marginal call.** A band considers adding concert dates. Each date
costs $5,000 (venue, crew). Expected profit per date: 1st: $12,000;
2nd: $9,000; 3rd: $6,000; 4th: $4,000; 5th: $2,000. How many dates should
they add? Write the decision rule in one sentence.

E7. **Systems argument.** "A mixed economy is just a market economy that
hasn't decided what it believes." Argue against this claim in one paragraph
using the what/how/for whom framework and one real example of a deliberate
mix (e.g., public schools inside a market economy).

## Retrieval (from this unit — no new teaching)

R1. Define scarcity, opportunity cost, incentive, marginal benefit, and
marginal cost from memory; then check your definitions against Lessons 1–4.
R2. Without looking: what three things can you read off a PPF?
(Efficient/inefficient/unattainable points; opportunity cost between points;
what shifts the curve.)
R3. One sentence: why is the best forgone alternative the opportunity cost,
not everything you gave up?
