# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Economic Reasoning

Adult-facing notes for all five lessons, the practice set, the project,
and the assessments. Student questions live in the lesson files; worked
solutions live in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) — keep the two separate.

## How to use this unit

- **Pacing:** 20 sessions over Weeks 3–6, ~50 minutes each, per the session
  table in [README.md](README.md). Each lesson file's exit check is the
  daily formative signal; the W3S5/W4S5/W5S5 reviews are the weekly ones.
- **Math load:** Lessons 2 and 4 need comfort with division and reading
  tables (diagnostic Weeks 1–2 verified this). If the arithmetic blocks the
  economics, do one computation aloud together, then hand the next one
  back — never reduce the unit to arithmetic drill.
- **Fictional data:** every schedule, wage, and price in the unit is
  labeled fictional practice data. Say so out loud in Lesson 1; learners
  must never quote these numbers as facts.
- **Nonpartisanship:** incentives and systems are analyzed through roles,
  rules, and evidence. Redirect slogans and present-day political commentary
  to mechanism-level questions ("who decides, by what process, with what
  evidence of results?"). Contested current questions get multiple sourced
  perspectives or are set aside for U04+.

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

### L1 — Scarcity and opportunity cost (W3S1)

- **Core move:** "best forgone alternative, not all forgone alternatives."
  If only one idea survives the week, it is this sentence.
- **Misconceptions:** adding up everything given up; treating the chosen
  option's price as its cost; letting sunk costs decide. The sunk-cost
  section is deliberately early — learners meet it before it can calcify.
- **Watch for:** learners who say "I had no choice, so there's no
  opportunity cost." Even forced choices have a best alternative among the
  remaining options — push them to name it.
- **Ready to move on:** the learner can compute an opportunity cost from a
  three-option scenario without prompting and explain the sunk-cost error.

### L2 — The production possibilities frontier (W3S2–W3S4)

- **Core move:** the curve *is* the tradeoff. Spend the image activity
  (Activity A) before the numbers — learners who can "see" the tradeoff
  compute it more accurately.
- **Misconceptions:** reading inside-the-curve points as tradeoffs;
  confusing shifts with movements along the curve; computing totals instead
  of per-unit costs. Re-teach with: "inside the curve you can have more of
  both — where is the sacrifice?"
- **The bowed-out shape:** do not let learners memorize "concave" without
  the reason — resources suited to one good get converted last, so later
  units cost more. Ask "why would the *last* corn cost more wheat than the
  first?"
- **Ready to move on:** the learner plots a 5-point PPF, classifies a new
  point, and computes a per-unit cost with the division shown.

### L3 — Incentives (W4S1–W4S2)

- **Core move:** "who changes behavior, and what do they gain or lose?"
  Every incentive analysis that cannot answer this is incomplete.
- **Misconceptions:** "positive" = morally good; assuming the intended
  response is the actual response; forgetting the incentive itself has an
  opportunity cost (subsidies are paid for).
- **Enforcement question:** ask it every time — "what makes this incentive
  real rather than written?" This pays off in U04's policy evaluation.
- **Field observation (W4S4):** approve the site or document in advance;
  the documentary alternative (a photographed sign or official policy page)
  is equally valid. No learner purchases or sign-ups.
- **Ready to move on:** the learner types an incentive, predicts a response,
  and maps gains/costs across at least two groups.

### L4 — Marginal analysis (W5S1–W5S2)

- **Core move:** decide the *next* unit, not the totals. Write "MB of the
  NEXT unit vs. MC of the NEXT unit" where the learner can see it all week.
- **Misconceptions:** total-benefit reasoning (the readiness check targets
  this); treating sunk costs as marginal costs; "more is always better."
  The crossing point — stop when MB < MC — is the entire lesson.
- **The argument template** (claim → marginal comparison → forgone
  alternative → conclusion) is reused in the project and U08's capstone.
  Insist on numbers in the comparison, even rough ones.
- **Ready to move on:** the learner finds an optimum from a marginal table
  step by step and writes the four-part argument.

### L5 — Economic systems (W6S1–W6S2)

- **Core move:** mechanism-level comparison — who answers what/how/for
  whom, by what process. "Which system is best?" is always reframed as
  "which questions does each system answer well, and at what cost?"
- **Guardrails:** country sketches are simplified models, not profiles.
  If a learner brings a current political claim, require a dated source or
  set it aside; the unit's comparisons must not become partisan commentary.
- **Property rights** (D2.Eco.9.9-12 preview) deserves its own emphasis:
  it is the institution economists cite across all system types.
- **Ready to move on:** the learner sorts economies by market/command
  features with cited evidence and argues the W6S4 deliberation question
  without slogans.

## Practice set

Use the guided items (A–E) in-lesson or as warm-ups; assign the extra set
(E3–E7) and retrieval (R1–R3) across the W3S5/W4S5/W5S5 reviews. Item E3b
(hindsight vs. decision-time opportunity cost) is the unit's hardest
conceptual item — save it for W5S5.

## Project — One Decision, Three Lenses

- **Approve the topic at W4S3.** Steer toward institutional decisions with
  public records; away from live partisan contests.
- **Check sources at W5S3.** Every real figure needs a source and date;
  reject unsourced statistics. Fictional modeling numbers must be labeled.
- **The defense (W6S4):** after the presentation, ask "what evidence would
  change your conclusion?" The learner should name what would flip the
  marginal call or the opportunity-cost ranking — that answer is the
  inquiry-arc performance check for the unit.

## Assessments

- **Week-6 quiz (W6S4, 28 pts):** formative. 25–28 = ready; 20–24 = review
  Lessons 2 and 4 before the summative; below 20 = re-teach with the
  practice set before W6S5.
- **Unit assessment (W6S5, 80 pts):** 72–80 exceeds; 64–71 meets; 52–63
  approaching; below 52 re-teach and reassess. Record standing against all
  six objectives.
- **Reteach triggers:** Task 1 errors → re-teach L2 with a fresh schedule;
  Task 3 thin group-mapping → re-teach L3's "who gains / who pays"; Task 4
  total-based reasoning → re-teach L4's margin rule with Guided practice 1;
  Task 5 slogans → re-teach L5's mechanism-level framing.

## Differentiation

- Every lesson file lists reading/language access, an alternate response
  mode (oral, tabular, or dictated responses accepted), and an extension.
- Learners who finish early: the extensions (real-policy incentive mapping,
  country-change research, cost-benefit analysis reading) — not acceleration
  into U02.
- Learners who struggle with the tables: keep the numbers but shrink the
  schedules (3 points instead of 5); the reasoning, not the arithmetic, is
  the objective.
