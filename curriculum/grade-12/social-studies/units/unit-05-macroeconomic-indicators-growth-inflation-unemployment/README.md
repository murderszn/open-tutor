# Unit 05 — Macroeconomic Indicators: Growth, Inflation, and Unemployment

**Grade:** 12 · **Subject:** Social Studies (Economics and policy inquiry)
**Track weeks:** 20–23 · **Backlog:** [issue #57](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/57), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Units 01–04 taught the learner to reason about one decision, one market,
one firm, and one policy. This unit zooms out: how do we know whether a
whole national economy is doing well? Three indicators carry most of the
answer — output (GDP), prices (inflation), and work (unemployment) — and
each one is a human-made measuring instrument with a definition, a formula,
a data source, and known blind spots. The learner computes each indicator
from practice data, reads three real, dated U.S. statistical releases (BEA
GDP, BLS CPI, BLS Employment Situation), and finishes by combining the
indicators into a sourced judgment about the state of the economy — and by
saying what the indicators cannot settle.

## Essential question

Three numbers — output, prices, jobs — are quoted every day as "the
economy." What does each one actually measure, what does it miss, and how
do you combine them into a judgment you can defend?

From Units 01–04 and the diagnostic weeks, described as skills (no

- Graph and interpret a linear relationship and read a data table
  (diagnostic weeks; Unit 02).
- Use marginal reasoning and benefit–cost reasoning to evaluate a policy
  (Unit 01, Lesson 4; Unit 04, Lesson 4). This unit re-teaches every
  macroeconomic concept from scratch rather than assuming it.
- Frame a claim with at least two pieces of evidence; distinguish a claim
  from its evidence and from an interpretation (Unit 01; Unit 04).
- Explain, in one paragraph, how a supply or demand shift moves

- Classify a market outcome as efficient or inefficient and name who
  gains and who loses (Unit 04) — needed for the Lesson 02 discussion of
  who inflation helps and hurts.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. **Compute GDP by the expenditure approach** — identify C, I, G, and NX
   in a data table, compute GDP, convert nominal GDP to real GDP with a
   price index, and explain why only final goods and services produced
   inside the country count. (D2.Eco.10.9-12; D2.Eco.11.9-12)
2. **Explain what GDP leaves out** — name at least four categories GDP
   misses (household production, the underground economy, leisure,
   environmental costs, distribution) and explain why GDP per capita is
   a better well-being proxy than GDP alone, and still an imperfect one.
   (D2.Eco.11.9-12)
3. **Compute inflation from a price index** — build a CPI-style basket,
   compute the index and the inflation rate, convert a nominal wage to a
   real wage, and explain who inflation helps (borrowers, holders of
   real assets) and who it hurts (lenders, savers, people on fixed
   incomes). (D2.Eco.11.9-12)
4. **Compute unemployment and participation rates** — classify people as
   employed, unemployed, or out of the labor force; compute the
   unemployment rate and the labor-force participation rate; distinguish
   frictional, structural, and cyclical unemployment; and explain what
   the headline rate hides (discouraged workers, underemployment).
   (D2.Eco.11.9-12)
5. **Explain growth and the business cycle** — name the four phases of
   the business cycle, identify peaks and troughs in a quarterly series,
   apply the rule of 70, and explain why technology, capital investment,
   and human capital raise long-run growth and living standards.
   (D2.Eco.13.9-12; D2.Eco.10.9-12)
6. **Read the economy from real, dated releases** — extract the headline
   figures from a BEA GDP release, a BLS CPI release, and a BLS
   Employment Situation release; state each figure's period, units, and
   revision status; combine the three indicators into a sourced,
   uncertainty-acknowledging judgment; and name one limitation of each
   indicator. (D2.Eco.10.9-12; D2.Eco.11.9-12)

In every unit the learner also develops compelling questions
(D1.1–D1.5.9-12), evaluates sources and their limits
(D3.1–D3.4.9-12), and communicates evidence-based conclusions
(D4.1–D4.4.9-12; D4.6.9-12).

## Differentiation, supports, and extensions

- **Reading/language access:** every lesson defines vocabulary in
  context before using it in a computation; formula steps are
  numbered; the adult may read a worked example aloud while the
  learner tracks the numbers. Release-reading tasks pre-mark the
  headline paragraph so the learner is not hunting through a long
  government document unaided.
- **Alternate response modes:** classifications, rate computations,
  and indicator readings may be answered in a table, a labeled
  diagram, or a short paragraph; the Week 21 argument and the project
  brief may be delivered orally with the adult scribing the evidence
  table.
- **Support:** a learner who cannot yet divide fluently starts from
  the table version of each worked example (all numbers visible) and
  computes one ratio at a time with a calculator; the adult supplies
  the formula sheet from the teacher guide.
- **Extension:** derive real GDP per capita growth from nominal GDP,
  population, and deflator series (FRED, dated); research how the
  October 2026 CPI release's missing 12-month change was handled in
  later releases; or extend the project brief with a second country's
  indicators and a comparability note (different baskets, different
  surveys).
