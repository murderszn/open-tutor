# Practice — Unit 05

All numbers are **fictional practice data** unless a source is named.
Show your work for every computation; a bare answer earns no credit.
Solutions are in the [answer key](answer-key.md).

## Set A — GDP (Lesson 01)

**A1.** Fictional practice data ($ billions): consumption 9,000;
investment 2,200; government purchases 2,800; exports 1,500; imports
1,900. Compute net exports and GDP. Then compute each component's
share of GDP.

**A2.** Classify each as counted in this year's GDP or not, with one
reason each: (a) a new textbook sold by a bookstore; (b) the same
textbook resold used a year later; (c) steel sold to a carmaker; (d) a
finished car sold by a dealer; (e) a government-built bridge; (f) a
Social Security check mailed to a retiree.

**A3.** Nominal GDP is $12,000 billion and the GDP deflator is 150.
What is real GDP? Show the division.

**A4.** A country's GDP is $500 billion and its population is 25
million. Compute GDP per capita. In one sentence, say what this number
does and does not tell you.

**A5.** Nominal GDP grew 10 percent while the GDP deflator rose from
100 to 108. Compute the real growth rate exactly (not by subtraction).
Show the division.

**A6. Error analysis.** A learner computes GDP as C + I + G + exports
and gets a number too large. What did the learner forget, and why does
forgetting it overstate GDP? Correct the formula in words.

**A7.** Name three categories of valuable activity GDP leaves out, and
give one concrete example of each.

**A8.** True or false, with one reason each: (a) GDP counts the value
of a house built this year. (b) GDP counts a haircut. (c) GDP counts
the sale of a used house. (d) GDP counts a company's purchase of its
own shares.

## Set B — Inflation (Lesson 02)

**B1.** A basket costs $320.00 in the base year and $336.00 this year.
Compute the CPI (base year = 100) and the inflation rate.

**B2.** The CPI rises from 180 to 189. What is the inflation rate?
Show the arithmetic in one line.

**B3.** A worker's wage rises from $30.00 to $31.50 an hour (a 5
percent raise) while the CPI rises 6 percent. Compute the real wage in
last year's dollars and the real wage change. Is the worker better or
worse off?

**B4.** For each person, say whether unexpected inflation helps or
hurts them, and why in one sentence each: (a) a homeowner with a
fixed-rate mortgage; (b) a retiree on a fixed pension with no COLA;
(c) a bank that lent money at a fixed interest rate.

**B5.** Prices rise 0.5 percent every month for a year. What is the
annual inflation rate, compounded? (Compute (1.005)^12 − 1, or estimate
and explain why the estimate is close.)

**B6.** A country's CPI rises 2 percent in Year 1, 1 percent in Year 2,
and falls 0.5 percent in Year 3. Label each year as inflation,
disinflation, or deflation.

**B7.** Name two limitations of the CPI as a measure of a household's
cost of living, and explain each in one sentence.

**B8.** The price of coffee rises 10 percent while the overall CPI
rises 4 percent. Has coffee become relatively more or less expensive?
Explain in two sentences.

## Set C — Unemployment (Lesson 03)

**C1.** Fictional practice data: labor force 60,000; employed 56,400;
unemployed 3,600; working-age population 100,000. Compute the
unemployment rate and the labor-force participation rate.

**C2.** Classify each person as employed, unemployed, or out of the
labor force, with one reason each: (a) a teacher on summer break who
will return to the same job; (b) a graduate who has applied for ten
jobs this month; (c) a person who retired last year; (d) a student who
works 15 hours a week at a café.

**C3.** Match each case to frictional, structural, or cyclical
unemployment: (a) a programmer between jobs for three weeks; (b) a
travel agent whose bookings collapsed in a recession; (c) a factory
worker whose job was automated and who lacks the new skills.

**C4.** Fictional practice data: 4,000 unemployed in a labor force of
80,000. Compute the rate. Then add 2,000 discouraged workers to both
the unemployed and the labor force and recompute. How much did the
rate change?

**C5.** The unemployment rate falls from 6 percent to 5 percent while
the participation rate falls from 64 percent to 61 percent. Explain in
two sentences why the jobs picture may not have improved.

**C6.** In October 2026 the U.S. unemployment rate was 4.1 percent
overall and 13.9 percent for teenagers. Write two sentences: what the
gap shows, and what it does not show without more evidence.

**C7.** Explain in two sentences the difference between "full
employment" and "zero unemployment."

**C8.** The BLS publishes two employment measures: the household survey
(the unemployment rate) and the establishment survey (payroll jobs).
In one sentence each, say what each survey counts and why the two can
disagree in a month.

## Set D — Growth and the Business Cycle (Lesson 04)

**D1.** Quarterly real GDP growth (annualized): +0.8%, −0.2%, −0.9%,
+0.4%. Identify the peak quarter, the trough quarter, and the
contraction's length in quarters.

**D2.** Use the rule of 70: estimate the doubling time for growth
rates of 7 percent, 3.5 percent, and 2.5 percent.

**D3.** Give one example each of technology, physical capital, and
human capital raising output per worker in a hospital.

**D4.** GDP per capita is $15,000 and grows 3 percent a year. Use the
rule of 70 to estimate the doubling time and the approximate GDP per
capita after that time.

**D5.** Classify each indicator as leading, coincident, or lagging:
(a) new building permits; (b) the unemployment rate; (c) new orders
for manufactured goods; (d) retail sales.

**D6.** Real GDP grows 2 percentage points faster than trend for a
year. By Okun's law (rule of thumb), about how much does the
unemployment rate change, and in which direction?

**D7.** A country's population grows 2 percent a year while its real
GDP grows 2 percent a year. What happens to GDP per capita? Explain in
two sentences why population growth matters for living standards.

**D8.** In the short run, unemployment falls below its natural rate.
What does the short-run Phillips curve predict about inflation, and why
does the long-run curve say the tradeoff fades?

## Set E — Reading the Economy (Lesson 05)

**E1.** Rewrite these three headlines so a careful reader can tell
them apart — name each figure's period, units, and source: "Economy
grows 3.4%!", "Prices up 0.4%!", "Unemployment 4.1%!" (Use the
verified releases in the unit README.)

**E2.** Using the three verified releases: (a) What period does the GDP
figure cover, and what period does the CPI figure cover? (b) What is
the next release date for the Q3 2026 GDP estimate?

**E3.** Explain in one paragraph why the October 2026 CPI release has
no 12-month inflation rate, and what a reader should do instead of
guessing.

**E4.** Why will the Q3 2026 advance GDP estimate of 3.4 percent be
revised, and when? Name two things that could change in the revision.

**E5.** Write a three-sentence judgment about the U.S. economy in late
2026: one sentence per indicator (GDP, CPI, unemployment), using the
verified figures.

**E6.** Name two pieces of evidence that would change your judgment in
E5, and say which direction each would push it.

**E7.** The October 2026 unemployment rate was 4.1 percent overall but
6.6 percent for Black workers and 13.9 percent for teenagers. Write
two sentences about what these gaps do and do not establish.

**E8.** Write one paragraph: GDP per capita is the best single summary
of material living standards — and it is not a well-being score. Defend
both halves with evidence from this unit.

## Challenge set

**CH1.** Fictional practice data. Year 1: nominal GDP $800 billion,
GDP deflator 100, population 50 million. Year 2: nominal GDP $880
billion, GDP deflator 110, population 51 million. (a) Compute real GDP
in both years and the real growth rate. (b) Compute real GDP per capita
in both years and its change. (c) In one sentence, explain how output
can be flat while living standards (by this measure) fall.

**CH2.** The CPI fixes its basket; a "chained" CPI lets the basket
change as people substitute toward cheaper goods. Explain in one
paragraph why the chained index usually rises more slowly — and why
that fact is both a better measure of the cost of living and a worse
measure of the price of a fixed standard of living.

**CH3.** Design one indicator GDP misses. Name it, define exactly what
it counts, say how you would collect the data (survey, records, or
observation), and name one way it could be misleading. (Example
starting points: unpaid care hours, leisure time, air quality.)
