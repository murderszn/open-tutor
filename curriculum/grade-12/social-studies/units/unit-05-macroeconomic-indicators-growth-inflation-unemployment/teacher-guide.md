# Teacher Guide — Unit 05: Macroeconomic Indicators

**Grade 12 · Social Studies · Track weeks 20–23 · Issue #57, section U05**

This guide is for the guiding adult. It holds pacing notes, misconception
alerts, reteaching moves, and scoring guidance. **Solutions live only in
the [answer key](answer-key.md)** — never beside a student question.

## Pacing at a glance

Five sessions a week, about 50 minutes each. Lessons 01–04 carry the
explicit teaching (two sessions each, split across S1–S2); Lesson 05 is
one session (S4 of Week 23) plus the Week 23 S3 data investigation.
Sessions S3 are data investigations using the real releases; S4 is
writing or deliberation; S5 is review or the quiz/assessment. The
project runs across Weeks 21–23 (proposal, data work, workshop, due in
Week 23 S5). Do not let the project swallow S1–S2 teaching time: the
dashboard is built in S3 sessions.

## Formula sheet (print for the learner)

- GDP = C + I + G + NX
- Real GDP = (Nominal GDP ÷ GDP deflator) × 100
- CPI = (current basket cost ÷ base basket cost) × 100
- Inflation rate = (CPI now − CPI before) ÷ CPI before × 100
- Unemployment rate = (Unemployed ÷ Labor force) × 100
- Participation rate = (Labor force ÷ Working-age population) × 100
- Doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ annual growth rate in percent

## Misconceptions and how to catch them

| Misconception | What to listen for | Correction move |
|---|---|---|
| GDP counts all money changing hands | "The used car sale adds to GDP" | Ask: was anything *produced* this year? Used goods, transfers, and financial trades move money without new production. |
| Nominal growth = real growth | "GDP grew 6%, so output grew 6%" | Ask for the deflator; run Worked example 2 (Lesson 01) again with the learner's own numbers. |
| Inflation means every price rises | "Bread is up, so inflation is up" | One price is a relative change. The basket is the measure; ask which prices fell. |
| A monthly change is an annual rate | "Inflation is 0.4%" (for October 2026) | Annualize: 0.4% a month compounded is about 4.9% a year. Always name the period. |
| A falling unemployment rate always means more jobs | "Unemployment fell, so the market improved" | Check participation and payrolls. People leaving the labor force lower the rate without adding a job. |
| Full employment means zero unemployment | "4.1% unemployment means 4.1% failure" | Frictional + structural unemployment remain at the natural rate. The question is cyclical unemployment. |
| Two negative quarters = a recession, officially | "Q3–Q4 fell, so it's a recession" | That is a shorthand. The NBER dating committee decides from monthly output, income, employment, and sales. |
| One indicator settles the question | "GDP is up, so we're fine" | Require the other two indicators and one limitation each before any judgment. |

## Reteaching triggers

- **After W20 S5 (Practice Set A):** if GDP computation errors
  persist, return to the table version of Worked example 1 and compute
  NX first, every time, before adding. If classification errors
  persist, re-run the image activity with the learner defending each
  of the five items aloud.
- **After W21 S5 (Practice Set B):** if index math fails, rebuild one
  basket from scratch with the learner pricing each item. If real-wage
  errors persist, drill the division (nominal ÷ price ratio) with
  three quick numbers before any interpretation.
- **After the W22 quiz:** any item below half credit on its set is a
  reteach before Week 23. The most common gap is the labor-force
  denominator (dividing by population instead of the labor force);
  reteach with a 100-person town.
- **After the unit assessment:** gaps feed the Week 36 final review
  and the R00 package. Record which objectives missed (the six unit
  objectives map to assessment parts A–C) and revisit them in the
  final review week.

## Scoring guidance

- **Computation items:** full credit requires the setup (formula with
  numbers substituted), the arithmetic, and the units. A bare correct
  answer earns half credit; a correct setup with an arithmetic slip
  earns half credit plus a note to recheck.
- **Classification items:** the reason is the point. A right
  classification with a wrong reason earns no credit for that item;
  a wrong classification with a sound partial reason earns half.
- **Paragraphs and arguments:** score on the rubric in the answer key
  (claim + evidence + limitation + tradeoff). Accept defensible
  alternatives where the answer key says so; the key gives sample
  reasoning, not the only wording.
- **Project:** use the rubric in [project.md](project.md). A brief
  that invents a figure, misdates a release, or quotes the GDP annual
  rate as a quarterly rise loses the dashboard points for that
  indicator and the summary points that depend on it. A brief that is
  wrong but honest about its limits scores higher than one that is
  confident and unsourced.

## Data verification (adult — do this before teaching)

1. Open the three releases from the Resource Pack and confirm the
   headline figures are still the current ones for their dates. The
   BEA Q3 2026 advance estimate (October 29, 2026) is superseded by
   the second estimate on **November 25, 2026** — if you teach after
   that date, use the second estimate and note the revision. The BLS
   CPI October 2026 release (November 10, 2026) and the Employment
   Situation October 2026 release (November 6, 2026) are the versions
   this unit was written against.
2. If a newer release exists for any indicator, teach from the newer
   one and have the learner record the revision: what changed, and by
   how much. That *is* the lesson about revisions.
3. Never update a figure in the learner's materials without updating
   its date. An undated figure is a failed item in the project rubric.

## Readiness to move on

The learner is ready for Unit 06 when they can, without prompting:
compute GDP from a table, convert nominal to real, compute a CPI and
an inflation rate, compute an unemployment rate and a participation
rate, name the four business-cycle phases, and read one release of
each type (headline figure, period, units, revision status). If any of
those is shaky, use the Week 19 midyear-review pattern (reteach the
weakest modeling skill) before starting Unit 06's policy material,
which assumes these indicators.

## Nonpartisanship and currency notes

Keep examples institutional (roles, rules, evidence), never partisan.
Contested questions get multiple sourced perspectives. All current-data
claims in this unit are dated November 13, 2026, and the adult
re-verifies them at teaching time. Financial and market examples are
educational, not investment advice.
