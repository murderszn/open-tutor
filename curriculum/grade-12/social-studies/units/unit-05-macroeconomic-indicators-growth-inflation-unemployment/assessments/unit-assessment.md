# Culminating Assessment — Unit 05

**40 points · about 60 minutes · closed book except the formula sheet
and the three releases listed below**

Formula sheet (supplied with the assessment): GDP = C + I + G + NX ·
Real GDP = (Nominal GDP ÷ GDP deflator) × 100 · CPI = (current basket
cost ÷ base basket cost) × 100 · Inflation rate = (CPI now − CPI
before) ÷ CPI before × 100 · Unemployment rate = (Unemployed ÷ Labor
force) × 100 · Participation rate = (Labor force ÷ Working-age
population) × 100 · Doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ growth rate.

Releases (adult supplies printed copies; figures verified 2026-11-13,
re-verified at teaching time): BEA, "GDP (Advance Estimate), 3rd
Quarter 2026," October 29, 2026; BLS, "Consumer Price Index — October
2026," November 10, 2026; BLS, "The Employment Situation — October
2026," November 6, 2026. All other numbers are **fictional practice
data** unless a source is named. Show your work; a bare answer earns
half credit. Solutions are in the [answer key](../answer-key.md).

## Part A — Computation (16 points, 4 points each)

**A1.** Fictional practice data ($ billions): consumption 7,200;
investment 1,900; government purchases 2,100; exports 1,300; imports
1,700. (a) Compute net exports and GDP. (b) Compute each component's
share of GDP.

**A2.** Fictional practice data. Year 1: nominal GDP $6,000 billion,
GDP deflator 100. Year 2: nominal GDP $6,720 billion, GDP deflator
112. (a) Compute real GDP in Year 2. (b) Compute the nominal growth
rate and the real growth rate. (c) In one sentence, explain what
happened to output.

**A3.** Fictional practice data. A basket costs $150.00 in the base
year and $168.00 this year. A worker's wage rises from $22.00 to
$24.00 an hour over the same period. (a) Compute the CPI and the
inflation rate. (b) Compute the real wage in base-year dollars and the
real wage change. (c) Is the worker better or worse off? One sentence.

**A4.** Fictional practice data. A town has 78,000 employed people,
4,500 unemployed people, and a working-age population of 130,000.
(a) Compute the labor force, the unemployment rate, and the
participation rate. (b) Suppose 1,500 discouraged workers are added to
both the unemployed and the labor force. Recompute the unemployment
rate and state how much it changed.

## Part B — Reading the releases (12 points, 2 points each)

**B1.** From the BEA release: state the real GDP growth figure for Q3
2026, say whether it is a quarterly change or an annual rate, and
state the period it covers.

**B2.** From the BLS CPI release: state the seasonally adjusted monthly
change for October 2026 and the change for September 2026. Did
inflation speed up or slow down?

**B3.** From the BLS Employment Situation release: state the
unemployment rate, the number of unemployed people, and the change in
nonfarm payroll employment for October 2026.

**B4.** From the Employment Situation release: state the unemployment
rate for teenagers and for the population overall. What does the gap
show, in one sentence?

**B5.** When is the second estimate of Q3 2026 GDP scheduled, and why
does its existence matter for anyone quoting the 3.4 percent figure?

**B6.** Name one limitation of GDP, one of the CPI, and one of the
unemployment rate, each in one sentence. (Three limitations total; two
points for all three, one point for two.)

## Part C — Argument (12 points)

**C1. (12 points)** Write 300–400 words answering: **"What should
count as economic success?"** Use at least one figure from each of the
three releases, name one limitation of each indicator, take a clear
position (for example: GDP per capita is the best single summary; or
well-being needs more than GDP), and name one tradeoff your position
accepts and one piece of evidence that would change your mind.

Scoring: figures used correctly with periods and sources (4 points);
one limitation per indicator (3 points); a clear position defended
with evidence (3 points); tradeoff and falsifying evidence named
(2 points). A position argued from taste without evidence earns at most
4 points total for this item.
