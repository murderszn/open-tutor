# Project — State of the Economy Brief

**Unit 05 · Weeks 21–23 · 40 points**

## The task

You are the briefing writer for a community group that wants an honest
answer to one question: **how is the economy doing?** Your brief must
use the three indicators from this unit — GDP, inflation, and
unemployment — from real, dated, official releases, and it must say
plainly what the indicators cannot settle.

## Deliverables

1. **A one-page indicator dashboard** (table or diagram) with, for each
   indicator: the figure, the period it covers, the source and release
   date, the units, and one limitation.
2. **A brief of 600–800 words** with:
   - a one-paragraph summary judgment (is the economy growing, are
     prices stable, are jobs plentiful — with the figures);
   - one paragraph per indicator, each quoting its figure with period,
     units, and source, and explaining what the figure does and does
     not show;
   - one paragraph on what the indicators miss (distribution, leisure,
     household work, environment, or another gap from Lesson 01);
   - a closing paragraph that takes a position on what "economic
     success" should count (Lesson 05, Worked example 2), names the
     tradeoff that position accepts, and states what evidence would
     change your mind.
3. **A five-minute presentation** of the brief to the adult (or a small
   audience the adult arranges), followed by two questions from the
   audience that you answer from the dashboard, not from memory.

## Data rules

- Use the three verified releases in the unit README (BEA GDP advance
  estimate Q3 2026, October 29, 2026; BLS CPI October 2026, November
  10, 2026; BLS Employment Situation October 2026, November 6, 2026)
  **or** newer releases the adult verifies and dates. Never mix a
  figure from one release with the date of another.
- Every figure carries its period, its units, and its source. A figure
  without a date is a failed item.
- All other numbers you use must be labeled fictional practice data or
  carry a named source with a date. No invented statistics.
- The brief is nonpartisan: institutions, roles, and evidence, not
  parties, candidates, or causes. If a current policy question comes
  up, present at least two sourced perspectives or leave it out.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | Excellent (full points) | Satisfactory (half) | Needs work (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dashboard accuracy (10) | All three figures correct with period, units, source, release date, and a real limitation each | One figure or label wrong or missing; limitations generic | Two or more figures wrong, undated, or unsourced |
| Summary judgment (10) | One paragraph combines all three indicators into a defensible judgment with the figures cited | Judgment uses only one or two indicators, or cites figures without dates | No combined judgment, or judgment contradicts the cited figures |
| What the indicators miss (8) | One paragraph names at least two genuine gaps with concrete examples and explains why each matters | One gap named, or gaps listed without explanation | No gaps discussed, or gaps invented (e.g., a fabricated statistic) |
| Position and tradeoff (6) | Takes a clear position on "economic success," names the tradeoff it accepts, and states what evidence would change it | Position stated but no tradeoff or no falsifying evidence named | No position, or a position argued from taste without evidence |
| Presentation and clarity (6) | Five minutes, clear structure, answers two audience questions from the dashboard | Overruns, rambles, or answers one question from memory instead of the dashboard | No presentation, or the brief is unreadable as written |

**Scoring note for the adult:** a brief that invents a figure, misdates
a release, or quotes the GDP annual rate as a quarterly rise loses the
dashboard points for that indicator and the summary-judgment points
that depend on it. A brief that is wrong but honest about its limits
scores higher than a brief that is confident and unsourced.

## Supports and extensions

- **Support:** the adult pre-marks the headline paragraph of each
  release; the learner may build the dashboard as a table before
  writing any prose.
- **Alternate response mode:** the brief may be delivered orally
  (recorded, 600–800 spoken words) with the dashboard as a handout.
- **Extension:** add a second country (adult-selected, verified and
  dated releases) and a comparability note — different baskets,
  different surveys, different base years — or track the November 25,
  2026 GDP second estimate and revise the brief with a dated
  correction note.
