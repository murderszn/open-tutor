# Answer Key — Unit 05: Macroeconomic Indicators

**For the guiding adult only.** Do not place this file beside student
questions. Every computation below was solved independently of the
lesson text and reconciled with it. Fictional practice data is labeled
as such in the lessons; real figures are the three releases verified
2026-11-13 (see the unit README).

## Lesson 01 — Measuring Output: GDP

**Guided practice**

1. Only the $25,000 car counts. The $400 tire set is an intermediate
   good: its value is already inside the car's price. Adding both
   would count the tires twice ($25,400).
2. NX = 1,800 − 2,300 = −500. GDP = 8,000 + 2,500 + 2,000 + (−500) =
   **$12,000 billion**.
3. Real GDP = (15,000 ÷ 120) × 100 = 15,000 ÷ 1.20 = **$12,500
   billion**.
4. No. G does not rise by $500 million: the checks are transfer
   payments, not government purchases of goods and services. The
   spending counts as consumption later, when the recipients spend it.

**Independent practice**

5. (a) Counts — a final good, consumption. (b) Does not count — a
   used good; it counted when first sold. (c) Does not count —
   household production, not sold in a market. (d) Counts — a final
   service, consumption. (e) Counts — new construction is investment.
   (f) Does not count — a financial transaction; no new productive
   asset is created.
6. Real GDP Year 1 = $10,000 billion. Real GDP Year 2 = 11,000 ÷ 1.10
   = $10,000 billion. Real growth = **0%**. No, output was not 10
   percent larger — the entire nominal rise was higher prices.
7. GDP per capita = $480 billion ÷ 40 million = **$12,000**. Doubling
   time at 2 percent ≈ 70 ÷ 2 = **35 years**.

**Applied task (sample reasoning — accept defensible alternatives).**
A 5 percent rise in GDP does not mean people are 5 percent better
off. GDP misses household production (unpaid care and housework) and
distribution (a total that says nothing about who gains). Before
answering I would want real GDP per capita growth and the change in
real wages — or one distributional figure, such as the unemployment
rate by group.

**Exit check**

1. GDP is the market value of all final goods and services produced
   inside a country in a given period.
2. The flour's value is included in the loaf's price; counting the
   flour and the loaf counts the flour twice.
3. No. Subtracting is an approximation. The exact method is to divide
   by the price ratio: real growth = (1.08 ÷ 1.05) − 1 ≈ 2.9%.

## Lesson 02 — Measuring Prices: Inflation

**Guided practice**

1. CPI = (189.10 ÷ 180.50) × 100 = **104.76**. Inflation rate =
   (104.76 − 100) ÷ 100 = **4.76%, about 4.8%**.
2. Real wage = 26.00 ÷ (210 ÷ 200) = 26.00 ÷ 1.05 = **$24.76**.
   Change = (24.76 − 25.00) ÷ 25.00 = **−0.95%, about −1.0%** — the
   real wage fell.
3. The payment is worth 1,500 ÷ 1.04 = **$1,442.31** in last year's
   dollars. Borrowers gain (they repay in dollars that buy less);
   lenders and people on fixed incomes lose.
4. No. Energy prices still rose — 0.7 percent after 5.2 percent — so
   this is **disinflation** (inflation slowing), not deflation (prices
   falling).

**Independent practice**

5. Year 2 CPI = (436 ÷ 400) × 100 = **109**; inflation = **9%**.
   Year 3 CPI = (445.72 ÷ 400) × 100 = **111.43**; Year 2→Year 3
   inflation = (111.43 − 109) ÷ 109 = **2.2%**. Inflation slowed down.
6. Easier. The loan payment is fixed at $300 in nominal dollars while
   the borrower's wage kept pace with prices (both +6 percent), so the
   payment takes a smaller share of real income: in real terms it is
   300 ÷ 1.06 ≈ $283.
7. (Sample.) The 12-month change needs the October 2025 index, and
   BLS did not collect October 2025 survey data because of a lapse in
   appropriations, so that index does not exist for most series. A
   reader should use the monthly changes and the index level, and wait
   for a later release — never guess the missing year-ago figure.

**Applied task (sample, using the October 2026 release).** (a)
Seasonally adjusted monthly change +0.4 percent. (b) Index level
337.511 (1982–84 = 100). (c) Fastest: energy, +0.7 percent (food
+0.5 percent); slowest: shelter and core, +0.3 percent. (d) No
12-month change is published — October 2025 data were not collected.
Two sentences: prices were still rising in October, but more slowly
than in September; the release says nothing about the year-on-year
inflation rate.

**Exit check**

1. Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level.
2. (260 − 250) ÷ 250 × 100 = **4%**.
3. Helps: borrowers (repay in cheaper dollars). Hurts: lenders and
   people on fixed incomes (repaid dollars buy less).

## Lesson 03 — Measuring Work: Unemployment

**Guided practice**

1. (a) Employed — working for pay. (b) Unemployed — available and
   actively looking. (c) Out of the labor force — retired, not
   seeking work. (d) Out of the labor force — student, not seeking
   work. (e) Out of the labor force — discouraged worker (stopped
   looking). (f) Employed — has a job, temporarily absent.
2. Labor force = 118,000 + 6,000 = **124,000**. Unemployment rate =
   6,000 ÷ 124,000 × 100 = **4.8%**. Participation rate = 124,000 ÷
   200,000 × 100 = **62.0%**. Out of the labor force = 76,000.
3. **Structural.** The jobs exist but not for the workers' skills
   (robot-tending, not welding); retraining or time, not a spending
   boost, is the fix.
4. Not clearly better. The rate fell while participation fell 3 points,
   so part of the decline is people leaving the labor force — they are
   no longer counted at all.

**Independent practice**

5. Labor force = 90 + 6 = **96 million**. Unemployment rate = 6 ÷ 96
   × 100 = **6.25%, about 6.3%**. Participation = 96 ÷ 160 × 100 =
   **60.0%**. With 2 million discouraged added: unemployed 8 million,
   labor force 98 million → 8 ÷ 98 × 100 = **8.16%, about 8.2%** — up
   **1.9 percentage points**.
6. (Sample.) Frictional: a graduate spends three weeks finding a first
   job. Structural: a typist's job disappears to software and the
   worker lacks the new skills. Cyclical: a server is laid off when a
   recession cuts restaurant spending.
7. (Sample.) The gap shows teenagers face far higher unemployment than
   the average worker. It does not show why — school enrollment,
   experience, local conditions, or discrimination would each need
   separate evidence.

**Applied task (sample, using the October 2026 release).** (a)
Unemployment rate 4.1 percent; 7.1 million unemployed. (b) Nonfarm
payrolls rose 165,000. (c) Teenagers 13.9 percent against 4.1 percent
overall. (d) "Both the unemployment rate, at 4.1 percent, and the
number of unemployed people, at 7.1 million, changed little in
October." Two sentences: the release shows a steady labor market with
low unemployment and continued job gains — but the headline hides
discouraged and underemployed workers, so the participation rate and
the broader U-6 measure are the next things to check.

**Exit check**

1. The labor force is the employed plus the unemployed.
2. 8,000 ÷ 160,000 × 100 = **5.0%**.
3. Frictional (between jobs); structural (skills/place mismatch);
   cyclical (recession layoffs).

## Lesson 04 — Growth and the Business Cycle

**Guided practice**

1. Peak: end of the second quarter (+0.5% is the last growth quarter).
   Trough: end of the fourth quarter (−1.1% is the last decline
   quarter). Contraction: the third and fourth quarters — **2
   quarters**. By the two-quarter shorthand, yes, this is a recession;
   the NBER would confirm from monthly data.
2. Doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ 3 = **23.3 years**; GDP per capita ≈ **$16,000**
   after that time.
3. (Sample.) Technology: a new convection oven that bakes twice as
   fast. Physical capital: the oven itself (or a delivery van).
   Human capital: a baker trained in a new technique.

**Independent practice**

4. Country A: doubling time 70 ÷ 1 = **70 years**; after 35 years,
   10,000 × (1.01)^35 ≈ **$14,200** (about half a doubling).
   Country B: doubling time 70 ÷ 5 = **14 years**; after 35 years,
   about 2.5 doublings → 10,000 × (1.05)^35 ≈ **$55,200** (rule-of-70
   estimate: about $56,000). The point is the gap: the same starting
   point, very different living standards after one generation.
5. Both contractions lose **2 percent** of output in total (2 × 1% and
   4 × 0.5%). The arithmetic does not settle which is a recession: the
   NBER weighs depth, duration, and diffusion across monthly
   employment, income, and sales data — the longer one has duration,
   the steeper one has depth, and neither fact alone decides.
6. (Sample.) GDP per capita can rise while well-being falls if the
   gains go to a few (distribution — GDP is a total) and if the extra
   output comes with longer hours (leisure — GDP records output, not
   lost time) or pollution (environmental costs — damage subtracts
   nothing from GDP).

**Applied task (sample).** Q3 2026 real GDP grew at a 3.4 percent
annual rate after 2.2 percent in Q2 — output was rising, so the
economy was in an **expansion**. Two quarters cannot establish a peak
or a trough (those need a turning point), and the figure is an advance
estimate that will be revised on November 25, 2026.

**Exit check**

1. Expansion, peak, contraction, trough.
2. 70 ÷ 3 ≈ **23.3 years**.
3. Technology, physical capital, and human capital.

## Lesson 05 — Reading the Economy

**Guided practice**

1. (Sample.) "Real GDP grew at a 3.4 percent annual rate in the third
   quarter of 2026 (July–September), BEA advance estimate, October 29,
   2026." "The CPI-U rose 0.4 percent in October 2026 (seasonally
   adjusted monthly change), BLS, November 10, 2026." "The
   unemployment rate was 4.1 percent of the labor force in October
   2026, BLS, November 6, 2026."
2. (a) Slower: 0.4 percent in October after 0.6 percent in September —
   **0.2 percentage points slower**. (b) "Both the unemployment rate,
   at 4.1 percent, and the number of unemployed people, at 7.1
   million, changed little in October."
3. It ignores that consumer prices were still rising 0.4 percent in
   October, and that unemployment was 4.1 percent with teenagers at
   13.9 percent. One GDP limitation: it is an advance estimate that
   will be revised on November 25, 2026 (or: it says nothing about
   distribution or well-being).

**Independent practice**

4. (Sample.) GDP grew only 1.0 percent at an annual rate — slow
   growth. Prices rose 0.9 percent in one month, about 11 percent
   annualized — fast inflation. Unemployment was 9.2 percent — high.
   Combined: a weak economy with high inflation and high
   unemployment. Limitation: one month and one quarter do not make a
   trend; the figures need revision status and the participation rate
   before the judgment is firm.
5. (Sample.) A 12-month rate compares this October with last October,
   and last October's index does not exist because BLS did not collect
   the data. The 0.4 percent monthly change is a different window — one
   month, not twelve — so it cannot substitute; the reader should use
   the monthly changes and the index level, and wait for a later
   release.
6. (Sample, either position accepted if it uses figures and names a
   tradeoff.) Position B: the economy grew 3.4 percent, but prices
   rose 0.4 percent in October and unemployment was 13.9 percent for
   teenagers — production alone does not show who is secure. Tradeoff
   accepted: well-being is harder to measure than output, so the
   judgment is less precise.

**Applied task (sample dashboard).**

| Indicator | Figure | Period | Source / release date | Units | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Real GDP growth | +3.4% | Q3 2026 (Jul–Sep) | BEA, Oct 29, 2026 (advance) | Annual rate | Advance estimate; revised Nov 25, 2026 |
| CPI inflation | +0.4% | October 2026 | BLS, Nov 10, 2026 | Monthly change, SA | No 12-month change published |
| Unemployment rate | 4.1% | October 2026 | BLS, Nov 6, 2026 | % of labor force | Excludes discouraged workers |

Judgment (sample): "In late 2026 the U.S. economy was growing, with
inflation still rising month to month but moderating, and a steady
labor market near 4 percent unemployment." What would change it: a
downward GDP revision, or a sharp rise in the unemployment rate.

**Exit check**

1. An annual rate is a quarterly pace projected over a year; a monthly
   change is one month's movement; a level is a share of a group at a
   point in time.
2. GDP: misses household production (or distribution, leisure,
   environment). CPI: fixed basket / substitution bias (or urban-only).
   Unemployment rate: excludes discouraged workers (or the
   underemployed).
3. November 25, 2026. It matters because the advance estimate is built
   on incomplete data and will be revised.

  environmental costs (pollution); distribution (who gets the output).

  True — a final service, consumption. (c) False — a used good.

**Set C**

- C8. The household survey (CPS) counts *people* by labor-force
  status; the establishment survey counts *payroll jobs*. They can
  disagree because one counts people (including the self-employed, and

**Set D**

## Culminating assessment

**Part A**

- A1. (a) NX = 1,300 − 1,700 = **−$400 billion**. GDP = 7,200 +
  1,900 + 2,100 − 400 = **$10,800 billion**. (b) Shares: C 7,200 ÷

  Nominal growth = (6,720 − 6,000) ÷ 6,000 = **12%**. Real growth =

**Part B**

- B1. Real GDP rose at a **3.4 percent annual rate** in Q3 2026
  (July–September) — an annual rate, not the quarterly change (0.8
  percent).
- B2. October **+0.4 percent** (seasonally adjusted) after September
  **+0.6 percent** — inflation slowed down (disinflation).
- B3. Unemployment rate **4.1 percent**; **7.1 million** unemployed;

  worker; it does not show why without more evidence.
- B5. **November 25, 2026.** The advance estimate is built on

**Part C — argument (12 points)**

Sample thesis (accept defensible alternatives): "GDP per capita is the
best single summary of material living standards — real GDP grew at a
3.4 percent annual rate in Q3 2026 — but it is not a well-being score:
consumer prices rose 0.4 percent in October, and unemployment was 4.1
percent overall but 13.9 percent for teenagers. Success should count
output per person *and* who shares in it *and* what prices do to real
wages. The tradeoff this position accepts is precision: well-being is
harder to measure than output. A downward GDP revision or a sharp rise
in unemployment would change my mind."

Score: figures correct with periods/sources (4); one limitation per
indicator (3); clear position with evidence (3); tradeoff and
falsifying evidence (2). A position argued from taste without evidence
earns at most 4 points total.

## Project scoring notes

Use the rubric in [project.md](project.md). Check every dashboard
figure against its release (figure, period, units, source, release
date). A brief that invents a figure, misdates a release, or quotes
