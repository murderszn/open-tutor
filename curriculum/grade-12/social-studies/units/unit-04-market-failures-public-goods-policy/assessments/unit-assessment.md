# Culminating Assessment — Unit 04: Market Failures, Public Goods, and Policy Tradeoffs

**Points: 40.** Open notes (lessons and practice only; the answer key
is not permitted). All numbers are fictional practice data. Show your
setup for every computation; a bare number earns at most half credit.
A complete response names tradeoffs and losers where asked — an
argument with no loser is unfinished.

## Task 1 — Externality graph and Pigouvian fix (10 points)

Demand is P = 110 − Q and marginal private cost is P = 30 + Q. Each
unit imposes a $20 external cost on third parties.

(a) Write MSC. (2 points)
(b) Find the market quantity and price. (2 points)
(c) Find the efficient quantity and the price at that quantity.
(2 points)
(d) Compute deadweight loss, and state the triangle's base and height.
(2 points)
(e) State the Pigouvian tax and verify in one sentence that it moves
the market to the efficient quantity. (2 points)

## Task 2 — Classify and explain (8 points)

Classify each good with both tests (excludable? rival?) and name its
box, with one reason each (1 point each, 4 points total):

(a) A campsite in a national park on a packed holiday weekend.
(b) A freely available podcast episode.
(c) A toll bridge with a per-crossing charge.
(d) Tuna in international waters.

Then (4 points): pick one good above that a private firm would
under-provide and one resource above that would be overused, and
explain each outcome with the free-rider or commons logic in 2–3
sentences each.

## Task 3 — Information failure (8 points)

Used phones: good phones are worth $500 to buyers and $450 to
sellers; bad phones are worth $100 to buyers and $80 to sellers. Half
the phones are good, and buyers cannot tell which is which.

(a) Compute the expected value a buyer will pay, and state whether
good-phone sellers stay in the market. (3 points)
(b) Propose one signal a good-phone seller could use, and explain why
it works only if it is costly to fake. (2–3 sentences, 2 points)
(c) Propose one screen a buyer could use, and explain what makes it
separate good phones from bad ones. (2–3 sentences, 2 points)
(d) Name the failure (adverse selection or moral hazard) and justify
it with the timing test in one sentence. (1 point)

## Task 4 — Policy evaluation (8 points)

Two pollution-control options (fictional annual data): Option R costs
$90 and benefits $180; Option S costs $190 and benefits $260.

(a) Compute both net benefits. (2 points)
(b) Compute the marginal net benefit of moving R → S, and state which
option is efficient and why. (3 points)
(c) Name one unintended consequence that could arise under your
chosen option, state whether it is a mechanism or a sourced claim
(you may reason it out as a mechanism — label it as such), and say
what evidence would confirm or refute it. (3 points)

## Task 5 — Distribution (6 points)

Before a tax, a good sells at $40. A $5 tax leaves buyers paying $43
and sellers keeping $38.

(a) Split the $5 between buyers and sellers in dollars and
percentages. (2 points)
(b) Household A (income $25,000) pays $150 of this tax in a year;
Household B (income $125,000) pays $180. Compute each share of income
and classify the tax. (2 points)
(c) State one meaning of fairness (equal treatment, equal
opportunity, or narrowing outcomes) and give a one-sentence verdict
on this tax under that meaning. (2 points)
