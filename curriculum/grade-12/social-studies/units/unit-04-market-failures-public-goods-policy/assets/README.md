- **Prompt (as given to the generator):** "A clear, simple educational

- **Learning purpose:** give learners a single shared scene in which
  private goods (picnic food), a congestible playground, lamp light,
  boats, ducks, and an open-access fishery appear together, so the
  two-tests classification is practiced on one scene rather than on
  abstract definitions alone.

  readable text, words, or numbers; it shows a wooden dock with three
  people fishing, a family picnicking on a blanket, a playground slide
  and swings with children, lamps along a path, a sailboat, a rowboat,
  a covered pedal boat, and ducks on the lake. No real place is
  depicted and no claim is made about any real park: the scene is an
  AI illustration used for classification practice, not evidence
  about a real location.
- **Alt text:** "A sunny lakeside public park: people fishing from a
  wooden dock, a family picnicking on a blanket, children on a
  playground slide and swings, lamps along a path, sailboats and a
  rowboat on the lake, and ducks on the water."
- **Caption:** "An AI-generated illustration of a shared lakeside
  park — dock fishing, a picnic, a playground, lamps, boats, and
  ducks. Used for the classification activity below."
- **Text-only alternative:** printed in Lesson 02 §3 alongside the
  image; it lists the same six elements (dock fishing, picnic food,
  playground, lamps, boats, ducks) so the activity is fully answerable

- **Known limitations:** the illustration is stylized, not to scale;
  it does not show prices, tickets, fences, or rules, so learners
  must state their assumptions when classifying (for example, whether
  the park is open-access). That ambiguity is intentional: the lesson
  teaches that classifications describe a good *as used*, not a
  permanent label.
