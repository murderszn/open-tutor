# Project — Policy Tradeoff Brief

**Deliverable:** a 3–5 page brief (written, or presented orally with a
one-page evidence table) plus a one-page appendix of sources and
figures. **Total: 40 points.** Work runs across Week 17 S3 (proposal)
and Week 18 S3 (workshop); the final brief is due Week 18 S5. An adult
approves the case and the sources before drafting begins.

## Task

Choose one real, local, adult-approved market failure — a congested
road, a polluting facility, a shared fishery or park, a vaccination
gap, a market with visibly asymmetric information, or another
age-appropriate case. Your brief must:

1. **Diagnose (10 points).** Name the failure type (externality,
   public good, common resource, information failure, market power,
   or a combination) and show the mechanism: who the third parties
   are, which curves shift and in which direction, and whether the
   market over- or under-produces. Use a labeled graph or a schedule
   with fictional practice numbers, or dated real figures with their
   source and date — never present an estimate as a measurement.
2. **Compare two policy options (10 points).** Choose two different
   tools from Lesson 04 (for example, a tax versus cap-and-trade, or
   regulation versus direct provision). For each, sketch benefits and
   costs in a table, apply a marginal check to any expansion choice,
   and state which option you recommend and why.
3. **Show who pays and who benefits (8 points).** Include a small
   table: at least two groups, what each pays or gains, and — where
   figures exist — burdens or gains as shares of income. State which
   meaning of fairness you are using (equal treatment, equal
   opportunity, or narrowing outcomes) and judge your recommendation
   by it.
4. **Name the tradeoffs honestly (8 points).** State at least one
   intended consequence, one unintended consequence (with a source
   or a reasoned mechanism, labeled as such), one group that loses
   under your recommendation, and the institutional precondition your
   policy needs (property rights, enforcement, or records) — plus what
   happens if that precondition is weak.
5. **Communicate clearly (4 points).** A non-specialist adult can
   follow the argument; every figure is labeled fictional or
   dated-real with a source; no party, candidate, or cause is endorsed.

## Steps

1. **Proposal (Week 17 S3).** One paragraph: the case, the failure
   type, and the two tools you will compare. Adult approves or
   redirects.
2. **Evidence gathering (Week 17 S3–S4).** Two perspectives on the
   policy's effects (see Lesson 04 §7). Record each source's
   publication and date, and one sentence of what it claims.
3. **Drafting (Week 18 S3 workshop).** Diagnosis graph, options
   table, distribution table, tradeoff paragraph. Peer or adult
   review against the rubric below.
4. **Revision and submission (Week 18 S5).** Final brief plus source
   appendix. A brief with no named loser and no unintended
   consequence is returned for revision before scoring.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | Full credit | Partial credit | Little/no credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diagnosis (10) | Failure type correct; mechanism traced to over/underproduction; graph or schedule labeled and consistent | Type correct but mechanism vague or graph inconsistent | Wrong type, or no mechanism shown |
| Options comparison (10) | Two distinct tools; benefit–cost tables; marginal check applied; recommendation follows the numbers | Two tools compared but no marginal check, or recommendation ignores the tables | One tool only, or no benefit–cost reasoning |
| Distribution (8) | Two+ groups; shares of income where figures exist; fairness meaning stated and applied | Groups named but no shares, or fairness meaning unstated | No distribution analysis |
| Tradeoffs (8) | Intended + unintended consequences; a losing group named; institutional precondition + weak-case outcome | One of the four elements missing | Tradeoffs absent or denied |
| Communication (4) | Clear to a non-specialist; figures labeled fictional or dated-real with sources; nonpartisan framing | Mostly clear; one labeling gap | Unclear, unlabeled figures, or partisan framing |

**Scoring notes for the adult.** A brief may recommend *no*
intervention, if the benefit–cost and government-failure analysis
supports it — "do nothing" is a policy option, not an evasion, when it
is argued with numbers. A brief that endorses a party, candidate, or
cause should be returned for reframing in institutional terms, not
scored down silently: the point is the argument's evidence, not its
conclusion.
