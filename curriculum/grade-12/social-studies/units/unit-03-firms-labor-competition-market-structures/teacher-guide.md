# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Firms, Labor, Competition, and Market Structures

**For the guiding adult.** Student materials never contain answers.
This guide gives teaching notes, misconception alerts, reteach
triggers, and scoring guidance. Full solutions are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## Pacing notes

- **Week 11 (firms and costs):** Lesson 1 is conceptual and fast for
  most learners; spend the saved time on Lesson 2's table work. Do
  not let the W11S3 investigation become a graphing exercise — the
  table is the evidence, the graph is optional. Lesson 2's extension
  (price $12) is a deliberate trap: at $12 the 70-loaf step has
  MC $10 < MR $12, so the best output *within the table* rises to 70
  (profit $340 vs. $320 at 60). If a learner first answers 60, praise
  the instinct, then rework the last step together — that correction
  is the lesson.
- **Week 12 (labor):** The BLS table is the unit's only real dataset.
  Spend time on its footnotes: medians, full-time workers, age 25+,
  2024 annual averages. Learners who skip the footnotes will overclaim
  in the argument task; require the "median / full-time / 25+ / 2024"
  qualifier in their first BLS sentence.
- **Week 13 (structures):** Classification must be evidence-first.
  Ban the sentence "X is a monopoly because it is big" from the first
  draft; require the three tests in order. The image activity's
  oligopoly scene (two towers) legitimately supports "duopoly" as an
  answer — accept it with evidence.
- **Week 14 (innovation and institutions):** The research lab source
  is adult-selected. Preview it for: a named new combination, a
  stated risk, and any displacement. If the source lacks one element,
  tell the learner to report the gap rather than inventing it — the
  rubric rewards honest source gaps.

## Misconceptions and reteach triggers

| Misconception | Correction | Reteach if... |
|---|---|---|
| Limited liability means the firm cannot fail | It limits the *owner's* loss, not the firm's failure; workers and creditors can still lose | Exit check for Lesson 1 misses the distinction |
| Maximize revenue, not profit | Revenue ignores cost; the 70-loaf bakery earns the most revenue ($630) but less profit ($130) than at 60 loaves ($140) | Lesson 2 guided practice Q2 or practice Q7 wrong |
| Shut down at any loss | In the short run, operate if price covers AVC — operating then loses less than the fixed cost alone | Quiz Q4 or assessment A3 wrong |
| VMP = total output, or average output | VMP is the *extra* revenue from *one more* worker; hiring compares that margin with the wage | Lesson 3 guided practice Q1 wrong |
| BLS gaps prove a degree causes the whole gap | Gaps are associations across groups differing in occupation, hours, experience, location, and more | Lesson 3 argument lacks the caution |
| Big = monopoly | Monopoly is one seller plus barriers; a huge firm with close substitutes and easy entry is not a monopoly | Lesson 4 guided practice or quiz Q10 wrong |
| Creative destruction helps everyone | It helps buyers on average and displaces specific workers and firms; both sides belong in the account | Lesson 5 practice Q18 names only gainers |

## Reteach paths

- **Costs:** Re-teach with a 3-row mini-schedule (Q = 0, 10, 20) and
  ask only "expand or stop?" at each step, before returning to the
  full bakery table.
- **Labor shocks:** Use the sentence frame "___ shifted, so wage ___
  and employment ___ because ___" for four shocks in a row, until
  wage and employment are predicted together every time.
- **Structures:** Return to the three tests as a physical checklist;
  classify five new markets orally before any written classification.
- **Institutions:** Reuse the Lesson 5 loan chain (property record →
  contract → loan → tractor → output) and break one link at a time,
  asking what shrinks.

## Assessment guidance

- **Quiz (30 points):** Formative. A score below 21, or any error on
  Q3 (marginal rule) or Q4 (shutdown), triggers a Week 14 reteach
  before the unit assessment; do not let a weak quiz pass silently
  into Unit 04.
- **Unit assessment (80 points):** Score A1 by the table, not by the
  final output alone — a learner with one arithmetic slip but correct
  marginal reasoning keeps most of A2. For E (synthesis), full marks
  require a tradeoff *and* an information need; a one-sided verdict,
  however well written, caps at 6/10.
- **Project (40 points):** Use the rubric in
  [project.md](project.md). Real-market claims without a dated source
  lose the affected criterion's top band, even if the classification
  is plausible.
- **Readiness for Unit 04:** The learner is ready if they can (a)
  find profit-maximizing output from a fresh schedule, (b) predict
  wage and employment effects of a labor shock, and (c) classify a
  market from evidence. Otherwise, reteach the weakest of the three
  before Unit 04's externality analysis, which assumes all three.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Table-first versions of every graph task; oral or
  drawn responses accepted; the BLS table pre-highlighted to the five
  rows used; the three-tests checklist printed beside classification
  tasks.
- **Extension:** Lesson extensions (LLC local rules, price-$12
  recomputation, BLS by-sex medians, two-oligopoly comparison,
  institutional-reform research) are optional and never prerequisites.
  The strongest extension for a fast learner is the project Part 1
  second market with a sourced barrier analysis.
