# Project — Market Structure Field Report (40 points)

**Deliverable:** A short report (about 4–6 pages, or the adult-approved
equivalent in slides or a spoken presentation with notes) covering
the four parts below. Real markets must use adult-selected, dated,
sourced figures; fictional practice data may be used only where a part
says so. All sources are listed at the end with dates checked.

## Part 1 — Classify two markets (12 points)

Pick two real markets you can observe or research (adult-approved;
one local, one national, is ideal). For each, apply Lesson 4's three
tests with one observable fact per test: approximate number of
sellers and shares, product sameness or differences, and a barrier to
entry (or its absence). Classify each market, state how confident you
are ("clear," "mostly," or "borderline") and why, and name one fact
that would change your classification.

## Part 2 — A firm's cost decision (12 points)

Use the Harbor Bakery schedule from the unit README. (a) Reproduce
the profit column and identify the profit-maximizing output at $9,
showing the marginal-rule reasoning, not just the largest profit
number. (b) Suppose the price falls to $5. Recompute profit at 30, 40,
50, and 60 loaves (TR = price × Q; TC from the schedule) and state
the new profit-maximizing output within the table, plus the shutdown
verdict at $5 (compare price with AVC at your chosen output). Show
every step so the adult can check the arithmetic.

## Part 3 — Wages in the data (8 points)

Using the BLS 2024 earnings table from Lesson 3: (a) compute two gaps
of your choice (a difference and a ratio for each, with the medians
named); (b) write a careful paragraph explaining what the gaps show
and what they do not prove, naming at least two non-education factors
and the correlation-causation caution; (c) name one additional data
source (occupation, hours, or experience breakdown) that would sharpen
the explanation, and say what it would help separate.

## Part 4 — Entrepreneur and institutions (8 points)

From the Week 14 research lab source: (a) describe one entrepreneur's
new combination and the risk borne; (b) name what, if anything, it
displaced; (c) argue which institution — property rights, rule of
law, or contract enforcement — the venture most depended on, with one
fact from the source (or state honestly that the source does not
answer this, and say what source would).

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | 100% | 70% | 40% | 0% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Part 1 classification (12) | Both markets classified with one fact per test; confidence stated; a classification-changing fact named | Both classified; one test thin or one confidence missing | One market classified, or classifications asserted without facts | No classification or facts |
| Part 2 cost analysis (12) | Profit column correct; $9 and $5 outputs justified by the marginal rule; shutdown verdict correct with AVC comparison | Arithmetic mostly correct; one step or the shutdown comparison weak | Profit column attempted; reasoning missing or shutdown verdict wrong | No usable analysis |
| Part 3 wage data (8) | Both gaps computed correctly; paragraph states the caution and two non-education factors; a sharpening source named | Gaps correct; caution or factors thin | Gaps attempted with errors; no caution | No data work |
| Part 4 entrepreneur (8) | New combination, risk, displacement, and institution argued with a sourced fact (or an honest source gap) | Three of four elements, with a sourced fact | Description only, no argument or source | Missing |

loaves is $150/$200/$250/$300; TC is $220/$260/$320/$400; profit is
