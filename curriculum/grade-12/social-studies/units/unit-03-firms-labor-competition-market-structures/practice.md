# Practice — Unit 03: Firms, Labor, Competition, and Market Structures

Work from the lessons. All firm and café numbers below are fictional
practice data, except where a question explicitly uses the BLS 2024
table from Lesson 3. Solutions are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md); do not read them before attempting
the set.

## Lesson 1 — Business organizations

1. A founder wants limited liability and the ability to sell shares
   to strangers, and accepts formal record-keeping. Which form, and
   which two features decide it?
2. Two partners disagree: one signed a supply contract the other
   opposed. In a general partnership, who bears the contract's cost?
   Why?
3. A shareholder invested $2,000 in a corporation that fails owing
   far more. What is the most she can lose as a shareholder?
4. Name one tax tradeoff of the C corporation form, in one sentence.

## Lesson 2 — Costs, revenue, profit-maximizing output

Use the Harbor Bakery schedule in the unit README (price $9; profit
peaks at 60 loaves, $140).

5. At 50 loaves, compute TR, TC, and profit. Show each step.
6. Compute MC per loaf for the step from 60 to 70 loaves. Should the
   bakery take that step at $9? Why?
7. At 30 loaves, compute AVC. If the price fell to $3, should the
   bakery operate at 30 loaves or shut down in the short run? Why?
8. A firm has FC $80 and VC $0, $40, $90, $150 for Q = 0, 10, 20, 30.
   Its price is $7. Find TR, TC, and profit at each output, and state
   its profit-maximizing output within this table.
9. Explain the difference between accounting profit and economic
   profit, and what "normal profit" means for entry and exit.

## Lesson 3 — Labor markets

10. Extra output from one more worker is 20 units a day at $6 each.
    The daily wage is $100. Compute VMP and state the hiring decision.
11. A new technology makes each worker in an occupation produce twice
    as much. Predict wage and employment effects, naming the curve
    that shifted and the mechanism (VMP).
12. From the BLS 2024 table: (a) the ratio of the bachelor's-only
    median ($1,543) to the less-than-high-school median ($738);
    (b) the weekly difference between the advanced-degree median
    ($1,901) and the some-college/associate median ($1,056).
13. Give two reasons, other than schooling itself, why median earnings
    differ across education groups, and explain why the table alone
    cannot separate them.

## Lesson 4 — Market structures

14. Classify each market, with one evidence phrase: (a) a city with
    40 identical wheat sellers and free entry; (b) a city with 30
    restaurants, each with its own menu and style; (c) a country
    with three mobile carriers and huge network costs; (d) a town
    with one water company and one pipe network.
15. A monopolist sells 80 units at $12, while a competitive market
    would sell 110 units at $8 (MC = $8, fictional illustration).
    What are the price and output effects of monopoly here, and what
    is the deadweight-loss idea in one sentence?
16. Why does a monopolistically competitive firm earn (tend toward)
    zero long-run economic profit despite having some price control?
17. A huge firm sells a product with many close substitutes and faces
    easy entry. Is it a monopoly? Explain with the three tests.

## Lesson 5 — Innovation and institutions

18. Give an original example of creative destruction and name both a
    group that gains and a group that loses.
19. A supplier refuses to deliver on credit because contracts are
    rarely enforced in its region. Which institution is weak, and what
    happens to trade as a result?
20. An entrepreneur risks her savings on a new delivery method. Name
    the two features of entrepreneurship this shows (new combination
    and risk), and explain each in one sentence.
21. **Synthesis:** A government considers breaking up a natural
    monopoly into five competing firms. Use Lesson 4's water-pipes
    reasoning to argue one reason for and one reason against, then
    state what information you would want before deciding.
