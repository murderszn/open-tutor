# Unit Assessment — Firms, Labor, Competition, and Market Structures (summative, 80 points)

Covers all six objectives. Fictional numbers are practice data unless
a question names the BLS table. Show arithmetic where asked.
Solutions and scoring guidance are in
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md) — do not open it until the adult
collects the assessment.

## A. Firm costs and profit (20 points)

A fictional firm, the Riverside Workshop, sells chairs at $20 each.
Its weekly costs are:

| Chairs per week (Q) | FC | VC | TC |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | $120 | $0 | $120 |
| 10 | $120 | $100 | $220 |
| 20 | $120 | $180 | $300 |
| 30 | $120 | $240 | $360 |
| 40 | $120 | $320 | $440 |
| 50 | $120 | $420 | $540 |

1. (8 points) Complete the TR, profit, and MC-per-chair columns
   (MC per 10-chair step) for all rows, showing at least one MC
   computation in full.
2. (6 points) State the profit-maximizing output within this table
   and its profit, justifying it *both* from the profit column and
   from the marginal rule.
3. (6 points) Suppose the price falls to $9. (a) At 20 chairs, compute
   TR and profit, and AVC. (b) Should the workshop operate or shut
   down in the short run at that output? Justify with the shutdown
   rule and a comparison with the $120 fixed-cost loss.

## B. Labor markets (16 points)

4. (5 points) A worker's extra output is 25 units a day at $8 each.
   The daily wage is $180. Compute VMP and state the hiring decision
   with a reason.
5. (5 points) A licensing change lets many more workers enter an
   occupation. Predict wage and employment effects, name the curve
   that shifted, and explain the mechanism in one sentence.
6. (6 points) From the BLS 2024 table: (a) compute the ratio of the
   advanced-degree median ($1,901) to the high school median ($930),
   to two decimal places; (b) write a careful paragraph (3–4
   sentences) explaining what the earnings gaps show and what they do
   not prove, naming at least two non-education factors.

## C. Market structures (20 points)

7. (12 points) Classify each market, with one evidence phrase per
   classification: (a) 200 wheat farmers selling identical wheat,
   free entry; (b) 25 cafés in a town, each with its own menu and
   atmosphere; (c) two airlines serving one route, with huge
   aircraft and gate costs; (d) one city water company, one pipe
   network.
8. (8 points) A monopolist (fictional illustration) sells 60 units at
   $15, while a competitive market would sell 100 units at $10
   (MC = $10). (a) State the price and output effects of monopoly.
   (b) Explain deadweight loss in this example: which trades are
   lost, and why would they have benefited both sides?

## D. Innovation and institutions (14 points)

9. (7 points) Define creative destruction and give one original
   example, naming both a group that gains and a group that loses.
10. (7 points) A lender refuses to lend to a workshop because
    property records are unreliable and contracts are rarely enforced.
    Explain, using the property-rights and contract-enforcement chain
    from Lesson 5, why lending shrinks — and what that means for the
    workshop's growth.

## E. Synthesis (10 points)

11. (10 points) A town council debates whether to break its single
    water company into four competing firms. Write a balanced
    paragraph: one reason the Lesson 4 natural-monopoly reasoning
    supports keeping one regulated firm, one reason competition might
    still help, and one piece of information you would want before
    deciding. (Full marks require the tradeoff and the information
    need, not a one-sided verdict.)
