# Unit 02 Assessment — Supply, Demand, Markets, and Price Signals (summative)

**Objectives 1–5 · 80 points · Week 10, session 5.** All data below
are fictional practice data. Show your work for every computation
and every prediction: state the curve, the direction, and the
mechanism, not just the final answer. Solutions are in
[../answer-key.md](../answer-key.md).

## Task 1 — Read and use a new market schedule (Objectives 1, 2; 20 pts)

A market for bottled water at a sports complex, per day (fictional
data):

| Price per bottle | Quantity demanded | Quantity supplied |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $10 | 20 | 80 |
| $8 | 40 | 60 |
| $6 | 60 | 40 |
| $4 | 80 | 20 |

Note: the equilibrium lies between two table rows — find it by
extending the pattern, not by picking a row where the two
quantities happen to match.

(a) Find the equilibrium price and quantity, showing how you know
they are the equilibrium. (6 pts)
(b) At $10, name and size the gap; at $4, name and size the gap.
(6 pts)
(c) Explain, in three or four sentences, how the market moves from
the $10 situation toward equilibrium: what sellers do, and what
happens to both quantities. (4 pts)
(d) Sketch both curves on one set of axes, label the axes, the
curves, and the equilibrium point. (4 pts)

## Task 2 — Four shift predictions (Objective 3; 16 pts)

For each event, name the curve, its shift direction, and the
predicted directions of the new equilibrium price and quantity.
(4 pts each.)

(a) Incomes rise in a city, and restaurant meals are a normal good.
(b) The wage paid to restaurant cooks rises sharply.
(c) The price of home cooking ingredients (a substitute for eating
out) falls.
(d) More restaurants open in the city.

## Task 3 — Two elasticity computations (Objective 4; 12 pts)

Show both percentage changes for each computation, classify, and
state the revenue direction with figures. (6 pts each.)

(a) A jacket's price rises from $12 to $15; jackets sold fall from
200 to 170 per week (fictional data).
(b) A streaming subscription's price falls from $40 to $36 per
month; subscribers rise from 500 to 650 (fictional data).

## Task 4 — Price floors and ceilings (Objective 5; 16 pts)

(a) An apartment market (fictional data): equilibrium rent
$1,100/month, 700 apartments. A rent ceiling of $850/month is set;
at $850, quantity demanded is 950 apartments and quantity supplied
is 500. Is the ceiling binding? Compute the gap and name it.
(6 pts)
(b) A labor market (fictional data): equilibrium wage $13/hour,
800 workers. A minimum wage of $16/hour is set; at $16, labor
demanded is 650 and labor supplied is 1,000. Is the floor binding?
Compute the gap and name it. (6 pts)
(c) The same apartment market considers a ceiling of $1,300/month
instead. Binding or non-binding? What is the outcome, and what
would have to change for this ceiling to start binding? (4 pts)

## Task 5 — Two shifts and a judgment (Objectives 3, 5; 16 pts)

(a) In a market, demand increases and supply decreases at the same
time. Explain what happens to equilibrium price for certain, what
happens to equilibrium quantity, and why the two answers differ.
Use the language of shifts and, if you sketch, describe the sketch
in words. (8 pts)
(b) The Riverside town council proposes a $2/lb ceiling on apples
(equilibrium $3, 60 lb/week; at $2, quantity demanded 80 lb and
quantity supplied 40 lb). Recommend for or against the ceiling in
a short paragraph that names at least one gaining group and one
losing group, and then state the strongest argument against your
own recommendation. (8 pts)
