# Unit 02 Practice — Supply, Demand, Markets, and Price Signals

Work with your adult on the guided set; do the extra set
independently. All numbers are the unit's fictional practice data
unless a question says otherwise. Answers are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md); try each set before looking.

## Guided practice (with your adult)

### A. Demand and supply curves (Lessons 1–2)

1. A demand schedule: $9 → 10 units, $7 → 30, $5 → 50, $3 → 70.
   Plot the curve. Is it consistent with the law of demand?
2. A supply schedule for the same good: $9 → 70, $7 → 50, $5 → 30,
   $3 → 10. Plot it on the same axes. Find equilibrium price and
   quantity, and name the gap at $9 and at $3.
3. Explain to your adult, without notes, why the demand curve slopes
   down and the supply curve slopes up, giving one reason for each.

### B. Shifts vs. movements (Lesson 3)

4. Classify each event as a demand shift, a supply shift, or a
   movement along a curve — and give the direction:
   (a) the good's own price falls; (b) a substitute's price rises;
   (c) a new technology cuts production cost; (d) buyers' incomes
   fall and the good is inferior; (e) sellers expect prices to rise
   and hold stock back; (f) a complement's price falls.
5. Demand for a good increases while supply is unchanged. Sketch
   the change and state the new price and quantity directions.
   Then do the same for a supply decrease.
6. **Explain your reasoning.** A town's coffee price rises and the
   quantity sold falls. Give one demand-side and one supply-side
   story that could each explain it, and say what evidence would
   separate them.

### C. Elasticity (Lesson 4)

7. Price $6 → $9; quantity 200 → 140. Compute the midpoint
   elasticity, classify, and predict the revenue direction. Verify
   with revenue numbers.
8. A good's demand is inelastic. The seller cuts its price 10%.
   Will revenue rise or fall? Explain without computing a schedule.
9. Two travelers face a 20% airfare rise: a business traveler who
   must fly tomorrow, and a vacation traveler choosing among
   destinations. Whose demand is more elastic, and which two
   determinants explain the difference?

### D. Price controls (Lesson 5)

10. Equilibrium rent $900, 400 apartments. A ceiling of $700 is
    set; at $700, Qd = 550 and Qs = 300. Compute the shortage and
    name one gaining group and one losing group.
11. Equilibrium wage $14, 1,200 workers. A floor of $12 is set.
    What happens? Explain with the binding test.
12. A price floor is set above equilibrium in a farm market, and
    the government buys the surplus. Who ultimately pays, and why
    does that matter for the benefit–cost evaluation?

## Extra practice (independent)

1. Qd = 90 − 10P, Qs = 10P − 10. Find equilibrium price and
   quantity. Find the gap at P = 7 and say which way price moves.
2. A demand increase and a supply decrease happen together. What
   happens to equilibrium price for certain? What is ambiguous
   about quantity? Sketch and explain.
3. Price $15 → $18; quantity 300 → 270. Compute midpoint
   elasticity, classify, and state the revenue direction. Verify.
4. A price ceiling is set exactly at equilibrium. Does it bind?
   What is the outcome? Explain in two sentences.
5. A seller faces elastic demand and raises price, expecting more
   revenue. Explain the error and what happens to revenue instead.
6. **Error analysis.** A classmate writes: "When the price of
   bread rose, demand for bread fell, so the demand curve shifted
   left." Identify every error in that sentence and rewrite it
   correctly.

## Retrieval (from this unit — no new teaching)

1. State the law of demand and the law of supply, each with its
   "all else equal" condition.
2. From memory, reproduce the four single-shift predictions
   (demand ↑/↓, supply ↑/↓) for price and quantity.
3. A surplus exists. Which way does price move, and what two
   quantity movements shrink the surplus?
4. Elasticity is 0.40. Price rises 10%. About how much does
   quantity demanded change, and does revenue rise or fall?
5. A ceiling is set above equilibrium. Binding or not? What is the
   shortage?
