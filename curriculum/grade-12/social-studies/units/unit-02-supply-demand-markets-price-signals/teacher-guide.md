# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Supply, Demand, Markets, and Price Signals

This guide is for the guiding adult. It is separate from the
learner-facing lessons: do not hand it to the learner before the
assessments. All numbers below were solved independently of the
lesson text and reconciled with [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## How to use this unit

- Sessions are ~50 minutes, five per week, Weeks 7–10. The unit
  README's 20-session table is the pacing contract; lessons carry
  the new teaching, and the investigation, writing, review, and
  assessment sessions are not optional filler — they are where
  the learner practices reading numbers off curves.
- Every fictional number in the unit traces to one dataset (the
  Riverside apple market) plus small, labeled fictional cases. If
  the learner asks "is that real?", the answer for every lesson
  number is no — it is practice data. Real cases enter only
  through adult-selected, dated sources in the Week 10 evidence lab
  and the project.
- Nonpartisan stance: price-control deliberations must present
  gains and losses on both sides. If the learner's argument names
  only winners or only losers, return it.

## Lesson-by-lesson notes

### L1 — Demand (W7S1)

- Watch for: demand vs. quantity demanded; price on the vertical
  axis; "willing and able" (ability to pay is part of demand).
- The market-scene image activity works without the image: the
  text alternative carries the same question and data. Accept oral
  or pointing answers.
- Reteach trigger: if the exit check confuses demand with quantity
  demanded, re-work Worked example A with the price column covered,
  then uncovered, until the learner says "quantity demanded"
  for a price-driven change unprompted.

### L2 — Supply and equilibrium (W7S2)

- Watch for: surplus/shortage named backwards; price moving the
  wrong way after a gap. Drill "surplus → price falls; shortage →
  price rises" with fresh numbers before W7S3.
- The equation check (Worked example B) is the learner's
  self-check tool: table, graph, and equation must agree. If they
  disagree, there is an arithmetic error — find it, do not average
  the answers.

### L3 — Shifts vs. movements (W8S1–W8S2)

- Watch for: any price change called a shift; taxes treated as
  demand shocks; inferior goods handled backwards (incomes fall →
  demand for an inferior good rises).
- Reteach: the card-sort support (eight shocks sorted into
  demand / supply / movement piles) with the sentence frame
  "_____ changed, so buyers/sellers want _____ at every price, so
  the _____ curve shifts _____."
- Two-shift rule to insist on: one outcome certain, one ambiguous.
  A learner who guesses both directions on a two-shift question
  has not understood the model, even if one guess lands right.

### L4 — Elasticity (W9S1–W9S2)

- Watch for: starting-value percentages instead of midpoints;
  negative elasticities reported without absolute value; revenue
  predicted from intuition instead of the elasticity rule.
- Require the four-step template (quantity change, quantity
  average, price change, price average) on every computation until
  the learner's answers match the key without it.
- The revenue test is the point of the lesson, not a bonus: always
  ask for the revenue prediction *before* the multiplication.

### L5 — Price controls (W10S1–W10S2)

- Watch for: skipping the binding test; gaps computed from
  equilibrium quantity instead of quantities at the control price;
  "helps all renters/workers" claims.
- Binding chant: "ceiling below, floor above, or it does nothing."
- Evidence lab (W10S3): the adult selects two sourced assessments
  of one policy that disagree, previews them for reading level and
  framing, and checks any real figure against its dated source.
  The lessons deliberately assert no real-world effect size.

## Practice set

See the answer key for full solutions. The guided set is for joint
work; do not let the learner copy key wording — the key's value is
in its worked steps, which the learner should reproduce, not quote.
The error-analysis items (Lesson 1 Q5, Lesson 3 Q5, Lesson 5 Q5,
practice extra Q6) are the best diagnostic items in the unit: a
learner who can correct someone else's error understands the idea.

## Project — Design a Market, Then Test a Price Control

- Approve the shock and the real case before drafting. A
  non-binding control evaluated alone is not a complete Part 3;
  return it with the rubric's requirement.
- Project Part 3, question 4 (elasticity note): the correct
  direction is that *inelastic* demand makes the ceiling's
  shortage *smaller* than elastic demand would, because quantity
  demanded does not rise as much when price is held low. If the
  learner argues the opposite, re-teach with the Riverside demand
  curve steepened through ($3, 60) and the $2 quantity compared.
- Score the graph page on labels and correctness (equilibrium,
  surplus, shortage, shift direction), not draftsmanship; accept a
  table-only version if graphing is the barrier.

## Assessments

- **Week 10 quiz (30 pts):** formative. Any score below 21
  indicates a Lesson 3 or Lesson 4 gap: re-teach shifts or
  midpoints before the unit assessment, using the practice set's
  retrieval items.
- **Unit assessment (80 pts):** summative. Suggested bands (adult
  judgment, not a standardized scale): 68–80 ready for Unit 03;
  52–67 review the weakest task's lesson and re-test with two
  fresh numbers; below 52 re-teach the failed objective(s) fully
  before Unit 03.
- Open-response scoring: accept any answer that states the
  correct curve, direction, mechanism, and (where asked) numbers.
  For Task 5(b), the recommendation's direction does not matter;
  the groups, the tradeoff, and the counterargument do.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** table-only answers for every graph question;
  four-step elasticity template; card sorts for shifts; oral
  answers for the image activity and deliberation.
- **Extension:** the Lesson 3 two-shift computation, the Lesson 4
  unit-elastic proof (midpoint elasticity of the $2 → $4 Riverside
  move is exactly 1.00; revenue peaks at $3), and the Lesson 5
  real-jurisdiction research paragraph. Extensions never gate the
  next lesson.
- **Retrieval:** each week's review session re-asks one prior
  idea (see the README pacing table); the unit assessment mixes
  all five objectives deliberately.
