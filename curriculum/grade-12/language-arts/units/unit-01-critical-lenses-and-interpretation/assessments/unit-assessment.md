## New passage

Read carefully — twice. It is from Anton Chekhov's "The Bet" (1889),
translated by Constance Garnett (public domain in the USA; passage
verified 2026-10-15). A banker and a young lawyer make a wager at a
party: the banker bets two millions that the lawyer will not stay in
solitary confinement for five years; the lawyer raises the term to
fifteen years in a lodge in the banker's garden.

> And this wild, senseless bet was carried out! The banker, spoilt and
> frivolous, with millions beyond his reckoning, was delighted at the
> bet. At supper he made fun of the young man, and said: "Think better
> of it, young man, while there is still time. To me two millions are a
> trifle, but you are losing three or four of the best years of your
> life. I say three or four, because you won't stay longer. Don't
> forget either, you unhappy man, that voluntary confinement is a great
> deal harder to bear than compulsory."

## Tasks

**1. Close reading (10 points).** Run the four-move routine on the
passage: list two genuine interpretive questions it raises (Move 2),
then choose one and write a claim (≤40 words) defended with a quoted
phrase (Move 4).

**2. Lens application — Marxist (10 points).** Apply the Marxist lens's
core questions to the passage in 6–8 sentences: what economic positions
do the two men occupy, what does money let each of them do, and what
does the passage treat as natural about wealth? Use at least two
quotations.

**3. Lens application — your choice (10 points).** Choose *one* of the
feminist, postcolonial, new-historicist, or reader-response lenses.
In 6–8 sentences with at least two quotations, produce a reading of
the passage through that lens — and then write 2–3 sentences on the
lens's *fit*: what does it reveal well here, and where does it reach
its limit?

**4. Competing readings (10 points).** Stage a competition between
your Task 2 reading and your Task 3 reading on *one* interpretive
question about the passage (e.g., "What is the bet really about?").
State each reading's answer with its best quotation, then render a
verdict: state your criterion, name the winner, and say what the
losing reading still sees. (8–12 sentences total.)

**5. Stated vs. meant (5 points).** The banker says, "To me two
millions are a trifle." In 4–5 sentences, distinguish what is stated
from what is meant: what does the line reveal about the banker *beyond*
its surface claim? Quote one supporting phrase.

**6. Reflection (5 points).** In 4–5 sentences: which lens is now your
default — the one you reach for first — and what does that reveal
about you as a reader? Name one specific moment in this unit when a
lens changed what you saw.

## Scoring

- 45–50: excellent — lens skills transfer to new texts.
- 36–44: proficient — ready to carry the skills into Unit 02.
- 28–35: review the missed moves with the adult (see teacher guide),
  then redo the missed tasks on a new passage.
- Below 28: rebuild with Practice Sets A–F before Unit 02.

Combined with the essay rubric score, this determines unit readiness
(see unit README session 20). Neither this assessment nor the quiz is
a standardized or validated instrument — they are course checks built
for this unit.
