# Unit 01 — Advanced Close Reading: Critical Lenses and Interpretation

**Grade 12 · Language Arts · Advanced literature, writing, and communication pathway · Weeks 2–5 of the course year**
(draft, delivered 2026-10-15 for issue #56; not educator-reviewed)

## Overview

Good readers do not just ask *what* a text says. They ask *what question
they are asking of it* — because the question changes what they can see.
A **critical lens** is a disciplined set of questions (about gender,
power, class, empire, history, or the reader's own response) that a
reader deliberately brings to a text. This unit makes lens-based
interpretation the year's signature reading move: the learner masters
the close-reading routine at the high end of the complexity band, learns
five lenses, applies them to one anchor story, and writes a multi-lens
interpretation essay that stages competing readings against each other
with quoted evidence.

The unit runs in five moves:

1. **The close-reading routine** — annotation as a four-move discipline
   (notice, question, connect, argue from evidence), practiced on the
   opening of Chekhov's "The Lady with the Dog."
2. **The feminist lens** — what the lens asks about gender, power, and
   voice; applied to Anna and Gurov; the generated illustration as a
   lens-concept activity.
3. **The Marxist lens** — what the lens asks about class, labor, and
   economic power; where it agrees and collides with the feminist
   reading; a reread of the valley-of-ashes passage from *The Great
   Gatsby*.
4. **The postcolonial and new-historicist lenses** — who speaks and who
   is silenced in Kipling's "The White Man's Burden"; what 1899 Russia
   lets a reader see in Chekhov; judging which lens fits which text.
5. **Reader-response and competing readings** — honoring the reader's
   response as evidence; a reread of Daisy's "beautiful little fool";
   comparing two lens readings of one passage; seminar.

Anchor texts are public domain in the USA. Chekhov's "The Lady with the
Dog" is read in full during the unit (about 7,000 words in the Garnett
translation — a demanding but manageable close-reading load across four
weeks); the Kipling poem and the *Gatsby* passages are short enough to
re-read closely several times, which is the point.

The essential question running through all four weeks is: **how does the
question you bring to a text change what the text can tell you — and how
do you prove a lens reading from the words on the page?**

## Prerequisites

From the track's entry skills (verified in the week-1 diagnostic;
insecure skills are re-taught in use):

- Cite strong, thorough textual evidence for explicit and inferential
  claims about literature (RL.11-12.1).
- Determine two or more themes, analyze their development, and write an
  objective summary (RL.11-12.2).
- Analyze word choice — figurative, connotative, technical — and its
  effect on meaning and tone (RL.11-12.4, L.11-12.5.a).
- Write organized analytical paragraphs with claims and evidence
  (W.11-12.1, W.11-12.9.a).

The unit does **not** assume fluency with: reading at the high end of
the 11–CCR complexity band independently (RL.11-12.10); any critical
lens vocabulary or method; distinguishing what a text states from what
it means in irony and understatement (RL.11-12.6); or staging competing
interpretations of one passage — those are this unit's new content.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Execute the close-reading routine (notice → question → connect →
   argue from evidence) on a high-complexity-band literary text,
   producing annotations that name at least two genuine interpretive
   questions the text raises and one claim defended with quoted
   evidence. (RL.11-12.1, RL.11-12.10; track objective 1)
2. Define the feminist, Marxist, postcolonial, new-historicist, and
   reader-response lenses in the learner's own words, and state the
   core question each lens asks of a text. (RL.11-12.6; track
   objective 3)
3. Apply the feminist and Marxist lenses to Chekhov's "The Lady with
   the Dog," producing two evidence-based readings and identifying at
   least one point where the lenses agree and one where they collide,
   each grounded in quoted passages. (RL.11-12.1, RL.11-12.3; track
   objective 3)
4. Apply the postcolonial lens to Kipling's "The White Man's Burden"
   and the new-historicist lens to Chekhov's 1899 context, judging —
   with quoted evidence and stated criteria — which lens fits each text
   best and where a lens reaches its limit. (RL.11-12.6; track
   objective 3)
5. Write a multi-lens interpretation essay (drafted, peer-checked, and
   revised) that advances a claim about one anchor text, develops it
   through at least two lenses, distinguishes what the text states from
   what it means, and acknowledges and answers a competing reading.
   (W.11-12.1, W.11-12.9.a, RL.11-12.6; track objectives 3, 8)

## Vocabulary

**Unit terms** (used in lessons and assessed): *critical lens, close
reading, annotation, textual evidence, inference, ambiguity, feminist
criticism, Marxist criticism, postcolonial criticism, new historicism,
reader-response, the canon, ideology, hegemony (recognition),
patriarchy (recognition), agency, subaltern (recognition), stated vs.
meant, irony, understatement, competing readings, counter-reading,
thesis, counterclaim.*

**Lens-question bank** (memorize the questions, not just the names):
each lesson opens with its lens's 3–4 core questions; the week-04 quiz
tests the learner's ability to match questions to lenses and to apply
them to a new passage.

## Standards notes

Codes and short descriptions verified 2026-10-08 against the official
Common Core State Standards for grades 11–12 at
<https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/> (the track audit's
source-backed crosswalk, scope-and-sequence §5). Descriptions here are
paraphrases; this crosswalk is a planning reference, not a claim of
state adoption, accreditation, or complete alignment.

- **RL.11-12.1** — cite strong, thorough evidence for analysis and
  inferences, including where the text leaves matters uncertain.
- **RL.11-12.3** — analyze the impact of the author's choices in
  developing story elements (used for the Chekhov character readings).
- **RL.11-12.4** — determine word/phrase meaning including figurative
  and connotative senses; analyze word-choice impact on meaning and
  tone.
- **RL.11-12.6** — analyze point of view requiring distinguishing what
  is stated from what is meant (satire, sarcasm, irony, understatement)
  — the lens unit's central reading standard.
- **RL.11-12.10** — read literature in the 11–CCR complexity band
  proficiently, independently at the high end by year's end.
- **L.11-12.5.a** — interpret figures of speech in context; analyze
  their role in the text.
- **W.11-12.1.a–e** — write arguments with precise claims, fair
  counterclaims, cohesion, formal style, and a supported conclusion
  (the multi-lens essay).
- **W.11-12.9.a** — draw evidence from literary texts to support
  analysis and reflection.
- **W.11-12.10** — write routinely over extended and shorter time
  frames (drafting studios, journals).
- **SL.11-12.1.a–d** — prepared, civil, evidence-based discussion:
  build on others' ideas, probe reasoning, synthesize, resolve
  contradictions (the lens seminar).

## 20-session pacing plan (weeks 2–5; ~50 minutes each)

**Week 2 — the routine and the first lens**

| Session | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lesson 01: the close-reading routine; modeled annotation of the Chekhov opening | Learner can name the four moves and performs one full cycle on a new paragraph |
| 2 | Annotation workshop: Chekhov part I, lens-neutral; readiness check on vocabulary | Annotations show at least 2 interpretive questions and 1 evidence-backed claim |
| 3 | Reading workshop: "The Lady with the Dog" parts I–II (read closely, annotate) | Reading log records plot, 2 character observations, 1 uncertainty |
| 4 | Lesson 02: the feminist lens; generated-illustration activity | Learner states the lens's core questions and applies one to Anna |
| 5 | Workshop: story parts III–IV with feminist annotations; exit check | Annotations include ≥3 feminist-lens observations with line references |

**Week 3 — lenses in conversation**

| Session | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | Lesson 03: the Marxist lens; Gurov reread | Learner produces one Marxist claim about Gurov with quoted evidence |
| 7 | Workshop: valley-of-ashes passage through both lenses; agree/collide chart | Chart has ≥1 agreement and ≥1 collision, each with a quotation |
| 8 | Independent practice: two-lens comparison paragraph (formative draft) | Paragraph states both readings and judges which is stronger on the evidence |
| 9 | Lesson 04: postcolonial lens on Kipling; new-historicist lens on 1899 Russia | Learner names who speaks / who is silent in the poem with line citations |
| 10 | Workshop: Kipling close read; new-historicist context notes for Chekhov | Context notes list 3 verifiable 1899 facts and 1 interpretive use each |

**Week 4 — response, seminar, and the essay**

| Session | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | Lesson 05: reader-response; Daisy's speech reread; competing readings | Learner writes a reader-response journal entry and one counter-reading |
| 12 | Seminar prep: evidence gathering, question drafting (SL.11-12.1) | Prep sheet has a claim, 3 quotations, 2 questions for the adult |
| 13 | Seminar: "Whose reading wins?" — adult-led discussion | Learner builds on the adult's ideas twice and probes once |
| 14 | Project launch: multi-lens essay planning; thesis workshop | Thesis states a claim developed through ≥2 named lenses |
| 15 | Drafting studio 1: introduction + first lens section | Draft has a thesis and ≥4 integrated quotations |

**Week 5 — drafting, assessment, review**

| Session | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | Drafting studio 2: second lens + competing reading; peer evidence-check | Every claim has a quotation; competing reading is answered, not ignored |
| 17 | Revision studio: adult conference; revision checklist | Revision plan names 3 specific changes; draft revised accordingly |
| 18 | Formative quiz (`assessments/week-04_quiz.md`); review of lens vocabulary | Quiz completed; errors corrected with lens questions reapplied |

## Differentiation

- **Reading access:** the Garnett translation is 19th-century-inflected;
  the adult may read the opening aloud while the learner follows, or
  pair reading with the free LibriVox recording (see resource pack).
  Chunk the story into the session-sized sections named above.
- **Language access:** lens vocabulary is taught with the question
  bank, not definitions alone; the learner may answer exit checks
  orally with the adult scribing.
- **Alternate response modes:** annotations may be marginal notes,
  voice memos, or a two-column notebook (quotation | lens question);
  the seminar may be a written dialogue.
- **Extensions:** apply a sixth lens (psychoanalytic or formalist) to
  one passage; compare two translations of the Chekhov opening;
  research the 1899 publication context and present findings.
- **Support:** each lesson names likely misconceptions and the
  teacher guide gives reteaching moves; insecure prerequisite skills
  are re-taught in use, not as a separate unit.
