# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Critical Lenses and Interpretation

**For the guiding adult only.** Do not place this file beside student
materials; the answer key (`answer-key.md`) is a separate file. This
guide covers pacing, misconceptions, discussion moves, and reteaching.
All quotations below are from the U.S. public-domain editions named in
the unit README.

## Pacing and workload

- The 20-session plan assumes ~50-minute sessions, five per week. If
  sessions run 30 minutes, split each lesson across two sessions and
  move the workshops as marked; do not cut the drafting studios
  (sessions 15–17) — the essay is the unit's primary summative
  evidence.
- Reading load: "The Lady with the Dog" (~7,000 words, Garnett) is
  chunked across sessions 2–5, 9–10. If the learner stalls, the
  LibriVox listen-while-reading route (resource pack) keeps the close
  reading on track without reducing the text.
- The seminar (session 13) needs the prep sheet from session 12;
  never run the seminar without it — unprepared discussion teaches
  that opinions need no evidence, the opposite of this unit.

## Misconceptions by lesson (and the redirect)

**Lesson 01.** *Annotating plot instead of language.* Redirect: "What
did Chekhov choose here that a different writer would not have?" *Claims
as summaries.* Require the quotation; a claim provable without quoting
is too big.

**Lesson 02.** *"The lens proves Chekhov is sexist."* Hold the line:
the lens describes what the *text does* (filters women through male
judgment); the author's attitude is a separate claim needing separate
evidence. Park it for the essay — it is a legitimate thesis *if*
argued, not a conclusion.

**Lesson 03.** *Lens as politics.* "The lens is a method, not a
verdict — show me the question it asks and the line that answers."
*False balance in agree/collide.* Demand the criterion: "Stronger *by
what measure*?"

**Lesson 04.** *Postcolonial = "calling Kipling racist, done."* Push
to mechanism: "Show me *how* the poem silences — which lines, which
grammar?" *New historicism = trivia.* Require the payoff: "…and
therefore this passage means ___."

**Lesson 05.** *"Any reading goes."* Enforce reader-response question
4: "Show me the line that earns the feeling." *Straw-manning the
rival reading.* Require the steelman: the rival's best quotation
first.

## Seminar moves (session 13)

- Open with the learner's prep-sheet claim; ask for the *strongest*
  counter-evidence against it before anyone agrees.
- When the learner agrees too fast, play the rival: "A Marxist reader
  would say your feminist reading misses the money — answer them."
- When the learner stalls, return to a quotation: "Read the line
  aloud. What word is doing the work?"
- Close by asking the learner to state one way their reading changed
  during the discussion (SL.11-12.1.d: synthesize and resolve).

## Conferencing the essay (session 17)

Use the 15-minute agenda in `project.md`. Additionally:

- Check the thesis against the draft's actual argument (the most
  common mismatch: thesis promises two lenses, draft delivers one
  plus plot summary).
- Check the competing-reading paragraph with the steelman test: "Is
  this the *strongest* form of the rival case? Would its defender
  recognize it?"
- If the essay is under 900 words, the usual cause is unglossed
  quotations or a missing competing reading — not a need for "more
  ideas."

## Reteaching moves

- **Lens questions won't stick:** return to Practice Set A as a daily
  5-minute drill; have the learner *generate* one new question per
  lens per day until the bank is fluent.
- **Annotations stay plot-level:** model one more four-move cycle
  aloud on a new paragraph (think-aloud), then have the learner
  imitate the *moves*, not the content.
- **Agree/collide collapses into "both valid":** re-run the verdict
  with a forced criterion — "Which reading explains *more words* of
  the passage?" Counting explained words makes the judgment concrete.
- **Essay competing reading is weak:** have the learner write the
  rival paragraph *first*, before the thesis paragraph, in the next
  draft cycle.

## Readiness signals

- Ready for Unit 02: the learner names all five lenses and one core
  question each from memory; produces a quoted claim on a new passage
  without prompting; stages a fair competing reading.
- Reteach before Unit 02 if: lens vocabulary is shaky (quiz < 20);
  annotations are still plot notes; the essay has no genuine
  competing reading.

## What this unit does not do

- It does not certify alignment, adoption, or accreditation — the
  standards notes are a planning crosswalk.
- It does not reproduce copyrighted texts — all passages are U.S.
  public domain; the adult selects any licensed editions privately.
- It does not ask the learner to publish anything — the essay stays
  in private storage; only blank instructional files belong in the
  public repo.
