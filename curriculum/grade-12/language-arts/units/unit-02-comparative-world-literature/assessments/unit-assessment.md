## Part 1 — Short answers (20 points, 5 each)

Answer each in 4–6 sentences with at least one quotation:

1. **Translation.** Compare Butler's "that ingenious hero" with
   Butcher–Lang's "that man, so ready at need." Explain two effects
   of the difference (not just that the words differ): one effect on
   the hero's character, one on what the reader expects next.
2. **Context method.** Choose one anchor text. State one context
   fact, show how it changes a specific reading of a specific
   passage (quote the passage), and state what the reading would be
   without the fact.
3. **Antigone.** State Creon's strongest claim and Antigone's
   strongest claim, each with a quotation, and explain in one
   sentence why the ending refuses to declare a simple winner.
4. **Satire.** Using either Dante or Cervantes, explain what is
   stated, what is meant, and what evidence the passage gives the
   reader to judge with (quote the passage). Then state, in one
   sentence, why the passage is satire rather than insult.

## Part 2 — Comparative essay (30 points)

Write (or submit, if drafted in sessions 17–18) a comparative essay
of 800–1,200 words on one theme across two anchor texts, following
Lesson 05's six-part structure:

- Introduction naming the theme as a question, ending in a thesis
  that names both texts.
- One body section per text, each with at least 2 integrated
  quotations and at least 1 context fact used as evidence (mark it
  in the margin: "context as evidence").
- A comparison paragraph stating what the juxtaposition shows that
  neither text alone shows.
- A counter-reading paragraph stating the strongest objection fairly
  and answering it.
- A conclusion restating the thesis as a finding.

Scoring (see `answer-key.md` for descriptors): thesis 6, evidence
and quotation 8, comparison 6, counter-reading 5, context as evidence
5.

## Part 3 — Exhibit check (10 points)

Present the comparative exhibit (project.md) or, if the exhibit is
submitted separately, answer here:

1. (5 points) State your exhibit's translation judgment in 3–4
   sentences: which translation serves which purpose, defended with
   one quoted difference and its effect.
2. (5 points) State your staging decision in 3–4 sentences: what it
   claims, what textual evidence supports it, and what it risks.

## Scoring

- 54–60: strong; file the essay and exhibit for the U08 capstone
  portfolio.
- 42–53: solid; revise the weakest part (essay comparison paragraph
  or context-as-evidence marking) with the adult before filing.
- Below 42: reteach the weakest objective with the teacher guide's
  moves, revise, and resubmit. The assessment is not a verdict on
  the learner; it is a map of what to re-teach.

This assessment is a classroom measure written for this unit. It is
not a standardized or validated test, and its scores are not
comparable across cohorts.
