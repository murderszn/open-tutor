## Part 2 — Passage application (12 points, 4 each)

Read each passage and answer with the method named:

6. **(Translation, Butler vs. Butcher–Lang)** Butler: "that ingenious
   hero." Butcher–Lang: "that man, so ready at need." In 3–4
   sentences, what does each phrase make Odysseus, and which hero
   would a reader expect to solve problems by tricks? Quote both
   phrases.
7. **(Context method)** "They were not born today nor yesterday;
   they die not; and none knoweth whence they sprang." (Antigone,
   Storr.) In 3–4 sentences, what context fact about Greek tragedy
   or Athenian law makes this claim intelligible, and what does the
   line claim about Creon's decree? Quote the line.
8. **(Satire)** "What we see there are not giants but windmills, and
   what seem to be their arms are the sails that turned by the wind
   make the millstone go." (Sancho, Ormsby.) In 3–4 sentences,
   explain why Sancho's sentence is the satiric hinge of the scene:
   what does it prove, and why does proving it change nothing for
   Quixote? Quote the line.

## Part 3 — Comparison (8 points)

9. **(4 points)** In 4–5 sentences, compare how *Antigone* and
   *A Doll's House* each use the word "duty" (or its equivalent
   claim). Quote one line from each play.
10. **(4 points)** In 4–5 sentences, state one theme Dante and
    Cervantes treat differently, name the difference, and name the
    form (ordered journey vs. parody) that makes the difference
    possible. Quote one text.
