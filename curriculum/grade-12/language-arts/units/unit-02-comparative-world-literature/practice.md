# Practice — Unit 02: Comparative World Literature and Historical Context

Additional guided and independent practice. Solutions and acceptable
variation are in `answer-key.md` — do not open that file until the
adult has reviewed your work. Each set names the lesson it follows;
sets may be done in any order after their lesson.

## Set A — Translation comparison (after Lesson 01)

1. Butler: "that ingenious hero." Butcher and Lang: "that man, so
   ready at need." In 2–3 sentences, explain which hero you would
   rather follow into danger, and why — quoting both phrases.
2. Butler: "he suffered much by sea." Butcher and Lang: "many the
   woes he suffered in his heart on the deep." Does the difference
   change the *cause* of the suffering, the *place* of the suffering,
   or both? Defend your answer in 2–3 sentences with both quotations.
3. A third translator (from the learner's library, adult-selected)
   opens by asking the Muse to *sing* rather than to *tell*. Compare
   that verb with Butler's "Tell me, O Muse." What does asking for
   song ask the Muse to do that asking for telling does not?
4. Error analysis: a classmate writes, "Butler is better because he
   is older." Name two things wrong with that sentence as literary
   judgment (age is not accuracy; "better" needs a criterion — better
   *for what*?).

## Set B — Context notebook checks (after Lessons 01–04)

For each statement, say whether it is a context fact, a reading, or a
confusion of the two — and rewrite any confusion correctly:

1. "The *Odyssey* was performed orally before it was written down,
   so Odysseus is a symbol of the sea."
2. "Antigone was performed at a festival before citizens, so its
   argument about law would have been heard as a civic question, not
   only a family story."
3. "Dante was exiled in 1302, so the dark forest means exile."
4. "Cervantes published Part I in 1605, so Don Quixote is funny."
5. "A married woman in 1879 Norway could not borrow money without her
   husband's consent, so Nora's forgery was the only way to save
   Torvald's life within the play's world."

## Set D — Satire identification (after Lesson 03)

For each passage, state what is said, what is meant, and whether it
is satire by the unit's evidence test (does it give the reader reasons
to judge, or only a verdict to repeat?):

1. Dante, Canto I: "Midway upon the journey of our life / I found
   myself within a forest dark, / For the straightforward pathway had
   been lost."
2. Quixote, Chapter VIII: "Fortune is arranging matters for us better
   than we could have shaped our desires ourselves, for look there,
   friend Sancho Panza, where thirty or more monstrous giants present
   themselves."
3. Sancho, Chapter VIII: "What we see there are not giants but
   windmills, and what seem to be their arms are the sails that turned
   by the wind make the millstone go."
4. Quixote, after the fall: the sage Friston "turned these giants
   into mills in order to rob me of the glory of vanquishing them."
5. Original exercise: write a 3-sentence satiric paragraph about a
   modern reading habit (for example, believing a headline without
   reading the article). Then, in one sentence, state what evidence
   your paragraph gives the reader to judge with.

## Set E — A Doll's House language trace (after Lesson 04)

1. List three pet names or diminutives Torvald uses for Nora in
   Act I (from the summary/scenes read). What does each one imply
   about who Nora is *for* in Torvald's language?
2. In Act III, Nora says: "I have other duties just as sacred." In
   3–4 sentences, explain the work the word "other" does in that
   sentence. (Hint: it does not deny the first duties; it changes
   their order.)
3. Helmer: "Before all else, you are a wife and a mother." Rewrite
   this sentence as Nora would rewrite it, keeping every word except
   changing the order or adding one word. Then explain, in 2–3
   sentences, what your rewrite changes.
4. Error analysis: a classmate writes, "Nora leaves because she
   doesn't love her children." Quote one line from the duties
   exchange that contradicts this reading, and explain why the
   contradiction matters.

## Set F — Comparative thesis workshop (after Lesson 05)

For each thesis seed, apply the three tests (contestable? needs both
texts? provable?) and rewrite the ones that fail:

1. "Antigone and Nora are both brave women."
2. "Dante sorts sins into circles, but Cervantes refuses to sort at
   all — Quixote's world will not stay in its categories, and that
   refusal is itself a judgment on Dante's ordered cosmos."
3. "The *Odyssey* and *Antigone* are both Greek."
4. "Sophocles and Ibsen both write about duty, but Sophocles stages
   duty as a clash of two public laws while Ibsen stages it as a
   private discovery — so Antigone dies certain and Nora leaves
   uncertain."
5. Write your own thesis seed for the exhibit (project.md) and apply
   the three tests to it in writing.

## Set G — Revision and error analysis (after Lesson 05)

Read this paragraph (an invented student draft — not a quotation from
any text) and revise it:

> "Dante and Cervantes are both old writers who wrote about journeys.
> Dante goes to Hell and Quixote goes around Spain. Both journeys are
> long. This shows that journeys are important in literature."

1. Name three faults in the paragraph (summary instead of thesis;
   no quotations; no comparison — only juxtaposition; "important"
   says nothing contestable).
2. Rewrite the paragraph (5–7 sentences) as a comparison paragraph:
   one thesis sentence, one quotation from each text, one sentence
   stating what the comparison shows.
3. Add a counter-reading sentence: "One might object that ___" and
   answer it in one sentence.
