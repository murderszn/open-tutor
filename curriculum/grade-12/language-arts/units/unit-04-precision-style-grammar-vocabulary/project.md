# Project — Precision Style Portfolio

**Deliverable:** a portfolio that proves the unit's method on
the learner's own writing: two revised passages, a personal
style sheet, an original precision paragraph, and a reflection
— each part showing its reasoning, not only its result.
**Time:** sessions 11–18 (studios and peer check); final
portfolio due at session 20, alongside the unit assessment.
**Materials:** Unit 01 and Unit 02 drafts, case-bank notebook,
word bank, dictionary with usage notes, the illustration in
`assets/` (title page, optional).

## What the portfolio contains (4 parts)

1. **Two revised passages (2 pages).** One paragraph from the
   Unit 01 lens essay and one passage (one paragraph, or two
   short paragraphs) from the Unit 02 comparative essay, each
   presented as before/after with a revision note. Each note
   must name at least three revision moves, each with: the
   pass step (Lesson 05, steps 1–5), the original phrase, the
   repaired phrase, and the reason in one sentence. At least
   one move per passage must be a usage decision with a
   reference named, and at least one must be a syntax choice
   with a stated effect. No revision that loses a true claim
   from the original counts as a move — the peer check
   (session 14) tests for exactly that.
2. **Personal style sheet (1 page, 10 rules).** Each rule in
   the form: *Rule, Example, Reason (reference or effect)*.
   At least three rules must be usage rules with a reference
   named; at least three must be syntax or sentence-level
   rules with a stated effect; at least one must be a
   vocabulary rule (connotation or register). At least one
   rule must show a revision after testing (session 16):
   the original rule, the sentence that broke it, and the
   revised rule with its condition.
3. **Original precision paragraph (150–200 words).** A
   paragraph answering: "What does precision add to an
   argument?" It must use at least five word-bank words
   accurately (underline them and list them below the
   paragraph with their parts of speech), vary sentence
   length deliberately (mark S/M/L in the margin), and contain
   no nominalization, redundancy, or vague verb that the
   writer cannot defend in a note. The paragraph is assessed
   on precision, not on agreeing with any particular answer.
4. **Reflection (1 page).** Answer three questions, each in
   its own paragraph: (a) Which single revision in this
   portfolio changed the meaning most, and what exactly
   changed? (b) Which usage rule from your style sheet are you
   least sure about, and what would settle it? (c) What will
   you do differently in Unit 05's first draft because of
   this unit? Vague reflections ("I learned a lot") score at
   the lowest band — name sentences, rules, and decisions.

The generated illustration
(`assets/precision-style-revision-desk.png`) may appear on the
portfolio's title page with its caption and a one-sentence
note: what the image claims about revision, and one
qualification (its strips are blank — the method, not any
sentence, is what it shows). It is a concept illustration, not
evidence about any text, and must not be labeled as a
photograph of the learner's work.

## Steps

1. **Choose (session 11).** The two passages and the paragraph
   topic. Get the adult's sign-off that the passages are
   drafts with room to revise (a finished, polished paragraph
   teaches nothing here).
2. **Pass (sessions 12–13).** Run Lesson 05's five-step pass on
   both passages; draft the ten-rule style sheet; draft the
   precision paragraph.
3. **Check (session 14).** Peer style-check: exchange
   portfolios-in-progress. The checker does only two things:
   verifies every usage claim against a reference, and flags
   any revision that lost a true claim, naming the lost claim.
   The writer accepts or rebuts each flag in writing.
4. **Test (session 16).** Test every style-sheet rule against
   a new sentence; revise at least one rule with a condition
   (Worked example 2, Lesson 05).
5. **Reflect and assemble (sessions 17–18).** Write the
   reflection; order the four parts; add the illustration
   note if used; file the portfolio for the U08 capstone.
6. **Submit (session 20).** Portfolio due with the unit
   assessment. Keep a private copy; the portfolio itself
   stays anonymous (no learner name, photo, or school on any
   page that could enter a shared repository).

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | 10–9 (strong) | 8–7 (solid) | 6–5 (developing) | 4–0 (needs rework) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revised passages (10) | 3+ moves per passage, each with step, before/after, reason; ≥1 usage decision with reference and ≥1 syntax choice with effect per passage; no lost true claims | Moves named with reasons; references/effects mostly present; at most one unrepaired lost claim | Moves listed without reasons, or references/effects missing; more than one lost claim | Revisions are copy-edits only (spelling/punctuation), or claims lost throughout |
| Style sheet (10) | 10 rules in Rule/Example/Reason form; ≥3 usage with references, ≥3 syntax/sentence-level with effects, ≥1 vocabulary; ≥1 rule revised after testing with the breaking sentence shown | 10 rules, form mostly complete; counts nearly met; one tested revision present | Fewer than 10 rules, or rules are slogans without examples/reasons; no tested revision | Rules copied from lessons without examples, or fewer than 6 rules |
| Precision paragraph (10) | 150–200 words; ≥5 word-bank words used accurately and listed; S/M/L marked with deliberate variation; no undefended faults | Word count and word use met; variation present; at most one undefended fault | Word count or word use missed; variation accidental; several undefended faults | Paragraph missing, far off length, or words used inaccurately |
| Reflection (10) | All three questions answered with named sentences, rules, decisions; the "changed meaning most" case quotes before and after | All three answered with specifics; one answer stays general | One question unanswered or all answers general ("I learned a lot") | Reflection missing or entirely vague |

**Passing:** 28/40 with no criterion below 5. Below that, revise
the weakest criterion with the adult and resubmit; the rubric,
not the adult's impression, decides.

## Adult notes

- Check at least one usage reference per passage yourself:
  the most common portfolio failure is a confident reference
  claim that the reference does not actually support.
- The peer check's two jobs are the whole check — do not let
  it drift into general praise or general criticism.
- Keep the portfolio anonymous: no learner name, photo, or
  school on any panel that could enter a shared repository;
  names stay in private storage.
