# Culminating Unit Assessment — Unit 04: Precision Style (Session 20)

**For the learner.** Complete under adult supervision in about
50 minutes. The answer key is a separate file
(`../answer-key.md`). The portfolio project is due at the same
session but is scored separately (see `../project.md`).

## Part A — Usage decisions (5 points each; 25 points)

For each case, write a full case card: the remembered rule,
the reference's verdict (paraphrased, reference named), the
audience (formal essay, unless stated otherwise), and the
choice with its cost. Mark each case **contested**,
**settled error**, or **settled correct**.

1. "The survey collected less responses than planned."
2. "Anyone who submits late will have their paper marked
   down." (Audience: a school policy document with no house
   style stated.)
3. "The committee is comprised of teachers, parents, and
   students."
4. "To boldly revise is sometimes the only honest option."
5. "She gave the award to James and I."

## Part B — Syntax revision (20 points)

6. (20 points) Rewrite the following paragraph (original,
   written for this assessment) using deliberate syntax
   variation and sentence-level repair. Your revision must:
   (a) vary sentence length (no three adjacent sentences the
   same length class S/M/L — mark S/M/L in the margin);
   (b) include one periodic sentence and one parallel
   structure (label them); (c) repair every nominalization,
   redundancy, and vague verb you can defend repairing, without
   losing any true claim; (d) be accompanied by a revision
   note naming each change with its reason.

   > "The utilization of multiple sources by the writer makes
   > possible the achievement of a stronger argument. Due to
   > the fact that the sources disagree, the writer is involved
   > in a process of weighing of evidence. The argument is
   > made stronger by the weighing. This is important."

## Part C — Vocabulary in context (5 points each; 25 points)

For each word in its sentence, (a) name the clue type or word
parts you used, (b) give the meaning in your own words,
(c) write an original sentence using the word accurately.
Dictionary verification is allowed and expected.

7. "The *implication* of the finding — that the method, not
   the sample, caused the difference — changed the next
   study's design."
8. "His *evaluation* of the evidence was careful, even where
   it contradicted his own earlier claim."
9. "The *ambiguity* was deliberate: the author wanted two
   readings to remain possible."
10. "She *derived* the conclusion from three premises, each
    stated and defended in turn."
11. "The *constraint* was not money but time: the archive
    closed in three weeks."

## Part D — Style analysis (30 points)

12. (30 points) Write 250–350 words answering: "When is a
    long, complex sentence better than two short ones, and
    when is it worse? Answer with two original example
    sentence pairs (one pair where the long sentence wins, one
    pair where the short sentences win), and explain each
    choice with syntax vocabulary from this unit (periodic /
    loose, subordination, end weight, parallelism)."

**Total: 100 points.** Passing: 70/100 with no part below half
marks. Below that, reteach the weakest part using the teacher
guide's moves and resubmit that part only. This assessment is
not standardized or validated beyond this curriculum; it
measures this unit's objectives as stated in `../README.md`.
