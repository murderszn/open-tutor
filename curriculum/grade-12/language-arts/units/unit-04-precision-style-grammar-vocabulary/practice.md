# Practice — Unit 04: Precision Style

Additional guided and independent practice. Worked solutions
and acceptable variation are in `answer-key.md` — do not open
the key until you have attempted a set. Sessions 10 and 16 use
these sets for review; the formative quiz and unit assessment
draw on the same skills, not the same sentences.

## Set A — Usage: the reference-work test (Lessons 01, 05)

For each sentence, write a case card: the remembered rule,
the reference's verdict (paraphrased, reference named), the
audience, and the choice with its cost. Mark each case
**contested**, **settled error**, or **settled correct** before
you write the card — the mark is part of the answer.

1. "Each student should bring their notebook to the seminar."
2. "The data are consistent across all three trials."
3. "The data is consistent across all three trials."
   (Compare with case 2: same claim, different audience.
   Write both cards and say which audience each fits.)
4. "Whom did the committee elect as chair?"
5. "Who did the committee elect as chair?"
   (Compare with case 4: when is the formal choice worth its
   stiffness, and when is it not?)
6. "The novel comprises three interwoven stories."
7. "The novel is comprised of three interwoven stories."
8. "To fully understand the argument, read the second chapter
   first." (Is anything here actually an error? Check before
   you "fix" it.)
9. "Between you and me, the budget will not pass."
10. "The gift was for my sister and I."

## Set B — Syntax and sentence-level repair (Lessons 02, 03)

1. Rewrite this paragraph in three different sentence-length
   patterns (short–long–short; long–medium–short; all medium)
   and state the effect of each pattern in one sentence:
   "The author introduces the claim in the first paragraph.
   The evidence follows in the second. The counterargument is
   answered in the third. The conclusion restates the claim
   with the evidence behind it."
2. Repair the faulty parallelism in each sentence, naming what
   was unequal: (a) "Good revision requires patience, careful
   reading, and to be honest about weak claims." (b) "The
   essay argues, explains, and a conclusion that follows."
   (c) "She is a careful reader, a sharp critic, and writes
   clearly."
3. For each sentence, decide whether the passive is justified
   or accidental, and rewrite or defend it in one sentence:
   (a) "The error was noticed too late." (b) "The samples
   were weighed before mixing." (c) "Mistakes were made in
   the budget." (d) "The treaty was signed in 1848."
4. Diagnose and repair this paragraph (original, written for
   this unit). Name every fault you find, by number from
   Lesson 03 (1 nominalization, 2 redundancy, 3 vague verb,
   4 modifier/parallelism):

   > "The utilization of evidence by the writer makes possible
   > the achievement of credibility. In the event that the
   > reader is given a process of checking the evidence, the
   > credibility is increased. Having checked the evidence,
   > the argument becomes convincing."

5. Rewrite this sentence as a periodic sentence and as a loose
   sentence, and state what each version emphasizes: "The
   proposal failed when the funding formula, which no one had
   explained, was finally published."
6. Move the emphasis in this sentence to three different end
   words, one version each, and name what each version
   stresses: "The committee, not the chair, wrote the report."
7. Write a paragraph of five sentences about any topic in
   which no two adjacent sentences share both length (S/M/L)
   and shape (simple/compound/complex). Mark each sentence
   S/M/L and its shape in the margin.
8. Find (or write) one nominalization you will keep, and
   defend it in two sentences, as in Lesson 03's independent
   practice. A kept nominalization without a defense counts as
   unrepaired.

## Set C — Vocabulary: inference, verification, use (Lesson 04)

For each item, show the four steps: clue or parts, inferred
meaning, dictionary-verified meaning in your own words, and an
original sentence using the word accurately.

1. "The *verdict* surprised no one: the evidence had pointed
   one way for weeks." (What does the legal context tell you
   before you check?)
2. "Her *tentative* conclusion — offered with three
   qualifications and an invitation to disagree — was the
   most honest sentence in the report."
3. "The *synthesis* of the two studies produced a claim
   neither study could make alone." (Parts: *syn-* + *thesis*?
   Check what *thesis* means here.)
4. "He *undermined* the argument not by attacking its
   conclusion but by removing one load-bearing premise."
   (The metaphor is doing work — name it.)
5. Build a word-change family for *press*: *pressure,
   pressing, pressurize, depress, suppress, repress, express*.
   For each form, give its part of speech and one original
   sentence. Mark any form whose meaning has drifted furthest
   from "push" and explain the drift in one sentence.
6. Connotation ladder: order these words for "saying something
   is true" from weakest to strongest commitment, and write
   one sentence in which only the word you placed fits:
   *suggest, claim, assert, maintain, concede, admit*.
7. Register check: for each pair, name the register (formal
   academic, general, informal) each word fits, and say which
   you would use in a college essay and why: (a) *ask /
   request*; (b) *kids / children / young people* (in a policy
   essay); (c) *a lot of / numerous*; (d) *get / obtain*.
8. Add five entries to your word bank from your own reading
   this week (not from this practice set), each with part of
   speech, definition in your own words, clue or parts, and an
   original sentence. Bring the bank to session 16's workshop:
   the adult checks that definitions are in your own words,
   not copied.

## How to use these sets

- Session 10 (mid-unit check): Sets A (cases 1–5), B (items
  1–4), C (items 1–4). Correct by reapplying the method, not
  by copying the key's sentence — a different correct sentence
  is still correct.
- Session 16 (style-sheet workshop): Sets A (cases 6–10), B
  (items 5–8), C (items 5–8). Use the results to test and
  revise style-sheet rules.
- Any set may be done orally with the adult scribing; the
  thinking and the justification, not the handwriting, are
  assessed.
