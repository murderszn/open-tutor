# Project — Media-Criticism Portfolio and Rhetorical Analysis Essay

**Deliverable:** a portfolio (source dossier + analysis essay +
seminar record) on one approved question. **Time:** Sessions 10, 15,
and 17, plus drafting between sessions; the seminar itself is
Session 16.

## The question

Choose one, with adult approval:

1. The unit question: Should the fictional town of Riverside extend
   its public library's evening hours (now, phased, or not at all)?
2. A different school-appropriate civic question approved by your
   adult (for example, a school schedule, facility, or community
   programme question). Real local questions must be handled as
   rhetoric to analyse, with no real person quoted without adult
   approval and no private information included.

## Steps

1. **Source dossier (Session 10).** Gather three sources in three
   different formats on your question (for the Riverside question,
   use the Lesson 05 media set plus one further adult-supplied
   source; for a new question, use adult-selected or adult-supplied
   sources). For each source, record: format and kind (news, opinion,
   advocacy, video, chart, speech); author/speaker and date if known;
   sourcing notes (who, for whom, with what interest); one SIFT move
   with its result; and what the source omits.
2. **Evidence map (Session 15).** On one page, list: your claim;
   your two strongest pieces of evidence with quotations or figures;
   the strongest counterclaim, fairly stated; your concession (if
   any); and your rebuttal. Mark every figure as counted, estimated,
   or fictional practice data, as it actually is.
3. **Essay (draft Session 15, revised Session 17).** Write a
   600–800-word rhetorical analysis essay that: states your position
   and its limits; analyses at least two sources' rhetorical
   situations and appeals; evaluates one argument's structure
   (claim, evidence, warrant, counterclaim); identifies one
   fallacy or, if you find none, explains what makes one argument's
   reasoning hold; integrates at least two formats; and names one
   discrepancy and one piece of missing evidence.
4. **Seminar (Session 16).** Present a two-minute opening, respond
   to one objection with evidence, and close with a synthesis: what
   both sides agree on, what remains disputed, and what evidence
   would settle it. Your adult records the seminar briefly (who
   spoke is private; the record kept in the portfolio is your own
   written synthesis, not a transcript).
5. **Assembly (Session 20).** File the dossier, evidence map, final
   essay, seminar synthesis, and this rubric with your self-scores.
   Keep the portfolio in private storage; do not publish it in the
   repository.

## Materials

Notebook or document, the unit lessons, the Lesson 05 media set, and
adult-selected sources. No purchased materials are required. If a
source is behind a paywall or requires an account, your adult
substitutes a free, no-account source covering the same question.

## Rubric — 40 points

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Sound | 2 — Partial | 1 — Weak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sourcing and SIFT (8 pts: 2 sources × 4) | Each source sourced (who, when, interest, kind) with a recorded SIFT move and result. | Sourcing mostly complete; SIFT move recorded for each. | Sourcing thin or SIFT move missing for one source. | Sources listed without sourcing or SIFT. |
| Rhetorical analysis (8 pts) | Two sources' situations and appeals analysed with quotations, audience effects, and limits. | Two sources analysed with quotations; effects or limits thin. | One source analysed, or analysis without quotations. | Appeals labelled without analysis. |
| Argument evaluation (8 pts) | Structure mapped (claim, evidence, warrant, counterclaim); fallacy correctly diagnosed or reasoning defended with evidence. | Structure mapped; diagnosis or defence mostly supported. | Structure partly mapped; diagnosis asserted, not shown. | No mapping; fallacy labels without reasoning. |
| Integration across formats (8 pts) | Two or more formats integrated; one discrepancy and one gap named, with effect on the conclusion. | Two formats used; discrepancy or gap named. | Formats listed side by side, not integrated. | One format only. |
| Seminar and synthesis (8 pts) | Opening, evidence-based objection response, and synthesis (agreement, dispute, evidence needed) all present and fair. | All three present; fairness or evidence thin in one. | One element missing or unfair to opposition. | No seminar record or synthesis. |

**Scoring note:** a portfolio that takes a side the adult disagrees
with, but meets the rubric, scores the same as one that agrees with
the adult. Position is never graded; evidence, fairness, and
reasoning are.

**Readiness / reteach:** if the dossier scores below 5 of 8 on
sourcing, reteach Lesson 05 with a new adult-supplied source before
the essay is revised. If argument evaluation scores below 5, reteach
Lessons 03–04 using the learner's own evidence map as the worked
example.
