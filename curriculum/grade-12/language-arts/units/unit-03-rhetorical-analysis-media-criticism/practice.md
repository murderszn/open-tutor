# Practice — Unit 03: Rhetorical Analysis and Media Criticism

Work in order. Answers are in `answer-key.md`, not here. All texts
below are original and fictional, written for this unit, unless a
task says to use an adult-selected text.

## Set A — Rhetorical situation, appeals, point of view (Lessons 01–02)

Read this fictional paragraph before items A1–A3.

> "As the volunteer who shelved books every Saturday for three
> years, I can tell you the children's corner is the busiest room in
> this building on a weekend morning. Last Saturday I counted
> forty-one children and parents there before noon, while the meeting
> room next door sat empty. A library that fills its children's
> corner and empties its meeting room is telling us, plainly, where
> its life is."

A1. Name the rhetorical situation in five parts: speaker, audience
(you may infer a plausible audience and say that you are inferring
it), purpose, topic, and context.
A2. Identify one ethos appeal, one pathos appeal, and one logos
appeal in the paragraph, quoting the words for each.
A3. Rewrite the final sentence in a flat, administrative tone.
What is lost and what is gained?

A4. A speaker says, "Trust me — I have lived in Riverside all my
life, so I know what this town needs." Name the appeal, and explain
in two sentences why long residence alone does not make this a strong
appeal for a library-hours decision.
A5. Compare these two framings of the same fictional count: (i) "242
visits in a sample week"; (ii) "fewer than 50 visits a night."
Explain in three sentences what each framing invites the audience to
conclude, and which facts neither framing changes.
A6. Write a three-sentence paragraph in which word choice alone
changes the tone from urgent to calm, on any school-appropriate
topic. Underline the words that do the tonal work.

## Set B — Structure, warrants, fallacies (Lessons 03–04)

B1. Map this fictional argument: "Riverside should keep the library
open later because 242 people visited in one sample week." Give its
claim, its evidence, and its warrant, in your own words.
B2. State one counterclaim to B1 that its author does not answer,
and one rebuttal the author could give. Both must be fair.
B3. Label the fallacy in each sentence and quote the breaking point:
(a) "Opponents of the garden just hate green spaces." (b) "Either we
fund the garden or the neighbourhood has no nature at all."
(c) "Two neighbours complained, so the whole street is unhappy."
(d) "If we allow one food truck, soon the street will be nothing but
food trucks."
B4. Repair sentence B3(d) so it argues the same conclusion (be
cautious about food trucks) without a fallacy. You may narrow the
claim or add evidence; state what you changed.
B5. Explain in three sentences why a speaker who concedes a strong
opposing point can become *more* persuasive, using Speech B
(Lesson 03) as an example.
B6. A student writes, "That speech used a straw man, so everything
it said is false." Explain in three sentences why this response is
itself a reasoning error, and what the correct response would be.

## Set C — Media, speakers, retrieval (Lessons 05–06, plus U01–U02)

C1. Using the media set in Lesson 05 (items A–D), state one fact on
which all four items agree, one fact reported by only one item, and
one claim made by only one item that the others do not support.
C2. Run SIFT in note form (one line per letter) on this fictional
post: "SHOCKING: Library to close FOREVER next week, insiders say!!
No news outlet will report this!" End with a verdict sentence.
C3. Evaluate a speaker you have heard recently (a teacher, a coach,
an adult-selected recording — no name needed in your written work
unless your adult asks for one) in the five parts from Lesson 06,
with one sentence per part.
C4. Retrieval — U02: state, in two sentences, how comparing two
texts on one theme (U02's method) differs from comparing two media
formats on one question (this unit's method).
C5. Retrieval — U01: a text leaves a question unanswered. Explain in
two sentences why "the text leaves this uncertain" can be a finding,
not a failure of your reading.
C6. Write a 100-word seminar opening on the library question that
states a position, cites one piece of evidence, and fairly states
one objection. Underline the objection sentence.
