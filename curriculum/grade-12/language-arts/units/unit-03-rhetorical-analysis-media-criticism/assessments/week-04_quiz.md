# Week-04 Quiz — Unit 03: Rhetorical Analysis and Media Criticism

**20 points · about 25 minutes · closed book except the unit corpus
texts your adult allows.** Answers are in `../answer-key.md`, not
here. All texts below are original and fictional, written for this
unit.

**Q1 (2 pts).** Name the five parts of a rhetorical situation.

**Q2 (2 pts).** Read this fictional sentence: "I have shelved books
in this library for nine years, and I have never seen a Tuesday as
busy as last Tuesday." Which appeal is primary — ethos, pathos, or
logos? Quote the words that carry it, and give one reason for your
choice.

**Q3 (2 pts).** What is a warrant? Define it in one sentence, then
give the warrant for this argument: "Riverside should extend library
hours because 242 people visited in one sample week."

**Q4 (2 pts).** A speaker argues, "Either we extend the hours or the
library abandons its readers." Name the fallacy and explain in one
sentence where the reasoning breaks.

**Q5 (2 pts).** A speaker argues, "One busy Tuesday proves every
evening will be busy all year." Name the fallacy and explain in one
sentence where the reasoning breaks.

**Q6 (2 pts).** What is the difference between corroboration and
repetition? Answer in two sentences, using the Lesson 05 media set
(items A and B) as your example.

**Q7 (2 pts).** Define lateral reading in one sentence, and state
which letter of SIFT it most closely matches.

**Q8 (2 pts).** A video claims "hundreds every single week" about
Riverside library evenings, citing the sample week in Lesson 05
(item D: 38, 51, 44, 62, 47). Is the video's wording fully supported
by item D? Answer yes or no, with one reason using the numbers.

**Q9 (2 pts).** Name two of the five parts of a speaker evaluation
from Lesson 06, and for each, write one question an evaluator should
ask.

**Q10 (2 pts).** A classmate says, "That argument used a fallacy, so
its conclusion must be false." Is the classmate right? Answer yes or
no, with one reason.
