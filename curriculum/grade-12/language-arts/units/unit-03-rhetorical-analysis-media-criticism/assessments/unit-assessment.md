# Unit Assessment — Unit 03: Rhetorical Analysis and Media Criticism

**40 points · about 50 minutes (Session 19) · independent work.**
Answers and scoring guidance are in `../answer-key.md`, not here.
All texts below are original and fictional, written for this unit.
This assessment is a classroom draft, not a standardised or validated
test.

Read this fictional address before Tasks 1–3. Speaker: Jo Park, a
fictional Riverside resident, speaking to a fictional town meeting.

> "I have lived on the same street as this library for twenty years,
> and I have watched children grow up walking to it after school.
> Last month, on the three evenings I visited, the reading room was
> full each time — not a seat free. Anyone who says evening readers
> do not exist has simply not looked. If we do not extend the hours
> now, we will lose a generation of readers, and a town that loses
> its readers loses its future. The council should act this month,
> before the budget is set, because waiting is the same as refusing."

## Task 1 — Rhetorical situation and appeals (10 pts)

(a) Name the rhetorical situation in five parts (speaker, audience,
purpose, topic, context), with a brief reason for each part.
(b) Identify one ethos, one pathos, and one logos appeal in the
address, quoting the words for each and explaining the audience
effect of each.
(c) State one limit of the address's evidence, in two sentences.

## Task 2 — Structure and reasoning (10 pts)

(a) Map the address's argument: claim, evidence, warrant, and
counterclaim handling (state what counterclaim, if any, is addressed,
and what is missing).
(b) Name one fallacy in the address, quote the breaking point, and
explain where the reasoning breaks.
(c) Rewrite one sentence of the address so the fallacy is repaired
without changing the speaker's conclusion. State what you changed.

## Task 3 — Style and power (10 pts)

Write a 150-word analysis of how word choice and syntax in the
address contribute to its power. Quote at least twice, name one
framing choice, and judge whether the style strengthens or weakens
the argument's persuasiveness for a town-meeting audience — with a
reason that goes beyond "it sounds good."

## Task 4 — Media integration (10 pts)

Use the Lesson 05 media set (items A–D) on the library question.
Write a 200-word integrated answer to the question, "Should
Riverside extend its evening hours, phase them, or keep them as they
are?" Your answer must: use at least two formats from the set; name
one discrepancy or omission among the sources; name one piece of
missing evidence; and state a position with its limits — what your
position does *not* yet prove.
