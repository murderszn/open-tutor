# Grade 12 Language Arts

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are
delivered; the eight units and the review package are planned, not yet written.
See [scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained
for one grade-12 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials,
and supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades
in private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 12 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-12 language arts course following the
**advanced literature, writing, and communication** pathway: applying critical
lenses to interpret complex literature; reading world literature in
translation alongside its historical contexts; analyzing rhetoric in argument
and contemporary media; refining precision style, grammar, and academic
vocabulary; writing advanced analytical, argumentative, and creative pieces;
conducting a sustained independent research synthesis with explicit source
evaluation; practicing professional, civic, and postsecondary communication;
and closing with an independent reading seminar and a revised capstone
portfolio.

Sessions run about 50 minutes, five per week: explicit lessons with modeled
analysis, close-reading workshops on original or public-domain texts (a guiding
adult selects any licensed editions through a library), writing studios with
same-day adult-reviewed drafting and revision, discussion seminars the learner
leads, and review. Anchor texts are public-domain works in translation and
later public-domain selections — Homer's *Odyssey* (translation excerpt),
Sophocles' *Antigone*, Dante's *Inferno* (excerpt), Cervantes' *Don Quixote*
(excerpt), Ibsen's *A Doll's House*, Chekhov short stories, Tagore poems —
with adult-selected licensed editions only where a later work is essential and
always through the learner's library. Contemporary speeches and media for the
rhetoric units are adult-selected and supervised; no copyrighted texts are
reproduced in the repository.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Read and analyze complex literary texts at the high end of the grades
   11–CCR text complexity band independently and proficiently, citing strong
   and thorough textual evidence for explicit and inferential claims,
   including where a text leaves matters uncertain.
2. Determine two or more themes or central ideas in literary and informational
   texts, analyze how they develop and interact to produce a complex account,
   and write an objective summary.
3. Apply critical lenses (such as feminist, Marxist, postcolonial,
   reader-response, and new historicist approaches) to interpret literature,
   and analyze cases where grasping a point of view requires distinguishing
   what is directly stated from what is really meant (satire, sarcasm, irony,
   understatement).
4. Compare works across cultures and historical periods — including world
   literature in translation and eighteenth-, nineteenth-, and
   early-twentieth-century foundational American works — analyzing how two or
   more texts treat similar themes or topics, and evaluate multiple
   interpretations of a work across versions.
5. Analyze word choice, including figurative, connotative, and technical
   meanings, and syntax for their impact on meaning and tone; interpret
   figures of speech in context and analyze their role in the text.
6. Delineate and evaluate reasoning in seminal texts and works of public
   advocacy; determine an author's point of view or purpose in highly
   rhetorical texts, analyzing how style and content create power,
   persuasiveness, or beauty; and evaluate whether an exposition's or
   argument's structure makes its points clear, convincing, and engaging.
7. Analyze and critique contemporary argument and media: evaluate a speaker's
   point of view, reasoning, evidence, and use of rhetoric; integrate and
   evaluate multiple sources presented in different media and formats to
   address a question.
8. Write arguments about substantive topics or texts using valid reasoning and
   relevant, sufficient evidence: precise claims distinguished from
   counterclaims, fair development of both sides, cohesion through varied
   syntax, formal style and tone, and a conclusion that follows from the
   argument; write informative and explanatory texts with precise,
   domain-specific vocabulary; and write narratives using effective technique,
   well-chosen details, and well-structured event sequences.
9. Conduct short and sustained research projects driven by self-generated
   questions: use advanced searches, gather from multiple authoritative print
   and digital sources, assess each source's strengths and limitations,
   synthesize findings, integrate information selectively without plagiarism,
   and follow a standard citation format.
10. Draw evidence from literary and informational texts to support analysis,
    reflection, and research; write routinely across extended and short time
    frames for a range of tasks, purposes, and audiences.
11. Initiate, lead, and participate effectively in collaborative discussions
    and seminars — prepared, evidence-based, and civil — propelling
    conversation with probing questions, synthesizing perspectives, and
    resolving contradictions; present findings with a clear and distinct
    perspective and strategic use of digital media, adapting speech to formal
    contexts.
12. Demonstrate command of standard English grammar and usage (including
    resolving complex or contested usage with reference works),
    capitalization, punctuation, and spelling; vary syntax deliberately for
    effect; and acquire and use accurately the general academic and
    domain-specific vocabulary needed for college and career readiness.

## Standards basis

Objectives are crosswalked to the Common Core State Standards for English
Language Arts, grades 11–12 band (Reading: Literature, Reading: Informational
Text, Writing, Speaking & Listening, Language). Standard codes and descriptions
were verified against the official standards pages at
<https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/> on 2026-10-08; the
scope-and-sequence paraphrases the expectations and cites exact codes. This is
a reference alignment for planning — it is not a claim of state adoption,
accreditation, or standards certification, and no state or district
requirements were specified.

## Planned units

- **U01** — Advanced close reading: critical lenses and interpretation
- **U02** — Comparative world literature and historical context
- **U03** — Rhetorical analysis: argument and media criticism
- **U04** — Precision style: grammar and vocabulary refinement
- **U05** — Advanced analytical, argumentative, and creative writing
- **U06** — Research synthesis: methodology and source evaluation
- **U07** — Professional, civic, and postsecondary communication
- **U08** — Independent reading seminar and revised capstone portfolio
- **R00** — Diagnostic, midyear and final review, cumulative assessment, and
  keys (delivered last)

Units are planned in prerequisite order: close reading with lenses before
comparative literature; comparative reading before rhetorical analysis;
rhetoric before precision style; style before advanced writing; writing before
sustained research; research before professional and civic communication;
everything before the independent seminar and capstone portfolio.

## For the guiding adult

- **Pacing:** eight four-week units (20 sessions each) plus four flexible
  weeks — diagnostic placement, catch-up, midyear review, final review — for
  about 36 weeks. The scope-and-sequence shows weekly goals and how lessons,
  reading, writing studios, seminars, and review fill each unit.
- **Entry skills:** the learner should arrive able to cite strong textual
  evidence, analyze theme and structure, evaluate seminal-document reasoning,
  write organized argument and exposition, conduct research with ethical
  source use, and participate in evidence-based seminars (the grade-11
  track's end-of-year objectives); the diagnostic week verifies these and the
  track re-teaches insecure skills in use. This track's new content is
  independent reading at the high end of the complexity band, critical-lens
  interpretation, world literature in translation, contemporary media
  criticism, precision style, sustained independent research, professional and
  civic communication, and seminar leadership with a capstone portfolio.
- **Texts:** prefer public-domain editions (Project Gutenberg, Library of
  Congress). Any licensed work comes through the learner's library; never
  reproduce copyrighted texts in the repo. Contemporary media and speeches
  are adult-selected, previewed, and supervised.
- **Research and media supervision:** preview every source the learner will
  use; supervise all internet searching and media viewing; keep the learner's
  name and identifying details out of research products and correspondence.
  Civic letters and portfolio pieces are reviewed by an adult and completed
  privately before any external sharing.
- **Supports:** audiobook alternatives (e.g., LibriVox) for long texts,
  chunked reading with guided annotation, speech-to-text for drafting,
  graphic organizers for argument and research, sentence frames for seminar
  leadership, extended time, and written discussion alternatives to live
  seminars. Extension: additional primary sources, comparative criticism,
  translation comparison, and publication-style revision.
- **Assessment:** each unit has a formative quiz and a culminating
  assessment; separate teacher guides and answer keys ship with each unit.
  The R00 package adds the diagnostic, midyear/final reviews, and cumulative
  assessment.
