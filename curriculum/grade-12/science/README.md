# Grade 12 Science

Independent, grade-specific materials in the public OpenTutor curriculum library.
This track is a **draft in progress**: the audit and scope-and-sequence are
delivered; the eight units are planned, not yet written. See
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**Do not refer a learner to another grade track.** This folder is self-contained
for one grade-12 learner at a time; a guiding adult adapts pacing, materials,
and supports. Keep completed learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades
in private storage — never in this public repository.

See also: [Grade 12 track](../README.md), [curriculum index](../../README.md).

## What this track covers

A full-year (about 36 weeks) grade-12 science course following the **Earth and
Space Science** pathway: the evidence for Earth's 4.6-billion-year history —
stratigraphy, index fossils, and radiometric dating; plate-tectonic evidence
from seafloor ages, magnetic striping, earthquakes, and volcanoes; mantle
convection and how internal and surface processes build continents and ocean
floors; atmosphere and ocean circulation driven by unequal heating and Earth's
rotation; weather systems and regional climates; the greenhouse effect,
climate feedbacks, and what global climate models can and cannot say;
quantitative carbon cycling among reservoirs; biodiversity, ecosystem services,
and conservation design; the water cycle and watershed stewardship; soil, land
use, and pollution; energy resources compared by cost, benefit, and risk; and
a capstone in which the learner plans and carries out a supervised local
environmental investigation and connects it to Earth–space evidence (the Sun's
energy, stellar element production, and the early universe).

Sessions run about 50 minutes, five per week: explicit concept lessons with
worked examples, quantitative data-analysis sessions using public datasets
(USGS, NOAA, and similar public sources verified at build time), adult-supervised
field observations and simple water/soil tests using low-cost apparatus, PhET
and other interactive simulations as the default alternative whenever a
procedure is hazardous, weather-dependent, or materials are unavailable, plus
reading and review. All field work follows the safety rules in the
scope-and-sequence (§6): no storm chasing, no drinking sampled water, adult
preview of every procedure, and simulation or structured data analysis
substituted whenever supervision or materials are inadequate.

## Track objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the year the learner will be able to:

1. Construct and interpret a geologic time scale: order Earth's major events
   with stratigraphic principles, index fossils, and radiometric-dating
   calculations; and explain the assumptions and uncertainties behind absolute
   ages.
2. Evaluate multiple lines of evidence (seafloor rock ages, magnetic striping,
   earthquake and volcano locations, fossil and rock-type matches across
   continents) to support plate tectonics; and explain how plate motions
   account for the ages of crustal rocks.
3. Describe how thermal convection in Earth's interior cycles matter and
   energy; connect mantle motion to plate motion; and explain how internal and
   surface processes operating at different spatial and temporal scales form
   continental and ocean-floor features.
4. Model how unequal heating and Earth's rotation drive atmospheric and ocean
   circulation; relate circulation patterns to observed weather systems and
   regional climates; and explain the properties of water that make it central
   to Earth's surface processes.
5. Explain how variations in the flow of energy into and out of Earth's systems
   produce changes in climate; identify reinforcing and balancing feedbacks
   (ice–albedo, water vapor, carbon cycle); and describe how feedbacks amplify
   or moderate a climate change.
6. Analyze geoscience data and published results from global climate models to
   make an evidence-based forecast of the current rate of climate change and
   its associated impacts; and state clearly what is well established, what is
   uncertain, and what a single model run cannot decide.
7. Build a quantitative model of carbon cycling among the hydrosphere,
   atmosphere, geosphere, and biosphere; use reservoir and flux numbers to
   estimate residence times; and evaluate claims about sinks and sources
   against the model.
8. Plan and conduct an investigation of the properties of water and their
   effects on Earth materials and surface processes: write a testable question,
   identify variables, record data in tables with units, represent results
   with graphs, discuss sources of error, and communicate evidence-based
   conclusions — with the adult approving every procedure before it begins.
9. Analyze biodiversity data for a real place: describe the ecosystem services a
   local or regional ecosystem provides; evaluate threats; and design,
   evaluate, and refine a conservation or restoration solution that reduces
   human impacts on the environment and biodiversity.
10. Construct evidence-based explanations for how the availability of natural
    resources, the occurrence of natural hazards, and changes in climate have
    influenced human activity; and evaluate competing design solutions for
    developing, managing, and using energy and mineral resources using explicit
    cost–benefit criteria.
11. Compare energy systems (solar, wind, hydro, nuclear, fossil fuels, and
    storage) by resource requirements, emissions, land use, reliability, and
    waste; and evaluate or refine a technological solution that reduces the
    impacts of human activities on natural systems.
12. Plan and carry out a safe capstone environmental investigation of a local
    question: define variables, choose apparatus or datasets, record data with
    units, represent relationships with graphs, evaluate sources of error and
    uncertainty, relate findings to at least one Earth–space science mechanism
    (for example solar energy input, the carbon cycle, or plate-boundary
    processes), and communicate evidence-based conclusions — with the adult
    approving every procedure before it begins.

## Safety summary

Field and bench work in this track involves outdoor observations, water and
soil sampling, simple weather instruments, and data from public sources. The
full rules — outdoor precautions, water and soil handling, weather limits,
sun exposure, simulation alternatives, and the adult's veto over every
procedure — are in the scope-and-sequence (§6). No investigation in this track
uses hazardous chemicals, open flames, mains electricity, or high-voltage
equipment; nothing collected outdoors is eaten or drunk.

## Adult guidance

- Preview every external dataset, simulation, and video for advertising,
  reading level, and age suitability before the learner opens it; record what
  you checked and when.
- Approve every investigation procedure in advance and keep veto power over
  all field work. Weather, terrain, and local regulations outrank the plan.
- Treat data-analysis sessions as the core skill work: insist on named
  columns, stated units, and explicit statements of whether values are real
  reference data, rounded practice data, or fictional practice data.
- Require text alternatives (alt text or a data table) for every image or
  simulation screenshot used in an activity.
- Keep completed work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in private storage.
- Do not refer the learner to another grade's track; this folder is
  self-contained.
