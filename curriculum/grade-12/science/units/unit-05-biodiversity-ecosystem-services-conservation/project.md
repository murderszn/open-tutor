# Project — A Conservation Plan for One Local Green Space

**Unit 05 investigation · Sessions S14–S19 (planning, two survey/design
sessions, write-up) · Adult approves every procedure before it begins**

## The question

*For one local green space I can actually visit or study, what lives
there, what is it worth to the people nearby, what threatens it most,
and what single conservation action — designed, evaluated, and
refined — would do the most good?*

The green space is the learner's choice, agreed with the adult: a
park, a school ground, a stream bank, a woodlot edge, a community
garden, a pond, or a roadside meadow — any place with living things
and public access. The plan is a proposal, not a permission slip:
nothing in it is built, planted, or removed. The adult reviews the
final plan before it is shown to anyone else.

## Materials

Notebook, pencil, ruler or measuring tape, calculator or spreadsheet
(for the Shannon index and the species–area model), a camera or phone
for site photos (optional; photos stay in private storage and are
never put in the repository), the unit image for comparison
(`assets/meadow-pond-biodiversity.png`), and the resource pack's
iNaturalist and GBIF pages for identification help (adult previews;
no account required to browse).

## Safety (adult reads first)

1. The adult knows the location, route, and return time before any
   field session, and is present for all of it. Work is cancelled
   for lightning, high winds, flooding, or extreme heat.
2. Stay on paths and public areas. Do not enter private land, climb
   fences, wade above the ankles, or approach steep or unstable
   banks.
3. Observe wild animals from a distance. Never touch nests, dens,
   young animals, or any animal that looks sick or injured. Do not
   feed wildlife.
4. Do not collect plants, animals, fungi, or water. Do not taste
   anything. Wash hands after handling soil or natural materials.
   Unknown or possibly contaminated sites are observed only, never
   sampled.
5. Check local rules before the survey: some parks forbid
   off-path walking, and some species are protected. If the rules
   forbid a survey, use the public-data alternative below.
6. **Public-data alternative:** if the site, weather, or supervision
   is inadequate, skip field work and build the survey from public
   data instead — iNaturalist observations for the area (browse
   without an account) or GBIF occurrence records (free, no
   account). Record the source, the access date, and the search
   area for every record used, and state plainly that the data are
   other people's observations, not the learner's own.

## Steps

### 1. Choose the place and write the plan (S14)

With the adult, pick the green space and write a one-page plan:
the question (in the learner's own words), the survey method
(counting walk, timed observation, or public-data search), the
variables (what will be counted, over what area, for how long),
the materials, and the safety check. The adult signs off before
any field work.

### 2. Survey: what lives there (S15)

Carry out the survey exactly as planned. Record every observation
in a table with columns: **date, time, location within the site,
organism type (or "unknown — describe it"), count, notes**. Count
carefully: for plants, count individuals only where that is
sensible (a flower bed can be counted; a lawn cannot — record
"grasses (not counted individually)" and say so). For animals,
count individuals seen. Unknown organisms are described, not
guessed at — a wrong name is worse than "unknown brown beetle,
about 1 cm." End the session with: richness (number of organism
types recorded), the three most abundant types, and one sentence
on what the survey certainly missed (nocturnal animals, soil
life, birds that were silent — every survey misses something).

### 3. Measure: Shannon index and species–area check (S17)

From the survey table, compute the Shannon index H′ and evenness
J for the site (Lesson 01 method; a spreadsheet is fine), and
state the sampling effort the numbers depend on. Then use the
species–area relationship (Lesson 02) with the project's stated
values (c = 8, z = 0.25, fictional model constants for a practice
calculation) to predict how richness would change if the site's
area were halved — and write one sentence on what the model
cannot say (which species would be lost).

### 4. Value one service (S17)

Pick one ecosystem service the site provides (Lesson 03) and
value it with stated data: the service, its MEA category, the
valuation method (replacement cost, avoided damage, or market
value), the numbers used, the total, and the assumptions — plus
one sentence naming what the valuation leaves out. All numbers
must be labeled real (with source and date), estimated (with the
basis), or fictional practice data.

### 5. Rank the threats (S17)

Rank the five IPBES drivers for the site (Lesson 04), strongest
first, with one evidence phrase per driver from the survey, the
site's history, or a public source the adult previews. Name the
driver ranked first and the evidence that put it there.

### 6. Design, evaluate, refine (S17–S19)

- **Design:** propose one conservation action against the
  top-ranked driver, choosing a tool from Lesson 05's menu
  (protected area, corridor, restoration, ex situ, sustainable
  use, OECM). Write the design in five sentences or fewer:
  what, where, how, who would do it, what it would cost in
  rough terms (a range with its basis, or "cost not estimated"
  stated honestly).
- **Evaluate:** score the design on the Lesson 05 matrix
  (biodiversity gain ×2; cost, feasibility, community support
  ×1; 1–3 each), with one evidence phrase per score. Total it.
  Also score one alternative action the same way, so the choice
  is a comparison, not a coronation.
- **Refine:** name one evaluation finding that weakens the
  design, change the design in response to that finding, and
  test the change in the spreadsheet model (change the area or
  the criterion score the finding touches, and record the before
  and after). A refinement that answers no finding does not
  count.
- **Present:** write the final plan (see Deliverables) with the
  tradeoff stated plainly: what the design gains, what it costs,
  who bears the cost, and what it does not fix.

## Deliverables

A written plan, kept in private storage, of 4–6 pages:

1. **Site and question** — the place, the question, a labeled
   sketch or map of the site with the survey route marked.
2. **Survey results** — the data table (with units and effort),
   richness, H′, evenness J, the three most abundant types, and
   what the survey missed.
3. **Species–area prediction** — the halved-area calculation and
   its limit, in the learner's words.
4. **Service valuation** — one service, method, numbers,
   assumptions, and what is left out.
5. **Threat ranking** — the five drivers ranked with evidence.
6. **Design, evaluation, refinement** — the first design, the
   matrix scores for it and one alternative, the refinement with
   its finding and its model test, and the final recommendation
   with its tradeoff.
7. **Sources** — every external source used, with access dates;
   every dataset labeled real, estimated, or fictional practice
   data.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | Excellent (full marks) | Developing (half) | Missing/weak (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Survey (8) | Table complete with units and effort; richness, H′, J computed correctly; misses stated | Table present but effort or units missing; one calculation error | No table, or counts without method |
| Species–area model (6) | Halved-area prediction correct with steps shown; limit stated | Prediction present with an arithmetic slip, or limit missing | No prediction, or formula misused |
| Service valuation (6) | Category correct; method named; total correct; assumptions and omissions stated | Total correct but assumptions missing, or category wrong | No valuation, or number without method |
| Threat ranking (6) | Five drivers ranked with place evidence; top driver justified | Ranking present but evidence thin or generic | No ranking, or global ranking pasted without place evidence |
| Design and matrix (7) | One action against the top driver; matrix scored with evidence per score; alternative compared | Design present but not tied to the top driver, or matrix unscored | No design, or design without evaluation |
| Refinement (4) | Refinement answers a named evaluation finding; model test shown before/after | Refinement present but not tied to a finding | No refinement |
| Communication (3) | Tradeoff stated plainly (gain, cost, who bears it, what is not fixed); sources listed with dates | Tradeoff partial; sources incomplete | No tradeoff, or no sources |

**Scoring note for the adult:** a narrow design done well scores
higher than a broad design asserted. A learner who writes "cost
not estimated" honestly outscores one who invents a precise cost
without a basis.

## Alternatives

- **No safe site:** use the public-data alternative (iNaturalist
  or GBIF) for a nearby public area, and state in the plan that
  the survey is a desk study of other people's observations.
- **No spreadsheet:** the Shannon and species–area calculations
  can be done by hand with the lesson tables; the model test is
  then a hand calculation, recorded the same way.
- **Limited mobility:** the survey can be done from one seated
  viewpoint (a bench, a window, a car) — record the viewpoint
  and its limits instead of a walking route.
