# Unit 05 — Biodiversity, Ecosystem Services, and Conservation

**Grade 12 · Science · Earth and Space Science pathway · Weeks 19–22 of the course year**
(draft, delivered for issue #55; not educator-reviewed)

## Overview

Life is the biosphere's memory: every species alive today is the
survivor of billions of years of Earth systems and life shaping each
other. This unit follows that story from measurement to action, in
five moves, and ends with the learner's own conservation design:

1. **What biodiversity measures** — three levels (genetic, species,
   ecosystem); richness versus evenness; the Shannon index as one
   quantitative tool, with its limits stated plainly (HS-LS2-2).
2. **How biodiversity changes** — speciation and extinction; the
   species–area relationship; the argument from evidence about the
   coevolution of Earth's systems and life (HS-ESS2-7).
3. **Ecosystem services** — the Millennium Ecosystem Assessment's
   four categories (provisioning, regulating, cultural, supporting);
   valuing a service from stated data; who depends on services and
   who decides.
4. **Threats to biodiversity** — the IPBES five drivers; reading the
   Living Planet Index and the IUCN Red List without overstating
   what each number means; ranking threats for a named place
   (HS-LS2-6).
5. **Conservation design** — protected areas, corridors, restoration,
   ex situ work, and sustainable use; the Kunming–Montreal
   framework's 30-by-30 target; designing, evaluating, and refining
   a solution with explicit criteria and cost (HS-LS2-7; HS-LS4-6).

The essential question running through all four weeks is: **life
depends on variety we did not build and cannot easily rebuild — how
do we measure what is there, judge what it is worth, and choose
actions that actually keep it?**

The unit's investigation is *"A Conservation Plan for One Local
Green Space"* (see [project.md](project.md)): the learner surveys
one local green space (field observation with adult supervision, or
a public-data alternative), values one of its services, ranks its
threats, and designs, evaluates, and refines a conservation action
— then presents the plan with its uncertainties and tradeoffs.

## Prerequisites

From Units 01–04 and the Weeks 1–2 diagnostic:

- Ecosystem vocabulary from grade-9 biology (producers, consumers,
  decomposers; food webs; photosynthesis and cellular respiration
  as carbon-cycle processes) and the bridge reference
  [`resources/biology_fundamentals.md`](../../../../../resources/biology_fundamentals.md).
- Quantitative models with stated assumptions and uncertainty
  (Units 01 and 04): reading and building graphs, proportional
  reasoning, logarithms at a conceptual level, spreadsheet
  calculations, and the rule that every dataset names its columns,
  units, and whether values are real reference data, rounded
  practice data, or fictional practice data.
- Feedback reasoning from Unit 04: a change in one Earth system
  creates feedbacks in others.
- Investigation habits: a testable question, variables, data tables
  with units, error discussion, and evidence-based conclusions —
  with the adult approving every procedure before it begins.

No calculus is assumed. Every Shannon-index and species–area
calculation is worked step by step; natural logarithms are
introduced as a tool with a table of values given, and a
spreadsheet or calculator does the arithmetic.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Measure biodiversity at three levels — genetic, species, and
   ecosystem — and compute and interpret a Shannon diversity index
   (H′) from count data, stating what the number captures
   (richness and evenness) and what it cannot say. (HS-LS2-2;
   track objective 9)
2. Explain how speciation increases and extinction decreases
   biodiversity; use the species–area relationship S = cA^z to
   predict how richness changes when habitat area changes; and
   argue from evidence about the coevolution of Earth's systems
   and life. (HS-ESS2-7; track objective 9)
3. Classify ecosystem services into provisioning, regulating,
   cultural, and supporting categories; estimate the value of one
   service from stated data with assumptions named; and explain
   who depends on the service and who decides its fate.
   (track objective 9)
4. Evaluate claims, evidence, and reasoning about biodiversity
   change: read the IPBES and Living Planet Index findings without
   overstating them, use the IUCN Red List categories correctly,
   and rank the five IPBES drivers of loss for a named place with
   evidence. (HS-LS2-6; track objective 9)
5. Design, evaluate, and refine a conservation or restoration
   solution for a real local place, judged against explicit
   criteria (biodiversity gain, cost, feasibility, community
   support), and present the tradeoff honestly, including what
   the design does not fix. (HS-LS2-7; HS-LS4-6; HS-ESS3-3;
   track objectives 9, 12)
6. Plan and carry out the unit's conservation investigation: a
   testable question, a biodiversity survey with data recorded in
   tables with units, a service valuation, a threat ranking, and
   evidence-based conclusions — with the adult approving every
   procedure before it begins. (track objective 12)

Key sources, all checked 2026-11-13 (see
[resource-pack.md](resource-pack.md) for check records): IPBES
Global Assessment Report on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services
(2019) — about 1 million of an estimated 8 million animal and
plant species threatened with extinction; five drivers of loss.
WWF / Zoological Society of London, *Living Planet Report 2024* —
average size of monitored wildlife populations fell 73% between
1970 and 2020 (about 35,000 populations of 5,495 species; an
average of population trends, not a count of species lost).
IUCN Red List of Threatened Species (checked 2026-11-13) — more
than 49,500 species threatened, 28% of assessed species; more
than 175,900 species assessed; nine categories. Convention on
Biological Diversity, Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity
Framework (adopted 19 December 2022) — 4 goals for 2050, 23
targets for 2030, including Target 3 (conserve at least 30% of
land, inland waters, and coastal and marine areas by 2030) and
Target 2 (restore at least 30% of degraded ecosystems). All
worked datasets in lessons and practice are **fictional practice
data**, labeled as such wherever they appear.

## Safety

All field work is adult-supervised: the adult previews every
procedure, knows the location, route, and return time, checks the
weather first, and stops work for lightning, high winds, flooding,
or extreme heat. No handling of wild animals; observe from a
distance and never touch nests, dens, or young. No collecting
plants, animals, or water samples without the adult's explicit
permission and a check of local rules; unknown or possibly
contaminated sites are observed only, never sampled. Wash hands
after handling soil or natural materials. The public-data
alternative in `project.md` replaces field work whenever the site,
weather, or supervision is inadequate. See scope-and-sequence §6
and `project.md` § Safety for the full rules.
