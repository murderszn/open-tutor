# Unit Assessment — Unit 05: Biodiversity, Ecosystem Services, and Conservation

**50 points · ~50 minutes · Calculator allowed · All datasets are
fictional practice data unless stated otherwise**

Work alone. Show every calculation step and name every assumption.
The adult scores same-day; worked solutions are in the adult-only
`answer-key.md`. This assessment is a learning check, not a
standardized or validated test.

## Part A — Measuring biodiversity (Objective 1; 12 pts)

1. (6 pts) A fictional meadow sample has counts 40, 40, 10, 10
   (total N = 100, richness S = 4). Compute the Shannon index H′
   and the evenness J (ln 4 = 1.39). Show each p·ln p term. (Use
   ln 0.40 = −0.92, ln 0.10 = −2.30.)
2. (6 pts) Two fictional plots both have richness 4. Plot X has
   H′ = 1.35; Plot Y has H′ = 0.70. (a) Which plot is more
   diverse by the Shannon index, and what single word explains
   the difference? (b) Name two pieces of information you would
   need before recommending Plot X for protection over Plot Y,
   and say why the index alone cannot supply them.

## Part B — Change and models (Objective 2; 12 pts)

3. (6 pts) A fictional reserve follows S = 5 · A^0.30. Compute S
   for A = 100 km² and for A = 10 km² (10^0.30 ≈ 2.00,
   100^0.30 ≈ 3.98). If the reserve shrank from 100 km² to
   10 km², what richness loss does the model predict? Show your
   steps.
4. (6 pts) Argue from evidence that Earth's systems and life
   coevolved. Give one piece of evidence for life changing Earth
   and one for Earth changing life, and finish with one sentence
   stating a limit of your argument.

## Part C — Ecosystem services (Objective 3; 10 pts)

5. (5 pts) Classify each service into one MEA category
   (provisioning, regulating, cultural, supporting): (a) drinking
   water drawn from a spring; (b) bees pollinating an orchard;
   (c) the beauty of a viewpoint; (d) soil forming under a
   forest; (e) fish caught for food.
6. (5 pts) A fictional 30-hectare reed bed filters water at a
   replacement cost of $9,000 per hectare per year. Compute its
   annual filtration value. Then state two assumptions the
   estimate depends on and one thing it leaves out.

## Part D — Threats and evidence (Objective 4; 10 pts)

7. (5 pts) A headline reads "73% of wildlife wiped out since
   1970." Write a three-sentence correction: what the figure
   actually measures, one thing it does not mean, and what the
   IPBES figure of around 1 million threatened species adds.
8. (5 pts) A fictional coastal town cleared mangroves for shrimp
   ponds, fishes its bay heavily, dredges a new port (stirring
   sediment into the water), and records rising sea levels; no
   invasive species have been reported. Rank the five IPBES
   drivers for this town, strongest first, with one evidence
   phrase per driver.

## Part E — Conservation design (Objective 5; 6 pts)

9. (6 pts) Two options for the coastal town in question 8 are
   scored on the Lesson 05 matrix (biodiversity gain ×2; cost,
   feasibility, community support ×1; 3 = best, and for cost
   3 = cheapest):
   - A. Restore 8 hectares of mangrove: biodiversity 3, cost 1,
     feasibility 2, support 2.
   - B. Fishing quota with patrols: biodiversity 2, cost 3,
     feasibility 2, support 1.
   (a) Compute both totals. (b) Which option ranks higher, and
   what do the totals hide? (c) Name one evidence-based
   refinement for the higher-ranked option and the finding it
   answers.
