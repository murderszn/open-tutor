# Practice Sets — Unit 03: Atmosphere, Ocean Circulation, and Weather

Student-facing. Work each set in your notebook and show every
calculation step with units. Solutions are in the adult-only
`answer-key.md` — do not peek before the adult scores your attempt.

## Set A — Unequal heating, pressure, wind (Lesson 01)

1. At solar noon the zenith angle is 45°. Using cos 45° ≈ 0.71,
   what fraction of the overhead energy per square metre reaches
   the surface? If the overhead value is 1,000 W/m², how many
   W/m² arrive?
2. A fresh snowfield (albedo 0.8) and a dark forest (albedo 0.15)
   each receive 600 W/m². Compute absorbed energy for each, and
   state which surface warms faster and why.
3. A ship at 25° S wants to sail westward across the Atlantic with
   the wind. Which wind belt should it use, and which way does
   Coriolis deflect its air?
4. Explain why air sinks near 30° latitude, and why sinking air
   makes deserts likely. (Two mechanisms: the cell, and what
   sinking does to cloud formation.)
5. At what latitude is the Coriolis deflection zero? At what
   latitude is it strongest? Give the rule (sin of latitude) and
   the value of sin 30°.
6. A Northern Hemisphere surface low is drawn spinning clockwise.
   Is the drawing correct? Explain using the Coriolis deflection of
   inflowing air.
7. **Explain your reasoning:** A friend says, "Summer is hotter
   because Earth is closer to the Sun." Give the correct
   explanation in three sentences (tilt, Sun angle, day length).

## Set B — Ocean circulation and heat capacity (Lesson 02)

1. List the four legs of the North Atlantic gyre in order, starting
   at the equator, and name the wind belt driving each leg.
2. A current flows north along the east coast of Asia. Warm or
   cold? Justify with the gyre rule, and name the current.
3. Two 500 g samples, one water (c = 4.18 J/g·°C) and one sand
   (c = 0.84 J/g·°C), each absorb 2,090 J. Compute each temperature
   change and show the division.
4. Explain, in a chain of at least three links, why a coastal city
   has a smaller seasonal temperature range than an inland city at
   the same latitude.
5. North Atlantic surface water becomes colder and saltier as it
   travels north. What happens to its density, and what does it do
   next? What would fresher surface water do to that process?
6. **Error analysis:** "The California Current is warm because it
   flows along California, which is warm." Correct the error: state
   the current's actual character, its origin, and the gyre rule
   behind it.

## Set C — Air masses, fronts, cyclones (Lesson 03)

1. Decode "mP" and "cT." For each, state temperature, humidity, and
   one likely weather effect when it arrives.
2. A warm front's red semicircles point north. Which air mass is
   advancing, and what weather change should a station expect over
   the next day?
3. Put these cyclone stages in order: mature, occluded, stationary
   front, wave. For the mature stage, name the three parts of the
   system (cold front, warm front, warm sector) and their positions.
4. A station reports: wind SE, temperature rising, pressure
   falling, clouds thickening over 12 hours. Is a warm front or a
   cold front approaching? Justify with two observations.
5. Why does a cold front usually bring a shorter, sharper burst of
   precipitation than a warm front? Name the slope difference and
   the lifting difference.
6. **Explain your reasoning:** Two towns are 200 km apart. Town A
   is in the warm sector; Town B is behind the cold front. Both
   report rain at noon. Can both reports be correct? Explain how
   the same cyclone produces rain in two different air masses.

## Set D — Weather maps and regional climates (Lesson 04)

1. A chart shows isobars 4 mb apart near a low and 16 mb apart
   under a high. Where are winds stronger? State the rule.
2. Convert 00Z to Central Standard Time (UTC−6). A chart valid
   00Z Monday is valid at what local time Sunday/Monday?
3. Use the fictional five-day record in Lesson 04, Worked example 2:
   on which day did the cold front pass? Cite three pieces of
   evidence (pressure, wind, temperature).
4. A moist air parcel at 15 °C is lifted 3 km dry-adiabatically
   (9.8 °C/km). Compute its final temperature. Would you expect
   condensation if its dew point at the surface was 12 °C? Explain
   (the parcel cools below its dew point during the lift).
5. Explain the rain shadow in four steps, naming the temperature
   change on the windward ascent and on the lee descent.
6. **Error analysis:** "The high-pressure center on the map means
   it will be stormy there, because high pressure pushes air
   around violently." Correct the error: state what sinking air in
   a high usually produces, and where the stormy weather actually
   sits on the chart.

## Set E — Forecasting, models, uncertainty (Lesson 05)

1. An ensemble of 40 members: 10 show rain over your town. What is
   the probability of precipitation? If the members disagree
   wildly on timing, what does that say about confidence?
2. A forecaster says 10% chance of rain on 200 days; rain falls on
   19 of them. Well calibrated? Show the comparison (expected ≈ 20
   days, observed 19).
3. Explain ice–albedo feedback as a four-link chain, stating the
   direction of each link and whether the loop amplifies or damps
   the original change.
4. Rewrite this forecast to state uncertainty honestly: "It will
   snow 5 inches in your town on Saturday." (Give a probability or
   range, a time window, and the condition that would change it.)
5. **Explain your reasoning:** A 7-day forecast for next Sunday's
   picnic says "sunny," but Sunday brings rain. A 2-day forecast
   for the same Sunday, issued Friday, says "rain likely (80%)"
   and it rains. Which forecast was better, and why is comparing a
   7-day forecast with a 2-day forecast unfair unless you account
   for lead time?
