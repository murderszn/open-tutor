# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Atmosphere, Ocean Circulation, and Weather

**Adult-facing. Keep from the learner until the adult decides an item
is ready for review.** This guide covers pacing, differentiation,
reteaching, and project scoring. Worked solutions are in
`answer-key.md`.

## Pacing notes

- The unit is 20 sessions (see the README pacing table). The most
  common failure is letting Lessons 01–02 run long and squeezing
  the investigation: protect Sessions S15 (planning) and S16–S17
  (data). If time is short, cut the Lesson 05 extension, never the
  investigation planning session.
- Lessons 01 and 03 carry the most new vocabulary. Pre-teach the
  vocabulary list orally before each lesson rather than assigning
  it as silent reading.
- The image observation (Session S13) works best as a paired
  discussion: one learner names an instrument, the partner states
  what it would read after a front passage.

## Differentiation

- **Reading access:** Lessons 01–03 may be read aloud in pairs;
  the chart-reading order in Lesson 04 (centers → fronts →
  isobars → wind) is designed to be spoken as a routine.
- **Alternate response modes:** every written application in the
  lessons has a sketch-or-speak alternative noted in the lesson.
  Accept labeled diagrams for the three-cell model and the cyclone
  sketch on the assessment.
- **Extensions:** each lesson names one; the Lesson 02 upwelling
  extension and the Lesson 05 forecast-skill extension are the
  strongest for learners who finish early.
- **Support for struggling learners:** if the Sun-angle calculation
  blocks Lesson 01, use the worked example with different numbers
  until cos 0° = 1 and cos 60° = 0.5 are automatic; the rest of the
  unit needs only those two anchors plus the heat-capacity ratio
  (water : sand ≈ 1 : 5).

## Reteaching by evidence

- **Wind belts wrong:** return to Lesson 01, section 3, and rebuild
  the chain: cell → surface flow direction → Coriolis bend → wind
  name. Learners who memorize names without the chain will fail
  every new latitude.
- **Gyre legs wrong:** trace on a globe, not a flat list. The
  east-coast-warm / west-coast-cold rule is the check, not the
  starting point.
- **Fronts confused:** compare the slopes (steep wedge vs. gentle
  ramp) and the timescales (hours vs. a day). If the learner still
  swaps them, use the Lesson 03 application narrative as a
  re-test.
- **Probability misread:** re-teach Lesson 05, section 2 with the
  100-day calibration example. Do not accept "40% of the day" as a
  paraphrase; require "40% of similar occasions."
- **Map time wrong:** drill UTC conversion (CST = UTC−6, CDT =
  UTC−5) with three chart times before the Lesson 04 application.

## Project scoring notes

- Score the project with the rubric in `project.md` (20 points).
  The most important scoring judgment is honesty: a report that
  admits a miss and revises the claim scores higher than a report
  that cherry-picks a hit. Say this to the learner before data
  collection, not after.
- A 7-day shortened sample can earn full marks only if the report
  states the weaker conclusion explicitly (see the project
  alternatives section).
- If the learner uses the fictional-data alternative, the report
  must label it fictional in the title and the conclusion; an
  unlabeled fictional test scores 0 on the data-table criterion.

## Safety checklist (adult initials each item before field work)

- [ ] Forecast checked; no severe-weather warning for the area.
- [ ] Observation site approved; no roofs, no roads, no waterways.
- [ ] Stop rules understood: lightning, flooding, high wind → stop.
- [ ] Public-data alternative agreed in case conditions change.

This log is the unit's contribution to the track's review section
