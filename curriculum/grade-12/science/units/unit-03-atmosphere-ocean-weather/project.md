# Project — Testing a Front-Passage Prediction

**Investigation for Unit 03 (Weeks 13–14). Adult approval required
before data collection begins.**

## Question

Can a circulation-based prediction — built from pressure trend,
wind direction, and cloud observations — successfully predict a
front passage at your location over two weeks?

## Prediction (state BEFORE collecting data)

Before Day 1, write a prediction in this falsifiable form:

> "If the surface pressure falls steadily for at least 24 hours AND
> the wind shifts to a southerly direction AND cloud cover increases,
> then a front will pass within the next 48 hours, marked by a wind
> shift, a temperature change of at least 3 °C within 6 hours, and a
> pressure minimum followed by rising pressure."

You may adjust the thresholds with the adult's agreement, but you
may not change the prediction after seeing the data. Record the
final wording and the date it was set.

## Materials

- A data source the adult approves: either a free public forecast /
  observation source (see the resource pack — for example, the
  National Weather Service) or home instruments (thermometer,
  barometer or barometer app, wind observation, rain gauge).
- Notebook and a data table (below); graph paper or a spreadsheet.
- No travel, no climbing, no outdoor instruments on roofs. If severe
  weather is forecast, use the public-data route only.

## Procedure

1. **Plan (Session S15).** With the adult, agree the data source,
   the observation time (same time each day, plus one extra
   observation when a front seems near), the prediction wording,
   and the safety rules. The adult signs the plan.
2. **Collect (Sessions S16–S17).** For 14 consecutive days, record
   at the agreed time: date and time (local and UTC), temperature
   (°C or °F — pick one and stay consistent), pressure (hPa) and
   its trend (rising / falling / steady), wind direction (the
   direction the wind comes *from*), cloud cover (clear / scattered
   / overcast), precipitation (type and amount if known), and a
   one-line weather note.
3. **Graph.** Plot daily temperature and pressure on the same time
   axis (two lines, labeled axes, units). Mark any day you judge a
   front passed, with the evidence for that judgment.
4. **Test.** For each front-passage candidate, check it against the
   prediction: did the three precursor signals appear in the 48
   hours before? Did the passage markers appear? Count hits,
   misses (front passed without precursors), and false alarms
   (precursors without a front).
5. **Write up.** A report of 2–4 pages: question, prediction, method
   and data source, data table, graph, results (hits / misses /
   false alarms), error discussion, and a revised claim.

## Deliverables

1. The signed plan with the dated prediction.
2. The 14-day data table with units on every column.
3. The temperature-and-pressure graph with labeled axes.
4. The written report, including an explicit statement of where the
   prediction failed and what you would change next time.

## Rubric (adult scores; 20 points total)

| Criterion | Full marks | Partial | Missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prediction stated before data, falsifiable (4 pts) | Dated, falsifiable, with thresholds | Vague or set after data | No prediction |
| Data table complete, units consistent (4 pts) | 14 days, all columns, units | Gaps or unit changes unexplained | Fragmentary |
| Graph correct and labeled (4 pts) | Both series, labeled axes/units, front days marked | One series or missing labels | No graph |
| Test honestly scored (4 pts) | Hits, misses, false alarms counted; failures admitted | Only successes reported | No test |
| Revised claim follows the evidence (4 pts) | Claim matches the count; limits named | Claim overstates the result | No conclusion |

Score 16–20: the investigation supports the unit's objectives.
Score 12–15: revise the error discussion and the revised claim.
Below 12: re-do the test section with the adult before the unit
assessment.

## Safety

The adult checks the forecast before any outdoor observation and
stops all outdoor work for lightning, flooding, high winds, or any
severe-weather warning. Observation from indoors (through a window,
or from public data) is always an acceptable substitute. Never
approach flooded roads or waterways, never shelter under a tree in
lightning, never handle instruments in a storm.

## Alternatives

- **Public-data only:** use the National Weather Service
  observations and forecasts for your town for all 14 days; cite
  the source and access dates. No instruments needed.
- **Shortened version:** if 14 days is impossible, use 7 days with
  the adult's agreement, and state in the report that a 7-day
  sample can show at most one or two front passages — the
  conclusion must be weaker accordingly.
- **Simulation alternative:** if no outdoor observation and no
  public data are available, use the fictional five-day record in
  Lesson 04 as a worked test case, labeled fictional, and test the
  prediction against it — but the report must say the test used
  practice data, not observations.
