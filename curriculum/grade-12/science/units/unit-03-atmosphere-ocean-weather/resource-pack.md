# Resource Pack — Unit 03: Atmosphere, Ocean Circulation, and Weather

**Goal:** Give a grade-12 learner (and guiding adult) free, no-account
routes to every core objective: three-cell circulation, gyres and
deep circulation, fronts and cyclones, weather-map reading, and
forecast uncertainty. **Age fit:** high-school Earth and Space
Science (grade 12); the adult previews every link for advertising,
comments, reading level, and suitability before the learner opens
it. This pack is an adult planning judgment, not a prescription.
Every core objective also has a free, no-account, text-based route
inside the unit itself — no video or simulation is required to learn
the material.

## Focused queries

1. `three cell model Hadley Ferrel polar circulation trade winds westerlies explained`

## Web references

Checked 2026-10-28 unless noted.

1. **NOAA Ocean Service — "What is a gyre?"** — free, no account;
   the five major gyres, wind and density drivers, and the Gulf
   Stream named. Page found and scope confirmed 2026-10-28. Use
   for: Lesson 02 gyre background; project current checks.
   <https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/gyre.html>
2. **NOAA — "Ocean Circulations"** — free, no account; why surface
   currents exist, warm currents on east coasts, cold currents on
   west coasts, Gulf Stream speeds. Page found and scope confirmed
   2026-10-28. Use for: Lesson 02; the east-coast/west-coast rule.
   <https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/ocean/circulations>
3. **NOAA Ocean Service — "Currents" tutorial** — free, no account;
   tides, wind-driven currents, and thermohaline circulation
   distinguished. Page found and scope confirmed 2026-10-28. Use
   for: Lesson 02; surface vs. deep circulation contrast.
   <https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/education/tutorial_currents/>
4. **NASA My NASA Data — "Ocean Circulation Patterns"** — free, no
   account; warm vs. cold surface currents, Coriolis deflection,
   and gyre rotation. Page found and scope confirmed 2026-10-28.
   Use for: Lesson 02; SST and current-map reading.
   <https://mynasadata.larc.nasa.gov/index.php/basic-page/Ocean-Circulation-Patterns>
5. **UK Met Office — "How to read synoptic weather charts"** — free,
   no account; isobars, pressure patterns, fronts, troughs, and the
   warm sector explained with the standard symbols. Page found and
   scope confirmed 2026-10-28. Use for: Lesson 04 map-reading
   practice; the chart-reading order in the lesson follows it.
   <https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/weather/how-weather-works/synoptic-weather-chart>
6. **National Weather Service (weather.gov)** — free, no account;
   the authoritative U.S. source for current surface-analysis
   charts, observations, and forecasts used in Lesson 04 and the
   project. The adult confirms the chart's valid time (UTC) with
   the learner on first use. Use for: Lesson 04 nowcast; the
   project's public-data route. Record access dates in the
   project report.
   <https://www.weather.gov/>
7. **NGSS performance expectations (California Department of
   Education / NSTA listings)** — free, no account; the official
   wording of HS-ESS2-4 and HS-ESS2-2 used for the unit's standards
   crosswalk, verified 2026-10-28. Use for: adult background on
   the crosswalk; not assigned as learner reading.
   <https://www2.cde.ca.gov/cacs/science?c0=7&c2=2>
   <https://my.nsta.org/ngss/DisplayStandard.aspx?view=pe&id=185>
