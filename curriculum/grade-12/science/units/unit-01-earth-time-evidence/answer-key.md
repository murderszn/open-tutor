# Answer Key — Unit 01: Earth-System Evidence, Geologic Time, and Quantitative Models

**Adult only.** Never beside a student question. Every calculation
shows substitution and intermediate values. Accept equivalent
reasoning and correctly rounded alternatives (rounding to 2–3
significant figures is fine throughout). "Fictional practice" items
are checked against reality in the Reconciliation notes at the end.

## Lesson 01 — Deep time and relative dating

**Readiness check**
1. The bread — the bottom layer was placed first (superposition in
   the kitchen).
2. 1 billion years = 1,000 million years → 1,000 × 1 mm = 1,000 mm =
   **1.0 m**.

**Guided practice**
1. The intrusion is **younger** than B — it cuts through B, and by
   cross-cutting relationships the cutter is younger than the cut.
2. The three index-fossil criteria: **geographically widespread**,
   **short-lived** (narrow time range), **abundant** (commonly
   fossilized).
3. Oldest → youngest: tilted layers, tilting, fault, flat top layer.
   The layers were deposited (superposition), then tilted (original
   horizontality — tilting is younger than the layers), then faulted
   (the fault cuts the tilted layers), then the flat layer was
   deposited on top, undisturbed by the fault (so the fault is older
   than the flat layer).
4. 66 Myr × 0.2203 mm/Myr = 14.54 mm = **1.45 cm ≈ 1.5 cm** from the
   "today" end.

**Independent practice**
1. Accept paraphrases: superposition — bottom layer oldest in an
   undisturbed stack; cross-cutting — the cutter is younger than
   what it cuts; unconformity — an eroded/missing-record surface,
   younger than the rocks below, older than the rocks above.
2. Oldest → youngest: **conglomerate, footprint sandstone, intrusion,
   ash layer, shale, top sandstone.** Superposition orders the stack;
   cross-cutting puts the intrusion after the two layers it cuts but
   before the ash it does not disturb.
3. The shales correlate (same index fossil → same age window). Above
   the shale, both columns show sandstone ✓. But X has limestone
   *below* the shale while Y has limestone *above* it. The intended
   reading: **Outcrop Y is structurally overturned** (or faulted) —
   the fossil says the shales are the same age, so the reversed order
   records deformation, not deposition. Accept also: the upper
   limestone in Y could be a *different, younger* limestone unit —
   the fossil alone cannot rule that out; the learner must say what
   extra evidence would decide (e.g., sedimentary structures showing
   which way was up). Either interpretation earns full credit if the
   reasoning cites the fossil correlation.
4. 541 Myr × 0.2203 mm/Myr = 119.18 mm = **11.9 cm ≈ 12 cm**.
   Percentage: 541 / 4,540 = 0.1192 = **11.9% ≈ 12%**.
5. The error: an unconformity cannot predate the rocks it erodes —
   erosion needs something to erode. The unconformity is **younger**
   than the rocks below it (and older than the rocks above); the
   principle is cross-cutting/erosion relationships (unconformities).

**Application**
a) Oldest → youngest: granite basement (everything rests on or cuts
   it), sandstone (superposition), trilobite shale (superposition),
   limestone (superposition), eroded surface/unconformity (younger
   than the limestone it bevels), upper sandstone (youngest,
   deposited on the unconformity).
b) The surface is an unconformity: a gap — time passed with erosion
   or non-deposition. Cannot conclude: how much time is missing, or
   what rock was removed.
c) The principles give only relative order ("older/younger"), never
   a number of years. An absolute age needs a radiometric clock —
   e.g., dating minerals in the shale or in bracketing igneous layers
   (Lesson 02).

**Exit check**
1. Accept three-words-or-fewer glosses that preserve meaning, e.g.:
   superposition — "bottom oldest"; original horizontality — "laid
   flat"; lateral continuity — "layers extend sideways";
   cross-cutting — "cutter younger"; inclusions — "fragment older";
   unconformity — "gap in record."
2. The intrusion is **older** than the unconformity — the wavy
   surface eroded the intrusion's top, so the gap postdates it.

**Image activity** (`assets/geologic-outcrop-strata.png`)
Expected labeling: bands 1–6 bottom to top = siltstone, coal seam,
limestone, shale, sandstone, topsoil (+ grassy surface strip);
"F" marks cluster in band 3 (limestone); dike "D" traced diagonally
across all bands. Expected event order, oldest → youngest: **1
siltstone, 2 coal, 3 limestone, 4 shale, 5 sandstone, 6 topsoil
(superposition throughout), then the dike (cross-cutting
relationships — it cuts every band, so it is younger than all of
them).** Accept "grass/topsoil surface is youngest-but-one" only if
the learner notes the grass grew after deposition; the dike remains
unambiguously last.

## Lesson 02 — Radiometric dating calculations

**Readiness check**
1. After each removal: 50, 25, 12.5 coins (accept 50, 25, 12 or 13
   if the learner insists on whole coins — note the model uses
   halves of atoms, which are divisible).
2. 3 removals; fraction remaining = (1/2)^(number of removals).

**Guided practice**
1. 1/16 = (1/2)^4 → **4 half-lives**; t = 4 × 4.468 Ga = **17.872 Ga
   ≈ 17.9 Ga**. ⚠️ Red flag: this exceeds Earth's 4.54-Ga age — see
   Reconciliation notes. The arithmetic is the point; the
   impossibility is the lesson.
2. 1/4 = 2 half-lives → t = 2 × 5,730 yr = **11,460 yr**.
3. 1/0.37 = 2.7027; ln(2.7027) = 0.9943; 0.9943 / 0.6931 = 1.4344;
   1.248 × 1.4344 = **1.790 Ga ≈ 1.79 Ga**.
4. The error: the learner plugged the parent:daughter *ratio* (1/3)
   in as the *remaining fraction*. Correct: N/N₀ = 1/(1+3) = **1/4**
   → **2 half-lives** (not 1.58). Rule restated: fraction =
   parent/(parent + daughter).

**Independent practice**
1. 1:1 → N/N₀ = 1/2 → t = 1 × 48.8 Ga = **48.8 Ga**. ⚠️ Red flag —
   older than the universe; fictional scenario (see Reconciliation).
2. t = 5,730 × ln(1/0.60)/ln 2. ln(1.6667) = 0.51083; 0.51083 /
   0.69315 = 0.73697; 5,730 × 0.73697 = 4,222.8 → **≈ 4.2×10³ yr
   (≈ 4,200 yr)**.
3. 3:1 → N/N₀ = 3/4 = 0.75. t = 1.248 × ln(1/0.75)/ln 2 =
   1.248 × 0.28768/0.69315 = 1.248 × 0.41504 = 0.51797 →
   **≈ 0.518 Ga = 518 Ma**.
4. Fair as a *summary of the two data points*, not as a certainty:
   "between 4.40 and 4.43 Ga" describes the measurements, but both
   could share a broken assumption (e.g., unrecognized initial
   daughter or ancient Pb loss). Accept: "reasonable, with the
   assumption caveat stated."
5. 65,000,000 / 5,730 = **11,344 half-lives**. Remaining fraction =
   (1/2)^11344 — indistinguishable from zero; no C-14 survives
   measurably past ~10 half-lives. The label's claim is impossible.

**Application**
a) Mineral X (U-238, N/N₀ = 0.82):
   t = 4.468 × ln(1/0.82)/ln 2 = 4.468 × ln(1.21951)/0.69315
   = 4.468 × 0.19844/0.69315 = 4.468 × 0.28629 = 1.2792 →
   **≈ 1.28 Ga**.
   Mineral Y (K-40, N/N₀ = 1/2): t = 1 × 1.248 Ga = **1.248 Ga ≈
   1.25 Ga**.
   Mineral Z (Rb-87, N/N₀ = 0.98):
   t = 48.8 × ln(1/0.98)/ln 2 = 48.8 × 0.020203/0.69315
   = 48.8 × 0.029147 = 1.4224 → **≈ 1.42 Ga**.
b) Differences from 1.25 Ga: X: 1.28 − 1.25 = 0.03 Ga = **30 Ma**;
   Y: 1.248 − 1.25 = −0.002 Ga ≈ **−2 Ma**; Z: 1.42 − 1.25 =
   0.17 Ga = **170 Ma**. X and Y agree closely with each other and
   the accepted age; Z runs high. The set is *approximately*
   concordant — good enough to trust the ~1.25 Ga age, with Z
   flagged for follow-up.
c) Trust **Y (K-40)** most: its half-life (1.248 Ga) matches the
   measured interval (~1.25 Ga) — the clock ticks at the right
   speed, with half its parent decayed (a large, robust signal).
   Z is fragile: only ~2% decayed, so small measurement errors swing
   the age. X agrees well and is a fine second witness.

**Exit check**
1. N = N₀·(1/2)^(t/t½) and t = t½·ln(N₀/N)/ln 2 (accept the
   parent-fraction conversion written alongside).
2. 0.125 = 1/8 → **3 half-lives**; K-40: 3 × 1.248 Ga = **3.744 Ga
   ≈ 3.74 Ga**.

## Lesson 03 — Dating assumptions and Earth's age

**Readiness check**
1. Measured 60 s vs. true 50 s — **too long** (the head start added
   10 s).
2. **Too short** — 5 s of the race went unmeasured.

**Guided practice**
1. **Too young** — the closed-system assumption failed (daughter
   lost); the parent fraction looks higher than it should.
2. Difference: 4.40 − 4.39 = 0.01 Ga = **10 Ma** < ±0.02 Ga
   (±20 Ma) uncertainties → **concordant**.
3. **Too old.** The naive calculation counts initial (non-radiogenic)
   daughter as if decay produced it, so N/N₀ is underestimated and
   the apparent number of half-lives is inflated.
4. Oldest first: **meteorites (4.56 Ga), Earth's accepted age
   (4.54 Ga), Jack Hills zircons (4.4 Ga), oldest oceanic crust
   (200 Ma = 0.2 Ga).** (a) 4.56 − 4.40 = 0.16 Ga = **160 Ma**.
   (b) 4.54 − 4.40 = 0.14 Ga = **140 Ma**.

**Independent practice**
1. Accept own-word statements + one failure mode each, e.g.:
   closed system — "nothing in or out"; fails by heating/groundwater
   leaching. Known initial daughter — "we know the starting
   amount"; fails by inherited daughter. Constant rate — "the
   half-life doesn't change"; fails only if the lab value is wrong
   (no known natural process changes it significantly).
2. Gap: 1.25 − 1.10 = 0.15 Ga = **150 Ma**; ranges 1.07–1.13 Ga and
   1.22–1.28 Ga do not overlap → **discordant**. Most likely: the
   K-Ar system lost argon during later heating (closed-system
   failure) → the 1.10 Ga age reads **too young**; the U-Pb age is
   the more trustworthy one here.
3. Accept a paragraph containing: Earth's oldest crust was recycled
   (subduction/tectonics destroyed it); meteorites are leftover
   planetary building blocks that were never melted into a planet;
   therefore meteorites preserve the original clock and set the
   ceiling on Earth's age.
4. Accept three sentences covering: many meteorites *and* ancient
   rocks dated by *multiple* isotope systems agree (~4.54–4.56 Ga);
   independent clocks with different failure modes agreeing is not
   one lab's opinion; the 4.4-Ga zircons are the oldest *Earth*
   material, consistent with (slightly younger than) the meteorite
   ceiling.
5. The classmate's *direction* is correct — lost daughter makes the
   computed age too young, so the true age is older than 2.5 Ga.
   The refinement: 2.5 Ga is now a **minimum age**, not an exact
   one; without more data you cannot say how much older.

**Application**
a) Fossil bracket: **110–120 Ma** for layer S (the organism's
   lifetime). The 105-Ma ash above S and the 90-Ma intrusion cutting
   S are both consistent (S is older than both) but do not tighten
   the bracket — the fossil window is the binding constraint.
b) Gap: 91 − 90 = **1 Ma**; ranges 87–93 Ma and 87–95 Ma overlap
   substantially → **concordant**.
c) A 130-Ma ash *above* a 110–120-Ma shale contradicts superposition
   (the upper layer cannot be older). Check first: the K-Ar system
   for excess argon or contamination (assumption 2/1), and whether
   the ash is truly in place above S (faulting could have shuffled
   the stack).

**Exit check**
1. Closed system; known initial daughter; constant known decay rate.
   Lost daughter → computed age **too young**.
2. Independent clocks have different chemistry and different failure
   modes — their agreement is much harder to fake than one precise
   number is to misread.

## Lesson 04 — Plate tectonics from crustal evidence

**Readiness check**
1. 2.5 cm/yr × 100,000,000 yr = 250,000,000 cm = 2,500,000 m =
   **2,500 km**.
2. Accept: each side is the reverse image of the other across the
   line, like a reflection in a mirror.

**Guided practice**
1. Half rate = 2.5/2 = 1.25 cm/yr = **12.5 km/Ma**;
   625 / 12.5 = **50 Ma**.
2. Half rate = 3 cm/yr = **30 km/Ma**; 900 / 30 = **30 Ma**.
3. One-way flow would stack all the stripes on one side (or make an
   asymmetric pattern). Mirror symmetry about the ridge means both
   flanks recorded the *same* reversal sequence moving *away* from
   the ridge — spreading in both directions.
4. Accept any honest two-sentence description of the USGS map (e.g.,
   "epicenters cluster in narrow belts along the western Americas
   and island arcs; interiors are nearly empty"). The point is
   *belt-like, not random*.
5. The **creation rate** (new crust at ridges) must roughly equal
   the **destruction rate** (subduction); otherwise the maximum
   seafloor age would drift up or down over time.

**Independent practice**
1. Half rate = 1.5 cm/yr = **15 km/Ma**; 200 Ma × 15 km/Ma =
   **3,000 km** from the axis.
2. West: half rate 2 cm/yr = 20 km/Ma → 800/20 = **40 Ma**. East:
   identical → **40 Ma**. The equality confirms symmetric
   spreading about the ridge.
3. Accept 3–4 sentences with: the claim (epicenters trace plate
   boundaries); the test (USGS latest-earthquakes map/list from the
   Resource Pack); the weakening result (epicenters scattered
   uniformly / no alignment with known boundaries).
4. Oceanic crust is dense and thin — it subducts and is recycled, so
   no ocean floor survives past ~200 Ma. Continental crust is
   thicker and buoyant — it resists subduction and can preserve
   4.4-Ga zircons.
5. (a) Half rate = 2.5 cm/yr = 25 km/Ma → 1,000/25 = **40 Ma**.
   (b) 300-Ma crust exceeds both the model prediction and the
   200-Ma global maximum → the rate was slower, spreading began
   earlier, the rate varied over time, or the crust came from an
   older basin (assumption to re-examine).

**Application**
a) Half rate 3 cm/yr = **30 km/Ma**; 600/30 = **20 Ma**.
b) Accept two sentences with the VMM logic: the N/R stripe sequence
   is identical on both flanks (mirror symmetry) → both flanks
   formed at the ridge and moved outward together, recording each
   reversal as a matching pair.
c) The signal is frozen into magnetic minerals *as the lava cools
   at the ridge* — it lives inside the rock, aligned at formation.
   Groundwater cannot reach into solid basalt and symmetrically
   re-magnetize minerals on both flanks.

**Exit check**
1. Accept one sentence each: seafloor ages — age increases away from
   ridges, max ~200 Ma; striping — mirror-symmetric polarity bands
   record spreading; belts — quakes/volcanoes outline the same
   boundaries independently.
2. Half rate = 1 cm/yr = **10 km/Ma**; 500/10 = **50 Ma**.

## Lesson 05 — Convection and quantitative models

**Readiness check**
1. Hot soup rises in the middle, cooler soup sinks at the edges —
   a rolling loop (convection cell).
2. Back into the mantle — matter is conserved, so "destroyed" crust
   must go somewhere (subduction).

**Guided practice**
1. At the **ridge** (upwelling: mantle → new lithosphere) and at
   the **trench** (subduction: lithosphere → mantle).
2. 3 cm/yr × 50,000,000 yr = 150,000,000 cm = 1,500,000 m =
   **1,500 km**.
3. Slow end: 2 × 5×10⁷ = 10⁸ cm = **1,000 km**; fast end: 10 ×
   5×10⁷ = 5×10⁸ cm = **5,000 km**. Range: **1,000–5,000 km**.
4. Two errors: **slab pull** does not push plates apart at ridges —
   **ridge push** does that; slab pull acts at **subduction zones**,
   where the sinking slab pulls its plate.

**Independent practice**
1. Accept one-sentence paraphrases: thermal convection — heat-driven
   rise/sink loop; lithosphere — rigid outer shell (plates);
   asthenosphere — weak creeping layer below; ridge push — downhill
   slide from the elevated ridge; slab pull — sinking slab dragging
   its plate.
2. Accept 4–5 sentences tracing: new crust at ridge → carried
   outward as plates move → subducted at trench → matter returns to
   the mantle → rises again; "conserved" must appear.
3. (a) 8 cm/yr × 25,000,000 yr = 200,000,000 cm = 2,000,000 m =
   **2,000 km**. (b) Slow: 2 × 2.5×10⁷ = 5×10⁷ cm = **500 km**;
   fast: 10 × 2.5×10⁷ = 2.5×10⁸ cm = **2,500 km**; range
   **500–2,500 km**. (c) Any two: constant rate, straight-line
   motion, no subduction losses, rate bracket covers this plate.
4. Accept three sentences with: the mantle is solid rock that
   **creeps** over millions of years; the honey analogy (flows if
   you wait, never like water); "liquid" misstates both the state
   and the speed.
5. 400 Ma = 0.4 Ga → 0.4/4.54 = 0.0881 = **8.8%**. The actual 4.4%
   means the seafloor samples only the most recent sliver of Earth
   history — the other ~95% survives only on continents (and in
   meteorites).

**Application**
a) Half rate = 4/2 = 2 cm/yr = **20 km/Ma**; 750/20 = **37.5 Ma ≈
   38 Ma**.
b) The **flank** moves at the half rate (2 cm/yr), not the full
   rate: 2 × 5×10⁷ = 10⁸ cm = **1,000 km**. Uncertainty: the 2–10
   cm/yr bracket is for *full* spreading rates, so the flank's half-rate
   bracket is 1–5 cm/yr → 1 × 5×10⁷ = 5×10⁷ cm = **500 km**;
   5 × 5×10⁷ = 2.5×10⁸ cm = **2,500 km**; range **500–2,500 km**.
c) Accept a report with: equation d = v×t, substituted numbers,
   **37.5 Ma** and **1,000 km (range 500–2,500 km)**, two stated
   assumptions (e.g., constant rate; straight-line motion), and one
   sentence on what breaks it (rate changes over time, subduction
   removing the flank, wrong rate choice for this ridge).

**Exit check**
1. Accept two sentences: convection carries new crust outward from
   ridges and returns old crust at trenches — which is exactly why
   seafloor is youngest at the axis and oldest at the margins.
2. **Units, stated assumptions, uncertainty** (any order).

Well matched: **yes** — about half a half-life elapsed, squarely in
