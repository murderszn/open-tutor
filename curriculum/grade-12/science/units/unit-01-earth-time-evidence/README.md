# Unit 01 — Earth-System Evidence, Geologic Time, and Quantitative Models

**Grade 12 · Science · Earth and Space Science pathway · Weeks 3–6 of the course year**
(draft, delivered for issue #55; not educator-reviewed)

## Overview

Earth is 4.54 billion years old, its crust is a patchwork of plates that
move, and the ocean floor is a tape recorder that tells us how fast. This
unit builds the evidence chain in four moves and ends with the learner's
first quantitative model of the year:

1. **Deep time and relative dating** — the 4.54-billion-year scale made
   graspable; stratigraphic principles (superposition, original
   horizontality, lateral continuity, cross-cutting relationships,
   inclusions, unconformities); index fossils as correlation tools.
2. **Radiometric dating** — parent–daughter decay, half-life, the age
   equation, worked calculations with real isotope systems, and what the
   numbers assume.
3. **Plate tectonics from crustal ages** — seafloor rock ages increasing
   away from mid-ocean ridges, symmetric magnetic striping as a tape
   recorder, earthquake and volcano belts at plate boundaries; how moving
   plates explain the ages of crustal rocks.
4. **Convection and the whole system** — thermal convection cycling
   matter in the mantle, its link to plate motion, and a first simple
   rate/flux model reported with units and uncertainty.

The essential question running through all four weeks is: **what does it
take to turn a rock into a clock, and a clock into a story about a
moving planet?**

The unit's investigation is *"The Evidence Case for Moving Plates"*
(see [project.md](project.md)): the learner builds a data table from
real public earthquake and volcano records, computes a spreading-rate
age estimate, and assembles an evidence portfolio with an explicit
uncertainty discussion.

## Prerequisites

From the Weeks 1–2 diagnostic (and the grade-11 physics pathway's
stated end-of-year objectives):

- Measuring with correct instruments and reporting uncertainty.
- Proportional reasoning, scientific notation, and evaluating or
  rearranging formulas; reading and interpreting graphs.
- From grade-12 math (in progress): algebraic modeling and spreadsheet
  calculations; interpreting slopes, rates, and exponential change at a
  conceptual level.
- From the grade-6 Earth/space strand (conceptual level): plate-tectonic
  evidence and boundary types, rock types and the rock cycle, natural
  hazards — this unit makes them quantitative and re-teaches none from
  scratch.

No calculus and no logarithms-as-a-skill are assumed; every use of the
natural logarithm in Lesson 02 is worked step by step, and integer
half-life counting is offered as a parallel path.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Build and interpret a geologic time scale using stratigraphic
   principles, index fossils, and radiometric-dating calculations.
   (HS-ESS1-6 strand)
2. Evaluate multiple lines of evidence (seafloor rock ages, magnetic
   striping, earthquake and volcano locations) to support plate
   tectonics, and explain how plate motions account for the ages of
   crustal rocks. (HS-ESS1-5)
3. Describe how thermal convection in Earth's interior cycles matter and
   energy, and connect mantle motion to plate motion. (HS-ESS2-3)
4. Build a simple quantitative model (a rate or flux estimate), report
   the result with correct units, and state the uncertainty and the
   assumptions behind it. (crosscutting: scale, proportion, and quantity)
5. Plan and carry out the unit's investigation, and communicate
   evidence-based conclusions with explicit uncertainty. (track
   objective 12)

## Vocabulary

deep time, geologic time scale, eon, relative age, absolute age,
superposition, original horizontality, lateral continuity,
cross-cutting relationships, principle of inclusions, unconformity,
index fossil, correlation, parent isotope, daughter isotope, half-life
(t½), closed system, radiometric dating, radiocarbon (C-14),
concordant ages, mid-ocean ridge, seafloor spreading, half-spreading
rate, magnetic striping, polarity reversal, Vine–Matthews–Morley,
subduction zone, oceanic crust, continental crust, thermal convection,
convection cell, ridge push, slab pull, lithosphere, asthenosphere,
quantitative model, uncertainty, flux, residence-time framing,
precision vs. accuracy.
