# Practice Sets — Unit 01

Five sets, one per lesson. Work in the notebook; the adult scores
same-day. Solutions are in `answer-key.md` (adult only). Datasets are
labeled **real reference data**, **rounded practice data**, or
**fictional practice data** — treat each label as part of the task.

---

## Practice Set A — Deep time and relative dating (Lesson 01)

1. A roadcut shows, bottom to top: basalt, sandstone, limestone. A
   fault cuts all three layers but is covered by an undisturbed layer
   of ash. Order every event oldest to youngest, citing one principle
   per step. (Fictional practice column.)
2. An index fossil lived from 90 to 85 Ma. It is found in a shale bed
   in two states 400 miles apart. What can you conclude about the two
   shale beds' ages? What can you *not* conclude? (Two sentences.)
3. On the one-meter Earth-history strip (1 m = 4.54 Ga): the first
   dinosaurs appeared about 240 Ma ago. How many centimeters from the
   "today" end is that? Show the multiplication. (Real reference
   values; your arithmetic is the practice.)
4. A student says, "Layer B is above layer A, so B is younger — unless
   the whole stack got flipped upside down." Is the student's caution
   reasonable? Which principle's fine print is being invoked?
5. Sketch a column with: two sedimentary layers, an intrusion cutting
   both, and an unconformity with one new layer above. Label which
   event is oldest and which is youngest, with the principle for each.
   (Fictional practice sketch — yours, not the lesson's.)
6. **Error analysis.** A classmate orders a column as "D, C, B, A,
   oldest to youngest" for layers stacked A (bottom) to D (top),
   saying "D was deposited last so it had the most time to get old."
   Identify the error and give the correct order with the principle.

## Practice Set B — Radiometric dating calculations (Lesson 02)

t½ values (real reference data): U-238 → Pb-206: 4.468 Ga; K-40 →
Ar-40: 1.248 Ga; Rb-87 → Sr-87: 48.8 Ga; C-14: 5,730 y. Assume a closed
system and zero initial daughter unless told otherwise.

1. A zircon has U-238:Pb-206 = 3:1. Find its age in Ga. (Convert the
   ratio to a parent fraction first.)
2. A volcanic ash has 1/32 of its original K-40 remaining. How many
   half-lives is that, and what is the age in Ga?
3. A wooden artifact has 0.80 of the C-14 of living wood. Use
   t = 5,730 × ln(1/0.80)/ln 2 to find its age in years. Show the
   substitution and each intermediate value.
4. A mineral's Rb-87 fraction is 0.90 (10% decayed). Estimate its age
   in Ga with the log form. Is this clock well matched to the
   interval? Explain in one sentence.
5. **Error analysis.** A learner is told a sample has "twice as much
   daughter as parent" and writes N/N₀ = 1/2. What is the correct
   N/N₀, and how many half-lives have actually passed?
6. Two labs date one rock: Lab 1 (U-Pb) reports 2.70 Ga ± 0.05 Ga;
   Lab 2 (K-Ar) reports 2.55 Ga ± 0.05 Ga. Are these concordant?
   Compute the gap in Ma and compare it with the uncertainties.
   (Fictional practice report.)

## Practice Set C — Dating assumptions and Earth's age (Lesson 03)

1. A sandstone's cement is dated by K-Ar at 300 Ma, but the sand
   grains are 1.2-Ga-old zircon. Which date tells you when the
   *sandstone formed*, and why is the other date misleading for that
   question? (Fictional practice scenario.)
2. A rock loses 20% of its daughter atoms to groundwater. Without
   computing: does the naive age come out too young or too old?
   Which assumption failed?
3. Three meteorites date (U-Pb) at 4.57, 4.55, and 4.56 Ga, each
   ±0.02 Ga. Are they mutually concordant? What does their agreement
   imply about Earth's maximum possible age? (Fictional practice
   numbers, realistic scale.)
4. Explain why a geologist would *not* use C-14 to date the Jack Hills
   zircons, using the half-life and the zircons' ~4.4-Ga age. (Two
   sentences; compute how many C-14 half-lives 4.4 Ga represents.)
5. A shale dated by two systems gives 500 Ma (System P) and 505 Ma
   (System Q), uncertainties ±10 Ma each. A learner says the rock is
   "exactly 502.5 Ma old." Critique the claim: what do the numbers
   actually support?
6. **Error analysis.** "The oldest Earth rock is 4.4 Ga, so Earth is
   4.4 Ga old." Name the missing evidence in this argument and state
   the accepted age with the two additional lines of evidence that
   support it.

## Practice Set D — Plate tectonics from crustal evidence (Lesson 04)

1. Full spreading rate 2 cm/yr; seafloor 750 km from the ridge axis.
   Estimate the age in Ma. (Half rate first; show conversions.)
2. A ridge spreads at a full rate of 8 cm/yr. How far (km) from the
   axis is 25-Ma-old seafloor?
3. Magnetic stripes are symmetric about a ridge. A student proposes:
   "The stripes formed when the whole seafloor slid sideways *after*
   the stripes were already there." Explain why the symmetry is still
   better explained by spreading during stripe formation. (Two to
   three sentences.)
4. Using the USGS Earthquake Hazards Program site (see Resource Pack):
   with the adult, open the latest-earthquakes map and describe in
   two sentences whether the epicenters look random or belt-like.
   (Real reference data; describe, do not copy coordinates.)
5. Explain the 200-Ma oceanic maximum as a *balance*: what two rates
   must be roughly equal for the maximum to stay near 200 Ma over
   geologic time?
6. **Error analysis.** A learner computes a seafloor age using the
   full spreading rate with a one-sided distance and gets half the
   correct age. Show the mistake with numbers (use 2.5 cm/yr full
   rate, 1,250 km), and state the rule that prevents it.

## Practice Set E — Convection and quantitative models (Lesson 05)

1. A plate moves at 4 cm/yr. How far does it travel in 75 Ma? Give
   the answer in km, then give the uncertainty range from the 2–10
   cm/yr bracket.
2. Model check: at 10 cm/yr, how long (in Ma) does it take a plate to
   travel 5,000 km? Show the division with units.
3. The oldest oceanic crust is 200 Ma; a planet's age is 4.54 Ga.
   (a) What percentage of the planet's history does its seafloor
   record? (b) If a second planet's oldest seafloor were 1.0 Ga and
   its age 4.0 Ga, what percentage would that be? (Part (b) is
   fictional practice.)
4. Write a three-line model report for this question: "How far does
   the Atlantic widen in one century at a full spreading rate of
   2.5 cm/yr?" Include units, one assumption, and the answer in
   meters. (This is the year's first century-scale rate estimate —
   keep the report format.)
5. Explain why subduction zones are *required* by the seafloor-age
   pattern, not just consistent with it. (Two to three sentences;
   use "200 Ma" and "new crust keeps forming.")
6. **Error analysis.** A model reports: "d = 5 cm/yr × 100,000,000 =
   500,000,000." List everything wrong with this report as a
   scientific communication (units, conversions, uncertainty,
   assumptions).
