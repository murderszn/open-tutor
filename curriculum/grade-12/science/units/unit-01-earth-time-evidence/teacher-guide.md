# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Earth-System Evidence, Geologic Time, and Quantitative Models

**Adult-facing.** Keep this file and `answer-key.md` separate from the
learner. Student pages never link here.

## How to use this guide

Each lesson below has: **readiness signals** (what "ready" looks like
after the readiness check), **reteaching moves** (what to do when the
lesson wobbles), **pacing notes**, and **differentiation**. Project
facilitation and the answer-key map are at the end. The misconception
notes also appear in each lesson's "Teacher note" — this guide adds
what to *do* about them.

## Lesson 01 — Deep time and relative dating

- **Readiness signals:** the learner converts 1 Ga → 1,000 mm →
  1.0 m without prompting, and orders the sandwich layers by "bottom
  first" with a reason. If the conversion needs the adult to drive,
  do the one-meter strip together in S1 and re-check in S2 — every
  later lesson's arithmetic assumes it.
- **Reteaching moves:** for cross-cutting confusion, use the
  "knife through a layer cake" demo (a real knife, a drawn cake):
  the cut is always younger than the cake. For unconformities, draw
  the gap as a torn-out book chapter — "the story skipped pages."
- **Pacing:** the image-labeling activity often runs long; cap it at
  15 minutes and finish in S2. The reading passage is for S4 — do not
  assign it as S1 homework unless the learner reads ahead willingly.
- **Struggling:** reduce the SVG column to three events (A, C,
  intrusion) and add the rest back one at a time. Accept card-sorting
  (alternate response mode) as the full exit check.
- **Advanced:** the Great Unconformity extension, plus: "Two index
  fossils in one layer give overlapping but different windows — how
  do you combine them?" (Answer: intersect the windows.)
- **Key pointer:** `answer-key.md`, Lesson 01.

## Lesson 02 — Radiometric dating calculations

- **Readiness signals:** the learner completes the coin-halving and
  states "fraction = (1/2)^n" unprompted. If logarithms are new,
  spend 10 minutes on "ln is just a button that undoes e^x" — the
  lesson never requires log theory, only button sequence.
- **Reteaching moves:** the ratio→fraction error (Guided Practice 4)
  is the lesson's hinge. When it appears, go concrete: "1 parent
  marble, 3 daughter marbles — what fraction of the *original*
  parents remain? 1 out of 4." Then re-derive N/N₀ =
  parent/(parent+daughter) from the marbles, not from the formula.
- **Pacing:** Route A (counting) must be fluent before Route B
  (logs). If S6 runs short, move the log-form examples to S7's
  calculation lab — but keep one log example in S6 so the form is
  not brand-new later.
- **Struggling:** stay on Route A for an extra session; assign only
  power-of-two fractions. Provide the ln button sequence on a card.
- **Advanced:** the derivation extension, plus: "A sample gives
  different ages by U-Pb and K-Ar. Which assumption do you test
  first, and what measurement would test it?" (Answer: closed
  system — check for heating/alteration; Ar loss is the usual
  suspect.)
- **Key pointer:** `answer-key.md`, Lesson 02. Note the
  reconciliation notes there: two practice items deliberately yield
  ages older than Earth — they are red-flag detectors, not errors.

## Lesson 03 — Dating assumptions and Earth's age

- **Readiness signals:** the learner correctly calls the stopwatch
  cases "too long" and "too short" *and* explains why. If they guess,
  replay the analogy with a real timer before the lesson.
- **Reteaching moves:** for "assumptions = opinions," run Example 1
  numerically: two clocks, different chemistry, agree within 10 Ma.
  Ask: "What are the odds both broke identically?" For
  precision-vs-accuracy, draw two dartboards (tight cluster off
  center; loose cluster on center).
- **Pacing:** the isochron paragraph is enrichment — 5 minutes max,
  conceptual only. Do not let it become a math lesson.
- **Struggling:** make the assumption→failure-direction chart (from
  Supports) the lesson's main artifact; quiz from it in S9.
- **Advanced:** the isochron extension (Resource Pack references),
  plus: "Why can't we date Earth's formation directly — what would
  a surviving 'day-zero' rock even look like?" (Answer: it would
  have to escape 4.54 Ga of recycling; none did.)
- **Key pointer:** `answer-key.md`, Lesson 03.

## Lesson 04 — Plate tectonics from crustal evidence

- **Readiness signals:** the readiness-check printer problem is
  solved with correct conversions (2,500 km). If not, re-do Lesson
  02's conversion drill — this lesson is 80% unit conversion.
- **Reteaching moves:** for the full-rate/half-rate error, draw one
  ridge with two arrows (one per flank) and label each arrow with
  the half rate; write "FULL = both arrows" above. Make the learner
  point to *whose* distance each problem gives before computing.
- **Pacing:** the USGS map look (Guided Practice 4 / Practice D4)
  needs the adult previewed and logged; budget 10 minutes. The
  magnetic-striping argument deserves a full whiteboard sketch —
  do not rush it.
- **Struggling:** give the km/Ma conversions precomputed in a table
  (1.25 cm/yr = 12.5 km/Ma; 5 cm/yr = 50 km/Ma) and focus the
  learner on *which* conversion each problem needs.
- **Advanced:** the Atlantic-width extension, plus: "The oldest
  Pacific crust is ~180 Ma, not 200. Propose two reasons the maxima
  differ between oceans." (Answers: different spreading histories;
  different subduction efficiency/age of basin.)
- **Key pointer:** `answer-key.md`, Lesson 04.

## Lesson 05 — Convection and quantitative models

- **Readiness signals:** the learner traces the convection loop and
  names ridge and trench as the matter-exchange points. If "liquid
  mantle" persists, do the honey demo (spoon in cold honey vs. warm
  honey — flow rate changes, but it is never water).
- **Reteaching moves:** for naked-number reports, use the template
  card from Supports and refuse to accept a report missing any of
  the five parts (equation, numbers, units, assumptions,
  uncertainty) — friendly, but absolute. The habit is the objective.
- **Pacing:** S16 teaches the model; S17 is the workshop where the
  spreadsheet (or hand) version gets built. Do not try to do both in
  S16.
- **Struggling:** pre-fill the equation with units (d [km] = v
  [km/Ma] × t [Ma]) and let the learner supply numbers; build the
  uncertainty range together the first time.
- **Advanced:** the convection-loop-time extension, plus: "Your
  model assumes constant rate. The Atlantic's rate changed over
  200 Ma. How would you improve the model with *two* rate
  intervals?" (Answer: piecewise — distance = v₁t₁ + v₂t₂.)
- **Key pointer:** `answer-key.md`, Lesson 05.

## Project facilitation — "The Evidence Case for Moving Plates"

- **Before S13:** preview both websites yourself (log date and what
  you checked: ads, reading level, map usability). Bookmark the
  USGS latest-earthquakes list view — it is easier to read than the
  map for data-table purposes. Decide the learner's boundary *with*
  them; the Peru–Chile Trench gives the richest quake+volcano combo.
- **During S13:** insist on units in every table cell before the
  learner leaves the session. "Magnitude 6.2" with no scale note is
  fine (moment magnitude is the site's default); depth must say km.
- **During S14:** the most common stall is choosing a rate without a
  reason — require the one-sentence justification before any
  arithmetic. Check the half-rate conversion explicitly.
- **During S17/S18:** the "what would change my mind" section is the
  intellectual core — if it is thin, ask: "What single discovery
  would break your case?" and do not accept "nothing."
- **If data access fails:** switch to the simulation alternative in
  `project.md` without penalty; grade the substitute table against
  the same rubric rows (observation quality, units, reasoning).
- **Scoring:** score with the learner present. For borderline
  portfolios, the reflection paragraph is the tiebreaker — genuine
  uncertainty expressed clearly outranks confident vagueness.

## Assessment notes

- The Week-06 quiz is formative: score it in S19, reteach the two
  weakest objectives the same session, and log the reteach plan in
  the notebook. Do not let a low quiz score stand without a
  same-week response.
- The unit assessment (S20) allows the isotope table and equation
  forms — it tests reasoning and setup, not memorization of t½
  values. Mental-math shortcuts are fine; require the setup line
  even when the learner "just sees" the answer.
- Neither instrument is standardized or validated; say so if the
  learner asks what the scores "mean."

## Answer-key map

`answer-key.md` solves, in order: Lesson 01 → Lesson 05 (readiness,
guided, independent, application, exit each), Practice Sets A–E, the
Week-06 quiz, the unit assessment, and project scoring guidance —
then **Reconciliation notes** describing every item checked twice
and every deliberate red-flag number. When a learner's answer
differs from the key, check the reconciliation notes first: several
tasks accept a range or an alternative interpretation by design.
