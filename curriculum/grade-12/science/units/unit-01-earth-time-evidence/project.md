# Project — The Evidence Case for Moving Plates

**Unit 01 investigation · Sessions S13, S14, S17 (studios) + S18 (gallery)**
**Track objective 12 · Unit objective 5**

## The task

You are the expert witness. A skeptic says: *"The continents have
always been where they are; the seafloor's ages mean nothing."* Your
job is to build an **evidence portfolio** — a short, organized case
file — that argues the opposite: **plate motions explain the ages of
crustal rocks.** The portfolio has three parts:

- **Part A — Boundary data table.** Pick one plate boundary. Record
  real recent earthquakes (USGS Earthquake Hazards Program) and real
  volcanoes (Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program) near it in a data
  table with units, and show the belt pattern.
- **Part B — Spreading-rate age estimate.** From a seafloor-age
  transect, compute how old the seafloor is at a chosen distance from
  the ridge (distance = rate × time), and compare the result with the
  200-Ma oceanic-crust maximum.
- **Part C — The argument.** Assemble Parts A and B plus magnetic
  striping into a written case: claim, evidence, reasoning — and an
  honest uncertainty discussion.

## Materials

Notebook or printed portfolio sheets, pencil, ruler, calculator,
internet access with the adult (for the USGS and Smithsonian sites),
and the Lesson 04 transect diagram. If internet access fails, use the
simulation alternative below — the project is never skipped silently.

## Part A — Boundary data table (Session S13)

1. With the adult, choose **one** plate boundary to investigate. Good
   choices: the Mid-Atlantic Ridge (divergent), the Peru–Chile Trench
   (convergent/subduction), or the San Andreas Fault (transform).
2. Open the USGS Earthquake Hazards Program
   (<https://earthquake.usgs.gov/>) with the adult and use the latest
   earthquakes map/list. Record **at least 6 recent earthquakes**
   near your boundary: date, latitude/longitude (as given), magnitude,
   and depth in km. (Real reference data — record what the site
   shows on the day you visit; note the access date.)
3. Open the Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program
   (<https://volcano.si.edu/>) with the adult. Record **at least 4
   volcanoes** near your boundary: name, country, and most recent
   known eruption (as listed). (Real reference data; note the access
   date.)
4. Build one combined data table with columns: *type
   (quake/volcano), identifier, location, key measurement with units,
   source, access date.* Every number carries its units.
5. Write a 3–4 sentence finding: do your points form a belt along the
   boundary, or do they scatter? Quote two rows of your table as
   evidence.

**Simulation/data alternative:** if the websites are unreachable, run
the PhET "Plate Tectonics" simulation
(<https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/plate-tectonics>) with the
adult and record, for each of the three boundary types in the sim,
what happens to the crust (builds up / sinks / slides). Label the
table "simulation observations (substitute data)" and say in one
sentence what real data would have shown instead.

## Part B — Spreading-rate age estimate (Session S14)

1. Choose a spreading rate from the real reference range 2–10 cm/yr
   and state *why* you chose it (e.g., "2.5 cm/yr, the Atlantic full
   rate from Lesson 04" or "10 cm/yr, the East Pacific Rise end").
2. Choose a distance from the ridge axis (in km) and compute the
   seafloor age there: **age = distance ÷ half-spreading rate**,
   showing the half-rate conversion and every unit step (Lesson 04,
   Example 1 is the template).
3. Compare your computed age with the **200-Ma oceanic-crust maximum**
   (real reference value): is your seafloor younger, and by how much?
   Compute the difference in Ma.
4. Write a 2–3 sentence interpretation: what does your estimate say
   about how the ridge builds the age pattern — and what would a
   computed age *older* than 200 Ma have implied?

## Part C — The argument (Session S17)

Assemble a 1–2 page written case with exactly these sections:

1. **Claim** (one sentence): plate motions explain the ages of
   crustal rocks.
2. **Evidence** (three bullets): your Part A belt finding, your Part B
   age estimate, and magnetic striping symmetry (Lesson 04).
3. **Reasoning** (one paragraph): connect each bullet to the claim —
   why does a belt of quakes, a computed seafloor age, and mirrored
   stripes each point to moving plates rather than fixed continents?
4. **Uncertainty** (one paragraph): name at least two sources of
   uncertainty in your case (e.g., rate variation 2–10 cm/yr, the
   access-date snapshot of quake data, assuming constant spreading)
   and say, for each, whether it weakens your conclusion or just
   widens the numbers.
5. **What would change my mind** (two sentences): name one observation
   that would seriously undermine the plate-tectonics explanation
   (e.g., finding 1-Ga-old oceanic crust, or stripes that are *not*
   mirror-symmetric).

## Deliverables (due Session S18)

1. The Part A data table (with units, sources, access dates).
2. The Part B calculation sheet (every step shown).
3. The Part C written case (claim, evidence, reasoning, uncertainty,
   what-would-change-my-mind).
4. A one-paragraph reflection: which part of the case convinced *you*
   most, and what is still uncertain in your own mind?

## Safety and adult role

- The adult previews both websites before the learner opens them
  (advertising, reading level, age suitability) and stays nearby
  during all browsing. Record what was previewed and when.
- No field work is required. If the learner proposes visiting a real
  outcrop or shoreline to extend the project, the adult applies the
  track safety rules (scope-and-sequence §6) and keeps veto power.
- Simulation alternative (above) is the approved substitute when data
  access fails — the data-analysis demand stays the same.

## Rubric — 40 points

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Proficient | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Data table** (8 pts: 2 per row-block) | ≥6 quakes + ≥4 volcanoes; every number has units; sources + access dates recorded | Counts met; most units present; sources named | Counts short by 1–2; units missing in places; sources vague | Few rows; numbers without units or sources |
| **B. Age estimate** (8 pts) | Correct half-rate use; every conversion shown; comparison with 200-Ma max computed and interpreted | Correct method; minor arithmetic slip, caught or not | Full rate used with one-sided distance, or conversions missing | No coherent calculation |
| **C. Evidence bullets** (8 pts) | Three distinct lines (belt, age, striping), each tied to a concrete number or observation | Three lines, one vague | Two lines, or evidence listed without numbers | One line or claim restated as evidence |
| **D. Reasoning** (8 pts) | Each bullet explicitly linked to the claim; fixed-continent alternative addressed | Links present; alternative unaddressed | Reasoning repeats evidence without linking | No reasoning paragraph |
| **E. Uncertainty + reflection** (8 pts) | ≥2 uncertainty sources named with effect judged; reflection names a genuine personal uncertainty | Two sources named; reflection present but generic | One source; reflection missing or perfunctory | Neither present |

Scoring: sum the five criteria (each 0–4; 0 = missing). 36–40 =
exceeds; 30–35 = meets; 24–29 = approaching (revise Part C with the
adult); below 24 = redo with reteaching (see teacher guide). The adult
scores with the learner present so the feedback is a conversation, not
a verdict.
