# Unit Assessment — Unit 01: Earth-System Evidence, Geologic Time, and Quantitative Models

**Session S20 · ~50 minutes · 75 points · the isotope table and
equation forms are provided below**

t½ values (real reference data): U-238 → Pb-206: 4.468 Ga; K-40 →
Ar-40: 1.248 Ga; Rb-87 → Sr-87: 48.8 Ga; C-14: 5,730 y.
Equations: N = N₀·(1/2)^(t/t½); t = t½·ln(N₀/N)/ln 2;
N/N₀ = parent/(parent + daughter). Spreading: age = distance ÷
half-spreading rate.

## Part I — Dating and rates (Objectives 1, 4)

**A1. (8 pts)** An outcrop shows, bottom to top: granite basement,
arkose sandstone, shale with index fossils, siltstone. A fault
displaces the granite, arkose, and shale but not the siltstone. Order
all events oldest to youngest, citing one principle per step, and
state what the unfaulted siltstone tells you about the fault's
relative age.

**A2. (8 pts)** A parchment fragment retains 0.70 of the C-14 of
living material. Compute its age in years with the log form, showing
the substitution, the value of ln(1/0.70), the division by ln 2 =
0.6931, and the final multiplication. Round to two significant
figures and state whether C-14 is well matched to this interval.

**A3. (8 pts)** A ridge spreads at a full rate of 6 cm/yr.
(a) How far from the ridge axis, in km, is 40-Ma-old seafloor?
(b) If drilling at 1,200 km from the axis recovered 40-Ma-old crust,
what would that imply about the assumed rate? Show all work.

## Part II — Evidence evaluation (Objectives 2, 3)

**B1. (10 pts)** Evaluate the case for plate tectonics from crustal
ages. In a structured paragraph each, explain what (a) seafloor rock
ages, (b) magnetic striping, and (c) earthquake/volcano belts
contribute — and finish with two sentences on how plate motion
explains why oceanic crust nowhere exceeds ~200 Ma while continents
hold 4-Ga rocks.

**B2. (6 pts)** Describe the thermal convection loop in the mantle and
explain, in two to three sentences, how it connects to the
seafloor-age pattern (young at ridges, older outward, destroyed at
trenches).

**B3. (8 pts — error analysis)** A classmate argues: "Magnetic stripes
prove the whole seafloor slid sideways *after* the stripes formed,
because the stripes are just lying there." Identify the flaw in this
reasoning and give the two observations from this unit that the
sideways-slide story cannot explain but seafloor spreading can.

## Part III — Modeling and communication (Objectives 4, 5)

**C1. (10 pts)** Build a quantitative model: how far does a plate
moving at 6 cm/yr travel in 80 Ma? Write it as a short report with:
the equation, substituted numbers, the result with correct units, two
stated assumptions, and an uncertainty range computed from the real
2–10 cm/yr spreading-rate bracket.

**C2. (7 pts)** A rock dates at 1.20 Ga ± 0.04 Ga by Rb-Sr and
1.26 Ga ± 0.05 Ga by K-Ar. (a) Compute the gap between the central
values in Ma. (b) Are the ages concordant? Justify numerically.
(c) In one sentence, say what concordance (or its absence) tells you
about the dating assumptions.

**C3. (10 pts)** "How do we know Earth is about 4.54 billion years
old?" Write a half-page answer citing three distinct lines of
evidence from this unit (zircons, meteorites, concordant clocks),
explaining what each contributes and why no single measurement would
suffice.
