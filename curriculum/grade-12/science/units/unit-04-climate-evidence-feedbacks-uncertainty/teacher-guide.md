# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Climate Evidence, Feedbacks, and Uncertainty

**Adult-facing. Keep from the learner until the adult decides an item
is ready for review.** This guide covers pacing, differentiation,
reteaching, and project scoring. Worked solutions are in
`answer-key.md`.

## Pacing notes

- The unit is 20 sessions (see the README pacing table). The most
  common failure is letting Lessons 01–03 run long and squeezing
  the investigation: protect Sessions S15 (planning) and S17
  (data). If time is short, cut the Lesson 05 extension, never
  the investigation planning session.
- Lessons 01 and 03 carry the most calculation. Do the first
  worked example together, the second with the learner leading,
  and only then assign independent practice — the unit's numbers
  (340 / 102 / 238; 2.12 GtC per ppm; 46%) recur, so early
  accuracy pays off all month.
- The image observation (Session S7) works best as a paired
  discussion: one learner orders the surfaces by albedo, the
  partner walks the feedback loop from that ordering.

## Differentiation

- **Reading access:** Lessons 02 and 04 may be read aloud in
  pairs; the loop-tracing routine (say each arrow, then write
  it) is designed to be spoken.
- **Alternate response modes:** every written application in
  the lessons has a sketch-or-speak alternative noted in the
  lesson. Accept labeled diagrams for the energy budget, the
  feedback loops, and the carbon model on the assessment.
- **Extensions:** each lesson names one; the Lesson 03 Global
  Carbon Budget update and the Lesson 05 projection-graphic
  comparison are the strongest for learners who finish early.
- **Support for struggling learners:** if the Stefan–Boltzmann
  fourth root blocks Lesson 01, use the worked example with
  different absorbed fluxes until 238 → 255 K is automatic;
  the rest of the unit needs only that anchor plus the
  ppm-to-GtC conversion. If the gain-model algebra blocks
  Lesson 02, use f = 0.5 only (doubling) until the idea lands,
  then vary f.

## Reteaching by evidence

- **390 vs. 238 confused:** return to Lesson 01, section 2, and
  rebuild the two-level picture: surface budget vs.
  top-of-atmosphere budget. Learners who treat them as one
  number will fail every greenhouse question.
- **Feedback sign wrong:** insist the loop returns to its
  starting quantity. A chain that ends somewhere else is not a
  feedback at all — redraw it.
- **Flux vs. stock confused:** return to the bathtub analogy
  (Lesson 03). Learners who compare 11 GtC/yr to 120 GtC/yr
  without asking about balance will repeat the "only 4%"
  error on the assessment.
- **Scenario vs. prediction confused:** return to Lesson 05's
  table. Any projection quoted without its scenario is sent
  back for its missing label.
- **One cold winter cited:** return to Lesson 04's
  weather-vs-climate and local-vs-global distinctions; ask for
  the 30-year global mean before discussing further.

## Assessment rubric (Part C, question 12; 6 points)

- Checkable claims with sources or stated values: 2 points
  (1 if claims are present but unsourced or unquantified).
- Scenario stated and used correctly (not averaged across
  scenarios): 1 point.
- Impact tied to the stated warming level: 1 point.
- Uncertainty named by kind (scenario / model / internal
  variability): 1 point.
- Statement of what one model run cannot decide: 1 point.

## Project scoring

See `project.md` for the 20-point rubric. Score the prediction
only on whether it was falsifiable, quantitative, and stated
before data — never on whether it turned out right. A
prediction that failed cleanly and was revised honestly scores
full marks on that criterion.

## Safety and sensitivity notes

- The albedo experiment uses sun, trays, and thermometers only.
  The adult checks the forecast, carries water and sun
  protection, and stops work for lightning, high winds,
  flooding, or extreme heat.
- Climate is a topic learners may have heard argued about at
  home. Keep the unit's rule throughout: claims are tested by
  prediction against observation, whichever side they come
  from. The applied task in Lesson 04 is deliberately
  side-neutral in its scoring — structure, not conclusion,
  earns the marks.
