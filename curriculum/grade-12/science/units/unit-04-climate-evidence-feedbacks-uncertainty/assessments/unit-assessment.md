# Unit Assessment — Unit 04: Climate Evidence, Feedbacks, and Uncertainty

## Part A — Short answers (1 point each unless noted)

1. Show the global-mean energy budget in three numbers (incoming,
   reflected, absorbed) starting from a solar constant of
   1,361 W/m² and albedo 0.30.
2. Compute the no-greenhouse effective temperature for an
   absorbed flux of 238 W/m², in K and °C. (σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸.)
3. Earth's surface emits about 390 W/m² while about 238 W/m²
   leaves at the top of the atmosphere. Explain the difference.
4. Classify each feedback as reinforcing or balancing, with one
   reason each: (a) ice–albedo, (b) Planck (emission rises as
   T⁴), (c) water vapor.
5. A forcing alone gives ΔT₀ = 1.4 °C and f = 0.5. Compute the
   total warming in the gain model and state one limit of the
   model.
6. The atmosphere holds about 900 GtC. Photosynthesis moves
   120 GtC/yr. Compute the turnover time against photosynthesis
   and explain why it is not the adjustment time for a CO₂ pulse.
7. Emissions are 11 GtC/yr; the observed rise is 2.4 ppm/yr.
   Compute the airborne amount (GtC/yr) and the airborne
   fraction.
8. Name the evidence line that measures each of the following:
   (a) ancient air composition, (b) where trapped heat is stored,
   (c) the integrated result of ocean warming plus ice loss.
9. State the fingerprint that argues against a brighter Sun as
   the main cause of recent warming, and the observation behind
   it.
10. A projection gives "3.3–5.7 °C by 2081–2100." Name the
    scenario family it belongs to and explain why the range is
    not a single prediction.

## Part B — Data task (4 points)

11. A fictional practice dataset gives September Arctic sea-ice
    extent (million km²) for five Septembers: 7.0, 6.6, 6.8,
    6.2, 6.0.
    (a) Compute the mean of the first two and of the last two
    values. (b) State the change between those means.
    (c) Explain why five Septembers cannot establish a trend on
    their own, and what comparison would be needed.
    (d) Name one mechanism (from this unit) that would make a
    declining trend reinforce itself.

## Part C — Extended reasoning (6 points)

12. Write an HS-ESS3-5 forecast in about 200 words: the observed
    rate of change (with its source), the scenario you assume,
    the warming level and timescale, one associated impact tied
    to that warming level, and what is uncertain — naming which
    kind of uncertainty it is. The adult scores with the
    assessment rubric in `teacher-guide.md`: checkable claims
    (2 points), scenario stated and used correctly (1 point),
    impact tied to the warming level (1 point), uncertainty
    named by kind (1 point), and a statement of what one model
    run cannot decide (1 point).
