Student-facing. Work each set in your notebook and show every

## Practice Set A — Energy budget (Lesson 01)

1. The solar constant is 1,361 W/m². Show why the global-mean
   incoming flux is about 340 W/m².
2. With planetary albedo 0.30 and incoming 340 W/m², find the
   reflected and absorbed fluxes.
3. A planet absorbs 260 W/m² with no greenhouse effect. Estimate
   its effective radiating temperature. (Use E = σT⁴.)
4. Earth's surface emits about 390 W/m² but only about 238 W/m²
   leaves at the top of the atmosphere. Explain the difference
   in two sentences.
5. If the solar constant fell 2%, to about 1,334 W/m², what would
   the new global-mean incoming flux be? (Divide by 4.)
6. A surface with albedo 0.15 receives 800 W/m². How much is
   absorbed? How much is reflected?
7. **Explain your reasoning:** A learner says the greenhouse
   effect "traps heat like a blanket that never lets it out."
   Correct the blanket: what leaves, what returns, and what
   balances in the end?
8. **Error analysis:** A worksheet writes "absorbed = 340 × 0.30
   = 102 W/m²." Find the error and give the correct absorbed
   value.

## Practice Set B — Feedbacks (Lesson 02)

1. Trace the ice–albedo loop in four arrows. Label it
   reinforcing or balancing.
2. Trace one balancing loop that starts with warming and ends
   with less warming. (The Planck response is acceptable.)
3. A forcing alone gives ΔT₀ = 1.5 °C and f = 0.4. Compute the
   total in the gain model.
4. A forcing alone gives ΔT₀ = 2.0 °C and f = 0.6. Compute the
   total. What happens to the total as f approaches 1?
5. Warm air holds about 7% more water vapor per 1 °C. After
   2 °C of warming, about how much more water vapor can the air
   hold? (Use compounding, not simple addition, when combining
   the two years' increases.)
6. **Explain your reasoning:** Why is water vapor a feedback
   rather than a forcing in the long-term warming story?
7. A diagram shows: warming → more clouds → more sunlight
   reflected → less warming. State one condition under which
   this loop's sign flips, and why.
8. **Error analysis:** "Positive feedback means good feedback."
   Correct the vocabulary in two sentences.

## Practice Set C — Carbon cycle (Lesson 03)

1. Convert 280 ppm to GtC and 426 ppm to GtC. What is the
   difference?
2. Human emissions are 11 GtC/yr and the airborne fraction is
   46%. How many GtC/yr stay airborne? What ppm/yr rise does
   that imply?
3. A reservoir of 2,400 GtC loses 80 GtC/yr. Find its residence
   time.
4. Photosynthesis moves 120 GtC/yr. How long, at that rate,
   would it take to move an amount equal to the whole
   atmosphere (~900 GtC)? What does that number mean, and what
   does it not mean?
5. **Explain your reasoning:** A country emits 0.5 GtC/yr and
   plants trees absorbing 0.5 GtC/yr. Has it reached net zero
   in any durable sense? What three questions must be answered
   first?
6. If the airborne fraction rose from 46% to 60% while emissions
   stayed at 11 GtC/yr, what would the new ppm/yr rise be?
7. The ocean exchanges about 90 GtC/yr each way and holds about
   38,000 GtC. Compute its rough residence time against that
   exchange and state one reason the single number is rough.
8. **Error analysis:** "The ocean absorbs 90 GtC a year, so
   human emissions of 11 GtC are no problem." Correct it using
   net vs. gross flux.

## Practice Set D — Evidence (Lesson 04)

1. Name four independent evidence lines and, for each, one
   thing it measures.
2. The stratosphere cools while the troposphere warms. Which
   proposed cause does this fingerprint argue against, and why?
3. A practice tide-gauge series (fictional) rises 4 mm/yr for
   20 years. How much total rise is that? What single extra
   fact would you need before calling it acceleration?
4. **Explain your reasoning:** Why do independent research
   groups (NASA, NOAA, and others) reaching the same warming
   strengthen the conclusion more than one group publishing
   twice?
5. Ice cores show CO₂ between about 180 and 300 ppm over
   800,000 years. Today's value is about 426 ppm. State in two
   sentences what that comparison does and does not prove.
6. A town records its coldest winter in 30 years. A resident
   says this disproves global warming. Give the correction,
   using weather vs. climate and local vs. global.
7. Ocean heat content rises while surface temperature pauses
   for a few years (a practice scenario). Is the energy budget
   broken? Explain where the heat is.
8. **Error analysis:** A graph plots one station's temperature
   for 5 years, trending down, titled "Global cooling." Name
   two errors (one station ≠ globe; five years ≠ climate trend).

## Practice Set E — Models and uncertainty (Lesson 05)

1. A model grid cell is 50 km × 50 km. Can it resolve a 10 km
   wide thunderstorm? Explain what it does instead.
2. Under SSP2-4.5, warming by 2081–2100 is 2.1–3.5 °C relative
   to 1850–1900. Under SSP1-1.9 it is 1.0–1.8 °C. What mostly
   explains the gap between the two ranges — scenario, model,
   or internal variability?
3. Within SSP2-4.5 alone, models range from 2.1 to 3.5 °C.
   What kind of uncertainty does *that* spread mostly reflect?
4. A 20-member ensemble under one scenario gives yearly values
   scattered ±0.3 °C around a smooth trend. Which uncertainty
   does the scatter mostly show?
5. **Explain your reasoning:** Why does internal variability
   dominate short forecasts but scenario uncertainty dominate
   century forecasts?
6. A projection says "2.8 °C by 2100" with no scenario named.
   List the two questions you must ask before using it.
7. Hansen's 1988 Scenario B tracked observed warming closely
   while Scenario A overshot. What does that comparison teach
   about separating scenario error from physics error?
8. **Error analysis:** "The ensemble mean is the most likely
   single future." Correct it: what the ensemble mean is, and
   why no single run should be quoted as *the* prediction.
