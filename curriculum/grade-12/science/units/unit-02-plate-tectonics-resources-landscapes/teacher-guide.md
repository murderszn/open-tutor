# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Plate Tectonics, Resources, and Landscape Processes

**Adult-facing.** Keep this file and `answer-key.md` separate from
the learner. Student pages never link here.

## How to use this guide

Each lesson below has: **readiness signals** (what "ready" looks
like after the readiness check), **reteaching moves** (what to do
when the lesson wobbles), **pacing notes**, and **differentiation**.
Project facilitation, the quiz/assessment notes, and the answer-key
map are at the end. The misconception notes also appear in each
lesson's "Teacher note" — this guide adds what to *do* about them.

## Lesson 01 — Plate boundaries and landforms (S1)

- **Readiness signals:** the learner names oceanic vs. continental
  crust with the density order, and answers "it subducts / is
  recycled into the mantle" for the oldest ocean floor. If the
  density order is insecure, run the PhET "Plate Tectonics" density
  meter first (S3 is the scheduled session, but readiness gates
  S1).
- **Reteaching moves:** for "all convergent boundaries make
  volcanoes," draw the three convergent cases side by side and ask
  "where is the sinking oceanic slab?" for each — the volcano
  column stays empty for continent–continent. For full-vs-half-rate
  confusion, always write "full rate = both sides together" above
  the arithmetic.
- **Pacing:** the matching task (Independent practice 1) often runs
  long; cap at 15 minutes and finish the fence problem in S2.
- **Struggling:** reduce the matching bank to three boundary types
  (divergent, ocean–continent convergent, transform) and add the
  rest back one at a time. Accept card-sorting as the full exit
  check.
- **Advanced:** the real-ridge-rate extension, plus: "The Andes
  convergence rate is about 6–8 cm/yr (real reference range) — at
  7 cm/yr, how far would the plates converge in 1 million years?"
  (Answer: 70 km.)
- **Key pointer:** `answer-key.md`, Lesson 01.

## Lesson 02 — Weathering, erosion, deposition (S6)

- **Readiness signals:** the learner names one wearing-down process
  from real life and predicts the sand (not the pebble) travels
  farther, with a reason about weight/size. If the pebble answer
  appears, do the transport section with two real objects in a bowl
  of water before the stream-table example.
- **Reteaching moves:** for weathering/erosion merging, run the
  movement test on every item aloud ("did it move?") until it is
  reflex. For "rocks melt," correct the word on the spot, every
  time — weathering breaks rock down; melting is a separate,
  high-temperature process.
- **Pacing:** the image observation activity is the S6 centerpiece;
  give it 15 minutes. The sketch (Guided practice 3) can finish in
  S7 before the stream model.
- **Struggling:** accept the spoken-alternative for the sketch;
  the four labels must still be named aloud with reasons.
- **Advanced:** "Two points show a direction, not a formula" — ask
  the learner to design the third stream-table run that would test
  whether the slope effect is linear, and to predict its result
  before running it (in the model or on paper).
- **Key pointer:** `answer-key.md`, Lesson 02.

## Lesson 03 — Topographic maps and relief (S11)

- **Readiness signals:** the learner picks the 200-ft horizontal
  hill as steeper and converts 0.25 miles → 1,320 ft. If the
  conversion needs the adult to drive, drill miles↔feet for 10
  minutes — every gradient in this lesson and Lesson 04's tonnage
  work assumes it.
- **Reteaching moves:** for feet÷miles without converting, make the
  learner write the unit bridge ("0.5 mi = 2,640 ft") as a separate
  line before any division, every time. For V's pointing the wrong
  way, finger-trace: "the V reaches upstream to find ground at its
  own elevation."
- **Pacing:** the profile sketch (Independent practice 5) is the
  long pole; start it in S11 and finish in S12's warm-up if needed.
- **Struggling:** give the profile with the west-side stream
  removed first; add it back as a second pass.
- **Advanced:** "A 7.5-minute quadrangle has a 1:24,000 scale and a
  10-ft contour interval — how many feet does one inch on the map
  represent?" (Answer: 2,000 ft per inch.)
- **Key pointer:** `answer-key.md`, Lesson 03.

## Lesson 04 — Resources from Earth processes (S12)

- **Readiness signals:** the learner gives one sentence per margin
  setting (convergent: sinking + melting; divergent: pulling apart
  + new crust; hot spot: rising plume, surface heat). If hot spots
  are blank, revisit Unit 01's convection lesson briefly.
- **Reteaching moves:** for "resources are everywhere," ask "then
  why does the copper map match the plate-boundary map?" and make
  the learner name the concentrating process for two resources.
  For porosity/permeability confusion: "a sponge vs. a sealed
  bottle — which holds water, which gives it up?"
- **Pacing:** the site-ranking task (S12 per the unit pacing) needs
  20 minutes; do not let the four-question frame become a fifth
  ranking criterion — it is the frame, not a score.
- **Struggling:** pre-fill the cost-benefit table's first row as a
  model; the learner fills the remaining three.
- **Advanced:** recompute Independent practice 3 with a cutoff
  grade: "If only ore above 0.6% copper is economic, how does that
  change which deposit is worth mining?" (Answer: it changes the
  *economic* ranking, not the total-copper arithmetic — a deliberate
  distinction.)
- **Neutrality check:** if the learner's table argues a position
  instead of describing tradeoffs, send it back for neutral
  language before scoring. The frame describes; the criteria judge.
- **Key pointer:** `answer-key.md`, Lesson 04.

## Lesson 05 — Hazards and human settlement (S13)

- **Readiness signals:** the learner names four hazards matched to
  processes and answers the volcano-rent question honestly with one
  decision-changing factor. There is no wrong answer to question 2
  — it is a hook, not a test.
- **Reteaching moves:** for "warning prevents the disaster," ask
  "after the warning sounds, does the volcano unerupt?" For
  "people near hazards are foolish," return to the four attractions
  and ask which one the learner's own family would find hardest to
  give up.
- **Pacing:** Example 1's model answer is long by design — it is a
  writing model, not a dictation. Have the learner outline it
  (attractions / tradeoffs / evidence) rather than copy it.
- **Struggling:** accept the dictated/spoken Application; the two
  evidence citations must still be named aloud.
- **Advanced:** the real-community extension, with the adult
  checking that the write-up stays neutral and factual and cites
  its source.
- **Sensitivity:** the Mount St. Helens reference is background
  evidence for hazard reach. Do not dramatize it; if the learner
  has personal experience with a disaster, let them set the tone
  and keep the lesson on mechanisms and decisions.
- **Key pointer:** `answer-key.md`, Lesson 05.

## Project — Reading My Landscape (S15–S19)

- **Facilitation:** approve the written plan before any field work;
  veto any site with traffic, unstable banks, or deep/fast water.
  Check the data table for units *before* the field session, not
  after.
- **Common failure:** single measurements with no repeats. Require
  the second measurement on site, in the moment — "we'll estimate
  the repeat later" is how it gets skipped.
- **Scoring:** use the rubric in `project.md`. The model
  comparison (8 pts) is the hinge: full marks require the learner
  to state where data *disagree* with the model, not just where
  they agree.
- **Alternatives:** the stream-table and simulation alternatives
  carry the same rubric; label every value "model data" or
  "simulation observation" in the report.

## Quiz (S18) and unit assessment (S20)

- **Quiz:** 40 points, ~40 minutes, closed notebook. Q2 and Q4 are
  the arithmetic gates — if both are missed, reteach unit
  conversion before the assessment. Q6 is open-response: score the
  evidence citations and the stated limits, not the conclusion.
- **Assessment:** 50 points, ~50 minutes, closed notebook. Q1's
  three panels are described in words so the assessment stands
  alone; the SVG is the same content for learners who want the
  visual. Q6 and Q7 are open-response: score criteria-stating,
  consistent application, tradeoffs named, and a stated condition
  that would change the recommendation — not the ranking or the
  recommendation itself.
- **Readiness:** the learner is ready for the next unit when the
  quiz is ≥ 32/40 and the assessment ≥ 40/50, or when every missed
  item has been corrected in the notebook with reasoning. Reteach
  targets: boundary mechanisms (Q1), unit conversion (Q2, Q4, Q5),
  and evidence citation (Q6, Q7).
