# Project — Reading My Landscape

**Sessions S15–S19 (planning, two field/data sessions, write-up) · 40 points**
**Adult approval required before any field work begins.**

## The question

Pick **one** of these investigation questions (or write your own
with the adult's approval — it must connect a local observation to
one process model from Lessons 01–03):

1. **Slope question.** How does the slope of a local hillside or
   ditch bank relate to signs of erosion (exposed roots, rills,
   bare soil) on it?
2. **Stream question.** Where along one short stream reach is the
   water fastest, and does the fastest water match where the bank
   looks most eroded?
3. **Soil question.** How do two local soils (for example garden
   soil and path soil) differ in how fast water soaks in, and what
   does that say about runoff and erosion?

## Materials

Tape measure or measuring wheel, a 1-meter stick or ruler, a watch or
phone timer, a bottle of water (for the infiltration test only —
never drink sampled water), a notebook, pencils, a phone/camera for
site photos only if the adult approves and no people are
photographed. For the stream question: a floating leaf or small
stick, and a marked 2-meter stretch of bank.

## Safety (adult reads first)

- The adult previews the site and the procedure and stays present.
- No climbing on steep or unstable slopes; no wading above the
  ankles; stop for lightning, flooding, or heavy traffic.
- Stay on public land or land you have permission to use.
- Wash hands after handling soil or stream water.
- **Alternative (no site, bad weather, or no adult available):** use
  the stream-table/sandbox model in § Alternatives and answer the
  same question with model data, labeled as model data.

## Steps

1. **Plan (S15).** Write the question, the variable you will change
   or compare, the variables you will keep the same, and a data
   table with units. The adult signs the plan.
2. **Sketch the site (S16).** Draw a labeled sketch: north arrow,
   slope direction or flow direction, where each measurement was
   taken, and any erosion/deposition features you can see.
3. **Measure (S16–S17).** Take every measurement at least twice and
   record both values plus the mean. Slope method: height change
   over a measured horizontal distance (gradient = rise/run, as in
   Lesson 03). Stream method: time a float over 2 meters, twice,
   and compute speed = distance/time. Infiltration method: pour
   200 mL of water onto a marked circle of soil and time how long
   it takes to disappear, twice.
4. **Analyze (S17).** Compare your measurements with the process
   model from the matching lesson: does steeper ground show more
   erosion? Does faster water match the eroded bank? Does slower
   infiltration match more runoff? State where the data agree with
   the model and where they do not.
5. **Explain (S19).** Write a short report (about 400–600 words)
   with: the question, the method, the data table with units, the
   sketch, the comparison with the model, one source of error and
   how it could change the conclusion, and one local resource or
   hazard connection (for example: "this slope drains toward the
   road, so heavy rain could wash sediment onto it" or "this soil
   holds water, which matters for the garden in a dry summer").

## Deliverables

- The signed plan (S15).
- A data table with units and repeated measurements.
- A labeled site sketch.
- A written report of about 400–600 words including the error
  discussion and the resource/hazard connection.

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | Full marks | Partial marks | Missing/weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question and plan (8) | Testable question; variables named; data table with units ready; adult signature | Question vague or one variable missing | No plan or no signature |
| Data quality (10) | Every measurement repeated; units on every value; means computed | One repeat or some units missing | Single measurements only, or no units |
| Sketch (6) | Direction, measurement points, and erosion/deposition features labeled | One of the three missing | No usable sketch |
| Model comparison (8) | States where data agree AND disagree with the lesson's model, with numbers cited | Only agreement stated, or no numbers cited | No comparison |
| Error and uncertainty (4) | One real error source named, with how it could change the conclusion | Error named but effect not stated | No error discussion |
| Resource/hazard connection (4) | Specific local connection, argued from the data | Generic statement, not tied to the data | Missing |

## Alternatives

- **Stream table / sandbox model.** Build a sloped tray of sand,
  pour a measured volume of water (for example 500 mL) from a fixed
  height, and measure how far grains travel at two slopes. Record
  slope, volume, and mean travel distance (two runs each). Label
  every value "model data" — models show the process, not the place.
- **Simulation alternative.** Use the PhET "Plate Tectonics"
  simulation (see `resource-pack.md`) to test how crust type
  changes subduction, and write the same report structure with
  simulation observations instead of field data, labeled as such.
  The adult verifies keyboard operability before assigning.
- **Text-only alternative.** If no image or site is available, work
  from the written reach description in Lesson 02 and the contour
  pattern in Lesson 03; the report compares those descriptions
  with the models instead of new measurements.
