# Unit 02 — Plate Tectonics, Resources, and Landscape Processes

**Grade 12 · Science · Earth and Space Science pathway · Weeks 7–10 of the course year**
(draft, delivered for issue #55; not educator-reviewed)

## Overview

The planet builds and dismantles its own surface. This unit follows
that engine from the inside out, in four moves, and ends with the
learner's own landscape investigation:

1. **Plate boundaries and the landforms they build** — divergent,
   convergent (ocean–ocean, ocean–continent, continent–continent), and
   transform boundaries; which boundary builds which features, and why
   the crust type and motion direction decide (HS-ESS2-1).
2. **Weathering, erosion, and deposition** — how surface processes
   take apart what tectonics builds; reading a river valley for where
   material is loosened, moved, and dropped; a stream/sandbox model
   with a real (fictional, labeled) dataset.
3. **Topographic maps and local relief** — contour lines, contour
   interval, gradient calculations, and drawing a topographic profile
   from a described map pattern.
4. **Resources and hazards** — how past and present geoscience
   processes distribute mineral, energy, and groundwater resources;
   why people settle near hazards; evidence-based explanations of how
   resource availability and hazards have influenced human activity;
   comparing mitigation options by explicit criteria (HS-ESS3-1).

The essential question running through all four weeks is: **the same
forces that build mountains and valleys also decide where people can
live and what they can use — how do we read that story in a
landscape, and what should we do about the hazards it contains?**

The unit's investigation is *"Reading My Landscape"* (see
[project.md](project.md)): the learner measures a local slope, soil,
or stream feature with adult supervision, records data with units,
sketches the site, and connects observations to a process model from
the unit — plus a short evidence-based explanation of one local
resource or hazard tradeoff.

## Prerequisites

From Unit 01 (Earth-system evidence) and the Weeks 1–2 diagnostic:

- Evaluating plate-tectonics evidence: seafloor rock ages, magnetic
  striping, earthquake and volcano belts at boundaries.
- Thermal convection cycling matter in the mantle and its link to
  plate motion; lithosphere vs. asthenosphere vocabulary.
- Building simple quantitative models (rate or flux estimates) with
  correct units, stated assumptions, and uncertainty.
- Proportional reasoning, unit conversion, and reading graphs.
- From grade-6 Earth/space science (conceptual level): boundary
  types, rock types, the rock cycle, natural hazards — this unit
  makes them quantitative and applies them to resources and human
  decisions; it re-teaches none of the concepts from scratch.

No calculus is assumed. Every gradient and rate calculation is worked
step by step with unit conversions shown.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to:

1. Classify a plate boundary from the plates' crust types and relative
   motion, and predict the landforms it builds (ridge, rift valley,
   trench, island arc, volcanic mountain range, folded range, fault
   system) with a mechanism for each prediction. (HS-ESS2-1)
2. Distinguish weathering from erosion from deposition in field
   descriptions and sketches, and explain how slope, water volume,
   and material properties control where each dominates.
   (HS-ESS2-1)
3. Read a topographic map: state the contour interval, estimate
   elevations, compute a gradient with correct unit conversions, and
   draw a topographic profile from a described contour pattern.
   (HS-ESS2-1)
4. Construct evidence-based explanations for how the availability of
   natural resources, the occurrence of natural hazards, and changes
   in climate have influenced human activity — citing at least two
   specific pieces of evidence per explanation. (HS-ESS3-1)
5. Compare resource-development or hazard-mitigation options against
   explicit criteria (cost, benefit, who bears the risk) and justify
   a reasoned judgment without claiming one option is universally
   best. (HS-ESS3-1; track objective 10)
6. Plan and carry out the unit's landscape investigation: a testable
   question, data recorded in tables with units, a labeled sketch,
   error discussion, and evidence-based conclusions — with the adult
   approving every procedure before it begins. (track objective 12)

## Safety

All field work is adult-supervised: the adult previews every
procedure, checks the site for traffic, unstable banks, and weather,
and stops work for lightning or flooding. No climbing on steep or
unstable slopes; no wading above the ankles; no drinking sampled
water. The stream-table/sandbox model is the default alternative
whenever the site, weather, or materials are inadequate. See
scope-and-sequence §6 and `project.md` § Safety for the full rules.
