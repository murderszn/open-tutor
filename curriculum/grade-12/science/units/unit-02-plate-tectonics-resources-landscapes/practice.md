# Practice Sets — Unit 02

Five sets, one per lesson. Work in the notebook; the adult scores
same-day. Solutions are in `answer-key.md` (adult only). Datasets
are labeled **real reference data**, **rounded practice data**, or
**fictional practice data** — treat each label as part of the task.

---

## Practice Set A — Plate boundaries and landforms (Lesson 01)

1. For each scenario, name the boundary type, predict one landform,
   and state the mechanism in one sentence: (a) a continental rift
   valley where the crust is thinning and volcanoes erupt along the
   rift floor; (b) two oceanic plates converging, one sinking
   beneath the other; (c) two plates sliding sideways along a fault,
   offsetting a road by several meters.
2. A ridge spreads at a full rate of 2.5 cm/yr. How far apart do two
   points on opposite sides move in 2 million years? Give the answer
   in kilometers, showing every conversion. (Rate: real reference
   value; the 2-million-year span is the practice question.)
3. A fault slips at 2 cm/yr (fictional practice rate). A fence
   built straight across the fault in 1945 is measured in 2025. How
   many centimeters of offset have accumulated, and how many meters
   is that? Show the steps.
4. **Error analysis.** A classmate writes: "When two continents
   collide, the collision melts rock and builds a volcanic mountain
   range." Identify the error and state what actually happens,
   with the mechanism.
5. The three panels of `assets/plate-boundaries-diagram.svg` are
   described in the unit assessment. Without looking at the diagram,
   write one sentence per panel: the motion, whether crust is
   created, destroyed, or neither, and one landform. Then open the
   diagram and check yourself.

## Practice Set B — Weathering, erosion, deposition (Lesson 02)

1. Classify each as weathering (physical or chemical), erosion, or
   deposition: (a) iron in a rock surface rusts reddish-brown;
   (b) a river drops gravel where it enters a still lake; (c) wind
   drives sand against a cliff face, wearing it smooth; (d) a
   landslide carries soil down a slope after heavy rain. (One
   sentence of reasoning each: did material move? Did its chemistry
   change?)
2. Stream-table runs (fictional practice data): same water volume
   and material, slope 10° gives mean grain travel 8 cm, slope 20°
   gives mean grain travel 29 cm. Compute the difference and state
   the claim, with one limit of the claim.
3. A delta and a beach are both piles of sediment. In two or three
   sentences, explain how their forming agents and energy drops
   differ.
4. A valley description (fictional practice data): the left bank of
   a bend is steep and undercut; the right bank is a gentle sandy
   slope; the valley floor is flat layered silt. (a) Where is
   erosion happening now, and where is deposition? (b) Was the silt
   floor deposited by fast water or slow water? Explain using grain
   size.
5. **Error analysis.** A classmate writes: "Weathering carried the
   boulder downhill." Identify the error and rewrite the sentence
   correctly using *weathering* and *erosion*.

## Practice Set C — Topographic maps and relief (Lesson 03)

1. A map has a contour interval of 20 ft and an index contour of
   1,000 ft. What is the elevation of the fourth contour line above
   that index contour? Show the multiplication.
2. A slope drops 120 ft over a horizontal distance of 0.25 miles.
   Convert the distance to feet, then give the gradient as a decimal
   and as a percentage. (1 mile = 5,280 ft.)
3. The highest point in a map area is 1,450 ft and the lowest point
   is 920 ft. What is the local relief?
4. A valley's contour V's point north. A hiker claims the stream
   flows north. Is the hiker right? Explain in one sentence.
5. **Error analysis.** A classmate computes a gradient as
   200 ÷ 0.5 = 400 for a slope that drops 200 ft over 0.5 miles.
   Explain what is wrong with the number 400, and give the correct
   gradient as a percentage.

## Practice Set D — Resources from Earth processes (Lesson 04)

1. In your own words, explain the process that concentrates copper
   into a porphyry deposit at a convergent margin, in two or three
   sentences. Name the role of water explicitly.
2. Groundwater budget (fictional practice data): an aquifer
   recharges at 200 units per year and is pumped at 260 units per
   year. (a) What is the net change per year? (b) If these rates
   hold for 5 years, how many units does the aquifer lose in total?
   (c) Name one thing that happens to wells before the aquifer
   reaches zero.
3. Ore tonnage (fictional practice data): Deposit P holds 500,000
   tonnes of ore at 1.0% copper; Deposit Q holds 800,000 tonnes of
   ore at 0.5% copper. Calculate the tonnes of copper in each.
   Which deposit holds more total copper?
4. Cost-benefit table: for geothermal energy, fill in the four
   questions from Lesson 04 — what it offers, what extraction (or
   construction) costs, the environmental tradeoffs, and who gains
   versus who bears the costs. One or two lines per question, in
   neutral language.
5. **Error analysis.** A classmate writes: "Oil forms at plate
   boundaries, because boundaries are where the action is." Identify
   the error and state where oil actually forms and what three
   things a usable deposit needs.

## Practice Set E — Hazards and human settlement (Lesson 05)

1. Classify each response as mitigation or adaptation, with one
   sentence of reasoning: (a) a levee along a river; (b) raising
   homes on stilts above expected flood levels; (c) insurance for
   hillside homeowners; (d) zoning that bans new homes in the lowest
   flood zone; (e) a tsunami warning system with an evacuation plan.
2. Willow Bend's fictional flood record shows floods about every
   20 years, the latest 18 years ago. A resident says, "We are safe
   for another 20 years." Explain the error in two sentences.
3. A coastal town sits beside a port that employs most of its
   residents, on land exposed to tsunamis. Write a paragraph
   explaining why residents stay, naming at least two attractions
   from Lesson 05's list and applying them to this town.
4. **Error analysis.** A classmate says, "After the town built a
   levee, flooding is solved." Identify two things wrong with that
   claim, using the lesson's discussion of design levels and
   building behind levees.
5. A landslide-prone hillside neighborhood proposes: (a) drainage
   pipes that carry rainwater away from the slope, and (b) insurance
   for hillside homeowners. Classify each as mitigation or
   adaptation, and state in one sentence each what it cannot do.
