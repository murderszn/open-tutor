# Unit Assessment — Unit 02: Plate Tectonics, Resources, and Landscape Processes

**Session S20 · ~50 minutes · 50 points · closed notebook**
**Adult scores same-day; solutions are in `../answer-key.md` (adult only).**

## Part A — Boundaries and landforms (Objectives 1; 14 pts)

**1. (8 pts)** Three panels show plate motion with red arrows
(described in words; the same panels appear as
`../assets/plate-boundaries-diagram.svg`):

- Panel 1: two plates move apart; a ridge sits between them.
- Panel 2: an oceanic plate sinks beneath a continental plate;
  a trench and volcanoes are marked.
- Panel 3: two plates slide sideways past each other along a
  fault line.

For each panel: name the boundary type, state whether crust is
created, destroyed, or neither there, and name one landform it
builds.

**2. (6 pts) Error analysis.** A classmate writes: "Transform
boundaries build volcanoes, because the plates grind together and
the friction melts rock." Identify the error, state what transform
boundaries actually produce, and explain why the friction claim
fails (one sentence each).

## Part B — Surface processes (Objective 2; 10 pts)

**3. (10 pts)** For each of the four descriptions, name the
dominant process — weathering, erosion, or deposition — and give
the evidence in one sentence:

(a) Water seeps into a crack in a rock, freezes overnight, and a
flake of rock falls off by morning.
(b) A river carries sand downstream and drops it where the water
slows at the river's mouth, building a fan of new land.
(c) Wind blows loose sand grains against a rock face, slowly
wearing a hollow into it.
(d) Rain dissolves limestone along a joint, widening the crack
without moving any solid pieces away.

## Part C — Maps and numbers (Objectives 1, 3; 12 pts)

**4. (6 pts)** A mid-ocean ridge spreads at a full rate of
2.5 cm/yr. How far apart do two points on opposite sides move in
10 million years? Give the answer in kilometers, showing every
conversion.

**5. (6 pts)** A topographic map has a contour interval of 40 ft.
A hiker walks from a point on the 800-ft contour to a point on the
1,000-ft contour along a steady slope. (a) What is the elevation
gain? (b) If the horizontal distance is 0.5 miles, what is the
gradient as a percent? Show all conversions. (1 mile = 5,280 ft.)

## Part D — Resources and hazards (Objectives 4, 5; 14 pts)

**6. (7 pts)** A mining company proposes three fictional sites
(practice data): Site A has high-grade ore at shallow depth but
sits beside a river used for drinking water; Site B has
lower-grade ore at greater depth, far from any water source; Site
C has medium-grade ore at medium depth, near a town that wants
the jobs. Rank the sites for development, stating your criteria
first. There is no single correct ranking — the reasoning and the
tradeoffs you name are what is scored.

**7. (7 pts)** Revisit Riverbend from the Week-10 quiz (floodplain
town, fertile soil, fictional flood record of about once every 20
years). The council now asks: "Should we build the levee, relocate
the lowest homes, or do something else?" Recommend ONE option and
defend it against the other two using two explicit criteria (for
example cost and who bears the risk). State one limit of your own
recommendation.
