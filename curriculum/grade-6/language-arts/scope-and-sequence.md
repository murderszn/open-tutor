## 2. Prerequisites

Learners typically enter grade-6 language arts with (the grade-5 track's
end-of-year objectives): reading unfamiliar multisyllabic words with morphology
and expression; clarifying words with context clues, Greek/Latin affixes/roots,
and dictionaries; interpreting similes, metaphors, idioms, and adages; quoting
text accurately for inferences; determining theme from details and summarizing;
comparing characters, settings, and events; explaining how point of view shapes
events; finding two or more main ideas in informational text and comparing text
structures; analyzing multiple accounts; writing narratives with dialogue,
pacing, and sensory detail; writing opinion pieces with ordered, evidenced
reasons; short multi-source research with paraphrase and source lists; and
prepared, evidence-based discussion.

The diagnostic weeks (Weeks 1–2) verify these. U01 re-teaches inference from
explicit text before assuming it is secure; U04 re-checks sentence-level
fluency before asking for stylistic variety.

## 3. Track objectives

Measurable, adult-assessed by end of year (12 objectives; numbered in the
track README):

1. Cite textual evidence to support analysis of what a literary or informational text says explicitly as well as inferences drawn from it (RL.6.1, RI.6.1).
2. Determine a theme or central idea and how it is conveyed through particular details; provide an objective summary distinct from personal opinion; describe how a story's or drama's plot unfolds in episodes and how characters respond or change (RL.6.2, RL.6.3, RI.6.2).
3. Analyze author's craft: determine figurative and connotative meanings and word choice's impact on meaning and tone; explain how a sentence, chapter, scene, or stanza fits the whole text and develops theme, setting, or plot; explain how the narrator's or speaker's point of view is developed (RL.6.4, RL.6.5, RL.6.6).
4. Compare and contrast the experience of reading a story, drama, or poem with listening to or viewing an audio, video, or live version; compare texts in different forms or genres on similar themes (RL.6.7, RL.6.9).
5. In informational text: analyze in detail how a key individual, event, or idea is introduced, illustrated, and elaborated; determine an author's point of view or purpose and explain how it is conveyed; trace and evaluate arguments, distinguishing claims supported by reasons and evidence from claims that are not; compare one author's presentation of events with another's; integrate information across media and sources (RI.6.3, RI.6.5, RI.6.6, RI.6.7, RI.6.8, RI.6.9).
6. Clarify unknown words with context clues, Greek and Latin affixes and roots, and reference materials; interpret figures of speech including personification; explain connotations and denotations; analyze the impact of specific word choices; acquire and use academic vocabulary including verbs signaling analysis and inference (L.6.4, L.6.5, L.6.6).
7. Use pronouns correctly (proper case, intensive pronouns, corrected shifts in number/person and vague pronouns); punctuate nonrestrictive and parenthetical elements with commas, parentheses, or dashes; vary sentence patterns for meaning, reader interest, and style while maintaining consistent tone (L.6.1, L.6.2, L.6.3).
8. Write arguments with clear claims, logically organized and well-supported reasons, relevant evidence, words/phrases/clauses that clarify relationships among claims and reasons, formal style, and a concluding statement (W.6.1).
9. Write narratives with natural event sequencing, dialogue, pacing, description, and reflection; write informative/explanatory texts with relevant facts, definitions, and concrete details; revise through planning, revising, editing, and rewriting; use technology to produce and publish writing (W.6.2, W.6.3, W.6.5, W.6.6).
10. Conduct short research projects; gather relevant information from multiple print and digital sources; quote and paraphrase data and conclusions while avoiding plagiarism; provide basic works-cited lists; draw evidence from literary or informational texts to support analysis and reflection (W.6.7, W.6.8, W.6.9).
11. Engage in collaborative discussions with preparation, collegial rules, and elaborated responses; delineate a speaker's argument and distinguish supported from unsupported claims; present claims and findings logically with multimedia components in formal English (SL.6.1–SL.6.6).
12. Read and comprehend literature and literary nonfiction in the grades 6–8 text-complexity band proficiently, with scaffolding as needed at the high end; write routinely over extended and shorter time frames for a range of tasks, purposes, and audiences (RL.6.10, RI.6.10, W.6.10).

## 5. 36-week sequence

Eight 4-week units plus four flexible weeks (diagnostic ×2 at the start, final
review ×2 at the end) = 36 weeks. Session model: **4 sessions per week, about
40 minutes each** (16 sessions per unit). Session types rotate across explicit
lesson, guided practice, reading or writing practice, and review — named per
unit below. Grade-6 learners do independent written practice (8–12 tasks) that
the adult reviews the same day; oral, pointing, and drawing response modes
remain available for checks and supports.

### Weeks 1–2 — Diagnostic and routines (flexible)

- **Goal:** establish session routines (word-study warm-ups, read-aloud
  turn-taking, writing-notebook habits, discussion norms, exit-check rituals)
  and baseline each objective's entry point.
- Sessions: decode unfamiliar multisyllabic words aloud with morphology
  (RF.6.3, L.6.4.b); read a grade-level literary and a short informational
  passage, citing one explicit detail and one inference from each (RL.6.1,
  RI.6.1); identify the narrator's point of view in a story opening (RL.6.6);
  write one claim with one supporting reason (W.6.1 entry); join a 5-minute
  prepared discussion following collegial rules (SL.6.1).
- No new instruction; record observations against the track objectives.

### Unit 01 — Close reading: inference and textual evidence (Weeks 3–6)

- **Standards:** RL.6.1, RI.6.1; RF.6.3, RF.6.4; L.6.4, L.6.6
- **Week 3 goal:** distinguish what a text says explicitly from inferences
  drawn from it; cite textual evidence for both (RL.6.1, RI.6.1).
- **Week 4 goal:** morphology word study — read unfamiliar multisyllabic
  words with Greek/Latin affixes and roots; self-correct with context
  (RF.6.3, RF.6.4.c, L.6.4.b); acquire academic verbs signaling inference and
  analysis (L.6.6).
- **Week 5 goal:** apply inference and evidence-citation across one literary
  and one informational passage; use context clues for unknown words (L.6.4.a).
- **Week 6:** review week — formative quiz on evidence/inference; fluency
  check (RF.6.4.a–b).
- Sessions rotate: modeled close-reading lesson → evidence practice →
  word-study practice → review and discussion.
- Reuses: `resources/language_arts_great_books_and_stories.md` (adult-selected
  public-domain passages in the grades 6–8 band).

### Unit 02 — Literature: theme, plot, and narrator (Weeks 7–10)

- **Standards:** RL.6.2–6.7, RL.6.9, RL.6.10; L.6.6
- **Week 7 goal:** determine a theme and how details convey it; summarize
  distinct from opinion (RL.6.2); describe how plot unfolds in episodes and
  how characters respond or change (RL.6.3).
- **Week 8 goal:** craft — figurative and connotative meanings; word choice's
  impact on meaning and tone (RL.6.4); how a chapter, scene, or stanza fits the
  whole and develops theme, setting, or plot (RL.6.5).
- **Week 9 goal:** how the narrator's or speaker's point of view is developed
  and shapes events (RL.6.6); read at the high end of the band with scaffolding
  (RL.6.10).
- **Week 10:** review week — compare reading vs. an audio version of the same
  text (RL.6.7); compare texts in different forms or genres on similar themes
  (RL.6.9); formative check.
- Sessions rotate: modeled read-aloud lesson → theme-and-plot practice →
  craft analysis practice → review and discussion.
- Reuses: `resources/language_arts_literary_terms.md` (theme, plot, point of
  view, tone — excerpted); great-books guide for text selection.

### Unit 03 — Informational reading: argument, structure, and author purpose (Weeks 11–14)

- **Standards:** RI.6.2–RI.6.9, RI.6.10
- **Week 11 goal:** central idea conveyed through details and objective
  summary (RI.6.2); how a key individual, event, or idea is introduced,
  illustrated, and elaborated (RI.6.3).
- **Week 12 goal:** how a paragraph, chapter, or section fits the whole and
  develops ideas (RI.6.5); author's point of view or purpose and how it is
  conveyed (RI.6.6); technical and connotative word meanings in context (RI.6.4).
- **Week 13 goal:** trace and evaluate the argument and specific claims,
  distinguishing claims supported by reasons and evidence from claims that are
  not (RI.6.8); compare one author's presentation of events with another's —
  e.g., a memoir and a biography of the same person (RI.6.9); integrate
  information across media and formats (RI.6.7).
- **Week 14:** review week — formative check across argument evaluation and
  source comparison.
- Sessions rotate: modeled informational lesson → structure/purpose practice →
  argument-evaluation practice → review and discussion.
- Reuses: the kept starter lesson's claim/evidence reasoning routine as a
  warm-up frame; it is not duplicated in the unit.

### Unit 04 — Grammar: pronouns, sentence variety, and vocabulary (Weeks 15–18)

- **Standards:** L.6.1, L.6.2, L.6.3, L.6.4, L.6.5; SL.6.6 (formal English)
- **Week 15 goal:** pronouns in proper case; intensive pronouns; correct
  shifts in number and person and vague pronouns (L.6.1.a–d).
- **Week 16 goal:** commas, parentheses, and dashes for nonrestrictive and
  parenthetical elements; spelling (L.6.2.a–b); vocabulary — connotation vs.
  denotation, personification, and the impact of specific word choices (L.6.5).
- **Week 17 goal:** vary sentence patterns for meaning, reader interest, and
  style; maintain consistent style and tone; formal English in writing (L.6.3).
- **Week 18:** midyear review — cumulative formative check (U01–U04) plus
  targeted reteaching; formative quiz.
- Sessions rotate: explicit grammar lesson → revision/editing practice →
  vocabulary practice → review.
- Reuses: `resources/language_arts_parts_of_speech.md` (pronoun tables),
  `resources/language_arts_sentence_structure.md` (clause/phrase/sentence
  models) — both excerpted by the adult, never assigned whole.

### Unit 05 — Narrative craft: dialogue, pacing, and revision (Weeks 19–22)

- **Standards:** W.6.3, W.6.4, W.6.5; RL.6.3, L.6.1, L.6.3 (in drafting)
- **Week 19 goal:** structure event sequences naturally; unfold experiences
  with reflection; develop narrator and characters (W.6.3.a–b).
- **Week 20 goal:** narrative techniques — dialogue, pacing, description,
  reflection (W.6.3.b); precise words and phrases and relevant descriptive
  details (W.6.3.d).
- **Week 21 goal:** transitions that convey sequence and signal shifts in
  time frame or setting (W.6.3.c); conclusions following from narrated
  experiences (W.6.3.e); peer-and-adult revision pass (W.6.5).
- **Week 22:** revision week — revise, edit, and rewrite (W.6.5); formative
  check; one polished narrative for the portfolio.
- Sessions rotate: craft lesson → drafting practice → revision/editing
  practice → share and review.

### Unit 06 — Argument writing: claims, reasons, and credible evidence (Weeks 23–26)

- **Standards:** W.6.1; RI.6.8; SL.6.3, SL.6.4; L.6.1, L.6.3
- **Week 23 goal:** introduce claims and organize reasons and evidence
  clearly; distinguish claim, reasons, and evidence (W.6.1.a).
- **Week 24 goal:** support claims with clear reasons and relevant evidence;
  trace which reasons support which points (W.6.1.b; RI.6.8 applied to
  learner drafting); transitions that clarify relationships among claims and
  reasons (W.6.1.c).
- **Week 25 goal:** formal style and objective tone (W.6.1.d, L.6.3.b);
  concluding statement or section (W.6.1.e); drafting with technology (W.6.6
  introduction).
- **Week 26:** revision and presentation — revise with adult feedback (W.6.5);
  present the argument orally with logical sequencing (SL.6.4); formative
  check; one polished argument for the portfolio.
- Sessions rotate: argument lesson → claim/evidence practice → drafting
  practice → review and presentation.
- Reuses (kept; key separated into the unit's teacher guide):
  `starter-lesson.md` — its three sessions become the unit's opening
  investigation on evidence sufficiency, extended with taught lessons on
  claim organization, formal style, and transitions.

### Unit 07 — Research: explanatory synthesis and source evaluation (Weeks 27–30)

- **Standards:** W.6.2, W.6.7, W.6.8, W.6.9; RI.6.3, RI.6.7; L.6.6
- **Week 27 goal:** conduct a short research project answering a question
  through investigation (W.6.7); gather relevant information from multiple
  print and digital sources; assess source credibility (W.6.8).
- **Week 28 goal:** quote and paraphrase accurately while avoiding
  plagiarism; basic works-cited lists (W.6.8); draw evidence from
  informational texts (W.6.9.b); domain-specific vocabulary (L.6.6).
- **Week 29 goal:** synthesize into an informative/explanatory text —
  relevant facts, definitions, concrete details, and transitions that clarify
  relationships (W.6.2); use technology to produce and publish (W.6.6).
- **Week 30:** review week — research process check; formative assessment;
  one polished explanatory piece for the portfolio.
- Sessions rotate: research lesson → source-finding and evaluation practice →
  drafting practice → review.
- Reuses: `resources/semester-resource-library.md` (Purdue OWL: MLA
  formatting basics; Purdue OWL: argumentative structure for synthesis
  organization) — adult-previewed.

### Unit 08 — Poetry, discussion, presentations, and portfolio reflection (Weeks 31–34)

- **Standards:** RL.6.4, RL.6.5; L.6.5; SL.6.1, SL.6.4, SL.6.5, SL.6.6; W.6.10
- **Week 31 goal:** poetry craft — figurative language including
  personification, connotation, and word choice's impact on tone (RL.6.4,
  L.6.5); how a stanza fits the whole poem (RL.6.5).
- **Week 32 goal:** prepared collaborative discussion — build on others'
  ideas, pose and respond to questions with elaboration (SL.6.1); delineate a
  speaker's argument in live discussion (SL.6.3).
- **Week 33 goal:** presentation design — logical sequencing, pertinent facts
  and details, multimedia components and visual displays (SL.6.4, SL.6.5);
  formal English (SL.6.6).
- **Week 34:** portfolio week — revise one piece from U05–U07 (W.6.5);
  reflective cover letter; presentation of the portfolio; unit formative
  check.
- Sessions rotate: poetry lesson → discussion practice → presentation
  practice → review and share.
- Reuses: `resources/language_arts_literary_terms.md` (figurative language —
  excerpted); the semester library's Poetry Foundation item remains
  enrichment-only unless the adult selects a poem.

### Weeks 35–36 — Final review and cumulative assessment (flexible)

- Revisit inference and evidence citation (U01), theme and craft (U02), argument
  evaluation (U03), conventions (U04), and writing process (U05–U07) through
  short mixed practice sets; administer the cumulative assessment; confirm the
  portfolio is complete. Assessment design and keys belong to R00; units are
  not expected to cover them.

## 6. Retrieval and review cadence

Each unit's week 4 is a dedicated review week (practice, formative quiz, and
reteaching). Week 18 adds a midyear cumulative check across U01–U04.
Conventions (L.6.1–6.3) recur in every unit's writing practice so skills do not
decay after U04; vocabulary routines (affixes/roots, context clues) recur in
U01, U04, and U08 poetry. Claims-and-evidence reasoning (RI.6.8/SL.6.3) is
introduced in the kept starter lesson, exercised in U03, and applied in U06
writing. Poetry craft in U08 is new, not assumed known. No unit assumes a
skill its predecessor has not taught.

## 8. Safe materials

All reading passages are original or lawful public-domain/licensed texts;
unit builds must not reproduce copyrighted books, poems, or worksheets.
Investigations use observation, discussion, and writing — no hazardous
materials. Multimedia work uses learner-safe tools under adult supervision;
presentations include only the learner's own writing or public-domain texts.
Privacy: keep learner work, names, photos, schedules, and grades in private
storage; nothing learner-identifying enters the repository.

## 9. Accessibility supports

Every unit carries the supports the starter lesson already models: read-aloud
and chunked text, pre-taught vocabulary, outline frames, speech-to-text or
adult-scribed options, extra planning time, and rubric transparency (2-1-0
criterion bands stated before the task). Oral, pointing, and drawing response
modes remain available for checks. Poetry and presentation units add
text-only alternatives for every multimedia component and captioning guidance
for recorded speech. Supports are differentiated per learner need, never
optional enrichment alone.

## 10. Gaps and paths for future units

The track starts from near zero: the starter lesson covers RI.6.8/SL.6.3/W.6.1
fragments only. U01–U08 build everything else new. Largest new builds: the
U04 grammar sequence (pronouns, sentence variety), the U02 literature sequence
(theme/plot/narrator with public-domain texts), and the U07 research sequence
(source evaluation, works-cited basics). U08's poetry component has no legacy
material and needs an original generated illustration plan when the unit is
built. R00 will reuse each unit's formative checks; nothing in the legacy
library can supply the diagnostic because no diagnostic exists.
