# Unit 02 — Literature: Theme, Plot, and Narrator

**Grade:** 6 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 7–10
**Backlog:** [issue #32](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/32), section U02
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Stories have a shape, a voice, and a point. The shape is the
**plot** — the episodes of a story in the order the author gives
them, rising and falling like a path over a hill. The voice is the
**narrator** — the one telling the story, who knows some things and
cannot know others. And the point is the **theme** — the idea the
story is really about, which the author never has to say out loud
because the details say it. In this unit the learner takes stories
apart the way a mechanic takes apart an engine: episode by episode,
choice by choice, to see how theme, plot, and narrator work
together. The weeks also build the craft tools that make stories
work: figurative language, connotation, word choice, and how one
scene fits into the whole.

## Essential question

How do a story's shape, its voice, and its details work together to
make a point?

## Prerequisites

From Unit 01 (track weeks 3–6) and the grade-5 language arts track:

- Distinguish explicit facts from inferences; cite textual evidence
  (quotation or close paraphrase with a paragraph reference).
- Summarize a story in a few sentences; reread to smooth rough
  spots (RF.6.4.a–b).
- Use sentence-level context clues to solve unknown words.
- Use academic verbs of analysis — *infer, suggest, indicate,
  demonstrate* — in discussion and writing (L.6.6).

Lesson 01 re-teaches the theme/topic distinction explicitly rather
than assuming it is secure, and Lesson 04 re-teaches narrator types
rather than assuming prior coverage transferred.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Determine a **theme** of a story or poem and explain how
   **particular details** convey it; provide an **objective
   summary** of the text distinct from personal opinions (RL.6.2).
2. Describe how a story's **plot unfolds in a series of episodes**
   and analyze **how characters respond or change** as the plot
   moves toward a resolution (RL.6.3).
3. Determine the meaning of **figurative and connotative** words
   and phrases as used in a text; analyze the impact of **specific
   word choices** on meaning and tone; analyze how a sentence,
   chapter, scene, or stanza **fits into the overall structure**
   and contributes to the development of theme, setting, or plot
   (RL.6.4, RL.6.5).
4. Explain how an **author develops the narrator's or speaker's
   point of view** — and how that point of view **shapes the
   events** the reader can and cannot see (RL.6.6).
5. Compare and contrast the experience of **reading** a story with
   **listening to** a version of the same text; compare and
   contrast texts in **different forms or genres on similar themes**
   (RL.6.7, RL.6.9); read grade-6–8 literature proficiently, with
   scaffolding at the high end of the band (RL.6.10); acquire and
   use grade-appropriate academic vocabulary (L.6.6).

## Unit passages (original; written for this unit)

All passages below are original curriculum-authored texts — no
story, poem, worksheet, or textbook is reproduced.

- **"The Bench at the Bus Stop"** (literary, ~200 words) — Lesson
  01. An old man's quiet morning habit: the theme-from-details
  workbench.
- **"The Tryout"** (literary, ~250 words) — Lesson 02. A nervous
  audition, mapped episode by episode onto the story mountain;
  also the Lesson 05 read-aloud text.
- **"The Storm at Marrow Bay"** (literary descriptive passage,
  ~190 words) — Lesson 03. Figurative language and connotation
  workbench.
- **"The Climb"** (original poem, 3 stanzas) — Lesson 03. How a
  stanza fits the whole and develops theme.
- **"The Spilled Paint"** (literary, ~150 words, first person) —
  Lesson 04. The same events retold from a second narrator; the
  generated-image activity ("Three narrators, one scene").
- **"The Lantern Path"** (literary, ~190 words) and **"Two Small
  Lanterns"** (original poem, 2 stanzas) — Lesson 06. A story and a
  poem on the same theme, compared.
- **"The Quiet Harbor"** (literary, ~170 words) — formative quiz
  reading.
- **"The Garden Behind the Fence"** (literary, ~210 words) and
  **"Rain, Rain"** (original poem, 2 stanzas) — unit assessment
  readings.

## Session pacing (16 sessions, ~40 minutes each)

Session model per the track audit: modeled read-aloud lesson →
theme-and-plot practice → craft analysis practice → review and
discussion.

| # | Session | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Week 7 | Lesson 01 — theme from details; objective summary | Theme sentence + 2 evidence details; 3-sentence objective summary |
| 2 | Week 7 | Theme practice — "topic vs. theme" sort; summary check on "The Bench at the Bus Stop" | 8 of 10 items sorted; summary contains no opinion |
| 3 | Week 7 | Lesson 02 — plot episodes and the story mountain | Episodes of "The Tryout" mapped to 5 plot stages |
| 4 | Week 7 | Plot practice + discussion — character change chart for "The Tryout" | Before/after chart with 2 quotes each side |
| 5 | Week 8 | Lesson 03 — word choice, connotation, figurative language, tone | 4 figurative examples found and explained; 3 connotation pairs compared |
| 6 | Week 8 | Craft practice — "The Climb": how each stanza fits the whole | 1 paragraph: how stanza 2 develops the theme |
| 7 | Week 8 | Lesson 04 — narrator and point of view (with generated image activity) | POV of 4 passages identified with evidence; 2 narrator retellings written |
| 8 | Week 8 | POV practice + review — "The Spilled Paint" from two narrators | Retelling changes at least 3 story facts the new narrator cannot know |
| 9 | Week 9 | Lesson 05 — reading vs. listening to "The Tryout" | T-chart with 4+ differences and 1 conclusion about how listening changes meaning |
| 10 | Week 9 | Lesson 06 — "The Lantern Path" vs. "Two Small Lanterns": same theme, different forms | Comparison paragraph naming shared theme + 2 form differences |
| 11 | Week 9 | Project — "Theme Through Three Lenses": choose passages, collect evidence | Evidence log: 1 theme claim + 3 cited details per passage |
| 12 | Week 9 | Project — draft comparative essay; prepare 2-minute talk | Essay meets all 2-point rubric bands; talk names theme, details, POV |
| 13 | Week 10 | Formative quiz ([week-10_quiz.md](assessments/week-10_quiz.md)) | Adult scores same day; reteach list made |
| 14 | Week 10 | Reteach and review — targets from the quiz; theme relay; POV card game | Missed items re-answered correctly with evidence |
| 15 | Week 10 | Unit assessment ([unit-assessment.md](assessments/unit-assessment.md)) | Completed under adult supervision |
| 16 | Week 10 | Unit wrap — reread "The Lantern Path"; reflect on the essential question | 3-sentence reflection naming one theme tool, one plot tool, one narrator tool |
