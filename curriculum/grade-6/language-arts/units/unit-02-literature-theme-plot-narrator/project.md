# Project — "Theme Through Three Lenses"

**Sessions 11–12 (Week 9).** You are the curator of a tiny
museum exhibit about **one theme**. Your exhibit has three
lenses: a story, a poem, and a narrator's retelling. Visitors
should leave able to state the theme in one sentence and point
to the details that built it.

## Materials

- Two unit passages of **different forms** on your chosen theme
  (for example: "The Lantern Path" + "Two Small Lanterns," or
  "The Bench at the Bus Stop" + "The Climb").
- [three-narrators-one-scene.png](assets/three-narrators-one-scene.png)
  (or its text-only alternative in Lesson 04).
- Paper or a notebook; the unit [answer key](answer-key.md) is
  adult-only and stays closed.

## Steps

### Session 11 — collect the evidence

1. **Choose your theme** in one sentence both passages support.
   Write it at the top of an evidence log.
2. **Lens 1 — story:** list **3 details** from the story that
   convey the theme. Each entry: the detail (quoted or closely
   paraphrased) + the paragraph number + one sentence on what
   the detail shows.
3. **Lens 2 — poem:** list **2 details** from the poem (quoted
   lines + stanza number) that convey the same theme, with one
   sentence each on what they show.
4. **Lens 3 — narrator:** choose the streetlamp picture (or its
   text alternative). Write **3 sentences as one narrator** (the
   child, the dog, or the cat) telling the scene, then write
   **2 sentences** explaining how *your* narrator's point of
   view shapes the story — what it shows and what it hides.
5. Adult check: the adult reviews the log against the rubric
   below and marks any lens that needs another detail.

### Session 12 — build the exhibit

6. **Comparative essay (1–2 paragraphs, 8–12 sentences):** name
   the shared theme; show how the story builds it (events) and
   how the poem builds it (images/sound); close with one
   sentence on what each form does best. Quote at least **two**
   details total.
7. **Two-minute talk:** present the theme, one story detail,
   one poem detail, and your narrator retelling's key choice
   (why that narrator). Speak in complete sentences; you may
   use a notecard with the theme sentence and quotes only.
8. Adult scores with the rubric; the learner revises any
   1-point band before the unit assessment.

## Deliverables

1. Evidence log (theme sentence + 3 story details + 2 poem
   details + narrator retelling with POV explanation).
2. Comparative essay (8–12 sentences, ≥2 quoted details).
3. Two-minute talk (theme, 2 details, narrator choice).

## Rubric

| Criterion | 2 — Strong | 1 — Developing | 0 — Missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Theme sentence | One clear sentence both passages support; specific, not vague ("kindness is nice") | Sentence is vague or fits only one passage | No theme sentence, or a topic label only |
| Story evidence | 3 details, each quoted/paraphrased with paragraph number and what it shows | Fewer than 3, or details without paragraph numbers | No story details |
| Poem evidence | 2 quoted lines with stanza numbers and what each shows | Lines without numbers, or no explanation | No poem details |
| Narrator lens | 3-sentence retelling obeys the narrator's knowledge limit; 2 sentences explain what the POV shows/hides | Retelling breaks the knowledge limit, or no POV explanation | No retelling |
| Essay | 8–12 sentences; theme named; both forms' tools described; ≥2 quoted details; closing comparison sentence | Missing a form's tools, or fewer than 2 quotes | No essay, or under 5 sentences |
| Talk | Theme + 2 details + narrator choice stated in complete sentences within ~2 minutes | Missing an element, or reads the whole essay aloud | No talk |

**Scoring:** 10–12 = exhibit ready; 7–9 = revise the 1-point
bands; below 7 = revisit Lessons 01, 04, and 06 with the adult
before the assessment.
