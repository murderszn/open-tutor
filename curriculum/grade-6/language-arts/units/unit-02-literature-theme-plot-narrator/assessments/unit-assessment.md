# Unit Assessment — Unit 02: Literature: Theme, Plot, and Narrator

**Adult-supervised. ~35 minutes.** Read each passage, then
answer. Solutions and scoring guidance are in the
[answer key](../answer-key.md) (adult only). Do not claim this
assessment is standardized or validated.

## Part A — Reading: "The Garden Behind the Fence" (original)

> **Paragraph 1.** The fence had stood between the two yards
> for as long as anyone on Birch Lane could remember — gray
> boards, leaning a little to the east, with a gate that had
> not opened in years.
>
> **Paragraph 2.** On Maya's side, the yard was a garden:
> tomato vines *climbing* their stakes like green ladders,
> marigolds standing guard in orange rows. On Mr. Abernathy's
> side, the yard was bare dirt and one rusted wheelbarrow.
>
> **Paragraph 3.** In June, Maya's tomatoes began to vanish —
> three one morning, five the next. She set her alarm for dawn
> and watched from the kitchen window. At sunrise, the old
> gate *creaked* open for the first time in years, and Mr.
> Abernathy stepped through with an empty basket.
>
> **Paragraph 4.** Maya threw the window open. "Those are
> *mine*!" she shouted. Mr. Abernathy froze. Then, slowly, he
> set the basket down, turned it over, and showed her the
> bottom: it was lined with seed packets — tomato, marigold,
> basil. "Yours grow," he said quietly. "Mine won't. Teach
> me?"
>
> **Paragraph 5.** By August, the fence still stood, but the
> gate stayed open. On both sides now, tomato vines climbed
> their stakes like green ladders, and Mr. Abernathy — who had
> not gardened in forty years — ate a tomato warm from the
> sun and said it tasted like being ten years old.

**A1. (RL.6.2)** Determine a theme of the story in **one
sentence**. Then cite **two particular details** (with
paragraph numbers) that convey it.

**A2. (RL.6.2)** Write a **three-sentence objective summary**
of the story. (No opinions or judgments.)

**A3. (RL.6.3)** Map the story onto the five plot stages —
**one sentence per stage** (exposition, rising action, climax,
falling action, resolution).

**A4. (RL.6.3)** How does **Mr. Abernathy** change from
paragraph 3 to paragraph 5? Describe his response in each
paragraph (quote one detail per paragraph) and state the
change in one sentence.

**A5. (RL.6.4)** In paragraph 2, the tomato vines are "climbing
their stakes **like green ladders**." Name the type of
figurative language, the two things compared, and what the
comparison adds.

**A6. (RL.6.4)** The gate "*creaked* open" (paragraph 3).
What connotation does *creaked* carry — welcoming or uneasy?
Explain in one sentence, and name one word the author could
have used instead to make the moment feel welcoming.

**A7. (RL.6.5)** Paragraph 5 mirrors paragraph 2 (the vines
"climbing their stakes like green ladders" appear in both).
In 2–3 sentences, explain what work this **repetition** does:
how does the repeated image develop the theme or show the
resolution?

**A8. (RL.6.6)** What is the story's **point of view**? Give
pronoun evidence. Then name **one thing the narrator cannot
tell us** about Mr. Abernathy's forty years without a garden.

## Part B — Reading: "Rain, Rain" (original poem)

> **Stanza 1.**
> Rain, rain on the window glass,
> Silver needles, soft and fast —
> The garden drinks, the gutters sing,
> The whole gray world is listening.
>
> **Stanza 2.**
> Rain, rain, do not stop too soon;
> Wash the dust from leaf and stone.
> What the dry weeks held apart,
> Water joins them, heart to heart.

**B1. (RL.6.2)** State a theme of the poem in **one sentence**,
supported by **one quoted line**.

**B2. (RL.6.4)** "The garden drinks, the gutters sing" — name
the type of figurative language and explain what feeling it
gives the rain (1–2 sentences).

**B3. (RL.6.5)** How does **stanza 2** fit into the whole poem?
In 2–3 sentences, explain what it develops (theme, setting, or
mood) and why the poem would be weaker without it.

## Part C — Comparison (RL.6.7, RL.6.9)

**C1.** "The Garden Behind the Fence" and "Rain, Rain" both
use **water/growing** images. In 3–4 sentences, compare how the
two texts use a similar idea **differently** — name the shared
thread, then contrast the story's approach with the poem's.

**C2.** Imagine hearing "The Garden Behind the Fence" **read
aloud** by the adult instead of reading it yourself. Name
**one thing the listening experience would likely emphasize**
that silent reading might not, and **one thing silent reading
lets you do** that listening does not. (2–3 sentences total.)

## Part D — Short writing (L.6.6)

**D1.** Using at least **three** of these academic words —
*theme, episode, climax, narrator, connotation, objective* —
write 4–5 sentences explaining what this unit taught you about
how stories work. Use each word correctly.
