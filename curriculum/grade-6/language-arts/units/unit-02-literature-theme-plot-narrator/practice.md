# Practice — mixed theme, plot, craft, and POV sessions

Use these in Sessions 2, 4, 6, and 8, or as homework/reteach
banks. Every item has a worked solution in the
[answer key](answer-key.md) (adult only). Session success
criteria are in the unit [README](README.md) pacing table.

## Practice A — theme and objective summary (Session 2)

**A1. Topic or theme?** Label each **Topic** or **Theme**. For
themes, say whether the sentence is *specific* (a real theme) or
*too vague*:

1. "honesty"
2. "Telling the truth can cost you a friend, and be worth it
   anyway"
3. "Never give up"
4. "Practicing a hard thing in secret can give you courage in
   public"
5. "animals"
6. "Small kindnesses can be repaid in ways the giver never
   expects"

**A2.** Reread "The Bench at the Bus Stop" (Lesson 01). Write a
**three-sentence objective summary**. Then trade with the adult:
the adult circles any opinion-word; you rewrite that sentence.

**A3.** New passage — "The Spare Key" (original, ~110 words):

> Every Saturday, Rosa unlocked the community garden shed for
> the neighborhood. She kept the spare key on a red ribbon, and
> she knew which plot belonged to whom — the Patels' tomatoes,
> Mr. Abernathy's stubborn roses. One Saturday the lock was
> already open. Inside, the tools were oiled and the broken
> rake handle was mended. Rosa never found out who had done it.
> She hung the spare key on its nail, tied the red ribbon a
> little tighter, and smiled at the empty shed.

Name a theme in one sentence and cite **two details** that
convey it.

## Practice C — word choice, connotation, figurative language (Session 6)

**C1.** Label each pair's **warmer** and **colder** word, then
use the colder word in a sentence where the coldness is the
point: *house / shack*, *confident / cocky*, *slim / scrawny*,
*curious / nosy*.

**C2.** Find the figurative language in each original sentence.
Name the type and the two things compared:

1. "The old truck *coughed* itself awake."
2. "Her excuse was *as thin as* tissue paper."
3. "The classroom was a *beehive* before the bell."
4. "The moon *poured* silver across the lake."

**C3.** Rewrite: "The *gloomy* hallway *swallowed* the sound of
her footsteps." Change two words so the hallway feels
**welcoming**. Explain what each change does to the tone.

**C4.** In "The Climb" (Lesson 03), which stanza develops the
**setting** most? Quote one line and explain.

## Practice D — narrator and point of view (Session 8)

**D1.** Identify the POV (first / second / third limited /
third omniscient) and give the pronoun or mind-limit evidence:

1. "I never meant to take the last cookie. It was just *there*."
2. "You can hear the gym go quiet before the free throw."
3. "Jonah hid the note. He was sure no one had seen."
4. "Jonah hid the note. Across the hall, Ms. Rivera had seen
   everything and was deciding what to do about it."

**D2.** Reread "The Spilled Paint" (Lesson 04). List **two
things the first-person narrator cannot know**, quoting the
sentence that shows each limit.

**D3.** Rewrite this third-person-limited paragraph in
**first person** (as Jonah). Then write one sentence on what
the new POV *hides* that the old one showed:

> Jonah hid the note behind the loose brick. His hands were
> shaking. He told himself it was just for one day.
