# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Literature: Theme, Plot, and Narrator

**Adult only.** No student answers are in this file; worked
solutions live in the [answer key](answer-key.md) (adult
only). Student pages link here only where a lesson says so.

## Pacing notes (16 sessions, ~40 minutes)

- **Week 7 (theme + plot):** Sessions 1–4. Lesson 01's
  theme/topic distinction needs the full 40 minutes — do not
  rush the modeled examples; learners must hear a theme
  *built* from details before they build one. Session 2's
  summary check is the week's gate: if summaries still carry
  opinions, reteach before Session 3.
- **Week 8 (craft + POV):** Sessions 5–8. Lesson 03 has the
  densest vocabulary (connotation/denotation, tone/mood);
  keep the launch's *cozy/cramped* contrast visible on the
  board all week. Lesson 04's image activity is the week's
  anchor — give it the full 7 minutes; the cat/child
  knowledge-limit question is the whole point of RL.6.6.
- **Week 9 (comparison + project):** Sessions 9–12. Lesson
  05's read-aloud works best with a genuinely expressive
  reading — practice paragraph 4 once beforehand. Project
  Session 12: cap talks at 2 minutes with a visible timer;
  score the rubric live so revision happens the same day.
- **Week 10 (assess + wrap):** Sessions 13–16. Score the quiz
  the same day and build the reteach list before Session 14.
  The assessment (Session 15) is closed-book but the learner
  may keep the story-mountain diagram.

## Misconceptions to watch (per lesson)

1. **Theme = topic.** Learners write "kindness" and stop.
   Require a *sentence* the details earn; reject any theme
   that fits every story ("be nice").
2. **Opinion in summaries.** "The poor man," "the kind girl" —
   circle the exact opinion word and ask for the detail
   beneath it.
3. **Climax = most exciting moment.** Excitement is a feeling;
   the climax is the *turning* moment — after it, the story
   cannot go back. Ask: "What changed direction here?"
4. **Character change = emotion swap.** "He was sad, then
   happy" is not a change analysis. Require quoted *responses*,
   before and after.
5. **Simile-spotting without comparison.** "Like drums" —
   drums compared to *what*? The missing half (raindrops) is
   the analysis.
6. **Tone = mood.** Keep it fixed: tone is the *author's*
   attitude (built by word choice); mood is how the *reader*
   feels.
7. **"First person = main character."** Teach *position*:
   "The Spilled Paint" is first person told by the sibling
   watching Marcus.
8. **Pronoun-swap rewrites.** Changing "I" to "she" without
   changing the *mind limit* is not a POV change. The rewrite
   must change what can be known.
9. **Listening is "better."** The RL.6.7 task is not to pick a
   winner but to name what each version reveals — insist on
   quoted words on both sides of the T-chart.
10. **Shared topic = shared theme.** "Both are about light"
    is a topic. Push for the sentence both texts *support*.

## Differentiation

- **Struggling readers:** pre-read every passage aloud;
  paragraph numbers pre-marked; episode strips for Lesson 02
  (pre-cut cards to order on the mountain); connotation word
  bank for Lesson 03; POV pronoun chart for Lesson 04; all
  sentence frames from the lessons' support boxes.
- **English learners:** pre-teach the unit vocabulary with
  examples first (per the README's "examples first, definitions
  second" rule); allow dictated or drawn responses for the
  image activity and the applied tasks.
- **Advanced learners:** the extension in each lesson
  (second theme, sub-climax, stanza-2 personification,
  streetlamp narrator, poem read-aloud comparison,
  cross-text theme check). The project rubric's 2-point bands
  are written to be reachable but not automatic.

## Readiness and reteach signals

- **Ready for the assessment:** the learner's project essay
  names a theme both passages support, quotes ≥2 details, and
  distinguishes the story's tools from the poem's tools.
- **Reteach theme (RL.6.2):** quiz Q1–Q3 missed → replay
  Lesson 01's modeled example 1 with "The Spare Key"; use
  Practice A.
- **Reteach plot (RL.6.3):** quiz Q4–Q5 missed → re-map "The
  Tryout" on the mountain together; use Practice B.
- **Reteach craft (RL.6.4–6.5):** quiz Q6–Q8 missed → redo
  the *cozy/cramped* launch and Lesson 03 modeled example 2;
  use Practice C.
- **Reteach POV (RL.6.6):** quiz Q9 missed → return to the
  streetlamp image; each narrator names one thing they cannot
  know; use Practice D.
- **Reteach comparison (RL.6.7/6.9):** quiz Q10 missed →
  re-do the Lesson 05 T-chart on a single paragraph before
  attempting cross-text comparison.

## Discussion facilitation

- Fishbowl (Session 4): "Did Dev change, or did he just get
  lucky?" — inner circle argues with quotes; outer circle
  tracks evidence.
- Read-aloud (Session 9): after the T-chart, ask "Which
  version would you give to someone who *hates* reading, and
  what would they miss?" — surfaces the no-winner point.
- Project talks (Session 12): one warm comment + one
  evidence question per talk ("Which detail convinced you?").
