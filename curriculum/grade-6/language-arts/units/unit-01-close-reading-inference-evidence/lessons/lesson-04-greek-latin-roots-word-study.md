# Lesson 04 — Word Study: Greek and Latin Roots Unlock Hard Words

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can break a long word into its root
and affixes, figure out its meaning, read it smoothly, and fix
myself when the meaning breaks down."
**Time:** 40 minutes · **Materials:** this lesson; the [unit word
sets](../README.md#unit-word-sets-authored-for-this-unit-reused-across-lessons-and-practice);
word cards the adult writes by hand; pencil and paper.
**Standards:** RF.6.3, RF.6.4; L.6.4.b, L.6.6.

## Launch (5 min)

"English stole word parts from Latin and Greek — and it stole the
*same* parts hundreds of times. If you learn eight roots today,
you unlock hundreds of words. Watch: *transport, portable,
import, export* — what piece repeats, and what do all four have to
do with?" (*port-* = "carry"; all four involve carrying things
somewhere. The adult writes the four words and circles *port*.)

## Readiness check (3 min)

"Break *preview* into prefix + base and assemble the meaning."
(*pre-* "before" + *view* "look at" = look at before. Reviews
grade-5 affix work; the adult checks the assemble step, not just
the split.)

## Explicit teaching

A **root** is the core word piece that carries the main meaning;
**affixes** (prefixes and suffixes) attach to it and adjust the
meaning. The detective method from Lesson 01 works on words too:

1. **Find the root** (cover the affixes; what core piece is left?).
2. **Name the root's meaning** from today's set.
3. **Assemble:** affix meaning + root meaning (+ suffix meaning).
4. **Check in a sentence** — does the assembled meaning fit?

Today's eight roots (from the unit word sets):

| Root | Means | Memory hook |
|---|---|---|
| dict- | say | *dictate* — say the words for someone to write |
| spect- | look, see | *inspect* — look into closely |
| struct- | build | *construct* — build up |
| graph- | write | *autograph* — your own writing (signature) |
| port- | carry | *transport* — carry across |
| ject- | throw | *reject* — throw back |
| tract- | pull, draw | *attract* — pull toward |
| form- | shape | *transform* — shape across (change shape) |

**The meaning test** (from grade 5, still the law): cover the
affix — if the leftover is not a real root or word, it is not
the affix. *Port* + *able* = "can be carried" — real. *Por* +
*table* in *portable* would be silly; the root is *port*.

### Modeled example 1 — *inspect*

*Adult writes* inspect. "Cover the front: *in-* ('into') +
*spect* ('look, see'). Assemble: 'look into.' Check: 'The
mechanic will inspect the engine before the trip.' Look into
the engine closely — fits."

### Modeled example 2 — *destruction*

*Adult writes* destruction. "Three pieces: *de-* ('down, away')
+ *struct* ('build') + *-ion* ('act of'). Assemble: 'the act of
un-building' — tearing down. Check: 'The storm caused the
destruction of the pier.' Yes — built things coming down."

### Modeled example 3 — *predict*

*Adult writes* predict. "*pre-* ('before') + *dict* ('say') =
'say before.' Check: 'She predicted rain for Saturday.' Saying it
before it happens — fits. Same *dict-* as *dictate* — one root,
two words, zero memorization."

### The self-correction routine (RF.6.4)

Fluent readers catch their own mistakes with this loop:

1. **Stop** — something didn't make sense.
2. **Reread** the sentence slowly.
3. **Ask:** "Did I misread a word part?" (re-split the root)
   or "Did I skip a word?"
4. **Fix and continue** — then reread once more to confirm.

*Modeled:* reading "The scientist will *inspect* the samples,"
a reader says "in-SPEKT" then "in-SPECKT"... stops: "that doesn't
sound like a word I know." Re-splits: *in-* + *spect* ("look") —
"oh, in-SPECT, look into." Continues, rereads the sentence.
**Self-correction is not failure; it is the skill.**

### Academic verbs of inference (L.6.6)

Six verbs, used not just defined:

- **infer** — the reader figures out from clues ("I *infer* the
  dog was hungry.")
- **imply** — the text hints without stating ("The empty bowl
  *implies* hunger.")
- **suggest** — points toward ("The data *suggest* morning is
  busiest.")
- **indicate** — shows or points to ("Paw prints *indicate* a dog
  passed through.")
- **conclude** — decides after weighing evidence ("I *conclude*
  she was worried, not angry.")
- **demonstrate** — shows clearly with evidence ("The squeeze
  *demonstrates* affection.")

Rule of thumb: the **text implies/suggests/indicates**; the
**reader infers/concludes**. *Demonstrate* needs the strongest
evidence of all.

## Guided practice (adult does it with the learner; 8 tasks)

1. "Split *transport* and assemble." (*trans-* "across" + *port*
   "carry" = carry across. **Hint:** "What crossed the ocean?")
2. "Split *rejected* and assemble." (*re-* "back" + *ject*
   "throw" + *-ed* = thrown back. **Hint:** "What happens to a
   rejected idea?")
3. "What does *autograph* mean from its parts?" (*auto-* "self"
   + *graph* "write" = self-writing — a signature. **Hint:**
   "What do you write when someone asks for yours?")
4. "Which root is in *attraction*? Assemble." (*tract* "pull" —
   "the act of pulling toward." **Hint:** "What pulls a magnet?")
5. "Read this aloud, then fix yourself: 'The workers will
   *construct* a new bridge.' Say what tripped you, if
   anything." (Practices the stop–reread–fix loop aloud.)
6. "Fill the verb: 'The paw prints ___ that a dog came
   through.'" (*indicate* — the prints point to it. **Hint:**
   "Who is doing the pointing — the prints or you?")
7. "Fill the verb: 'After weighing the squeeze and the joke, I
   ___ she was worried.'" (*conclude* — decided after evidence.
   **Hint:** "Which verb means 'decide after weighing'?")
8. Error-analysis: "A reader says *portable* means 'a table for
   pouring.' What went wrong?" (Split at the wrong place —
   *port-* is the root "carry"; *por* + *table* fails the
   meaning test. **Hint:** "Cover *port-*: is *able* a real
   suffix?")

## Independent practice (6 tasks)

1. Split and assemble: *prediction, spectator, instructor,
   export.* (One line each: parts + assembled meaning.)
2. Use each in a sentence that shows its meaning: *inspect,
   transform.*
3. Write two sentences about "The Quiet Seat" — one using
   *imply* (about the text), one using *infer* (about you,
   the reader).
4. A reader meets *unpredictable* and freezes. Write the exact
   self-correction script they should say to themselves
   (stop → reread → re-split → fix → confirm).
5. Which academic verb fits? "The empty bowl ___ the dog ate."
   Explain your choice in one sentence. (*suggests* — points
   toward; hunger isn't proven. Accept *implies* with reasoning.)
6. Find one word in today's lesson containing *struct-* that was
   NOT in the table, split it, and assemble it. (*instruct*,
   *destruction*, *construct* — any one.)

## Application

Make six word cards (root on front, meaning + two example words
on back) for *dict-, spect-, struct-, graph-, port-, ject-*.
Then write a four-sentence "root detective report" on the word
*manuscript*: split it (*manu-* "hand" + *script* "write" — the
adult supplies *manu-* = "hand" as a bonus root), assemble, and
check it in a sentence. Keep the cards for the Week 6 review
game.

## Exit check

"Teach me the four-step word detective method using the word
*television*. Then tell me which step catches you when you
misread." (*tele-* "far" + *vision* "see" = see far; the
*check-in-a-sentence* step catches misreadings.)

## Supports, alternate response mode, extension

- **Support:** the adult pre-writes the eight roots with meanings
  on a bookmark the learner keeps; the learner points to the root
  before splitting.
- **Alternate response mode:** the learner may build words with
  letter tiles or magnetic word-part cards instead of writing
  splits.
- **Extension:** find *scrib/script-* ("write") words in a
  dictionary (describe, prescribe, inscription) and add them to
  the card set with assembled meanings.

## Teacher note

Misconceptions: (1) splitting at the wrong boundary (*por* +
*table*) — always run the meaning test; (2) using *infer* for
what the text does — drill "text implies, reader infers"; (3)
reading fast past nonsense — reward every caught self-correction
out loud. The root cards become the Week 6 review game deck.
