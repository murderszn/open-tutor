# Project — Case File: The Missing Seed Packets

**Type:** evidence report (investigation) · **Sessions:** 11–12 in
the unit pacing (Week 5) · **Standards:** RL.6.1, W.6.10 (write
routinely)
**Deliverables:** (1) an annotated passage, (2) a written evidence
report, (3) a 2-minute oral summary.
**Materials:** this project sheet; the passage below; pencil and
paper; the Lesson 04 root cards (optional, for the report's word
choices).

## The case

Read the passage twice. First read: enjoy it. Second read: mark
every detail that could be evidence — underline exact words, and
write E (explicit) or I (inference) in the margin.

### "Case File: The Missing Seed Packets" (original)

> **Paragraph 1.** The community garden shed had one rule, taped
> inside the door in Mrs. Abernathy's careful handwriting: *Take
> only what you signed for.* On Saturday morning, three packets
> of sunflower seeds were missing from the shelf, and nobody had
> signed for them.
>
> **Paragraph 2.** Mrs. Abernathy found the shed door unlocked,
> which was strange, because she always locked it on Friday
> nights. A muddy footprint — small, with a zigzag tread — led
> from the door to the shelf and back again. The footprint did
> not match her boots.
>
> **Paragraph 3.** On the shelf, everything was neat except for
> one thing: a packet of marigold seeds lay on its side, and a
> few marigold seeds had spilled onto the shelf. Sunflower seeds
> were gone; marigold seeds were merely messy.
>
> **Paragraph 4.** That afternoon, ten-year-old Theo knocked on
> Mrs. Abernathy's door holding a paper bag. "I took the
> sunflower seeds," he said, looking at his sneakers. "My
> grandma's birthday is tomorrow and I wanted to plant them with
> her. I meant to sign, but the pen was out of ink, and then I
> got — I got in a hurry." In the bag were three empty
> sunflower-seed packets and a muddy sneaker with a zigzag tread.

## The report

Write an evidence report (8–12 sentences) with four parts:

1. **Claim:** Who took the sunflower seeds? State it in one
   sentence.
2. **Evidence:** Cite at least **three** pieces of textual
   evidence (exact quotes or close paraphrases with paragraph
   numbers) that support the claim.
3. **Ruled-out alternative:** Name one *other* inference a
   reader could make (for example, about the marigold seeds or
   the unlocked door) and explain, with evidence, why it is
   weaker.
4. **Word craft:** Use at least two academic verbs correctly
   (*infer, conclude, suggest, imply, indicate, demonstrate*).

## Oral summary (Session 12)

Summarize the case in 2 minutes for the adult: claim, two best
evidence quotes, and the ruled-out alternative. Speak in formal,
complete sentences. The adult scores with the rubric below.

## Rubric (adult scores; 2-1-0 per criterion, 10 points total)

| Criterion | 2 — Strong | 1 — Developing | 0 — Missing/Weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claim | Clear, one-sentence, answers the question | Present but vague or multi-part | No clear claim |
| Evidence (×2 weight) | 3+ exact quotes/paraphrases, all with paragraph numbers, all relevant | 2 citations, or missing paragraph numbers, or one irrelevant | 0–1 citations, or quotes don't support the claim |
| Ruled-out alternative | Names a plausible alternative and defeats it with evidence | Names an alternative but the rebuttal lacks evidence | No alternative considered |
| Academic verbs | 2+ used correctly (*text implies / reader infers*) | 1 correct, or 2 with one misused | None, or *infer/imply* swapped |
| Oral summary | Claim + 2 quotes + alternative, formal complete sentences | Missing one element or informal fragments | Not delivered or off-topic |

**Scoring note:** the Evidence row counts double (max 4 points);
total = 10. 8–10 = ready for the unit assessment; 5–7 = revise
one section with the adult; below 5 = re-read the passage with
the adult and rebuild the report together.

## Accessibility and safety

- The learner may dictate the report for the adult to scribe;
  the oral summary may be recorded instead of delivered live.
- All characters are fictional; the "investigation" is a reading
  exercise, not a real accusation — the adult says so out loud
  before starting.

## Teacher note (summary; full guidance in teacher-guide.md)

The intended claim is that Theo took the sunflower seeds — he
confesses it outright in paragraph 4, which makes this a
*confirmation* case, not a whodunit: the skill being tested is
evidence assembly, not guessing. Strong reports cite the zigzag
tread match (paragraphs 2 and 4), the three empty packets
(paragraph 4), and the unlocked door or unsigned log (paragraphs
1–2). The best ruled-out alternatives address the marigold
spill (messy but present — a red herring) or the possibility of
a second visitor (no evidence for one). Full scoring guidance:
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md#project).
