# Unit 01 Culminating Assessment

**Adult-supervised, ~40 minutes.** Matched to the unit's five
objectives. Original passages; no passage repeats unit lessons.
Full solutions and scoring in the
[answer key](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment). Total: 30 points.

**Directions to the learner:** For every inference, use the cite
routine: state the inference, quote the exact words, give the
paragraph number. Answer in complete sentences.

## Passage 1 — Literary (original)

> **Paragraph 1.** The old library closed at five, but the side
> door's latch had been broken since spring, and Elena slipped
> in at half past. Dust hung in the last bars of sunlight. She
> went straight to the biography shelf, the way she always did,
> and pulled down the thick book about the woman who flew
> alone across the ocean.
>
> **Paragraph 2.** She read until the light went thin and gray.
> Twice she heard footsteps in the hall and froze, the book half
> open on her lap. The second time, Mr. Okafor the librarian
> appeared in the doorway with his keys. He looked at her for a
> long moment. Then he set a small brass lamp on the table
> beside her and walked out without a word.

### Questions (14 points)

1. (2 pts) Give two explicit facts from paragraph 1, each with
   its exact quote.
2. (3 pts) What can you infer about how often Elena visits the
   library? Cite two evidence details and explain your
   reasoning in one sentence.
3. (3 pts) How does Elena feel when she hears the footsteps?
   Cite two evidence details.
4. (3 pts) What does Mr. Okafor setting down the lamp suggest
   about him? Give the inference, two evidence quotes, and one
   sentence explaining the connection.
5. (3 pts) A reader infers: "Mr. Okafor is angry that Elena is
   there after hours." Evaluate this inference: does the
   passage support it, weaken it, or say nothing about it?
   Explain with evidence.

## Passage 2 — Informational (original; facts verified 2026-10-12)

> **Paragraph 1.** A wood frog in Alaska can survive being
> frozen solid. As temperatures fall, the frog's liver floods
> its body with glucose — a sugar — which acts like antifreeze
> in a car radiator, keeping ice from forming inside its cells.
> The frog's heart stops. It does not breathe. For months, it
> is a frogsicle.
>
> **Paragraph 2.** When spring warms the pond edge, the frog
> thaws from the inside out. Its heart stutters, then beats.
> Within a day it is hopping — with no damage to its brain,
> muscles, or organs. Scientists study this trick because the
> same chemistry might one day help preserve human organs for
> transplant.

### Questions (10 points)

6. (2 pts) What does the glucose do, according to the passage?
   Quote the exact comparison the author uses.
7. (3 pts) What can you infer about why scientists study the
   wood frog? Cite two evidence details.
8. (2 pts) The author calls the frozen frog "a frogsicle."
   What does this word choice suggest about the author's
   attitude? One inference, one quote.
9. (3 pts) A reader infers: "The frog is dead all winter and
   comes back to life in spring." Does the passage support or
   weaken this? Explain with two evidence quotes.

## Word study (6 points)

10. (2 pts) Split *transported* and *rejection*. For each: name
    the root, its meaning, and the assembled word meaning.
11. (2 pts) Complete with the correct academic verb and
    explain your choice: "The thawing heart ___ that the frog
    was never truly dead." (*suggests / demonstrates* — pick
    one.)
12. (2 pts) "The instructions were *explicit*: no food past
    this point." Use the surrounding words to define
    *explicit*. Name the clue type.

*Source note for Passage 2:* wood-frog freeze tolerance is well documented —
glucose and urea act as cryoprotectants ("antifreeze"); ice forms
*outside* cells while cell interiors are protected; the heart stops
and breathing stops during freezing; the frog thaws and revives in
spring; researchers study the mechanism for organ-preservation
insights. Facts checked 2026-10-12 against Discover Wildlife's
wood-frog explainer
(https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/amphibians/wood-frog-facts).
The passage itself is original.
