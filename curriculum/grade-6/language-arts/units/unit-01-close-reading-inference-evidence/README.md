# Unit 01 — Close Reading: Inference and Textual Evidence

**Grade:** 6 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 3–6
**Backlog:** [issue #32](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/32), section U01
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

Reading is detective work. Some clues sit right on the page — a muddy
paw print, an open door, an empty bowl. Others have to be assembled
from those clues: *someone's dog came home hungry.* In this unit the
learner practices the two moves strong readers make hundreds of times
a day: naming exactly what a text says, and drawing careful inferences
from it. Every inference gets handcuffed to its evidence — quoted or
closely paraphrased words from the text itself — so claims stay honest.
The same weeks build the word-solving tools close reading demands:
Greek and Latin roots that unlock long words, context clues, the
self-correction routine that catches misreadings, and the academic
verbs (infer, conclude, suggest, imply) that let a reader say what the
text *means* without claiming it said what it didn't.

## Essential question

How do I prove that what I *think* a text means is really *in* the text?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks (track weeks 1–2) and the grade-5 language
arts track:

- Read unfamiliar multisyllabic words using common prefixes
  (un-, re-, pre-, dis-, mis-, in-) and suffixes (-ful, -less, -tion,
  -able, -ness, -ly); reread to smooth out rough spots.
- Quote accurately from a text; summarize a story or article in a few
  sentences distinct from personal opinion.
- Use sentence-level context clues (definitions, examples,
  restatements) to guess unknown words; beginning dictionary routines.
- Participate in short prepared discussions, building on a partner's
  idea.

The diagnostic re-teaches nothing here; Lesson 01 re-teaches the
explicit/inference distinction explicitly rather than assuming it is
secure, and Lesson 04 re-teaches the root set rather than assuming
the grade-5 affix work transferred.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Distinguish what a text says **explicitly** from **inferences**
   drawn from it, and **cite textual evidence** (exact quotation or
   close paraphrase with paragraph reference) to support both
   (RL.6.1, RI.6.1).
2. Make and justify inferences about **literary** texts — characters'
   feelings, motives, and relationships — with at least two pieces of
   textual evidence per inference (RL.6.1).
3. Make and justify inferences in **informational** texts using stated
   facts, numbers, and word choices as evidence; explain how evidence
   supports or weakens an inference (RI.6.1).
4. Decode unfamiliar multisyllabic words using **Greek and Latin
   affixes and roots**; read grade-level text with accuracy and
   appropriate rate, **self-correcting** when meaning breaks down
   (RF.6.3, RF.6.4).
5. Clarify unknown words with **context clues** and verify with
   reference materials; use academic verbs of analysis and inference —
   *infer, conclude, suggest, imply, indicate, demonstrate* — precisely
   in discussion and writing (L.6.4, L.6.6).

## Unit passages (original; written for this unit)

All passages below are original curriculum-authored texts — no
worksheet, textbook, or web article is reproduced. The two
informational passages rest on facts verified 2026-10-12 (see
Standards and source notes); the literary passages are fiction.

- **"The Lantern in the Window"** (literary, ~160 words) — Lesson 01.
  A girl, a dark porch, a lit window: the explicit/inference
  workbench.
- **"The Quiet Seat"** (literary, ~210 words) — Lesson 02.
  A new student, a lunchroom, an empty chair: inferring feelings and
  motives from actions.
- **"The Dance That Points to Flowers"** (informational, ~200 words)
  — Lesson 03. How honeybees use the waggle dance to tell hive-mates
  where food is.
- **"Rain Check"** (literary, ~170 words) — Lesson 05 Part A. A
  rained-out game and a gruff coach: independent inference practice.
- **"Why Your Ears Pop on Airplanes"** (informational, ~190 words) —
  Lesson 05 Part B. Pressure, eardrums, and the Eustachian tube.
- **"Case File: The Missing Seed Packets"** (literary mystery,
  ~230 words) — project. The learner writes an evidence report.

## Unit word sets (authored for this unit; reused across lessons)

**Greek/Latin roots (meaning → example words):**
- dict- ("say"): dictate, predict, dictionary, verdict
- spect- ("look, see"): inspect, respect, spectator, spectacle
- struct- ("build"): construct, structure, instruct, destruction
- graph- ("write"): autograph, paragraph, biography, photograph
- port- ("carry"): transport, portable, import, export
- ject- ("throw"): reject, eject, project, inject
- tract- ("pull, draw"): attract, subtract, tractor, traction
- form- ("shape"): transform, inform, format, formula

**Academic verbs (use, don't just define):** infer ("figure out
from clues"), conclude ("decide after weighing evidence"),
suggest ("point toward without stating"), imply ("hint at without
saying"), indicate ("show or point to"), demonstrate ("show clearly
with evidence").

**Context-clue practice words (solved in lessons, never memorized
cold):** reluctant, frigid, luminous, quarrel, debris, tranquil,
astonish, perish.

All sets are original lists; no worksheet or vocabulary program is
reproduced.

evidence practice → word-study practice → review and discussion.

## Standards and source notes

Standards codes and descriptions follow the track's verified
crosswalk (scope-and-sequence §12, verified 2026-10-05 against the
published Common Core State Standards for English Language Arts,
thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/): RL.6.1, RI.6.1, RF.6.3, RF.6.4,
L.6.4, L.6.6. The framework is a subject reference only; nothing
here claims state adoption, accreditation, or complete alignment.

Factual basis for informational passages (checked 2026-10-12):
- Waggle dance: performed by returning honeybee foragers on the
  vertical comb inside the dark hive; the straight "waggle run's"
  angle against vertical matches the angle between the sun's
  direction and the food source outside; the run's duration encodes
  distance (longer run = farther food); decoded by Karl von Frisch,
  Nobel Prize 1973; bees gauge flight distance from the visual
  "optic flow" of ground passing beneath them. (PNAS 10.1073/pnas.2213068120; fauna-focused explainer reviewed same day.)
- Ear popping: air pressure falls as altitude rises; the eardrum
  separates outer ear from the air-filled middle ear; the Eustachian
  tube connects the middle ear to the back of the throat and
  equalizes pressure when it opens; swallowing, yawning, and chewing
  open the tube — the "pop" is the sudden equalization. (KidsHealth
  "Flying and Your Child's Ears"; Popular Science audiologist
  explainer; University of Arizona atmospheric-sciences course page;
  all reviewed 2026-10-12.)
