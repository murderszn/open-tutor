# Resource Pack — Unit 01: Close Reading, Inference, and Textual Evidence

**Goal (adult planning judgment):** Give the guiding adult free,
no-account videos and references that reinforce this unit's five
objectives — distinguishing explicit text from inference, citing
evidence in literary and informational passages, Greek/Latin root
word study, context clues, and academic inference verbs — for a
grade-6 learner (ages 11–12). Videos supplement the lessons; they
never replace explicit teaching, practice, or the adult's check.

All external links below were opened and confirmed on-topic and
loading on the stated check date. Runtimes and captions were not
checked — preview before assigning. YouTube serves ads; use an
ad-blocked or supervised session with the learner.

## Web references (6 verified)

1. [Inferencing — Reading Rockets classroom strategy
   page](https://www.readingrockets.org/classroom/classroom-strategies/inferencing)
   — research-backed strategy guide: what inferencing is, how to
   teach it (clues + background knowledge, "It says, I say, and
   so"), differentiation, and inference graphic organizers.
   Checked 2026-10-12: page loads, on-topic, full strategy with
   references. Adult-facing; supports Lessons 01–02.
2. [Inferences Worksheets —
   EreadingWorksheets.com](https://www.ereadingworksheets.com/free-reading-worksheets/reading-comprehension-worksheets/inferences-worksheets/)
   — ten free inference worksheet sets (grades 3–8); every item
   requires answering inferential questions *and* explaining the
   answer with textual evidence — the same routine this unit
   teaches. Checked 2026-10-12: page loads, free, on-topic.
   Optional extra practice; never a substitute for the lessons.
3. [Using Historical Fiction to Learn About the Civil War —
   ReadWriteThink (NCTE)](https://www.readwritethink.org/classroom-resources/lesson-plans/using-historical-fiction-learn?tab=1)
   — lesson plan whose core routine is the "How Do You Know?"
   think-aloud: stop at inferential moments, ask the question,
   record inferences on a chart, revise as new evidence
   arrives. Checked 2026-10-12: full lesson plan loads.
   Adult-facing; the think-aloud routine mirrors this unit's
   cite routine (Lesson 02).
4. [Flip-a-Chip: Examining Affixes and Roots to Build
   Vocabulary — ReadWriteThink
   (NCTE)](https://www.readwritethink.org/classroom-resources/lesson-plans/flip-chip-examining-affixes?tab=4)
   — lesson where learners combine affix/root "chips" into words
   and place them in a context-clue paragraph. Checked
   2026-10-11: page loads, full lesson with extensions.
   Supports Lesson 04 root work.
5. [The Honeybee Waggle Dance: Code, Sun Compass and Proof —
   FaunaTV](https://faunatv.com/en/blog/honeybee-waggle-dance)
   — accessible explainer confirming the Lesson 03 facts: the
   waggle run's angle against vertical encodes direction
   relative to the sun, duration encodes distance, decoded by
   Karl von Frisch (Nobel 1973). Reviewed 2026-10-12.
   Adult reference for the bee passage's source notes.
6. [Flying and Your Child's Ears — KidsHealth (Nemours
   KidsHealth)](https://kidshealth.org/AetnaBetterHealthKentucky/en/parents/flying-ears.html?WT.ac=pairedLink)
   — parent-facing explainer confirming the Lesson 05 facts:
   the Eustachian tube equalizes middle-ear pressure; yawning
   and swallowing open it; narrower tubes in children make
   popping harder. Reviewed 2026-10-12. Adult reference for the
   ears passage's source notes.
