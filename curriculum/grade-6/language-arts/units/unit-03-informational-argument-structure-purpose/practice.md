# Practice — Mixed Informational Reading Practice

**Use:** Sessions 2, 6, 8, and 14 (or any reteach session).
**Standards:** RI.6.2, RI.6.3, RI.6.4, RI.6.5, RI.6.6, RI.6.8, RI.6.9.
**How to use:** work one section at a time; the adult checks the
same day. All practice passages below are original and fictional;
all numbers are invented practice data.

## A. Central-idea sorting (Session 2; Lesson 01)

Read each statement about "Riverbend's River" (Lesson 01). Label
it **C** (central idea), **S** (supporting detail), or **O**
(off-topic for a summary). Give a one-phrase reason for each.

1. Over time, Riverbend's river changed from a neglected waterway
   into a shared community project because residents and the town
   kept working on it.
2. The river is twelve miles long inside the town limits.
3. The Saturday crew grew from a dozen people to more than eighty
   volunteers.
4. Rivers are important all over the world.
5. The town council paid for bins, plantings, and a footbridge.
6. Herons have returned to the reeds.
7. The river changed because neighbors kept showing up and the
   town kept investing.
8. Anglers fish from the footbridge at dawn.
9. The best time to visit Riverbend is spring.
10. The river did not change because one person ordered it changed.

## C. Argument relevance check (Session 8; Lesson 04)

For each evidence item offered for the claim in brackets, judge
**relevant** or **irrelevant**, with a one-sentence reason.

Claim: "The riverwalk will be popular."

1. On one Saturday in May, 300 walkers crossed the footbridge.
2. The river is twelve miles long inside the town limits.
3. Neighbors on three streets said they would use a riverwalk.
4. The state grant will pay half the construction cost.
5. A new bench design was chosen for the riverwalk.
6. The Saturday crew has more than eighty volunteers.
7. Homes near the river may change in value.
8. The footbridge has no benches and is narrow.

## D. Two-account comparison drill (Session 14; Lesson 05)

Use Texts A and B from Lesson 05.

1. Which text is written in the first person ("I")? Which in the
   third person ("she")?
2. Name one feeling Text A reports that Text B does not report.
3. Name one role Text B reports that Text A does not report.
4. Do the texts disagree about any fact? Answer yes or no, with
   one reason.
5. Write one sentence that both texts would agree with.

## E. Paragraph-function drill (Session 14; Lesson 02)

Use "Mara Ellison and the River" (Lesson 02).

1. Which paragraph introduces Ellison? Which illustrates her?
   Which elaborates her role? Which reflects on the project?
2. If paragraph 3 were removed, what would a reader no longer
   know about Ellison's effect on the project? Answer in one
   sentence.
3. Write one sentence explaining what paragraph 4 adds that
   paragraphs 1–3 do not.
