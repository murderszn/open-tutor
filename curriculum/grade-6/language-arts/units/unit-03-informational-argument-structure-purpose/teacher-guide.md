# Teacher Guide — Unit 03 (Adult Only)

**Role of this guide:** pacing, misconception, support, and
readiness guidance. It contains **no student answers**. All
solutions live in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) (adult only).
Do not show learners either file.

## Pacing notes (16 sessions, ~40 minutes each)

- **Week 11 (Sessions 1–4):** Lesson 01 must land before Lesson
  02: learners need the central-idea sentence habit before they
  trace how a person is built across paragraphs. Session 2's
  sorting drill is the diagnostic for Lesson 01 — if sorting
  fails, re-teach with paragraph 5 of "Riverbend's River" (the
  most explicit central-idea statement) before moving on.
- **Week 12 (Sessions 5–8):** Lesson 03's two-text contrast is
  the unit's lightest lift; protect Session 6 (word meanings)
  from being squeezed, because RI.6.4 errors reappear in the
  assessment. Lesson 04 needs the full 40 minutes: do not rush
  the relevance/sufficiency distinction, and insist on the
  three verdict words (supported / partly / unsupported) rather
  than "good / bad."
- **Week 13 (Sessions 9–12):** Session 9's image discussion must
  include the text-only alternative for every learner, whether or
  not they can see the image — the alternative is the lesson's
  equity check, not a fallback. Project Sessions 10–12: read the
  rubric aloud before Session 10's planning; learners who plan
  against the rubric rarely need a full rewrite.
- **Week 14 (Sessions 13–16):** score the quiz the same day and
  build Session 14's reteach list from it, standard by standard.
  The unit assessment (Session 15) uses a new passage on purpose:
  do not pre-teach "The Mill Pond Decision" in Session 14.

## Misconceptions and reteaching moves

| Misconception | What you will hear | Reteach move |
|---|---|---|
| Central idea = topic | "It's about the river." | Ask: "What does the text *say about* the river, across all paragraphs?" Use Lesson 01's paragraph 5 as the model sentence. |
| Summary = opinion | "The river is the best." | Cover the summary and ask of each sentence: "Does the text say this?" Cut what fails the test. |
| Every paragraph weighs the same | "Paragraph 1 is most important because it's first." | Use the removal test from Lesson 02: "What does the text lose without this paragraph?" |
| Purpose comes from topic | "It's about volunteering, so it persuades." | Cover the topic; ask "What do the *words* do?" Use Lesson 03's signal hunt (commands, praise/blame words, direct address). |
| Agreement = evaluation | "I agree, so the argument is strong." | Separate the two judgments explicitly: "You may agree *and* the argument may be weak. Judge the evidence, not your vote." |
| Any number = proof | "It has a number, so it's supported." | Apply the relevance test, then the sufficiency test: one day, one season, one group is thin evidence even when the number is real (here, invented practice data). |
| Memoir = true, biography = formal-true | "She was there, so it's true." / "It's a biography, so it's official." | Ask: "What can this source see from where it stands, and what can it not see?" Compare selection, not sincerity. |
| Illustration = photograph | "The picture proves the cleanup happened." | State plainly: the image is an AI-generated illustration for this lesson. It shows a *kind* of scene; it names no one and dates nothing. |

## Differentiation and supports

- **Reading access:** read every passage aloud once before
  silent reading; chunk Lesson 04's article paragraph by
  paragraph; pre-teach the vocabulary list in the unit README
  with examples first, definitions second.
- **Language access:** sentence frames are in the project file;
  the same frames work for Lessons 01, 04, and 05 ("The central
  idea is ___ because ___." / "The claim is ___ because the
  evidence is ___.").
- **Response modes:** oral answers, dictated reports, and the
  three-box / three-stone drawings in Lessons 01–02 are full
  responses, not shortcuts. The rubric scores content.
- **Extensions:** each lesson carries one; the strongest is
  Lesson 04's counterclaim paragraph and Lesson 05's council-minute
  account. Offer extensions only after the core exit check is met.
- **Accessibility:** every image activity has a text-only
  alternative; every multimedia suggestion in the resource pack
  is adult-previewed, never assigned unpreviewed.

## Readiness and reteach signals

- **Ready for Unit 04** when the learner can, without hints:
  state a central idea with two cited details; trace one claim
  to a verdict with a relevance-or-sufficiency reason; and name
  one presentation difference between two accounts with a
  position explanation.
- **Reteach Lesson 01** if quiz Q1–Q2 or assessment Q1–Q2 miss:
  return to paragraph 5 of "Riverbend's River" and rebuild the
  central-idea sentence together.
- **Reteach Lesson 04** if quiz Q7–Q8 or assessment Q7/Q10 miss:
  return to the Lesson 04 readiness check (one Saturday ≠
  year-round popularity) and re-run the trace table on one claim
  only, slowly.
- **Reteach Lesson 05** if quiz Q9 or assessment Q8–Q9 miss:
  return to the position question and the image-limit discussion
  in Lesson 05's launch.
- **Portfolio note:** a project scoring 7–8 with a complete oral
  summary is portfolio-ready. File the report privately; do not
  publish learner work in the repository.

## What this unit does not do

- It does not teach grammar (Unit 04), narrative writing
  (Unit 05), argument writing (Unit 06), or research (Unit 07).
  When learners ask "but how do I *write* an argument?", note
  the question for Unit 06; this unit's job is to *evaluate*
  arguments, not to draft them.
- It does not use real town data, real people, or copyrighted
  texts. If a learner asks whether Riverbend is real, answer
  plainly: it is fictional, invented for teaching, and that is
  why its numbers are labeled practice data.
