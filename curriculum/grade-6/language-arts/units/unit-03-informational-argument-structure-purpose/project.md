# Project — Two Accounts, One River

**Type:** comparison and argument-evaluation report (written) +
2-minute oral summary.
**Sessions:** 10 (plan), 11 (draft), 12 (revise + present).
**Standards:** RI.6.2, RI.6.3, RI.6.5, RI.6.6, RI.6.8, RI.6.9,
RI.6.7; W.6.2 (informative writing, applied here in report form);
SL.6.4 (present claims and findings logically).

## Task

Write a report (about 300–400 words, 4 parts) that a new reader
could use to understand the Riverbend cleanup from more than one
angle. Use three sources only: Text A (memoir-style excerpt) and
Text B (biography-style excerpt) from Lesson 05, the generated
image in Lesson 05 (or its text-only alternative), and — for Part
4 — the argument article "Build the Riverwalk Now" from Lesson 04.
Do not invent new facts about Riverbend; every factual sentence
must trace to one of those sources.

**Part 1 — Central ideas (about 75 words).** State the central
idea of Text A and the central idea of Text B, each in one
sentence, with one supporting detail cited for each.

**Part 2 — How the presentations differ (about 125 words).**
Explain two differences between the accounts, using selection,
emphasis, or voice. Quote or closely paraphrase one phrase per
difference, and explain each difference by the author's position
(participant vs. observer).

**Part 3 — What the image adds, and what it cannot prove
(about 50 words).** State one thing the image adds that neither
text states, and one thing the image cannot prove. Explain why
the image cannot prove it (illustration, names no one, shows the
present, not the past).

**Part 4 — Argument check (about 100 words).** Evaluate one claim
from "Build the Riverwalk Now" (choose any of the four specific
claims traced in Lesson 04). State the claim, the evidence given,
and your verdict (supported / partly supported / unsupported)
with a reason. Do not argue for or against building the
riverwalk; evaluate the argument.

**Oral summary (2 minutes, Session 12).** Present your report's
four parts in order, using at least two academic verbs (*infer,
conclude, suggest, imply, indicate, demonstrate*) correctly.

## Materials

Lesson 05 texts and image (or text-only alternative); Lesson 04
article; notebook or device for drafting; the rubric below (read
it before drafting).

## Steps

1. **Annotate (Session 10).** Reread Texts A and B. Underline one
   phrase per text that best shows its voice. Circle one
   selection difference. List the image's visible elements from
   the text-only alternative if needed.
2. **Plan (Session 10).** Fill in a four-part plan: central-idea
   sentence per account; two differences with quotes; image
   add / image limit; chosen claim + verdict + reason.
3. **Draft (Session 11).** Write all four parts in order. Put a
   paragraph reference or source label after each cited detail
   (e.g., "Text A," "Lesson 04, paragraph 3").
4. **Revise (Session 12).** Check your draft against the rubric
   line by line. Cut any opinion sentence that no source
   supports. Read the draft aloud once for flow.
5. **Present (Session 12).** Give the 2-minute oral summary. The
   adult notes whether each part is present and whether the two
   academic verbs are used correctly.

## Deliverables

- Written report (300–400 words, 4 labeled parts, sources noted).
- 2-minute oral summary (adult-observed; may be read from notes).

## Rubric (8 points; adult scores; 2 = meets, 1 = partly, 0 = missing or incorrect)

| Criterion | 2 points | 1 point | 0 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Part 1 — Central ideas** | Both central ideas correct, each with a cited supporting detail | One central idea correct, or details missing | Central ideas wrong or absent |
| **Part 2 — Presentation differences** | Two differences explained by author position, each with a quoted or paraphrased phrase | One difference explained, or quotes missing | No explained difference |
| **Part 3 — Image use and limits** | One genuine image addition + one correct limit, with a reason | Only the addition or only the limit | Image treated as proof of a named past event, or part missing |
| **Part 4 — Argument evaluation** | Claim, evidence, and verdict all correct, with a reason tied to relevance or sufficiency | Verdict correct but reason weak, or evidence misstated | Verdict wrong, or the report argues for/against the riverwalk instead of evaluating |

**Scoring note (read carefully):** the four written parts are
scored 2 points each = **8 points total**. The oral summary is
scored separately as **complete / incomplete**: complete means all
four parts were presented in order and two academic verbs were
used correctly. A report scoring 7–8 with a complete oral summary
is ready for the portfolio; 5–6 means revise one part (the adult
names it); below 5 means re-plan with the adult before revising.

## Accessibility and supports

- The report may be dictated to the adult or written with
  speech-to-text; the rubric scores content, not handwriting.
- The image may be replaced entirely by its text-only alternative.
- Sentence frames (optional): "Text A emphasizes ___ because the
  author was a participant, while Text B emphasizes ___ because
  the author was an observer." / "The image adds ___, but it
  cannot prove ___ because ___." / "The claim is ___ because the
  evidence is ___."
