# Unit Assessment — Unit 03 (Week 14, Session 15)

**Time:** about 40 minutes · **Adult-supervised.**
**Standards:** RI.6.2, RI.6.3, RI.6.4, RI.6.5, RI.6.6, RI.6.7,

**Instructions to the learner:** read the new passage carefully.
Answer in your own words; cite paragraph numbers for evidence.
This passage is not previewed in any lesson. It is original and
fictional; all numbers are invented practice data.

## Part A — New passage

> **"The Mill Pond Decision" (original).**
>
> **Paragraph 1.** For years, the old mill pond on Riverbend's
> north edge sat unused. Its wooden dock had rotted, and the
> town posted a sign warning people to stay off it.
>
> **Paragraph 2.** Last spring, a group of residents proposed
> turning the pond into a learning pond, where school groups
> could test water clarity, count pond plants, and record what
> they found. The proposal's author, a retired science teacher
> named Ana Ruiz, wrote that students learn more from a pond
> they can visit than from a pond they only read about.
>
> **Paragraph 3.** Ruiz's proposal gave three reasons. First,
> the pond is within walking distance of two schools. Second,
> a local builders' group offered to rebuild the dock for the
> cost of materials only. Third, Ruiz herself offered to train
> adult volunteers to lead the visits. The proposal did not
> give a total cost, a maintenance plan, or a safety plan for
> visits.
>
> **Paragraph 4.** The town council is now considering the
> proposal. Some council members worry about safety and cost;
> others say the learning value is worth the work. No decision
> has been made yet.
>
> **Paragraph 5.** Whatever the council decides, the pond has
> already done one thing: it has given Riverbend a question
> worth arguing about carefully — what a town owes its children,
> and what it owes its budget.

## Part B — Questions (each 2 points unless noted)

1. (RI.6.2) State the central idea of "The Mill Pond Decision"
   in one sentence.
2. (RI.6.2) Write a three-sentence objective summary of the
   passage. Lose one point for each opinion or new fact added.
3. (RI.6.3) How is Ana Ruiz introduced, illustrated, and
   elaborated in the passage? Answer in three sentences, one per
   stage, citing one detail per stage.
4. (RI.6.5) What does paragraph 5 add that paragraphs 1–4 do not?
   Answer in one or two sentences.
5. (RI.6.4) In paragraph 2, Ruiz says students learn more from a
   pond they can "visit" than from one they only "read about."
   Is "visit" here mainly technical, connotative, or plain
   descriptive? Explain in one sentence.
6. (RI.6.6) What is the author's purpose in paragraph 4 — to
   inform, explain, or persuade? Give one signal.
7. (RI.6.8) Evaluate this claim from the proposal: "The learning
   pond will be cheap to build." Using only paragraph 3, is the
   claim supported, partly supported, or unsupported? Give one
   reason. (3 points)
8. (RI.6.9) Suppose Ruiz later wrote a memoir saying, "I
   proposed the pond because I missed teaching." Name one
   difference you would expect between that memoir and this
   passage, and explain it by author position in one sentence.
9. (RI.6.7) Suppose the lesson image (Lesson 05) were placed
   beside this passage. Name one thing the image would add to a
   reader's understanding of Riverbend, and one thing it could
   not show about the mill pond decision. (2 points)
10. (RI.6.8 / writing, 6 points) Write a five-sentence evaluation
    paragraph about Ruiz's proposal as presented in the passage:
    sentence 1 states the proposal; sentences 2–4 evaluate one
    reason each (walking distance, builders' offer, volunteer
    training) as supported, partly supported, or unsupported,
    with a reason; sentence 5 gives an overall judgment about
    whether the proposal, *as presented*, is ready for a
    decision. Do not argue for or against building the pond;
    evaluate the case as presented.

**Total: 25 points.** 22–25: unit objectives met; 17–21: review
the missed standards before Unit 04; below 17: reteach the two
lowest standards with the adult before moving on.
