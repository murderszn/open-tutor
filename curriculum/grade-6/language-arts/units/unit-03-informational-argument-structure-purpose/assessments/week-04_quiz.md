# Formative Quiz — Unit 03 (Week 14, Session 13)

**Time:** about 25 minutes · **Adult scores the same day.**
**Standards:** RI.6.2, RI.6.3, RI.6.5, RI.6.6, RI.6.8, RI.6.9.
**Instructions to the learner:** answer in your own words unless
a question asks you to choose. Cite a paragraph or text when a
question asks for evidence. This quiz is formative: it shows what
to reteach, not a final grade.

Read the short passage first. It is original and fictional; all
numbers are invented practice data.

> **"The Bank Path" (original, 4 paragraphs).**
>
> **Paragraph 1.** After the footbridge was built, Riverbend
> volunteers marked a bank path with wooden posts so walkers
> would stay off the newly planted reeds.
>
> **Paragraph 2.** The posts worked at first. Walkers followed
> the path, and the reeds grew tall in their first summer.
> Then, in the second summer, heavy rain washed out two posts,
> and walkers began cutting across the reeds again.
>
> **Paragraph 3.** Mara Ellison's yearly note that autumn
> recorded the washed-out posts and recommended replacing them
> with stone markers. The council followed her recommendation
> the next spring, and the path has held since.
>
> **Paragraph 4.** The bank path story shows the same pattern as
> the river project itself: a good start, a setback, a recorded
> observation, and a fix that lasts — because someone wrote it
> down.

## Questions

1. (RI.6.2) In one sentence, state the central idea of "The Bank
   Path." (2 points)
2. (RI.6.2) Write a two-sentence objective summary of the passage.
   Lose one point for each opinion or new fact added. (2 points)
3. (RI.6.3) Trace Ellison's role in this passage: what does she
   do, and what result follows? Answer in two sentences, citing
   one detail. (2 points)
4. (RI.6.5) What does paragraph 4 add that paragraphs 1–3 do not?
   Answer in one or two sentences. (2 points)
5. (RI.6.6) Is the author's purpose in this passage mainly to
   inform, explain, or persuade? Give one signal for your answer.
   (2 points)
6. (RI.6.4) The passage says the reeds "grew tall in their first
   summer." Is "tall" here a technical, connotative, or plain
   descriptive word? Explain in one sentence. (2 points)
7. (RI.6.8) Claim: "Stone markers are better than wooden posts
   for the bank path." Does the passage support, partly support,
   or not support this claim? Give one reason. (2 points)
8. (RI.6.8) A reader claims, "Every walker in Riverbend follows
   the bank path." Using only this passage, is that claim
   supported? Answer yes or no, with one reason. (2 points)
9. (RI.6.9) Suppose a memoir by a walker said, "I loved the new
   stone markers; they felt permanent." Name one difference you
   would expect between that memoir and this passage, and explain
   it by author position in one sentence. (2 points)
10. (RI.6.2 / RI.6.8) Which sentence is an objective summary
    sentence, and which is an opinion? (a) "The path has held
    since the stone markers were placed." (b) "The stone markers
    were the smartest thing the town ever did." Explain your
    choice in one sentence. (2 points)

**Total: 20 points.** 17–20: ready for the unit assessment;
13–16: reteach the missed standards in Session 14; below 13:
re-plan Sessions 14–15 with the adult, reteaching the two lowest
standards first.
