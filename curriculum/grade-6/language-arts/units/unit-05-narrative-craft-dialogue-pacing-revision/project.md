# Project — A Riverbend Story

**Type:** complete narrative (written, 400–600 words) + revision
trail + 1–2 minute oral reading of the opening.
**Sessions:** 10 (plan), 12 (draft), 13 (revise + read aloud).
**Standards:** W.6.3.a–e, W.6.4, W.6.5, W.6.6; RL.6.3 (applied);
L.6.1, L.6.3 (applied in drafting).

## Task

Write a complete narrative (about 400–600 words) set in the
Riverbend School community — the same fictional world as "The
Missing Guitar" — or in a fictional place of your own with the
same rules: original characters, no real people, no real school.
Choose one prompt:

- **Prompt A — The picture.** Use the unit illustration (or its
  text-only alternative in Lesson 03) as your opening moment:
  a girl kneels on a footbridge at dusk, picking up a small red
  guitar pick, an empty guitar case beside her. Tell the story
  of what happened just before this moment and what happens
  just after it. You may use Maya, Ben, and Ana, or invent your
  own characters — but the pick, the case, and the footbridge
  must matter to the story.
- **Prompt B — The garden gate.** One morning the Riverbend
  school garden gate is standing open and the seedling trays are
  knocked over. Tell the story of who left it open, how your
  narrator finds out, and what changes because of it. The story
  must include one slowed-down scene, one summary, and one
  conclusion that follows from the events.
- **Prompt C — Your own lost-and-found.** A character loses
  something that matters on an important day. Plan five beats,
  and include dialogue, one slowed scene, precise words, and a
  conclusion that follows. (Your Lesson 01 plan may be the seed
  for this prompt.)

Your story must show all five unit objectives: a natural event
sequence with a clear orientation; dialogue, pacing, and
description that develop the characters; precise words and
sensory language; transitions and a conclusion that follows; and
a visible revision — your final draft must differ from your first
draft in at least three concrete ways, listed in your revision
note (see Steps).

**Oral reading (1–2 minutes, Session 13).** Read the opening of
your story aloud (orientation + first episode). The adult notes
whether the opening orients the reader and whether the reading is
clear and audible.

## Materials

Your Lesson 01 five-episode plan, Lesson 03 dialogue scene,
Lesson 06 conclusion paragraph, and Practice E story map; the
unit illustration or its text-only alternative (Prompt A);
notebook or device for drafting; the rubric below (read it
before drafting).

## Steps

1. **Plan (Session 10).** Fill in the story map from Practice E,
   item 7: five beats, narrator choice with a reason, opening
   line, and the minute you will slow into a scene. Check the
   map against the rubric — every 2-point band should be
   reachable from the plan.
2. **Draft (Session 12).** Write the full draft (400–600 words)
   in one sitting if you can. Put a paragraph break every time
   the speaker changes in dialogue. Mark the slowed scene with
   a star in the margin. Do not edit while drafting.
3. **Peer feedback (Session 13).** Exchange drafts with a peer
   or the adult. The reader answers three questions in writing:
   (a) What is the one minute that matters most — did the writer
   slow it down? (b) Which line of dialogue does the most work?
   (c) What is one place where a transition is missing? The
   writer lists the feedback and chooses what to use.
4. **Revise (Session 13).** Revise against the rubric line by
   line, then write a short revision note (3–5 sentences) naming
   at least three concrete changes you made and why (for
   example: "I cut two lines of small talk," "I slowed the
   footbridge minute," "I replaced 'nice' with 'warm and
   steady'"). Check pronoun clarity (L.6.1) and sentence variety
   (L.6.3) in the final pass — Unit 04 skills, applied here.
5. **Polish and share (Session 13).** Produce a clean final
   copy. Read the opening aloud (1–2 minutes). File the plan,
   first draft, peer feedback, revision note, and final copy
   privately as your portfolio piece; do not publish learner
   work in the repository.

## Deliverables

- Story map (Practice E, item 7) and five-episode plan.
- First draft (400–600 words, dialogue punctuated, slowed scene
  marked).
- Peer feedback (three written answers) and revision note
  (3–5 sentences, 3+ concrete changes).
- Final copy (400–600 words) + 1–2 minute oral reading of the
  opening (adult-observed; may be read from the final copy).

## Rubric (8 points; adult scores; 2 = meets, 1 = partly, 0 = missing or incorrect)

| Criterion | 2 points | 1 point | 0 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sequence and orientation (W.6.3.a)** | Events unfold naturally, each following from the last; orientation establishes context, narrator, and characters | Sequence mostly natural with one gap, or orientation missing one part | Episodes out of order or unexplained; no working orientation |
| **Dialogue, pacing, description (W.6.3.b)** | Dialogue does plot/character work; one scene is slowed and the rest summarized with clear pacing control | Dialogue present but partly small talk, or pacing uneven (one wrong speed) | No working dialogue, or everything one speed (all scene or all summary) |
| **Precise words and sensory detail (W.6.3.d)** | Precise verbs and adjectives throughout; at least two sensory details; feelings shown, not only named | Some precise words; one sensory detail; some telling where showing was needed | Vague words throughout; no sensory detail; feelings only named |
| **Transitions and conclusion (W.6.3.c, W.6.3.e)** | Transitions name time/place shifts; conclusion follows from the events and settles the problem | Transitions mostly "and then," or conclusion loosely connected | No working transitions, or a conclusion that contradicts the story |

**Scoring note (read carefully):** the four written criteria are
scored 2 points each = **8 points total**. The oral reading is
scored separately as **complete / incomplete**: complete means
the opening orients the reader (context, narrator, character
audible) and the reading is clear. A story scoring 7–8 with a
complete oral reading and a revision note naming 3+ concrete
changes is ready for the portfolio; 5–6 means revise one
criterion (the adult names it); below 5 means re-plan with the
adult before revising.

## Accessibility and supports

- The story may be dictated to the adult or written with
  speech-to-text; the rubric scores content, not handwriting.
- The illustration may be replaced entirely by its text-only
  alternative (Prompt A).
- Sentence frames (optional): "The story opens ___ because ___."
  / "The minute that matters most is ___; I will slow it down
  by ___." / "The ending follows because ___."
- A shorter story (300–400 words) is acceptable with adult
  agreement when the learner needs a smaller drafting load; the
  rubric bands stay the same.
