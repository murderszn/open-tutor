# Teacher Guide — Unit 05 (Adult Only)

**Role of this guide:** pacing, misconception, support, and
readiness guidance. It contains **no student answers**. All
solutions live in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) (adult only).
Do not show learners either file.

## Pacing notes (16 sessions, ~40 minutes each)

- **Week 19 (Sessions 1–4):** Lesson 01 must land before Lesson
  02: learners need the because-chain habit before they trace a
  character's change. Session 2's ordering drill is the
  diagnostic for Lesson 01 — if ordering fails, re-teach with
  the four episodes of "The Morning of the Show" before moving
  on. Lesson 02's three-step change (old response / middle step /
  new response) is the unit's load-bearing idea; protect
  Session 4 for it.
- **Week 20 (Sessions 5–8):** Lesson 03's image discussion must
  include the text-only alternative for every learner, whether
  or not they can see the image — the alternative is the lesson's
  equity check, not a fallback. Lesson 04 needs the full 40
  minutes: do not rush the scene-vs.-summary distinction, and
  insist on the reader's question ("be there" vs. "just know")
  rather than "long vs. short."
- **Week 21 (Sessions 9–12):** Lesson 05's showing work is the
  hardest single skill in the unit; protect Session 10's project
  plan from being squeezed, because a weak plan becomes a weak
  draft in Session 12. Lesson 06's three conclusion tests should
  be practiced on the learner's own draft in Session 12, not
  only on Maya's story.
- **Week 22 (Sessions 13–16):** score the quiz the same day and
  build Session 15's reteach list from it, standard by standard.
  The unit assessment (Session 16) revises a new flat draft on
  purpose: do not pre-teach "The Rainy Rehearsal" in Session 15.
  Session 13's peer feedback needs the three written questions
  answered before revising starts; feedback given after revising
  is wasted.

## Misconceptions and reteaching moves

| Misconception | What you will hear | Reteach move |
|---|---|---|
| Orientation = first sentence | "The first sentence says it all." | Ask: "Where? When? Who tells it? Who is it about?" Use Lesson 01's paragraph 1 as the four-part model. |
| Order = excitement | "The best part should go first." | Use the because test: "Did this happen *because* the last thing happened?" Rebuild the chain from Lesson 01. |
| Change = announcement | "She became brave." | Ask: "What did she *do* differently, and what happened right before that?" Rebuild the three steps from Lesson 02. |
| Narrator = author | "Maya wrote this." | Ask: "Who says 'I'? Who is the character, and who is the person who wrote the character?" |
| Dialogue = talking | "They talked for a while." | Apply the three-jobs test to every line: reveal character, move the plot, or raise tension — or cut it. |
| Tag every line with "said" + adverb | "'I am sad,' she said sadly." | Replace tags with action beats: "What is the body doing?" Use Lesson 03's worked example 2. |
| Slow = more adjectives | "Very, very beautiful and amazing." | Ask: "What would a camera see, one small action at a time?" Use Lesson 04's Part A as the model. |
| Fast = short sentences only | "I wrote it short, so it's fast." | Ask: "Does it cover time with time words and lists?" Use Lesson 04's Part B as the model. |
| Showing = more description | "I described the room a lot." | Ask: "What can the reader conclude from this? What is the body doing?" Use Lesson 05's shaking-hands pair. |
| Transition = "and then" | "And then… and then… and then…" | Ask: "What changed — time or place? Name it." Use Lesson 06's "By seven o'clock" as the model. |
| Conclusion = moral | "Always be nice." | Apply the three tests: this story only? What was learned? Problem closed? Use Lesson 06's wrong-ending test. |
| Revision = fixing spelling | "I fixed the commas." | Ask: "What did you cut, slow down, or change — and why?" Require the revision note's three concrete changes. |

## Differentiation and supports

- **Reading access:** read every passage aloud once before
  silent reading; chunk Lesson 03's dialogue by speaker (two
  voices); pre-teach the vocabulary list in the unit README
  with examples first, definitions second.
- **Language access:** sentence frames are in the project file;
  the same frames work for Lessons 01, 03, and 06 ("This happens
  because ___." / "The line does the job of ___ because ___." /
  "The ending follows because ___.").
- **Response modes:** oral answers, dictated stories, the
  three-box change strip (Lesson 02), the timeline bar (Lesson
  04), and the five-senses table (Lesson 05) are full responses,
  not shortcuts. The rubric scores content.
- **Extensions:** each lesson carries one; the strongest is
  Lesson 03's Ben-finds-the-pick rewrite and Lesson 05's
  guitar's-point-of-view personification. Offer extensions only
  after the core exit check is met.
- **Accessibility:** every image activity has a text-only
  alternative; every multimedia suggestion in the resource pack
  is adult-previewed, never assigned unpreviewed. A shorter
  project story (300–400 words) is acceptable with adult
  agreement; the rubric bands stay the same.

## Readiness and reteach signals

- **Ready for Unit 06** when the learner can, without hints:
  order five episodes with a because-link each; show a character
  change in three steps; write dialogue that does a job with
  correct punctuation; slow one moment into a scene and
  summarize the rest; and write a conclusion that passes the
  three tests.
- **Reteach Lesson 01** if quiz Q1–Q2 or assessment Q5/Q8 miss:
  return to the because-chain on "The Morning of the Show" and
  rebuild the orientation paragraph together.
- **Reteach Lesson 02** if quiz Q3/Q9 or assessment Q7 miss:
  return to Maya's three-step change in "Two Searches" and
  rebuild the middle step together.
- **Reteach Lesson 03** if quiz Q4 or assessment Part 1 Q2 miss:
  return to the three-jobs test on "On the Footbridge" and cut
  one small-talk line together.
- **Reteach Lesson 04** if quiz Q5 or assessment Q7 miss:
  return to the reader's question on "The Same Minute, Two
  Speeds" and re-sort one scene and one summary.
- **Reteach Lesson 05** if quiz Q6 or assessment Q6 miss:
  return to the body test ("What is the character's body doing?")
  on "The Music Room" and rewrite one telling sentence together.
- **Reteach Lesson 06** if quiz Q7/Q8/Q10 or assessment Q9/Q10
  miss: return to the three conclusion tests on "The Note" and
  test one wrong ending together.
- **Portfolio note:** a project scoring 7–8 with a complete oral
  reading and a revision note naming 3+ concrete changes is
  portfolio-ready. File the plan, first draft, peer feedback,
  revision note, and final copy privately; do not publish learner
  work in the repository.

## What this unit does not do

- It does not teach argument writing (Unit 06), research
  (Unit 07), or poetry (Unit 08). When learners ask "but how do
  I *argue* in writing?", note the question for Unit 06; this
  unit's job is to *tell* a story, not to prove a claim.
- It does not use real school data, real students, or copyrighted
  texts. If a learner asks whether Riverbend School is real,
  answer plainly: it is fictional, invented for teaching, and
  that is why its numbers and times are labeled practice data.
- It does not treat the generated illustration as evidence: the
  image shows a *kind* of moment for a writing prompt; it names
  no one and proves nothing about any real event.
