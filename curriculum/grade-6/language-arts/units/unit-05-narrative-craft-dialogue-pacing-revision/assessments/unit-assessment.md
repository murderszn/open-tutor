# Unit Assessment — Unit 05 (Week 22, Session 16)

**Time:** about 40 minutes · **Adult supervises; scores the same day.**
**Standards:** W.6.3.a–e, W.6.4, W.6.5; RL.6.3; L.6.1, L.6.3.
**Instructions to the learner:** Part 1 asks you to revise a flat
first draft. Part 2 asks you to analyze it. Write in your own
words; use the unit's tools (orientation, dialogue, pacing,
precise words, transitions, conclusions). This assessment is not
standardized or validated; it checks this unit's objectives only.

## The draft — "The Rainy Rehearsal" (original, deliberately flat)

A first draft with problems to fix. It is original fiction; all
names, places, and events are invented practice material.

> **Paragraph 1.** Maya had a rehearsal on a rainy day. It was
> raining a lot. She was nervous about the show. She was worried.
>
> **Paragraph 2.** She played her song. It sounded good. Then she
> went to the auditorium. Ana was there. The auditorium was big.
> The chairs were in rows. The piano was on the stage. Ana said
> the strings were nice. Maya felt better.
>
> **Paragraph 3.** And then Maya went home. And then she ate
> dinner. And then she did her homework. And then she went to bed.
> And then it was over. The end.

## Part 1 — Revise the draft (12 points)

1. (W.6.3.a, 3 points) Rewrite the draft's opening as an
   orientation paragraph (3–4 sentences): establish the context
   (where and when), introduce the narrator, and introduce the
   character.
2. (W.6.3.b, 3 points) Add two lines of dialogue to the draft —
   one that reveals character and one that moves the plot.
   Punctuate them correctly, with a new paragraph for each
   speaker. Label each line with its job.
3. (W.6.3.b, 3 points) Slow down one moment from the draft into
   a 3-sentence scene. Name the moment, and use small actions
   one at a time and at least one sense detail.
4. (W.6.3.d, W.6.3.c, 3 points) Replace three vague words in the
   draft with precise words (quote the old word and write the new
   one), and add one transition that names a time or place shift
   where the draft now says "And then."

## Part 2 — Analyze the draft (12 points)

5. (W.6.3.a, 2 points) Which episode in the draft is out of
   order? Name it and explain in one sentence how the order
   breaks the because-chain.
6. (W.6.3.d, 2 points) Quote one telling sentence from the draft
   and explain in one sentence what showing detail is missing.
7. (W.6.3.b, 2 points) Which moment in the draft deserves a scene
   and which deserves a summary? Name one of each and give a
   reason for each in one sentence.
8. (W.6.3.a, 2 points) Who narrates the draft, and how can you
   tell? Give one piece of evidence.
9. (W.6.3.e, 2 points) Why is the draft's ending ("And then it
   was over. The end.") weak? Explain in two sentences, using
   the three conclusion tests.
10. (W.6.3.e, 2 points) Write a one-sentence conclusion for this
    draft that follows from the events, and explain in one
    sentence why it follows.

**Total: 24 points.** 21–24: objectives met; the portfolio piece
may be filed. 16–20: revise one part (the adult names it) before
filing. Below 16: re-plan with the adult, reteaching the two
lowest standards first, then revise.
