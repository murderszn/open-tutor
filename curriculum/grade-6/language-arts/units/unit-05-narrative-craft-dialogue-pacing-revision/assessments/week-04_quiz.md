# Formative Quiz — Unit 05 (Week 22, Session 14)

**Time:** about 25 minutes · **Adult scores the same day.**
**Standards:** W.6.3.a–e; RL.6.3; L.6.3.
**Instructions to the learner:** answer in your own words unless
a question asks you to choose. This quiz is formative: it shows
what to reteach, not a final grade.

Read the short passage first. It is original and fictional; all
names, places, and events are invented practice material.

> **"The Garden Gate" (original, 4 paragraphs).**
>
> **Paragraph 1.** On Tuesday morning, Ana found the Riverbend
> school garden gate standing open and the seedling trays knocked
> over in the mud. The spring fair was on Saturday, and the
> seedlings were supposed to be the fair's centerpiece.
>
> **Paragraph 2.** Ana was sure she knew who had done it. Ben
> had been in the garden yesterday with the wheelbarrow, and Ben
> always forgot to close gates. She marched to the auditorium
> to tell him so.
>
> **Paragraph 3.** But on the way, she passed the garden shed and
> saw the truth through its open door: her own watering can,
> still full, sitting where she had left it yesterday — right in
> front of the gate's latch, holding the gate open all night.
> The wind had done the rest.
>
> **Paragraph 4.** Ana went back to the garden, set the trays
> upright, and replanted the seedlings that could be saved. When
> Ben arrived, she said, "I was about to blame you. It was my
> watering can." Ben laughed. "Next time," he said, "I'll close
> the gate — and you'll move the can."

## Questions

1. (W.6.3.a) In one sentence, state the orientation of "The
   Garden Gate": where and when it happens, who narrates it, and
   who the main character is. (2 points)
2. (W.6.3.a) List the episodes of the passage in order, one short
   phrase each. (2 points)
3. (RL.6.3) How does Ana change in this passage? Name her first
   response and her new response, with one piece of evidence for
   each. (2 points)
4. (W.6.3.b) Which line of dialogue moves the plot, and which
   line reveals character? Quote one of each and label them.
   (2 points)
5. (W.6.3.b) Is paragraph 2 a scene or a summary? Explain in one
   sentence, using the reader's question ("be there" vs. "just
   know"). (2 points)
6. (W.6.3.d) Quote one precise word or sensory detail from
   paragraph 3 and explain in one sentence what it shows.
   (2 points)
7. (W.6.3.c) The passage moves from the garden (paragraph 1) to
   the auditorium (paragraph 2) to the shed (paragraph 3). Name
   the transition words or phrases that carry the reader across
   those two shifts. (2 points)
8. (W.6.3.e) Write a one-sentence conclusion for this passage
   that follows from the events, and explain in one sentence why
   it follows. (2 points)
9. (W.6.3.b) A classmate says the watering can is "just a
   detail." Explain in two sentences why the watering can is the
   turning point of the story. (2 points)
10. (W.6.3.a / W.6.3.e) Which sentence is a conclusion that
    follows, and which is not? (a) "From then on, Ana checked
    where she left her watering can." (b) "From then on, Ana
    never trusted Ben again." Explain your choice in one
    sentence. (2 points)

**Total: 20 points.** 17–20: ready for the unit assessment;
13–16: reteach the missed standards in Session 15; below 13:
re-plan Sessions 15–16 with the adult, reteaching the two lowest
standards first.
