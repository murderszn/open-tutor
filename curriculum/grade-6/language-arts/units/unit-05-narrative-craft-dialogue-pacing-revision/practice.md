# Practice — Unit 05

**How to use:** one set per practice session (see the unit README
pacing table). Sets A–D follow Lessons 01–04; Set E follows
Lesson 05 and includes the project plan (Session 10). The adult
reviews each set the same day. Solutions are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md) (adult only). Each set has 8 items;
7 of 8 correct meets the session's success criterion.

---

## Practice A — Event sequences and orientation (Session 2)

Read this short story, "The Lost Key," then answer items 1–4.
The episodes are in order.

> Ana kept the garden key on a blue ribbon. One morning the
> ribbon was empty. She searched her pockets, her school bag,
> and the kitchen drawer. Then she remembered: yesterday she
> had hung the ribbon on the garden gate while she watered the
> seedlings. She ran to the gate, and the key was still there,
> swinging in the wind.

1. List the five episodes of "The Lost Key" in order, one short
   phrase each.
2. Write the orientation for "The Lost Key" in one sentence:
   name the context, the narrator, and the character.
3. For the episode "She ran to the gate," write a sentence
   starting "This happens because…" using the story.
4. Which episode is the turning point — the moment the problem
   starts to be solved? Explain in one sentence.
5. Put these episodes of a different story in natural order and
   number them 1–4: (a) Ben finds his shoes by the door.
   (b) Ben wakes up late. (c) Ben runs to school. (d) Ben cannot
   find his shoes.
6. A story begins: "The lights went out. Maya lit a candle. The
   show went on." Which episode is missing between sentences 1
   and 2? Name it in one sentence.
7. Which of these two orders unfolds naturally? Explain in one
   sentence. Order X: "Maya loses her guitar. Maya searches.
   Maya finds a pick." Order Y: "Maya finds a pick. Maya loses
   her guitar. Maya searches."
8. Write a two-sentence orientation for a story about a character
   who finds a strange box on the first day of school. Your
   orientation must name the context, the narrator, and the
   character.

## Practice B — Narrator and character change (Session 4)

1. Read these two sentences. Which is first person, and which is
   third person? (a) "I opened the case and my heart sank."
   (b) "Maya opened the case and her heart sank."
2. A classmate writes: "Ana is very kind and helpful." Rewrite
   the idea as one showing sentence (action or dialogue, no
   "kind" or "helpful").
3. Put Maya's change from Lesson 02 in order (1–3): (a) "She
   leads Ben to the footbridge with a plan." (b) "She searches
   fast and loud like Ben." (c) "She stops on the stage and asks
   where she last had the guitar."
4. A character is rude in scene one and generous in scene two,
   with nothing between them. Explain in two sentences why a
   reader will not believe the change, and name the missing
   middle step.
5. Which narrator fits this story best, and why? "A story about
   what three friends were each doing at the same moment on the
   night of the talent show." Answer in two sentences.
6. Ben shouts, "Found it?" every minute while searching. What
   does that show about Ben? Name the trait and explain in one
   sentence why the action shows it better than the word alone.
7. Write two sentences of dialogue in which Maya shows she is
   becoming calm, without using the word "calm."
8. Explain your reasoning: Maya's hands shake in Lesson 02 and
   stop shaking in Lesson 05. Why does that pair of details show
   a change better than the sentence "Maya became brave"? Answer
   in two sentences.

## Practice C — Dialogue that does work (Session 6)

1. Punctuate this exchange correctly, with a new paragraph for
   each speaker: *did you find it Ben asked not yet Maya said
   but I found this pick*
2. This exchange runs two speakers together in one paragraph.
   Rewrite it correctly: *"Where were you?" Ben asked. "In the
   garden," Maya said. "All afternoon?" Ben asked. "Until dusk,"
   Maya said.*
3. Replace the speaker tags in these two lines with action beats
   (keep the spoken words): "'I dropped it here,' Maya said."
   "'Are you sure?' Ben said."
4. These three lines are small talk. Cross out the two that do no
   job, and explain in one sentence why the third stays: (a)
   "Hi, Ben." (b) "The pick was here all along." (c) "Nice
   weather today."
5. Write two lines of dialogue for Ana explaining why she
   borrowed the guitar. Line 1 must reveal character; line 2
   must move the plot.
6. Find and fix three punctuation errors in this dialogue:
   *"My guitar is gone" Maya said. Ben asked "when did you see
   it last"? "Yesterday" Maya said.*
7. Write the same line of dialogue three ways with different
   tags or beats: "The show starts at seven." Make one version
   sound worried, one sound excited, and one sound tired — using
   tags and beats, not adverbs like "worriedly."
8. Which line does the most work, and why? (a) "The case was
   empty." (b) "I carried an empty case home and never looked
   inside." Answer in two sentences.

## Practice D — Pacing: scene vs. summary (Session 8)

1. Label each snippet S (scene) or U (summary). (a) "We searched
   the auditorium, the shed, and the library. By noon we had
   found nothing." (b) "I knelt. The planks were cold through
   my jeans. The pick lay between two boards, red against the
   grey wood."
2. Slow down this moment into a 3-sentence scene: "Ben opened
   the cupboard." Use one small action at a time and one sense.
3. Summarize this stretch in two sentences: Maya woke up, opened
   the case, searched the house, called Ben, and waited for him
   at the gate. Use a time word and a list.
4. Which deserves a scene, and which a summary? Give a reason
   for each in one sentence. (a) The week of rehearsals before
   the show. (b) The minute the curtain opens.
5. This scene should be a summary. Rewrite it in two sentences:
   "Maya walked to school. She passed the bakery. The bakery
   smelled like bread. She passed the park. A dog barked. She
   passed the library. The library was closed. She reached the
   gate."
6. This summary should be a scene. Expand it into 3 sentences:
   "Ana handed Maya the note."
7. Choose the best time word for each summary sentence, and
   explain each choice in one sentence: (a) "___ the show, the
   hall was empty." (before / suddenly) (b) "___ an hour, Ben
   had opened every cupboard." (after / meanwhile)
8. Explain your reasoning: why did Lesson 04 slow down the
   footbridge minute instead of the morning search? Answer in
   two sentences, using the reader's question ("be there" vs.
   "just know").

## Practice E — Precise words, transitions, and project plan (Session 10)

1. Replace each vague word with a precise one, and explain your
   choice in one sentence each: (a) "Maya said something to
   Ben." (b) "The music room was a good place." (c) "Ana did a
   nice thing."
2. Add one sensory detail (sound, smell, or touch) to this
   sentence, and explain in one sentence what it adds: "Maya
   stood in the doorway of the music room."
3. Rewrite this telling sentence as a showing sentence (one or
   two sentences): "Ana felt guilty about borrowing the guitar."
4. Which word has the right connotation, and why? Ana's note was
   (a) "a sneaky trick" or (b) "a kind surprise"? Answer in two
   sentences.
5. Fill each blank with a transition that names the time or
   place shift: (a) "___ dusk, we reached the footbridge."
   (b) "___ the footbridge, the school windows glowed."
   (c) "___ the show, Maya walked home under the stars."
6. Which conclusion follows from Maya's story, and why? (a) "She
   never left her guitar anywhere again." (b) "She asked before
   she assumed — and she checked the case before she carried it
   home." Answer in two sentences.
7. **Project plan (Session 10).** Choose one project prompt from
   [project.md](project.md) and fill in a story map: (a) the five
   beats (opening / rising action / climax / falling action /
   resolution) in one line each; (b) the narrator (first or
   third person) with one reason; (c) the opening line (one
   sentence that orients the reader); (d) the one minute you
   will slow into a scene, and why.
8. Self-check: list the unit's five objectives from the unit
   README, and for each one write one sentence saying where in
   your story map that objective will show up (for example,
   "Objective 2 — dialogue: the footbridge talk in beat 3").
