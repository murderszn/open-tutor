# Resource Pack — Unit 05: Narrative Craft, Dialogue, Pacing, and Revision

**Goal (adult planning judgment):** Give the guiding adult free,
no-account videos and references that reinforce this unit's five
objectives — natural event sequences and orientation, dialogue
and pacing and description, precise words and sensory language,
transitions and conclusions, and revision — for a grade-6 learner
(ages 11–12). Videos supplement the lessons; they never replace
explicit teaching, practice, or the adult's check.

All external links below were found via web search on 2026-11-07
and are described only as far as the search results establish
them. Runtimes and captions were not checked — preview before
assigning. YouTube serves ads; use an ad-blocked or supervised
session with the learner. Search links are labeled as such: they
are not single verified videos.

## Web references (7, found 2026-11-07)

1. [Narrative Essays — Purdue
   OWL](https://OWL.Purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/essay_writing/narrative_essays.html)
   — university writing guide: a narrative essay tells a story
   with an introduction, plot, characters, setting, climax, and
   conclusion, written from a clear point of view with clear,
   concise language. Adult-facing; supports Lessons 01, 02, and
   06 (structure, point of view, conclusion).
2. [A Picture's Worth a Thousand Words: From Image to Detailed
   Narrative — ReadWriteThink](https://www.readwritethink.org/classroom-resources/lesson-plans/picture-worth-thousand-words?tab=1)
   — lesson plan: learners look at an image, brainstorm the
   events and characters it suggests, then write from one
   character's point of view, describing what led up to the
   picture or what followed. Adult-facing; supports Lesson 03's
   image activity and Project Prompt A.
3. [Make a Splash! Using Dramatic Experience to "Explode the
   Moment" — ReadWriteThink](https://www.readwritethink.org/classroom-resources/lesson-plans/make-splash-using-dramatic)
   — lesson plan: learners elaborate or "explode" a moment with
   descriptive, sensory language, using a graphic organizer of
   what they saw, felt, thought, did, said, and heard. Adult-
   facing; supports Lesson 04's slow-scene work and Lesson 05's
   sensory detail.
4. [Story Elements Alive! — ReadWriteThink](https://www.readwritethink.org/classroom-resources/lesson-plans/story-elements-alive?tab=5)
   — lesson plan: setting, characters, problem/solution, and
   plot through read-alouds, character maps, and story maps.
   Adult-facing; supports Lessons 01–02 (episodes, character,
   plot) and the project story map.
5. [Writing a Narrative Paragraph & Punctuating dialogue —
   Henrico County Public Schools](https://teachers.henrico.k12.va.us/short_pump/douthit_c/site/NarParDiag.html)
   — teacher page: narrative paragraphs in chronological order,
   transition words, and dialogue rules (new paragraph per
   speaker, quotation marks, speaker tags, varied verbs).
   Adult-facing; supports Lesson 03's dialogue rules and Lesson
   06's transitions.
6. [Narrative Writing: Steps, Structure, Examples & Prompts —
   Wisdom Point](https://www.wisdom-point.org/post/narrative-writing-how-to-plan-write-and-improve-a-story)
   — guide: plan five story beats, open with a hook, build the
   middle with scenes not summary, make every dialogue line
   reveal character or raise tension. Adult-facing; supports the
   project plan and Lessons 01–04.
7. [Grade 6 — Claim 2, Target 2: Compose Full Texts —
   Smarter Balanced Content Explorer](https://contentexplorer.smarterbalanced.org/target/e-g6-c2-t2)
   — assessment-target page: write full narrative texts using a
   complete writing process, demonstrating narrative strategies,
   text structures, and transitional strategies for coherence and
   closure (W.6.3.b–e). Adult reference for the unit's standards
   notes; the authoritative framework is the published Common
   Core ELA standards.
