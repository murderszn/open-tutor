# Unit 05 — Narrative Craft: Dialogue, Pacing, and Revision

**Grade:** 6 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 19–22
**Backlog:** [issue #32](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/32), section U05
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A story is not a list of things that happened. It is a set of
choices: which moment to slow down until the reader can hear it,
which morning to cover in four sentences, what a character says
out loud instead of what the narrator tells you, which exact word
makes a room feel warm or a wait feel long, and how the ending
grows out of everything before it. This unit teaches those
choices one at a time, then puts them together: the learner reads
one continuous Riverbend story — Maya's guitar goes missing on
the morning of the talent show — studies how each scene is built,
and then writes, revises, and polishes a complete narrative of
their own. The unit's final product is a portfolio-ready story
with a visible revision trail, not a first draft with a grade on it.

All unit passages are original and set in the fictional Riverbend
School community introduced in Unit 04. Maya, Ben, Ana, the
talent show, the garden, the footbridge, and the auditorium are
invented practice material for this unit — not real students, not
a real school, and not evidence about any real place. Numbers,
times, and events in the passages are invented for teaching.

## Essential question

How do a writer's choices — what to show in a scene, what to
say in dialogue, what to slow down or speed past, which words to
choose, and how to end — make a reader feel inside a story
instead of just hearing about it?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks and Units 01–04:

- Cite textual evidence for explicit meaning and inferences
  (RL.6.1, RI.6.1); determine a theme from details and describe
  how plot unfolds in episodes and how characters respond or
  change (RL.6.2, RL.6.3); explain how a narrator's or speaker's
  point of view is developed (RL.6.6).
- Use context clues, Greek and Latin affixes and roots, and
  reference materials to clarify unknown words (L.6.4); interpret
  figures of speech including personification and explain
  connotation vs. denotation and the impact of specific word
  choices (L.6.5).
- Use pronouns in proper case, correct vague pronouns and shifts
  in number and person, punctuate nonrestrictive elements, vary
  sentence patterns for meaning and style, and maintain a
  consistent formal or narrative tone (L.6.1, L.6.2, L.6.3).
- Write complete sentences and revise a paragraph for clarity,
  punctuation, sentence variety, and word choice (Unit 04).

Lesson 01 re-teaches the episode structure of a plot (RL.6.3)
as a writing tool rather than assuming the Unit 02 reading
version transferred on its own. Lesson 03 re-teaches quotation
and dialogue punctuation from the ground up.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. **Organize an event sequence** that unfolds naturally and
   logically, engaging and orienting the reader by establishing
   a context and introducing a narrator and characters (W.6.3.a).
2. **Develop experiences, events, and characters** with narrative
   techniques — **dialogue, pacing, and description** — showing
   how characters respond and change as the plot moves toward a
   resolution (W.6.3.b; RL.6.3).
3. Use **precise words and phrases, relevant descriptive details,
   and sensory language** to convey experiences and events
   (W.6.3.d; L.6.5).
4. Use a **variety of transition words, phrases, and clauses** to
   convey sequence and signal shifts in time frame or setting,
   and provide a **conclusion that follows from** the narrated
   experiences or events (W.6.3.c, W.6.3.e).
5. **Develop and strengthen narrative writing** through planning,
   revising, editing, and rewriting with peer and adult guidance,
   producing **clear, coherent writing** appropriate to task,
   purpose, and audience (W.6.5, W.6.4; L.6.1, L.6.3 applied in
   drafting).

## Unit passages (original; written for this unit)

The six lesson passages tell one continuous story, "The Missing
Guitar," scene by scene. Each passage is labeled with its lesson
so the learner can reread the whole story in order at the end.

- **"The Morning of the Show"** (~230 words) — Lesson 01. Maya
  opens her guitar case on the morning of the talent show and
  finds it empty.
- **"Two Searches"** (~240 words) — Lesson 02. Ben searches fast
  and loud; Maya searches slowly and carefully. Maya changes
  from frantic to methodical.
- **"On the Footbridge"** (~200 words, mostly dialogue) —
  Lesson 03. Maya and Ben talk on the footbridge at dusk, where
  Maya finds a small red guitar pick on the planks. The unit's
  generated illustration shows this moment.
- **"The Same Minute, Two Speeds"** (~230 words) — Lesson 04.
  The footbridge minute told in slow motion, then the whole
  morning's search told in four sentences: scene vs. summary.
- **"The Music Room"** (~200 words) — Lesson 05. Maya opens the
  music-room door: the guitar on its stand, new strings, and
  Ana's note. A sensory-description scene.
- **"The Note"** (~200 words) — Lesson 06. Ana's note, the duet,
  and Maya's reflection — a conclusion that follows from the
  events, beside one that does not.

## Session pacing (16 sessions, ~40 minutes each)

Session model per the track audit: craft lesson → drafting
practice → revision/editing practice → share and review.

| # | Session | Activity | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Week 19 | Lesson 01 — event sequences that unfold naturally | 5 episodes ordered with a reason each; orientation names context, narrator, characters |
| 2 | Week 19 | Practice A — sequence ordering and orientation | 7 of 8 items correct; orientation paragraph has all three parts |
| 3 | Week 19 | Lesson 02 — narrator and characters; response and change | Narrator named with a reason; Maya's change traced in 2 steps with evidence |
| 4 | Week 19 | Practice B — narrator choice and character change | 7 of 8 items correct; change shown through action/dialogue, not just named |
| 5 | Week 20 | Lesson 03 — dialogue that does work; image activity | Dialogue punctuated correctly; image question answered from image + text |
| 6 | Week 20 | Practice C — dialogue punctuation and tags/beats | 7 of 8 items correct; every dialogue line does plot or character work |
| 7 | Week 20 | Lesson 04 — pacing: slow the moment, summarize the rest | Slow-motion rewrite names 3 slowed details; summary covers the morning in ≤4 sentences |
| 8 | Week 20 | Practice D — pacing decisions | 7 of 8 items correct with a reason each (slow vs. summarize) |
| 9 | Week 21 | Lesson 05 — precise words and sensory detail | 3 vague words replaced with precise ones; one showing sentence written per sense named |
| 10 | Week 21 | Practice E — word choice, transitions; project plan | 7 of 8 items correct; project story map has 5 beats + opening line |
| 11 | Week 21 | Lesson 06 — transitions and conclusions | 4 transitions placed correctly; two conclusions judged (follows / does not follow) |
| 12 | Week 21 | Project — draft the Riverbend story | Draft has 5 beats, dialogue, one slowed scene, and a conclusion |
| 13 | Week 22 | Project — revise with rubric + peer feedback; read opening aloud | Every rubric line checked; revision list has 3+ concrete changes made |
| 14 | Week 22 | Formative quiz ([week-04_quiz.md](assessments/week-04_quiz.md)) | Adult scores same day; reteach list made |
| 15 | Week 22 | Reteach and review — targets from the quiz; revision relay | Missed items re-answered correctly with evidence |
| 16 | Week 22 | Unit assessment ([unit-assessment.md](assessments/unit-assessment.md)) + portfolio selection | Completed under adult supervision; one polished narrative filed for the portfolio |

## Standards and source notes

Standards codes and descriptions follow the track's verified
crosswalk (scope-and-sequence §12, verified 2026-10-05 against
the published Common Core State Standards for English Language
Arts, thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/), re-checked for this
unit's codes on 2026-11-07: W.6.3 (write narratives to develop
real or imagined experiences or events using effective technique,
relevant descriptive details, and well-structured event
sequences); W.6.3.a (engage and orient the reader by establishing
a context and introducing a narrator and/or characters; organize
an event sequence that unfolds naturally and logically); W.6.3.b
(use narrative techniques, such as dialogue, pacing, and
description, to develop experiences, events, and/or characters);
W.6.3.c (use a variety of transition words, phrases, and clauses
to convey sequence and signal shifts from one time frame or
setting to another); W.6.3.d (use precise words and phrases,
relevant descriptive details, and sensory language to convey
experiences and events); W.6.3.e (provide a conclusion that
follows from the narrated experiences or events); W.6.4 (produce
clear and coherent writing in which the development, organization,
and style are appropriate to task, purpose, and audience); W.6.5
(with some guidance and support from peers and adults, develop
and strengthen writing as needed by planning, revising, editing,
rewriting, or trying a new approach); W.6.6 (use technology,
including the Internet, to produce and publish writing and to
interact and collaborate with others); RL.6.3 (describe how a
particular story's or drama's plot unfolds in a series of episodes
as well as how the characters respond or change as the plot moves
toward a resolution); L.6.1 (demonstrate command of the
conventions of standard English grammar and usage when writing or
speaking — pronouns in proper case, intensive pronouns, corrected
shifts and vague pronouns); L.6.3 (use knowledge of language and
its conventions when writing, speaking, reading, or listening —
vary sentence patterns for meaning, reader interest, and style;
maintain consistency in style and tone); L.6.5 (demonstrate
understanding of figurative language, word relationships, and
nuances in word meanings — interpret figures of speech including
personification; distinguish connotations and denotations; analyze
the impact of specific word choices on meaning and tone). The
framework is a subject reference only; nothing here claims state
adoption, accreditation, or complete alignment.

No passage in this unit reproduces a copyrighted text, worksheet,
or article. "The Missing Guitar" and every practice, quiz, and
assessment passage are original fiction written for teaching; any
resemblance to a real school or person is coincidental. Numbers
and times inside the passages are invented practice data, labeled
as such wherever a learner is asked to judge a story event.
