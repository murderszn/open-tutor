# Unit 04 — Grammar: Pronouns, Sentence Variety, and Vocabulary

**Grade:** 6 · **Subject:** Language Arts · **Track weeks:** 15–18
**Backlog:** [issue #32](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/32), section U04
**Status:** validated draft (this unit); not educator-reviewed, not merged

A sentence can be correct and still fail its reader. A pronoun can
point at two people at once. A row of short, identical sentences can
put a reader to sleep. A single word — *cheap* instead of *thrifty* —
can change what the reader thinks of a person. This unit is the
Sentence Studio: the learner takes flat, confusing, or tonally
careless writing and rebuilds it into sentences that are clear,
varied, correctly punctuated, and matched to their audience. The
work runs from the smallest choice (is it *I* or *me*?) to the whole
piece (does this letter sound like it belongs in a principal's
mailbox?), and it ends with a midyear review that looks back across
Units 01–04.

All unit passages and sentences are original and set in the
fictional Riverbend School community introduced in Unit 03. Ben,
Maya, Ana, and the school's talent show, garden newsletter, and
auditorium are invented practice material — not real students, not
real school records, and not evidence about any real place. Numbers
and events in the examples are invented for teaching.

## Essential question

How do small choices — which pronoun, which punctuation mark, which
word, which sentence shape — change what a reader understands and
how the writing feels?

## Prerequisites

From the diagnostic weeks and Units 01–03:

- Cite textual evidence for explicit meaning and inferences
  (RL.6.1, RI.6.1); determine a central idea and summarize
  objectively (RI.6.2); trace and evaluate claims (RI.6.8).
- Use context clues, Greek and Latin affixes and roots, and
  reference materials to clarify unknown words (L.6.4); use academic
  verbs — *infer, conclude, suggest, imply, indicate, demonstrate* —
  precisely.
- Write complete sentences with a subject and a predicate, and tell
  a fragment from a complete sentence (grade-5 sentence basics,
  re-checked in Lesson 05 rather than assumed).

Lesson 01 re-teaches pronoun case from the job a pronoun does in its
sentence, rather than assuming the learner remembers a case table.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (adult-observed):

1. Use pronouns in the **proper case** (subjective, objective,
   possessive), use **intensive pronouns** for emphasis, and
   recognize and correct **inappropriate shifts** in pronoun number
   and person and **vague pronouns** with unclear or ambiguous
   antecedents (L.6.1.a–d).
2. Use **commas, parentheses, and dashes** to set off
   **nonrestrictive and parenthetical elements**, and **spell
   correctly**, including commonly confused homophones and
   possessive pronouns that never take apostrophes (L.6.2.a–b).
3. **Vary sentence patterns** for meaning, reader interest, and
   style, combining choppy sentences and repairing fragments and
   run-ons; **maintain consistency in style and tone** and use
   **formal English** when indicated (L.6.3.a–b, SL.6.6).
4. **Distinguish connotations from denotations**, **interpret
   personification** and other figures of speech in context, and
   explain how a **specific word choice** changes meaning or tone;
   clarify words with context clues, affixes and roots, and
   reference materials (L.6.5.a–c, L.6.4.a–d, L.6.6).
5. **Revise a full paragraph** for pronoun clarity, punctuation,
   sentence variety, word choice, and consistent formal tone, and
   present the revision orally in formal English (L.6.1, L.6.2,
   L.6.3, L.6.5, SL.6.6).

## Standards and source notes

Standards codes and descriptions follow the track's verified
crosswalk (scope-and-sequence §12, verified 2026-10-05 against the
published Common Core State Standards for English Language Arts,
thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/), re-checked for this unit's codes
on 2026-11-01: L.6.1.a (ensure that pronouns are in the proper case
— subjective, objective, possessive); L.6.1.b (use intensive
pronouns, e.g., *myself, ourselves*); L.6.1.c (recognize and correct
inappropriate shifts in pronoun number and person); L.6.1.d
(recognize and correct vague pronouns, i.e., ones with unclear or
ambiguous antecedents); L.6.1.e (recognize variations from standard
English in their own and others' writing and speaking, and identify
and use strategies to improve expression in conventional language —
touched in Lesson 06's formal/informal work, introduced rather than
mastered here); L.6.2.a (use punctuation — commas, parentheses,
dashes — to set off nonrestrictive/parenthetical elements);
L.6.2.b (spell correctly); L.6.3.a (vary sentence patterns for
meaning, reader/listener interest, and style); L.6.3.b (maintain
consistency in style and tone); L.6.4.a (use context as a clue to
the meaning of a word or phrase); L.6.4.b (use common, grade-
appropriate Greek or Latin affixes and roots as clues); L.6.4.c
(consult reference materials, print or digital, to find a
pronunciation or clarify a precise meaning or part of speech);
L.6.4.d (verify the preliminary determination of a word's meaning,
e.g., by checking the inferred meaning in context or in a
dictionary); L.6.5.a (interpret figures of speech, e.g.,
personification, in context); L.6.5.b (use the relationship between
particular words, e.g., cause/effect, part/whole, item/category, to
better understand each word); L.6.5.c (distinguish among the
connotations — associations — of words with similar denotations —
definitions — e.g., *stingy, scrimping, economical, thrifty*);
L.6.6 (acquire and use accurately grade-appropriate general
academic and domain-specific words and phrases; gather vocabulary
knowledge when considering a word or phrase important to
comprehension or expression); SL.6.6 (adapt speech to a variety of
contexts and tasks, demonstrating command of formal English when
indicated or appropriate). The framework is a subject reference
only; nothing here claims state adoption, accreditation, or
complete alignment.

No passage in this unit reproduces a copyrighted text, worksheet,
or article. The talent-show, garden, and fair sentences are original
fiction written for teaching; any resemblance to a real school or
person is coincidental.
