# Lesson 01 — Pronouns in Proper Case

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can choose the right pronoun form —
subject, object, or possessive — by checking the job the pronoun
does in its sentence."
**Time:** 40 minutes · **Materials:** this lesson; pencil and paper.
**Standards:** L.6.1.a (pronouns in proper case).

## Launch (5 min)

"Read these two sentences. One sounds right; one sounds wrong —
and your ear already knows which. *Me and Ben set up the chairs.*
*Ben and I set up the chairs.* The second one sounds right because
*I* is doing the setting-up. A pronoun changes its form depending
on its job. That job is called its **case**."

## Readiness check (3 min)

Which pronoun fits, and what job is it doing?

(a) "___ gave Maya the program." (She / Her)
(b) "The program was given to ___." (she / her)

((a) *She* — it is the subject, doing the giving. (b) *her* — it
receives the giving, so it is an object. If you said "Her gave
Maya the program," your ear probably objected — trust that
objection, then check it with the rule below.)

## Explicit teaching

A **pronoun** takes the place of a noun. Its **antecedent** is the
noun it replaces. A pronoun's **case** is the form it takes for its
job in the sentence:

| Case | Job | Pronouns |
|---|---|---|
| Subjective (subject) | Subject of a verb; also after a linking verb (*is, was*) | I, you, he, she, it, we, they |
| Objective (object) | Object of a verb or of a preposition (*to, for, with, between*) | me, you, him, her, it, us, them |
| Possessive | Shows ownership | my/mine, your/yours, his, her/hers, its, our/ours, their/theirs |

Three rules do most of the work:

1. **Ask the job, not the sound.** Cover the pronoun and ask:
   "Who is doing the action?" (subject) or "Who receives it?"
   (object) or "Whose is it?" (possessive).
2. **The drop test for compounds.** When two people share a job —
   "Maya and ___" — drop the other person and test what is left.
   "Maya and I practiced" passes because "I practiced" is correct;
   "Maya and me practiced" fails because "Me practiced" is wrong.
   The same test works for objects: "The teacher thanked Maya and
   me" passes because "thanked me" is correct.
3. **Possessive pronouns never take an apostrophe.** *Hers, ours,
   theirs, its* show ownership already. *It's* means "it is";
   *her's* is never a word.

Comparisons hide a verb: "Ben practiced longer than I" means
"longer than I [practiced]." Finish the hidden verb in your head
and the case follows — subject case, because the pronoun is the
subject of that hidden verb.

## Worked example 1 — compound subject and compound object

**Problem:** Choose: "The judges spoke to Ben and (I / me) after
the show." Then choose: "(I / Me) and Ben carried the trophy."

**Model:** First sentence: the judges spoke *to* someone. *To* is
a preposition, so its object needs objective case. Drop "Ben and":
"spoke to me" is right, "spoke to I" is wrong. Answer: *me*.
Second sentence: who carried? "I and Ben" — drop "and Ben":
"I carried" is right, "Me carried" is wrong. Answer: *I*. Note the
polite order — put the other person first ("Ben and I"), but the
case comes from the job, not the order.

## Worked example 2 — comparison and possessive

**Problem:** Choose: "Maya played longer than (I / me)." Then
choose: "The guitar is (her / hers)."

**Model:** "Longer than I" — finish the hidden verb: "longer than
I [played]." *I* is the subject of *played*, so subject case:
*I*. Second: "The guitar is hers" — the pronoun stands alone
showing ownership, so it is the possessive form *hers*, with no
apostrophe. (Before a noun it would be *her*: "her guitar.")

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. "Ben and ___ (I / me) carried the chairs." *Hint: drop "Ben
   and" and test.*
2. "The teacher thanked Maya and ___ (I / me)." *Hint: who
   receives the thanks?*
3. "___ (Her / She) and Ben tuned the guitar." *Hint: who tuned?*
4. "Between you and ___ (I / me), the show ran long." *Hint:
   *between* is a preposition.*

## Independent practice

1. "The judges spoke to Ben and ___ (I / me) after the show."
2. "___ (Him / He) and Maya opened the show."
3. "The first song was played by Maya and ___ (I / me)."
4. "Ben practiced more than ___ (I / me) did."
5. "The trophy is ___ (his / he)."
6. Fix this sentence, and name the rule it breaks: "Me and Ben set
   up the chairs."

## Application

Write three sentences about the talent show:

(a) one with a compound subject that includes *I*;
(b) one with an object pronoun after *for*;
(c) one with a possessive pronoun standing alone (like *theirs*).

Read each back and run the drop test on (a) and the job question
on (b) and (c).

## Exit check

"The award went to Maya and (I / me)." Choose, and say in one
sentence why.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading/language access:** keep the case table visible during
  practice; allow the learner to point at the job column instead
  of naming the case.
- **Alternate response mode:** say the drop test aloud ("Me
  practiced? No — I practiced") before writing.
- **Extension:** find the case error in this sentence and explain
  both the error and the fix: "The prize was given to Maya and I,
  and she thanked the judges." (The first pronoun is an object of
  *to*, so it must be *me*.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: "Maya and I" sounds polite, so learners use
*I* everywhere in compounds, including objects ("for Maya and I").
The drop test breaks that habit. Answer-key reference:
