# Lesson 02 — Intensive Pronouns, Shifts, and Vague Pronouns

**Goal (learner hears):** "I can use intensive pronouns for
emphasis, fix pronouns that shift number or person, and rewrite
vague pronouns so the reader always knows who or what I mean."
**Time:** 40 minutes · **Materials:** this lesson; the stage
image below (or its text-only alternative); pencil and paper.
**Standards:** L.6.1.b (intensive pronouns), L.6.1.c (shifts in
number and person), L.6.1.d (vague pronouns).

## Launch (5 min)

"Two sentences: 'Ben himself carried the trophy onstage.' 'Ben
taught himself the song.' Both use a *-self* word, but they do
different jobs. In the first, *himself* is extra emphasis — cross
it out and the sentence still works. In the second, *himself* is
the object — Ben taught *whom*? Himself. Cross it out and the
sentence breaks. One word, two jobs. Today you will learn to tell
them apart — and to catch pronouns that shift or point nowhere."

## Readiness check (3 min)

Which sentence has a pronoun problem, and what kind?

(a) "The musicians tuned their instruments, and then she played
first."
(b) "Maya herself tuned the guitar."

((a) has two problems: *she* shifts from the plural *musicians*
and has no clear antecedent — which musician? (b) is fine:
*herself* adds emphasis to Maya.)

## Explicit teaching

**Intensive pronouns** — *myself, yourself, himself, herself,
itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves* — emphasize a noun or
pronoun that is already in the sentence. **Removal test:** cross
the pronoun out. If the sentence is still complete and keeps its
core meaning, the pronoun is intensive. "The principal herself
handed Ben the trophy" → "The principal handed Ben the trophy"
still works, so *herself* is intensive.

**Reflexive pronouns** look identical but do a different job: they
are the object of the verb, and the object is the same person as
the subject. "Maya taught herself the song" — *herself* receives
the teaching, so it is reflexive, not intensive. Never use a
*-self* word as a subject: "Maya and myself tuned the guitar" is
wrong; use "Maya and I."

**Shifts** break the promise a pronoun makes. A pronoun must match
its antecedent in **number** (singular/plural) and stay in the
same **person** (first/second/third) through a sentence or
passage. "The students took their seats, and then he opened his
notebook" shifts from plural *students* to singular *he*. Fix it
by matching: "…and then they opened their notebooks." "When you
join the chorus, a singer must attend rehearsal" shifts from *you*
(second person) to *a singer* (third person). Fix it by staying
with *you*: "When you join the chorus, you must attend
rehearsal."

**Vague pronouns** have no clear antecedent, or more than one
possible antecedent. "When Maya told Ana she had won, she smiled"
is vague: *she* could be Maya or Ana. Three fixes: repeat the
noun ("…Ana smiled"), rewrite the sentence ("Maya told Ana the
good news, and Ana smiled"), or recast it ("Ana smiled when Maya
told her she had won" — still vague! Naming is the safest fix).
Also watch vague *this, that,* and *it* pointing at a whole idea:
"Ben practiced every day. This paid off." What is *this*? Name
it: "That practice paid off."

**Text-only alternative (same information):** Two students stand
on a wooden stage in front of a dark curtain. The student on the
left wears a blue hoodie and holds a large gold trophy up with
both hands. The student on the right wears a pink sweater and
holds an acoustic guitar. A small potted plant sits at the right
edge of the stage. Call the trophy-holder Ben and the
guitar-holder Maya.

**Questions:**

1. Write three sentences describing the image. Do not use
   *he, she,* or *they* for the two students — use their names.
   (A reader who cannot see the picture must still know who holds
   what.)
2. This sentence is unclear to a reader who cannot see the
   picture: "She held the guitar." Rewrite it so the reader knows
   exactly who held the guitar.
3. Why is "Ben showed Maya his trophy" clear, while "When Ben
   told Maya he had won, he smiled" would be unclear if both
   students were boys? (Answer in your own words: one antecedent
   per pronoun, or the reader guesses.)

## Worked example 1 — intensive or reflexive?

**Problem:** Name the job of *herself* in each sentence:
(a) "Maya herself tuned the guitar." (b) "Maya taught herself
the song."

**Model:** (a) Cross out *herself*: "Maya tuned the guitar" is
complete and means the same thing. Intensive — emphasis only.
(b) Cross out *herself*: "Maya taught the song" changes the
meaning (taught it to whom?). *Herself* is the object and equals
the subject, so it is reflexive.

## Worked example 2 — fixing a shift and a vague pronoun

**Problem:** Fix: "The musicians tuned his instruments."
Fix: "When Maya told Ana she had won, she smiled."

**Model:** *Musicians* is plural, so the pronoun must be plural:
"The musicians tuned their instruments." For the second sentence,
*she* has two possible antecedents (Maya, Ana) — twice over: it
is unclear both who won and who smiled. Name both: "Maya told Ana
that Maya had won, and Ana smiled." (If Maya smiled instead,
write "Maya told Ana that Maya had won, and Maya smiled." When
in doubt, use the name twice — a repeated name is never vague.)

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. "Maya herself tuned the guitar." Intensive or reflexive?
   *Hint: use the removal test.*
2. "Ben blamed himself for the late start." Intensive or
   reflexive? *Hint: who receives the blame?*
3. Fix the shift: "The students took their seats, and then he
   opened his notebook." *Hint: match the plural antecedent.*
4. Fix the vague pronoun: "When Maya told Ana she had won, she
   smiled." *Hint: replace the vague pronoun with a name.*

## Independent practice

1. "The principal herself handed Ben the trophy." Intensive or
   reflexive? Why?
2. "We ourselves set up the chairs." Intensive or reflexive? Why?
3. Fix the shift: "When you join the chorus, a singer must attend
   rehearsal."
4. Fix the vague pronoun: "Maya put her guitar in its case, and
   then she gave it to Ana." (What is unclear? Rewrite it.)
5. Fix the vague *this*: "Ben practiced every day. This paid
   off."
6. Fix the vague pronoun: "Ana and Maya practiced. She played the
   guitar." (Rewrite with a name.)

## Application

Rewrite this note so it has no vague pronoun and no shift. You
may change word order and repeat names:

> "Maya and Ana entered the contest. She practiced daily. When
> you perform, a singer should bow at the end. This made the
> judges happy."

## Exit check

Name the two pronoun problems in this sentence and fix it:
"The band members tuned their instruments, and then she played
first."

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading/language access:** circle every pronoun and draw an
  arrow to its antecedent before judging it; a pronoun with no
  arrow, or two arrows, is the problem.
- **Alternate response mode:** explain fixes orally ("I changed
  *she* to *Ana* because…") before writing them.
- **Extension:** explain why "The team finished its banner, and
  then it hung the banner up" is consistent (singular *team*
  acting as one unit takes *its/it*) while "The team finished
  its banner, and then they hung it up" shifts number.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: learners "fix" a vague pronoun by swapping
in another pronoun ("she → they") instead of a name. Insist on
names for the fix. Also watch for the belief that intensive
pronouns can stand as subjects ("myself and Maya") — they cannot.
