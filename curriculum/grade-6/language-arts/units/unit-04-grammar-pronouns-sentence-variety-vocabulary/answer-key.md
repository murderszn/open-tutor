# Answer Key — Unit 04 (Adult Only)

Worked solutions for every lesson item, practice item, quiz
question, assessment task, and project criterion. Do not show
learners this file. Where a revision has several correct forms,
the key gives one and states what else to accept.

## Lesson 01 — Pronoun case

**Guided practice:** 1. *I* — drop "Ben and": "I carried" is
correct. 2. *me* — object of *thanked*. 3. *She* — subject of
*tuned*. 4. *me* — object of the preposition *between*.

**Independent practice:** 1. *me* (object of *to*). 2. *He*
(subject of *opened*). 3. *me* (object of *by*). 4. *I* — "than
I [did]": subject of the hidden verb. 5. *his* (possessive
standing alone; *he* cannot follow *is* as a possessive).
6. "Ben and I set up the chairs." Rule broken: subject pronouns
(*I*) serve as subjects; *me* cannot be a subject, even in a
compound.

**Application (sample):** (a) "Ben and I carried the chairs."
(b) "The teacher made programs for Ana and me." (c) "The trophy
is his." Accept any sentences meeting each condition with the
case justified by the pronoun's job.

**Exit check:** *me* — it is the object of the preposition
*to* ("went to me").

**Extension:** "The prize was given to Maya and me" — *me*
because it is an object of *to*; the original *I* fails the
drop test ("given to I").

## Lesson 02 — Intensive, vague, shifted pronouns

**Image activity:** 1. Sample (names only): "Ben holds a large
gold trophy. Maya holds an acoustic guitar. Ben and Maya stand
on a stage." Accept any three accurate, name-based sentences.
2. "Maya held the guitar." — the guitar-holder (right side, pink
sweater) is Maya. 3. "Ben showed Maya his trophy" is clear
because Ben is the only male antecedent for *his*. If both
students were boys, "he smiled" could point at either boy, so
the reader would have to guess — that guessing is what makes a
pronoun vague.

**Guided practice:** 1. Intensive — removing *herself* leaves
"Maya tuned the guitar," complete with the same core meaning.
2. Reflexive — *himself* is the object of *blamed* and equals
the subject Ben. 3. "The students took their seats, and then
they opened their notebooks." 4. "Maya told Ana that Maya had
won, and Ana smiled." (Accept the Maya-smiled version only if
both people are named: "Maya told Ana that Maya had won, and
Maya smiled.")

**Independent practice:** 1. Intensive — "The principal handed
Ben the trophy" still works. 2. Intensive — "We set up the
chairs" still works. 3. "When you join the chorus, you must
attend rehearsal." 4. Unclear: *it* could be the guitar or the
case. Sample: "Maya put her guitar in its case, and then she
gave the guitar to Ana." (Accept the case version if it names
the case.) 5. "Ben practiced every day. That practice paid
off." 6. "Ana and Maya practiced. Maya played the guitar."
(Accept the Ana version.)

**Application (sample):** "Maya and Ana entered the contest.
Maya practiced daily. When you perform, you should bow at the
end. Their performance made the judges happy." Fixes: *she* →
*Maya* (named); *a singer* → *you* (person consistent);
*this* → *their performance* (named idea).

**Exit check:** Two problems: number shift (plural *band
members* → singular *she*) and unclear antecedent (which
member?). Fix: "The band members tuned their instruments, and
then Maya played first." (Any named member is acceptable.)

**Extension:** *Team* acting as one unit is singular, so
*its/it* is consistent. Switching to *they* shifts to plural
and breaks the match.

## Lesson 03 — Nonrestrictive punctuation and spelling

**Guided practice:** 1. "Ben, our lead singer, forgot his
music." 2. Leave as is — *that Maya borrowed* is restrictive:
it identifies which guitar. 3. "The talent show — the first one
of the year — filled the hall." (Commas also accepted; both ends
must be set off.) 4. "My brother, a sixth grader, played drums."

**Independent practice:** 1. "Mrs. Alvarez, the music teacher,
announced the winners." 2. Leave as is — *that the chorus sang*
is restrictive: it identifies which songs. 3. "The final act, a
surprise duet, brought the crowd to its feet." 4. Leave as is —
*printed on yellow paper* is restrictive: it identifies which
program. 5. *principal* (the person) praises; *principle* (a
rule) is followed. 6. "They're putting their instruments there."
(*They're* = they are; *their* = ownership; *there* = place.)

**Application (sample):** "The garden club, our newest school
club, planted beans. The beans which sprouted first grew fastest.
The harvest festival (a school tradition) will feature the beans
in soup." Accept swapping which appositive takes commas and
which takes parentheses. The restrictive clause stays
unpunctuated (*that* for *which* also accepted).

**Exit check:** "The trophy, a tall gold cup, stood on the
table." The commas are needed because *a tall gold cup* is
extra, removable information renaming the trophy.

**Extension:** With commas: one sister, and Chicago is extra
information. Without commas: more than one sister, and the clause
picks out the Chicago one. The punctuation changes the meaning.

## Lesson 04 — Vocabulary

**Guided practice:** 1. *Curious* is positive (praise);
*nosy* is negative (criticism). 2. Yes — thunder cannot grumble
like a person; *grumbled* is the human quality. 3. *Weary* ≈
tired, exhausted (clue: dragging feet after a long rehearsal).
4. *re-* = again; *rewrite* = write again.

**Independent practice:** 1. *Determined* is the compliment
(firm, admirable); *stubborn* is the criticism (unreasonably
unyielding). 2. Yes — the garden "slept" (human action); effect:
quiet, still, waiting for spring. 3. Simile — it compares with
*like*; it does not give the drums a human action. 4. *Brief* ≈
short (clue: just two sentences). 5. *dis-* = not; *disagree* =
not agree. 6. *Erupted* fits a finale better — it adds
suddenness, explosive noise, and excitement that *cheered*
does not carry.

**Application (sample):** (a) "The garden waited patiently for
rain." Human quality: waiting patiently; effect: the garden
feels hopeful and alive, not just dry. (b) "The greenhouse was
cozy." Explanation: "I chose *cozy* because it praises the small
space as warm and comfortable, while *cramped* would criticize
it as too small." Accept any pair with a correct
praise/criticism explanation.

**Exit check:** Personification — *groaned* is a human sound of
complaint or weariness; effect: the piano sounds old and worn,
slightly comic or sad.

**Extension:** *Examined* = careful, neutral study (fits a
scientist and a sample). *Interrogated* = aggressive questioning
of a person (wrong for a sample; it makes the scientist seem
hostile).

## Lesson 05 — Sentence patterns and variety

**Guided practice:** 1. Complex — "while Maya played" is
dependent. 2. Simple — one subject, compound predicate, one
clause. 3. Sample: "Maya practiced, and she improved." (Also
accepted: "Because Maya practiced, she improved.") 4. "The show
ended, and everyone cheered loudly." (Two sentences also
accepted.)

**Independent practice:** 1. Complex (*After* clause).
2. Compound (two independent clauses joined by comma + *but*).
3. Sample: "Although the rain fell, the show went on."
4. Sample: "Because the lights went out, the show paused."
(Accept any added independent clause.) 5. Sample: "When Ben
forgot his music, Maya shared hers, and the duet went on."
6. Sample: "After Maya bowed and Ben bowed, the crowd stood up."
(Must be complex or compound-complex with no fact lost.)

**Application (sample):** "The garden grew beans. The students
watered and weeded them while the beans grew tall. When the
harvest came, the students cooked soup. The soup was good, and
everyone ate it." Patterns: simple, complex, complex, compound.
No fact lost; no fact added.

**Exit check (sample):** "While Maya played the guitar and Ben
sang, the crowd clapped."

**Extension:** Repeated "The…" openings make every sentence
the same beat even when all are grammatical, and they hide which
idea matters. Sample repair: "After the applause, Maya bowed."
— moving a phrase to the front breaks the pattern.

## Lesson 06 — Style, tone, formal English

**Guided practice:** 1. Formal — complete sentence, polite
form. 2. Informal. Formal version: "May I have the microphone,
please?" 3. The slip is "It was awesome and stuff." — slang
(*awesome*), vague filler (*and stuff*), wrong register for a
principal. 4. "The show was excellent."

**Independent practice:** 1. "We need the gymnasium as soon as
possible." 2. "Please let us know whether you can attend."
3. "Sincerely," — a formal closing; "Thanks!" is casual.
4. Slip: "It'll be a blast!" Fix: "We hope it will be an
enjoyable evening." (Accept similar formal revisions.)
5. Sample: "Our garden grew twelve kinds of beans." (Accept any
complete, formal sentence stating the finding.) 6. Sample: "We
invite you to attend our show on Friday evening. We hope you
will enjoy it."

**Application (sample letter):** "Dear Principal Alvarez, We
invite you to attend our spring show in the auditorium on Friday
evening. The program will include music and short plays
performed by sixth graders. We would appreciate your attendance.
Sincerely, The Sixth-Grade Class" — check: no slip, formal
throughout, 4–6 sentences plus salutation and closing.

**Exit check:** "The concert was excellent." (Accept
"impressive" or "wonderful.")

**Extension:** Informal English is right for a note to a friend,
dialogue in a story, or a joke — the audience and purpose call
for it. "Always formal" would sound stiff and distant there;
register is a choice matched to the situation, not a ladder.

## Practice A — Pronoun case

1. *me* (object of *to*). 2. *She* (subject). 3. *me* (object of
*thanked*). 4. *We* (subject). 5. *I* (than I [played]).
6. *his* (possessive standalone). 7. *me* (object of *to*).
8. "He and Maya opened the show." Rule: subject pronouns serve
as subjects; *him* cannot be a subject.

## Practice B — Intensive, vague, shifted pronouns

1. Intensive — "Ana wrote the invitations" still works.
2. Reflexive — *herself* is the object and equals the subject
Ana. 3. "The singers warmed up their voices, and then they
stepped forward." 4. "When you enter the hall, you should sign
in." 5. Sample: "Maya told Ana that Maya had won, and Ana
cried." (Accept the Maya-cried version if fully named.)
6. "Ana practiced for a month. That month of practice impressed
everyone." 7. Unclear: *it* = the guitar or the chair. Sample:
"Ana put the guitar on the chair, and then she knocked the
guitar over." (Accept the chair version.) 8. Unclear: *they* =
the people or the lights. Sample: "The principal and Maya
checked the lights, and the lights worked." (Accept the
people-worked reading if it names the people: "…and they worked
on the lights until late.")

## Practice C — Punctuation and spelling

1. "Ana, our stage manager, called places." 2. Leave as is —
*that Ana sewed* is restrictive (which costumes). 3. "The show,
a spring tradition, sold out." 4. Leave as is — *printed by the
art club* is restrictive (which banner). 5. "The judges — one
teacher and two parents — took notes." (Commas accepted.)
6. *principal* (the person). 7. *It's* (= it is). 8. "The
students left their coats there."

## Practice D — Vocabulary

1. *Cozy* praises (warm, comfortable); *cramped* criticizes (too
small, uncomfortable). 2. Yes — *whispered* is a human sound;
effect: a quiet, secretive night. 3. Simile — comparison with
*like*, not a human action given to a thing. 4. *Restless* ≈
unable to sit still; impatient or excited (clues: shifting seats,
checking the clock). 5. *mis-* = wrongly; *misread* = read
wrongly. 6. *trans-* = across, *port* = carry; *transport* =
carry across (verify in a dictionary). 7. *Stormed* adds anger
and force; it fits an angry entrance, *walked* does not.
8. A menu uses *inexpensive* (neutral/positive); *cheap*
suggests low quality as well as low price.

## Practice E — Sentence variety and formal style

1. Complex. 2. Compound. 3. Sample: "Ana sewed the costumes,
and Ben built the sets." 4. "The curtain rose, and the actors
froze." (Two sentences accepted.) 5. Sample: "After the final
song, the cast bowed." 6. Sample: "When the show ended, the
crowd stood and the cast bowed." 7. "We need the gymnasium on
Friday." (Accept "We must have the gymnasium on Friday.")
8. Slip: "The show is gonna be great!" Fix: "The show will be
wonderful." (Accept "excellent.")

## Formative quiz (week 18)

1. *me*. 2. Intensive — removing *herself* leaves "Maya
planted the beans," complete with the same core meaning.
3. "The students finished their work, and then they left."
4. Sample: "Ana told Maya that Ana had won, and Maya cheered."
(Accept any fully-named, consistent version.) 5. "Ben, our
drummer, missed rehearsal." 6. Leave as is — *that the band
played* is restrictive (which songs). 7. "The cabin was cozy."
(Accept *snug*.) 8. Complex. 9. Sample: "The library's book sale
raised money for new shelves by selling donated books." (Accept
any one-sentence central idea naming the sale, the donated
books, and the shelves.) 10. Not supported — the passage says
*some* visitors argued for a monthly sale; *some* is not
*every*, and no evidence shows every visitor wanted one.
11. *Donated* = gave for free. Clue: the librarian explains the
sale worked "because families donated books" — the books were
given to the sale, not bought by the library. 12. Sample: "When
the sale ended, the tables were empty."

## Unit assessment

**Part A:** A1. "Maya and I are planning the games." A2. "The
fair, our biggest event of the year, needs volunteers." A3.
Leave as is — *who sign up early* is restrictive (which
volunteers). A4. Sample: "Ana told Maya that Ana had won the
poster contest, and Maya cheered." (Accept any fully-named,
consistent version.) A5. "When you sell tickets, you should
smile." A6. Sample: "The fair will have food, music, and
games." A7. *there* (place), not *their* (possession): "The
games will be held there on the field." A8. "The fair will be
wonderful." (Accept "excellent.")

**Part B:** B1. Complex. B2. Compound. B3. Sample: "When the
fair opened, the band played and the crowd danced."

**Part C:** C1. *Flashy* criticizes (gaudy, overdone). Praising
word: *vivid* or *eye-catching* — "Vivid praises the poster's
bright colors without suggesting bad taste." C2. Personification
— *smiled* is a human expression; effect: a warm, happy,
welcoming mood. C3. *re-* = again; *reopen* = open again.
C4. *Eager* ≈ excited and willing (clue: lining up before the
gates opened).

**Part D (sample letter, 5 points):** "Dear Principal Alvarez,
We invite you to attend the Riverbend School spring fair on
Saturday, May 16. The fair will include food, music, games, and
the poster contest. We would appreciate your attendance.
Sincerely, The Sixth-Grade Class" — score: 2 for formal tone
throughout (no slip), 2 for content (invitation + date + one
detail), 1 for the formal closing. Deduct the tone points if any
sentence slips into slang or fragments.

## Project — Sentence Studio Portfolio (sample + scoring notes)

**Sample Part 1 revision (patterns labeled):** "The garden grew
beans, and Ana, our garden captain, led the work. [Cd] Ana and I
watered them. [S] The beans which grew tallest were picked
first. [S] Ana told Maya that Ana had won the garden prize, and
Maya smiled. [Cd] When the students cooked soup, they stirred it
slowly. [Cx] The soup was excellent, and everyone ate it. [Cd]"
Checks: case fixed (*Ana and I*); appositive set off with
commas; restrictive clause left alone; vague pronoun fully
named (Ana won, Maya smiled — the reverse choice is equally
acceptable); shift fixed (third-person *students/they*
throughout); tone formal (*excellent* for *awesome and stuff*);
all three patterns present; no fact lost, none added.

**Scoring notes:** award full Pronoun points only when winner
and smiler are both named. Award full Punctuation points only
when the appositive is set off at *both* ends and the restrictive
clause is untouched — a revision that commas off "which grew
tallest" loses a point there even if everything else is right.
For Part 2, accept any correct connotation pair, personification
with a named effect, and affix breakdown verified against a
dictionary. For Part 3, the adult notes only whether the reading
stayed formal throughout; a stumble over a word is not a tone
slip.
