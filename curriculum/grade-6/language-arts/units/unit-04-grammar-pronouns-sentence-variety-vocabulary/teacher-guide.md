# Teacher Guide — Unit 04 (Adult Only)

**Role of this guide:** pacing, misconception, support, and
readiness guidance. It contains **no student answers**. All
solutions live in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) (adult only).
Do not show learners either file.

## Pacing notes (16 sessions, ~40 minutes each)

- **Week 15 (Sessions 1–4):** Lesson 01 must land before Lesson
  02: learners need the case-by-job habit before they judge
  shifts and vagueness. Session 2's drill is the diagnostic for
  Lesson 01 — if compounds still fail, re-teach with the drop
  test on two-person subjects before moving on. Session 3's image
  activity must include the text-only alternative for every
  learner, whether or not they can see the image — the
  alternative is the lesson's equity check, not a fallback.
- **Week 16 (Sessions 5–8):** Lesson 03's removal test is the
  unit's hinge: protect Session 6 from being squeezed, because
  restrictive/nonrestrictive errors reappear in the assessment.
  Lesson 04's connotation work is the lightest lift; do not let
  it swallow the affix/context-clue routine, which Unit 05's
  reading will assume.
- **Week 17 (Sessions 9–12):** Lesson 05 needs the full 40
  minutes: do not rush the simple-vs.-compound distinction (one
  subject with a compound predicate is still simple). Project
  Session 12: read the rubric aloud before drafting; learners
  who plan against the rubric rarely need a full rewrite.
  Lesson 06's formal letter is the project's tone rehearsal —
  keep it.
- **Week 18 (Sessions 13–16):** score the quiz the same day and
  build Session 14's reteach list standard by standard. The unit
  assessment (Session 15) uses a new passage on purpose: do not
  pre-teach "The Spring Fair Announcement" in Session 14.
  Session 16's reflection is the midyear close: ask each learner
  to name one grammar move and one vocabulary move they will
  carry into Unit 05.

## Misconceptions and reteaching moves

| Misconception | What you will hear | Reteach move |
|---|---|---|
| Polite *I* everywhere | "For Maya and I" (object) | Drop test: "for I" fails, "for me" passes. Politeness is order ("Maya and I"), not case. |
| *-self* word as subject | "Myself and Maya tuned…" | *-self* words are never subjects. Replace with the subject pronoun and test. |
| Intensive = reflexive | "Herself is always emphasis" | Removal test: if the sentence breaks or changes meaning without it, it is reflexive (object), not intensive. |
| New pronoun fixes vagueness | "She → they" as a fix | A fix must name the person or thing. Insist on names for vague-pronoun repairs. |
| Every middle clause gets commas | "The songs, that the chorus sang, …" | Removal test + "which one?" If the clause picks out which one, it is restrictive — no commas. |
| One-sided punctuation | "Maya, who plays guitar opened…" | An element set off at the start must be set off at the end. Read aloud and listen for the missing pause. |
| Possessive apostrophes | "Her's, their's, it's" for ownership | Possessive pronouns never take apostrophes. *It's* = "it is" only; test by expanding. |
| Comparison = opinion | "Cheap and thrifty mean the same" | Denotation overlaps; connotation decides. Ask: "Would most readers hear praise or criticism?" |
| Every comparison is personification | "Like a heartbeat = personification" | Personification gives a human action to a nonhuman subject; *like/as* comparisons are similes. |
| Long = complex; *and* = compound | "The judges watched and took notes is compound" | Count clauses, not words. One subject + compound predicate = one clause = simple. |
| Comma splice as a fix | "The show ended, everyone cheered" | Two independent clauses need a conjunction, a semicolon, or a split — a comma alone never joins them. |
| Formal = inflated | "We hereby utilization-request…" | Formal means complete, precise, and polite — not longer or harder words. Reward plain formal sentences. |
| Illustration = evidence | "The picture proves Ben won" | State plainly: the image is an AI-generated illustration for this lesson. It shows a *kind* of scene; it names no real student and records no real event. |

## Differentiation and supports

- **Reading access:** read every lesson sentence aloud once
  before silent work; keep the Lesson 01 case table and the
  Lesson 05 pattern table visible all unit.
- **Language access:** let learners point at a table column,
  circle pronouns and draw antecedent arrows, or explain fixes
  orally before writing them.
- **Alternate response modes:** oral drop tests, clapped
  sentence rhythms (Lesson 05), and acted personification
  (Lesson 04) all count as full participation; writing follows
  speech, not the reverse.
- **Extension:** learners who finish early take the Lesson 06
  extension (when informal is the right choice) and the project
  rubric's 3-point bands as a revision checklist for their own
  draft before Session 14.
- **Midyear review:** Session 14 reteaches from quiz evidence
  only — do not re-teach the whole unit. Learners who miss only
  vocabulary items join the vocabulary station; learners who
  miss only punctuation join the punctuation station.

## Readiness and reteach signals

- **Ready for Unit 05 (narrative craft)** when the learner can:
  fix a compound-subject case error with the drop test; name a
  vague pronoun and repair it with a name; set off one
  nonrestrictive element at both ends; combine three choppy
  sentences without losing a fact; and hold a formal tone for
  four sentences.
- **Reteach now** if the learner still: uses *I* as an object in
  compounds; "fixes" vague pronouns with other pronouns; commas
  off restrictive clauses; or writes a paragraph of identical
  simple sentences after Lesson 05.
- **Next teaching steps:** Unit 05 will assume this unit's
  sentence-variety and tone work when learners draft narratives
  with dialogue and pacing; carry forward any learner whose
  Lesson 05/06 work is still shaky into Unit 05's first drafting
  session as a named support, not a repeat of this unit.
