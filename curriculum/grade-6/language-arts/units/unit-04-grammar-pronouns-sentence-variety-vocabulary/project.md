# Project — Sentence Studio Portfolio

**Time:** Sessions 12 and 14 (draft, then polish and oral reading).
**Standards:** L.6.1, L.6.2, L.6.3, L.6.5, SL.6.6.

## The task

You are the editor of "The Garden Log," the school garden
newsletter. The draft paragraph below is flat, confusing, and
informal. Your portfolio has three parts. All draft sentences are
original and fictional.

### Part 1 — Revise the paragraph

Revise all eight draft sentences into one polished paragraph.
Your revision must:

- fix the pronoun-case error and the vague pronoun (use names
  where the reader would otherwise guess);
- fix the pronoun shift so person and number stay consistent;
- punctuate the appositive correctly — and leave the restrictive
  clause unpunctuated;
- use formal English throughout (no slang, no shortened forms);
- include at least one simple, one compound, and one complex
  sentence;
- lose no fact and add no new fact.

> **Draft.** "The garden grew beans. Ana our garden captain led
> the work. Me and Ana watered them. The beans which grew tallest
> were picked first. Ana told Maya she had won the garden prize,
> she smiled. The students cooked soup. When you cook soup, a cook
> should stir it slowly. The soup was awesome and stuff."

### Part 2 — Vocabulary showcase

On a separate page, show three vocabulary moves:

(a) **A connotation pair:** two words with the same denotation
but different connotations (for example, *cozy* vs. *cramped*),
each used in its own sentence, with one sentence explaining which
word praises and which criticizes.
(b) **A personification sentence** about the garden, plus one
sentence naming the human quality and the effect it creates.
(c) **An affix breakdown:** one word broken into affix + root
(for example, *rewrite* = *re-* [again] + *write*), with the
whole word's meaning and one sentence using it correctly.

### Part 3 — Formal oral reading

Read your revised paragraph aloud to the class or the guiding
adult (about one minute). Use formal English, complete sentences,
and a steady pace. Rehearse your first and last sentences; that
is where tone slips cluster.

## Deliverables

1. The revised paragraph (Part 1), with the three sentence
   patterns labeled in the margin (S = simple, Cd = compound,
   Cx = complex).
2. The vocabulary showcase page (Part 2).
3. The oral reading (Part 3), delivered live; the adult notes
   whether the reading stayed formal throughout.

## Rubric (12 points; adult scores)

| Criterion | 3 — Meets fully | 2 — Mostly | 1 — Partly | 0 — Missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pronoun clarity and case | Case error fixed; vague pronoun named; shift fixed; no new pronoun error | Two of the three pronoun fixes complete | One fix complete, or fixes introduce a new error | No pronoun fix attempted |
| Punctuation and spelling | Appositive set off at both ends; restrictive clause left alone; spelling correct | One punctuation slip, or one spelling slip | Two slips, or the restrictive clause wrongly punctuated | No punctuation work shown |
| Sentence variety | At least one simple, one compound, and one complex sentence, correctly labeled; no fragment or run-on | Two patterns present, or one labeling error | One pattern only, or a fragment/run-on remains | No variety attempted |
| Word choice and formal tone | Formal English throughout; vocabulary showcase complete (pair, personification, affix) with explanations | One informal slip, or one showcase part thin | Two slips, or a showcase part missing | Informal throughout, or showcase missing |

**Scoring guidance (adult):** see [answer-key.md](answer-key.md)
§ Project for a sample revision and scoring notes. Accept any
fully-named version of the prize sentence: the draft does not say
who won or who smiled, so the learner must choose and name both
consistently (for example, Ana won and Maya smiled, or Maya won
and Ana smiled). The adult checks that both people are named and
the sentence is consistent — not which names were chosen. A
revision that still leaves a pronoun with two possible antecedents
does not earn full points for Pronoun clarity.
