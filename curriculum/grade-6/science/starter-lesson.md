# Starter Lesson — Model water moving through a system

**Status:** draft supplement. **Time:** three 35-minute sessions. **Goal:** Draw a simple water-cycle model, distinguish model from reality, and use sample observations to explain where water may move.

## Adult preparation and readiness

Use paper, colored pencils, and a clear lidded container with warm tap water only if adult can supervise; otherwise use the diagram text below. Avoid hot/boiling water. Readiness: learner identifies evaporation as liquid water changing to vapor or is willing to learn the terms.

## Teach and model

Explain: water can evaporate into vapor, cool and condense into droplets, then return as liquid. In the environment, the Sun supplies energy and gravity moves water downhill. A small sealed container models some steps but leaves out clouds, wind, landscapes, and groundwater.

**Worked example 1 (model):** A few droplets form on the inside lid above warm water. The model suggests water vapor cooled at the lid and condensed; droplets are evidence, but the container is not a cloud.

**Worked example 2 (sample table):** At minute 0, 3 droplets are visible; at minute 10, 11 droplets; at minute 20, 18 droplets. The count increased by 8, then 7. It shows a pattern in this sample, not a universal rate.

## Pacing and tasks

Session 1: adult explains terms and models diagram; guide tasks 1–2. Session 2: observe container or analyze sample table; learner records. Session 3: learner explains, error-checks, and revises model.

1. **Guided:** Put `liquid water`, `vapor`, and `droplets` in a labeled arrow diagram.
2. **Guided:** Point to the step represented by droplets on a cooler lid.
3. **Independent:** Describe what changed from minute 0 to minute 20 in the sample data.
4. **Independent:** Name one thing the closed-container model does not represent well.
5. **Adult-guided error check:** A learner says, “The water disappeared.” Use the model to explain where water may have gone.
6. **Application:** Write a short explanation linking an observation to condensation and one limit of the model.

**Answer checks:** Order may cycle: liquid → vapor → droplets/liquid. Droplets indicate condensation; count rose by 15 overall; model omits weather/land/large-scale movement; water changed state and stayed in the closed system, though some vapor may be invisible. Do not claim that sample data are learner observations.

## Exit check and product

Learner labels two arrows and explains one limit. Product: annotated model plus evidence sentence, based on own safe observation or clearly marked sample data.

## Review rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model | Shows correct state changes and labels | One label needs prompt | Use word cards |
| Evidence | Describes an actual/sample observation accurately | Gives a vague observation | Review table together |
| Limits | Names a feature omitted by the model | Names with choices | Compare container to outdoor water cycle |

Use verbal arrows, tactile cards, text-only diagram, large print, or oral response. Adult handles warm water/container and prevents spills; never use hot water or seal a glass jar with heated liquid. Offline text-based route is complete. This sample is an introductory model, not evidence for climate claims.
