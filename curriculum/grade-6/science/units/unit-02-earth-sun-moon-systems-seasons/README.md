# Unit 02 — Earth–Sun–Moon Systems and Seasons

**Grade:** 6 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#31](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/31)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 7–10 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 45 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

Every clear night the sky tells a story about three moving bodies: the Earth
you stand on, the Moon that changes shape, and the Sun that seems to travel
overhead. This unit builds a **working Earth–Sun–Moon model** — first with a
shadow stick and a flashlight, then a lamp and two balls, then a tilted globe —
and uses it to explain four sky patterns: **day and night** (Earth's rotation),
**lunar phases** (viewing geometry as the Moon orbits), **eclipses** (rare
alignments of the three bodies), and **seasons** (axial tilt, not distance).
The Unit 01 discipline comes along for the ride: for every model the learner
names what it **includes** and what it **leaves out**, and every number set is
labeled *your measurement*, *sample data*, or *reference value* — never
presented as proof about the whole world.

Two ideas this unit deliberately attacks are the most stubborn misconceptions
in school astronomy: that the Moon's phases are caused by Earth's shadow, and
that summer happens because Earth is closer to the Sun. The learner tests both
against evidence — and finds that the geometry itself says no.

## Prerequisites (U01 skills re-used; re-taught as needed)

- Testable questions, labeled data tables with units, repeated trials.
- Line graphs for change over time (shadow-length vs. time in Lesson 01).
- Models include some things and leave others out; a model is not the real
  system.
- Safety: an adult supervises every investigation; **never look directly at
  the sun**; lamp bulbs get hot — the adult handles the lamp.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (mapped to the track
objectives in parentheses):

1. **Explain day and night by Earth's rotation** (west-to-east spin, about
   24 hours) and **use shadow direction and length** to infer the Sun's
   position and the time of day; distinguish **rotation** from **revolution**.
   (Track objective 5)
2. **Model lunar phases** with a lamp-and-ball setup: given a Moon position,
   **predict the phase seen from Earth** and justify it with viewing geometry;
   state and reject the "Earth's shadow causes phases" explanation with
   evidence. (Track objective 4)
3. **Model solar and lunar eclipses** as Sun–Earth–Moon alignments; **explain
   why eclipses do not happen every month** using the Moon's ~5° orbital tilt;
   describe safe eclipse viewing. (Track objective 4)
4. **Explain seasons by axial tilt** (23.5°, fixed direction) combined with
   revolution: name which hemisphere leans toward the Sun and why that brings
   longer days and more direct light; **use the perihelion/aphelion evidence**
   to reject the distance explanation. (Track objective 5)
5. **Audit the unit's models** (shadow stick, lamp-and-ball, tilted globe):
   name what each **includes and leaves out** — especially that desk models
   are not to scale — and revise a claim when the model is overextended.
   (Track objective 3)

## Essential question

*How can three moving bodies and one tilted axis explain everything the sky
seems to do — and where do our tabletop models stop being trustworthy?*

## Vocabulary

**Rotation** — spinning on an axis (Earth: about once per 24 hours).
**Revolution** — orbiting around another body (Earth around the Sun: about one
year). **Axis** — the imaginary line a body spins around. **Phase** — how much
of the Moon's lit half faces Earth at a given time. **Waxing** — the lit part
is growing (toward full). **Waning** — the lit part is shrinking (toward new).
**Gibbous** — more than half lit but not full. **Crescent** — less than half
lit. **Solar eclipse** — the Moon blocks sunlight from reaching part of Earth.
**Lunar eclipse** — Earth blocks sunlight from reaching the Moon. **Axial
tilt** — the 23.5° lean of Earth's axis relative to its orbit. **Solstice** —
the year's longest or shortest daylight day. **Equinox** — the year's two days
when day and night are about equal. **Perihelion** — Earth's closest point to
the Sun (early January, ~147.1 million km). **Aphelion** — Earth's farthest
point from the Sun (early July, ~152.1 million km). **Hemisphere** — half of
Earth (northern or southern).

   tilted-globe demonstration, hemisphere-opposite seasons, the

   model-vs-reality audit across the unit, scale honesty, misconception recap.
