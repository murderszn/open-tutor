# Teacher Guide — Unit 02 (Earth–Sun–Moon systems and seasons)

For the adult facilitating this unit. Student materials never contain answers;
all solutions live in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## Pacing and session notes

- **S2 shadow observation** needs a sunny day; have the flashlight-and-ball
  backup ready. Four readings minimum; hourly is ideal but two (morning +
  midday) still show the pattern.
- **S6 lamp-and-ball lab** is the unit's hinge — do not rush it. The learner
  must *operate* the model (walk the Moon ball around), not watch the adult
  do it. Prediction-before-checking is what kills the shadow misconception.
- **S9 pinhole viewer:** the adult handles the pin and checks the viewer.
  Never look at the Sun; the viewer is used with the learner's back to the
  Sun.
- **S14 showcase:** require the model, not memory. If the learner recites
  without posing the model, send them back to it — the rubric rewards
  demonstration.

## Misconceptions by lesson

| Lesson | Watch for | Move |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | "The Sun moves around Earth." | Flashlight never moves, yet sunrise/sunset happen. Ask: "What moved?" |
| 01 | "Shadows point toward the Sun." | Panel A/C: light from the east → shadow west. Opposite, always. |
| 02 | "Earth's shadow causes phases." | The big one. Make the learner produce a crescent with no shadow in the model, then ask what that proves. Keep the chant: *phases = geometry, eclipses = shadows.* |
| 02 | "Quarter = 25% lit." | Worked example 2: count the lit fraction at position 3 (50%). "Quarter" = position in cycle. |
| 03 | "Eclipses happen monthly." | Tilt the orbit ~5° and run six "months" — count the misses. |
| 03 | Merging L02/L03 ("phases are mini-eclipses"). | Ask: "In your phase model, what cast a shadow on the Moon?" (Nothing.) |
| 04 | "Summer = closer to the Sun." | Two killer facts: perihelion in **January**; hemispheres have **opposite** seasons. Agree the orbit is slightly elliptical, then ask what an ellipse predicts about shared seasons. |
| 04 | Axis "follows" Earth around. | Finger-trace both axis lines in the SVG: parallel = same direction in space. |
| 05 | "The model proves sizes/distances." | Model-audit table; "desk models get geometry right, scale wrong." |

## Reteaching moves (triggered by the Week-10 quiz)

- Q1–Q2 weak → re-run the flashlight-ball demo; learner narrates sunrise to
  sunset while turning.
- Q3–Q4 weak → back to the lamp-and-ball with prediction-before-checking;
  re-do guided practice 3 of Lesson 02.
- Q5–Q6 weak → pose the three balls for both eclipse types; learner states
  middle body + shadow direction each time.
- Q7–Q9 weak → tilted-globe re-demo with the learner calling "which
  hemisphere leans in?"; re-read worked example 2 of Lesson 04 aloud.
- Q10 weak → Lesson 05 guided practice 2 (safe/overreach sort) as a card game.

## Differentiation

- **Reading load:** all worked examples are designed to be read aloud by the
  adult; the learner's job is the reasoning, not decoding.
- **Response modes:** pointing, shading blank circles, arranging balls, and
  dictated answers are all first-class (see each lesson's supports).
- **Extension paths:** Lesson 01 sundial research; Lesson 02 tidal locking;
  Lesson 03 next-eclipse lookup; Lesson 04 solstice day-length computation;
  Lesson 05 model improvement. Each is optional and never assumed later.
- **Math:** the only arithmetic is the 29.5-day cycle and the
  perihelion/aphelion subtraction — both worked in the key; a calculator is
  fine.

## Project facilitation

- Approve claim sets in S7: each claim must name its phenomenon and be
  *demonstrable* (reject "the Moon is interesting").
- During S12, if a model can't show the 5° tilt, a tilted orbit ring drawn
  on paper is acceptable — note it on the limits card as an improvement.
- Challenge questions for S14 (pick two): "Show me why there's no eclipse at
  this month's new moon." / "Pose the model for a lunar eclipse and name the
  phase." / "What breaks if the tilt were 0°?" / "Point to something your
  model leaves out and tell me why it matters."
- Score with the project rubric; 9–12 means revise *one* weak criterion and
  re-defend, not redo everything.

## Safety (non-negotiable)

- Never look directly at the Sun — including during any eclipse, through
  sunglasses, or through optics. The pinhole viewer is used back-to-Sun.
- Lamp bulbs get hot: the adult positions and adjusts the lamp; prefer LED.
- Sharp skewers: adult handles, or substitute clay bases.
- Moon journal (Lesson 02): the adult supervises timing/location; "clouded
  out" is a valid entry.

## Assessment notes

- The unit assessment allows the two SVG diagrams and the shadow-stick image
  (they are models to read, not answer sheets).
- Part C is the unit's thesis in miniature: a correct answer names both the
  factual correction *and* the audit principle. Score it 4 only if both
  appear.
- Do not describe this assessment as standardized or validated; it is a
  curriculum-embedded check written for these objectives.
