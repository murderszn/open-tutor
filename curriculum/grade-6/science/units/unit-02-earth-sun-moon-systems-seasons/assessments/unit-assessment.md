# Unit Assessment — Unit 02 (Earth–Sun–Moon systems and seasons)

**Use:** S15, ~45 minutes. Closed-book except the two SVG diagrams and the
shadow-stick image, which are provided. Scored with
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md). Do not place the key beside these
questions.

## Part A — Selected response (1 point each)

**A1.** Day and night are caused by…
- a. the Sun traveling around Earth once a day
- b. Earth rotating on its axis about once every 24 hours
- c. the Moon blocking the Sun each night
- d. Earth revolving around the Sun once a day

**A2.** Near midday, a vertical stick's shadow is…
- a. long and pointing east
- b. long and pointing west
- c. very short, near the stick's base
- d. pointing toward the Sun

**A3.** The Moon's phases are caused by…
- a. Earth's shadow falling on the Moon each month
- b. the Moon changing shape as it orbits
- c. how much of the Moon's always-lit half faces Earth
- d. clouds passing in front of the Moon

**A4.** "First quarter" means…
- a. one-quarter of the Moon's disk looks lit
- b. the Moon is one-quarter of the way through its monthly cycle
- c. the Moon is one-quarter of its real size
- d. the first eclipse of the quarter year

**A5.** A solar eclipse happens when…
- a. Earth is between the Sun and the Moon
- b. the Moon is between the Sun and Earth
- c. the Sun is between Earth and the Moon
- d. the Moon's shadow falls on the Sun

**A6.** There is no solar eclipse at every new moon mainly because…
- a. the Moon is too far away most months
- b. the Moon's orbit is tilted about 5°, so its shadow usually misses Earth
- c. new moons only happen in winter
- d. Earth's shadow blocks the Moon's shadow

**A7.** Seasons are mainly caused by…
- a. Earth's changing distance from the Sun
- b. Earth's 23.5° axial tilt combined with revolution around the Sun
- c. the Moon's gravitational pull on the oceans
- d. sunspots making the Sun hotter in summer

**A8.** Earth is closest to the Sun (perihelion) in…
- a. early July
- b. early January
- c. late March
- d. late September

## Part B — Constructed response (3 points each)

**B1.** Using the shadow-stick image (`assets/shadow-stick-three-times.png`):
match Panels A, B, and C to morning / near midday / evening, and for *one*
panel explain the full reasoning (Sun position → shadow direction and length
→ time of day).

**B2.** With the orbit diagram: the Moon is at position 6. (a) Name the phase.
(b) Is it waxing or waning? (c) Explain how you used the Sun's direction to
decide how much of the disk looks lit.

**B3.** A friend insists Earth's shadow causes the Moon's phases. Give the
two strongest counter-arguments from this unit (one from the lamp-and-ball
model, one about eclipses).

**B4.** Draw and label the top-down lineup for a **solar eclipse**. Below the
drawing, write two sentences: why it must happen at new moon, and why it
still doesn't happen at *every* new moon.

**B5.** It is June. Explain why it is summer in the northern hemisphere and
winter in the southern hemisphere. Use **tilt**, **direct light**, and
**day length**.

**B6.** The distance claim says summer comes from being closer to the Sun.
(a) State the perihelion and aphelion values with units. (b) Explain why
these values, plus the fact that hemispheres have opposite seasons,
contradict the claim.

## Part C — Model audit task (4 points)

**C1.** Your lamp-and-ball model shows the Moon going through all eight
phases. Your classmate concludes from it: "The model proves the Moon takes
exactly 30 days to orbit Earth, and that the real Moon–Earth distance is what
the balls show."
(a) Identify the **factual error** in the timing claim and state the correct
reference value. (b) Explain, with the model-audit rule, why the model cannot
prove distance or timing claims even when a number it suggests happens to be
close.
