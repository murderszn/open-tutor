# Practice Bank — Unit 02

Extra practice keyed to the lessons. Work a few per session (S3, S7, S11);
your adult picks which. All items are solved in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md).

## Lesson 01 — Day and night

**P1.** A stick's shadow points **west** and is long. (a) Where is the Sun?
(b) Morning or evening? Explain.
**P2.** It is noon at your town. Your cousin lives exactly halfway around the
world. What time is it for her, and is it day or night there? Explain with
rotation.
**P3.** Draw two diagrams: Earth rotating (arrows on the ball) and Earth
revolving (arrow around the Sun). Label which motion makes day/night and which
makes the year.
**P4.** Error analysis: "In summer the Sun is higher, so shadows are longer."
Use the shadow rules to correct this sentence — what is the real
shadow-length pattern through a single day?
**P5.** A sundial's markings are spaced for a particular latitude. Using this
lesson, explain why a sundial built for the equator would read wrong near the
poles. (Two to three sentences.)

## Lesson 02 — Moon phases

**P6.** Tonight is a full moon. (a) Draw the Sun–Earth–Moon lineup (top-down,
labeled). (b) About how many days until the next new moon? Show the math.
**P7.** The Moon is at position 4 in the orbit diagram. Name the phase, say
waxing or waning, and estimate the fraction of the disk that looks lit.
**P8.** The Moon is at position 8. Same three questions as P7.
**P9.** Explain-your-reasoning: an astronaut standing on the Moon's near side
watches Earth for a month. Describe what *Earth's* phases would look like —
when Earth looks "full" from the Moon, what phase do we see from Earth?
(Hint: draw the lineup for our full moon and stand on the Moon.)
**P10.** A website says, "The dark part of the Moon during a crescent is
Earth's shadow." Write a two-sentence correction a 4th-grader could
understand, using the lamp-and-ball model as evidence.

## Lesson 03 — Eclipses

**P11.** Sketch the lineup for a lunar eclipse (top-down, labeled). Mark
whose shadow falls on whom and name the Moon's phase.
**P12.** Why can a solar eclipse never happen at full moon? Answer with the
lineup, not with memorized words.
**P13.** The Moon's orbit were *not* tilted (0° instead of 5°). Describe what
would happen to eclipses: more, fewer, or the same? Explain.
**P14.** During a total solar eclipse, daytime birds go quiet and the sky
darkens. A friend says this proves the Sun "turned off." Give the model-based
correction in two sentences.
**P15.** Your pinhole viewer shows a small bright disk with a bite taken out
of one side. What is happening in the sky, and why is the viewer the safe way
to watch it?

## Lesson 04 — Seasons

**P16.** It is December. (a) Which hemisphere leans toward the Sun? (b) What
season is it in each hemisphere? (c) Which hemisphere has longer days?
**P17.** A planet has an axial tilt of 0° but the same orbit as Earth.
Describe its year: are there seasons? What *does* still change? (Two to three
sentences.)
**P18.** A planet has an axial tilt of 90° (axis lying in its orbital plane).
Predict: what would its seasons be like at the poles? Reason from the
tilt-and-revolution mechanism, not from memory of any real planet.
**P19.** Data check: perihelion ≈ 147.1 million km (early January), aphelion
≈ 152.1 million km (early July). (a) Which is larger, and by how much?
(b) A friend says "3% closer in January, so January should be warmer." Give
the two-sentence rebuttal using the hemisphere evidence.
**P20.** Explain-your-reasoning: the tilt diagram's two axes are drawn
parallel. Why is that the single most important detail in the whole diagram?
What would change if the axis "followed" Earth around (always leaning toward
the Sun)?

## Mixed review (S13)

**P21.** Sort these into *rotation* or *revolution* effects: day/night, the
year, sunrise/sunset, the seasons' cycle, the Moon's phases.
**P22.** Three claims — mark each **supported**, **contradicted**, or
**untestable by our models**, and say why:
  a. "The Moon's phases repeat about monthly."
  b. "Earth's shadow causes the seasons."
  c. "The Sun is bigger than Earth."
**P23.** You show a younger student the lamp-and-ball model and they ask,
"Is the lamp really as close to the balls as the Sun is to Earth?" Give the
honest answer, including what the model is still good for.
**P24.** Write a four-sentence "tour guide" script for the whole unit: start
with day/night, visit phases, eclipses, and seasons, and end with the
model-audit rule. Every sentence must contain one vocabulary word used
correctly.
