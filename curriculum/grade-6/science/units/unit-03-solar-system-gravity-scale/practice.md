# Practice — Unit 03 (Solar System, Gravity, and Scale)

Use the dataset table in the [unit README](README.md) for every set. Show
your working for calculations. Solutions are in the [answer key](answer-key.md)
— do not open it until you have attempted a set.

## Set A — Planet order and groups (Lesson 01)

1. Write the eight planets in order from the Sun.
2. Which planets are rocky, and which are giant? Write two lists.
3. What lies between Mars and Jupiter? What is it made of, in one phrase?
4. Which planet is 5th from the Sun, and which group is it in?
5. A picture shows nine planets including Pluto. Explain in two sentences
   why that picture is out of date.
6. The orbit illustration draws a large blue planet at the top that looks
   bigger than the banded brown planet at the bottom. If the banded planet
   is Jupiter (diameter 139,820 km), use the table to explain why that
   drawing distorts sizes: which planet, if any, should ever look bigger
   than Jupiter?

## Set B — Gravity and weight (Lesson 02)

1. In one sentence, what is gravity?
2. Why does Earth not fall into the Sun? Use the words "pull" and
   "sideways" in your answer.
3. A rock weighs 60 N on Earth. Calculate its weight on Mars (3.71 m/s²)
   and on Jupiter (24.79 m/s²). Round to the nearest whole newton.
4. A rock weighs 200 N on Earth. Calculate its weight on Venus (8.87 m/s²)
   and on Uranus (8.69 m/s²). Which is heavier, and by how much?
5. Does an astronaut's mass change on the Moon? Does her weight? Answer
   each in one sentence.
6. **Error analysis:** "The Moon stays up because there is no gravity in
   space." Identify the error and write the correction in two sentences.

## Set C — Size ratios (Lesson 03)

1. Compute Earth's ratio (it should be 1.00) and Mercury's ratio from the
   table. Show both divisions.
2. Order these planets from widest to narrowest using ratios: Mars, Saturn,
   Venus, Neptune. Give each ratio.
3. On the Earth = 1 cm scale, how wide is Uranus's circle? Round to the
   nearest millimetre.
4. If Earth is a 3 cm ball, how wide is Jupiter's ball? Show the
   multiplication.
5. The Sun's ratio is about 109. On the Earth = 2 cm scale, how wide is the
   Sun in centimetres? Would it fit on a 30 cm strip?
6. **Error analysis:** A learner says, "Saturn is 103,718 km bigger than
   Earth, so Saturn is 103,718 times as wide." What is confused here, and
   what is Saturn's correct ratio? (Saturn 116,460 km; Earth 12,742 km.)

## Set D — Distances and AU (Lesson 04)

1. Compute Venus's distance in AU (108.2 ÷ 149.6). Round to two decimals.
2. On the 1 AU = 10 m line, where do Venus's and Neptune's markers go?
3. How far apart are Mars's and Jupiter's markers on the 1 AU = 10 m line?
4. A hallway is 25 m long. On the 1 AU = 10 m scale, which planets' markers
   fit inside it? (Markers at 10 × AU metres.)
5. On the Earth = 1 cm size scale, how far is Saturn from the Sun marker?
   (1,433.5 million km ÷ 12,742 km per cm, converted to metres, rounded to
   the nearest 10 m.)
6. **Explain your reasoning:** Why is "Neptune is 30 AU away" more useful
   than "Neptune is 4,495.1 million km away" when comparing planets?
   Give one reason about comparison and one about memory.

## Set E — Models, misconceptions, mixed review (Lesson 05)

1. Audit the size strip: one thing it shows truly, one thing it distorts.
2. Audit the distance line: one thing it shows truly, one thing it distorts.
3. A poster claims "Planets orbit because the Sun's light pushes them
   around." What is wrong, and what actually holds them in orbit?
4. Order these events by year length, shortest first: a Neptune year, a
   Mars year, a Mercury year, a Jupiter year. Give each year in Earth days
   (or years where the unit uses years).
5. A planet is found at 12 AU. Predict: is its year longer or shorter than
   Saturn's (9.58 AU)? Would you expect it to be rocky or giant? Give one
   reason for each prediction.
6. **Explain your reasoning:** Two museums each show the solar system. One
   shows true sizes with wrong distances; the other shows true distances
   with wrong sizes. Which one should a visitor trust for "how big is
   Jupiter?" and which for "how far is Neptune?" Why is neither museum
   lying, as long as its labels are honest?
