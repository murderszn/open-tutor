# Unit Assessment — Unit 03 (Solar System, Gravity, and Scale)

**Use the dataset table below for every calculation. Show your working.
Open-response items are scored with the rubric in the [teacher guide](../teacher-guide.md)
and the sample reasoning in the [answer key](../answer-key.md). This
assessment is not standardized or validated; it checks this unit's five
objectives.**

| Planet | Mean distance from Sun (million km) | Orbital period (days) | Equatorial diameter (km) | Surface gravity (m/s²) |

| Mercury | 57.9 | 88 | 4,879 | 3.7 |
| Venus | 108.2 | 225 | 12,104 | 8.87 |

| Mars | 227.9 | 687 | 6,779 | 3.71 |

1. **Order and groups (Objective 1).** Complete the table: list the eight
   planets in Sun order and mark each R (rocky) or G (giant).
2. **The belt (Objective 1).** Between which two planets does the asteroid
   belt mostly orbit? What kind of bodies is it made of?
3. **Gravity model (Objective 2).** Write a paragraph (4–6 sentences)
   explaining how gravity holds the solar system together. Your paragraph
   must mention: what gravity is, why the Sun dominates, and why planets
   neither crash into the Sun nor fly away.
4. **Orbit diagram (Objective 2).** Describe a labeled diagram for a planet
   in orbit: what two arrows would you draw, where would each point, and
   what would each label say? (You may draw it instead of describing it.)
5. **Weight calculations (Objective 2).** A rock weighs 120 N on Earth.
   (a) Calculate its weight on Jupiter. (b) Calculate its weight on
   Mercury. Round each to the nearest whole newton and show your working.
6. **Size ordering (Objective 3).** Order Jupiter, Earth, Neptune, and
   Mercury from widest to narrowest. Give each planet's diameter ratio
   (diameter ÷ 12,742 km, two decimals).
7. **Changing the scale (Objective 4).** On a scale where Earth is a 2 cm
   ball, how wide is Saturn's ball? Show the multiplication and round to
   one decimal.
8. **AU gaps (Objective 3).** (a) How far is Neptune from the Sun in AU?
   (b) What is the gap between Saturn's and Uranus's orbits, in AU?
   Show both calculations.
9. **Distance line (Objective 4).** A hallway is 60 m long. On the
   1 AU = 10 m scale, which planets' markers fit inside the hallway?
   List them and show how you decided.
10. **The Sun problem (Objective 4).** On the Earth = 1 cm size scale, how
    wide would the Sun be? (Sun's diameter ≈ 1,392,700 km.) Explain in two
    sentences why the Sun cannot appear on your size strip.
11. **Model audit (Objective 5).** Choose one model from this unit (orbit
    illustration, size strip, distance line, or ball-and-string demo).
    State one thing it shows truly, one thing it distorts or leaves out,
    and one claim that would be wrong if you trusted it too far.
12. **Misconception correction (Objective 5).** A classmate says, "Planets
    stay in orbit because they ride on invisible tracks." Write a
    three-sentence correction that explains what an orbit really is.
13. **Data patterns (Objective 3).** State two patterns the dataset shows,
    each with numbers as evidence: (a) a pattern about planet groups and
    distance; (b) a pattern about distance and year length.
14. **Limits of patterns (Objectives 3, 5).** A small body is found orbiting
    at 2 AU. (a) Would its year be longer or shorter than Mars's year
    (687 days)? Give one reason. (b) Can the rocky/giant pattern alone
    tell you whether this body is rocky-sized or giant-sized? Explain in
    two sentences.
