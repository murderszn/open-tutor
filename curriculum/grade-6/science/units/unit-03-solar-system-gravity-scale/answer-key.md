**Teacher material — keep separate from student pages.** Every value below
was computed from the dataset table in the [unit README](README.md) and
reconciled against the lessons, practice sets, quiz, assessment, and
project rubric. Rounding: ratios and AU values to two decimals; weights to
the nearest whole newton; distances to the precision stated in each item.

## Lesson 02 — Guided practice

1. Sample answer: the Moon is pulled toward Earth by gravity and moves
   sideways at the same time, so it keeps falling around Earth instead of
   falling onto Earth or flying away.
2. Faster. The model suggests Mercury (close to the Sun) moves faster and
   has a shorter year than Neptune.
3. Jupiter — surface gravity 24.79 m/s², the highest in the table.
4. Mercury: 80 × (3.7 ÷ 9.81) = 30.2 → **30 N**. Neptune: 80 × (11.15 ÷
   9.81) = 90.9 → **91 N**.
5. False. Gravity needs mass, not air or an atmosphere — the Sun, which
   has no solid surface or Earth-like atmosphere, holds the planets by
   gravity.
6. The "tracks" idea is wrong because there is nothing in space to ride
   on. What replaces it: the Sun's pull plus the planet's sideways motion,
   producing a continuous fall around the Sun.

## Lesson 02 — Independent practice

1. Two arrows: one pointing toward the Sun labeled "gravity pulls inward";
   one tangent to the orbit labeled "planet moves sideways." Sentence:
   together they make the planet curve around the Sun instead of falling
   in or flying off.
2. Sample answer: Earth would fly off in a straight line, because nothing
   would bend its path into a curve anymore.
3. Venus: 100 × (8.87 ÷ 9.81) = 90.4 → **90 N**. Saturn: 100 × (10.44 ÷
   9.81) = 106.4 → **106 N**. Saturn's weight is closer to Earth's 100 N
   (difference 6 N vs. Venus's 10 N), because Saturn's gravity (10.44) is
   closer to Earth's (9.81) than Venus's (8.87) is.
4. Planet A (close) has the shorter year. Evidence: the ball-and-string
   model (shorter string → faster circling) and the dataset (Mercury,
   88 days, vs. Neptune, 60,190 days).
5. Sample answer: brightness exerts no meaningful holding pull; gravity
   depends on mass, and the Sun holds the system because it contains more
   than 99% of the system's mass.

## Lesson 02 — Applied task (fair test)

Question: how does string length change the speed needed for a steady
circle? Changed variable: string length. Controlled: same ball, same
swinger, same effort description. Repeated trials: three per length.
Conclusion (sample): shorter strings needed faster motion to stay steady;
longer strings circled more slowly — matching Mercury's short, fast orbit
vs. Neptune's long, slow one.

## Lesson 02 — Exit check

2. Mass: no, it stays the same. Weight: yes, it changes with each planet's
   surface gravity.
3. The Sun's gravity — because gravity is a pull between masses, and the
   Sun holds more than 99% of the system's mass.

    the Sun or enlarged the planets.

    Distorts — distances entirely; the Sun is missing. Wrong-if-trusted:
    "the planets sit next to each other."

    orbit because the Sun's gravity pulls it inward while its sideways
    motion carries it around — a continuous fall that keeps missing the
    Sun.
13. (a) Groups and distance: the four rocky planets sit within 1.52 AU
    while the four giants sit from 5.20 AU outward (diameters: rocky ≤
    12,742 km; giants ≥ 49,244 km). (b) Distance and year: Mercury at

    for it; patterns describe the groups, not every possible body.

## Project rubric — scoring notes

- **Size strip accuracy:** check order and the eight widths against the
  Earth = 1 cm list (0.4, 1.0, 1.0, 0.5, 11.0, 9.1, 4.0, 3.9 cm); allow
  2 mm tolerance for hand drawing.
- **Distance line accuracy:** check markers against the stated scale
  (1 AU = 10 m: 3.9, 7.2, 10.0, 15.2, 52.0, 95.8, 192.0, 300.5 m; or the
  1 AU = 1 cm paper version: 0.4 … 30.1 cm).
- **Gravity explanation:** must contain the three elements (pull between
  masses; Sun's dominant mass; orbit as falling-around). "Tracks" language
  caps this criterion at 1 until corrected.
- **Limits cards:** each card needs all three lines; a card that only
  repeats the model's claims scores 0 for that card.
- **Lab notes:** must show question, changed variable (string length),
  controlled variables, three trials per length, and the Mercury–Neptune
  conclusion.
