# Project — Build a Two-Scale Solar System Exhibit

**Question:** How can you show a museum visitor both how big the planets
are *and* how far apart they are, honestly?
**Sessions:** launched S8, built S12, showcased S14 (with S11 data-task
support).

## Materials

- A paper strip at least 30 cm long; ruler in centimetres; scissors;
  coloring pencils.
- A measuring tape or marked rope (outdoors) or a long paper strip
  (indoors, 1 AU = 1 cm version); planet marker cards or cones.
- The dataset table in the [unit README](README.md); the size strip from
  Lesson 03 and the distance-line sketch from Lesson 04 (drafts are fine —
  this project finishes them).
- Card for the visitor explanation and two limits cards.

## Steps

1. **Finish the size strip.** Draw eight circles in Sun order at the
   Earth = 1 cm scale: Mercury 0.4 cm, Venus 1.0 cm, Earth 1.0 cm, Mars
   0.5 cm, Jupiter 11.0 cm, Saturn 9.1 cm, Uranus 4.0 cm, Neptune 3.9 cm
   (rounded to the nearest millimetre). Label each circle with its name
   and its diameter ratio (Mercury 0.38, Venus 0.95, Earth 1.00, Mars 0.53,
   Jupiter 10.97, Saturn 9.14, Uranus 3.98, Neptune 3.86).
2. **Finish the distance line.** Outdoors at 1 AU = 10 m, place markers at:
   Mercury 3.9 m, Venus 7.2 m, Earth 10.0 m, Mars 15.2 m, Jupiter 52.0 m,
   Saturn 95.8 m, Uranus 192.0 m, Neptune 300.5 m. Indoors, use the
   1 AU = 1 cm paper version (Mercury 0.4 cm … Neptune 30.1 cm). Sketch the
   line with its scale marked.
3. **Write the visitor explanation (one paragraph, 4–6 sentences).** It
   must explain: what gravity is, why the Sun holds the system together,
   and why planets neither crash into the Sun nor fly away (orbit as
   falling-around).
4. **Write two limits cards** (one per model), each with three lines:
   *Shows truly:* … / *Distorts or leaves out:* … / *Do not use it to
   claim:* …
5. **Lab notes (one page).** Attach the Lesson 02 ball-and-string fair-test
   notes: question, changed variable (string length), controlled variables
   (same ball, same swinger, same effort description), three repeated
   trials per length, and a three-sentence conclusion linking the result
   to Mercury vs. Neptune.
6. **Showcase (S14).** Present the exhibit to your adult as a museum
   visitor: point to each model, read its limits card aloud, and answer
   two visitor questions — "How big is Jupiter, really?" (answer from the
   size strip) and "How far is Neptune, really?" (answer from the distance
   line).

## Deliverables

1. The finished size strip (8 labeled circles with ratios).
2. The finished distance line (markers + sketch with scale).
3. The visitor explanation paragraph.
4. Two limits cards (one per model).
5. The ball-and-string lab notes page.

## Rubric

Each criterion is scored 2 (meets), 1 (partial), or 0 (not yet). Total /10.
"Ready" for the showcase is 8 or above with no criterion at 0; otherwise
reteach the weak criterion before the unit assessment.

| Criterion | 2 — Meets | 1 — Partial | 0 — Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Size strip accuracy | All 8 circles in Sun order, sizes within 2 mm of the Earth = 1 cm scale, ratios labeled | Most circles correct; 1–2 sizes or ratios wrong | Order wrong, or sizes not from the scale |
| Distance line accuracy | All 8 markers at correct positions for the stated scale, scale marked | Most markers correct; 1–2 positions wrong or scale unmarked | Positions not computed from AU values |
| Gravity explanation | Names gravity as a pull between masses, the Sun's dominant mass, and orbit as falling-around | Two of the three ideas present | Gravity described as Earth-only, or as tracks/rails |
| Limits cards | Both cards name something true, something distorted, and a barred claim | One card complete, or cards missing one line each | No limits cards, or cards that repeat the model's claims |
| Lab notes | Question, changed/controlled variables, 3 trials per length, Mercury–Neptune conclusion | Missing one element (e.g. no repeats, or no conclusion) | No fair-test structure, or conclusion unrelated to the test |

**Adult note:** the showcase is a teaching conversation, not a performance.
If the learner cannot support a claim with a number, that claim goes on the
reteach list — it is not a failure of the exhibit.
