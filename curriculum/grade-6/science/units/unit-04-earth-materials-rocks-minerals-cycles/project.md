# Project — Rock Cycle Storyboard

**Deliverable:** a storyboard (paper panels, slides, or a poster) that
follows one batch of Earth matter through at least three forms, plus a
model-limits card. **Sessions:** launched S8, worked S12, showcased S14.
**Safety:** an adult supervises any handling of real samples (eye
protection; adult handles the nail and any hammering; never taste a
sample; wash hands afterwards). Drawn or described samples are a full
alternative — no real samples are required.

## The task

Pick a starting rock (igneous, sedimentary, or metamorphic — real or
from Lessons 01–02). Tell its story in 4–6 panels:

1. **Panel 1 — the starting rock.** Draw or describe it and name its
   family, with one piece of formation evidence.
2. **Middle panels — at least two changes of form.** Each panel shows
   the new form and names the process that produced it (melting,
   cooling and crystallization, weathering, erosion, deposition,
   compaction and cementation, heat and pressure without melting, or
   uplift). Your story must visit **at least three forms** in total
   (counting the start) and must be traceable on the Lesson 03 diagram
   — every arrow you draw must be a real arrow on that diagram.
3. **Energy tags.** Every arrow carries a tag: **S** (Sun's energy),
   **E** (Earth's interior heat), **E-with-a-note** (cooling: heat
   escaping from interior-made magma), or **neither directly**
   (compaction and cementation: burial weight and groundwater
   chemistry). At least one arrow must be S and at least one must be
   E (or E-with-a-note).
4. **Model-limits card (separate, attached).** Name two things the
   storyboard (like the diagram) leaves out — for example timescales,
   locations, rates, or living things — and write one sentence for
   each explaining why leaving it out is acceptable for this model.
5. **Closing line.** One sentence stating what happened to the matter
   (changed form, never created or destroyed) — the conservation
   claim in your own words.

## Materials

Paper or slides; colored pencils; the Lesson 03 diagram; Lessons 01–02
notes. Optional: real samples for Panel 1, handled under the safety
rules above.

## Steps

1. **S8 — plan.** Choose the starting rock and sketch the path on the
   Lesson 03 diagram first. Check every arrow exists on the diagram.
   If an arrow is not on the diagram, the path is invalid — redraw it.
2. **S12 — build.** Draw or write the panels, label every process and
   energy tag, and draft the limits card. Trade with a partner: can
   they trace your path on the diagram without your help? Fix what
   they cannot trace.
3. **S14 — showcase.** Present in two minutes: the starting rock, the
   path, the energy story, and one limit. Answer one challenge question
   from the adult or a partner (for example: "What would break your
   path?" or "Where does the Sun appear in your story?").

## Rubric (40 points)

| Criterion | Full marks | Partial | Missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starting rock + family evidence (6) | Family correct, evidence is a formation feature (crystals, layers, banding), not color alone | Family correct, evidence weak (color only) | Family wrong or no evidence |
| Path validity: ≥3 forms, every arrow real (10) | ≥3 forms; every arrow traceable on the Lesson 03 diagram | 3 forms but one arrow invalid, or only 2 forms | Path untraceable or single form |
| Process labels (8) | Every arrow labeled with the correct process | One label wrong or missing | Two or more wrong/missing |
| Energy tags (8) | Every arrow tagged; ≥1 S and ≥1 E (or E-with-a-note); "neither directly" used correctly for compaction/cementation if present | One tag wrong, or an engine missing | Tags missing or systematically wrong |
| Model-limits card (4) | Two real limits, each with an acceptable-omission reason | One limit, or reasons missing | No card |
| Conservation closing line (4) | States matter changed form and was not created/destroyed | Vague ("the rock changed") without conservation | Missing or claims destruction/creation |

**Scoring notes for the adult:** a storyboard that names a mineral as
part of a rock description (for example, "granite contains quartz") is
acceptable — the assessment boundary excludes *identifying and naming
minerals from tests*, not mentioning minerals in prose. Do not deduct
for a path that skips boxes: shortcuts are valid. Do deduct for an
arrow the diagram does not contain (for example, sediment melting
directly into igneous rock).
