# Unit 04 — Earth Materials, Rocks, Minerals, and Cycles

**Grade:** 6 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#31](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/31)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 15–18 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 45 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

A rock in your hand looks permanent. It is not. It is a snapshot: the same
matter has been magma, crystal, sand, layered rock, and banded rock before,
and given enough time and the right conditions it will be something else
again. This unit follows that matter. First the learner tests minerals —
not to memorize mineral names, but to learn how geologists use properties
as evidence, and why one property (especially color) is never enough.
Then the learner sorts rocks by the story their formation tells: cooled
from melt, built from sediment, or changed by heat and pressure without
melting. Then the learner builds the rock-cycle model and tags every arrow
with the energy that drives it: the Sun's energy at the surface, Earth's
interior heat below it. The unit ends with the midyear review, pulling
Units 01–04 together: investigation practices, the Earth–sun–moon system,
gravity and scale, and now Earth materials.

One boundary governs the whole unit, and the teacher guide states it
plainly: **NGSS MS-ESS2-1's assessment boundary excludes the identification
and naming of minerals.** The unit therefore teaches mineral *properties*
and property-based reasoning, and every quiz and assessment item is about
properties, rock formation, cycle processes, energy flow, and model limits
— never "name this mineral." Practice sample cards use letters (Sample A,
Sample B), never mineral names, for the same reason.

## Prerequisites (earlier-unit skills re-used; re-taught as needed)

- Testable questions, labeled data tables with units, repeated trials, one
  variable changed at a time (Unit 01).
- Models include some things and leave others out; a model is not the real
  system (Units 01–03).
- Describing patterns from data without overclaiming (Units 01, 03).
- Safety: an adult supervises every investigation. **Eye protection for
  rock handling; the adult handles the iron nail and any hammering; never
  taste a sample; wash hands after handling rocks.** The streak test only
  works for minerals softer than the streak plate; do not force a sample
  that will not streak.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (mapped to the track
objectives in parentheses):

1. **Describe a mineral sample with testable properties** — color, streak,
   hardness, luster, and cleavage or fracture — and **explain why color
   alone is unreliable**, using at least two properties together as
   evidence for a claim about a sample. (Track objective 8)
2. **Classify rock samples as igneous, sedimentary, or metamorphic from
   formation evidence** (crystal size, layers, grains, banding, fossils)
   and justify each classification with the process that formed it.
   (Track objective 8)
3. **Build a rock-cycle model** that traces matter through at least two
   complete paths, naming the process at every arrow (melting,
   crystallization, weathering, erosion, deposition, compaction and
   cementation, deformation by heat and pressure, uplift).
   (Track objective 9)
4. **Explain the energy flow that drives the cycle:** the Sun's energy
   drives surface processes (weathering, erosion, transport, deposition);
   Earth's interior heat drives melting, crystallization, deformation,
   and uplift — and **defend the claim that matter is conserved** through
   the cycle (it changes form; it is not created or destroyed).
   (Track objective 9)
5. **Audit the unit's models** (test-station illustration, rock-cycle
   diagram, storyboard project): name what each **includes and leaves
   out** — especially timescales, locations, and rates — and revise a
   claim when a model is overextended. (Track objective 3)

## Essential question

*If a rock looks permanent, how do you know its matter has already lived
several lives — and what energy keeps the story going?*

## Vocabulary

**Mineral** — a naturally occurring, inorganic solid with a definite
chemical composition and an orderly crystal structure. **Rock** — a solid
made of one or more minerals (and sometimes other materials such as glass
or once-living matter). **Streak** — the color of a mineral's powder, found
by rubbing it on an unglazed porcelain streak plate. **Hardness** — how
resistant a mineral is to being scratched (not how hard it is to break).
**Mohs scale** — a 1–10 ranking of scratch hardness, from talc (1) to
diamond (10). **Luster** — how a mineral's surface reflects light
(metallic, glassy, dull, pearly, and others). **Cleavage** — breaking
along flat planes; **fracture** — breaking along irregular surfaces.
**Igneous rock** — rock formed when molten material cools and
crystallizes. **Magma** — molten rock below Earth's surface; **lava** —
molten rock at the surface. **Sedimentary rock** — rock formed when
sediment is deposited, compacted, and cemented. **Sediment** — loose
fragments of rock and mineral moved by water, wind, ice, or gravity.
**Compaction** — squeezing sediment together under burial;
**cementation** — minerals gluing sediment grains together.
**Metamorphic rock** — rock changed by heat, pressure, or reactive fluids
without melting. **Weathering** — breaking rock down in place;
**erosion** — moving the broken material; **deposition** — laying it down.
**Uplift** — rock at depth being raised toward the surface.
**Crystallization** — minerals forming crystals as molten material cools.
**Deformation** — bending, squeezing, or otherwise changing a rock's
shape and mineral structure under stress. **Rock cycle** — the model of
Earth materials changing form through melting, crystallization,
weathering, erosion, deposition, compaction and cementation, and
metamorphism, driven by energy from the Sun and Earth's interior.
**Conservation of matter** — in a closed system, matter is not created or
destroyed; it changes form.

## Standards and sources

- **NGSS MS-ESS2-1** (grades 6–8 band): "Develop a model to describe the
  cycling of Earth's materials and the flow of energy that drives this
  process." Clarification: emphasis is on the processes of melting,
  crystallization, weathering, deformation, and sedimentation, which act
  together to form minerals and rocks through the cycling of Earth's
  materials. Assessment boundary: **assessment does not include the
  identification and naming of minerals.** Code, wording, clarification,
  and boundary verified 2026-11-01 against the NSTA NGSS listing and the
  California Department of Education's official NGSS reproduction. No
  claim of state adoption or accreditation is made. NGSS does not assign
  specific performance expectations to grade 6; this unit is the grade-6
  year of the middle-school band, as the track audit establishes.
- **DCI ESS2.A (Earth Materials and Systems):** "All Earth processes are
  the result of energy flowing and matter cycling within and among the
  planet's systems. This energy is derived from the sun and Earth's hot
  interior." Verified 2026-11-01 from the same two sources.
- Mineral properties (definition, streak, hardness, luster, cleavage and
  fracture, the Mohs scale, the unreliability of color): Utah Geological
  Survey, "Glad You Asked: How Do Geologists Identify Minerals?" and the
  MIT OpenCourseWare mineral-identification lab guide — both checked
  2026-11-01. Classroom hardness references used in this unit:
  fingernail ≈ 2.5, copper penny ≈ 3, glass plate ≈ 5.5, steel nail ≈ 5.5
  (approximate values; a scratch test shows only which of two materials
  is harder, never an exact hardness).
- Rock families and cycle processes (igneous cooling rates and crystal
  size; sedimentary compaction and cementation; metamorphism without
  melting; weathering vs. erosion vs. deposition; uplift; the two energy
  drivers): Utah Geological Survey, "Glad You Asked: Igneous, Sedimentary,
  & Metamorphic Rocks" and the LibreTexts rock-cycle chapter — both
  checked 2026-11-01. All reading passages in this unit are original;
  these sources were used to verify facts, not copied.
- Internal reuse: `resources/thermodynamics_laws.md` (energy-flow passages
  for Lesson 04, adult-selected and explained at grade level — the full
  guide sits above band, as the audit notes); `resources/chemistry_fundamentals.md`
  and `resources/periodic_table_elements.csv` (enrichment only, e.g.
  quartz = SiO₂, adult-selected rows; element-fact columns are sample
  text and are not assessed); `resources/biology_fundamentals.md`
  (limited reuse: the Sun's energy entering Earth systems and matter
  cycling through living things, bridging grade 5 into MS-ESS2-1); the
  track's scope-and-sequence U04 weekly goals and standards crosswalk.
