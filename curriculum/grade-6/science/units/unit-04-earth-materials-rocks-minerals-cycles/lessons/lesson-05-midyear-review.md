# Lesson 05 — Review: Midyear Retrieval

**Student goal:** I can retrieve and connect Units 01–04: investigation
practices, the Earth–sun–moon system, gravity and scale, and Earth
materials.
**Time:** ~45 minutes · **Sessions:** S13 (plus S16 debrief)
**Materials:** your notes from Units 01–04; the Lesson 03 diagram;
the [week-18 quiz](../assessments/week-18_quiz.md) follows this lesson.

**Readiness check (2 minutes):** Without looking anything up, write
one sentence for each unit: U01 (investigations), U02 (Earth–sun–moon),
U03 (solar system), U04 (rocks). Star the sentence you are least sure
about — that is where your review starts.

## Teaching

Midyear review is not re-reading. It is **retrieval**: pulling answers
out of memory, checking them, and fixing the gaps you find. Work each
station, then check against your notes — not before.

### Station 1 — Investigation practices (Unit 01)

A fair test changes **one variable** and controls the rest, repeats
trials, and records results in a labeled table with units. A model
includes some things and leaves others out; the first question about
any model is always "what does it distort?" **Retrieval task:** design
a fair test for this question — "Does a rougher surface slow a rolling
ball more than a smooth surface?" — naming the variable you change,
two variables you control, and how many trials you would run.

### Station 2 — Earth–sun–moon (Unit 02)

Day and night come from Earth's **rotation**; seasons come from Earth's
**axial tilt**, not from Earth's distance from the Sun. Lunar phases,
eclipses, and seasons are cyclic patterns a model can show.
**Retrieval task:** a classmate says "summer happens when Earth is
closest to the Sun." Write the two-sentence refutation: what actually
causes seasons, and what evidence kills the distance story? (Hint: the
two hemispheres have opposite seasons at the same time.)

### Station 3 — Gravity and scale (Unit 03)

Gravity is a pull between **any two masses**; the Sun's pull holds the
planets in orbit; an orbit is forward motion combined with a continuous
fall toward the Sun. No single classroom model can show true planet
sizes *and* true distances — every model distorts one or the other and
must carry a limits card. **Retrieval task:** Jupiter's diameter is
about 11 times Earth's. If Earth is drawn as a 1 cm circle, how wide is
Jupiter's circle — and why would a single true-scale model still fail
to show distances in the same room?

### Station 4 — Earth materials (Unit 04)

Minerals are tested by properties, never by color alone; rocks sort by
formation story (igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic); the rock cycle is
a network driven by the Sun at the surface and Earth's interior heat
below, and it conserves matter. **Retrieval task:** trace one path from
sediment to magma on the Lesson 03 diagram, naming every process and
tagging every arrow S, E, or "neither directly."

### Misconception clinic

For each claim, write "agree" or "disagree" and one reason:

1. "A mineral's color tells you what it is."
2. "Metamorphic rock melted and re-hardened."
3. "Seasons happen because Earth moves closer to the Sun in summer."
4. "Gravity is something only Earth has."
5. "The rock cycle always visits every rock type in order."
6. "A rock that weathered away was destroyed."
7. "A model that distorts something is a bad model."
8. "Changing one variable at a time is slower, so changing three at
   once is better science."

## Guided practice

Work in pairs, out loud, before writing:

1. Connect two units: how does Unit 01's "model vs. reality" rule apply
   to Unit 04's rock-cycle diagram? Name one thing the diagram includes
   and one thing it leaves out.
2. Connect two more: how does Unit 03's "one model cannot show both
   sizes and distances" limit resemble Unit 04's limits list? What is
   the general lesson about models?
3. Which misconception above did you believe at the start of the year?
   What evidence changed your mind? (Honesty counts; the adult reads
   this one, not a score.)

## Independent practice

Re-take your weakest readiness-check sentence and rewrite it as a full
paragraph with one example and one piece of evidence. Then take the
[week-18 quiz](../assessments/week-18_quiz.md) — it is formative, which
means its job is to show what to reteach, not to punish a gap.

## Application

Write a one-page "midyear letter" to a learner starting grade-6 science
next year: the four biggest ideas of the year so far, one warning about
a trap (a misconception), and one piece of advice about models. The
adult may keep anonymous excerpts as next year's orientation material
— nothing with your name leaves private storage.

## Exit check

Answer in writing: (1) the one Unit 01–04 idea you can now explain to
someone else without notes; (2) the one idea you still need to
re-study before the unit assessment, and the exact page or activity
you will re-study.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading/language access:** stations can be done in any order;
  read the station question aloud before answering.
- **Alternate response mode:** answer stations orally, or by tracing
  and tagging on the diagrams, instead of writing.
- **Extension:** write one new quiz question (with answer) for the
  station you found hardest, and trade questions with a partner.

## Teacher note
