# Unit Assessment — Unit 04: Earth Materials, Rocks, Minerals, and Cycles (30 points)

**Name (private — do not publish):** ______________ **Date:** ______________

No item asks you to name a mineral. Use the Lesson 03 rock-cycle
diagram where a question says to trace a path. Answers and scoring
guidance are in the [answer key](../answer-key.md).

## Part A — Selected response (8 points, 1 each)

1. Which feature is **required** for a substance to count as a mineral?
   - A. It is colorful
   - B. It has an orderly crystal structure
   - C. It is hard to break
   - D. It is found underground

2. A mineral peels into thin, flat sheets. This is an example of:
   - A. Luster
   - B. Fracture
   - C. Cleavage
   - D. Streak

3. A sample scratches a glass plate (≈ 5.5). What can you conclude
   about its hardness?
   - A. It is softer than 5.5
   - B. It is exactly 5.5
   - C. It is harder than 5.5
   - D. Nothing — scratch tests give no information

4. Basalt is dark and fine-grained, with crystals too small to see
   easily. What does that texture tell you about how it cooled?
   - A. Quickly, at the surface
   - B. Slowly, deep underground
   - C. It cooled under the sea only
   - D. It never cooled — it was squeezed

5. A rock shows flat layers and a shell fossil. Which family is it?
   - A. Metamorphic
   - B. Igneous
   - C. Sedimentary
   - D. Cannot tell from this evidence

6. Metamorphic rock forms when an existing rock is heated and squeezed
   but does **not** do what?
   - A. Melt
   - B. Form bands
   - C. Get buried
   - D. Change its minerals

7. Melting in the rock cycle is driven mainly by which energy source?
   - A. Burial weight
   - B. The Sun
   - C. Moving water
   - D. Earth's interior heat

8. Which of these is a **valid** rock-cycle path on the Lesson 03
   diagram?
   - A. Sediment → melting → igneous rock
   - B. Igneous rock → weathering, erosion, deposition → sediment →
     compaction and cementation → sedimentary rock
   - C. Sedimentary rock → cooling and crystallization → igneous rock
   - D. Metamorphic rock → weathering → magma

## Part B — Cycle tracing (10 points)

9. (6 points) Trace this path, naming the process at each arrow and
    tagging each arrow S, E, E-with-a-note, or "neither directly":
    limestone (sedimentary) → marble (metamorphic) → magma → new
    igneous rock.
    - Arrow 1 process (1 pt) + tag (1 pt)
    - Arrow 2 process (1 pt) + tag (1 pt)
    - Arrow 3 process (1 pt) + tag (1 pt)

10. (4 points) Trace granite to sandstone: name the three processes
    in order (3 points), and tag the weathering step with its energy
    driver (1 point).
    - Step 1: ______________
    - Step 2: ______________
    - Step 3: ______________

## Part C — Energy and conservation (6 points)

11. (2 points) Explain, in two sentences, how the Sun's energy and
    Earth's interior heat drive *different* parts of the rock cycle.
    Name one process for each engine.

12. (2 points) A classmate tags compaction and cementation as S.
    Explain why the correct tag is "neither directly," and name what
    actually drives this step.

13. (2 points) A gravel driveway is re-covered with new gravel every
    year because the old gravel "wears away." Use conservation of
    matter to explain where the old gravel went, in two sentences.

## Part D — Model audit and classification (6 points)

14. (3 points) Name two things the rock-cycle diagram leaves out
    (1 point each), and write one sentence explaining why the model
    is still useful despite those limits (1 point).

15. (3 points, 1 each) Classify each sample with its family and one
    formation reason:
    - (a) Bold light and dark bands, minerals lined up, no fossils.
    - (b) Flat layers with a shell fossil on one surface.
    - (c) Black and glassy, smooth curved break, no crystals.
