# Practice — Unit 04

Work from the [unit README](README.md) sample cards and the Lesson 03
diagram. Sample cards A–D are **fictional practice data**, not real
observations. No practice item asks you to name a mineral. Answers are
in the [answer key](answer-key.md) — try first, then check.

## Set A — Mineral properties (Lesson 01)

1. Write the definition of a mineral in your own words, naming all
   five parts.
2. Why is color the weakest identification test? Use the quartz
   example from Lesson 01.
3. What is streak, how is it tested, and why can it beat color? Use
   Sample B (dark gray; red-brown streak).
4. A student says, "This mineral is hard to break, so it must be high
   on the Mohs scale." Explain the error, using diamond.
5. Explain cleavage and fracture in your own words, with one example
   of each from Lesson 01.
6. Samples A and C are both glassy. Name two properties from the cards
   that separate them, quoting the values — and explain why you do
   not rely on their colors (pink vs. white).

## Set B — Property reasoning (Lesson 01)

1. Samples C and D are both white with a white streak. Name three
   properties that separate them, quoting the card values.
2. A sample leaves no mark on the streak plate no matter how firmly you
   rub. What does that tell you, and what should you **not** do next?
3. Two pink, glassy samples: one is scratched by a penny, the other is
   not. What have you learned, and what do you still not know?
4. Which two properties together best separate Sample A from Sample C?
   Defend your choice in two sentences.
5. Error analysis: "Sample B is dark, so it belongs with the other
   dark minerals." Rewrite the reasoning correctly, using streak and
   luster.
6. Rank the evidence for the claim "Sample D is very soft" from
   strongest to weakest: its white color, its dull luster, the fact
   that a fingernail scratches it, the fact that it crumbles. Give a
   reason for the ranking.

## Set C — Rock families (Lesson 02)

1. A sample has visible interlocking crystals and no layers. Which
   family, which cooling story, and why?
2. Why are fossils found in sedimentary rock and not in igneous or
   metamorphic rock? Give a reason for each of the other two families.
3. Shale and slate: one is sedimentary, one is metamorphic. Which is
   which, and what process turns the first into the second?
4. Pumice is full of holes and light enough that some pieces float.
   Explain the holes using its formation story, and name its family.
5. Error analysis: "Metamorphic rock melted and re-hardened." Correct
   the sentence and explain what actually happens.
6. A sample is rounded pebbles of many colors stuck together in a
   cemented mix. Which family, and which two processes made it a rock
   (after deposition)?

## Set D — The six-sample sort (Lesson 02)

Classify each Lesson 02 sort sample (R1–R6) with its family and the
formation reason. One sentence of reasoning each.

1. R1 — dark, fine-grained, no visible crystals, no layers, near an
   old lava flow.
2. R2 — light tan, sand-sized grains, flat layers, small shell fossil.
3. R3 — bold light and dark bands, minerals lined up, no fossils.
4. R4 — rounded pebbles of many colors in a cemented mix.
5. R5 — black and glassy, smooth curved break, no crystals, sharp
   edges.
6. R6 — white, interlocking crystals; adult note: formed where
   limestone was heated and pressed deep underground.

## Set E — Cycle paths (Lesson 03)

1. Fill in the two missing processes: limestone → ??? → marble →
   ??? → magma. For the first step, state the condition that must
   hold (what must *not* happen).
2. Error analysis: a classmate traces sediment → melting → igneous
   rock. Find the error and give the corrected path.
3. Find a three-arrow path that starts and ends at the same box on the
   Lesson 03 diagram. Is your path the only three-arrow loop? Give a
   second one or explain why none exists.
4. Which arrow on the diagram could run fastest in real life, and
   which slowest? Give a reason for each — and name what the diagram
   does not tell you about speed.
5. Trace the Lesson 03 full loop from granite back to new igneous
   rock, naming all six processes in order.
6. Write one "impossible chapter" for a sedimentary rock — a step the
   diagram forbids — and explain why it is forbidden.

## Set F — Energy and model limits (Lesson 04)

1. Tag every arrow in this path — metamorphic rock → melting → magma
   → cooling → igneous rock → weathering → sediment — with S, E,
   E-with-a-note, or "neither directly." Which engine appears most,
   and does that surprise you?
2. A classmate tags compaction and cementation as S "because the Sun
   made the river that carried the sand." What is right about that
   reasoning, and where does it overreach?
3. A beach loses sand every winter and gains it every summer. Has
   matter been created or destroyed? Apply the conservation rule.
4. Name one question the rock-cycle diagram *cannot* answer and one
   question it answers well. Explain the difference in one sentence
   each.
5. Choose a real landscape you know (a riverbank, a cliff, a gravel
   driveway, a mountain photo). Write five sentences: the process
   visible or implied, its engine, what the matter was before, what
   it could become next, and one thing the diagram leaves out about
   this place.
6. Model audit: name two things the rock-cycle diagram leaves out,
   and explain in two sentences why the model is still useful despite
   those limits.
