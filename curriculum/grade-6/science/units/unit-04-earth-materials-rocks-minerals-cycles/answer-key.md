# Answer Key — Unit 04 (separate from student materials)

Every item below was solved independently and reconciled with the
student materials. Accept equivalent wording that carries the same
evidence or reasoning. Never use this key as a learner handout before
an assessment.

## Lesson 01 — Guided practice

1. The two samples differ in hardness relative to a penny (≈ 3): one
   is softer than 3, the other harder than 3. You still do not know
   either sample's identity — and under this unit's assessment
   boundary you are never asked for it; you know one property
   difference, and you need more properties before claiming anything.
2. Sample C vs. Sample D: hardness (C: 7; D: 1–2) and luster (C: glassy;
   D: dull, earthy) separate them; their breaks also differ (C: curved
   fracture; D: crumbles). Color and streak are identical (white /
   white) and separate nothing.
3. Faulty because hardness measures scratch resistance, not
   breakability. Diamond is Mohs 10 (hardest) and can still shatter
   when struck: hard to scratch, possible to break.
4. The sample is harder than the streak plate, so it leaves no powder
   mark. Do **not** force it, grind it, or press harder — record "no
   streak; harder than the plate" and use other tests (hardness,
   luster, break).

## Lesson 01 — Test-station activity

1. Hand lens: helps with luster and break patterns — look for how the
   surface reflects light and whether breaks are flat or irregular.
2. White tile: the streak test — rub the sample on the unglazed
   porcelain and observe the powder color.
3. Penny (≈ 3) and nail (≈ 5.5): the hardness (scratch) test — if the
   tool scratches the sample, the tool is harder; if the sample
   scratches the tool, the sample is harder; neither result gives an
   exact hardness number.
4. Notebook: recording results is part of the test because
   identification rests on comparing recorded properties, not on
   memory of a look — and because a second tester must be able to
   repeat and check the work.

## Lesson 01 — Exit check

(1) A mineral is a naturally occurring, inorganic solid with a
definite chemical composition and an orderly crystal structure.
(2) Color varies within a single mineral (quartz: clear, pink,
purple, smoky, white), so one test can mislead; two agreeing
properties are stronger evidence than one.

## Lesson 02 — Guided practice

1. Same: both cooled from molten material (igneous). Different:
   cooling speed — R1 cooled fast at the surface (fine grains);
   R5 cooled even faster into glass (no crystals).
2. The adult note gives the formation story (limestone heated and
   pressed underground, without melting = metamorphic). "White with
   crystals" alone is unsafe: color is unreliable, and several
   families can be white and crystalline.
3. Refute: pebbles stuck together are cemented sediment, not
   squeezing. Correct argument: rounded pebbles + cement = deposition,
   then compaction and cementation = sedimentary.
4. Igneous: rock was molten — remains could not survive melting.
   Metamorphic: heat and pressure recrystallize the rock — remains
   would be destroyed. Only sedimentary burial is gentle and
   low-temperature enough for fossils to survive.

## Lesson 02 — Six-sample sort

- R1: **Igneous, extrusive.** Fine grains + no layers + lava-flow
  setting = fast surface cooling.
- R2: **Sedimentary.** Sand grains + flat layers + shell fossil =
  deposition, compaction, cementation.
- R3: **Metamorphic.** Bold banding (foliation) + no fossils = heat
  and pressure without melting.
- R4: **Sedimentary.** Rounded pebbles cemented together = deposition,
  compaction, cementation.
- R5: **Igneous, extrusive.** Glassy, no crystals, curved break =
  lava cooled too fast for crystals.
- R6: **Metamorphic.** Limestone heated and pressed underground
  (recrystallized, not melted) = metamorphism; marble is the example.

## Lesson 02 — Exit check

Igneous: evidence — interlocking crystals (or glassy/fine texture);
process — cooling and crystallization of melt. Sedimentary: evidence —
layers, grains, pebbles, or fossils; process — deposition, compaction,
cementation. Metamorphic: evidence — banding or a changed-former-rock
story; process — heat and pressure without melting. No color words
accepted as evidence.

## Lesson 03 — Guided practice

1. Limestone → **heat and pressure, without melting** → marble →
   **melting** → magma. Condition for step 1: the rock must **not**
   melt; melting would make magma, not marble.
2. Error: sediment cannot melt directly into igneous rock. Corrected
   path: sediment → compaction and cementation → sedimentary rock →
   (burial, heat) → melting → magma → cooling and crystallization →
   igneous rock. (The minimal correction is the compaction/cementation
   step; full credit for any path that inserts sedimentary rock before
   melting.)
3. Three-arrow loops exist and are not unique. Example 1: metamorphic
   → melting → magma → cooling and crystallization → igneous →
   heat and pressure → metamorphic. Example 2: magma → cooling →
   igneous → heat and pressure → metamorphic → melting → magma.
   Accept any three-arrow loop whose arrows all exist on the diagram.
4. Fastest: cooling of lava at the surface (days to years — directly
   observable). Slowest: deep burial, compaction/cementation, or
   metamorphism (thousands to millions of years). The diagram does
   not show speed at all: all arrows look alike regardless of rate.

## Lesson 03 — Exit check

Five boxes (magma, igneous rock, sediment, sedimentary rock,
metamorphic rock) with labeled arrows: cooling and crystallization;
weathering, erosion, deposition; compaction and cementation; heat and
pressure; melting; uplift. Credit the circled question, not the
drawing's neatness.

## Lesson 04 — Guided practice

1. Tags: melting **E**; cooling and crystallization **E-with-a-note**
   (interior heat made the magma; cooling is heat escaping);
   weathering **S**. Most frequent: E (2 of 3). Not surprising: this
   path runs mostly underground; a surface-heavy path (for example,
   igneous → sediment → sedimentary) would be S-heavy.
2. Right: the Sun did drive the river that carried the sand, so solar
   energy is in the story's history. Overreach: the tag belongs to
   the arrow's own work — compaction/cementation is done by burial
   weight and groundwater minerals, not by sunlight. Correct tag:
   "neither directly."
3. No. The sand moved (to the sea, down the beach, into dunes) or
   broke smaller; it was not created or destroyed. Seasonal loss and
   gain is transport, and transport conserves matter.
4. Accept any honest pair, for example: cannot answer — "How many
   years did this burial take?" (no timescales); answers well — "Which
   process turns sediment into sedimentary rock?" (compaction and
   cementation). The difference: the diagram models processes and
   paths, not dates, places, or amounts.

## Lesson 04 — Exit check

Weathering: **S** (Sun-driven water, wind, ice). Melting: **E**
(interior heat). Compaction and cementation: **neither directly**
(burial weight + groundwater chemistry). Conservation sentence: the
rock's matter changed form or moved; it was not created or destroyed.

## Lesson 05 — Stations

- Station 1: change = surface roughness (rough vs. smooth); control =
  same ball, same ramp height, same release point, same distance;
  trials = at least 3 per surface (Unit 01: repeated trials).
- Station 2: seasons come from Earth's axial tilt (varying sunlight
  angle and day length). The distance story fails because both
  hemispheres share Earth's distance at any moment yet have opposite
  seasons.
- Station 3: Jupiter's circle = 11 cm (11 × 1 cm). A single true-scale
  model fails because true distances dwarf true sizes: at Earth =
  1 cm, Neptune sits about 3.5 km away (Unit 03) — no room fits both.
- Station 4 (example): sediment → compaction and cementation (neither
  directly) → sedimentary rock → heat and pressure, no melting (E) →
  metamorphic rock → melting (E) → magma. Any diagram-valid path with
  correct tags earns full marks.

## Lesson 05 — Misconception clinic

1. Disagree — color varies within one mineral (quartz).
2. Disagree — metamorphism is change *without* melting; melting makes
   magma.
3. Disagree — seasons come from axial tilt, not distance.
4. Disagree — gravity acts between any two masses.
5. Disagree — the cycle is a network; shortcuts are valid paths.
6. Disagree — weathered matter moved or changed form; matter is
   conserved.
7. Disagree — every model distorts; judge it by what it is for.
8. Disagree — changing three variables at once makes the cause
   unknowable; a fair test changes one.

## Practice — Set A

1. Naturally occurring, inorganic, solid, definite chemical
   composition, orderly crystal structure.
2. Color varies inside one mineral: quartz is clear, pink, purple,
   smoky, and white yet remains quartz (same composition, hardness 7,
   glassy luster, curved fracture).
3. Streak is the color of the mineral's powder, tested by rubbing on
   an unglazed porcelain plate. It beats color when they disagree
   (Sample B: dark gray sample, red-brown streak) because powdering
   removes surface color and weathering.
4. See Lesson 01 guided practice 3 (diamond: hardest, yet shatterable).
5. Cleavage: breaking along flat planes (mica peels into sheets).
   Fracture: breaking along irregular surfaces (quartz: curved,
   shell-like pieces).
6. Hardness (A: between 3 and 5.5; C: 7) and break (A: flat-sheet
   cleavage; C: curved fracture). Pink vs. white is color — the
   weakest evidence — and must not carry the argument.

## Practice — Set B

1. Three separators: hardness (C: 7; D: 1–2), luster (C: glassy; D:
   dull, earthy), break (C: curved fracture; D: crumbles). Color and
   streak are identical and separate nothing.
2. The sample is harder than the streak plate. Do not force, grind,
   or press harder — record "no streak; harder than the plate" and
   use other tests.
3. Learned: one sample is softer than a penny (≈ 3) and the other is
   harder than a penny. Still unknown: everything else — one hardness
   comparison cannot identify a sample, and no name is asked for.
4. Hardness + breakage: hardness separates them numerically
   (3–5.5 vs. 7) and breakage separates them mechanically (flat-sheet
   cleavage vs. curved fracture); two independent properties agreeing
   is the strongest simple case.
5. Corrected: "Sample B looks dark, but its streak is red-brown and
   its luster is metallic — the powder test and the light test
   disagree with color, so color must not decide."
6. Ranking, strongest to weakest: (1) fingernail scratches it —
   a direct hardness measurement (1–2); (2) it crumbles — a break
   behavior consistent with softness; (3) dull luster — weakly
   consistent, many hard minerals are also dull; (4) white color —
   weakest, color identifies nothing. Accept crumbles/luster swapped
   with a defended reason.

## Practice — Set C

1. Igneous; large interlocking crystals mean slow cooling underground
   (crystals had time to grow) — intrusive igneous, granite-like.
2. Fossils survive only sedimentary burial: gentle, low-temperature
   deposition and cementation. Igneous: melting destroys remains.
   Metamorphic: heat and pressure recrystallize and destroy remains.
3. Shale is sedimentary (mud layers deposited and cemented); slate is
   metamorphic. Heat and pressure without melting turn shale into
   slate.
4. The holes are gas bubbles trapped as lava hardened quickly at the
   surface. Family: igneous, extrusive (pumice).
5. Corrected: "Metamorphic rock was changed by heat and pressure
   *without* melting." If it melted it would be magma, and cooling
   magma makes igneous rock.
6. Sedimentary (conglomerate). After deposition: compaction
   (squeezing under burial) and cementation (minerals gluing grains).
