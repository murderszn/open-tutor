# Unit 01 — Culminating Assessment (Session 16)

Untimed. The learner may use the unit vocabulary page but not the answer key.
Oral, drawn, and dictated responses accepted; the adult scribes when writing
stamina lags. **No answers are printed here — see
[answer-key.md](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment) (for the adult only).**

## A1 — Critique a flawed investigation plan (variable design)

A learner writes this plan for the question *"Does the height of a ramp change
how far a toy car rolls?"*

> "I will test a low ramp with the small red car and a high ramp with the big
> blue car. I will roll each car one time and measure how far it goes in
> centimeters. Then I will write down which height won."

Name **three** things wrong with this plan. For each one, say which rule it
breaks (one-variable-at-a-time, repeated trials, or units/labels) and how to
fix it.

## A3 — Build a labeled data table

A learner runs the pendulum investigation from the project: string lengths of
30 cm, 60 cm, and 90 cm; three trials each; the measured variable is the time
for 10 complete swings, in seconds. Draw the **empty** data table she should
use — column headers with quantities **and** units, and enough rows for every
trial. Then explain in one sentence why the changed-variable column matters
even for trials where it stays the same.

## A4 — Choose and sketch a graph

A learner collects this fictional practice data:

| Ramp height (cm) | Mean roll distance (cm) |
|---|---|
| 10 | 63 |
| 20 | 90 |
| 30 | 119 |

a. Line graph or bar graph? Give the one-sentence reason.
b. Sketch the graph: both axes labeled with units, the three points plotted,
   a title naming the test. (Neatness counts less than correct labels and
   correctly placed points.)
c. Write one honest sentence describing the pattern, starting with "In this
   sample…".

## A5 — Model vs. reality

A learner's project report includes this side-view diagram of the ramp
investigation: a labeled board, a stack of books setting the height, a toy car
at the top, an arrow showing the roll direction, and a ruler marking the
landing zone.

Write a **model-vs.-reality statement** (3–4 sentences): name two things the
model includes, two things it leaves out, and why the model is still useful
despite leaving things out.

## A6 — Reading: Maya's thermometer (original passage)

*Read the passage, then answer the questions. This passage was written for
this assessment.*

> Maya left a cup of water on the kitchen counter and measured its temperature
> every 10 minutes with the same thermometer, holding the thermometer at eye
> level each time. Her table read: 0 minutes — 70 °C; 10 minutes — 64 °C;
> 20 minutes — 59 °C; 30 minutes — 55 °C. She drew a line graph with time on
> the horizontal axis and temperature (°C) on the vertical axis, and she
> wrote: "In this sample, the water cooled from 70 °C to 55 °C over 30
> minutes, cooling faster at the start than at the end." Her brother looked at
> the graph and said, "So all water everywhere cools exactly like this."

a. Is Maya's question — "How does this cup's water temperature change over
   30 minutes?" — a testable question? Explain what she measured to answer
   it.
b. The 10-minute readings dropped 6 °C (70 → 64), 5 °C (64 → 59), then
   4 °C (59 → 55). What trend does this show? Use one of Maya's numbers in
   your answer.
c. Her brother's claim: observation, inference, or overclaim? Explain in two
   sentences.
d. Maya measured only one cup, once. Name one thing she could do to make her
   conclusion steadier, and explain why it would help.
