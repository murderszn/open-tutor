# Unit 01 — Measurement, Graphing, Models, and Investigation Design

**Grade:** 6 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#31](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/31)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 3–6 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 45 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

This unit rebuilds the investigation toolkit that every later unit depends on:
how to ask a question science can answer, how to name the **changed**,
**measured**, and **controlled variables** in a test, how to run a **fair
test** with **repeated trials**, how to **measure** length, mass, volume, and
temperature with standard tools (and convert within the metric system by place
value), how to keep data in **labeled tables with units**, how to choose and
read **line graphs** (change over time) and **bar graphs** (categories), and
how to build **models** — and, from the first week, how to say honestly what a
model includes and what it leaves out. The unit's discipline, reused all year:
label every number set as *your measurement* or as *sample/fictional practice
data*, and describe patterns as "the pattern in this sample," never as proof
about the whole world.

The Grade-5 entry skills (testable questions, one-variable tests, measuring to
the nearest centimeter, reading simple graphs) are **re-taught here as needed**
in the early sessions rather than assumed secure — per the
scope-and-sequence, Weeks 1–2 only probed entry points.

## Prerequisites (re-taught here as needed)

- Asking questions that can be explored by observing or testing.
- Planning a simple fair investigation (one thing changed, repeated trials).
- Measuring length to the nearest centimeter with a ruler; recording in
  labeled drawings and simple tables.
- Reading a simple bar graph.
- Science safety: the adult supervises every investigation; nothing near the
  mouth; living things handled gently; never look directly at the sun.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (mapped to the track
objectives in parentheses):

1. Ask a **testable question** and name exactly what would be measured or
   observed to answer it. (Track objective 1)
2. Plan and carry out a **controlled investigation**: name the **changed
   variable**, the **measured variable**, and the **controlled variables**;
   change one thing at a time; run at least three **repeated trials**; record
   data in a **labeled table with units** in every column. (Track objective 1)
3. **Measure** length to the nearest millimeter, mass to the nearest gram,
   volume to the nearest 10 milliliters, and temperature to the nearest
   degree Celsius with standard tools; **convert within the metric system**
   (mm/cm/m/km, g/kg, mL/L) by place value; record equivalents and data in
   labeled tables with units. (Track objective 1)
4. **Graph** data with **labeled axes and units**; choose **line graphs** for
   change over time and **bar graphs** for categories; describe **patterns**,
   **trends**, and **variability** in sample data without overclaiming ("the
   pattern in this sample"). (Track objective 2)
5. Develop and use a **model** to describe an investigation system (ramp or
   pendulum); **distinguish the model from the real system**; name what the
   model **includes** and what it **leaves out**. (Track objective 3)

## Essential question

*How do careful questions, careful measurements, and honest data displays
help us trust what an investigation found — and what can they never prove?*

## Vocabulary

**Testable question** — a question answerable by observing, measuring, or
testing. · **Observation** — something noticed with the senses or a tool. ·
**Inference** — an idea about what an observation *means*; one step beyond
what was directly seen. · **Variable** — something in a test that can change.
· **Changed variable** — the one thing the investigator deliberately changes
(also called the independent variable). · **Measured variable** — what is
measured to see the effect (also called the dependent variable). ·
**Controlled variable** — something kept the same on purpose so the test
stays fair. · **Repeated trials** — running the same test more than once to
check for consistency. · **Model** — a drawing, object, or description that
represents and helps explain a real system; every model includes some things
and leaves other things out. · **Axis** (plural: **axes**) — a labeled line
on a graph that shows a scale with units. · **Trend** — a pattern in how
data changes (for example, rising or falling over time). · **Variability** —
how much repeated measurements spread out; a sign of error or natural
difference. · Supporting terms used in the unit: *measure, convert, estimate,
data, data table, line graph, bar graph, claim, evidence.*

## Standards and source notes

- Reference framework: **Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS),
  middle-school band**, from the track audit's crosswalk (verified 2026-10-05
  against the official framework at nextgenscience.org and the California
  Department of Education's official NGSS reproduction). **No state adoption,
  accreditation, or alignment certification is claimed.**
- **This unit has no performance expectations of its own.** It builds the
  NGSS **Appendix F science and engineering practices** the later
  performance expectations depend on:
  - *Planning and carrying out investigations* — feeds MS-ESS1-3 (solar-system
    scale data) and MS-ESS2-5 (weather data collection).
  - *Analyzing and interpreting data* — feeds MS-ESS1-3 and MS-ESS2-5.
  - *Developing and using models* — feeds every later unit's modeling work
    (MS-ESS1-1, MS-ESS1-2, MS-ESS2-1, MS-ESS2-4, MS-ESS2-6).
- All practice datasets in lessons are **fictional practice data**, clearly
  labeled, never presented as the learner's own observations. The project's
  pendulum physics (longer string → longer swing time; about 2.0 s per swing
  for a 1-meter string) matches the standard small-angle result and is used
  only as a sanity check on sample data, never as a formula to memorize.
- Measurement facts checked against NIST (2026-10-11): metric prefixes
  (kilo = 1,000, centi = 1/100, milli = 1/1,000) and SI base units
  (meter, kilogram, second, kelvin).

## 16-session pacing (4 × ~45 min per week)

Sessions rotate: **investigation lesson → measuring-and-modeling practice →
reading-and-talk about evidence → review**, per the scope-and-sequence. Each
session names its activity and what "done well" looks like.

| Week | Sessions | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1–4 | **Questions and fair-test design.** S1: Lesson 01 — testable questions; observation vs. inference. *Success: learner sorts 8 questions and names a measurement for each testable one.* S2: observation-station practice (touch-only mystery object, sealed jar, plant, ice cube) + start the year-long question board. *Success: labeled observation/inference cards; 3 testable questions on the board with a named measurement each.* S3: Lesson 02 — variables (changed, measured, controlled), one-variable-at-a-time ramp or pendulum plan, repeated trials. *Success: learner names all three variable types for a ramp test and explains why one variable changes.* S4: plan-your-own-fair-test practice on paper + reading-and-talk (variables in everyday investigations: cooking, sports, pets). *Success: a written one-variable plan naming all variable types.* |
| 4 | 5–8 | **Measurement and data tables.** S5: Lesson 03 — measuring with standard tools (meter stick, graduated cylinder, balance, thermometer); metric conversions by place value (5 cm = 0.05 m); labeled data tables with units in every column. *Success: 8 conversions correct; a table with units in every column.* S6: measurement stations (length of 5 objects to the nearest mm, mass to the nearest g, volume to the nearest 10 mL, water temperature to the nearest °C) recorded in labeled tables. *Success: complete station tables, units everywhere, estimates recorded before measuring.* S7: reading-and-talk — the metric story (why one worldwide system); estimate-then-measure game with the printed ruler SVG. *Success: learner can explain in one sentence why scientists share units, and measures within 2 mm of the adult's check.* S8: conversion drill practice + data-table catch-up; start mini ramp trials to gather real data for Week 5 graphing. *Success: practice set complete; first trial table started.* |
| 5 | 9–12 | **Graphing and models.** S9: Lesson 04 — data tables to line graphs; line graphs show change over time; axis labels and units; describe trends and variability without overclaiming. *Success: learner reads the sample cooling-water line graph and describes its trend in one honest sentence.* S10: Lesson 05 — bar graphs for categories; when to choose line vs. bar; patterns in data; models vs. reality (what a model includes and leaves out). *Success: learner picks the right graph type for 4 new datasets and writes an includes/leaves-out statement for the ramp diagram.* S11: graphing practice from the learner's OWN Week-4 ramp/pendulum data + the cross-unit practice set begins. *Success: a labeled line or bar graph drawn from the learner's own measurements.* S12: project launch — choose ramp OR pendulum; write the plan; build the system-model diagram draft; adult checks materials and safety. *Success: approved plan + draft model with labeled parts.* |
| 6 | 13–16 | **Project and review.** S13: project trials — at least 3 repeated trials, measurements recorded in the labeled data table with units. *Success: complete data table, 3+ trials, units in every column.* S14: project graph + written claim + model-vs-reality statement; report assembly. *Success: report draft complete with graph, claim, and includes/leaves-out statement.* S15: review games (fair-test redesign, graph-choice challenge), then the **formative quiz** (`assessments/week-04_quiz.md`, scheduled here in review week). *Success: quiz complete; every miss turned into a corrected example.* S16: project showcase talk + **unit assessment** (`assessments/unit-assessment.md`). *Success: learner presents the investigation and defends the model honestly; assessment complete.* |

The five lessons fill Sessions 1, 3, 5, 9, and 10. The remaining eleven
sessions are practice, reading-and-talk, the investigation project, and
review — the lessons teach the skills; the sessions give them time to become
secure. Written load: 12–16 tasks per session, reviewed the same day; oral,
drawn, and manipulative responses always available.
