# Answer Key — Unit 05 (separate from student materials)

Every item in the lessons, practice sets, quiz, and assessment is
solved here. For open responses, the key gives a model answer and the
acceptable variation. Do not hand this file to the learner before
assessments are complete.

## Lesson 01 — A restless surface

**Guided practice**
1. Fast and small: earthquake, landslide, volcanic eruption.
   Slow and large: mountain uplift, seafloor spreading, continental
   drift. Both: earthquakes at a plate boundary (gradual creep,
   sudden slip); volcanoes (slow magma buildup, sudden eruption).
   Accept any correct sorting with a reason.
2. The mountain range changed Earth's surface more in total: an
   earthquake moves the ground meters in seconds, while 5 mm a year
   for 50 million years is 250,000 meters of uplift. "More" needs a
   time (how long) and a place (how much ground) — a fast event wins
   on speed, a slow event wins on total change.
3. Five of the six are on the Pacific rim. That suggests the Pacific
   rim is especially earthquake-prone (it is ringed by plate
   boundaries). It does not prove a rule: six famous quakes are a
   small, chosen sample.

**Independent practice**
4. Model answer: "Earth's surface changes at very different speeds:
   an earthquake can move the ground in seconds, while a continent
   creeps a few centimeters a year for millions of years. It also
   changes at very different sizes: a landslide changes one hillside,
   while plate motion rearranges whole continents." Accept any two
   sentences with one time-scale example and one spatial-scale example.
5. Error: earthquakes are quick but mountain building is slow — the
   speed of one process says nothing about the speed of another.
   Corrected: "Mountains form slowly, over millions of years, even
   though the earthquakes on their faults happen in seconds."

**Applied task.** Model report (Tohoku, Japan, 2011, magnitude 9.1):
"On March 11, 2011, a magnitude 9.1 earthquake struck off Tohoku,
Japan. It fits the Pacific-rim pattern: Japan sits on the Pacific
rim, where the Pacific plate subducts. The exception matters because
it shows the pattern is about plate boundaries, not about the Pacific
itself — Haiti's 2010 quake happened on a different boundary."
Accept any event with place, year, size, pattern fit, and the
exception's meaning.

**Exit check.** (a) Any fast, small-scale change: earthquake,
landslide, volcanic eruption. (b) Any slow, large-scale change:
mountain uplift, continental drift, seafloor spreading. (c) The table
suggests big earthquakes cluster around the Pacific rim (5 of 6),
because the rim is ringed by plate boundaries.

**Map activity (S2).** 1. Five. 2. Any correct pair, e.g., Haiti
(Caribbean, Atlantic side) and Eyjafjallajokull (Iceland,
mid-Atlantic). 3. It shows "volcanoes only happen at plate
boundaries" is false — Kilauea is a hotspot volcano in the middle of
a plate. 4. Accept any sentence naming the cluster pattern and the
need for a larger, fair sample before calling it a rule.

## Lesson 02 — Fossils and rocks that match across oceans

**Guided practice**
1. E1 *Glossopteris*: southern Africa, South America, Australia,
   Antarctica, India — seeds too heavy to cross an ocean. E2
   *Mesosaurus*: southern Africa, eastern South America — a
   freshwater reptile that could not cross salt water. E3
   *Cynognathus*: South Africa, South America — a land animal that
   could not cross an ocean. E4 *Lystrosaurus*: Antarctica, India,
   South Africa — a land animal; it also lived in Antarctica, where
   it could not live in today's cold.
2. The order matters because the same sequence of five layers in the
   same order is far harder to explain by coincidence than the same
   rock types alone. Two coasts could both have shale by chance; the
   same five layers stacked the same way means the same history.
3. Missing: the fossil and rock evidence. The fit alone is one clue;
   E1–E5 (fossils on both sides, matching rock layers) are needed to
   turn a suggestive shape into a case.

**Independent practice**
4. Model case: "*Glossopteris* fossils appear in southern Africa and
   South America, and their heavy seeds could not have crossed an
   ocean. The same five rock layers appear in the same order on the
   two facing coasts. The two continents fit together like torn
   paper. Together these support the claim that the continents were
   once joined. What the case still cannot explain is what force
   moved the continents." Accept any four-sentence case with one
   fossil, one rock, the fit, and the missing-force sentence.
5. Two errors: (1) *Mesosaurus* did not swim across the Atlantic — it
   was a freshwater animal that could not cross salt water; (2) a
   swimmer crossing would argue *against* joined continents, not for
   them. Corrected: "*Mesosaurus* fossils on two shores separated by
   an ocean support the claim that the shores were once joined,
   because a freshwater reptile could not have crossed the Atlantic."

**Applied task.** Model paragraph: "Three lines of evidence point to
joined continents. The same land fossils — *Glossopteris* and
*Mesosaurus* — appear on shores now separated by the Atlantic, and
neither could have crossed an ocean. The same five rock layers appear
in the same order on the African and South American coasts. The two
continents fit together, including their shelves. The objection I
cannot yet answer is what force could move a continent." Accept any
paragraph with three evidence items and the missing-force objection.

**Exit check.** E1 *Glossopteris*, E2 *Mesosaurus*, E3 *Cynognathus*,
E4 *Lystrosaurus*. Any one with the land/freshwater reason: a land or
freshwater organism cannot cross an ocean, so its fossils on two
shores mean the shores were joined; an ocean animal could have swum
across, so it proves nothing.

**Puzzle (S6).** 3. The fossil bands form one continuous band when
the continents are fitted together. 4. Model argument: "The southern
continents were once joined in Pangaea. *Glossopteris* and
*Mesosaurus* fossils form continuous bands across the join, and the
rock layers match — which is what you expect if the lands were one
land, and hard to explain if they were always apart."

## Lesson 03 — The seafloor tells the story

**Guided practice**
1. Model: "At a mid-ocean ridge, two plates pull apart and hot
   material rises to fill the gap, cooling into new ocean floor. The
   new floor moves outward as the plates separate, getting older with
   distance from the ridge. At a trench, the old, cold floor sinks
   back into the mantle."
2. The seafloor is young because it is recycled: new floor is made at
   ridges and old floor is destroyed at trenches. Continental rock is
   not destroyed the same way, so it can survive for billions of
   years.
3. The error is treating the ocean's age as the floor's age. The
   ocean is old, but its floor is young — under 300 million years —
   because it is constantly remade.

**Independent practice**
4. A wide ocean with young floor needs fast destruction: the Pacific
   floor is destroyed quickly at the many trenches around its rim
   (the Ring of Fire), so it never gets old despite the ocean's width.
5. Corrected: "Ridges make new seafloor and trenches destroy it."
   The age pattern matches: youngest floor at the ridges, oldest
   floor at the trenches. If trenches made floor, the oldest floor
   would sit at the trenches.

**Applied task.** Model paragraph: "Plates move because new seafloor
forms at mid-ocean ridges and old seafloor sinks at trenches. The
age pattern proves it: the floor is youngest at the ridges and gets
older toward the trenches, exactly as a spreading floor should. The
continents do not plow through the ocean floor; they **ride along on
the moving plates**." Accept any paragraph using the ridge, the age
pattern, and the trench, ending with the filled blank.

**Exit check.** (a) Youngest at the mid-ocean ridges, because new
crust forms there. (b) Old ocean floor sinks at trenches
(subduction). (c) About 2 centimeters a year — about as fast as your
fingernails grow.

**Graphing activity (S8).** 1. Yes, the line is straight: the age
grows steadily with distance. A straight line means a steady
spreading rate. 2. 2,000 km ÷ 20 km per million years = **100 million
years**. 3. 4,000 km ÷ 20 km per million years = **200 million years**
— matching the real age of the Atlantic's oldest floor. 4. Model:
"The graph shows age growing steadily with distance from the ridge,
which supports seafloor spreading — assuming the spreading rate
stayed steady over that time."

**Extension.** The Mariana Trench is about 11 km deep; Everest is
about 8.8 km high. 11 − 8.8 = **2.2 km deeper** than Everest is tall.

## Lesson 04 — Three boundaries, three fates

**Guided practice**
1. Divergent — plates move apart — ridge / new crust / rift valley.
   Convergent — plates move together — trench / volcanoes / mountains.
   Transform — plates slide past each other — fault / earthquakes.
2. A ridge: look for a long underwater mountain chain with young
   seafloor on both sides and the island sitting on the ridge line.
3. At a transform boundary nothing is created or destroyed — the
   plates only shear past each other. There is no new magma source
   from the boundary motion, so no volcanoes form from the boundary
   itself (a nearby hotspot is a separate cause).

**Independent practice**
4. Mid-Atlantic Ridge — divergent boundary; it builds a mid-ocean
   ridge and new seafloor. Himalayas — convergent boundary
   (continent–continent); it builds mountains. San Andreas Fault —
   transform boundary; it produces earthquakes along the fault.
   Mariana Trench — convergent boundary (ocean–continent); the
   Pacific plate subducts, forming a trench and volcanoes.
5. Error: India did not subduct under Asia — continents are too light
   and thick to subduct easily. Corrected: "The Himalayas formed
   because India collided with Asia and the crust crumpled upward
   into mountains."

**Applied task.** Model paragraph: "Before preparing, we must find out
which boundary type the town sits near: whether the plates are moving
apart, together, or past each other, and which plates are involved.
A convergent boundary means preparing for volcanoes, large
earthquakes, and possibly tsunamis; a transform boundary means
preparing mainly for shaking; a divergent boundary on land means
rift valleys and volcanic activity. The preparation follows the
motion." Accept any paragraph naming the needed facts and linking
them to different preparations.

**Exit check.** (a) Divergent — apart; convergent — together;
transform — sliding past. (b) Any correct place per type: e.g.,
Mid-Atlantic Ridge; Himalayas / Mariana Trench; San Andreas Fault.
(c) Convergent boundaries build the highest mountains, because
colliding continents crumple upward instead of sinking.

**Illustration activity.** 1. Left: divergent — the ridge and the
rising lava / new crust. 2. Middle: convergent — the trench, the
sinking plate, the volcano, the mountains. 3. Right: transform — the
straight fault cutting the crust with offset ground. 4. Model: "The
picture shows ridge lava glowing above the water, but real ridge lava
erupts underwater and cools immediately; the picture is still useful
because it shows the three motions clearly in one place."

**Boundary sort (S10).** Divergent: mid-ocean ridge, rift valley.
Convergent: deep ocean trench, Himalayas, island volcanoes
(Indonesia). Transform: San Andreas Fault. Island volcanoes can sit
in two columns (convergent above a subducting plate; divergent on a
ridge like Iceland) — the trench or the ridge decides.

## Lesson 05 — Deep time: strata and the geologic time scale

**Guided practice**
1. The trilobite lived first. Superposition: in an undisturbed stack
   the bottom layer is oldest, so the bottom fossil is oldest.
2. The two shale layers formed at about the same time — the same
   index fossil marks the same time in both places. You cannot
   conclude they formed in the same environment or from the same
   sediment.
3. Oldest to youngest: Earth's formation (Precambrian), first fish
   (Paleozoic), first dinosaurs (Mesozoic), first humans (Cenozoic).
   (First life also belongs in the Precambrian if listed.)

**Independent practice**
4. Yes — it passes both tests: it lived for a short time (10 million
   years is short on geologic scales) and it spread widely (four
   continents). Its presence marks the same time in layers in
   different places.
5. Two errors: (1) thickness is not age — thickness depends on how
   fast sediment piled up; (2) in a stack, the lower layer is older,
   not the thicker one. Corrected: "Layer D is above layer B, so D is
   younger than B; D's greater thickness only means sediment piled up
   faster there."

**Applied task.** Model paragraph: "The rocks themselves record a
history far longer than 6,000 years. On our rope timeline, where 1
meter stands for 1 billion years, 6,000 years would measure six
millionths of a meter — far thinner than a hair. But the rope runs
4.6 meters from Earth's formation to today, with the first life 3.3
meters back and the first dinosaurs 24 centimeters back. The layers
and the fossils in them are the evidence for that long history."
Accept any respectful paragraph using the rope arithmetic and the
rock evidence.

**Exit check.** (a) Superposition: in undisturbed strata, the oldest
layer is on the bottom and the youngest is on the top. (b) An index
fossil must have lived for a short time and spread widely. (c)
Precambrian → Paleozoic → Mesozoic → Cenozoic.

**Strata activity.** 1. Oldest to youngest: A, B, C, D, E (bottom to
top). 2. The fault is younger than layer D's absence shows: it cuts
A, B, and C but not D, so it formed after C and before D. 3. The shell
fossil in A lived first — A is the lowest layer, so it is oldest.
4. No: thickness depends on how fast sediment piled up, not only on
time. A thick layer can form quickly (a flood) and a thin layer can
take ages.

**Extension (calendar date for first fish).** 530 ÷ 12.6 ≈ 42 days
before December 31 → **November 19**.

## Lesson 06 — Review: evidence sorting

**Guided practice**
1. (a) Claim 1. (b) Claim 2. (c) Claim 3. (d) Claim 1.
2. Kilauea limits "volcanoes only happen at plate boundaries" — it is
   a hotspot volcano in the middle of a plate. Haiti limits "big
   earthquakes only happen around the Pacific rim" — it happened on a
   Caribbean boundary instead.
3. Objective 5 (auditing models and claims), with Objective 2 (the
   evidence argument).

**Independent practice**
4. Strongest for Claim 2: E2 (*Mesosaurus*) — a freshwater animal on
   two shores is very hard to explain without joined continents; or
   E7/E8, the seafloor pattern, because it supplies the mechanism.
   Accept either with the reason that it rules out the most
   alternatives. Strongest for Claim 3: superposition itself (the
   stack order) or the ash layer as a time stamp — accept either with
   a reason.
5. Error: the fossils are not decoration — they are independent
   evidence from a different family, and a case needs convergence.
   Corrected: "The fit of South America and Africa supports Pangaea,
   and the matching fossils and rock layers strengthen the case
   because they are independent lines of evidence."

**Applied task.** Model verdict (under 60 words): "Verdict: yes, the
continents moved. The strongest evidence is *Mesosaurus* on two
shores a freshwater animal could not cross, and the seafloor growing
older away from the ridges. One gap: the seafloor age table is
practice data, so the real surveys still need checking." Accept any
three-sentence verdict with a decision, two evidence items, and a
limit.

**Exit check.** (a) E5 → Claim 2. (b) E8 → Claim 2. (c) Any real
limit of the rope: it leaves out the events between the marks / the
continuity of time / exact dates within divisions / how crowded the
recent end is. (d) Any real limit of the illustration: the ridge lava
shown above water / no scale / the fault drawn as a simple crack.

**Sorting task.** Worked example 1: E7 supports Claim 2; its limit is
that it is practice data, not real measurements — the real evidence
is the pattern in E8. Trap 4: sorting E2 under Claim 3 is mostly
wrong (it is mainly plate-motion evidence), though a fossil in a
layer does say something about that layer's time — sort by the claim
it supports *best*. Trap 5: safer sentence — "E8 is consistent with
seafloor spreading and hard to explain without it."
