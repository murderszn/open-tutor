## Part A — Choose the best answer (1 point each; 6 points)

**Q1.** (Objective 1) An earthquake shakes a town for 30 seconds. A
mountain range rises 5 millimeters a year for a million years. Which
statement is true?
(a) The earthquake changed Earth's surface more, because it was faster
(b) The mountain range changed Earth's surface more in total, because
small yearly change adds up over a million years
(c) Neither changed Earth's surface at all
(d) Both changed Earth's surface by the same amount

**Q2.** (Objective 2) Which fossil is the strongest evidence that South
America and Africa were once joined?
(a) A shark fossil found on both coasts
(b) A *Mesosaurus* fossil found only in southern Africa and eastern
South America
(c) A fossil of a tree that still grows today on both continents
(d) A fossil found in only one valley in Africa

**Q3.** (Objective 2) The same five rock layers appear in the same
order on the west African coast and the east South American coast.
What does this best support?
(a) The two coasts have the same weather today
(b) The two coasts were once joined and have since separated
(c) The two coasts were formed by volcanoes last year
(d) The two coasts are at the same latitude

**Q4.** (Objective 3) The Himalayas are the highest mountains on
Earth. Which boundary formed them?
(a) A divergent boundary under the ocean
(b) A transform boundary with sideways sliding
(c) A convergent boundary where two continents collided
(d) A hotspot under the Indian plate

**Q5.** (Objective 4) A fossil species lived for a short time but spread
across four continents. Why is it useful for ordering rock layers?
(a) Because it is rare and hard to find
(b) Because it marks the same time in layers in different places
(c) Because it proves the exact age of the rock in years
(d) Because it lived in the ocean

**Q6.** (Objective 5) A learner looks at the boundary illustration and
says, "The ridge lava proves lava erupts above the ocean at ridges."
What is the correct response?
(a) Agree — the picture is a photograph of a real ridge
(b) Disagree — the picture is a stylized drawing, and real ridge lava
erupts underwater
(c) Agree — all volcanoes erupt above water
(d) Disagree — ridges never have lava at all

## Part B — Short answers (2 points each; 12 points)

**Q7.** (Objective 1) Give one example of a geoscience process that is
fast and small-scale and one that is slow and large-scale. For each,
state its approximate time scale and spatial scale.

**Q8.** (Objective 2) Card E7 gives seafloor ages: 0 km from a ridge →
0 million years; 1,000 km → 50 million years. Compute the spreading
rate in centimeters per year. Show both steps (km per million years,
then cm per year).

**Q9.** (Objective 3) Name the three plate boundary types, the motion
of each, and one surface feature each produces.

**Q10.** (Objective 4) A stack has four layers, bottom to top: W
(sandstone with shell fossils), X (shale), Y (thin ash layer), Z
(sandstone with leaf fossils). List them oldest to youngest. Then say
which fossil (shell or leaf) is from the organism that lived first,
and why.

**Q11.** (Objective 4) Use the calendar-year squeeze (1 day ≈ 12.6
million years). The first dinosaurs appeared 240 million years ago.
On what December date do they appear? Show the division.

**Q12.** (Objective 2) A learner has two cards: E2 (*Mesosaurus* on
two continents) and E7 (the seafloor age table). Explain in two
sentences why together they argue past plate motion better than
either one alone — and name one limit of E7.

## Part C — Evidence and models (4 points each; 16 points)

**Q13.** (Objective 2) Sort each evidence card to the claim it
supports best — Claim 1 (surface changes at many scales), Claim 2
(continents have moved), or Claim 3 (time scale organizes Earth's
history): E1 (*Glossopteris*), E5 (matching rock layers), E8
(seafloor age pattern). For one card, write one sentence saying what
it cannot prove alone.

**Q14.** (Objective 3) A new island appears in the middle of the
Atlantic Ocean. A scientist says it formed at a divergent boundary.
(a) What two features would you look for to check that claim?
(b) What would you expect to find if the scientist is right?
(c) What would make you doubt the claim?

**Q15.** (Objective 4) On the rope timeline (1 meter = 1 billion
years), the first-fish mark is 53 cm from "today" and the first-birds
mark is 15 cm from "today." (a) How many centimeters apart are the
two marks? (b) How many years apart are the two events? (c) Which
time division does each event fall in?

**Q16.** (Objective 5) Audit one model from this unit — the rope
timeline or the boundary illustration. Name two things the model
includes (shows correctly or usefully) and two things it leaves out
or gets wrong. Then write one revised claim that respects those
limits.

## Scoring guide

- Part A: 6 points (1 each).
- Part B: 12 points (2 each; 1 for a correct answer with no reasoning,
  2 for a correct answer with reasoning shown).
- Part C: 16 points (4 each; see the answer key for partial-credit
  splits).
- **Total: 40 points. Ready to move on: 30 or more with no Part C
  item at 0.** Below that, reteach the weakest objective with the
  teacher guide's moves before Unit 06.
