# Unit 05 — Plate Tectonics, Earth History, and Geologic Evidence

**Grade:** 6 · **Subject:** Science · **Track issue:** [#31](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/31)
**Status:** draft — awaiting educator review and merge. **Weeks:** 19–22 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 45 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

The ground under your feet is moving. Not in a way you can feel — about
two centimeters a year, roughly as fast as your fingernails grow — but
over millions of years that slow creep has split a supercontinent,
opened an ocean, and pushed up the highest mountains on Earth. This
unit follows the evidence for that motion, the way a detective follows
clues. First the learner reads a map of real earthquakes and volcanoes
and notices they are not scattered at random: they cluster in lines.
Then the learner weighs the fossil and rock evidence that convinced
scientists the continents were once joined — the same land fossils on
shores now separated by an ocean, the same rock layers on two sides of
the Atlantic. Then the learner reads the seafloor itself: the youngest
rock sits at the mid-ocean ridges and the floor gets older toward the
trenches, exactly as a spreading seafloor should. Then the learner learns
the three ways plates meet — pulling apart, colliding, sliding past —
and predicts what each one builds. Finally the learner zooms out to deep
time: rock layers stacked oldest-first, fossils marking the order of
life, and a 4.6-billion-year timeline squeezed onto a rope, where all of
human history is the last three millimeters.

Two boundaries govern the whole unit, and the teacher guide states them
plainly. **NGSS MS-ESS2-3's assessment boundary excludes paleomagnetic
anomalies** in oceanic and continental crust, so magnetic stripes are
mentioned only as background and never assessed. **NGSS MS-ESS1-4's
assessment boundary excludes recalling the names of specific periods or
epochs**, so the unit teaches the big divisions (Precambrian, Paleozoic,
Mesozoic, Cenozoic) as organizers and never tests period or epoch
names. Every quiz and assessment item is about evidence, boundary
reasoning, relative age, and model limits.

## Prerequisites (earlier-unit skills re-used; re-taught as needed)

- Testable questions, labeled data tables with units, reading a pattern
  from data without overclaiming (Unit 01).
- Models include some things and leave others out; a model is not the
  real system (Units 01–03).
- Rock families and formation evidence: sedimentary rock forms in layers
  from deposited sediment; igneous rock cools from melt (Unit 04).
  Strata in this unit are sedimentary layers.
- Scale reasoning: comparing sizes and distances on a number line and
  in a model (Unit 03). The rope timeline in Lesson 05 reuses it.
- Map reading: the learner has read simple maps since Unit 01; this
  unit's maps are region lists and boundary diagrams, not new map skills.
- Safety: an adult supervises every investigation. The rope timeline
  needs floor space; clear it before measuring. No hammers, no heat,
  no samples to break in this unit.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (mapped to the track
objectives in parentheses):

1. **Explain how geoscience processes change Earth's surface at varying
   time and spatial scales** — giving at least one fast, small-scale
   example (an earthquake, a volcanic eruption, a landslide) and at
   least one slow, large-scale example (plate motion, mountain uplift)
   — and explain that most processes are gradual but punctuated by
   sudden events. (Track objective 10)
2. **Analyze fossil, rock, continental-shape, and seafloor data to give
   evidence of past plate motions** — using at least one item from each
   of three evidence families (fossils, rocks/continental fit,
   seafloor structures) and stating what each item supports and what
   it cannot prove alone. (Track objective 10)
3. **Describe the three plate boundary types** — divergent, convergent,
   transform — **and predict the surface features each produces**
   (ridge and new seafloor; trench, volcanoes, and mountains; fault
   and earthquakes), using a real place as the example for each.
   (Track objective 10)
4. **Order events by relative age from rock strata** — applying
   superposition and index fossils — **and explain how the geologic
   time scale organizes Earth's 4.6-billion-year history**, placing
   at least six major events in order on a scaled timeline without
   recalling period or epoch names. (Track objective 11)
5. **Audit the unit's models** (boundary illustration, rope timeline,
   evidence board): name what each **includes and leaves out** —
   especially scale, time, and the difference between an illustration
   and an observation — and revise a claim when the evidence is weak
   or the model is overextended. (Track objective 3)

## Essential question

*If the ground moves too slowly to feel, how do we know it moves at
all — and how do we read 4.6 billion years of that movement in rocks?*

## Vocabulary

**Plate** — a large, rigid slab of Earth's lithosphere (crust plus the
uppermost solid mantle) that moves slowly over the hotter, weaker
material below. **Lithosphere** — the rigid outer layer of Earth: the
crust and the top of the mantle, broken into plates. **Divergent
boundary** — where two plates move apart; new crust forms in the gap.
**Convergent boundary** — where two plates move together; one may dive
beneath the other. **Transform boundary** — where two plates slide past
each other sideways. **Subduction** — one plate sinking beneath another
at a convergent boundary. **Trench** — a deep, narrow valley in the
ocean floor where one plate subducts. **Mid-ocean ridge** — a long
underwater mountain chain where plates pull apart and new seafloor
forms. **Seafloor spreading** — new ocean floor forming at a ridge and
moving outward as plates separate. **Fault** — a crack in Earth's crust
along which rocks have moved. **Rift valley** — a long valley formed
where land is pulling apart. **Pangaea** — the supercontinent that held
nearly all Earth's land together before it began breaking apart about
225–200 million years ago. **Continental drift** — Wegener's early-1900s
idea that continents move; the forerunner of plate tectonics.
**Fossil** — the preserved remains or traces of an organism that lived
long ago. **Strata** — layers of sedimentary rock, stacked in order.
**Superposition** — in undisturbed strata, the oldest layer is on the
bottom and the youngest is on the top. **Index fossil** — a fossil of
an organism that lived for a short time but spread widely, so its
presence marks the same time in rock layers in different places.
**Relative age** — how old something is compared with something else
(older or younger), without a number of years. **Geologic time scale**
— the organized divisions of Earth's 4.6-billion-year history, from
oldest to youngest. **Precambrian** — all time before 539 million years
ago, about 88% of Earth's history. **Paleozoic** — the era from 539 to
252 million years ago. **Mesozoic** — the era from 252 to 66 million
years ago, when dinosaurs lived. **Cenozoic** — the era from 66 million
years ago to today. **Earthquake** — shaking of the ground when rocks
along a fault suddenly move. **Volcano** — an opening where magma, gas,
and ash reach the surface. **Magma** — molten rock below Earth's
surface. **Evidence** — observations or data used to support a claim.
**Claim** — a statement about the world that evidence can support or
challenge.

## Standards and sources

- **NGSS MS-ESS2-2** (grades 6–8 band): "Construct an explanation
  based on evidence for how geoscience processes have changed Earth's
  surface at varying time and spatial scales." Clarification: emphasis
  is on how processes change Earth's surface at time and spatial
  scales that can be large (such as slow plate motions or the uplift
  of large mountain ranges) or small (such as rapid landslides or
  microscopic geochemical reactions), and how many geoscience processes
  (such as earthquakes, volcanoes, and meteor impacts) usually behave
  gradually but are punctuated by catastrophic events. Code, wording,
  and clarification verified 2026-11-08 against the California
  Department of Education's official NGSS reproduction.
- **NGSS MS-ESS2-3** (grades 6–8 band): "Analyze and interpret data
  on the distribution of fossils and rocks, continental shapes, and
  seafloor structures to provide evidence of the past plate motions."
  Clarification: examples of data include similarities of rock and
  fossil types on different continents, the shapes of the continents
  (including continental shelves), and the locations of ocean
  structures (such as ridges, fracture zones, and trenches).
  Assessment boundary: **paleomagnetic anomalies in oceanic and
  continental crust are not assessed.** Code, wording, clarification,
  and boundary verified 2026-11-08 against the same CDE reproduction.
- **NGSS MS-ESS1-4** (grades 6–8 band): "Construct a scientific
  explanation based on evidence from rock strata for how the geologic
  time scale is used to organize Earth's 4.6-billion-year-old history."
  Clarification: emphasis is on how analyses of rock formations and
  the fossils they contain are used to establish relative ages of major
  events in Earth's history. Assessment boundary: **assessment does
  not include recalling the names of specific periods or epochs and
  events within them.** Code, wording, clarification, and boundary
  verified 2026-11-08 against the NSTA NGSS listing and the CDE
  reproduction.
- **DCI ESS2.B (Plate Tectonics and Large-Scale System Interactions):**
  "Maps of ancient land and water patterns, based on investigations of
  rocks and fossils, make clear how Earth's plates have moved great
  distances, collided, and spread apart." Verified 2026-11-08 from the
  NSTA NGSS listing for MS-ESS2-3.
- **DCI ESS1.C (The History of Planet Earth):** "The geologic time
  scale interpreted from rock strata provides a way to organize Earth's
  history. Analyses of rock strata and the fossil record provide only
  relative dates, not an absolute scale." Verified 2026-11-08 from the
  NSTA NGSS listing for MS-ESS1-4. No claim of state adoption or
  accreditation is made. NGSS does not assign specific performance
  expectations to grade 6; this unit is the grade-6 year of the
  middle-school band, as the track audit establishes.
- Plate-motion evidence (continental fit, *Glossopteris*,
  *Mesosaurus*, *Cynognathus*, *Lystrosaurus*, matching rock layers,
