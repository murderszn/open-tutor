# Practice — Unit 05: Plate Tectonics, Earth History, and Geologic Evidence

Work each set after its lesson (see the [unit README](README.md) pacing).
Show your reasoning, not just the answer: a claim without its evidence
earns partial credit only. Every item is solved in the
[answer key](answer-key.md). Practice data is labeled where it is
invented; real events are marked as real.

## Set A — Timescales (after Lesson 01)

**A1.** Sort each process into **fast and small** (seconds to days;
one place) or **slow and large** (thousands to millions of years; a
region or a continent): earthquake · mountain uplift · landslide ·
seafloor spreading · volcanic eruption · continental drift.

**A2.** A mountain range rises 5 millimeters a year. How high will it
rise in 2 million years? Show the multiplication and give the answer
in meters.

**A3.** Use the historic earthquake table in Lesson 01: how many of
the six earthquakes happened on the Pacific rim? What does that number
suggest, and what does it not prove?

**A4.** A volcanic eruption buries a valley in one day. A mountain
range rises for 50 million years. Which one changed Earth's surface
more *in total*? Explain why the word "total" needs a time and a
place attached.

**A5. Error analysis.** A learner writes, "Earthquakes happen in
seconds, so plate motion must also happen in seconds." Find the error
and rewrite the sentence correctly.

**A6.** Write NGSS MS-ESS2-2 in your own words in two sentences: one
about time scales, one about spatial scales. Use one example from
Lesson 01 in each sentence.

**A7.** The 2010 Haiti earthquake (magnitude 7.0) did not happen on
the Pacific rim. Does that break the Pacific-rim pattern? Explain why
or why not in two sentences.

## Set B — Fossil and rock evidence (after Lesson 02)

**B1.** For each fossil card, list the continents where it is found:
E1 *Glossopteris*; E2 *Mesosaurus*; E3 *Cynognathus*; E4
*Lystrosaurus*. (Use the [unit README](README.md) evidence table.)

**B2.** Why is a *Mesosaurus* fossil stronger evidence for joined
continents than a fossil of an ocean shark? Give the one-sentence
reason about what each animal could cross.

**B3.** Card E5 describes the *same five rock layers in the same
order* on the African and South American coasts. Explain in two
sentences why the *order* matters, not just the rock types.

**B4.** Suppose scientists discovered that *Glossopteris* seeds were
light enough to float across an ocean. Would *Glossopteris* still be
good evidence that the continents were joined? Explain why or why
not.

**B5.** The fit of South America and Africa gets better when you match
the edges of their **continental shelves** rather than just their
coastlines. Why would the shelves fit better? (Hint: coastlines are
where the land meets today's sea level.)

**B6. Error analysis.** A learner writes, "Wegener proved continental
drift because he found fossils on two continents." Find the two errors
and rewrite the sentence correctly.

## Set C — Seafloor evidence (after Lesson 03)

**C1.** Use card E7 (0 km → 0 million years; 500 km → 25; 1,000 km →
50; 1,500 km → 75). Compute the spreading rate in kilometers per
million years, then convert it to centimeters per year. Show both
steps.

**C2.** Use your rate from C1 to predict the age of seafloor 2,000 km
from the ridge. Show the division.

**C3.** The oldest continental rocks are over 4 billion years old, but
the oldest seafloor is under 300 million years old. Explain the
difference in two steps (made at ridges; destroyed at trenches).

**C4.** Suppose the mid-ocean ridges stopped making new crust, but the
trenches kept destroying old crust. What would happen to the oceans
over millions of years? Give the one-sentence answer and the reason.

**C5. Error analysis.** A classmate writes, "Trenches make new
seafloor and ridges destroy it." Swap the two words that are wrong
and explain why the corrected sentence matches the age pattern.

**C6.** Plot card E7 (see Lesson 03's graphing activity) or describe
the plot in words: is the line straight? What does a straight line
mean about the spreading rate?

## Set D — Plate boundaries (after Lesson 04)

**D1.** Match each place to its boundary type and one feature it
produces. Write each as a full sentence: Mid-Atlantic Ridge ·
Himalayas · San Andreas Fault · Mariana Trench.

**D2.** Two ocean plates converge. Predict two features you expect to
find there, and name a real example.

**D3.** A new volcano appears in the middle of the Atlantic Ocean on a
ridge, and another appears in the middle of the Pacific, far from any
plate edge. Which one is more likely a hotspot volcano, and why?

**D4.** Why do transform boundaries produce earthquakes but no
volcanoes from the boundary itself? Answer in two sentences.

**D5. Error analysis.** A classmate writes, "Divergent boundaries
destroy crust and convergent boundaries create it." Swap the two
words that are wrong and explain why the corrected sentence is right.

**D6.** A coast has a deep ocean trench offshore and a chain of
volcanoes inland. Which boundary type is this, which plates are
involved, and what is happening to the ocean plate?

## Set E — Strata and deep time (after Lesson 05)

**E1.** Use the strata diagram in Lesson 05 (layers A–E, bottom to
top; a fault cuts A, B, and C but stops below D). List the layers
oldest to youngest, and say whether the fault is older or younger
than layer D, with the evidence.

**E2.** A fossil species lived for only 10 million years but spread
across four continents. Is it a good index fossil? Explain using both
tests (short-lived and widespread).

**E3.** Use the calendar-year squeeze (1 day ≈ 12.6 million years).
First mammals appeared 220 million years ago. On what December date
do they appear? Show the division.

**E4.** On the rope timeline (1 meter = 1 billion years), the first
fish mark sits 53 cm from "today" and the first birds mark sits 15 cm
from "today." How far apart are the two marks in centimeters? How
many years apart are the two events?

**E5. Error analysis.** A classmate writes, "Layer D is thicker than
layer B, so D is older than B." Find the two errors and rewrite the
sentence correctly.

**E6.** Place these in order, oldest to youngest, and name the time
division each one falls in: first humans · first fish · Earth's
formation · first dinosaurs · first life (first cellular life).

## Set F — Mixed evidence sorting (after Lesson 06)

**F1.** Sort each statement to Claim 1, 2, or 3 (see Lesson 06):
(a) "The Himalayas are still rising a few millimeters a year."
(b) "The same five rock layers appear on two coasts."
(c) "The oldest layer is on the bottom."
(d) "A volcano erupted in 1980 and buried a valley."

**F2.** Rewrite this sentence so it is scientifically honest: "The fit
of South America and Africa (E6) proves Pangaea." (Hint: replace
"proves" and add what else the case needs.)

**F3.** Pick two cards from different evidence families that together
make the strongest case for Claim 2 (past plate motions). Name the
cards and explain in two sentences why two families beat one.

**F4.** Kilauea is a volcano in the middle of the Pacific, far from any
plate boundary. What claim does it limit, and what is the corrected
claim?

**F5.** Audit the rope timeline model: name two things it includes and
two things it leaves out. (Hint: think about what happens between
the marks, and about how crowded the recent end is.)

**F6.** Audit the boundary illustration: name one thing it shows
correctly about each boundary type, and one thing it gets wrong or
leaves out.
