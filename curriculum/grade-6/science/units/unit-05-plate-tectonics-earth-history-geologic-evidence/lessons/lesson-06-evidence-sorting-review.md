# Lesson 06 — Review: Evidence Sorting

**Student goal:** I can sort every piece of evidence in this unit to
the claim it supports, say what it cannot prove, and spot the
misconceptions that would mislead me on the quiz.
**Time:** ~45 minutes · **Sessions:** S13 (plus quiz in S13)
**Materials:** evidence cards E1–E8 in the [unit README](../README.md);
the boundary illustration; the rope timeline; a notebook.

**Readiness check (2 minutes):** Without looking at the README, write
down as many evidence cards as you can remember (E1–E8) and one thing
each one shows. Then check yourself against the README and circle the
ones you missed — those are the ones to study first.

## Teaching

A detective's case is only as good as the sorting: which clue supports
which claim, and which clue is weaker than it looks. This lesson sorts
the whole unit. There are three claims and eight evidence cards:

- **Claim 1 — Earth's surface changes at many time and spatial
  scales.** Supported by: Lesson 01's fast/slow examples and the
  historic earthquake table (fast, local) plus mountain uplift and
  plate motion (slow, continental).
- **Claim 2 — The continents have moved (past plate motions).**
  Supported by: the fossils (E1–E4), the matching rocks (E5), the
  continental fit (E6), and the seafloor age pattern (E7–E8).
- **Claim 3 — The geologic time scale organizes Earth's 4.6-billion-
  year history from rock strata.** Supported by: superposition,
  index fossils, the ash layer, and the rope timeline (Lesson 05).

The sorting rule from Lesson 02 still holds: **one card is a clue;
cards that agree are an argument; a card that disagrees is a warning,
not a disaster.** The warning cards in this unit are Kilauea (a
volcano far from any boundary — it limits "volcanoes only happen at
boundaries") and Haiti (an earthquake away from the Pacific rim — it
limits "big earthquakes only happen around the Pacific"). A good
scientist keeps the warnings attached to the claim, not hidden.

### Worked example 1 — Sorting a hard card

**Question:** Card E7 is the seafloor age table (0 km → 0 million
years; 500 km → 25; 1,000 km → 50; 1,500 km → 75). Which claim does
it support, and what is its limit?

**Reasoning:** E7 supports Claim 2 (past plate motions): the age
grows steadily with distance from the ridge, which is exactly what a
spreading seafloor predicts, and the steady rate (2 cm a year) matches
the continents' drift. Its limit: the table is **practice data** —
invented numbers that are consistent with the real rate, not
measurements from a real ridge. It teaches the reasoning; it is not
itself evidence from the ocean. The real evidence is the pattern in
E8 (young at ridges, old at trenches, oldest seafloor under 300
million years), which comes from actual surveys.

### Worked example 2 — The misconception clinic

**Question:** A learner says, "The continents float on the ocean
like rafts." What is wrong, and what is the correction?

**Reasoning:** The continents do not float on water. They are part of
rigid plates of lithosphere — crust plus solid upper mantle — that
move over hotter, weaker mantle rock. The plates carry the continents;
the continents do not sail. The correction: "Continents ride on
moving plates; the plates move over the mantle, not on the ocean."

## Evidence-sorting task (20 minutes)

Work with the adult. Lay out cards E1–E8 (write each on a slip of
paper if you have not already). For each card, decide:

1. Which claim (1, 2, or 3) it supports best.
2. One sentence saying what it shows.
3. One sentence saying what it cannot prove alone.

Then answer the two trap questions:

4. A learner sorts E2 (*Mesosaurus*) under Claim 3 (time scale).
   Is that wrong? (Mostly: *Mesosaurus* is mainly plate-motion
   evidence. But it is not nonsense — a fossil in a layer also
   says something about that layer's time. The best sort is the
   claim the card supports *best*, and the honest note is that
   some cards touch two claims.)
5. A learner says, "E8 proves the seafloor is spreading." What is the
   safer sentence? ("E8 is *consistent with* seafloor spreading and
   hard to explain without it — proof would need the ridge, the age
   pattern, and the trenches together.")

## Guided practice

1. Sort these four statements to Claim 1, 2, or 3: (a) "The Himalayas
   are still rising a few millimeters a year." (b) "The same five rock
   layers appear on two coasts." (c) "The oldest layer is on the
   bottom." (d) "A volcano erupted in 1980 and buried a valley."
2. Name the two warning cards (Kilauea, Haiti) and, for each, write
   the claim it limits and how.
3. Which objective (1–5 in the unit README) is this lesson mainly
   practicing? (Objective 5 — auditing models and claims — with
   Objective 2, the evidence argument.)

## Independent practice

4. Pick the strongest single card for Claim 2 and the strongest
   single card for Claim 3. For each, write one sentence explaining
   why it is the strongest of its family.
5. **Error analysis:** A classmate writes, "The fit of South America
   and Africa proves Pangaea, and the fossils are just decoration."
   Find the error and rewrite the sentence correctly. (The fit alone
   is one clue; the fossils and rocks are independent evidence, not
   decoration — a case needs convergence.)

## Applied task

Write a three-sentence verdict, as if you were the judge of the
plate-tectonics case: (1) the verdict — did the continents move?
(2) the two strongest pieces of evidence; (3) the one gap or limit
you would still want checked. Keep it under 60 words.

## Exit check

(a) Sort E5 to its claim. (b) Sort E8 to its claim. (c) Name one
thing the rope timeline leaves out. (d) Name one thing the boundary
illustration gets wrong.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading access:** the adult reads each card aloud; the learner
  places it on the claim it supports before writing anything.
- **Alternate response mode:** the sort may be done by placing cards
  on three labeled claim mats instead of writing the table.
- **Extension:** add one card of your own — a real place or event
  from Lessons 01–05 not already on E1–E8 — and sort it, with its
  limit stated. (For example: the 1960 Chile earthquake, the San
  Andreas Fault, or the first-birds event on the rope.)

## Teacher note

The sorting task is the unit's real test of Objective 2: learners
who can sort can argue. Watch for two things: cards sorted by
keyword instead of by meaning (E2 under Claim 3 because "fossil"
appears in Lesson 05 — catch it and ask "what does this card
*show*?"), and the word "prove" used for a single card (redirect to
"supports" and "consistent with"). If the sort is shaky, reteach with
Lesson 02's worked example 2 before the quiz — the quiz assumes the
sort is solid. Answer-key reference: [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md),
Lesson 06.
