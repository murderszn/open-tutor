# Teacher Guide — Unit 05 (separate from student materials)

This guide is for the guiding adult. Do not hand it to the learner
before assessments are complete; the [answer key](answer-key.md) is a
separate file for the same reason.

## The assessment boundaries — read first

**NGSS MS-ESS2-3's assessment boundary states that paleomagnetic
anomalies in oceanic and continental crust are not assessed.** Honor
it exactly: magnetic stripes are mentioned once, as background in
Lesson 03, and never appear in a quiz, assessment, practice, or
project item. If a learner brings them up, praise the knowledge and
redirect to the ridge/trench/age evidence.

**NGSS MS-ESS1-4's assessment boundary states that assessment does not
include recalling the names of specific periods or epochs and events
within them.** Honor it exactly: the unit teaches the four big
divisions (Precambrian, Paleozoic, Mesozoic, Cenozoic) as organizers
and never tests period or epoch names. If a learner knows "Jurassic"
or "Cretaceous," acknowledge it and steer back to the big divisions.

## Pacing guidance

- **Week 19 (restless surface)** is the vocabulary-setting week. Do
  not rush Lesson 01's fast/slow distinction: learners who cannot
  attach a time and a place to "change" will misuse every later
  claim. Session S2's map activity matters more than finishing Set A
  early.
- **Week 20 (evidence)** is the argument week. The S6 puzzle is the
  emotional core — the moment the continents fit and the fossil bands
  join is the moment the theory clicks. Protect that session's time.
  S8's graph is the quantitative core: insist the rate (2 cm a year)
  is computed, not quoted.
- **Week 21 (boundaries and deep time)** is the densest week. The
  hand-motion trick (two hands apart / together / sliding past) is
  worth doing every time a boundary is named. The rope timeline needs
  a clear floor — measure it in S11, label it in S12, and do not let
  the build eat the labeling.
- **Week 22 (review)** is diagnostic. The quiz is formative: use its
  results to choose the S16 reteach topic from the learners' own exit
  checks, not from a fixed script.

## Safety procedures

- Clear the floor before the rope timeline; never run with the tape
  measure extended; the adult holds the tape's end.
- No hammers, no heat, no samples to break in this unit. The only
  physical materials are paper, rope, and poster board.
- The generated boundary illustration is a drawing, not a photograph
  and not evidence. Never present it as an observation or a
  measurement. The ridge lava in it is stylized: real ridge lava
  erupts underwater.
- The applied task in Lesson 05 touches the age of the Earth, which
  some families view differently. Keep the discussion on the evidence
  (the rope, the rocks, the dating) and never on the learner's family
  or beliefs.

## Misconceptions and reteaching moves

| Misconception | Reteach move |
|---|---|
| Slow means unimportant | 5 mm a year × 1 million years = 5,000 m (Lesson 01, Worked example 1) |
| One fossil proves the continents moved | Lesson 02, Worked example 2: one card is a clue; convergence of evidence is the case |
| Continents float on the ocean like rafts | Plates carry continents over the mantle; continents do not sail (Lesson 06, Worked example 2) |
| Trenches make new seafloor / ridges destroy it | Reversed: ridges make, trenches destroy; the age pattern proves it (Lesson 03, Practice C5) |
| A volcano alone identifies the boundary | One feature is a clue; two agreeing features are an argument (Lesson 04, Worked example 2) |
| Continents subduct like ocean plates | Continent–continent collision crumples upward into mountains (Lesson 04, Practice D5) |
| Thicker layer = older layer | Thickness is deposition rate, not time; lower is older (Lesson 05, Practice E5) |
| Relative age gives an age in years | Superposition gives order only; numbers come from radioactive dating, named but not taught here |
| The rope timeline's marks are the only events | The marks are samples; time is continuous between them (Project limits card) |
| "Proves" from a single card | Redirect to "supports" / "is consistent with" (Lesson 06, sorting task) |
| The illustration is an observation | It is a stylized drawing; the ridge lava and the fault are artistic choices (Lesson 04 limit check) |

## What "ready to move on" looks like

- The learner can give one fast and one slow surface change with time
  and spatial scales attached (Objective 1).
- The learner can argue past plate motion from three evidence families
  and state one limit per family (Objective 2).
- The learner can name each boundary's motion and predict its features
  with a real place (Objective 3).
- The learner can order a five-layer stack and place six events on
  the rope timeline (Objective 4).
- The learner can name two limits of any model in the unit (Objective 5).
- If any of these is missing after S16, reteach that objective's
  lesson (not the whole unit) before Unit 06.

## Reteaching moves by objective

- **Objective 1 weak:** re-run Lesson 01's worked example 1 with a
  new number (3 mm a year for 10 million years = 30,000 m) and re-sort
  the readiness list.
- **Objective 2 weak:** re-do the S6 puzzle with only E1 and E2, then
  add E5, then E7 — one family at a time, asking after each, "what
  does this add that the last card did not?"
- **Objective 3 weak:** use the hand motions for a full minute of
  rapid-fire naming ("divergent!" — hands apart) before re-attempting
  the boundary sort.
- **Objective 4 weak:** re-build a three-layer stack with classroom
  objects (books, paper) and re-derive the calendar-year dates from
  the 12.6-million-years-per-day rate.
- **Objective 5 weak:** pick the illustration and ask only, "What is
  wrong with this picture?" until the learner finds the ridge lava
  and the missing scale.
