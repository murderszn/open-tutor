# Unit 01 — Historical Inquiry: Archaeology, Maps, and Chronology

**Grade:** 6 · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track issue:** [#33](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/33)
**Status:** validated draft — delivered 2026-10-12; not yet educator-reviewed or merged.
**Weeks:** 3–6 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 40 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

Before studying any ancient civilization, a historian needs a toolkit: how to
ask questions that history can actually answer, how to read a map as
**evidence** (and know what it cannot show), how to sort and judge
**historical sources**, and how to put events in **chronological order** —
including how archaeologists read the layers of a dig site. This unit builds
that toolkit at the grade-6 entry level. Every later unit in the track reuses
it: asking compelling questions (U08 capstone), reading maps of river valleys
and trade routes (U02–U07), classifying and evaluating sources (U07), and
sequencing events across millennia (all units).

The kept starter lesson ([starter-lesson.md](../../starter-lesson.md)) is
folded into this unit as Lesson 02: its fictional town sketch and source-note
activities are reused for observation/inference and source-limit work, and its
"Answer checks" now live only in [teacher-guide.md](teacher-guide.md) and
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md) — never beside the student tasks.

## Prerequisites (re-taught here as needed)

- Reading a simple map: title, key/legend, compass rose, symbols, cardinal
  directions (grade-5 entry skill; re-taught at world scale in Lesson 03).
- Telling an observation apart from a guess about what it means.
- Writing in complete sentences; the adult scribes when writing is not the
  assessed skill.
- Safety: the adult supervises every hands-on investigation; nothing from a
  dig box goes near the mouth.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (mapped to the track
objectives in parentheses):

1. Write a **compelling question** and the **supporting questions** that help
   answer it, and explain how the supporting questions reinforce the
   compelling one. (Track objective 1; D1.1–1.4.6-8)
2. **Read and construct** a map with title, key/legend, compass rose, scale
   bar, grid, and symbols; use a scale bar to measure distance and a grid to
   locate a place; explain what a given map **cannot show**. (Track
   objective 2; D2.Geo.1–3.6-8)
3. **Classify** historical sources as artifacts, documents, images, or oral
   accounts; **detect limits** in the historical record from what different
   source kinds can and cannot tell us. (Track objective 9; D2.His.9–10.6-8)
4. **Evaluate** a source's credibility from its relevance and intended use,
   and **infer** a plausible maker, date, place of origin, or intended
   audience from clues inside the source. (Track objective 9;
   D3.2.6-8; D2.His.11.6-8)
5. **Sequence** events on a BCE/CE timeline across millennia and compute
   elapsed time; **order** finds by archaeological layer and explain what the
   order implies about what changed. (Track objective 9; D2.His.9–10.6-8)

## Essential question

*How do historians turn clues — questions, maps, sources, and layers of
earth — into trustworthy stories about the past?*

## Vocabulary

**Compelling question** — a big, open historical question with no single
answer; it is worth arguing about. · **Supporting question** — a smaller,
answerable question whose answer helps answer a compelling question. ·
**Observation** — something directly shown or stated; what is there. ·
**Inference** — an idea about what an observation *means*; one step beyond
what was directly shown. · **Historical source** — anything that gives
information about the past. · **Artifact** — a made or used object from the
past (a pot, a tool, a coin). · **Document** — a written record (a letter, a
list, a law). · **Oral account** — history passed down by speaking (stories,
interviews, traditions). · **Scale bar** — the marked line on a map showing
what a map length equals in real distance. · **Grid** — lettered columns and
numbered rows on a map for locating places exactly. · **Key (legend)** — the
box explaining what each map symbol means. · **Projection** — a way of
drawing the round Earth on a flat map; every projection distorts something
(shape, size, distance, or direction). · **Chronology** — the order of events
in time. · **BCE** — "Before the Common Era"; years counted backward from
year 1. · **CE** — "Common Era"; years counted forward from year 1. ·
**Stratigraphy** — the study of the layers (strata) in a dig; deeper layers
are usually older because new soil settles on top. · **Context** — where an
artifact was found, including its layer and what surrounded it; context is
what makes a find meaningful. · **Credibility** — how much a source can be
trusted for a particular question. · Supporting terms used in the unit:
*map, symbol, compass rose, timeline, sequence, maker, audience, purpose,
relevance, intended use, limitation, evidence, claim.*

## Standards and source notes

- Reference framework: the **College, Career, and Civic Life (C3) Framework
  for Social Studies State Standards** (National Council for the Social
  Studies, 2013), grades 6–8 band, from the track audit's crosswalk (verified
  2026-10-05 against the framework's grades 6–8 indicator tables). **No state
  adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification is claimed.**
- This unit teaches each indicator at the **grade-6 entry-band level**:
  sources are adult-curated, inquiry scaffolds are heavy, and evaluation is
  observation-level.
- Unit indicators: D1.1–1.5.6-8 (developing questions and planning
  inquiries), D2.Geo.1–3.6-8 (maps as evidence), D2.His.9–11.6-8 (classifying
  sources, detecting record limits, inferring maker/date/audience),
  D3.1–3.2.6-8 (gathering relevant information; evaluating credibility from
  relevance and intended use).
- All investigation datasets are **fictional practice data** (the dig trench,
  the practice map, the town sketch) or the labeled internal dataset
  `resources/un_countries.csv` (columns used: `name_common`, `cca2`,
  `cca3`, `region`, `subregion`, `capital`, `area_km2`; the `population`,
  `lat`, and `lng` columns are empty and are never used). The generated dig
  image is an **AI illustration of a fictional dig — not evidence** of any
  real site.
- Sensitive-content framing: archaeology is presented as the careful study of
  places people once lived — **never as treasure hunting**. Sites may have
  living descendant communities; their histories are told with respect, and
  the adult pre-selects all real-world examples.

## 16-session pacing (4 × ~40 min per week)

Each session names its activity and what "done well" looks like.

| Week | Sessions | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1–4 | **Framing inquiries.** S1: Lesson 01 — compelling vs. supporting questions; turning curiosity into questions. *Success: learner writes one compelling question and three supporting questions, and explains how one supporting question helps answer the compelling one.* S2: questioning practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set A) + start the unit **inquiry notebook** (kept all year). *Success: 8/10 sorts correct; notebook holds one compelling + three supporting questions on the unit's essential question.* S3: Lesson 02 — the kept starter lesson's fictional town sketch and source note: observation vs. inference, source limits. *Success: learner labels observations vs. inferences with reasons and names two things the map and note cannot show.* S4: review + week check — question-sort recap, observation/inference recap, misconceptions. *Success: learner names one observation, one inference, one source limit, and one next inquiry step (the starter's exit check).* |
| 4 | 5–8 | **Maps as evidence.** S5: Lesson 03 — scale bars, grid coordinates, key/legend, symbol conventions, what projections distort; the deterministic [map grid + scale SVG](assets/map-grid-scale.svg). *Success: learner locates the well at C1, measures the town–well road as 4 km, and names one thing the map cannot show.* S6: map practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set B) — measuring, locating, symbol reading, plus `un_countries.csv` lookups (exact columns named; population column never used). *Success: ≥80% correct with a cited map feature or CSV row per answer.* S7: investigation — **construct a simple map** of a familiar-to-fictional place with all six map elements: title, key, compass rose, scale bar, grid, symbols (D2.Geo.1.6-8). *Success: map has all six elements and the key matches the symbols.* S8: review + week check + **formative quiz** ([assessments/week-04_quiz.md](assessments/week-04_quiz.md)). *Success: quiz complete; learner explains one projection distortion in their own words.* |
| 5 | 9–12 | **Sources and their limits.** S9: Lesson 04 — source kinds (artifacts, documents, images, oral accounts); credibility from relevance and intended use (D3.2.6-8); inferring maker/date/audience clues (D2.His.11.6-8); the starter lesson's source-note activity revisited for limits. *Success: learner classifies four sources and infers a maker and audience for one mystery source with in-source reasons.* S10: source practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set C) — classification, limit detection, maker/audience inference. *Success: ≥80% correct; every inference cites a clue.* S11: investigation — **what is missing?** Given a set of source kinds about a fictional town event, detect limits in the record (D2.His.10.6-8, adult-guided); write what other source kinds would fill the gaps (D3.1.6-8). *Success: learner names two missing perspectives and the source kinds that would supply them.* S12: review + week check. *Success: learner explains why three palace documents alone cannot tell the whole story.* |
| 6 | 13–16 | **Chronology and archaeology.** S13: Lesson 05 — BCE/CE number lines ([bce-ce-timeline.svg](assets/bce-ce-timeline.svg)); sequencing across millennia; how archaeologists read layers and context; the AI-illustrated fictional dig ([fictional-dig-trench.png](assets/fictional-dig-trench.png)) ordering activity with text-only alternative. *Success: learner computes 2500 BCE → 1500 BCE = 1000 years and orders the dig layers oldest-to-youngest with reasons.* S14: sequencing/dating practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set D) + timeline construction. *Success: 10-event sequence correct; elapsed-time computations show work.* S15: **site investigation** — "The Fictional Tell" ([project.md](project.md)) work session: order finds by layer, infer what changed (D2.His.9–10.6-8, adult-guided). *Success: layer table complete with oldest-to-youngest ordering and one supported inference about change.* S16: unit review + **unit assessment** ([assessments/unit-assessment.md](assessments/unit-assessment.md)); project due. *Success: assessment complete; project submitted with rubric.* |

The project is launched in Week 6 S13 (Lesson 05's ordering activity doubles
as the project warm-up) and completed through S15; it is due at S16.
