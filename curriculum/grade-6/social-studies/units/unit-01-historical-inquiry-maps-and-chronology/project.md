# Project — The Fictional Tell

**Unit:** [U01 — Historical Inquiry: Archaeology, Maps, and Chronology](README.md) · **Grade 6**
**Time:** Week 6, S13 (launch) through S15 (work session); **due S16** with the unit assessment.
**Adult role:** supervises the hands-on option, pre-selects any real-world
examples, and scores with the rubric below.

## The scenario (fictional)

A **tell** is a hill made by people — layer upon layer of houses, floors,
and trash built up over centuries in one spot. Your site is **Tell Mirra**,
a completely fictional mound. No real place, no real remains: this is a
practice dig for learning how archaeologists read layers.

The (fictional) excavation team has cleared one trench and recorded five
layers. Your job: reconstruct what happened at Tell Mirra, oldest to
youngest, and write a one-page **site report** — a claim about what changed,
the evidence behind it, and the limits of what you can know.

### The trench record (fictional data)

| Layer (top → bottom) | Soil | Finds |
|---|---|---|
| Layer 1 — surface | dark topsoil, grass roots | a plastic bottle cap; a coin stamped "2019" |
| Layer 2 | brown soil | a glazed pottery shard (shiny coating); an iron nail; a clay tablet with wedge-shaped marks |
| Layer 3 | reddish soil | an unglazed pottery shard (rough, no coating); a bronze pin |
| Layer 4 | pale sandy soil | a chipped stone blade; a bone fragment |
| Layer 5 — deepest | gray clay | charcoal flecks; a pebble painted with a handprint |

*All finds, dates, and the site itself are fictional practice data. The
"2019" coin is part of the fiction — it marks the made-up "modern" surface
of the tell.*

## Steps

1. **Read the trench record.** List the five layers top to bottom in your
   table.
2. **Order the layers** oldest (1) to youngest (5). Write the layer rule
   you used in one sentence.
3. **Classify every find** as an **artifact** or a **document**. (Hint: is
   anything here written? What kind is the clay tablet?)
4. **Order three finds** oldest to youngest: the stone blade, the bronze
   pin, the iron nail. Cite the layers.
5. **Infer one change.** Compare Layer 4 with Layer 2: what is different
   about how people made things? Write your inference as "probably"
   language, then write **one more find** that would support it and **one**
   that would weaken it.
6. **Check the limits.** Write two things the trench record *cannot* tell
   you (exact dates? names? beliefs?).
7. **Optional hands-on dig.** With the adult: build the tub in layers,
   bury the stand-ins, then excavate one spoonful at a time, logging each
   find's layer before lifting it. Or do the paper-card simulation. Either
   way, log: find → layer → your order guess.
8. **Write the site report** (one page): (a) your ordered story of Tell
   Mirra, oldest to youngest; (b) your inference about what changed, with
   the evidence; (c) two limits of the record; (d) one question you would
   investigate next, and which source kind would answer it.

## Deliverables

1. Completed stratigraphy table (all five layers ordered, finds classified).
2. One-page site report (story + inference with evidence + two limits +
   next question with source kind).
3. (If hands-on chosen) the dig log: each find with its layer.

## Rubric — 20 points

| Criterion | 5 | 3 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Layer ordering** (5) | All five layers in correct oldest→youngest order; the "deeper = older" rule stated correctly | Four correct, or order right but rule misstated | Two–three correct | No correct ordering |
| **Sequencing reasoning** (5) | Stone blade → bronze pin → iron nail ordered by layer with citations; no exact dates claimed from layers alone | Order right, citations thin; or one exact-date overclaim | Order partly right | No valid sequencing |
| **Source classification** (4) | Every find classified; the clay tablet correctly named a **document** with a reason | All classified, tablet mislabeled | Most classified | Little/no classification |
| **Inference about change** (4) | One "probably" inference about craft/material change, tied to specific layers, plus one supporting and one weakening find named | Inference present but vague, or missing the support/weaken check | Inference is a guess with no layer tie | No inference |
| **Communication** (2) | Report organized (story → evidence → limits → next question); unit vocabulary used correctly | — | Report incomplete or vocabulary misused | No report |

**Scoring:** 18–20 = strong; 14–17 = solid, review one criterion; 10–13 =
re-teach ordering or classification, then revise; below 10 = redo with the
adult modeling each step.

## Respect note (read together before starting)

Real digs are slow, careful, and done with permission. Many real sites are
the heritage of **living communities** — their ancestors' homes, not
treasure chests. Archaeologists record *context* (exact layer and
surroundings) because ripping an object out of its place destroys the story.
Our tell is fictional, but the habit is real: **observe, record, and
respect — never just collect.** If this project sparks interest in real
archaeology, the [Resource Pack](resource-pack.md) lists museum and
institute sources to explore with the adult — including how descendant
communities partner in the work.
