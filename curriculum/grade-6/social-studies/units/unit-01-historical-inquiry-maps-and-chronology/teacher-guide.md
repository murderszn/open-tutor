# Unit 01 — Teacher Guide

**For the guiding adult.** Worked solutions live in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md) — this guide gives session-by-session notes,
misconception notes, reteach moves, readiness signals, and scoring guidance
without duplicating solutions. Assessment is evidence-based and untimed;
never speed-test. Review each lesson's **Teacher note** the evening before.

## How to use this unit

- The learner's written work is reviewed the **same day**. A wrong answer is
  a teaching moment: ask "show me your thinking" before correcting.
- **Oral, drawn, pointing, and manipulative responses are always valid.**
  Scribe dictated answers when writing is not the assessed skill.
- The **inquiry notebook** (started in Lesson 01) is a year-long artifact —
  keep it visible; every later unit adds questions to it.
- Lessons 01–05 are the five S1/S3/S5/S9/S13 launches; the S2/S6/S10/S14
  sessions use [practice.md](practice.md); S7/S11/S15 are investigations;
  S4/S8/S12/S16 are review and checks.

## Session-by-session notes

### Week 3 — Framing inquiries

**S1 — Lesson 01 (compelling vs. supporting questions).** Model the sort
aloud before the learner tries: think *out loud* through "big and arguable
vs. small and answerable." The reword move (opinion → historical question)
is the session's core skill — praise keeping the curiosity while changing
the question. *Ready for S2:* learner can sort 6/8 and reword one opinion
question.

**S2 — Practice Set A + inquiry notebook.** Work guided items 1–3 together,
then release. Watch for supporting questions that are secretly second
compelling questions (the "one fact answers it" test fixes this). Date the
notebook page; the notebook must hold one compelling + three supporting
questions by session end.

**S3 — Lesson 02 (starter lesson: observation/inference, source limits).**
This is the kept starter lesson's core — run the sketch and note activities
as written. **Never show the answer checks below beside the student tasks;**
they are yours only. Readiness: learner can distinguish "directly shown"
from "a guess about what it means." If the learner calls the sketch "proof"
of short distances, stop and re-teach "not to scale" before continuing.

**S4 — Review + week check.** Recap: question sorts, observation vs.
inference, one source limit. The week check is the starter's exit check:
one observation, one inference, one limit, one next inquiry step — all four
required. *Reteach trigger:* fewer than 3 of 4 correct → replay Lesson 02's
guided practice with new statements before Week 4.

### Week 4 — Maps as evidence

**S5 — Lesson 03 (scale, grid, key, projection).** Open the
[map-grid-scale.svg](assets/map-grid-scale.svg) large (print or project).
Model measuring once with a real ruler before the learner tries. Grid
labels are letter-first — drill "C1" aloud until automatic. The projection
example (Greenland/Africa) often surprises; let the surprise land, then name
the trade: direction kept, size stretched. *Ready for S6:* learner locates
C1 and measures 4 km unprompted.

**S6 — Practice Set B.** Guided items 1–3 together. For the CSV items, open
`resources/un_countries.csv` together first and read the header aloud;
state explicitly: **the `population`, `lat`, and `lng` columns are empty —
we never use them.** Item 5's estimate (~8 km) is intentionally un-clean;
accept 8–8.5 km with shown work. *Reteach trigger:* grid labels backwards
("1C") → letter-first drills on the printed map until 5/5 clean.

**S7 — Investigation: construct a map.** The learner draws a simple map of
a familiar-to-fictional place (their room, an invented town) with **all six
elements**: title, key, compass rose, scale bar, grid, symbols. Check: does
the key match the symbols? Does the scale bar have numbers and units? Is
there a north arrow? This is D2.Geo.1.6-8 in action — constructing, not
just reading. Allow a fictional place if a real room feels too personal;
nothing identifiable goes in the repo (work stays private anyway).

**S8 — Review + formative quiz.** Review misconceptions (vague "far,"
projection = "wrong map"), then administer
[week-04_quiz.md](assessments/week-04_quiz.md). Score the same day; any item
below 70% class accuracy gets re-taught in Week 5 S12's review, not skipped.

### Week 5 — Sources and their limits

**S9 — Lesson 04 (source kinds, credibility, maker/audience).** Post the
four kinds with icons. The ranking task ("for *this* question") is the
session's hinge — if the learner ranks sources in general, redirect with:
"for *which* question?" The mystery-receipt inference models "probably"
language; require it in every inference the learner makes. *Ready for S10:*
learner classifies 4/4 and infers one maker/audience with a cited clue.

**S10 — Practice Set C.** Guided items 1–3 together; item 2's ranking needs
the relevance-first reminder. Item 10 ("3,000 years old, so it must be
true") is the session's key error analysis — connect to *intended use* (an
ancient royal boast is still a boast).

**S11 — Investigation: what is missing?** Give the learner 3–4 sources on
one fictional event that all share one perspective (e.g., all palace-made).
The learner lists present kinds/makers, names the missing voices, and
writes which source kinds would fill the gaps (D2.His.10.6-8, D3.1.6-8).
Adult-guided: if the learner cannot see the absence, ask "who is not
speaking here — and why might that be?" Accept "their words were never
written down" and "their materials rotted" as valid mechanisms.

**S12 — Review + week check.** Recap: four kinds, credibility-for-a-question,
maker/audience inference, missing voices. Re-teach any Week 04 quiz misses.
*Ready for Week 6:* learner explains why three palace documents cannot tell
the whole story.

### Week 6 — Chronology and archaeology

**S13 — Lesson 05 (BCE/CE, layers, context).** Teach the number line with a
finger trace: BCE counts *down* to year 1. Drill the no-year-0 check (1 BCE
→ 1 CE = 1 year) until the learner uses it to self-correct the naive add.
For the dig illustration, read the caption language aloud: **AI illustration
of a fictional dig — not evidence.** The text-only alternative carries the
same data; either route answers the ordering questions. Launch the project
([project.md](project.md)) — read the scenario and the respect note
together.

**S14 — Practice Set D + timeline construction.** Guided items 1–3 together
(item 3's 44 BCE → 44 CE = 87 years is the trap: 44 + 44 − 1). The
classroom timeline (Lesson 05 application) is built here; "not to scale"
labeling is acceptable — order matters more than precision at this step.

**S15 — Site investigation (project work session).** The learner completes
the stratigraphy table, classifications, orderings, and the inference. Work
the hands-on dig-box or paper simulation here if chosen. Coach the
"probably" language and the support/weaken check (rubric criterion 4) —
this is new and needs modeling. *Ready for S16:* table complete, report
drafted.

**S16 — Unit review + unit assessment + project due.** Spiral review of all
five objectives, then the [unit assessment](assessments/unit-assessment.md)
(~40 min, untimed). Collect the project; score with the rubric within two
days and return it with one specific praise and one revision target.

## Lesson 02 — the starter lesson's answer checks (adult only)

Moved here from the student-facing starter lesson; never place beside
student tasks:

- The creator of the source note is **not** named. Do not assume the town
  council wrote the note merely because the council is mentioned.
- "Two requests were received" is **stated information** (observation).
- The clinic is **southwest** of the library in the sketch.
- The sketch lacks scale, path surface, traffic, and distances; the note
  lacks the requesters' identities, path-use rates, and costs.
- "Everyone wants a path change" goes beyond the source — two requests do
  not speak for a whole town.
- Useful next evidence: a count of path users, a safety/accessibility audit
  of the path, a cost estimate, and voluntary perspectives from *different*
  path users.

## Per-lesson misconception notes and reteach moves

### Lesson 01 — Compelling vs. supporting questions
- **Misconception:** any "why" question is compelling. **Move:** the
  arguability test — "could two people with evidence disagree?" If yes,
  compelling; if one fact settles it, supporting.
- **Misconception:** supporting questions that are second compelling
  questions. **Move:** the "one fact answers it" test; shrink until a map,
  dig, or document could answer it.
- **Reteach:** reword drills — give five opinion questions, learner rewords
  each into a historical question keeping the curiosity.

### Lesson 02 — Observation, inference, source limits
- **Misconception:** "the council is mentioned, so the council wrote it."
  **Move:** "what do the words *directly state*?" — creator unnamed means
  creator unknown.
- **Misconception:** the sketch is scaled evidence. **Move:** "where is the
  scale bar?" — the "not to scale" label is the lesson.
- **Reteach:** statement-sorting cards (observation/inference piles) until
  8/8 clean with reasons.

### Lesson 03 — Maps as evidence
- **Misconception:** grid labels read backwards ("1C"). **Move:**
  letter-first drills, spreadsheet analogy (column, then row).
- **Misconception:** vague distance words ("far") without measuring.
  **Move:** no distance claim without a scale-bar number.
- **Misconception:** distortion means the map is "wrong." **Move:** a
  projection is a *trade* — name what was traded (size for direction, etc.).
- **Reteach:** re-measure the town–well road together; then learner measures
  a new pair unprompted.

### Lesson 04 — Sources and their limits
- **Misconception:** "old = true." **Move:** intended-use analysis — an
  ancient boast is still a boast.
- **Misconception:** credibility as a fixed trait. **Move:** always append
  "*for which question?*"; re-rank the same three sources for two different
  questions.
- **Misconception:** no signature = useless. **Move:** clue-hunting on the
  mystery receipt; "probably" is honest historian language.
- **Reteach:** four-box sort of 8 new source cards with one limit named per
  card.

### Lesson 05 — Chronology and archaeology
- **Misconception:** bigger BCE number = younger. **Move:** the countdown
  anchor — BCE counts down toward year 1.
- **Misconception:** naive add across eras (2802 for 776 BCE → 2026 CE).
  **Move:** the 1 BCE → 1 CE = 1 year self-check; "no year 0" as a chant.
- **Misconception:** "deeper = older" as absolute. **Move:** teach the
  pit-digging exception; archaeologists record because earth gets disturbed.
- **Misconception:** the AI illustration is a real dig photo. **Move:**
  repeat the caption; contrast with how real digs are slow, permitted,
  respectful work.
- **Reteach:** floor timeline with event cards; layer-ordering with the
  text-only alternative until the rule is stated unprompted.

## Sensitive-content guidance

- **Archaeology framing:** this unit never presents digs as treasure hunts.
  Sites may have living descendant communities; their histories are told
  with respect and in the present tense where communities are living. All
  dig scenarios in the unit are fictional; any real-world example the adult
  adds must be pre-selected for accuracy and respectful framing (see the
  [Resource Pack](resource-pack.md), references 5 and 7).
- **Difficult content:** this unit contains none of the track's sensitive
  topics (forced labor, conquest, exclusion) — but the "missing voices"
  routine (Lesson 04, S11) is the foundation those later units build on.
  Keep the routine honest and plain: name who is absent and why, without
  graphic detail.
- **Belief systems:** not taught in this unit; when dating systems (BCE/CE)
  come up, present them as a shared scholarly convention, nothing more.

## Scoring guidance

- **Week-04 quiz:** 18 points; review same day; re-teach misses in S12.
- **Unit assessment:** 36 points; bands in the assessment file. Evidence
  cited per answer is part of the score — an uncited correct answer earns
  partial credit; press for the citation.
- **Project:** 20-point rubric in [project.md](project.md); return within
  two days with one praise and one revision target.
- **Inquiry notebook:** not scored — it is a working tool. Date pages;
  completeness (one compelling + supporting sets per lesson) is the only
  expectation.
