# Project — The Grain Vote

**Investigation question:** How did Athens decide about the grain
that kept it alive — and who paid for the decision?

## Overview

Build a five-part dossier that follows one question through Athens'
civic life and its trade: the assembly must decide whether to spend
League money on more triremes to protect the grain route, or to
spend it on a new storehouse at Piraeus. You will run the
deliberation yourself (Session 7 lab), trace the grain that the
decision is about (Session 11 simulation), and weigh who gained and
who lost (Lesson 05). Your dossier is a classroom research file, not
a real ancient document: every invented element (names, prices,
vote counts) must be labeled "classroom model," and every real fact
must name its evidence (lesson, map, timeline, or Resource Pack
source). The dossier is due at Session 16. Work sessions: Session 7
(deliberation lab warm-up), Session 11 (grain simulation), Session
13 (significance draft), Session 15 (full work session).

## Materials

- Adult-approved Resource Pack sources for any fact beyond the
  lessons.

## Steps

1. **Choose and locate (Sessions 3–4).** On the schematic map,
   mark Athens, Piraeus, Delos, and the Black Sea grain route.
   Write two sentences: where Athens is, and why it needed grain
   from abroad.
2. **Run the deliberation (Sessions 7–8).** Using the Session 7
   lab procedure, run a mini-council and a mini-assembly on the
   proposal: "Spend League money on ten more triremes, or on a new
   storehouse at Piraeus?" Record the proposal, two speeches for
   and two against, the vote count (classroom model), and one
   sentence on how the two bodies' procedures changed the result.
3. **Trace the cargo (Sessions 10–12).** Build the trade ledger:
   one cargo out (olive oil, wine, or pottery) and one cargo in
   (grain). For each leg, name the goods, the route, and one
   barrier. Use the fictional grain-price table from Lesson 03 to
   set a price for a good year and a bad year, and explain the
   difference with supply and demand.
4. **Weigh who gained and who lost (Sessions 13–14).** Write the
   who-gained/who-lost analysis: one gainer and one loser from the
   grain trade, one gainer and one loser from the Delian League,
   and one group the record does not let us hear from — with the
   honest sentence "the record does not say" where it belongs.
5. **Judge the legacy (Sessions 14–15).** Write the one-page
   legacy judgment from Lesson 05's applied task: should we call
   Athens "the birthplace of democracy"? Give two pieces of
   evidence, name one group the label leaves out, and end with
   what the label should really mean.
6. **Review and submit (Session 16).** Check your dossier against
   the rubric below. Label every invented element "classroom
   model." Submit all five parts together.

## Deliverables

1. **Map page:** schematic map marking with Athens, Piraeus,
   Delos, and the grain route labeled, plus the two location
   sentences, and a note stating the map is a classroom schematic,
   not to scale.
2. **Deliberation record:** the proposal, two speeches for and two
   against (each labeled "classroom model"), the vote count, and
   the one-sentence procedure note.
3. **Trade ledger:** one cargo out and one cargo in, each with
   goods, route, and one barrier; good-year and bad-year prices
   from the fictional price table, with a supply-and-demand
   explanation for the difference.
4. **Who-gained/who-lost analysis:** one gainer and one loser from
   trade, one gainer and one loser from the League, and one
   "the record does not say" group.
5. **Legacy judgment:** one page answering "Should we call Athens
   'the birthplace of democracy'?" with two pieces of evidence,
   one excluded group named, and a closing sentence on what the
   label should mean.

## Rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | 4 points | 2–3 points | 0–1 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Map and grain need | Athens, Piraeus, Delos, and the grain route correctly located and labeled; two sentences explain why Athens needed imported grain | Most places correct; grain-need explanation vague or missing a reason | Places wrong or unlabeled; no grain-need explanation |
| Deliberation record | Proposal, 2 speeches for + 2 against, vote count, and a procedure note that names how council and assembly differed | One element missing, or the procedure note does not name a real difference | Several elements missing; no procedure note |
| Trade ledger | Cargo out and cargo in, each with goods, route, and a barrier; good-year and bad-year prices with a correct supply-and-demand explanation | One leg or one price missing, or the price explanation does not use supply and demand | Ledger missing a leg; prices with no explanation |
| Who gained / who lost | A gainer and a loser for trade and for the League, plus one "the record does not say" group named honestly | One gainer/loser pair missing, or the silent group missing | Pairs missing; no silent group |
| Legacy judgment | Judgment + 2 pieces of evidence + one excluded group named + a closing sentence on what the label should mean | One element missing or evidence not tied to the judgment | Several elements missing; no excluded group |
