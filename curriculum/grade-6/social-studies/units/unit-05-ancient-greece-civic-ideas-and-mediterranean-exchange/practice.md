# Practice Sets — Unit 05

**How to use:** Sets A–D match Sessions 2, 6, 10, and 14. Work a set,
then check it with your adult the same day (solutions are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md), adult only). Aim for at least 80%
correct before moving on; below that, re-read the matching lesson
and retry the missed items.

## Set A — Citizenship and maps (Session 2; Lesson 01)

A1. Match each term to its meaning: (a) polis, (b) citizen,
(c) metic, (d) agora.
A2. Explain the difference between a polis and a city, using each
word correctly in one sentence.
A3. Why did Greece have hundreds of separate city-states instead
of one big country? Give a geography reason and a sea reason.
A4. Name the three groups who lived in Athens but could not vote,
and give one fact about each group's life in the city.
A5. Athens had about 30,000–40,000 adult male citizens in a city
of about 250,000–300,000 people. Explain why historians say
"about" instead of giving an exact number.
A6. Using the schematic map ([assets/aegean-trade-map.svg](assets/aegean-trade-map.svg)),
state which marked city is Athens' harbor, which sea lies between
Greece and Asia Minor, and which two places the grain route
connects.
A7. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "Athens was a democracy, so it was
fair to everyone who lived there." Agree or disagree, with two
reasons.
A8. Draw or describe a simple diagram of how an Athenian law was
made: council proposes → assembly debates → assembly votes. Label
what each body did and who took part in each step.

## Set B — Systems and timelines (Session 6; Lesson 02)

B1. Define *ostracism* and explain, in two sentences, why Athens
used it.
B2. Name the four Spartan institutions from Lesson 02 (two kings,
gerousia, assembly, ephors) and state what each one could do.
B3. Cleisthenes mixed citizens into 10 new tribes from city,
coast, and countryside. Explain how mixing people from different
places weakened the old rich families.
B4. Why did Athens elect its generals instead of choosing them by
lot? Give the reason from the lesson.
B5. Place in order, earliest first: Peloponnesian War begins
(about 431 BCE), Cleisthenes' reforms (about 508 BCE), Solon's
reforms (about 594 BCE), democracy ends (about 322 BCE).
B6. Compute, showing work: (a) about 594 BCE → about 508 BCE;
(b) about 478 BCE → about 454 BCE; (c) about 508 BCE → about
322 BCE.
B7. **Error analysis:** "The Council of 500 made the laws, and the
assembly just watched." Correct this sentence.
B8. Explain one way Sparta's government was stronger than Athens'
and one way it was weaker, using a concrete detail for each.

## Set C — Trade and prices (Session 10; Lesson 03)

C1. List two imports and two exports of Athens, and explain what
each one was used for.
C2. Explain the difference between a merchant ship and a trireme,
using each word correctly in one sentence.
C3. What were the Long Walls, and why did they matter most during
a war? Give two reasons.
C4. The Delian League's treasury moved from Delos to Athens in
454 BCE. Explain what changed because of that move, in two
sentences.
C5. The grain-price table below is **fictional classroom-model
data** — invented numbers for practice, not real ancient prices.
Year 1 (good harvest): 120 ships arrive, price 4 drachmas a
measure. Year 2 (pirates active): 60 ships arrive, price 10
drachmas. Year 3 (League clears pirates): 130 ships arrive, price
3 drachmas. (a) Which year had the most grain arriving? (b) Which
year had the highest price, and why? (c) What changed between
Year 2 and Year 3 to bring the price down?
C6. Name two barriers to trade from Lesson 03 and explain how each
one could raise the price of grain in Athens.
C7. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "If Athens banned grain exports,
its grain price would fall." Agree or disagree, with two reasons.
(Hint: Athens imported grain; it did not export it.)
C8. A Black Sea farmer sells grain to Athens, and an Athenian
potter sells pottery to the Black Sea. Explain who gains on each
side and name one risk each side takes.

## Set D — Diffusion and significance (Session 14; Lesson 04)

D1. Define *diffusion* and name the three carriers from Lesson 04.
D2. Explain how the Greek alphabet differed from the Phoenician
alphabet, and why the difference mattered.
D3. Name two things that spread through Greek colonies besides
goods, and explain how a colony carried each one.
D4. Apply the two-question significance test to the Olympic games:
did they change how people lived, and did their effects last?
Give one reason for each answer.
D5. The historian Thucydides warned that Athens' grand temples
would make its power look twice as great as it was. Explain in two
sentences why buildings alone are bad evidence for a city's power.
D6. Using the schematic map, trace one idea's journey: the alphabet
from the eastern Mediterranean to Sicily. Name two stopping places
and say what kind of place each one was (port, colony, island).
D7. **Error analysis:** "Greek ideas spread because Greek armies
conquered every place they reached." Correct this sentence with
two facts from Lesson 04.
D8. Choose Cleisthenes or the alphabet. Write a two-sentence
significance case: one sentence applying the "changed how people
lived" test, one sentence applying the "effects lasted" test.
