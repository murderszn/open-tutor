# U02 Practice Sets

**Unit:** [U02 — Early Humans: Agriculture and Settlements](README.md)
Four sets matched to the pacing: A → S2, B → S6, C → S10, D → S14. Each set
mixes recall, reasoning, and one error-analysis or explain-your-reasoning
task. Solutions live only in the [answer key](answer-key.md).

---

## Set A — Environments and forager life (S2, after Lesson 01)

1. Define **foraging** and **mobility** in your own words (one sentence each).
2. Match each group to the mobility the environment suggests — **high** or
   **low** — and give one environmental reason:
   a. A band on a plain where herds migrate hundreds of miles each year.
   b. A band at a warm spring where fish and wild grains are always available.
   c. A band in hills with summer berries and deep winter snow.
3. For the place below, check the four needs (water, wild food, shelter,
   safety), then decide: **camp, pass through, or avoid?** Defend your
   decision in two sentences.
   **Cedar Cove:** a freshwater stream, oak trees with autumn acorns, a dry
   rock overhang, and a cliff path above flood level — but wolf packs den
   nearby in spring.
4. Explain in two or three sentences: how can two forager groups have very
   different yearly routines even though both "just forage"?
5. Name one **constraint** the environment puts on each choice:
   a. Building wooden houses on a treeless plain.
   b. Eating river fish in a desert with one small spring.
6. Error analysis: "Foragers never stayed anywhere long because they didn't
   know how to build permanent houses." Identify the mistake and rewrite the
   sentence correctly. (Hint: think about the Lake People and *why* groups
   moved.)
7. Draw a quick sketch map of a forager camp you invent: label water, one
   wild food source, shelter, and one safety feature. Write a one-sentence
   caption naming its best feature.
8. **Explain your reasoning:** A friend says early foragers were "primitive"
   because they didn't farm. Write three sentences respectfully disagreeing,
   using one specific example of forager knowledge from Lesson 01.

## Set B — Causes, trade-offs, change and continuity (S6, after Lesson 02)

1. List three causes of the agricultural revolution. Mark any debated cause
   with "(debated)."
2. Explain the seed-selection cause in two or three sentences: how did
   saving the biggest seeds, repeated for generations, change the harvest?
3. Build a benefit/cost chart for **Card 2 (the Dry Hills Band)** from
   Lesson 02: at least three benefits and three costs of starting to plant
   in the little valley.
4. Sort each as **change** or **continuity**, with a one-sentence defense:
   a. "People began to live with animals they had tamed."
   b. "People told stories about their ancestors."
   c. "Work took more hours each day."
   d. "People needed drinking water."
5. A learner's chart lists four benefits of farming and zero costs. What is
   missing from their thinking? Write two sentences explaining why the cost
   column matters before any verdict.
6. **Explain your reasoning:** Why is "about 10,000 years ago" more honest
   than "in 8000 BCE" when dating the start of farming? (Two sentences —
   think about how the change actually happened.)
7. Choose **Card 4 (the New Mothers' Question)**. Write two sentences for
   the first mother's evidence and two for the second mother's. Then write
   one sentence: which evidence matters more to *you*, and why?
8. Write a one-paragraph verdict: "Was farming a good trade for the
   Riverbend Band?" Use at least two benefits and two costs, and name what
   was sacrificed.

## Set C — Surplus, specialization, innovation (S10, after Lesson 03)

1. Define **surplus** and explain why storage is part of the definition.
   (Two sentences.)
2. List the five-step surplus chain in order, then write one sentence for
   each arrow explaining *why* the step leads to the next.
3. Trace a production chain for **a woven basket**: name at least four
   workers from raw material to finished basket, and say what each does.
4. Explain how **irrigation** changed both farming output and village
   organization. (Two or three sentences.)
5. Using the village illustration (or its text-only alternative), name two
   features that exist only because of a surplus. For each, apply the test:
   "who is freed, and what feeds them?"
6. A village harvests enough for 100 people but has 120 people. Is there a
   surplus? Explain in one or two sentences. (Hint: re-read the definition.)
7. Error analysis: "Specialists appeared because the village chief ordered
   some people to stop farming and make pots." What is wrong with this
   explanation? Rewrite it correctly in two sentences. (Hint: think about
   Çatalhöyük — what did archaeologists *not* find?)
8. **Explain your reasoning:** A learner says towns grew "because farming
   made life easy." Write three sentences disagreeing with evidence: was
   farm labor easy? What actually pulled people into towns?

## Set D — Comparing regions, timeline, multi-cause (S14, after Lesson 04)

1. Name the two comparison regions. For each, name one starter crop and one
   starter animal (or note "few large domesticated animals" where true).
2. State two similarities and two differences between farming in the two
   regions.
3. Using the agriculture timeline: about how many years passed between the
   Natufian culture (about 12,500 BCE) and early cultivation (about
   9,600 BCE)? Show your subtraction.
4. Using the timeline: which came first — the tower of Jericho (about
   8,000 BCE) or the founding of Çatalhöyük (about 7,100 BCE)? About how
   many years apart?
5. Write a 3–4 sentence multi-cause explanation for why farming began in the
   **Fertile Crescent**. Include at least three causes of different kinds
   (environmental, biological, human) and mark any debated cause.
6. Error analysis: "Maize proves Mesoamericans learned farming from the
   Fertile Crescent, since wheat came first." Identify two mistakes and fix
   them. (Hint: what plant did Mesoamerica start with? What does the timing
   show?)
7. Write the **forager's** perspective on a band settling down to farm:
   3–4 sentences with at least two pieces of evidence.
8. **Explain your reasoning:** Why do historians call the independent
   invention of farming in two regions *stronger* evidence of a general
   human pattern than if it had happened only once? (Two or three sentences.)
