# Project — The Settlement Council

**Unit:** [U02 — Early Humans: Agriculture and Settlements](README.md)
**Launched:** S9 (with Lesson 03) · **Work sessions:** S11, S15 · **Due:** S16

## The situation (fictional)

It is about 9,000 BCE, at the edge of the Fertile Crescent. You are the
trusted advisor to the **Riverbend band** — thirty people who forage along
a slow river where wild wheat and barley ripen thick every autumn, wild
goats water at dawn, and a limestone cliff offers dry caves. Some families
want to clear ground and plant wheat on purpose this spring, pen a few
goats, and stay. Others want to keep the moving life their grandparents
knew.

The band's council has asked **you** for a recommendation: **settle and
farm, or keep moving?** There is no perfect answer — the council wants your
honest reasoning, your evidence, and your respect for the people who will
live with the choice.

## Your deliverables (all five)

1. **The recommendation** (one page or dictated equivalent): your claim —
   settle or keep moving — supported by **three evidence-based reasons**
   drawn from this unit (environment, causes, trade-offs, surplus).
2. **The sketch map** (one page): your proposed camp or village site, with
   a proper map **key** showing at least four features: water, fields or
   wild-food grounds, shelter/houses, and storage or safety. Mark where
   each of the four forager needs is met.
3. **The benefit/cost table**: at least **three benefits** and **three
   costs** of the choice you recommend — the costs must be genuine, not
   straw men.
4. **The counterclaim paragraph**: "The best argument *against* my advice
   is..." — state the other side's strongest case fairly, in 3–5 sentences.
5. **The forager's reply** (3–4 sentences): if you advised settling, write
   the response of a band member who disagrees and wants to keep moving (or
   the reverse). Use at least one piece of unit evidence.

## Steps

1. **S9 — Launch.** Re-read your inquiry-notebook page "The farming trade."
   List what the Riverbend band knows: their environment (Lesson 01), the
   farming option (Lesson 02), what a surplus would make possible
   (Lesson 03).
2. **S9–S10 — Gather evidence.** Fill a two-column notes page: "Evidence for
   settling" / "Evidence for moving." Every note must trace to a lesson
   (e.g., "Lesson 02: stored grain survived winter").
3. **S11 — Work session 1.** Draft the sketch map with its key, and the
   benefit/cost table. The adult checks: does the map show all four needs?
   Are the costs genuine?
4. **S11–S14 — Draft the recommendation.** Claim first, then three reasons,
   then the counterclaim paragraph. Read it aloud: does it sound like
   advice to real people?
5. **S15 — Work session 2.** Write the forager's reply. Peer-check with the
   adult: is the other side's view fair and evidence-based, or a caricature?
   Revise.
6. **S16 — Deliver.** Submit all five deliverables together with the rubric
   below. The adult scores against the rubric; the learner keeps a copy in
   the inquiry notebook.

## Materials

- Notebook, pencil, ruler (for the map key and scale bar — reuse U01's map
  conventions: title, key, compass rose, scale bar)
- The four scenario cards (Lesson 02), the village illustration or its text
  alternative (Lesson 03), the agriculture timeline (Lesson 04)
- Optional: colored pencils for the map

## Rubric (20 points total)

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Good | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Claim + 3 reasons** (5 pts: claim 1, reasons up to 4) | Clear settle/move claim; 3 reasons, each tied to unit evidence | Claim clear; 3 reasons, 2 tied to evidence | Claim vague or only 2 reasons | No clear claim or reasons without evidence |
| **Sketch map + key** (4 pts) | Title, key, compass rose, scale bar; 4+ features; all four needs visibly met | Key + 4 features; 3 needs met | Key incomplete or 2–3 features | No key or features unlabeled |
| **Benefit/cost table** (4 pts) | 3+ genuine benefits and 3+ genuine costs | 3+ each, but one cost is weak | Fewer than 3 per side | One side missing |
| **Counterclaim** (3 pts) | Strongest opposing argument stated fairly, 3–5 sentences | Opposing argument stated, slightly weak | Caricature of the other side | Missing |
| **Forager's reply** (2 pts) | Respectful, evidence-based, 3–4 sentences | Respectful, 2–3 sentences, some evidence | Dismissive or unevidenced | Missing |
| **Clarity** (2 pts) | Organized, complete sentences, all five deliverables | Minor gaps | Hard to follow in places | Incomplete submission |

**Scoring the claim row:** 1 point for a clear claim + 1 point per
evidence-based reason (max 4) = 5 points.

**What "done well" looks like:** the council can tell what you advise, why
(in their terms — food, work, children, safety), what it will cost them,
and that you heard the other side. A recommendation that admits real costs
scores higher than a one-sided sales pitch.

## Adult notes

- Keep the band fictional and the advice framed as reasoning practice, not
  a real decision. If the learner asks "what really happened?" — both
  happened: some bands settled, others kept moving for thousands of years.
  There is no single right answer, only better-defended ones.
- Watch for present-day judgment language ("they were stupid to farm").
  Redirect: "What did *they* know, and what did they value?"
- The forager's reply is the perspectives objective (D2.His.4.6-8) —
  score it on fairness and evidence, not on which side the learner picked.
