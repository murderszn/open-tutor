# U02 Teacher Guide — ADULT ONLY

**Unit:** [U02 — Early Humans: Agriculture and Settlements](README.md)
Do not show this file — or the [answer key](answer-key.md) — to the learner.
Student files never contain solutions.

## How to use this guide

Sessions are ~40 minutes. Each entry gives the session's goal, the key
teaching moves, what to watch for, and the reteach move if the exit check
fails. Formative checks are scored the same day; the adult records gaps in
the inquiry notebook's back page.

## Week 7 — Environments and early human life

**S1 — Lesson 01.** Goal: the four needs + environment-as-schedule.
Teaching moves: read the readiness items slowly — #3 is the trap that
teaches the lesson's thesis (settled ≠ farming). In Part A, have the learner
physically check off the four needs for Willow Bend vs. Dry Ridge before
hearing the verdict. Watch for: learners deciding from one feature ("nice
view"). Reteach: cover the verdict, re-describe a new place, and require
all four checks aloud before any decision.

**S2 — Practice Set A.** Goal: independent environment reasoning. Let the
learner attempt Set A solo, then talk through #3 (Cedar Cove) together —
the wolf-den detail tests whether they weigh safety against the other
three needs. Accept "camp in winter, avoid in spring" as a strong answer.
Watch for: treating constraints as opinions. Reteach: sort constraints vs.
preferences ("no water" vs. "ugly view").

**S3 — Investigation: camp-spot mapping (D2.Geo.4.6-8).** Goal: defend two
camp choices with marked evidence. The learner draws or builds a simple
landscape (paper, tokens, or the printed practice map), marks water, wild
food, and shelter with the key, and defends two camp spots — one for summer,
one for winter. Require the defense to name the season: a good summer camp
can be a bad winter camp. Watch for: marking features without defending
choices. Reteach: ask "what changes in winter?" until the seasonal argument
appears.

**S4 — Review + week check.** Goal: consolidate Lesson 01. Recap: the four
needs, mobility as expertise, settled ≠ farming. Week check (oral or
written): name two constraints that shaped forager life; explain one in a
sentence. If the learner cannot: re-run S1's Willow Bend/Dry Ridge pair
with a new invented place.

## Week 8 — The agricultural revolution

**S5 — Lesson 02.** Goal: multi-cause thinking + benefit/cost charts.
Teaching moves: present the four causes as a stack, not a sequence — stress
that no single cause was enough. The population-pressure cause is the
unit's first "debated" label; model saying "we don't fully know" with
confidence. Do Card 1's chart together, thinking aloud through the cost
column — learners will try to skip it. Watch for: the genius-inventor story;
single-cause answers ("the climate did it"). Reteach: cover the four
causes, name three new ones from the learner's own life ("why did our
family move?" — never one reason).

**S6 — Practice Set B.** Goal: independent benefit/cost + change/continuity.
Set B #8 (the Riverbend verdict paragraph) is the week's best diagnostic:
a verdict with no named sacrifice is incomplete thinking, not a wrong
opinion. Score it against the answer key's exemplar, not against your own
view. Watch for: benefits-only charts. Reteach: require the cost column
first — "three costs before one verdict."

**S7 — Investigation: the trade-off debate.** Goal: argue both sides with
evidence. Use the scenario cards: the learner picks the hardest card and
argues it both ways, two minutes per side, then the adult cross-examines
("what did the other side get right?"). Record the strongest evidence each
way in the notebook. This is deliberation practice for U05/U08 — keep it
respectful, keep evidence attached. Watch for: straw-manning the other
side; confusing "I feel" with "the evidence shows." Reteach: make the
learner steelman — state the other side's case so well its supporters would
nod.

**S8 — Review + formative quiz.** Goal: check Objectives 1–2. Review the
week's charts and sorts, then administer
[week-08_quiz.md](assessments/week-08_quiz.md). Score the same day. Items
9–10 are the reasoning stretch — partial credit for defended answers even
when the verdict differs from the key. Reteach plan for Week 9: any learner
below ~70% on items 4–8 re-does one benefit/cost chart with the adult
before S9's surplus chain (the chain assumes the chart skill).

## Week 9 — Surplus, specialization, settlements

**S9 — Lesson 03 + project launch.** Goal: the surplus chain + the surplus
test. Teaching moves: walk the flow diagram one arrow at a time, demanding
the "why" at each arrow ("why does stored surplus free people? — because
someone else's stored grain feeds them"). The village illustration activity
is the project warm-up — do it together and keep the learner's five-item
list; it becomes project evidence. Launch the project: read the situation
aloud, confirm the five deliverables are understood, and set the S11/S15
work sessions. Watch for: reading the illustration as a real place — repeat
the caption. Reteach: if the chain won't stick, act it out — ten farmers,
fifteen mouths, five freed people; assign roles.

**S10 — Practice Set C.** Goal: production chains + surplus definition.
Set C #6 (100 people, food for 100) is the definition check: no surplus.
Learners who answer "yes, barely" have memorized the word without the
concept — re-teach with Readiness #3 from Lesson 03. Watch for:
chief-ordered specialization (the Çatalhöyük correction in #7).
Reteach: "nobody voted — the math of extra food made it possible."

**S11 — Investigation: settlement-pattern mapping (D2.Geo.8.6-8) + project
work session 1.** Goal: explain how farming extended settlement; draft the
project map and benefit/cost table. Investigation: on a simple drawn
landscape, the learner places three settlements (forager camp, early farm
village, larger town) and explains for each *why there* — water, fields,
trade route — and one new pressure each creates (more land cleared, more
waste, more mouths: D2.Geo.12.6-8, plainly, no graphic detail). Then
project work: the adult checks the draft map (key? four needs?) and the
table (genuine costs?). Watch for: settlements placed without reasons;
costs that are secretly benefits. Reteach: "what does this settlement need
that the last one didn't?"

**S12 — Review + week check.** Goal: consolidate the chain. The learner
explains surplus → specialists → towns without notes. Week check: one
innovation, explained as productivity ("more food per worker — how?").
If the chain is shaky, rebuild it from the S9 role-play before Week 10.

## Week 10 — Comparing early farming regions

**S13 — Lesson 04.** Goal: comparison + multi-cause + perspectives.
Teaching moves: build the two-column chart *with* the learner before
showing Part A's version — the effort of recall is the learning. Do the
timeline subtraction together the first time (BCE direction is the
hard part: bigger number = older). The perspectives segment needs adult
guidance: model respectful disagreement explicitly, and stop any
"farmers dumb / foragers smart" framing on the spot. Watch for:
copy-theory ("Mesoamerica copied"); ranking regions ("better"). Reteach:
cover Part A, rebuild the chart from the timeline and the crop lists alone.

**S14 — Practice Set D.** Goal: independent comparison + timeline math.
Set D #8 (why two inventions = stronger evidence) is the unit's highest
reasoning bar — accept any defended answer that invokes independent
evidence for a general pattern. Check every subtraction: 12,500 − 9,600 =
2,900; 8,000 − 7,100 = 900; 9,600 − 8,500 = 1,100. Watch for: subtracting
without converting "years ago" (Lesson 04 #5). Reteach: write both dates in
the same form first (BCE and BCE), then subtract.

**S15 — Perspectives task + project work session 2.** Goal: D2.His.4.6-8 in
the learner's own words; finish the project. Perspectives: the learner
writes both the farmer's and the forager's view of the Riverbend choice
(3–4 sentences each, evidence attached). Score on fairness + evidence, not
on the side picked. Project: the forager's reply draft, then the full
five-deliverable check against the rubric — anything missing is homework
before S16, not a surprise at submission. Watch for: caricature of either
side; missing counterclaim (the most-skipped deliverable). Reteach: read a
strong counterclaim from the answer key's exemplar aloud as a model.

**S16 — Unit review + assessment; project due.** Goal: consolidate and
assess. Review: the essential question, revisited — the learner reads their
Lesson 02 sentence and their Lesson 04 revision aloud and explains what
changed their mind (or what confirmed it). Administer
[unit-assessment.md](assessments/unit-assessment.md) (~40 min). Collect the
project; score with the rubric within two days and return it with one
"keep doing" and one "next time" note. Score bands: 34–40 ready for U03;
26–33 review flagged objectives then proceed; below 26 re-teach before U03.

## Misconception bank (all lessons)

- Settled = farming. (Lesson 01 #3; Natufian quiz item.)
- A genius invented farming in a year. (Lesson 02; dates are "about.")
- Benefits only; costs are optional. (Every benefit/cost task.)
- Surplus = any extra, even rotted. (Lesson 03 Readiness #3.)
- Specialists were ordered by chiefs. (Çatalhöyük: no palaces found.)
- Mesoamerica copied the Fertile Crescent. (Lesson 04: crops, animals,
  timing differ.)
- "Better" region / "obviously great idea." (Readiness and value language —
  redirect to evidence.)

## Sensitive-content guidance

- **Human remains:** burials under Çatalhöyük floors are "the remains of
  real people, treated with respect." No detail, no images of remains, no
  handling framing. If the learner asks more, the honest answer is "that's
  a question for archaeologists and descendant communities."
- **Disease and hunger:** plain language, never graphic. "Bones show..."
  with no further detail at this grade.
- **The progress debate:** never let the unit become "farming = progress"
  or "farming = mistake." Both readings have evidence; the learner's job is
  weighing, not cheering.
- **Living peoples:** Mesoamerican and other Indigenous peoples in the
  present tense, always. Maize's living food culture is named, not implied.
- **No real learner data** leaves the house: the inquiry notebook stays
  private; nothing from it is published or shared.

## Reteach moves (quick reference)

| If the learner can't... | Do this |
|---|---|
| Decide camp vs. move from evidence | Re-run Willow Bend/Dry Ridge with a new place; require all four checks aloud |
| Fill the cost column | Require 3 costs before any verdict, every time, for a week |
| Explain the surplus chain | Role-play: 10 farmers, 15 mouths, 5 freed people; assign jobs |
| Subtract BCE dates | Write both dates in the same form (BCE/BCE) first; bigger number = older |
| Argue the other side fairly | Steelman: state the opposing case until its supporters would nod |
| Keep perspectives respectful | Stop, model: "the farmer weighs ___, the forager weighs ___; both use evidence" |
