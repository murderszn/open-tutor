# Unit 02 — Early Humans: Agriculture and Settlements

**Grade:** 6 · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track issue:** [#33](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/33)
**Status:** validated draft — delivered 2026-10-19; not yet educator-reviewed or merged.
**Weeks:** 7–10 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 40 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

For most of human history, people lived by **foraging** — hunting animals
and gathering wild plants — and moved with the seasons. Then, about ten
thousand years ago, some groups began to **farm**: planting seeds, tending
crops, and raising animals. This unit asks the track's second big question:
*did learning to grow food make life better, or just different?*

The learner first explains how **environments shaped forager life** (water,
food, shelter, climate), then weighs the **causes and trade-offs of the
agricultural revolution** with benefit/cost thinking, then traces how a
farming **surplus produced specialists and growing settlements**, and finally
**compares two early farming regions** — the Fertile Crescent and
Mesoamerica — to explain how the same big change happened independently in
different places. The unit keeps the track's honesty rule: historians
disagree about whether farming improved daily life, and the evidence supports
both sides.

The two comparison regions are the audit's **adult-selected pair**
(scope-and-sequence §8): the Fertile Crescent (wheat, barley, sheep, goats)
and Mesoamerica's Central Balsas River Valley (maize from teosinte). U07
revisits Mesoamerica through its civilizations; U03 picks up the Fertile
Crescent's river-valley civilizations — this unit covers only the
agricultural transition itself.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (mapped to the track
objectives in parentheses):

1. **Explain** how environments — water, wild food, shelter, climate —
   shaped early human foraging life and where forager groups would choose to
   camp or move. (Track objective 3; D2.Geo.4.6-8)
2. **Explain** the agricultural revolution's causes (warming climate, seed
   selection, domestication of plants and animals) and its **trade-offs**:
   what farming gained (storable surplus, larger groups) and what it cost
   (harder labor, narrower diets, new diseases). (Track objective 3;
   D2.His.1, 2, 14.6-8; D2.Eco.1–2.6-8)
3. **Trace** how a farming surplus led to specialization of labor and
   settlement growth, and **describe** two innovations (tools, irrigation)
   that raised farm productivity. (Track objectives 3, 11; D2.Eco.7,
   13.6-8; D2.Geo.8, 12.6-8)
4. **Compare** early farming in the Fertile Crescent and Mesoamerica —
   crops, animals, environments, timing — and **explain multiple causes**
   of the agricultural change in each region. (Track objectives 3, 10;
   D2.His.1, 2, 14.6-8)
5. **Infer** how a forager and a farmer would view the same change
   differently, supporting each perspective with evidence. (Track
   objective 10; D2.His.4.6-8)

## Essential question

*Was learning to farm a good trade? How did growing food change what it
meant to be human?*

## Vocabulary

**Foraging** — living by hunting wild animals and gathering wild plants. ·
**Mobility** — how much a group moves; foragers had high mobility. ·
**Sedentism** — settling in one place and not moving around. ·
**Domesticate** — to tame and breed a wild plant or animal so it is more
useful to people; the process is **domestication**. ·
**Agriculture** — raising domesticated plants or animals as a main food
source. · **Neolithic Revolution** — the emergence of agriculture during the
"new stone age," about 10,000 years ago. · **Surplus** — extra food beyond
what the growers need; it can support people doing other work. ·
**Specialization of labor** — when people focus on a few specific tasks
(pottery, weaving, building) because a surplus feeds them. ·
**Specialist** — a person who does one kind of work full-time. ·
**Irrigation** — bringing water to fields through channels or ditches. ·
**Innovation** — a new tool or method that changes how work is done. ·
**Trade-off** — giving up one good thing to get another. · **Benefit** — a
good result of a choice. · **Cost** — what a choice gives up or risks. ·
**Cause** — something that makes an event happen. · **Effect** — what
happens because of a cause. · **Change** — how something became different. ·
**Continuity** — what stayed the same. · **Perspective** — how a person or
group sees an event, shaped by their situation. · **Region** — an area with
shared environmental or cultural characteristics. · **Teosinte** — the wild
grass that people bred into maize. · Supporting terms: *environment,
constraint, domesticate, harvest, store, granary, artisan, settlement,
region, evidence, claim.*

## 16-session pacing (4 × ~40 min per week)

Each session names its activity and what "done well" looks like.

| Week | Sessions | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | 1–4 | **Environments and early human life.** S1: Lesson 01 — foraging, mobility, and environmental constraints (water, food, shelter, climate). *Success: learner explains why a forager group would camp at one fictional site and not another, citing two environmental reasons.* S2: environment→way-of-life practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set A) + map matching. *Success: 8/10 matches correct with an environmental reason each.* S3: investigation — **trace likely camp spots from environmental evidence** on the practice map (D2.Geo.4.6-8): mark water, food, shelter, and defend two camp choices. *Success: map shows all three needs marked and each camp choice defended with evidence.* S4: review + week check — foraging vs. farming preview, misconceptions. *Success: learner names two constraints that shaped forager life.* |
| 8 | 5–8 | **The agricultural revolution.** S5: Lesson 02 — causes (climate, seed selection, domestication) and trade-offs; benefit/cost charts (D2.Eco.1–2.6-8); the pre-written scenario cards. *Success: learner completes a benefit/cost chart for one scenario with ≥3 benefits and ≥3 costs.* S6: benefit/cost practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set B) + change/continuity sorting (D2.His.2.6-8). *Success: ≥80% correct; every "change" or "continuity" label defended.* S7: investigation — **the trade-off debate**: using the scenario cards, the learner argues both sides of "was farming a good trade?" and the adult records the strongest evidence each way. *Success: learner states two genuine benefits and two genuine costs with evidence.* S8: review + week check + **formative quiz** ([assessments/week-08_quiz.md](assessments/week-08_quiz.md)). *Success: quiz complete; learner explains one cause and one trade-off in their own words.* |
| 9 | 9–12 | **Surplus, specialization, and settlements.** S9: Lesson 03 — surplus → specialists → towns; tools and irrigation as innovation (D2.Eco.7.6-8); the generated village illustration and the "surplus test" activity (text-only alternative provided); Çatalhöyük as the real example. *Success: learner lists five village features that exist only because of a surplus, with a reason for each.* S10: production-chain and specialization practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set C) + the deterministic [surplus flow diagram](assets/surplus-flow.svg). *Success: learner traces one full production chain and names who is freed to specialize.* S11: investigation — **settlement-pattern mapping**: how human–environment relations extended settlement and movement (D2.Geo.8.6-8); project work session 1. *Success: learner explains one way farming let settlements grow and one new land-use pressure it created (D2.Geo.12.6-8).* S12: review + week check. *Success: learner explains the surplus→specialist→town chain without notes.* |
| 10 | 13–16 | **Comparing early farming regions.** S13: Lesson 04 — Fertile Crescent vs. Mesoamerica (crops, animals, environments, timing); the deterministic [agriculture timeline](assets/agriculture-timeline.svg); multi-cause explanation (D2.His.14.6-8). *Success: learner names two similarities and two differences between the regions with evidence, and lists three causes of the agricultural change.* S14: comparison and timeline practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set D) — elapsed-time computations re-solved and checked. *Success: timeline ordering correct; elapsed-time math shows work.* S15: **perspectives task** — a forager's and a farmer's view of the same change (D2.His.4.6-8, adult-guided); project work session 2. *Success: learner writes both perspectives with at least one evidence-based reason each.* S16: unit review + **unit assessment** ([assessments/unit-assessment.md](assessments/unit-assessment.md)); project due. *Success: assessment complete; project submitted with rubric.* |

The project ("The Settlement Council") is launched in Week 9 S9 (Lesson 03's
