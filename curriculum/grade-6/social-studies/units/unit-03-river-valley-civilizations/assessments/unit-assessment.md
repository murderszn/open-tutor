# Unit Assessment — Unit 03: River-Valley Civilizations

**Session 16 · About 40 minutes · 50 points · Adult-scored.**
Answer in full sentences. Use evidence from the lessons, the map,
and the timeline. No key appears in this file; solutions and scoring
guidance are in [answer-key.md](../answer-key.md), §Assessment.

## Part A — Map task (10 points)

Use [river-valley-map.svg](../assets/river-valley-map.svg) and your
notes.

A1. (4 points) Label the four river valleys on the blank schematic
provided by your adult (Mesopotamia, Egypt, Indus Valley, Yellow
River valley) with their river(s).
A2. (3 points) For two valleys of your choice, state one flood fact
and one water-control method each.
A3. (3 points) Explain why cities grew beside rivers rather than
far from them, using the words *surplus* and *irrigation*.

## Part B — Explanation task (15 points)

B1. (10 points) Choose Mesopotamia or Egypt. Write a sequenced,
multi-cause explanation (about 10–12 sentences) of its rise with at
least three causes in order, one labeled change, and one labeled
continuity.
B2. (5 points) Add a perspective note: whose view is most likely
missing from palace and temple sources about your civilization,
and what source kind might supply it?

## Part C — Law as evidence (15 points)

C1. (5 points) State Hammurabi's Code: who, about when, how many
laws, what material and script, and where the stele is now.
C2. (5 points) Analyze Law 196 or Law 53: what the law says (in
your own words), what it reveals about Babylonian society, and one
limit of the law as evidence.
C3. (5 points) Assess written laws as a solution to a public
problem: give one strength and one limit, each with a reason, and a
final judgment sentence.

## Part D — Perspective and comparison (10 points)

D1. (5 points) The Indus script is undeciphered. Explain what
historians therefore cannot claim from Indus writing, and name two
things city remains (streets, drains, seals, weights, the Great
Bath) can still show.
D2. (5 points) Compare Mesopotamia and Egypt in four sentences:
one shared feature of their cities, one difference, and one reason
for that difference linked to geography.

**Scoring:** 45–50 = exceeds; 35–44 = meets; 25–34 = approaching
(targeted reteach, then a short oral re-check); below 25 = re-teach
the unit's weakest objective and reassess with the adult's alternate
prompts from [teacher-guide.md](../teacher-guide.md).
