# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: River-Valley Civilizations

**Adult only.** This guide contains session notes, misconception
alerts, and reteach moves. Solutions to every student question are
in [answer-key.md](answer-key.md) — do not hand the answer key to
the learner.

## Session-by-session notes

- **S1 (Lesson 01):** Open with the readiness check; learners who
  cannot name a river benefit and a river danger should re-read the
  "bargain" paragraph before the map work. Show the riverside image
  *after* asking what a riverside city would need — the observation
  task works better as a check than as a reveal. Stress the caption:
  AI illustration, not evidence.
- **S2 (Set A + map):** Check Set A the same day. The common miss
  is A3 (Nile predictability): accept any answer naming regular
  timing and gentler flooding, contrasted with violent/unpredictable
  Mesopotamian floods.
- **S3 (canal-model investigation):** Tabletop tray only, adult
  pours or closely supervises pouring. Require a written prediction
  *before* water is poured; the learning is in prediction vs.
  observation, not in a "right" layout. Debrief costs: straight
  canals are simple but serve less area; branched canals serve more
  but need more digging and repair.
- **S4 (Week 11 review):** Rebuild the chain chorally (river →
  control → surplus → specialists → cities → writing/laws). Any
  learner who cannot order the chain needs S1–S3 re-teaching, not
  more new content.
- **S5 (Lesson 02):** The honest note about forced labor belongs
  in this session, plainly and briefly. If a learner is distressed,
  stay factual, skip graphic elaboration, and move to the jobs-sort
  task — do not debate the existence of slavery in the ancient
  world.
- **S6 (Set B):** B5 is the unit's first "missing evidence" item;
  praise answers that refuse to invent a palace. If learners invent
  Indus kings, redirect to the drains-and-streets evidence.
- **S7 (city-jobs sort):** Pre-make 12 cards: farmer, potter,
  weaver, merchant, boat crew, scribe, tax official, priest,
  soldier, ruler's official, metalworker, builder. Accept dual
  placements with reasons (scribe = records/rules and serves
  temple/palace; tax official likewise).
- **S8 (Week 12 review):** Exit prompt: "Why does a city need
  record-keepers?" Any answer naming taxes, trade, or grain stores
  with an example passes.
- **S9 (Lesson 03):** Do the clay activity only if materials and
  supervision allow; the paper-drawn alternative assesses the same
  skill. Check the subtraction in Guided practice Q5 (3300 − 1754
  = 1546) before moving on.
- **S10 (Set C + timeline):** C2 arithmetic: (a) 200 years,
  (b) 1346 years, (c) 746 years. C8 must balance; if it does not,
  the learner re-adds before proceeding.
- **S11 (Lesson 04):** Read the four laws aloud. Law 229's death
  consequence may be replaced by Law 53 for a distressed learner;
  note the substitution in your own records — the assessed skill
  (law as evidence) is unchanged. Do not add graphic detail beyond
  the lesson wording.
- **S12 (Week 13 review):** Spiral check: one river fact, one city
  fact, one writing fact, one law fact, from memory. Preview the
  Week 14 quiz format (12 short items) without revealing items.
- **S13 (Lesson 05):** Model the chain on a board before learners
  write. The asterisk-marked revision in the applied task is
  required — it is evidence of peer feedback, not decoration.
- **S14 (Set D + quiz):** Give the quiz closed-note, then score
  the same day. Below 80%, use the reteach moves below before the
  unit assessment.
- **S15 (project work):** Check each dossier part against the
  rubric as it is drafted; do not wait until S16 to discover a
  missing part. Verify tablet numbers balance and that invented
  elements are labeled "classroom model."
- **S16 (review + assessment + project due):** Score the
  assessment with the answer-key guidance. File the dossier and
  assessment privately; nothing from this unit is published.

## Misconception notes

- "Rivers always help" → Re-teach Lesson 01 contrasts: flood
  timing, drought, salinization (Lesson 05 Q9).
- "Everyone in cities stopped farming" → Most people still farmed;
  specialists were a minority fed by surplus.
- "Temples were only for prayer" → Temples/palaces stored grain,
  paid workers, organized building, kept records.
- "Writing started with stories" → It started with lists and
  accounts; stories came later on the same tool.
- "Written = fair / carved = read by all" → Rank changed
  punishments; most people could not read.
- "One cause explains a civilization" → Require three-cause chains
  with sequence labels in every explanation task.
- "A law proves what happened" → Laws state intent; outcomes need
  other evidence.

## Reteach moves

- **Map/river confusion:** Return to the schematic map; trace each
  river with a finger while naming it; re-do Set A Q1/Q6 orally.
- **Chain order errors:** Cut the chain steps onto cards and sort
  them physically before rewriting.
- **Specialization confusion:** Re-run Worked example A (grain →
  potter → scribe) with a different good (cloth) before retrying
  Set B.
- **Timeline arithmetic errors:** Use a number line marked every
  500 years; for two BCE dates, subtract directly (no year-0
  adjustment — that adjustment applies only across BCE→CE).
- **Law-analysis vagueness:** Require the three-part sentence
  frame: "This law reveals ___; it does not show ___; as a solution
  it helps by ___ but falls short because ___."
- **Perspective blindness:** Ask "Who wrote this, and who could not
  write?" about every source before accepting an explanation.

## Sensitive-content guidance

- Slavery and forced labor: named plainly (who was forced, who
  decided), no graphic detail, no glorification, no debate about
  whether it existed.
- Belief systems (ziggurats, temples, Shamash, Egyptian gods):
  described, never evaluated; learners are not asked to rank
  beliefs.
- Indus Valley: never invent Indus rulers, religion details, or a
  "fall" story; the script is undeciphered and decline causes are
  debated — teach the debate, not a verdict.
- Living peoples: Egyptian, Iraqi, Pakistani, Indian, and Chinese
  peoples are present-tense communities; ancient achievements are
  not presented as belonging to a vanished "other."

## Readiness and next steps

- **Ready for Unit 04** when the learner meets the assessment's
  "meets" band (35–50) and can state the unit chain in order.
- **Not yet ready:** Below 25, re-teach the weakest objective
  (identified from Part scores) and reassess orally with alternate
  prompts: a different river pair for Part A, the other
  civilization for Part B, the other law for Part C.
- **Carry forward:** The chain, the three-part law analysis, and
  the perspective note are reused in Units 04–08; keep the learner's
  inquiry notebook for the year.
