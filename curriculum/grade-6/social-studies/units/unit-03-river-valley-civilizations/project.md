# Project — River-Valley City Dossier

**Investigation question:** How did one river-valley civilization
turn river water into a city — and what did that city cost its
people?

## Overview

Build a five-part dossier for **one** river-valley civilization:
Mesopotamia, Egypt, the Indus Valley, or the Yellow River valley.
Your dossier is a classroom research file, not a real ancient
document: every invented element (city name, tablet numbers, case
details) must be labeled "classroom model," and every real fact
must name its evidence (lesson, map, timeline, or Resource Pack
source). The dossier is due at Session 16. Work sessions: Session 7
(jobs sort warm-up), Session 13 (rise explanation draft), Session
15 (full work session).

## Materials

- This project sheet, the five lessons, the schematic map and
  timeline ([assets/](assets/)), [practice.md](practice.md).
- Paper or a digital document, colored pencils or markers, a ruler.
- Optional: modeling clay and a blunt stylus for the writing sample
  (adult-supervised); otherwise draw the sample on paper.
- Adult-approved Resource Pack sources for any fact beyond the
  lessons.

## Steps

1. **Choose and locate (Session 7–8).** Choose your civilization.
   On the schematic map, mark its river(s) and write two sentences:
   where it is, and what its flood pattern was like.
2. **Trace the chain (Sessions 8–10).** Write the flood → control →
   plant → surplus chain for your civilization, naming its
   irrigation method(s) and one flood risk.
3. **Map the jobs (Sessions 10–12).** Build the city jobs board
   from Lesson 02's applied task (at least eight jobs, three groups,
   arrows showing flow between three jobs, one honest note about
   hard work and grain control).
4. **Read the writing and the law (Sessions 12–13).** Write a
   classroom-model tablet record (Lesson 03 format) and a law
   analysis: if you chose Mesopotamia, analyze one Code law from
   Lesson 04; for any other civilization, analyze what its writing
   (or, for the Indus, its undeciphered script and seals) reveals
   and cannot reveal.
5. **Explain the rise (Sessions 13–15).** Write the one-page rise
   explanation from Lesson 05's applied task: claim, sequenced
   chain of at least three causes, one labeled change, one labeled
   continuity, and a perspective note.
6. **Review and submit (Session 16).** Check your dossier against
   the rubric below. Label every invented element "classroom
   model." Submit all five parts together.

## Deliverables

1. **Map page:** schematic map marking with river(s) labeled,
   flood-pattern sentences, and a note stating the map is a
   classroom schematic, not to scale.
2. **Chain page:** flood → control → plant → surplus chain with
   irrigation method(s) and one flood risk.
3. **Jobs board:** at least eight jobs, three groups, flow arrows
   between three jobs, honest note on hard work and grain control.
4. **Writing and law pages:** classroom-model tablet record with
   balanced numbers, plus the law analysis (Mesopotamia) or
   writing/script analysis (other civilizations).
5. **Rise explanation:** one page with claim, three-cause chain,
   labeled change and continuity, perspective note, and one
   revision marked with an asterisk.

## Rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | 4 points | 2–3 points | 0–1 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Map and flood pattern | River(s) correctly located and labeled; flood pattern described accurately with a control method | River(s) mostly correct; flood pattern vague or control method missing | River(s) wrong or unlabeled; no flood pattern |
| Chain accuracy | Flood → control → plant → surplus in order, each step with a concrete detail and one flood risk | Chain mostly in order; one step vague or risk missing | Chain out of order or missing steps |
| Jobs and flows | ≥8 jobs, ≥3 groups, arrows show flow between 3 jobs, honest note on hard work and grain control | Jobs or groups short, or arrows/note partial | Few jobs, no groups, no arrows, no note |
| Writing and law | Tablet numbers balance (received − paid = balance); law/script analysis states what is revealed and one limit | Numbers unbalanced or analysis names only a reveal or only a limit | No tablet, or analysis missing |
| Rise explanation | Claim + ≥3 causes in sequence + labeled change + labeled continuity + perspective note + marked revision | One element missing or causes out of sequence | Several elements missing; no perspective note |

**Scoring:** 18–20 = exceeds; 14–17 = meets; 10–13 = approaching
(revise one rubric row with adult help); below 10 = re-teach the
matching lesson(s) and resubmit. The adult scores with
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md)'s project exemplar as a guide, not as
a script — a different civilization or a different defensible
judgment can still earn full marks.

## Supports and extensions

- **Supports:** The dossier may be assembled as a poster, a booklet,
  or a dictated document; drawing quality is not scored. The adult
  may scribe any part where writing is not the assessed skill.
- **Extension (optional, +0 points, adult-approved):** Add a sixth
  page comparing your civilization with a second river-valley
  civilization in four sentences (one similarity, two differences,
  one reason for a difference).
- **Safety and honesty:** No real learner names, photos, or grades
  appear in the dossier. Invented elements are labeled; the
  generated riverside picture, if reused, is captioned as an AI
  illustration, never as evidence.
