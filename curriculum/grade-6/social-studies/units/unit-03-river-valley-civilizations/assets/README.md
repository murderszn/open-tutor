- **Used in:** [Lesson 01](../lessons/lesson-01-rivers-that-made-cities.md),

- **Prompt (verbatim, condensed):** "Educational illustration for a
  grade-6 history lesson, in a clear flat picture-book style with no
  text or labels: a wide ancient Mesopotamian riverside scene at
  harvest time. In the foreground, farmers in simple ancient
  clothing carry baskets of grain along a narrow irrigation canal
  with wooden water gates; a stepped mud-brick ziggurat rises in
  the middle distance beside flat-roofed mud-brick houses and date

  sky, with a small reed boat. Calm, sunny, historically plausible,
  no modern objects, no people in distress."
- **Generation tool:** Muse built-in image generation,
  2026-10-25.
- **Learning purpose:** Observation task — learners distinguish
  observations from inferences about water control and surplus,
  and name what a picture cannot show.
- **Factual review (2026-10-25, inspected before linking):**
  stepped mud-brick ziggurat, flat-roofed houses, date palms, grain
  baskets, canal gates, and a reed boat are all plausible for an
  imagined Mesopotamian riverside scene; no text, no modern objects,
  no anachronistic machinery, no distressing content. The scene is
  **imagined**: it depicts no real city, person, or event.
- **Caption:** "An imagined Mesopotamian riverside city at harvest
  time — a modern AI illustration, not a photograph of the past
  and not historical evidence. What can you observe, what can you
  infer, and what can this picture not tell you?"
- **Alt text:** "Illustration of an imagined ancient Mesopotamian
  riverside city: three people carry baskets of grain beside an
  irrigation canal with wooden gates; behind them are mud-brick
  houses, grain piles, date palms, and a large stepped ziggurat;
  a wide river with a small reed boat curves past the town."
- **Known limitations:** Artistic reconstruction only. It must
  never be cited as evidence for any real city's layout, population,
