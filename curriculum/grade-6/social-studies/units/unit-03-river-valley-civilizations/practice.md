# Practice Sets — Unit 03

**How to use:** Sets A–D match Sessions 2, 6, 10, and 14. Work a set,
then check it with your adult the same day (solutions are in
[answer-key.md](answer-key.md), adult only). Aim for at least 80%
correct before moving on; below that, re-read the matching lesson
and retry the missed items.

## Set A — Rivers and maps (Session 2; Lesson 01)

A1. Match each civilization to its river(s): Mesopotamia, Egypt,
Indus Valley, Yellow River valley.
A2. Explain the difference between a floodplain and a levee, using
each word correctly in one sentence.
A3. Why was the Nile's flood more useful for planning than the
Tigris–Euphrates floods? Give two reasons.
A4. Name two irrigation methods or structures from Lesson 01 and
explain what each one controls.
A5. A farmer says, "Silt is just mud." Correct the farmer in two
sentences, explaining what silt is and why it mattered.
A6. Using the schematic map ([assets/river-valley-map.svg](assets/river-valley-map.svg)),
state which valley lies farthest west and which lies farthest east,
and name one sea or ocean each of two valleys reaches or nears.
A7. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "A river valley with no floods at
all would be the best place to farm." Agree or disagree, with two
reasons.
A8. Draw or describe the flood → control → plant → surplus chain
for Egypt, labeling each step with one concrete detail.

## Set B — Cities and specialists (Session 6; Lesson 02)

B1. Define *surplus* and *specialization*, then explain how the
first makes the second possible in two sentences.
B2. A city has farmers, potters, merchants, scribes, priests, and
soldiers. Explain what happens to the scribes and potters if the
harvest fails two years in a row.
B3. Name two economic jobs of temples or palaces, one from
Mesopotamia and one from Egypt.
B4. Why did Mesopotamia have many independent city-states while
Egypt tended toward one kingdom? Answer with a geography reason and
a river reason.
B5. Mohenjo-daro had grid streets and covered drains but no
identified palace or royal tomb. State two conclusions historians
can draw from the drains and streets, and one conclusion they should
*not* draw from the missing palace.
B6. Sort and defend: place "tax official" and "boat crew" in the
Session 7 groups (grows/makes, moves/sells, records/rules, serves
temple/palace). For each, give a reason and note any second group
it could fit.
B7. **Error analysis:** "In ancient cities, everyone worked in a
specialist job and nobody farmed." Correct this sentence.
B8. Explain who controlled stored grain in a temple storehouse
system, and why that control mattered to ordinary farmers.

## Set C — Writing and timelines (Session 10; Lesson 03)

C1. Place in order, earliest first: Hammurabi's Code (about
1754 BCE), cuneiform begins (about 3300 BCE), Egypt unified (about
3100 BCE), Mohenjo-daro flourishing (about 2500 BCE).
C2. Compute, showing work: (a) about 3300 BCE → about 3100 BCE;
(b) about 3100 BCE → about 1754 BCE; (c) about 2500 BCE → about
1754 BCE.
C3. Name one material cuneiform was written on and one material
hieroglyphs were written on, and the tool used for each.
C4. Explain two ways writing helped trade and one way it helped
taxation, each with a concrete example.
C5. A scribe writes a tax list. Name two groups who benefit from
the list existing and one group who might be harmed by it.
C6. **Explain-your-reasoning:** "Written records are always more
trustworthy than spoken stories." Agree in part and disagree in
part, using the scribe's point of view.
C7. The Indus script is undeciphered. List three things historians
therefore cannot learn from Indus writing, and two things city
remains (drains, seals, weights) can still show.
C8. Write a three-entry classroom-model tablet record (received,
paid out, balance) for a grain delivery of your own numbers, and
check that received − paid = balance.

## Set D — Causes, change, and continuity (Session 14; Lesson 05)

D1. Put these Mesopotamian developments in a cause chain, in order:
cities grow; canals and levees built; surplus grain stored; scribes
keep records. Then add one arrow label explaining each link.
D2. For Egypt, name one change and one continuity between a
farming village and a kingdom city, and explain each.
D3. Two causes are proposed for Egypt's organization: the Nile's
predictable flood and the pharaoh's government. Argue which
mattered first in the chain, with a reason tied to sequence.
D4. A palace inscription praises a king for a great canal. Explain
two reasons historians should not take the inscription as the whole
story of the canal.
D5. **Error analysis:** "Irrigation only ever helped ancient
farmers." Correct this with the salinization example.
D6. Explain the difference between a *cause* and a *continuity*,
using one example of each from this unit.
D7. A learner claims, "Mesopotamia and Egypt developed identically
because both had rivers." Give one similarity and two differences
that refute "identically."
D8. Write a four-sentence sequenced explanation of one
civilization's rise that includes a cause, a change, a continuity,
and a perspective note.
