# Unit 03 — Mesopotamia, Egypt, and River-Valley Civilizations

**Grade:** 6 · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track issue:** [#33](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/33)
**Status:** validated draft — delivered 2026-10-25; not yet educator-reviewed or merged.
**Weeks:** 11–14 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 40 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

Farming villages grew into cities where rivers could be managed. This unit
asks why cities grew beside rivers, what changed when people no longer all
farmed, how writing and laws helped large groups live together, and how we
know any of this when most ancient people left no writing of their own.
Mesopotamia and Egypt carry the detailed work; the Indus Valley and the
Yellow River valley appear as comparisons, so learners see river-valley
civilizations as a pattern with real differences, not one story told four
times. Ancient South and East Asian societies get their own full treatment
in Unit 04.

The unit's through-line is a chain the learner builds and tests:
**river water → controlled water → surplus food → specialist jobs →
cities → writing and laws → larger, more organized societies**.
Every lesson asks what the chain explains well and where it breaks down:
rivers also flooded violently, canals needed constant repair, surplus was
not shared equally, and the written record mostly speaks for rulers,
priests, and scribes.

## Prerequisites (re-taught here as needed)

- Reading a map with title, key/legend, compass rose, scale bar, and grid
  (Unit 01, Lesson 03); this unit reads maps at river-valley scale.
- Telling an observation apart from an inference, and classifying a source
  as an artifact, document, image, or oral account (Unit 01, Lessons 02
  and 04).
- Placing dates on a BCE timeline and computing elapsed time across
  millennia (Unit 01, Lesson 05).
- Explaining how environments shaped early human life and how farming
  produced surplus and specialization (Unit 02).
- Safety: the adult supervises every hands-on investigation; canal and
  river models use tabletop water trays only, never real rivers or
  unsupervised water.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (mapped to the track
objectives in parentheses):

1. **Locate** Mesopotamia, Egypt, the Indus Valley, and the Yellow River
   valley on a map, and **explain** how each river's flooding, silt, and
   water control shaped settlement, farming, production, and trade.
   (Track objectives 2 and 4; D2.Geo.1, 3, 5, 11.6-8)
2. **Explain** how surplus food made specialist jobs, cities, social
   order, and temple/palace organization possible, using at least two
   river-valley civilizations as evidence. (Track objective 4;
   D2.Civ.6.6-8; D2.Eco.7.6-8)
3. **Explain** writing as an innovation: what cuneiform and hieroglyphs
   recorded, who could write, and how writing changed trade, taxation,
   law, and memory. (Track objective 4; D2.Eco.7.6-8; D2.His.1.6-8)
4. **Examine** Hammurabi's Code as historical evidence — its origins,
   purposes, and impact — and **assess** whether writing laws down was a
   good way to address a public problem, noting who benefited and who
   did not. (Track objective 4; D2.Civ.3, 12.6-8)
5. **Build** a sequenced, multi-cause explanation of one civilization's
   rise, **classify** its developments as change and/or continuity, and
   **note** how a scribe's or ruler's perspective shaped what survives.
   (Track objectives 9 and 10; D2.His.2, 6, 14.6-8; D4.2.6-8)

## Essential question

*How did rivers help ordinary farming villages become cities — and what
did cities cost the people who lived in them?*

## Vocabulary

**Civilization** — a large, organized society with cities, specialist
jobs, writing or record-keeping, and shared institutions such as temples,
palaces, and laws. · **Irrigation** — moving water to fields on purpose,
through canals, ditches, dikes, and water-lifting devices. ·
**Floodplain** — flat land beside a river that floods regularly. ·
**Silt** — fine, fertile soil carried and left behind by floodwater. ·
**Levee** — a raised bank, natural or built, that holds back river water.
· **Canal** — a human-made channel that carries water to fields or
between places. · **Surplus** — more food than the growers themselves
need right away. · **Specialization** — different people doing different
jobs (farmer, potter, scribe, merchant) instead of everyone doing
everything. · **City-state** — a city and the farmland around it, ruled
as one independent unit. · **Artisan** — a skilled maker (potter,
weaver, metalworker). · **Merchant** — a person who buys and sells
goods, often over long distances. · **Scribe** — a person trained to
read and write, usually working for a temple, palace, or merchant. ·
**Cuneiform** — wedge-shaped writing pressed into clay with a reed
stylus, used in Mesopotamia. · **Hieroglyph** — a picture-sign in the
Egyptian writing system, written on stone or papyrus. ·
**Papyrus** — a writing material made from a river reed plant in Egypt.
· **Stele** (pronounced STEE-lee) — a tall, upright stone slab carved
with writing or pictures. · **Code of laws** — a written collection of
laws for a whole kingdom or state. · **Ziggurat** — a stepped temple
platform in Mesopotamian cities. · **Dynasty** — a series of rulers
from the same family. · **Salinization** — the slow build-up of salt in
irrigated soil, which over time makes farming harder. · **Change** —
something becoming different over time. · **Continuity** — something
staying the same while other things change. · Supporting terms used in
the unit: *delta, basin irrigation, shaduf, polytheism (belief in many
gods, described not evaluated), tribute, tax, record, perspective,
evidence, limitation.*

## Standards and source notes

- Reference framework: the **College, Career, and Civic Life (C3)
  Framework for Social Studies State Standards** (National Council for
  the Social Studies, 2013), grades 6–8 band, from the track audit's
  crosswalk (verified 2026-10-05 against the framework's grades 6–8
  indicator tables). **No state adoption, accreditation, or alignment
  certification is claimed.**
- This unit teaches each indicator at the **grade-6 entry-band level**:
  sources are adult-curated, inquiry scaffolds are heavy, and
  evaluation is observation-level.
- Unit indicators: D2.Geo.1, 3, 5, 11.6-8 (maps as evidence;
  similar-and-different places; environment and world trade);
  D2.Civ.3, 6, 12.6-8 (origins/purposes/impact of laws; organizations
  shaping lives; assessing laws as problem-solving);
  D2.Eco.3, 5, 7, 8.6-8 (buyers and sellers; money easing exchange;
  innovation; external benefits and costs); D2.His.1, 2, 6, 14.6-8
  (connections in broader contexts; change/continuity; perspectives
  shaping surviving sources; multiple causes and effects);
  D2.His.3.6-8 (historical significance, enrichment only);
  D4.2.6-8 (sequenced explanations with noted strengths and
  weaknesses).
- Dates in this unit are **approximate ("about")** unless a source gives
  an exact year; BCE arithmetic is taught as subtraction across the
  BCE number line with no year 0 (see Lesson 05 and the answer key).
- All investigation datasets are **fictional practice data** (the
  riverside-city observation image, the canal-model measurements, the
  law-case cards, the dossier evidence sets) or labeled museum/library
  references in the Resource Pack. The generated riverside image is an
  **AI illustration of an imagined scene — not evidence** of any real
  city. Hammurabi's Code excerpts are short, age-appropriate
  paraphrases with two brief quotations from the public-domain
  L. W. King translation (1910); the adult reads the full translation
  only from the Resource Pack links.
- Sensitive-content framing: slavery and forced labor are named
  plainly and briefly (who was forced, who decided) with no graphic
  detail and no glorification; belief systems are **described, never
  evaluated**; Egyptian and Mesopotamian peoples are presented as
  real societies, not as puzzles or curiosities; the Indus script is
  honestly labeled **undeciphered**, so no Indus "royal story" is
  invented.

## 16-session pacing (4 × ~40 min per week)

Each session names its activity and what "done well" looks like.

| Week | Sessions | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | 1–4 | **Rivers that made cities.** S1: Lesson 01 — four river valleys on maps; flood, silt, and irrigation; the generated riverside-city illustration as an observation task. *Success: learner locates all four valleys and explains, for two of them, how water control turned flood risk into farmland.* S2: river practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set A) + map work with [river-valley-map.svg](assets/river-valley-map.svg). *Success: ≥80% correct; every answer cites a map feature or a river fact.* S3: investigation — **canal-model tabletop test**: build two canal layouts (straight vs. branched) in a tray, predict and observe which waters more field area, and name one cost of each layout. *Success: model built, prediction recorded before pouring, observation table complete, one cost named per layout.* S4: review + week check — river → surplus chain, part 1. *Success: learner states the chain in order and names one way a river could hurt as well as help.* |
| 12 | 5–8 | **Cities, specialists, and belief.** S5: Lesson 02 — surplus → specialists → cities; social order; temples and palaces as organizers. *Success: learner traces one good (grain, pot, or cloth) from grower/maker to user through at least three specialist jobs.* S6: cities practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set B). *Success: ≥80% correct; the specialist-chain answer names who gains and who does extra work.* S7: investigation — **city jobs sort**: sort 12 job cards into "grows/makes," "moves/sells," "records/rules," and "serves the temple/palace," then defend two placements that could fit two groups. *Success: all cards placed with a reason; two dual-placement defenses given.* S8: review + week check. *Success: learner explains why a city needs record-keepers, using a concrete example.* |
| 13 | 9–12 | **Writing and laws as evidence.** S9: Lesson 03 — cuneiform and hieroglyphs; who wrote, what writing recorded, how it changed trade, tax, and law. *Success: learner explains two jobs writing made possible and one group writing left out.* S10: writing practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set C) + timeline work with [river-valley-timeline.svg](assets/river-valley-timeline.svg). *Success: timeline events ordered; elapsed-time computations show work.* S11: Lesson 04 — Hammurabi's Code as evidence: origins, purposes, impact; the law-case cards. *Success: learner states what one law reveals about Babylonian society and one limit of laws as evidence.* S12: review + spiral check — one river fact, one city fact, one writing fact, one law fact, from memory; quiz format previewed. *Success: learner names all four facts without notes.* |
| 14 | 13–16 | **Explaining a civilization's rise.** S13: Lesson 05 — multi-cause explanations; change and continuity; whose perspective survives. *Success: learner builds a three-cause chain for one civilization and labels one change and one continuity.* S14: cause/effect practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set D) + **formative quiz** ([assessments/week-14_quiz.md](assessments/week-14_quiz.md)). *Success: ≥80% on the quiz; every explanation answer cites evidence.* S15: **project work session** — "River-Valley City Dossier" ([project.md](project.md)): map, specialist chain, writing sample analysis, law analysis, and rise explanation. *Success: dossier draft has all five parts; each part cites its evidence set.* S16: unit review + **unit assessment** ([assessments/unit-assessment.md](assessments/unit-assessment.md)); project due. *Success: assessment complete; dossier submitted with rubric.* |

The project is launched in Week 12 S7 (the city-jobs sort doubles as the
project warm-up) and completed through S15; it is due at S16.
