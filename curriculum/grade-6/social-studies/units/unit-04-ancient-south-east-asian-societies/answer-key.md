# Answer Key — Unit 04: Ancient South and East Asian Societies

**Adult only.** Every item below was solved independently of
the student files and reconciled with them. Accept wording
that matches the reasoning, not only the exact phrasing.
Open responses accept defensible alternatives that cite
evidence; the notes say what must be present.

## Lesson 01 — Geography, monsoons, rivers

**Guided:** 1. Himalayas; Indus and Ganges are the Indian
plain's rivers (the question asks the range plus the plain's
two rivers). 2. Summer: land heats faster than ocean, warm
air rises over land, moist ocean air flows in to replace it,
bringing heavy rain. 3. Terrace the slope (cut flat steps);
cost: hard, slow building work and repair after storms.
4. Sea advantage: large loads, long distances, monsoon winds
help; overland advantage: reaches inland places ships cannot,
and can travel in seasons when seas are rough. Either
advantage each is acceptable if tied to the map.
**Independent:** 5. The Yellow River carries yellow loess silt
blown from northern uplands; silt fertilized fields (helpful)
but settled in the riverbed, raising it so floods grew more
dangerous and dikes had to keep rising (dangerous). 6.
Similarity: both watered farming plains and supported cities
and trade. Difference: the Indus flows southwest through an
arid plain to the Arabian Sea with a monsoon-fed regime, while
the Yangtze flows east through a warmer, wetter region suited
to rice — accept any difference tied to a map feature (course,
climate, crops). 7. Recommend the river/coast route: down the
Ganges to the Bay of Bengal, around the coast to the Arabian
Sea (or overland across the peninsula — accept either with a
reason); risk: monsoon storms at sea, or slow/difficult
overland transport and tolls. 8. Error: a monsoon is not a
single storm; it is a seasonal wind pattern with a wet summer
season and a dry winter season. Rewrite: "The summer monsoon
is a seasonal wind pattern that brings months of rain to
South Asia each year."
**Application:** Accept either site if it names the water
problem, the solution, one cost, and one comparative reason
for the first ten years (for example, hillside: terracing
costs labor but avoids the worst floods; floodplain: rich
silt and easy water but flood risk and drainage work).
**Exit check:** A monsoon is a seasonal wind reversal; it
mattered more to a farmer because the timing and amount of
rain decided the harvest, while a merchant could shift routes
or timing more easily (accept equivalent reasoning).

## Lesson 02 — Indus cities and later kingdoms

**Guided:** 1. Any three: grid streets (planned layout);
standard-size baked bricks (shared standards/enforcement);
covered drains (sanitation planning); Great Bath (public
works/possible ritual use); standardized weights and seals
(trade organization). Each must pair feature with an
organization inference. 2. (a) The Indus script is
undeciphered — no bilingual text exists; (b) no readable
royal records, palaces, or tombs name a ruler. 3. Proves:
Ashoka publicly promoted dhamma (respect, kindness, tolerance)
across his empire in durable writing. Does not prove: that
officials or subjects obeyed, or what daily life was like.
4. Any one: decimal place-value numbers with zero (basis of
modern numerals); Aryabhata's astronomy (year length,
eclipse explanation); Nalanda as a learning center drawing
students across Asia. The "still matters" sentence must link
the achievement to today (for example, place-value numerals
are used in every calculation students do).
**Independent:** 5. The "no king's palace" argument is weak:
planning evidence (grid, drains, standard bricks/weights)
proves organization without needing a palace, and absence of
a palace may reflect building materials, excavation gaps, or
a different form of rule — absence of evidence is not
evidence of absence. 6. Similarity: both held large areas
through officials, records, and public works (Maurya: edicts
and administration; Gupta: administration and patronage).
Difference: Maurya is known for Ashoka's edicts and dhamma
policy; Gupta for decimal mathematics, astronomy, and
Nalanda — accept other sourced differences. 7. 2600 − 221 =
**2379 years**. 8. Accept either with defense: weights are
stronger for trade specifically (standard measures only make
sense for exchange across distances); the Great Bath is
stronger for public organization/ritual life but weaker for
trade. The defense, not the choice, earns credit.
**Application (museum label):** Must contain: what the object
is, what it shows, what it cannot show, and one visitor
question. Example (seal): a carved Indus seal; shows trade
marking and craft skill; cannot show what its signs mean or
who owned it; question: "What would you need to read this
seal?" Labels inventing a king's name or a translation fail
this task.
**Exit check:** Confident: cities were planned (grid, drains,
standard bricks/weights). Cannot tell: rulers' names, laws,
stories, or the script's meaning.

## Lesson 03 — Belief systems and spread

**Guided:** 1. Karma: actions have consequences shaping
future lives; reincarnation: rebirth into a new life (accept
accurate paraphrases; evaluation language fails). 2. Four
Noble Truths in order: (1) life involves suffering;
(2) suffering has causes, including craving; (3) suffering can
end; (4) the Eightfold Path leads to its end. 3. Any real
difference in emphasis: Confucianism emphasizes right
relationships, respect, learning, and moral government;
Daoism emphasizes harmony with the Dao, simplicity, and
non-forcing. 4. Three steps: India (monks, monasteries,
stupas) → Silk Road oases / sea routes with merchants and
monks → China (about 1st century CE) with local schools and
art — diffusion with change.
**Independent:** 5. Rewrite must remove the ranking and
explain spread via carriers and routes (monks, merchants,
rulers; trade routes; translations; patronage). 6. Similarity:
both present moral example as a ruler's duty. Difference:
Ashoka's dhamma was a carved public policy across an empire;
Confucius's teaching was a philosophy of relationships and
learning later adopted for training officials — accept
equivalent sourced differences. 7. Merchants travel long
routes, meet many peoples, and carry goods that fund
monasteries and temples; they also need shared trust, which
religious communities can provide. Any two travel/meeting
reasons count. 8. Error: sharing a region does not make two
teachings the same; difference: Confucian emphasis on social
roles and duties vs. Daoist emphasis on natural harmony and
non-striving.
**Application (traveler's guide):** Must include three
observables (for example, a stupa, a monk translator, a
translated text or donated object), each tied to a belief
system and a route, with imagined details labeled imagined.
**Exit check:** One tradition described without evaluation
plus one named route (for example, Buddhism along Silk Road
oases).

## Lesson 04 — China: dynasties and bureaucracy

**Guided:** 1. Order: Shang (oracle bones, bronze), Zhou
(Mandate of Heaven), Qin (standardization, First Emperor),
Han (civil service by recommendation/testing, Silk Roads,
paper tradition). 2. Mandate of Heaven: Heaven grants rule to
a just ruler and withdraws it from an unjust one; a later
ruler could claim a predecessor lost the Mandate, justifying
rebellion or a new dynasty. 3. Any three: script, currency,
weights, measures, axle widths, laws, road widths. Each needs
a trade/government reason (for example, one script lets
orders be read everywhere; one currency removes exchange
confusion). 4. Farmer: burden of grain/labor, worry in bad
years; official: funds granaries, roads, defense, famine
reserves; merchant: cost, but standards and roads make wider
trade possible. Each sentence must stay in that person's
terms.
**Independent:** 5. (a) Continuity — writing used by rulers
from oracle bones onward. (b) Change — one standard script
only from Qin. (c) Continuity — farmers paid grain tax/labor
across dynasties. (d) Change — Han selection by
recommendation/testing (an early civil-service form).
6. 1600 − 221 = **1379 years**. 7. "All bad" is too simple:
Qin standards (script, currency, measures), roads, canals,
and centralized administration outlived the dynasty and
were kept by the Han — a bad regime can leave useful
institutions, and harshness (forced labor, book burning)
explains the fall better than "bad ideas." 8. Accept either
with defense; the bureaucracy answer must name an
official-only job (tax records, canal maintenance, census,
standard enforcement). Army-only answers that ignore
administration fail.
**Application (Han report):** Three paragraphs in order
(farmer, records, recommendation answering both concerns).
Label as classroom reconstruction.
**Exit check:** One change and one continuity (for example,
change: one standard script; continuity: grain tax and
irrigation as state work) plus: farmers' daily perspective is
hardest to recover, because records were written by/for
government.

## Lesson 05 — Comparing two societies

**Guided:** 1. Mauryan India, questions 1–2: monsoon-fed
rivers and plains; answers: irrigation, granaries, roads,
edicts, officials, armies (accept sourced answers). 2.
Similarity (water): both managed floods/drainage for dense
settlement. Difference (cohesion): Indus — shared planning
standards and trade, no readable royal voice; Han —
bureaucracy, one script, armies, Confucian official ideals.
3. Buddhism carriers: monks and merchants (plus royal
patrons); condition: Silk Road/sea routes, patronage, or a
felt need (a path out of suffering). 4. Accept any ranking
with a reason; the strongest defensible ranking puts
appointed officials first (only officials could enforce one
script and one measure empire-wide), then the army (conquest
made enforcement possible), then traders (beneficiaries, not
enforcers) — other rankings pass if the reason addresses
enforcement vs. benefit.
**Independent:** 5. Similarity: both used officials, writing,
and trade to hold large areas; difference: Gupta cohesion
leaned on learning/patronage and religious pluralism, Han on
a tested bureaucracy and Confucian official ideals (accept
other sourced pairs). 6. 268 + 320 − 1 = **587 years**.
7. Longevity argument fails: length of survival does not
measure achievement; Indus planning (grid, drains, standard
weights) matched or exceeded later cities in sanitation and
trade organization, and "advanced" needs a defined measure.
8. Accept either with two concrete effects: Himalayas —
monsoon rain, river sources, barrier to invasion/movement;
Yellow River — loess fertility, devastating floods, state
flood-control as a legitimacy test.
**Application:** Five paragraphs (one per question) plus a
synthesis paragraph with one change per society, strongest
cause each, and a perspective sentence. Missing the
synthesis paragraph caps the draft at partial credit.
**Exit check:** One fair comparison with evidence (for
example, "Both Indus cities and Han China standardized
measures to support trade — Indus weights and seals; Qin/Han
coins and measures").

## Practice sets

**Set A:** A1. Himalayas; Arabian Sea. A2. Summer wind blows
from sea to land (moist ocean air replaces rising warm land
air), bringing heavy rain farmers depend on. A3. Benefit:
holds water and soil on slopes, makes hillside farming
possible; cost: labor to build and constant repair. A4.
Route: Ganges east to the Bay of Bengal, then coastwise to
the Arabian Sea (or overland across the peninsula); risk:
monsoon storms, piracy/coastal hazards, or slow overland
transport and tolls. A5. Loess silt colors the river;
silt fertilized fields but raised the riverbed, making floods
more dangerous. A6. Correction: mountains do not warm a
region by trapping heat; the Himalayas block cold northern
winds, force moist air upward to drop rain, and feed rivers
with meltwater.
**Set B:** B1. Any four from Lesson 02 key (grid, standard
bricks, covered drains, Great Bath, seals/weights) each with
an organization inference. B2. The script is undeciphered
because no bilingual text exists; a bilingual text could let
scholars match signs to a known language and read Indus
writing. B3. Proves: Ashoka's public dhamma policy;
does not prove: obedience or daily life. B4. Any Gupta
achievement with a present-day link (decimal numerals, zero,
astronomy, Nalanda). B5. (a) 2600 − 221 = **2379**;
(b) 563 − 221 = **342**. B6. (i) proves trade (shared
measures exist for exchange); (ii) proves planning (grid
needs layout decisions); (iii) cannot prove yet (unread
signs); (iv) proves trade (Indus objects found abroad mean

**Set C:** C1. Shang, Zhou, Qin, Han with one idea each

Lesson 04 key). C5. 1600 − 221 = **1379**. C6. See Lesson 04

**Set D:** D1. Similarity: both faced flood/drainage problems
for dense settlement; difference: Indus answered with city

granaries, and officials. D2. Accept any ranking with a
reason; defensible top: monks traveling routes (without
carriers, patronage and donations have nothing to carry).
D3. See Lesson 05 key, item 7. D4. 268 + 320 − 1 = **587**.
D5. See Lesson 05 key, item 8. D6. Synthesis must name one
change per society, the strongest cause in each, and the
favored perspective — otherwise partial credit.

## Formative quiz (week-18)

1. Summer monsoon; blows from sea to land. 2. Indian plain:
Indus, Ganges. China: Yellow, Yangtze. 3. A terrace is a flat
step cut into a hillside; it holds water and soil so crops
can grow and slopes do not wash away. 4. Any two: grid

## Unit assessment (40 points)

**Task 1 (10):** (a) 1 point per correct label: Himalayas,
Indus, Ganges, Yellow, Yangtze. (b) Arrow sea → land in
summer, 1 point; rain explanation (moist ocean air rises over
heated land), 1 point. (c) 1 point per route marked, 1 point
for the pair of explanations (goods/people/ideas named for
each); award the explanation point only if both routes have
a sensible cargo.
**Task 2 (10):** Card 1 proves (5): planned, standardized city
organization — grid, uniform bricks, drains. Cannot prove
(5): rulers, laws, stories, or the seal's meaning, because
the script is undeciphered. Card 2 proves (5): Ashoka's
public dhamma policy, its language/script, and empire-wide
display. Cannot prove (5): obedience, daily life, or dissent,
because a ruler's order is not a behavior report.
**Task 3 (10):** (a) Four Noble Truths, 1 point each, in own
words, without evaluation. (b) Difference in emphasis, 3
points (Confucian roles/learning vs. Daoist natural harmony);
ranking language loses 1 point. (c) 1 point per step
(start, route, arrival-with-change); the carrier and route
must be named for full marks.
**Task 4 (10):** (a) Same question applied to both societies
with evidence, 4 points. (b) One change, two causes, ranked
with a defended top choice, 4 points (unranked lists cap at
2). (c) Favored perspective with a reason (for example,
officials' records over farmers' experience), 2 points.

## Project rubric exemplar

An exemplary exhibit (18–20 points) shows: a schematic map
with both societies, rivers, routes, and shaping features;
three evidence items per society, each with a proves /
cannot-prove pair, including at least one honest limit (for
example, the undeciphered script); a belief panel describing
two systems (or one traveling system) without ranking and
with a traced route; a change explanation with two ranked
causes per society and a perspective sentence per society;
and a complete source list with imagined details labeled.
Common deductions: missing "cannot prove" sentences (−2),
ranked beliefs (−2), unranked causes (−2), unsourced facts
(−1 to −2), map without "schematic — not to scale" (−1).
