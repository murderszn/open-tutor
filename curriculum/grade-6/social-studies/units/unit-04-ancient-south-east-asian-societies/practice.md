# Practice — Unit 04: Ancient South and East Asian Societies

Work in your inquiry notebook. Show your reasoning for every
explanation answer; a bare answer without a reason earns
partial credit only. All dates are approximate ("about").
Fictional practice statements are labeled as such — they are
not ancient quotations.

## Set A — Places, monsoons, and routes (Week 15, S2)

Use [asia-map.svg](assets/asia-map.svg) and Lesson 01.

A1. Name the mountain range that runs across the north of the
Indian subcontinent, and the sea into which the Indus flows.
A2. Explain the summer monsoon in your own words (wind
direction, source of moisture, result for farmers).
A3. A hillside village terraces its slope. Name one benefit
and one cost of terracing.
A4. A merchant in the Ganges plain wants to send cotton cloth
to a port on the Arabian Sea. Using the map, describe the
route in two sentences (rivers, coast, or overland) and name
one risk on that route.
A5. Why is the Yellow River yellow, and why was its silt both
helpful and dangerous? (Two sentences.)
A6. **Error analysis:** "The Himalayas made South Asia warm,
because mountains trap heat." Correct the error and explain
what the Himalayas actually do to winds and rivers.

## Set B — South Asia: cities, kingdoms, evidence (Week 16, S6)

Use Lessons 02–03 and [asia-timeline.svg](assets/asia-timeline.svg).

B1. List four features of Indus city planning, and state what
each one suggests about organization.
B2. Why can't historians read Indus seals? What would change
if a bilingual Indus text were found?
B3. State one thing Ashoka's edicts prove and one thing they
do not prove.
B4. Name one Gupta-era achievement in mathematics or learning
and explain why it still matters today (two sentences).
B5. Compute, showing your work: (a) about 2600 BCE → about
221 BCE; (b) about 563 BCE → about 221 BCE.
B6. Sort these into "proves planning," "proves trade," or
"cannot prove yet": (i) standardized Indus weights; (ii) a
grid street plan; (iii) an unread Indus seal inscription;
(iv) Indus seals found in Mesopotamia. Give a one-sentence
reason for each placement.

## Set C — China: dynasties, standardization, perspectives (Week 17, S10)

Use Lesson 04 and [asia-timeline.svg](assets/asia-timeline.svg).

C1. Put Shang, Zhou, Qin, Han in order and give one idea or
achievement for each.
C2. Explain the Mandate of Heaven and how it could justify
replacing a dynasty (two sentences).
C3. Name three things the Qin standardized and explain why
each one helped trade or government (one sentence each).
C4. A Han grain tax is announced. Write one sentence each for
how a farmer, an official, and a merchant might see it —
each in that person's own terms, without saying who is right.
C5. Compute, showing your work: about 1600 BCE → about
221 BCE.
C6. **Explain your reasoning:** Which held the Han empire
together more — the army or the bureaucracy? Defend your
answer in three sentences, naming one job officials did that
soldiers could not.

## Set D — Comparisons, causes, and synthesis (Week 18, S14)

Use Lesson 05.

D1. Compare Indus cities and Han China on water: one
similarity in the problem they faced and one difference in
their solution.
D2. Rank these causes of Buddhism's spread from strongest to
weakest, with one reason for your top choice: (a) monks
traveling trade routes; (b) royal support such as Ashoka's;
(c) merchants donating to monasteries.
D3. A classmate says, "Han China was more advanced than Indus
cities because it lasted longer." Answer in three sentences,
using one Indus achievement.
D4. Compute, showing your work: about 268 BCE → about 320 CE.
(Remember: subtract 1 across the BCE/CE boundary.)
D5. Which shaped its society more — the Himalayas for South
Asia or the Yellow River for China? Defend your choice with
two concrete effects.
D6. **Synthesis:** One change happened in both of your two
chosen project societies. Name the change, give the strongest
cause in each society, and state whose perspective the
surviving evidence favors (two to three sentences per society).
