# Unit 04 — Ancient South and East Asian Societies

**Grade:** 6 · **Subject:** Social Studies · **Track issue:** [#33](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/33)
**Status:** validated draft — delivered 2026-11-01; not yet educator-reviewed or merged.
**Weeks:** 15–18 of the 36-week track (see [scope-and-sequence](../../scope-and-sequence.md)).
**Session model:** 4 sessions per week, about 40 minutes each (16 sessions total).

## What this unit is about

South and East Asia are taught here in their own terms — not as
footnotes to the Mediterranean, and not as one story told twice.
The unit asks how very different environments produced very
different societies, what held large societies together without
modern communication, and how ideas traveled along the same
routes as goods. The Indus Valley, the Ganges plain, and the
Yellow and Yangtze valleys carry the detailed work. Two
through-lines run through every lesson:

1. **Environment shapes, but does not decide.** Monsoon rains,
   Himalayan meltwater, loess silt, mountains, and coastlines set
   the problems each society had to solve — floods to control,
   hills to terrace, distances to cross. People answered with
   different tools: planned drains, terraced fields, canals,
   standardized writing, and bureaucracies.
2. **Societies are compared, not ranked.** Each lesson asks what
   a society did well, what it cost, and who paid that cost.
   Belief systems are described, never evaluated. Present-day
   connections are included only for living traditions, and only
   descriptively.

## Prerequisites (re-taught here as needed)

- Reading a map with title, key/legend, compass rose, scale bar,
  and grid (Unit 01, Lesson 03); this unit reads maps at
  continental scale, where a schematic map shows direction and
  relative position, not exact distances.
- Telling an observation apart from an inference, and classifying
  a source as an artifact, document, image, or oral account
  (Unit 01, Lessons 02 and 04).
- Placing dates on a BCE timeline and computing elapsed time
  across millennia, including across the BCE/CE boundary, which
  has no year 0 (Unit 01, Lesson 05; Unit 03, Lesson 05).
- Explaining how environments shaped early human life and how
  farming produced surplus and specialization (Unit 02), and how
  river-valley cities organized writing, laws, and belief
  (Unit 03).
- Safety: the adult supervises every hands-on investigation;
  monsoon and irrigation models use tabletop water trays only,
  never real rivers or unsupervised water.

## Objectives (measurable, adult-assessed)

By the end of the unit the learner will be able to (mapped to the
track objectives in parentheses):

1. **Locate** South and East Asia's key features — the Himalayas,
   the Indus, Ganges, Yellow, and Yangtze rivers, the monsoon
   wind pattern, and the major sea and overland routes — and
   **explain** how each feature shaped farming, settlement, and
   trade in at least two regions. (Track objectives 2 and 5;
   D2.Geo.5, 10.6-8)
2. **Explain** how Indus cities were planned and organized, and
   how later South Asian kingdoms (Maurya, Gupta) governed
   large, diverse populations, using city plans, edicts, and
   trade evidence — and **note** what the evidence cannot show,
   including the undeciphered Indus script. (Track objectives 5
   and 9; D2.His.1, 2.6-8)
3. **Describe** Hindu traditions, Buddhism, Confucianism, and
   Daoism in their own terms (beliefs, practices, and ideas
   about right conduct — described, never evaluated) and
   **trace** how at least one idea spread along trade or
   official routes. (Track objective 5; D2.Geo.7.6-8;
   D2.His.1.6-8)
4. **Explain** dynastic change and continuity in early China —
   Shang, Zhou, Qin, and Han — including writing, bronze,
   irrigation, bureaucracy, and standardization, and **compare**
   how a farmer, an official, and a merchant might have seen
   the same tax. (Track objectives 5 and 10; D2.His.2, 4.6-8)
5. **Compare** two ancient Asian societies, **explain** how
   physical and human characteristics connected to their
   cultures, and **build** a sequenced, multi-cause explanation
   of one change in each society, noting whose perspective the
   surviving evidence favors. (Track objectives 5, 10, and 12;
   D2.Geo.6.6-8; D2.His.14, 15.6-8; D4.2.6-8)

## Essential question

*How did people in very different Asian environments build large
societies that lasted — and what did living in those societies
cost different kinds of people?*

## Vocabulary

**Monsoon** — a seasonal wind pattern: wet summer winds blow in
from the ocean, dry winter winds blow out from the land. ·
**Terrace** — a flat step cut into a hillside so a slope can hold
water and crops. · **Loess** — fine, wind-blown yellowish soil;
the Yellow River carries it, which is why the river looks
yellow and why its bed rises as silt settles. ·
**Planned city** — a city laid out on a pattern (such as a grid)
before or as it is built, rather than growing street by street.
· **Drain** — a covered channel that carries wastewater away
from houses and streets. · **Seal** — a small carved stone
pressed into clay to mark ownership or approve goods. ·
**Standardized** — made to one shared measure, weight, coin, or
writing form so people across a large area can trade and
communicate. · **Dynasty** — a series of rulers from the same
family. · **Mandate of Heaven** — the Zhou idea that Heaven
grants the right to rule to a just ruler and withdraws it from
an unjust one, which could justify replacing a dynasty. ·
**Bureaucracy** — government run by appointed officials who
carry out set duties (collecting taxes, keeping records,
maintaining canals and roads) rather than by the ruler alone. ·
**Edict** — a public order issued by a ruler, in Ashoka's case
carved on rocks and pillars. · **Dhamma** — Ashoka's public
word for right conduct: respect for parents and elders,
kindness to servants and animals, and respect between religious
groups (described, not evaluated). · **Stupa** — a mound-shaped
Buddhist shrine, often holding relics. · **Dharma** — in Hindu
traditions, duty and right conduct according to one's place in
life; in Buddhism, the Buddha's teaching. · **Karma** — the idea
that actions have consequences that shape future lives. ·
**Reincarnation** — rebirth into a new life. · **Varna** — the
four broad social classes described in early Indian texts
(priests, warriors/rulers, merchants/farmers, laborers);
historians debate how closely daily life matched the texts,
and later **jati** (birth-groups) organized much of social
life. · **Confucianism** — the teachings associated with
Confucius about right relationships, respect, learning, and
good government. · **Daoism** — teachings associated with
Laozi about living in harmony with the Dao ("the Way") and
with nature. · **Legalism** — the Qin approach that strict,
clearly written laws and harsh punishments keep order. ·
**Silk Roads** — the overland and sea trade networks linking
China, Central Asia, South Asia, and the Mediterranean; goods
and ideas traveled both directions. · **Diffusion** — the
spread of a good, practice, or idea from one place to another.
· **Change** — something becoming different over time. ·
**Continuity** — something staying the same while other things
change. · Supporting terms: *citadel, granary, oracle bone,
bronze casting, civil service, tribute, caravan, pilgrimage,
sect, perspective, evidence, limitation.*

## Standards and source notes

- Reference framework: the **College, Career, and Civic Life (C3)
  Framework for Social Studies State Standards** (National Council
  for the Social Studies, 2013), grades 6–8 band, from the track
  audit's crosswalk (verified 2026-10-05 against the framework's
  grades 6–8 indicator tables; unit indicators re-confirmed
  against the track scope-and-sequence 2026-11-01). **No state
  adoption, accreditation, or alignment certification is
  claimed.**
- This unit teaches each indicator at the **grade-6 entry-band
  level**: sources are adult-curated, inquiry scaffolds are
  heavy, and evaluation is observation-level.
- Unit indicators: D2.Geo.5, 6, 7, 10.6-8 (similar-and-different
  places; physical/human characteristics and cultures;
  diffusion; how characteristics vary among regions);
  D2.His.1, 2, 4, 14, 15.6-8 (connections in broader contexts;
  change and continuity; perspectives; multiple causes and
  effects; evaluating the relative influence of causes);
  D2.His.9.6-8 (classifying kinds of sources, in the Indus
  evidence task); D4.2.6-8 (sequenced explanations with noted
  strengths and weaknesses).
- Dates in this unit are **approximate ("about")** unless a
  source gives an exact year. BCE arithmetic within one era is
  subtraction; across the BCE/CE boundary, subtract 1 because
  there is no year 0 (see Lesson 05 and the answer key).
- All investigation datasets are **fictional practice data**
  (the monsoon observation image, the trade-route cards, the
  tax-perspective statements, the comparison evidence sets) or
  labeled museum/library references in the Resource Pack. The
  generated monsoon image is an **AI illustration of an
  imagined scene — not evidence** of any real place. Ashoka's
  edicts appear only as short, age-appropriate paraphrases;
  no ancient quotation is invented.
- Sensitive-content framing: social hierarchy, forced labor on
  Qin building projects, and warfare are named plainly and
  briefly (who was forced, who decided) with no graphic detail
  and no glorification; belief systems are **described, never
  evaluated**; South and East Asian peoples are presented as
  real societies with living traditions, not as puzzles or
  curiosities; the Indus script is honestly labeled
  **undeciphered**, so no Indus "royal story" is invented;
  where historians disagree (Indus decline, Laozi's dates,
  the size of ancient populations), the lessons say so.

## 16-session pacing (4 × ~40 min per week)

Each session names its activity and what "done well" looks like.

| Week | Sessions | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | 1–4 | **South and East Asia on the map.** S1: Lesson 01 — monsoons, rivers, mountains, coastlines; the generated monsoon-valley illustration as an observation task. *Success: learner locates the Himalayas, four rivers, and two routes, and explains the monsoon in one sentence.* S2: place practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set A) + map work with [asia-map.svg](assets/asia-map.svg). *Success: ≥80% correct; every answer cites a map feature.* S3: investigation — **monsoon tray model**: predict and observe which of two slopes (terraced vs. straight) keeps more soil when "rain" is poured, and name one cost of each design. *Success: prediction recorded before pouring, observation table complete, one cost named per design.* S4: review + week check — monsoon, rivers, barriers, routes. *Success: learner states the monsoon pattern and names one barrier and one route.* |
| 16 | 5–8 | **South Asia: cities, kingdoms, beliefs.** S5: Lesson 02 — Indus planned cities, specialization, and hierarchy; Maurya and Gupta government. *Success: learner traces one good from maker to user through at least three jobs and names one thing the Indus evidence cannot tell us.* S6: South Asia practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set B) + timeline work with [asia-timeline.svg](assets/asia-timeline.svg). *Success: timeline events ordered; elapsed-time computations show work.* S7: Lesson 03 — belief systems described in their own terms; trace one idea's spread. *Success: learner describes two belief systems without ranking them and traces one idea along a route.* S8: review + week check. *Success: learner explains one way a belief system traveled and one limit of that evidence.* |
| 17 | 9–12 | **China: dynasties, bureaucracy, innovation.** S9: Lesson 04 — Shang, Zhou, Qin, Han; change and continuity; writing, bronze, irrigation, standardization. *Success: learner labels one change and one continuity across two dynasties.* S10: China practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set C) + perspectives task — farmer, official, merchant on the same tax (adult-guided). *Success: all three perspectives stated in their own terms; the learner names whose view the tax records favor.* S11: investigation — **standardization test**: weigh and measure with two "local" sets vs. one shared set (fictional practice data) and decide which system a merchant would prefer, with a reason. *Success: table complete; preference justified from the data, not from opinion alone.* S12: review + spiral check — one geography fact, one South Asia fact, one belief fact, one China fact, from memory; quiz format previewed. *Success: learner names all four facts without notes.* |
| 18 | 13–16 | **Comparing two societies.** S13: Lesson 05 — modeled comparison; physical/human connections; multi-cause synthesis. *Success: learner builds a two-society comparison and labels one cause as stronger than another, with a reason.* S14: comparison practice set ([practice.md](practice.md), Set D) + **formative quiz** ([assessments/week-18_quiz.md](assessments/week-18_quiz.md)). *Success: ≥80% on the quiz; every explanation answer cites evidence.* S15: **project work session** — "Two Societies, One Change" comparison exhibit ([project.md](project.md)): map, evidence panels, belief panel, and change explanation. *Success: exhibit draft has all four parts; each part cites its evidence set.* S16: unit review + **unit assessment** ([assessments/unit-assessment.md](assessments/unit-assessment.md)); project due. *Success: assessment complete; exhibit submitted with rubric.* |

The project is launched in Week 16 S7 (the belief-spread trace
doubles as the project warm-up) and completed through S15; it is
due at S16.
