# Starter Lesson — Ask what a map and source can show

**Status:** draft supplement. **Time:** three 35-minute sessions. **Goal:** Analyze a fictional map and source note, distinguish observation from inference, and identify evidence needed to evaluate a civic proposal.

## Adult preparation and readiness

Use the map and note below. No internet, local address, or political disclosure is required. Readiness: learner can distinguish something directly shown from a guess about what it means.

```text
Fictional town sketch — not to scale
N ↑
┌────────────────────┐
│ School     Library  │
│     Main Road       │
│ Clinic     Market   │
└────────────────────┘
```

Original fictional source note: “The town council received two requests to improve the path near the clinic. The note gives no information about who made the requests, how often the path is used, or what an improvement would cost.”

## Teach and model

Explain: a source has a creator, purpose, and limits. A map can show relative location, but this sketch does not show scale, path conditions, or travel time. An inference must be tied to an actual clue.

**Worked example 1:** Observation: “The clinic is southwest of the library in the sketch.” Inference: “The clinic may be busy.” The sketch gives no evidence about how many people visit.

**Worked example 2:** The note directly states there were two requests. It does not establish that only two people care, or that the proposed improvement will work.

## Pacing and tasks

Session 1: adult models source/map limits and guides tasks 1–2. Session 2: learner independently completes tasks 3–4. Session 3: compare possible evidence, error-check, and present a next-step recommendation.

1. **Guided:** Is the creator of the source note named? Identify one detail the note states.
2. **Guided:** Name two details the sketch does not provide.
3. **Independent:** Label “two requests were received” as observation or inference.
4. **Independent:** Explain why “everyone wants a path change” is unsupported.
5. **Adult-guided error check:** Learner says the map proves the clinic path is short. Identify missing map information.
6. **Application:** Suggest two pieces of evidence to gather before a decision, including whose perspective to ask.

**Answer checks:** The creator is not supplied; do not assume the town council wrote the note merely because the council is mentioned. “Two requests were received” is stated information. The clinic is southwest of the library. The map lacks scale, path surface, traffic, and distances; “everyone” goes beyond the source; useful evidence includes path-use count, safety/access audit, cost estimate, and voluntary perspectives from different users.

## Exit check and product

Learner gives one observation, one limit, and a next inquiry step. Product: source-analysis table or one-page recommendation with two evidence needs.

## Review rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Observation/inference | Separates direct information from a conclusion | Labels with support | Model and relabel one statement |
| Source limits | Identifies missing context | Names one limit | Prompt with “what don't we know?” |
| Inquiry | Gathers evidence from more than one relevant perspective | Suggests one useful check | Use who/what/how question stems |

Adult may read aloud, enlarge the map, provide a tactile layout, or accept dictated response. Case is fictional and not a request for real-world contact. Avoid treating a sketch as scaled evidence or assuming a group speaks with one voice. Keep learner work private; no outside links are necessary.
