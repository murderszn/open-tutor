# Starter Lesson — Represent ratios and find a unit rate

**Status:** draft supplement. **Time:** three 35-minute sessions. **Goal:** Describe a ratio, generate equivalent ratios, and calculate a unit rate with units.

## Adult preparation and readiness

Use paper, colored counters (optional), and a basic calculator only after estimating. Readiness: learner can multiply/divide whole numbers and read a two-column table. If needed, build equal groups first.

## Teach and model

Explain: a ratio compares quantities in a particular order. Equivalent ratios scale both quantities by the same factor. A unit rate tells the amount for one unit of the second quantity.

**Worked example 1:** A trail mix recipe uses 2 cups oats per 3 cups cereal. For 2 batches, scale both by 2: 4 cups oats per 6 cups cereal. Ratio remains 2:3.

**Worked example 2:** A cyclist travels 42 km in 3 h. Divide both quantities by 3: 14 km per 1 h. Units say kilometers per hour. At a steady rate, 5 h gives 5×14=70 km.

## Pacing and tasks

Session 1: model example 1 and guide tasks 1–2. Session 2: teach unit rate; learner works tasks 3–4, adult checks units. Session 3: learner completes tasks 5–6 and justifies an answer.

1. **Guided:** In ratio 3 red beads to 5 blue, name the order and quantities.
2. **Guided:** Make an equivalent ratio for 2:3 using factor 4.
3. **Independent:** A car travels 150 miles in 3 hours. Find miles per hour.
4. **Independent:** At that steady rate, how far in 5 hours?
5. **Adult-guided error check:** A learner changes 2:3 to 4:5 by adding 2 to each part. Explain why this is not equivalent.
6. **Application:** 4 notebooks cost $12 at the same price each. Find price per notebook and cost for 7 notebooks.

**Answer checks:** 1) 3 red for every 5 blue; order matters; 2) 8:12; 3) 50 miles/hour; 4) 250 miles; 5) scale both by the same multiplier (2:3→4:6), not add the same amount; 6) $3/notebook, $21 for 7.

## Exit check and product

Prompt: “A 6-cup batch uses 4 cups water. How much water per cup of batch?” Expected rate 4/6 = 2/3 cup water per cup of batch. Product: ratio table, unit-rate work with units, and one sentence interpreting the rate.

## Review rubric

| Criterion | 2 — shows skill | 1 — developing | 0 — not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ratio | States quantities and order | Correct after prompt | Model with labeled objects |
| Equivalent ratio | Scales both quantities consistently | One factor error corrected | Use counters/table |
| Unit rate | Divides to one unit and labels units | Correct number, missing units | Revisit “per one” meaning |

Use ratio strips, pre-labeled table headings, calculator for arithmetic after setup, and oral explanation. Learner can dictate or use a spreadsheet offline only if available. Avoid current real-world prices unless adult verifies them. Common error: mixing units or ratio order. This standalone lesson does not cover all Grade 6 number and ratio objectives.
