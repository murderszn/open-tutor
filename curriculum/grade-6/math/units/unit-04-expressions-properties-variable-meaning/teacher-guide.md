# Teacher Guide — Unit 04: Expressions, Properties, and Variable Meaning

**Adult-facing. Do not hand this guide to the learner before they
have attempted the work; the answer key is a separate file.**

## How this unit fits

Unit 04 is the bridge from arithmetic to algebra. It deliberately
stops short of solving equations: the learner who can write, read,
evaluate, and reshape expressions will find Unit 05's "which values
make this true?" natural, while a learner rushed into solving will
memorize steps. If the learner struggles here, slow down — do not
borrow Unit 05 content to "move ahead."

## Lesson-by-lesson guidance

### Lesson 01 — Exponents

- **Core move:** expanded form before evaluation, every time, until
  automatic. Let the learner write 3⁴ = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 on the page;
  do not accept a bare answer at first.
- **Misconceptions:** (1) exponent as multiplier (3⁴ = 12);
  (2) miscounting factors (3⁴ computed as 3³); (3) "bigger exponent
  always wins" (2³ vs 3² disproves it — use it).
- **Reteach:** if errors persist, restrict to squares and cubes for a
  day, with area/volume models (a 3-by-3 square has 9 unit squares;
  a 3-by-3-by-3 cube has 27 unit cubes).

### Lesson 02 — Variables and parts

- **Core move:** words → symbols, then symbols → words, both
  directions. Every new expression gets a sentence: "3n + 4 means
  three bags of n apples plus four loose apples."
- **Misconceptions:** (1) "subtract y from 5" → y − 5; (2) 3n read as
  3 + n; (3) (8 + 7) treated as two factors in 2(8 + 7) instead of
  one entity. For (3), cover (8 + 7) with a hand and ask: "how many
  things is 2 multiplying?" — one.
- **The illustration:** the bags picture is a story prompt, not data.
  Never ask the learner to count anything in the picture. All counts
  come from the lesson words.

### Lesson 03 — Evaluating and order of operations

- **Core move:** two-line evaluations — substituted form, then
  step-by-step computation. The substituted line is where most errors
  are caught.
- **Misconceptions:** (1) strict left-to-right computation ignoring
  order; (2) 3x evaluated as 3 + x; (3) fraction bases cubed without
  parentheses; (4) "PEMDAS means multiplication before division" —
  correct this explicitly: ×/÷ and +/− work left to right as pairs.
- **Reteach:** if substitution itself is shaky, use value tables
  (x | 3x + 2) for x = 1, 2, 3, 4 before returning to single-value
  evaluation.

### Lesson 04 — Distributive property

- **Core move:** the area model first, symbols second. Draw the split
  rectangle for 3(2 + x) before writing 6 + 3x.
- **Misconceptions:** (1) distributing to the first term only;
  (2) dropping a variable when factoring (24x + 18y = 6(4x + 3));
  (3) treating factoring as "dividing everything" without checking
  by expanding back. Rule: every expansion or factorization gets an
  expansion-back check.
- **GCF link:** factoring quality depends on Unit 02 GCF fluency.
  If the learner factors out 2 when 6 is available, praise the valid
  factorization, then ask for the greatest one.

### Lesson 05 — Equivalence

- **Core move:** the asymmetry — one counterexample disproves;
  transformation proves. Teach the x + 3 / 3x pair explicitly: they
  match at x = 3 and still are not equivalent.
- **Misconceptions:** (1) one matching value proves equivalence;
  (2) equivalent means identical-looking (6 + 2x vs 2x + 6 are
  equivalent by commutativity); (3) "equivalent" confused with "equal
  at the value I care about."
- **Language:** insist on "equivalent" for expressions and "equal"
  for values. "2(x + 3) is equivalent to 2x + 6; at x = 4, both equal
  14."

## Readiness, reteach, and next steps

- **Ready for Unit 05 when:** the learner evaluates expressions with
  exponents reliably, expands and factors with checks, and can
  explain the one-counterexample rule in their own words.
- **Reteach targets from the quiz (Session 08):** items 1–2 missed →
  revisit Lesson 01 expanded form; items 3–4 missed → revisit Lesson
  02 translations and part names; items 5–9 missed → revisit Lesson
  03 two-line evaluations; item 10 missed → the exponent-as-multiplier
  error needs another day, not another worksheet.
- **Reteach targets from the assessment (Session 14):** items 1–3 →
  Lessons 01–02; items 4–6 → Lessons 02–03; items 7–8 → Lessons 03–04;
  items 9–10 → Lesson 05; item 11 → the equivalence definition needs
  re-teaching with the x + 3 / 3x example, not more practice items;
  item 12 → project skills, revisit the project rubric.
- **Midyear signal (Session 16):** Practice Set E retrieval items
  show what Units 01–03 retained. A missed retrieval item is a
  Unit 05 prerequisite gap: note it, do not re-teach the whole prior
  unit. The midyear review week is diagnostic, not a second pass
  through three units.

## Accessibility and supports

- Keep the expanded form, substituted form, and transformation steps
  visible on the page; do not require mental arithmetic as a gate
  for algebra reasoning.
- Accept oral explanations of parts and equivalence, dictated
  expressions, and tile/counter models as full evidence of the
  objectives; written symbols are the goal, not the only route.
- The generated illustration has a text alternative in Lesson 02;
  the same information is fully available in words, so no learner
  depends on the picture.
