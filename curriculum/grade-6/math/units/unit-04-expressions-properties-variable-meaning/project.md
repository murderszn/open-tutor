# Project — The Market-Stall Expression Challenge

**Type:** investigation / design project (Sessions 09–11, presented
in Session 15) · **Unit objectives:** UO14–UO17

## The challenge

You run a market stall. Design a stall whose stock can be described
with **one variable expression**, then prove that a second,
different-looking expression describes the **same** stock. You will
present your stall, both expressions, and your equivalence proof to
the class (or to your adult, who plays the customer).

## Materials

Paper, pencil, the market-stall illustration from Lesson 02 (for
inspiration, not for counting), and optionally small objects (counters,
blocks) to model bags and loose items.

## Steps

### Part 1 — Write and evaluate (Session 09)

1. Choose your stall's goods (apples, oranges, marbles — anything
   countable) and your variable: a letter standing for the number of
   items in **one bag**. State it in words: "Let b stand for the
   number of apples in one bag."
2. Choose a stock: some number of full bags plus some loose items.
   Write the expression for your total stock (for example, "4 bags
   plus 6 loose" → 4b + 6).
3. Evaluate your expression for **two** different bag sizes. Show
   the substitution each time. Explain in one sentence what changed
   and what stayed the same when the bag size changed.

### Part 2 — Two equivalent expressions (Session 10)

4. Write a **second** expression for the same stock that looks
   different from the first. Use a property from Lesson 04 to build
   it (for example, factor your expression, expand a product form,
   or regroup like terms). Name the property you used.
5. Prove the two expressions are equivalent **two ways**: (a) show
   the transformation step by step, and (b) check both expressions
   at two bag sizes and show they agree.
6. Also write a **non-equivalent** expression for contrast — one
   that looks close to yours but describes different stock — and
   give a counterexample value where it differs from your expression.

### Part 3 — Peer check and revision (Session 11)

7. Trade expressions with a partner (or present them to your adult).
   Your checker tries to (a) evaluate both of your expressions at a
   bag size you did not use, (b) find any error in your
   transformation, and (c) guess which of your expressions is the
   non-equivalent decoy and why.
8. Revise based on the check. Write one sentence about what the check
   caught or confirmed.

## Deliverables

1. A stall description: goods, variable definition in words, and the
   stock (bags + loose items).
2. Expression 1 (your stock) with two evaluations shown.
3. Expression 2 (equivalent form) with the property named, the
   transformation shown, and two checks.
4. A non-equivalent expression with a counterexample value.
5. A short presentation (spoken or written): what your variable
   means, why the two expressions are equivalent, and how the
   counterexample works.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Full credit (3) | Partial credit (2) | Needs work (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Variable meaning (UO14) | Letter defined in words; expression matches the described stock exactly | Letter defined but expression slightly mismatches the stock | Letter undefined or expression does not match the stock |
| Evaluation (UO15) | Two evaluations with substitutions shown and correct values | One evaluation correct, or substitutions missing | Evaluations missing or mostly incorrect |
| Equivalent form (UO16) | Second expression built with a correctly named property; transformation shown step by step | Second expression equivalent but property unnamed or steps incomplete | Second expression not equivalent, or no transformation shown |
| Equivalence proof (UO17) | Transformation plus two matching checks; counterexample correctly distinguishes the decoy | One valid proof route (transformation or checks) and a decoy with a value | Proof missing, or "one matching value" treated as proof |
| Presentation | Explains variable, equivalence, and counterexample clearly | Explains two of the three clearly | Unclear or missing explanations |

**Scoring:** 13–15 = exceeds; 10–12 = meets; 7–9 = approaching, revise
the weakest criterion with the adult; below 7 = rework with adult
support and re-present. The project is not timed; accuracy of the
reasoning matters more than speed.

## Adult notes

- Accept any goods and any bag/loose counts, as long as the
  expression matches the description. Watch for the classic slip:
  "3 bags plus 4 loose" written as 3(b + 4) instead of 3b + 4.
- If the learner's equivalent form is just a reordering (3b + 4 vs
  4 + 3b), accept it but ask for a second, distributive-based form —
  reordering alone does not show property use.
- The decoy must actually differ: "4b + 3" as a decoy for "4b + 6"
  works (any value separates them); "4(b + 6)" as a decoy also works
  (it equals 4b + 24). Reject decoys that turn out equivalent.
