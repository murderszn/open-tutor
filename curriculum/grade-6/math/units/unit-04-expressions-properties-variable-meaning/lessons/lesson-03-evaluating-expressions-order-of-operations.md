# Lesson 03 — Evaluating Expressions and the Order of Operations

**My goal:** I can evaluate an expression at a given value of its
variable, including expressions from real-world formulas, using the
conventional order of operations.

**Time:** about 45 minutes (5 readiness · 15 teaching · 10 guided ·
12 independent · 3 exit).

**Materials:** paper, pencil.

**Standards:** 6.EE.A.2.c · **Unit objectives:** UO15

## Readiness check (5 minutes)

Answer before we start — this tells us what is already solid.

1. Evaluate 3 × 4 + 5. Which operation did you do first? Why?
2. Evaluate (3 + 4) × 5. How did the parentheses change the answer?
3. What is 2²? What is 2³?

*(Today adds one new step to the order you already know: exponents
come right after parentheses.)*

## Explicit teaching

### Substitution: give the letter a value

To **evaluate** an expression means to find its value. When the
expression has a variable, first **substitute**: replace the letter
with the given number, then compute. Write the substitution out — do
not do it in your head:

- Expression 3x + 2, with x = 4: substitute → 3 × 4 + 2, then compute
  → 14.
- The substituted form shows every step, so an error can be found.

### The conventional order

When no parentheses say otherwise, the conventional order is:

1. **Parentheses** (and other grouping symbols) — innermost first.
2. **Exponents** — evaluate powers next.
3. **Multiplication and division** — left to right, as they appear.
4. **Addition and subtraction** — left to right, as they appear.

The common memory aid is PEMDAS (Parentheses, Exponents,
Multiplication/Division, Addition/Subtraction), but remember the last
two pairs work **left to right together**: multiplication does not
always come before division, and addition does not always come before
subtraction. In 10 − 4 + 3, work left to right: 6 + 3 = 9, not
10 − 7 = 3.

### Worked example 1 — evaluate 3x + 2² for x = 4

Substitute x = 4: 3 × 4 + 2².

Now follow the order. There are no parentheses. Exponents come before
multiplication and addition:

- 2² = 4, so the expression becomes 3 × 4 + 4.
- Multiplication next: 3 × 4 = 12, so we have 12 + 4.
- Addition last: 12 + 4 = 16.

So 3x + 2² = 16 when x = 4. Notice what goes wrong if exponents are
done last: 3 × 4 + 2 = 14, and then "squaring" 14 would be nonsense —
the exponent belongs to the 2 only, not to the whole expression.

### Worked example 2 — a real formula: the cube

A cube's volume and surface area come from its side length s:

- Volume: V = s³ (side cubed).
- Surface area: A = 6s² (six faces, each s²).

For a cube with side s = 1/2 (a half-unit cube):

- V = (1/2)³ = 1/2 × 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/8. (Parentheses matter here: the
  base is the fraction 1/2, so the whole fraction is cubed.)
- A = 6 × (1/2)² = 6 × 1/4 = 6/4 = 3/2.

So a half-unit cube has volume 1/8 cubic units and surface area 3/2
square units. For a cube with side s = 3, check yourself: V = 27 and
A = 6 × 9 = 54. Formulas are just expressions waiting for a value —
substitute, then follow the order.

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. Evaluate 2n + 5 for n = 3. *(Hint: substitute first: 2 × 3 + 5.)*
2. Evaluate 4² − 3 × 2. *(Hint: exponent first: 16 − 6.)*
3. Evaluate (5 + 3)². *(Hint: parentheses first: 8².)*
4. Evaluate 5 + 2 × 3². *(Hint: exponent, then multiplication, then
   addition: 5 + 18.)*

## Independent practice

1. Evaluate x + x² for x = 5.
2. Evaluate 3a² for a = 4.
3. Evaluate 20 − 2³.
4. Evaluate (9 − 4)² ÷ 5.
5. Evaluate 6 + 4 ÷ 2. (Watch the left-to-right rule.)
6. Evaluate 2(3 + 4) − 5.
7. A rectangle's perimeter formula is P = 4s for a square of side s.
   Find P when s = 6.
8. A stall charges C = 2n + 7 dollars for n bags of fruit. Find C
   when n = 10.

## Application

A wooden gift box is a cube with side 3 inches. (a) Write its volume
using V = s³ and evaluate it, with units. (b) Write its surface area
using A = 6s² and evaluate it, with units. (c) Explain in one sentence
why the surface-area value (54) is larger than the volume value (27)
here, even though volume is "three-dimensional."

## Exit check

Evaluate 2 × 3² + 1. Then explain why 37 is the wrong answer to this
problem (it comes from computing (2 × 3)² + 1).

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading/language access:** Write each evaluation in two lines:
  the substituted expression, then the step-by-step computation.
  Circle the operation you are about to do at each step.
- **Alternate response mode:** The learner may dictate the order of
  steps ("parentheses, then exponents…") while the adult records the
  arithmetic, or use a step-numbered template.
- **Extension:** Evaluate 2³ + 3² × 2 − 10 ÷ 5. (Answer: 8 + 18 − 2
  = 24.) Then invent an expression of your own whose value is 100
  when x = 5, and prove it by evaluating.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: doing operations strictly left to right and
ignoring the order; treating 3x as 3 + x when substituting; cubing a
fraction without parentheses, e.g. writing 1/2³ when (1/2)³ is meant.
See the teacher guide and the answer-key reference.
