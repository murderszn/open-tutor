# Lesson 04 — The Distributive Property

**My goal:** I can use the distributive property to expand a product
into a sum and to factor a sum into a product.

**Time:** about 45 minutes (5 readiness · 15 teaching · 10 guided ·
12 independent · 3 exit).

**Materials:** paper, pencil; grid paper (optional, for the area
model).

**Standards:** 6.EE.A.3 · **Unit objectives:** UO16

## Readiness check (5 minutes)

Answer before we start — this tells us what is already solid.

1. Evaluate 3 × (2 + 4) two ways: parentheses first, and by
   multiplying 3 × 2 and 3 × 4 and adding. Do you get the same answer?
2. What is 3 × 2 + 3 × 4? How does it relate to question 1?
3. In 2(8 + 7), what is the single entity being multiplied by 2?

*(Today names the pattern you just used: multiplying a sum
distributes the multiplication to each term.)*

## Explicit teaching

### One multiplication, shared across a sum

The **distributive property** says that multiplying a sum is the same
as multiplying each term and then adding:

**a(b + c) = ab + ac.**

The factor outside the parentheses "distributes" — it reaches every
term inside. It also works in reverse, which is called **factoring**:
ab + ac = a(b + c). Factoring pulls out a factor shared by all terms.

An area model makes this visible. A rectangle 3 units wide and
(2 + x) units long can be split into two smaller rectangles: one
3 by 2, and one 3 by x. Their areas are 6 and 3x, so the whole area
is 6 + 3x. And the whole rectangle's area computed directly is
3(2 + x). Same rectangle, two descriptions — so 3(2 + x) = 6 + 3x.

The distributive property also explains repeated addition: y + y + y
is three copies of y, so y + y + y = 3y. Grouping like terms is
distribution in disguise: 4x + 3x = (4 + 3)x = 7x.

Two more properties you already use, now named: the **commutative
property** (order does not matter: a + b = b + a, ab = ba) and the
**associative property** (grouping does not matter: (a + b) + c =
a + (b + c), (ab)c = a(bc)). They let us rearrange terms; the
distributive property lets us expand and factor.

### Worked example 1 — expand 3(2 + x) and check it

Distribute the 3 to each term inside:

- 3 × 2 = 6.
- 3 × x = 3x.
- So 3(2 + x) = 6 + 3x.

Check with x = 4 (Lesson 05's habit, started early): original,
3(2 + 4) = 3 × 6 = 18; expanded, 6 + 3 × 4 = 6 + 12 = 18. They match,
as they must — expanding never changes the value.

### Worked example 2 — factor 24x + 18y

Factoring asks: what factor do both terms share? 24x and 18y share the
factor 6 (24 = 6 × 4, 18 = 6 × 3). Pull it out:

- 24x + 18y = 6(4x + 3y).

Check by expanding: 6 × 4x = 24x and 6 × 3y = 18y. The factored form
is correct. (Could we also factor out 2 or 3? Yes — 2(12x + 9y) is
also equal — but 6 is the *greatest* common factor, from Unit 02's
GCF work, and gives the simplest factored form.)

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. Expand 4(x + 3). *(Hint: 4 × x and 4 × 3.)*
2. Expand 5(2a + 1). *(Hint: 5 × 2a = 10a.)*
3. Expand 2(7 − n). *(Hint: distribution works over subtraction too:
   2 × 7 and 2 × n, with subtraction between.)*
4. Factor 12 + 18 as a product using the GCF. *(Hint: GCF of 12 and
   18 is 6.)*
5. Factor 15b + 10. *(Hint: GCF of 15 and 10 is 5.)*

## Independent practice

1. Expand: (a) 6(m + 2) (b) 3(4 + 2k) (c) 8(p + 5) (d) 4(3x + 1).
2. Factor: (a) 20x + 12 (b) 9 + 27y (c) 4a + 4 (d) 18m + 24.
3. Combine like terms: (a) 5x + 2x (b) 3n + n + 4n (c) 7a + 2 + 3a.
4. Error analysis: Jo says 3(x + 4) = 3x + 4. Explain Jo's error,
   give the correct expansion, and check both versions with x = 2.
5. A garden has 4 rows, each with (6 + t) tomato plants. Write two
   expressions for the total number of plants (factored and expanded
   forms) and evaluate both for t = 3.

## Application

A market stall packs apples in boxes of (10 + n) apples, and sells
6 boxes. (a) Write the total as a product (factored form). (b) Expand
it. (c) If n = 5, evaluate both forms and confirm they agree.

## Exit check

Expand 2(5 + n). Then check your answer with n = 3.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading/language access:** Draw the area model for every
  expansion at first: one rectangle split into strips, each strip
  labeled with its product. Arrows from the outside factor to each
  inside term make "distributes to every term" literal.
- **Alternate response mode:** The learner may build the expansion
  with algebra tiles or paper strips (a row of x-tiles and unit tiles,
  copied the required number of times) instead of writing symbols.
- **Extension:** Expand 3(2x + 4y + 1) and factor 12x + 20y + 8.
  (Answers: 6x + 12y + 3, and 4(3x + 5y + 2).) Explain why the factored
  form's GCF is 4, not 2.

## Teacher note

Likely misconceptions: distributing to only the first term
(3(x + 4) = 3x + 4); forgetting that distribution works over
subtraction; factoring out a common factor but leaving a term behind
(24x + 18y = 6(4x + 3) — the y vanished). Always check by expanding
back. See the teacher guide and the answer-key reference.
