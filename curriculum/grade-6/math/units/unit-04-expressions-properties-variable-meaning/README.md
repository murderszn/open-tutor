# Unit 04 — Expressions, Properties, and Variable Meaning

**Grade 6 Mathematics · Weeks 15–18 · 16 sessions (~45 minutes each, 4 per week)**

**Standards:** 6.EE.A.1, 6.EE.A.2.a–c, 6.EE.A.3, 6.EE.A.4

**Issue:** [#30](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/30)
(section U04 — draft delivery; not educator-reviewed)

## Overview

Unit 01 built ratio reasoning, Unit 02 built fraction and decimal
computation, and Unit 03 built the signed number line and the
four-quadrant plane. This unit builds the **language of algebra**: it
teaches the learner to write a calculation once, with a letter standing
for a number that can change, and then to read, evaluate, and reshape
that writing without changing what it means. Week 15 starts with
**whole-number exponents** — a power is repeated multiplication, not
multiplication by the exponent — and the order of operations with
exponents. Week 16 turns to **variables**: letters stand for numbers,
expressions record operations, and every expression has nameable parts
(sum, term, product, factor, quotient, coefficient). Week 17 turns to
**equivalent expressions**: the properties of operations, especially
the distributive property, let us rewrite an expression into a
different-looking one that names the same number for every value of
the variable — and substitution is how we test whether two expressions
really match. Week 18 is the **midyear review week**: the project
finishes, the culminating assessment checks this unit, and retrieval
practice reaches back to ratios, fraction and decimal operations, and
integers from Units 01–03.

Solving equations (x + p = q, px = q) and inequalities belong to
Unit 05, not this unit. This unit never asks the learner to "solve for
x." It asks what an expression *says*, what it is *worth* when the
letter takes a value, and when two expressions say the *same thing*.
That restraint is deliberate: Unit 05's equation work depends on it.

## Essential question

*How can one short piece of writing stand for a whole family of
calculations — and how do we know when two different-looking pieces of
writing always say the same thing?*

## Prerequisites (re-checked in Lessons 01–02)

- Multiplication and division fluency, including multi-digit
  multiplication (Unit 02)
- Order of operations with parentheses from grade 5 (parentheses,
  multiplication/division, addition/subtraction)
- Fraction and decimal computation for checking values (Unit 02)
- Reading a formula as a rule: "V = s³" means "cube the side length"
  (introduced here, not assumed)

Sessions 01–02 re-check multiplication fluency and the grade-5 order of
operations before any exponent or variable is introduced. The
diagnostic weeks (Weeks 1–2) baselined these before Unit 01.

## Vocabulary

base · exponent · power · expanded form · exponential form · variable ·
expression · numerical expression · algebraic expression · evaluate ·
substitute / substitution · term · sum · product · factor · quotient ·
coefficient · like terms · distributive property · commutative property ·
associative property · equivalent expressions · order of operations ·
formula · cube · square (as a power: "squared," "cubed")

(Vocabulary is taught with examples first, the word second;
home-language labels are welcomed alongside English terms. "Squared"
means "to the power 2" and "cubed" means "to the power 3" — a square
with side 5 has area 5², a cube with side 5 has volume 5³.)

## Verified standards / source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**,
Grade 6, Expressions and Equations, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/Math](https://thecorestandards.org/Math) (Grade 6
EE page at <https://thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/6/EE/>; codes and
descriptions re-verified 2026-10-31 for this unit). Exact official
wording used:

- **6.EE.A.1** — "Write and evaluate numerical expressions involving
  whole-number exponents."
- **6.EE.A.2.a** — "Write expressions that record operations with
  numbers and with letters standing for numbers. *For example, express
  the calculation 'Subtract y from 5' as 5 − y.*"
- **6.EE.A.2.b** — "Identify parts of an expression using mathematical
  terms (sum, term, product, factor, quotient, coefficient); view one
  or more parts of an expression as a single entity. *For example,
  describe the expression 2 (8 + 7) as a product of two factors; view
  (8 + 7) as both a single entity and a sum of two terms.*"
- **6.EE.A.2.c** — "Evaluate expressions at specific values of their
  variables. Include expressions that arise from formulas used in
  real-world problems. Perform arithmetic operations, including those
  involving whole-number exponents, in the conventional order when
  there are no parentheses to specify a particular order (Order of
  Operations). *For example, use the formulas V = s³ and A = 6s² to
  find the volume and surface area of a cube with sides of length
  s = 1/2.*"
- **6.EE.A.3** — "Apply the properties of operations to generate
  equivalent expressions. *For example, apply the distributive
  property to the expression 3 (2 + x) to produce the equivalent
  expression 6 + 3x; apply the distributive property to the expression
  24x + 18y to produce the equivalent expression 6 (4x + 3y); apply
  properties of operations to y + y + y to produce the equivalent
  expression 3y.*"
- **6.EE.A.4** — "Identify when two expressions are equivalent (i.e.,
  when the two expressions name the same number regardless of which
  value is substituted into them). *For example, the expressions
  y + y + y and 3y are equivalent because they name the same number
  regardless of which number y stands for.*"
