## Lesson 05 — Equivalent Expressions and Substitution Tests

**Readiness:** 1. Both 6 — they match at y = 2. 2. 4 vs 3 — they do
not match. 3. 2x + 6.

**Guided:** 1. Equivalent: 4m + m = 5m (combine like terms).
2. Equivalent: 2(3 + n) = 6 + 2n (distributive property; order of
terms differs by commutativity). 3. Not equivalent: 3(k + 1) = 3k + 3;
counterexample k = 0 gives 1 vs 3. 4. Equivalent: four copies of a
is 4a.

**Independent:** 1. (a) Equivalent: 2(x + 1) = 2x + 2. (b) Not
equivalent: 3(t + 7) = 3t + 21; counterexample t = 0 gives 7 vs 21.
(c) Equivalent: 5(n + 1) = 5n + 5. (d) Not equivalent: any value
separates them (e.g., p = 0: 3 vs 4). (e) Equivalent: 6y + 2y = 8y.
2. Lee is wrong: 2(m + 5) = 2m + 10, not 2m + 5. At m = 1: 7 vs 12.
The 2 must distribute to both terms. 3. No. Example: x + 3 and 3x
both give 6 at x = 3, yet they are not equivalent (x = 1 gives 4
vs 3). 4. Learner's own pairs; check that the equivalent pair has a
valid transformation and the non-equivalent pair has a genuine
counterexample value.

**Application:** (a) 3n + 3 + 1 = 3n + 4. (b) Yes, equivalent: Ben's
expression transforms into Ana's. (c) n = 5: both give 19.

**Exit:** Equivalent: 2(3 + m) = 6 + 2m. Check m = 2: both give 10.

**Extension:** 2(x + 3) + 4 = 2x + 10, equivalent. x² and 2x are not
equivalent: x = 3 gives 9 vs 6. They happen to agree at x = 0 and
x = 2, which is why agreement at some values never proves
equivalence.
