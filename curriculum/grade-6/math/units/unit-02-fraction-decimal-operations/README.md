# Unit 02 — Fraction and Decimal Operations

**Grade 6 Mathematics · Weeks 7–10 · 16 sessions (~45 minutes each, 4 per week)**

**Standards:** 6.NS.A.1, 6.NS.B.2, 6.NS.B.3, 6.NS.B.4

**Issue:** [#30](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/30)
(section U02 — draft delivery; not educator-reviewed)

## Overview

Unit 01 built the ratio-reasoning engine; this unit builds the computation
engine the rest of the year runs on. Learners first learn to **ask the right
question when fractions divide**: a division like (2/3) ÷ (3/4) is a question
about groups — "how many three-quarter-cup servings fit in two-thirds of a
cup?" — answerable with story contexts and visual models. Then they learn
**why the shortcut works**: rewriting with a common denominator shows that
dividing fractions is dividing same-size pieces, which is exactly what
"invert and multiply" computes. Week 8 moves to **fluency** — multi-digit
whole-number division and all four decimal operations with the standard
algorithm for each, always with an estimate first to check reasonableness.
Week 9 turns to **structure**: greatest common factor (≤ 100) and least common
multiple (≤ 12) found by listing factors and multiples, and the distributive
property used to rewrite sums like 36 + 8 as 4(9 + 2). The unit closes with
a week of mixed-operation problem solving across fractions and decimals, the
Rosa's Panadería investigation project, and the culminating assessment.

This unit's fraction-division work feeds Unit 03 (rational numbers),
Unit 04–05 (expressions and equations), and the U08 modeling unit, where
decimal and fraction computation must be automatic. Nothing here assumes
Unit 01 beyond story-context familiarity — but Lesson 05 deliberately
revisits U01's unit-pricing context with fraction and decimal quantities,
closing the loop between ratio reasoning and computation.

## Essential question

*When a division question involves fractions or decimals, what is the
question really asking, how can I compute it exactly, and how do I know my
answer makes sense?*

## Prerequisites (grade-5 track end-of-year expectations, re-checked)

- Fluent multi-digit multiplication with the standard algorithm; division
  with up to four-digit dividends, interpreting remainders from context
- Fraction equivalence and scaling reasoning; fraction-as-division
  (a/b = a ÷ b); adding/subtracting fractions with unlike denominators
- Decimal place value to thousandths; decimal addition/subtraction and
  multiplication to hundredths with models; rounding to any place

Sessions 01–02 re-check fraction equivalence (a common-denominator refresher
opens Lesson 02) before invert-and-multiply is introduced; decimal place
value is re-baselined in Lesson 03's readiness check. The diagnostic weeks
(Weeks 1–2) baselined these before U01.

## Vocabulary

quotient · dividend · divisor · reciprocal · invert and multiply · visual
fraction model · estimate · standard algorithm · decimal places · place
value · factor · multiple · greatest common factor (GCF) · least common
multiple (LCM) · distributive property · regroup

(Vocabulary is taught with objects and drawings first, the word second;
home-language labels are welcomed alongside English terms. "Reciprocal" is
introduced as "the flipped version" — 3/4 flipped is 4/3.)

## Verified standards / source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**,
Grade 6, The Number System, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/Math](https://thecorestandards.org/Math) (Grade 6 NS
page at <https://thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/6/NS/>; codes and
descriptions re-verified 2026-10-18 for this unit). Exact official wording
used:

- **6.NS.A.1** — "Interpret and compute quotients of fractions, and solve
  word problems involving division of fractions by fractions, e.g., by using
  visual fraction models and equations to represent the problem. *For
  example, create a story context for (2/3) ÷ (3/4) and use a visual fraction
  model to show the quotient; use the relationship between multiplication
  and division to explain that (2/3) ÷ (3/4) = 8/9 because 3/4 of 8/9 is 2/3.
  (In general, (a/b) ÷ (c/d) = ad/bc.)*"
- **6.NS.B.2** — "Fluently divide multi-digit numbers using the standard
  algorithm." (text from the track's 2026-10-01 audit crosswalk; A.1, B.3,
  and B.4 re-confirmed live on the official site 2026-10-18)
- **6.NS.B.3** — "Fluently add, subtract, multiply, and divide multi-digit
  decimals using the standard algorithm for each operation."
- **6.NS.B.4** — "Find the greatest common factor of two whole numbers less
  than or equal to 100 and the least common multiple of two whole numbers
  less than or equal to 12. Use the distributive property to express a sum
  of two whole numbers 1-100 with a common factor as a multiple of a sum of
  two whole numbers with no common factor. *For example, express 36 + 8 as
  4 (9 + 2).*"
