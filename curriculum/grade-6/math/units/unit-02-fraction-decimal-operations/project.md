# Project — Rosa's Panadería (investigation)

**Sessions:** S09–S11 (launch, costing, packaging + peer check) and S15
(presentations). **Objectives:** UO5, UO6, UO7.

Rosa's neighborhood panadería has a big weekend coming: a test batch for
a new pan dulce recipe, a party order to cost out, and gift boxes to pack
for the street festival. You are her math assistant. All prices and
quantities below are **fictional practice data** for this project.

## Part 1 — Recipe scaling (S09; UO5)

Rosa's pan dulce recipe (one full batch) calls for:

- 2 1/3 cups flour (7/3 cups)
- 3/4 cup sugar
- 1/2 cup milk

**Task A — test batch.** Rosa wants a small test batch: **2/3 of the full
recipe**. Compute the scaled amount of each ingredient as a fraction.
Show the multiplication for each.

**Task B — festival batch.** For the festival she needs **3 full batches**.
Compute each ingredient for the triple batch. Write any improper fraction
as a mixed number.

**Task C — explain.** In two sentences, explain why scaling a recipe by
2/3 is *multiplication* (2/3 × amount) while the serving questions in
Lessons 01–02 were *division*. What is different about the question?

*Check: A — flour (2/3)(7/3) = 14/9 = 1 5/9 cups; sugar (2/3)(3/4) =
6/12 = 1/2 cup; milk (2/3)(1/2) = 2/6 = 1/3 cup. B — flour 3(7/3) = 7
cups; sugar 3(3/4) = 9/4 = 2 1/4 cups; milk 3(1/2) = 3/2 = 1 1/2 cups.*

## Part 2 — Order costing (S10; UO6)

A party order arrives:

- 24 conchas at $1.75 each
- 36 orejas at $1.25 each

**Task D — the bill.** Compute each line total and the order total.
Estimate first, then compute exactly.

**Task E — the change.** The customer pays with a $100 bill. Compute the
change with decimal subtraction. Write a one-sentence check that the
change is reasonable.

**Task F — unit price.** The customer asks: "What is the price per dozen
for each pastry?" Compute both unit prices (price ÷ 12) and state them
with units.

*Check: D — 24 × 1.75 = $42.00; 36 × 1.25 = $45.00; total $87.00.
E — $100 − $87.00 = $13.00. F — $42.00 ÷ 12 = $3.50/dozen conchas;
$45.00 ÷ 12 = $3.75/dozen orejas.*

## Part 3 — Packaging (S11; UO7)

**Task G — gift boxes.** Rosa has **48** mini muffins and **36** conchas
for festival gift boxes. Every box must be identical, everything must be
used, and she wants the *greatest* number of boxes. How many boxes?
What goes in each? Write the total as a multiple of a sum and check it.

**Task H — tray batches.** Empanadas are baked in trays and packed two
ways: boxes of **6** and bags of **8**. What is the smallest batch size
that packs evenly *both* ways? Explain with multiple lists. (This is the
"repeating events lining up" idea from Lesson 04.)

**Task I — decide and defend.** For Task G you used the GCF and for Task
H the LCM. In three sentences, explain how you knew which one each task
needed, using the "must divide both / must be a multiple of both"
language from Lesson 04.

*Check: G — GCF(48, 36) = 12 → 12 boxes; 4 muffins + 3 conchas each;
48 + 36 = 12(4 + 3) = 12 × 7 = 84 ✓. H — LCM(6, 8) = 24.*

## Deliverables (due S15)

1. **Scaled recipe sheet** — Tasks A–B with work shown, C answered.
2. **Order invoice** — Tasks D–F: line totals, order total, change,
   per-dozen prices, with estimates noted.
3. **Packaging plan** — Tasks G–I: box count and contents, batch size
   with multiple lists, the GCF/LCM defense.
4. **Presentation** (S15, ~5 minutes): walk the class through one part,
   showing your model or computation and one check you used.

## Rubric

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Proficient | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fraction work (A–C) | All scalings correct and simplified; explanation correctly distinguishes scaling from serving questions | Computations correct; explanation present but vague | 1–2 computation errors or missing simplification | Multiple errors; no explanation |
| Decimal work (D–F) | Estimates + exact totals all correct; change reasonable and checked; unit prices with units | Totals correct; estimate or check missing | 1–2 arithmetic errors | Multiple errors; no estimates |
| GCF/LCM (G–I) | Correct GCF and LCM with lists shown; distributive rewrite correct; defense uses divide/multiple language precisely | Correct answers; lists or rewrite incomplete | One of GCF/LCM wrong or unexplained | Both wrong or missing |
| Presentation & checks | Clear walkthrough; at least one verification (multiplication check, estimate, or both-sides check) demonstrated | Clear; check mentioned but not shown | Unclear order or missing parts | No presentation or no mathematics shown |

**Scoring:** 14–16 = exceeds; 11–13 = meets; 8–10 = approaching (reteach
target: the lowest criterion); below 8 = revisit the unit's practice sets
before presenting.
