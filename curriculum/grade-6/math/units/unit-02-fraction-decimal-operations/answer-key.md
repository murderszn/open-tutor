## Lesson 01

**Readiness:** 1. 6 halves. 2. Less than 1/2 (it is a third of the half).
3. Accept any valid story, e.g. "6 apples shared between 2 people."

**Guided:** 1. 4 ÷ (1/3) = 12 slices (4 wholes × 3 thirds). 2. (2/3) ÷ 2
= 1/3 cup each — the half of 2/3 is one of three equal parts of the
whole. 3. 2 ÷ (1/4) = 8 servings; model: 2 wholes, 4 quarters each.
4. Sample story: "3/4 of a pizza is shared equally between 2 friends."
Solution: (3/4) ÷ 2 = 3/8 of the whole pizza each.

**Independent:** 5. 5 ÷ (1/2) = 10 loaves; 10 > 5 as the sense-check
rule predicts (divisor < 1). 6. (1/4) ÷ 2 = 1/8 gallon per room; model:
a quarter-bar split in two — each piece is 1/8 of the whole gallon.
7. Leo's errors: (a) division by a fraction smaller than 1 makes the
quotient *larger*, not smaller; (b) he multiplied 2 × 1/4 instead of
dividing. Correct: 2 ÷ (1/4) = 8. 8. (3/5) ÷ 3 = 1/5 liter per glass;
model: 3/5 of a liter bar split into 3 equal pieces, each 1/5 of a liter.

**Application:** (a) (2 1/2) ÷ (1/4) = (5/2) × 4 = 10 conchas.
(b) 2 1/2 cups = 10 quarter-cups → 10 marks → count 10. (c) Smaller:
larger servings fit fewer times; (5/2) ÷ (1/2) = 5 < 10 ✓.

**Exit:** a. "How many third-cup servings are in 2 cups?" → 2 ÷ (1/3) =
6. b. "2/3 of a cake shared between 2 people — how much each?" →
(2/3) ÷ 2 = 1/3. c. Sample: "Dividing by 1/2 asks how many halves fit in
4, and each whole holds two halves, so the count (8) exceeds 4."

## Lesson 02

**Readiness:** 1. 2/3 = 8/12; 3/4 = 9/12. 2. (1/2) ÷ (1/4) = 2. 3. If
□ ÷ (3/4) = 8/9 then (3/4) × (8/9) = □, i.e. □ = 24/36 = 2/3.

**Guided:** 1. (5/6) ÷ (2/3) = (5/6) ÷ (4/6) = 5/4; flip-and-multiply:
(5/6) × (3/2) = 15/12 = 5/4 ✓. 2. Divisor (1/2) > dividend (3/8), so
quotient < 1; (3/8) ÷ (1/2) = (3/8) × 2 = 6/8 = 3/4 ✓ (< 1 ✓).
3. (7/8) ÷ (1/4) = (7/8) × 4 = 28/8 = 7/2 = 3 1/2 rolls; picture: 7
eighths grouped into quarter (2-eighth) servings → 3 full servings and
one half-serving. Both methods agree. 4. Maya flipped the *dividend*;
only the divisor flips. Correct: (1/2) ÷ (3/4) = (1/2) × (4/3) = 4/6 =
2/3; check: (3/4) × (2/3) = 6/12 = 1/2 ✓.

**Independent:** 5. (a) (2/5) ÷ (3/10) = (2/5) × (10/3) = 20/15 = 4/3;
(b) (7/9) ÷ (2/3) = (7/9) × (3/2) = 21/18 = 7/6; (c) (5/4) ÷ (1/2) =
(5/4) × 2 = 10/4 = 5/2. 6. "How many groups?" → (3/4) ÷ (1/8) =
(3/4) × 8 = 6 markers. 7. Sample story: "5/6 of a pan of cornbread is
cut into servings of 1/2 pan." Solution: (5/6) ÷ (1/2) = (5/6) × 2 =
10/6 = 5/3 = 1 2/3 servings; check: (1/2) × (5/3) = 5/6 ✓. 8. Accept any
clear version of: "With denominator 12, (2/3) ÷ (3/4) becomes
(8/12) ÷ (9/12) = 8 ÷ 9 = 8/9 — same-size pieces, so just divide the
counts"; or "3/4 of 8/9 is 2/3, and division undoes multiplication, so
(2/3) ÷ (3/4) = 8/9." Targets: procedure-without-meaning.

**Application:** (a) 8 cells shaded = 8/12 = 2/3. (b) 2 full quarters
fit; 2 cells remain = 2/3 of a quarter → 2 2/3 quarters total.
(c) (2/3) ÷ (1/4) = 2 2/3; flip-and-multiply: (2/3) × 4 = 8/3 = 2 2/3 ✓.

**Exit:** a. (3/4) ÷ (2/5) = (3/4) × (5/2) = 15/8 = 1 7/8. b. (a/b) ×
(d/c). c. Dividing by 1/2 asks "how many halves fit in 3/4" (more than
one), while dividing by 2 asks "3/4 shared between 2" (less than 3/4) —
different questions, different sizes.

## Lesson 04

**Readiness:** 1. 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 (pairs 1×12, 2×6, 3×4 meet in the
middle). 2. 6, 12, 18, 24, 30. 3. Add first: 4 × 11 = 44; distribute:
36 + 8 = 44 ✓.

**Guided:** 1. GCF(24, 36) = 12; 24 + 36 = 12(2 + 3) = 12 × 5 = 60 ✓.
2. LCM(4, 10) = 20 (4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20; 10: 10, 20). 3. GCF — the
answer must divide both: GCF(28, 42) = 14 → 14 bags with 2 stickers and
3 stamps each. 4. LCM — the answer must be a multiple of both:
LCM(9, 12) = 36 → 36 seconds (9: 9, 18, 27, 36; 12: 12, 24, 36).

**Independent:** 5. (a) GCF(30, 45) = 15; (b) LCM(9, 12) = 36;
(c) GCF(16, 40) = 8; (d) LCM(5, 7) = 35. 6. (a) 25 + 15 = 5(5 + 3) =
40 ✓; (b) 16 + 24 = 8(2 + 3) = 40 ✓; (c) 28 + 42 = 14(2 + 3) = 70 ✓.
7. Dev is right that GCF(18, 24) = 6, but the LCM is not 6: 6 is not a
*multiple* of 18 or 24. Correct LCM(18, 24) = 72 (18: 18, 36, 54, 72;
24: 24, 48, 72). 8. Greatest number of plates → GCF(20, 30) = 10
plates, 2 + 3 = 5 pastries per plate.

**Application:** (a) LCM(15, 20) = 60 → next joint performance at 1:00
PM (15: 15, 30, 45, 60; 20: 20, 40, 60). (b) GCF(54, 81) = 27 → 27
boxes; 54 ÷ 27 = 2 empanadas and 81 ÷ 27 = 3 cookies per box;
54 + 81 = 27(2 + 3) = 27 × 5 = 135 ✓. (c) Sample: "(a) asks when two
repeating schedules line up, so the answer must be a multiple of both
times — LCM. (b) asks how to split two quantities into identical groups,
so the answer must divide both — GCF."

**Exit:** a. GCF(14, 35) = 7. b. LCM(3, 8) = 24. c. 18 + 27 = 9(2 + 3).

## Lesson 05

**Readiness:** 1. (3/4) ÷ (1/2) = 3/2. 2. Estimate ≈ 4.5; 2.5 × 1.8 =
4.50. 3. The LCM (72 > 6) — LCM ≥ both numbers, GCF ≤ both.

**Guided:** 1. Plan: common denominator 6 in parentheses, multiply,
subtract. (2/3 + 1/6) × 2 − 0.25 = (5/6) × 2 − 1/4 = 10/6 − 1/4 =
5/3 − 1/4 = 20/12 − 3/12 = 17/12. 2. A: 1.20 ÷ (2/5) = $3.00/kg; B:
1.45 ÷ (1/2) = $2.90/kg → B is the better buy. 3. Estimate ≈ 8,500 ÷
25 ≈ 340; 8,544 ÷ 24 = 356 ✓. 4. Sam multiplied by the divisor instead
of its reciprocal — he flipped nothing. Correct: (5/6) ÷ (2/3) =
(5/6) × (3/2) = 15/12 = 5/4.

**Independent:** 5. (a) Plan: parentheses (common denominator 8), then
divide, then add. (7/8 − 1/4) ÷ (1/2) + 0.5 = (5/8) ÷ (1/2) + 0.5 =
(5/8) × 2 + 1/2 = 10/8 + 4/8 = 14/8 = 7/4. (b) Plan: parentheses,
then left-to-right division/multiplication. 3.6 ÷ 0.9 × (1/2 + 1/3) =
3.6 ÷ 0.9 × (5/6) = 4 × (5/6) = 20/6 = 10/3. 6. (2.5) ÷ (3/4) = 10/3
= 3 1/3 → 3 full batches; leftover: 2.5 − 3(0.75) = 0.25 lb = 1/4 lb.
7. Accept any solvable two-step story, e.g.: "A 3/4-gallon jug is poured
into 1/2-gallon bottles (fraction division), then each full bottle sells
for $2.50 (decimal multiplication)." Solution: (3/4) ÷ (1/2) = 3/2 =
1.5 → 1 full bottle; 1 × 2.50 = $2.50. Plan line required. 8. The
reasoning is correct: dividing by 2/3 with a common denominator is
dividing the numerators after scaling to 3rds/… — the general form
(a/b) ÷ (c/d) = (ad/bd) ÷ (bc/bd) = ad/bc = (a/b) × (d/c), i.e. multiply
by the reciprocal 3/2. Targets: rule-without-reason.

**Application:** (a) Plan: empanada subtotal → 10% discount → discounted
empanadas; cake price (1.5 × 9.60); total; change. (b) 4 × 6.75 =
$27.00; discount 0.10 × 27.00 = $2.70; discounted $24.30. (c) 1.5 ×
9.60 = $14.40; total $24.30 + $14.40 = $38.70. (d) Estimate ≈ $40 −
$39 = $1; exact: $40.00 − $38.70 = $1.30 ✓.

**Exit:** a. (5/6) ÷ (2/3) = 5/4; check: (2/3) × (5/4) = 10/12 = 5/6 ✓.
b. Estimate ≈ 8; 24 × 35 = 840, two places → 8.40 ✓. c. Sample: "Read
the whole problem and write a plan line naming the operations in order
before computing anything."
