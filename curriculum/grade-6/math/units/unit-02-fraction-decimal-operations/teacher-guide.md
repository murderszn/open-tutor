# Teacher Guide — Unit 02: Fraction and Decimal Operations

For the adult guiding the learner. Student materials never contain these
notes; full worked solutions live in the [answer key](answer-key.md).

## Unit arc

Lessons 01–02 build *meaning* before *method*: learners who can read a
division as a question about groups (L01) and rebuild invert-and-multiply
from a common denominator (L02) rarely make the signature "flipped the
wrong fraction" error later. Lessons 03–04 build fluency and structure in
parallel; Lesson 05 forces the two to cooperate on multi-step problems.
The project (S09–S11) is the unit's true assessment of transfer — grade
it with the rubric in [project.md](project.md), not just for right
answers.

## Lesson 01 — fraction division: what the question means

**Watch for:** learners who compute 3 ÷ (1/2) as 3/2 (they divided by 2
instead of by 1/2) or who cannot say which question type a division asks.
The one-sentence "this asks ___" routine is the intervention — do not
let them compute until they can state it.

**Readiness signals to move on:** the learner correctly sorts three new
divisions into "how many groups?" vs "how much per group?" and predicts
whether each quotient will be larger or smaller than the dividend.

**Reteach:** if the sense-check rule ("dividing by a fraction < 1 grows
the quotient") is shaky, return to measuring cups: pour 2 cups, count
half-cup scoops. The body remembers what the rule states.

## Lesson 02 — why invert-and-multiply works

**Watch for:** flipping the dividend (Maya's error in guided practice
#4) and unsimplified answers left as 12/10. Both are caught by the
lesson’s two disciplines: "flip only the divisor" and "verify by
multiplication."

**Readiness signals:** the learner can do (a/b) ÷ (c/d) *both* ways
(common denominator and flip-and-multiply) and explain in their own
words why the answers match.

**Reteach:** a learner who can only do the rule gets Way A only —
common denominator, divide the numerators — until the rule is re-derived
rather than recalled. The deterministic SVG model
([assets/fraction-division-model.svg](assets/fraction-division-model.svg))
is the concrete anchor for (2/3) ÷ (1/4) = 2 2/3.

## Lesson 03 — decimal operations fluency

**Watch for:** decimal-point placement by feel, misaligned addition,
and shifting only one number in division. The estimate-first discipline
is the whole lesson — a learner who estimates honestly will catch 90% of
their own errors.

**Readiness signals:** four mixed decimal computations, each with an
estimate written *before* the algorithm and a one-line comparison after.

**Reteach:** persistent point errors mean place-value is the gap, not
the algorithm. Rebuild with the fraction form (3.2 = 32/10) before
re-drilling.

## Lesson 04 — GCF, LCM, and the distributive property

**Watch for:** the GCF/LCM swap and stopping the multiple list at a
non-least common multiple. Drill the *choice* ("must divide both" vs
"must be a multiple of both") with new story pairs before drilling the
listing.

**Readiness signals:** the learner sorts four new stories into GCF/LCM
correctly and completes the listing without prompting.

**Reteach:** learners who list factors incompletely need the outside-in
pair strategy (1 × n, 2 × …, meeting in the middle) made explicit —
write the pairs, don't just list.

## Lesson 05 — mixed operations problem solving

**Watch for:** missing plan lines and form-switching avoidance (doing
everything in decimals when fractions are cleaner, or vice versa). Score
the plan line separately — it is a UO5/UO6 skill, not garnish.

**Readiness signals:** a two-step problem solved with a written plan,
correct order of operations, and a check (estimate, second form, or
multiplication).

**Reteach:** if multi-step problems collapse, shrink to one new idea per
problem (fraction division *or* decimal work, not both) for a session,
then recombine.

## Assessments

- **Week-08 quiz (S08):** formative. Same-day review; the scoring guide
  in the quiz tells you when to shift the project a session.
- **Unit assessment (S14):** summative for UO5–UO7. Items 3, 8, 10, and
  12 carry the explanation weight — a learner who computes perfectly but
  cannot explain (item 3) or diagnose (item 10) has a fragile UO5.
- **Project (S09–S11, S15):** the transfer check. Use the rubric in
  [project.md](project.md).

## Differentiation

- **Support:** let the learner keep a "division dictionary" (question
  type → example → picture) through the whole unit; allow the
  deterministic SVG and fraction bars on the assessment's Part A.
- **Extension:** the GCF × LCM = product conjecture (Lesson 04), trap
  problems (Lesson 05), and the project's Task C/I explanations at full
  paragraph length.

## Pacing notes

- If S06–S07 run long, the games compress to 15-minute warm-ups rather
  than full sessions — never cut S13 (error-analysis review).
- The project needs its three sessions; if the quiz (S08) forces a
  reteach, borrow from S12's game time, not from the project.
