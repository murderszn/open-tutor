# Teacher Guide — Unit 03: Integers, Rational Numbers, and the Coordinate Plane

Adult-facing guidance for the unit's five lessons. Every worked solution is
in the [answer key](answer-key.md); this guide explains *how to teach it* —
misconceptions, what to watch, when to reteach, and what "ready" looks like.

## Unit at a glance

| Lesson | Sessions | Objective | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 — Opposite quantities and zero | S01 | UO8 | Sign = direction; number = distance from a stated zero |
| 02 — Opposites and the number line | S02 | UO8, UO9 | Opposite = same distance, other side; −(−a) = a; 0 is its own opposite |
| 03 — Ordering and inequalities as position | S03 | UO10 | < and > are left/right claims, not digit-size claims |
| 04 — Absolute value: distance and magnitude | S04 | UO11 | Order (farther right?) vs magnitude (farther from 0?) are different questions |
| 05 — Four quadrants, reflections, distances | S05 | UO9, UO12 | Signs name quadrants; reflections flip one coordinate; shared-coordinate distances use absolute value |

**Time budget:** 16 × ~45 min. If a lesson's exit check shows fewer than
two-thirds correct, convert the next practice session into a reteach (see
signals below) rather than pushing on.

**Materials that matter most:** a long floor or wall number line, a floor
coordinate grid, two-color counters, and the sea-level illustration for
Lesson 01. The quadrant diagram (`assets/coordinate-plane-diagram.svg`)
is exact — use it whenever a learner's hand-drawn grid gets messy.

## Lesson 01

**Opposite quantities and zero.**

**Teach the idea:** spend the first minutes on stories, not symbols.
Ask "what is zero here?" before any number is written. The sentence
frame — "Zero means ___. Positive means ___. Negative means ___." —
should be spoken for every context before the learner writes a signed
number.

**Likely misconceptions:**

- *Minus as subtraction.* The learner sees −4 and tries to subtract
  something. Fix: ask "subtract from what?" — there is nothing to take
  away; the fish simply *is* 4 below. The sign is a direction label.
- *Unsigned answers.* The learner writes "4 m" for the fish. Fix: ask
  "4 above or 4 below?" until the sign is offered unprompted.
- *Zero as "nothing happened."* A $0 balance after +$25 and −$25 is
  not "nothing" — it is two real movements that cancel. Fix: retell the
  movements, then name the balance.

**Watch for:** the learner who can write −4 but cannot say what 0 is.
That learner has memorized a notation, not the idea — return to the
table in the lesson and fill it orally for a new context (floors,
scores, game points) before moving on.

## Lesson 02

**Opposites and the number line.**

**Teach the idea:** walk the line. Physically stepping from 0 to 3 and
then to −3 makes "same distance, other side" bodily obvious in a way
symbols do not. For fractions, always split the step on paper first —
never plot a quarter by eye.

**Likely misconceptions:**

- *Rounding toward zero.* −2.5 plotted between −2 and −1, as if the
  ".5" pulled it back toward zero. Fix: the two-step read — sign first
  (which side of 0), then size (how far). "Negative, so left; two and a
  half steps."
- *Fraction sign blindness.* −3/4 plotted on the positive side because
  "3/4 is positive." Fix: the sign belongs to the whole fraction;
  cover the numerator and ask which side first.
- *−(−a) confusion.* The learner reads −(−3) as "negative negative
  three" and answers −3. Fix: "the opposite of the opposite" in words,
  then symbols; flip twice, return home.

**Reteach signal:** if the learner cannot place −1/4, −2.5, and 3 on
one line without help, do not start Lesson 03 — ordering on a line the
learner cannot plot is guesswork.

## Lesson 03

**Ordering and inequalities as position.**

**Teach the idea:** every comparison gets a position word before a
symbol. "−5 is left of −2, so −5 < −2." The symbol is the shorthand;
the position sentence is the reasoning. Insist on it for at least the
first two practice sets.

**Likely misconceptions:**

- *The digit-size trap.* −5 "bigger" than −2 because 5 > 2. This is the
  unit's most persistent error and it returns in Lesson 04 disguised
  as a magnitude question. Fix: plot, point, and say "left means
  smaller" — every single time, until the learner says it first.
- *Symbol direction.* The learner writes the inequality backwards
  (opens toward the smaller number). Fix: the alligator/crocodile
  image is fine if it helps, but anchor it to position: the open end
  faces the number farther right.
- *Story/number conflation.* "−$45 is a bigger debt, so −45 > −10."
  Fix: two sentences, always — one for the number claim, one for the
  story claim. They can point in opposite directions, and that is the
  lesson.

**Reteach signal:** if the learner orders positives correctly but
reverses every negative pair, the number line itself is not yet the
authority — go back to Lesson 02's floor line before reteaching
symbols.

## Lesson 04

**Absolute value: distance and magnitude.**

**Teach the idea:** start with footsteps, not bars. Count steps from 0
to the number; the count is the absolute value. Only then introduce the
| | notation. The debt story (a balance of −$30 has size $30) should be
told in the learner's own words before any computation.

**Likely misconceptions:**

- *|−a| = −a.* The learner keeps the sign inside the bars. Fix:
  "bars ask how far, not which side — can a distance be negative?"
- *Answering the wrong question.* Given two debts, the learner answers
  the order question when magnitude was asked, or vice versa. Fix: the
  "O:" / "M:" habit — label the question (order or magnitude) before
  computing anything.
- *"Absolute value makes it positive, so it's better."* Magnitude is
  not a value judgment. A $90 debt is not "better" than a $40 debt
  because 90 > 40 — it is bigger. Keep story judgments out of the
  computation.

**Reteach signal:** if the learner can compute |−7| = 7 but cannot say
whether a balance less than −$30 is a bigger or smaller debt, the
magnitude idea has not landed — reteach with the two-accounts example
from the lesson, not with more computation.

## Lesson 05

**Four quadrants, reflections, distances.**

**Teach the idea:** read every ordered pair as a walk ("3 right, 2 up")
and act it on a floor grid before marking paper. Reflections should be
mirrored physically across a taped axis at least once — the body learns
which coordinate flips before the hand does.

**Likely misconceptions:**

- *Coordinate swap.* Plotting (2, 3) for (3, 2). Fix: "x, then y;
  across, then up" — chanted, pointed, and walked until automatic.
- *Flipping the wrong coordinate.* Reflecting across the x-axis by
  flipping x. Fix: the mirror anchor — a floor mirror (x-axis) flips
  you vertically, so y changes; a wall mirror (y-axis) flips you
  horizontally, so x changes.
- *Negative distances.* Reporting "−7 units" for a distance. Fix:
  distance is a length; if subtraction gives a negative, take the
  absolute value — or subtract smaller from larger.
- *Axis points in quadrants.* (0, 4) called "Quadrant I" or "Quadrant
  II." Fix: quadrants need two nonzero signs; a zero coordinate puts
  the point on an axis, in no quadrant.

**Reteach signal:** if the learner plots correctly but reflects
wrongly, the issue is the mirror mapping, not plotting — reteach with
the floor mirror, not with more plotting drills.

## Quiz, assessment, project

**Week-12 quiz (S08):** target 8 of 10. Misses on items 4, 5, or 9
(order/magnitude/reflection basics) mean Lessons 03–04 need a reteach
session before the project — use Practice Sets B and C, not new
material. A miss only on item 10 (the digits trap in words) is a
language fix: have the learner restate the comparison as a position
sentence.

**Unit assessment (S14):** target 10 of 12 for readiness for Unit 04.
Below 8, reteach the missed objectives specifically: UO8–UO9 misses →
Lessons 01–02; UO10–UO11 misses → Lessons 03–04; UO12 misses → Lesson 05
plus Practice Set C. Do not reteach the whole unit.

**Project (S09–S11, S15):** there is no single correct map — check
relationships, not feature choices: quadrant signs correct, reflections
flip exactly one coordinate, distances use absolute-value subtraction,
legend zeros stated, order and magnitude statements distinguished.
Score with the rubric in `project.md` (13–15 ready; 9–12 revise one
criterion; below 9 rework with adult support). A map with no sharing
pair is incomplete by design — the learner must move a feature, not
skip the distance work.

## Readiness for Unit 04

The learner is ready for expressions and variables when they can, without
prompting: name the zero in a new opposite-quantity context; plot a
rational number on a line; order a mixed set including negatives;
separate an order claim from a magnitude claim in one breath; and plot
and reflect a point in any quadrant. If reflections or the order/
magnitude distinction are still shaky, they will resurface as sign
errors in Unit 04 — fix them here, while they are still the lesson.
