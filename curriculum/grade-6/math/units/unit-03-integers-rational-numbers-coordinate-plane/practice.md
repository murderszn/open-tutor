# Practice — Unit 03: Integers, Rational Numbers, and the Coordinate Plane

**Sessions:** S06 (Set A) · S07 (Set B) · S12 (Set C) · S13 (Sets D and E)

Work with a number line and a coordinate grid beside you. Show position
reasoning (left/right, above/below) before you write a symbol. Solutions
are in the separate [answer key](answer-key.md) — attempt first, then
check.

## Set A — Opposite quantities and number lines (UO8, UO9)

A1. Write the opposite of each: 9, −9, 0, 3.5, −2/3.
A2. Plot on a horizontal line from −6 to 6: −6, −1.5, 0.75, 4, −0.25.
    Then list them left to right (least to greatest).
A3. A lift serves floors from −3 (basements) to 6. (a) Plot floors −2
    and 4 on a vertical line. (b) What is the opposite of −2? (c) What
    is −(−2)?
A4. True or false, with a reason: (a) −(−5) = 5. (b) The opposite of 0
    is 1. (c) −4 and 4 are opposites. (d) 2.5 has no opposite.
A5. A submarine sits at −60 meters and a bird flies at +60 meters above
    the same sea level. Are their positions opposites? Explain with
    distance from sea level.
A6. Fill in: the opposite of −13 is ___; −(−13) = ___; the opposite of
    0 is ___.
A7. Plot −5/4, 7/4, and −1/2 on one number line. (a) Between which two
    integers does each sit? (b) Which is farthest from 0?
A8. **Error analysis:** Leo says "−2.5 sits between −2 and −1, because
    2.5 rounds to 2." What is wrong with that reasoning, and where does
    −2.5 really sit?

## Set B — Ordering and absolute value (UO10, UO11)

B1. Write < or > to make each true: (a) −7 ___ −3; (b) −1.25 ___ −1.5;
    (c) 0 ___ −8; (d) −4/5 ___ −3/5.
B2. Order from least to greatest: 3, −5, 0, −5.5, 2.
B3. Find |−12|, |5|, |−0.5|, and |0|. Which original number has the
    greatest magnitude?
B4. Two fictional balances: −$70 and −$40. (a) Which balance is
    greater? (b) Which debt is greater? (c) Write one inequality for
    each answer.
B5. A fictional station reports −15 °C, −8 °C, and 3 °C. (a) Which
    reading is warmest? Coldest? (b) Is −8 °C > −15 °C true? Explain
    with position.
B6. True or false, with a counterexample if false: "If a < b, then
    |a| < |b|."
B7. Order these by magnitude, smallest to largest: |−6|, |3|, |−1|,
    |0|, |−10|.
B8. A fictional account starts at −$55. A deposit of $55 arrives, then
    a withdrawal of $20. (a) What is the balance after the deposit?
    (b) What is the balance after the withdrawal? (c) What is the
    magnitude of the final debt? (d) Is the final balance greater than
    the starting balance? Explain with an inequality.

## Set C — Quadrants, reflections, distances (UO9, UO12)

C1. Name the quadrant (or axis) for each point: (6, −2), (−6, 3),
    (−1, −1), (0, −4), (7, 0), (−2, 5).
C2. Start at P(−5, 3). Write its reflection across the x-axis, across
    the y-axis, and across both axes — with the quadrant of each result.
C3. Find each distance, showing the absolute-value subtraction:
    (a) (3, 8) and (3, −5); (b) (−6, 2) and (1, 2); (c) (0, −4) and
    (0, 4).
C4. Points A(2, 7), B(2, −1), C(−4, −1). (a) Find AB and BC. (b) AB is
    vertical and BC is horizontal. What kind of angle do they form at
    B? (c) Name the quadrant (or axis) of each point.
C5. A fictional town map places a library at (−3, 4) and a school at
    (−3, −2). (a) How far apart are they, in map units? (b) Reflect
    the school across the x-axis. Where is it now, and in which
    quadrant?
C6. **Error analysis:** Sam reflects (4, −1) across the y-axis and
    writes (4, 1). What did Sam flip wrongly, and what is the correct
    reflection?
C7. A path visits (0, 0), then (4, 0), then (4, 3), then (0, 3). (a)
    Name the quadrant (or axis) of each stop after the origin.
    (b) Which two stops share an x-coordinate, and how far apart are
    they? (c) Which two share a y-coordinate, and how far apart are
    they?
C8. Start at (1, −2). Reflect it across the x-axis; reflect that result
    across the y-axis. Then reflect the original point across both
    axes. Compare the two final points. What does this show?

## Set D — Mixed review (UO8–UO12)

D1. A lift starts at floor 0, rises 5 floors, then descends 8 floors.
    (a) Write each movement as a signed number. (b) What floor does it
    end on, as a signed number? (c) In words, what does that floor
    mean?
D2. Order from least to greatest: −2.25, −0.75, 1.5, 0, −3.
D3. Find |−4.5|. Then write two true sentences: one comparing −4.5
    and 4 by order, one comparing them by magnitude.
D4. Plot (−2.5, 3.5). (a) Which quadrant? (b) Reflect it across the
    x-axis; name the new point and quadrant. (c) How far apart are the
    original and the reflected point?
D5. Write −12 < −5 in words as a temperature comparison. Is −12 °C
    warmer or colder than −5 °C? Explain.
D6. Find the distance between (−7, 4) and (2, 4). Show your subtraction.
D7. Find −(−0.8). On a number line, between which two integers does
    −0.8 sit?
D8. Three fictional balances: −$90, $30, −$15. (a) Order them from
    least to greatest. (b) Which is the largest debt? (c) Order the
    three magnitudes from smallest to largest.

## Set E — Challenge and error analysis (UO8–UO12)

E1. Find a rational number strictly between −1/3 and 0. Prove it with
    a number line and two inequalities.
E2. Can two different numbers have the same absolute value without
    being opposites? Answer, and prove it or explain why it is
    impossible.
E3. Points P(1, 2) and Q(4, 6) share neither coordinate. (a) Why does
    this lesson's distance rule not apply directly? (b) How far apart
    are they horizontally? Vertically? (c) This unit does not compute
    diagonal distances — say why in one sentence.
E4. **Error analysis:** "−20 °C is warmer than −10 °C, because 20 is
    greater than 10." Identify the two questions being confused, and
    give the correct comparison both ways (order and magnitude).
E5. A rectangle has corners (2, 1), (−2, 1), (−2, −3), (2, −3).
    Reflect the whole rectangle across the y-axis. List the new
    corners. What do you notice, and why?
E6. **Create:** Choose three points, each in a different quadrant, so
    that two of them form a vertical leg of length 5 and two of them
    form a horizontal leg of length 4. List your points with quadrants
    and show both distances. (More than one answer works.)
