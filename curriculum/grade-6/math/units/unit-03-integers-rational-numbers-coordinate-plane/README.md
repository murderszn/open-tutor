# Unit 03 — Integers, Rational Numbers, and the Coordinate Plane

**Grade 6 Mathematics · Weeks 11–14 · 16 sessions (~45 minutes each, 4 per week)**

**Standards:** 6.NS.C.5, 6.NS.C.6.a–c, 6.NS.C.7.a–d, 6.NS.C.8

**Issue:** [#30](https://github.com/murderszn/open-tutor/issues/30)
(section U03 — draft delivery; not educator-reviewed)

## Overview

Unit 01 built ratio reasoning and Unit 02 built fraction and decimal
computation. This unit builds the **number line itself**: it extends the
numbers the learner already knows — whole numbers, fractions, and decimals —
to include their opposites, and then extends the coordinate plane the
learner met in grade 5 from one quadrant to all four. Week 11 starts with
**opposite quantities** (temperature above and below zero, elevation above
and below sea level, credits and debits) and the question every new signed
number must answer: *what does 0 mean here?* Week 12 turns to **order and
magnitude**: inequalities as statements about position on the number line,
and absolute value as distance from 0 — two ideas that must never be
confused, because −7 < 4 even though |−7| > |4|. Week 13 opens **all four
quadrants**: plotting ordered pairs with negative coordinates, reflections
across the axes, and distances between points that share a coordinate.
Week 14 is review, the four-quadrant park-map project, and the culminating
assessment.

Nothing here assumes Unit 02 beyond fraction and decimal fluency: the
rational numbers plotted in this unit (for example −2.5, −3/4, 1/2) are
numbers the learner can already read, write, and compare as positives.
Unit 02 itself is mentioned in prose only — its files live in a separate
draft (issue #30, section U02) and are not linked from this unit.

## Essential question

*When a quantity can point in two opposite directions, how do we name it,
place it, compare it, and measure how far it is from nothing?*

## Prerequisites (re-checked in Lessons 01–02)

- Whole-number, fraction, and decimal place value; reading and writing
  fractions and decimals (including halves, quarters, and tenths)
- Comparing positive fractions and decimals; ordering positive numbers
- The Quadrant I coordinate plane from grade 5: ordered pairs (x, y),
  the x-axis (horizontal) and y-axis (vertical), the origin (0, 0)
- Fraction and decimal computation from Unit 02 (used only for checking
  distances and reading coordinates, never as new content here)

Sessions 01–02 re-check positive-number ordering and Quadrant I plotting
before any negative coordinate is introduced. The diagnostic weeks
(Weeks 1–2) baselined these before Unit 01.

## Vocabulary

integer · rational number · opposite · positive · negative · zero (0) ·
credit · debit · elevation · sea level · number line · coordinate plane ·
ordered pair · x-coordinate · y-coordinate · x-axis · y-axis · origin ·
quadrant · reflect / reflection · inequality · absolute value · distance ·
magnitude

(Vocabulary is taught with objects and drawings first, the word second;
home-language labels are welcomed alongside English terms. "Opposite" is
introduced as "the same distance from 0, on the other side" — the opposite
of 3 is −3, and the opposite of −3 is 3.)

## Verified standards / source notes

Reference framework: **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics**,
Grade 6, The Number System, as published at
[thecorestandards.org/Math](https://thecorestandards.org/Math) (Grade 6 NS
page at <https://thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/6/NS/>; codes and
descriptions re-verified 2026-10-25 for this unit). Exact official wording
used:

- **6.NS.C.5** — "Understand that positive and negative numbers are used
  together to describe quantities having opposite directions or values
  (e.g., temperature above/below zero, elevation above/below sea level,
  credits/debits, positive/negative electric charge); use positive and
  negative numbers to represent quantities in real-world contexts,
  explaining the meaning of 0 in each situation."
- **6.NS.C.6** — "Understand a rational number as a point on the number
  line. Extend number line diagrams and coordinate axes familiar from
  previous grades to represent points on the line and in the plane with
  negative number coordinates."
  - **6.NS.C.6.a** — "Recognize opposite signs of numbers as indicating
    locations on opposite sides of 0 on the number line; recognize that
    the opposite of the opposite of a number is the number itself, e.g.,
    -(-3) = 3, and that 0 is its own opposite."
  - **6.NS.C.6.b** — "Understand signs of numbers in ordered pairs as
    indicating locations in quadrants of the coordinate plane; recognize
    that when two ordered pairs differ only by signs, the locations of the
    points are related by reflections across one or both axes."
  - **6.NS.C.6.c** — "Find and position integers and other rational
    numbers on a horizontal or vertical number line diagram; find and
    position pairs of integers and other rational numbers on a coordinate
    plane."
- **6.NS.C.7.a** — "Interpret statements of inequality as statements
  about the relative position of two numbers on a number line diagram.
  *For example, interpret -3 > -7 as a statement that -3 is located to
  the right of -7 on a number line oriented from left to right.*"
- **6.NS.C.7.b** — "Write, interpret, and explain statements of order
  for rational numbers in real-world contexts. *For example, write
  -3 °C > -7 °C to express the fact that -3 °C is warmer than -7 °C.*"
- **6.NS.C.7.c** — "Understand the absolute value of a rational number
  as its distance from 0 on the number line; interpret absolute value as
  magnitude for a positive or negative quantity in a real-world situation.
  *For example, for an account balance of -30 dollars, write |-30| = 30
  to describe the size of the debt in dollars.*"
- **6.NS.C.7.d** — "Distinguish comparisons of absolute value from
  statements about order. *For example, recognize that an account balance
  less than -30 dollars represents a debt greater than 30 dollars.*"
- **6.NS.C.8** — "Solve real-world and mathematical problems by graphing
  points in all four quadrants of the coordinate plane. Include use of
  coordinates and absolute value to find distances between points with
  the same first coordinate or the same second coordinate."

No state adoption, accreditation, or complete-alignment claim is made.
All place names, temperatures, elevations, and account balances in this
unit are fictional practice data unless a task says otherwise; the
sea-level illustration is an AI-generated story prompt, not a photograph
or a measurement.
