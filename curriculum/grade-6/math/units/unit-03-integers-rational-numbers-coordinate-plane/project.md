# Project — Design a Four-Quadrant Park Map

**Sessions:** S09 (Part 1) · S10 (Part 2) · S11 (Part 3) · S15 (presentations)

**Objectives:** UO8, UO9, UO10, UO11, UO12 · **Standards:** 6.NS.C.5,
6.NS.C.6.a–c, 6.NS.C.7.a–d, 6.NS.C.8

## The task

You are designing a park for a fictional town. The park map is a
coordinate plane with both axes running from −6 to 6. Your job is to
place the park's features, describe them with signed numbers, and prove
every placement with coordinates, reflections, and distances. All place
names are invented; no real park is being mapped.

## Materials

Coordinate grids from −6 to 6 on both axes (at least two copies),
paper, pencil, ruler, colored pencils (optional).

## Part 1 — Points in all four quadrants (S09)

1. Choose and place **at least 8 features**, with:
   - at least one feature in **each** of the four quadrants;
   - at least one feature **on an axis** (not in any quadrant);
   - no two features sharing the same point.
2. Name each feature (for example: pond, playground, oak tree, gate,
   picnic shelter, bridge, garden, fountain) and record its ordered
   pair in a table with its quadrant (or axis).
3. Write one sentence for each quadrant explaining how you know its
   features belong there, using the signs of the coordinates.

## Part 2 — Reflections and distances (S10)

4. Choose one feature and reflect it across the **x-axis**. Record the
   original point, the reflected point, and the quadrant of each.
   Explain which coordinate changed and why.
5. Choose a different feature and reflect it across the **y-axis**.
   Record the same information.
6. Choose two features that share an **x-coordinate** and find the
   distance between them with an absolute-value subtraction. Then
   choose two features that share a **y-coordinate** and do the same.
   Show both subtractions; if your map has no sharing pair, move one
   feature (and update Part 1) until it does — sharing pairs are
   required, not optional.
7. Draw both distance segments on your map in a different color and
   label each with its length in map units.

## Part 3 — Signed-number legend (S11)

8. Add a **legend** that uses signed numbers in three contexts:
   - an **elevation** feature (for example, a hill at +3 m and a pond
     bottom at −2 m, relative to the park's ground level, 0);
   - a **temperature** note (for example, a winter low of −4 °C and a
     summer high of 28 °C — or your own fictional readings);
   - an **account** note (for example, the park fund's balance after a
     $50 donation and a $75 repair bill — or your own fictional
     figures, worked out with signs).
9. For each context, state what **0 means** in one sentence.
10. Write two comparison sentences: one **order** statement (using <
    or >) and one **magnitude** statement (using | |) drawn from your
    legend numbers — and in one sentence each, say what each statement
    means in the story.
11. **Peer check:** swap maps with a partner (or present to your adult).
    Your partner checks: every quadrant filled, one axis feature,
    both reflections correct, both distances shown with subtraction,
    legend zeros stated. Fix anything they catch before presenting.

## Deliverables

1. The finished park map (grid, features plotted and labeled, both
   distance segments drawn and labeled).
2. The feature table (name, ordered pair, quadrant/axis).
3. The reflection and distance work (Parts 2, items 4–7, with
   subtractions shown).
4. The signed-number legend with zeros, the order statement, and the
   magnitude statement (Part 3, items 8–10).
5. A short presentation (S15, about 3 minutes): walk the audience
   through one quadrant, one reflection, and one distance on your map.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Full credit (3) | Partial credit (2) | Needs work (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quadrant coverage (UO9) | 8+ features; every quadrant filled; one axis feature; table matches the map | 6–7 features, or one quadrant empty, or table has 1–2 errors | Fewer than 6 features, or several quadrants empty, or table does not match the map |
| Reflections (UO9) | Both reflections correct with quadrants; the changed coordinate explained | One reflection correct, or explanations missing/wrong | Both reflections wrong, or no explanation |
| Distances (UO12) | Both sharing pairs found; absolute-value subtractions shown and correct; segments drawn and labeled | One distance correct, or a subtraction missing, or a segment unlabeled | Distances wrong or missing, or no sharing pairs |
| Signed-number legend (UO8, UO10, UO11) | Three contexts present; every zero stated; order and magnitude statements both correct and explained in story terms | One context or statement missing, or a zero unstated | Legend missing, or order confused with magnitude |
| Presentation and care (all) | Map legible and labeled; presentation covers a quadrant, a reflection, and a distance | Map partly legible, or presentation misses one element | Map illegible/unlabeled, or no presentation |

**Scoring:** 13–15 points = ready to move on; 9–12 = revise one
criterion with your adult and re-present that part; below 9 = rework
the map with adult support before the assessment. Scores guide
teaching; they are not a standardized or validated measure.

## Adult notes

- The map is learner-designed, so there is no single correct answer:
  check the *relationships* (quadrant signs, flipped coordinates,
  distance subtractions), not the feature choices.
- If a learner cannot find a sharing pair, suggest moving a feature
  vertically or horizontally rather than redrawing the whole map.
- Watch for the two classic confusions: flipping the wrong coordinate
  in a reflection, and reporting a negative distance. Both are
  reteach signals for Lessons 05 and 04 respectively.
