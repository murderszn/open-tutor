# Unit Assessment — Unit 01: Ratios, Unit Rates, and Ratio Reasoning

**Session S16** · 16 items · all objectives UO1–UO4 ·
[answer key](../answer-key.md#unit-assessment) is separate — do not show it
to the learner. Target: 12 of 16 correct; every rate and conversion must
carry units. This assessment is a practice instrument, not a standardized
or validated test.

## Section 1 — Ratio language and equivalent ratios (UO1)

1. A fruit stand sells 9 melons for every 4 pineapples sold. (a) Write the
   ratio of melons to pineapples. (b) Write the ratio of pineapples to the
   total fruit sold that day (melons + pineapples). (c) Explain why (a) and
   "4:9 melons to pineapples" are different claims.
2. Complete the ratio table for 4:9 (cement to sand) through scale factor 5.
   Circle the row you would use to find how much sand goes with 16 cups of
   cement, and write that amount.
3. On a double number line for the 4:9 mixture (cement line: 0, 4, 8, 12,
   16, 20; sand line: 0, 9, 18, 27, 36, 45), what value on the sand line
   aligns with 20 on the cement line? Show how the scale factor confirms it.
4. **Error analysis:** "To scale 4:9 by 5 I added 5 to each part: 9:14."
   (a) Is 9:14 equivalent to 4:9? Show a check. (b) What is the correct
   scale-factor-5 ratio?

## Section 2 — Unit rates (UO2)

5. A 12-pack of sparkling water costs $6.60 and an 8-pack costs $4.80
   (fictional practice prices). (a) Find the price per can for each.
   (b) Which pack is the better buy? Justify with the unit prices.
6. A cyclist rides 66 km in 3 hours at constant speed. (a) Find the speed in
   km/h. (b) How long will a 154-km ride take at that speed? Check by
   multiplying back.
7. A factory makes 450 toys in 6 hours. (a) Find toys per hour. (b) At that
   rate, how many toys in a 40-hour week? (c) A supervisor claims "75 toys
   per hour means 75 hours per toy." Correct the supervisor.

## Section 3 — Percent as a rate per 100 (UO3)

8. Shade reasoning on paper, then compute: 25% of 96; 60% of 45; 10% of
   250. Show the "out of 100" work for at least one.
9. A school has 800 students; 35% ride the bus. How many ride the bus? Build
   the percent ratio table (0 / 35 / 100) to solve it.
10. **Explain your reasoning:** "30% of a quantity means 30/100 times the
    quantity." Use this meaning to explain why 30% of 50 must be less than
    30, without computing the exact value.

## Section 4 — Unit conversion by ratio reasoning (UO4)

11. Convert 5 yards to inches. Show the conversion ratio and the unit
    factor with units canceling.
12. A water bottle holds 1.5 liters. How many milliliters? (1 L : 1,000 mL)
13. A road race is 13.1 miles (a half marathon). About how many feet is
    that? (1 mi : 5,280 ft) Show the unit-factor setup.
14. **Error analysis:** "2 hours = 2 × 60 = 120 minutes, so 120 minutes =
    120 × 60 = 7,200 hours." The first conversion is right. (a) What went
    wrong in the second? (b) Show the correct unit-factor setup for
    120 minutes → hours.

## Section 5 — Integrated reasoning (all objectives)

15. Amara's stand (fictional practice data): her recipe is 2:3 (concentrate
    to water). She buys concentrate at $0.08 per ounce and uses 8 oz of
    concentrate per batch-equivalent serving set of 5 cups. (a) What is the
    ratio of concentrate to the whole 5-cup batch? (b) What does the $0.08
    per ounce rate tell her that "$0.08" alone does not? (c) If she doubles
    the recipe, which quantities change and which relationship stays the
    same?
16. **Reflection (2–3 sentences):** This unit taught four tools — ratio
    language, equivalent ratios, unit rates, and percent/conversion by ratio
    reasoning. Pick the tool that felt *most* powerful to you and describe
    one real situation (outside math class) where you would actually use it.
