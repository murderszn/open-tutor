# Project — Amara's Lemonade Stand: A Ratio Investigation

**Sessions:** S14 (workshop) + S15 (finish and review) · **Objectives:**
UO1–UO4 · **Standards:** 6.RP.A.1, 6.RP.A.2, 6.RP.A.3.a–d

**Story:** Amara is opening the lemonade stand from the unit illustration
for a neighborhood fair. She hires *you* as her ratio consultant. Your job:
scale her recipe fairly, price her supplies by unit rate, convert her
measurements, and present a stand poster she can actually use.

All supplier numbers below are **fictional practice data** (not real store
prices); the math is real.

## Part 1 — Scale the recipe fairly (UO1)

Amara's recipe: **2 cups concentrate : 3 cups water** (one 5-cup batch).

1. The fair expects 60 customers, each served one 5-cup batch's worth of
   lemonade (5 cups per customer). How many batches is that, and what are
   the concentrate and water totals? Build a ratio table to the needed row
   and name the scale factor.
2. Amara's cousin suggests "just add 58 cups to each part" (2:3 → 60:61).
   Show with a tape diagram or the table why this breaks the taste, and
   state the correct totals from #1.
3. Draw a double number line for the recipe (concentrate 0–120, water
   0–180) and mark where 60 batches lands. Read the totals from the line
   and confirm they match #1.

*Check: 60 batches = scale factor 60 → 120 cups concentrate, 180 cups
water; 60 + 60 = 120 cups is wrong for concentrate (60:61 ≠ 2:3).*

## Part 2 — Buy smart: unit pricing (UO2)

Two suppliers sell lemon concentrate (fictional prices):

- Sunny Supply: 96-oz jug for **$7.68**
- Citrus Co.: 64-oz jug for **$5.76**

4. Compute the unit price (dollars per ounce) for each. Which should Amara
   buy? Show the division with units.
5. Amara needs 120 cups of concentrate. Note 1 cup = 8 oz. How many ounces
   is that? At the winning unit price, what is her total concentrate cost?
6. Write a two-sentence "supplier recommendation" for Amara's poster,
   naming the unit prices as evidence.

*Check: $7.68 ÷ 96 = $0.08/oz; $5.76 ÷ 64 = $0.09/oz → Sunny Supply;
120 cups × 8 = 960 oz; 960 × $0.08 = $76.80.*

## Part 3 — Convert for the crew (UO4)

7. Amara's banner is 12 feet long. Convert to inches for the sign maker.
   Show the unit factor with units canceling.
8. Prep time is 45 minutes. Write it as a fraction of an hour for the
   schedule. (1 h : 60 min)
9. Each serving cup holds 10 oz. Amara's 5-cup batch is how many ounces?
   (1 cup : 8 oz) — and does that match "5 cups per customer" sensibly?

*Check: 144 in; 3/4 h; 40 oz per batch — a 40-oz serving is unrealistic,
which is exactly the point of #10's reflection.*

## Part 4 — Percent check (UO3)

10. At last year's fair, 40% of 150 visitors bought lemonade. How many
    buyers is that? Then reflect honestly in 2–3 sentences: your Part 1
    math serves 60 batches × 5 cups — but #9 shows a "serving" of 40 oz is
    far more than anyone drinks. What does this tell you about checking
    *reasonableness*, not just correctness, in a real project?

*Check: 60 buyers.*

## Deliverables

1. **Ratio table + double number line** (Parts 1–3 work shown, units on
   every number)
2. **Supplier recommendation card** (Part 2: both unit prices, the pick,
   total cost, 2-sentence justification)
3. **Crew conversion poster** (Part 3: three conversions with canceling
   units + one sentence each on why you multiplied or divided)
4. **Reflection paragraph** (Part 4: the percent computation + the
   reasonableness reflection)

## Rubric

| Criterion | 3 — Strong | 2 — Solid | 1 — Developing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ratio reasoning (Parts 1, 3) | All rows correct, scale factors named, additive trap refuted with model | Totals correct, factor named, trap identified without model | Totals wrong or scaling inconsistent |
| Unit rates & pricing (Part 2) | Both unit prices correct with units, pick justified, total cost correct | Rates correct, pick correct, cost has minor error | Rates wrong or pick unjustified |
| Conversions (Part 3) | All three correct with units shown canceling/transforming | Correct numbers, units partially shown | Direction errors (× vs ÷) |
| Percent & reflection (Part 4) | Percent correct; reflection names reasonableness explicitly with the 40-oz evidence | Percent correct; reflection generic | Percent wrong or no reflection |
| Communication | Poster/card readable by a stranger; every number has units | Mostly clear; a few missing units | Unclear or units missing throughout |

**Scoring:** 13–15 = exceeding · 10–12 = meeting · 7–9 = approaching ·
below 7 = revisit with the teacher guide's reteach plan.

## Materials and safety

Paper, rulers, grid paper, calculators (check only after written method),
the unit illustration (story prompt). No real purchasing, no real prices,
no food handling required — this is a paper investigation; if the family
actually makes lemonade, an adult supervises (hot water, knives, and
allergen checks stay adult jobs).
