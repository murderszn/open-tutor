## Game 1: Ratio Table Relay

**Session S04.** **Setup:** teams of 2; each team gets a blank 6-row ratio table and a
starting ratio card (e.g., 3:5, 2:7, 4:3).

**Play:** the adult calls a scale factor (2, 3, 4, 5, 10 — in any order).
The team writes the next row: both parts scaled. First team to finish the
row *and say the factor aloud* scores a point; a wrong row loses the point.

**Win condition:** most points after 5 rounds. **Success criterion:**
4 of 5 rounds correct with factors named aloud.

**Debrief (required):** each team explains one round's row — "we scaled
both parts by ___, so ___:___ is equivalent to ___:___." Additive rows
(e.g., 3:5 → 5:7) are caught and corrected by the opposing team for a
bonus point.

## Session S05/S07 — Equivalent ratios and rates (guided practice)

1. Complete the table for 5:2 through scale factor 6.
2. Find the missing values: (a) 3:8 = 12:___, (b) 7:4 = ___:28,
   (c) 9:6 = 3:___.
3. Draw a tape diagram for 3:8 and show the scale-factor-2 version. Name
   the equivalent ratio.
4. On a double number line for 5:2 (line 1: 0, 5, 10, 15; line 2: 0, 2, 4,
   6), what aligns with 15 on line 1?
5. 9 pens cost $6.30. Price per pen? Check by multiplying back.
6. A hiker walks 14 km in 4 hours. Speed in km/h? Distance in 10 hours at
   the same pace?

## Game 2: Price-per-Unit Hunt

**Session S07.** **Setup:** the adult prepares 8–10 offer cards from a real flyer or a
verified list (prices must be adult-verified; the offers below are fictional
practice data): e.g., "24 oz peanut butter $4.80", "16 oz peanut butter
$3.52", "12-pack soda $7.20", "6-pack soda $4.20".

**Play:** learners draw two competing cards for the *same product*, compute
both unit prices with units, and declare the better buy with evidence. Each
correct call with labeled units scores a point; a correct number with
missing units scores half.

**Win condition:** most points after 5 comparisons. **Success criterion:**
4 of 5 better-buys chosen with unit-price evidence.

**Debrief (required):** "When is the cheaper total price *not* the better
buy?" — each learner answers with one example from the game.

## Session S10 — Percent practice (guided, UO3)

1. Shade grids for 65% and 5%; write each as a fraction and decimal.
2. Find: (a) 10% of 90, (b) 50% of 74, (c) 25% of 160, (d) 75% of 160.
3. 12% of 50 = ? Show the fraction-of-100 work and a ratio-table check.
4. **Error analysis:** "45% of 200 = 45 × 200 = 9,000." Fix it and name the
   forgotten meaning.

## Session S12 — Conversion practice (guided, UO4)

1. 6 ft = ___ in; 96 in = ___ ft. Show units canceling.
2. 4.5 L = ___ mL; 800 g = ___ kg.
3. Amara's cooler holds 5 gallons. How many cups? (1 gal : 16 cups)
4. A race is 10 km. How many meters? A 2-hour movie: how many minutes?

## Session S13 — Mixed review (all objectives)

1. A smoothie recipe is 3:2 (fruit:yogurt). For 15 cups of fruit, how much
   yogurt? Name the scale factor.
2. 18 oz cereal costs $4.50. Find the price per ounce. A 12-oz box costs
   $3.24 — which is the better buy?
3. Find 30% of 140.
4. Convert: 2.5 miles to feet (1 mi : 5,280 ft). Show the unit factor.
5. **Explain your reasoning:** A friend says "equivalent ratios are just
   fractions that reduce to the same thing." What is right about this, and
   what does it miss about ratios (hint: think about 3:5 describing a
   mixture vs 3/5 of a pizza)?

## Mixed set — extra challenge (optional)

1. A recipe ratio 2:3:1 (oats:cereal:raisins) makes 30 cups total. How many
   cups of each? *(Hint: how many "parts" in the whole?)*
2. Store A: 20 oz for $5.00. Store B: 28 oz for $6.72. Store C: 12 oz for
   $3.12. Rank the three by unit price.
3. A car uses 12 gallons to travel 360 miles. (a) Find mpg. (b) How many
   gallons for a 150-mile trip? (c) Explain which division answers (b).
