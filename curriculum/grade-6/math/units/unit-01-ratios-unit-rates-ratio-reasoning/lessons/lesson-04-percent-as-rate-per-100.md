# Lesson 04 — Percent as a Rate per 100

**My goal:** I can read a percent as "that many out of 100" and find a
percent of a quantity using fraction reasoning and ratio tables.

**Time:** about 45 minutes (5 readiness · 15 teaching · 10 guided ·
12 independent · 3 exit).

**Materials:** 10×10 grid paper (hundredths grids), paper, pencil.

**Standards:** 6.RP.A.3.c (first pass: percent as a rate per 100; finding
the whole given a part and the percent arrives in U05/U08) ·
**Unit objectives:** UO3

## Readiness check (5 minutes)

1. What does the word "percent" sound like it means? (Think: *per* +
   *cent*.)
2. Shade 30 out of 100 squares on a grid. What fraction of the grid is
   shaded? What decimal?
3. In Lesson 02 you built ratio tables. What would a ratio table with a
   100-row let you compare?

*(#1's word root is the whole lesson: "cent" as in century, cents,
centimeter — one hundred.)*

## Explicit teaching

### Percent = a rate per 100

A **percent** is a ratio that compares a quantity to **100**. The symbol %
means "out of 100":

- 30% = **30 out of 100** = 30/100 = 0.30
- 7% = **7 out of 100** = 7/100 = 0.07
- 100% = **100 out of 100** = the whole thing

So "30% of a quantity" means **30/100 times the quantity** — exactly the
standard's wording. Percent is just ratio reasoning with a fixed second
quantity of 100.

### Worked example 1 — shading and the ratio table

**Problem:** Find 30% of 250 students.

**Method A — fraction of 100:**
30% of 250 = 30/100 × 250 = (30 × 250) ÷ 100 = 7,500 ÷ 100 = **75**.
Simplify first if you like: 30/100 = 3/10, and 3/10 × 250 = 750 ÷ 10 = 75.

**Method B — ratio table (percent row):**

| Percent | 0 | 30 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Students | 0 | ? | 250 |

Scale from 100% to 30%: factor 0.3. Apply to the quantity: 250 × 0.3 = 75.
**75 students.**

**Sanity check:** 30% is less than half (50%), and 75 < 125 = half of 250. ✓

### Worked example 2 — a benchmark blend

**Problem:** Find 15% of 60 pencils.

Use the table again — or blend benchmarks:

- 10% of 60 = 6 (one-tenth)
- 5% of 60 = 3 (half of 10%)
- 15% = 10% + 5% → 6 + 3 = **9 pencils**

**Table check:** 15/100 × 60 = 900 ÷ 100 = 9. ✓ Both routes agree — that
agreement is the point. Percent problems are ratio-table problems wearing a
100-row.

**A common error:** writing 15% of 60 as 15 × 60 = 900 and forgetting to
divide by 100. The % sign is *not* decoration — it carries the "÷ 100."
Always ask: *out of 100 what?*

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. Shade a 10×10 grid to show 40%. Write 40% as a fraction and a decimal.
   *(Hint: count shaded squares.)*
2. Find 25% of 80 using the fraction method. *(Hint: 25/100 simplifies.)*
3. Find 10% of 45, then use it to find 20% of 45. *(Hint: double the 10%
   answer.)*
4. Build a percent ratio table (0 / 60 / 100) and use it to find 60% of 35.
   *(Hint: scale factor from 100 to 60.)*

## Independent practice

5. A class has 28 students; 50% walk to school. How many walk? How do you
   know without computing?
6. Find 75% of 120 two ways: (a) as 75/100 of 120, (b) as 3/4 of 120.
   Explain why both give the same answer.
7. **Error analysis:** "30% of 90 is 30 × 90 = 2,700." What went wrong,
   and what is the correct value? Name the meaning of % the learner
   ignored.
8. A store advertises 20% off a $35 shirt. Find the discount amount. (You
   do not need the sale price yet — just the discount.)

## Application

**Stand survey.** Amara surveys 200 passersby: 35% say they would buy
lemonade. How many is that? Show the ratio table (percent row and people
row). Then write one sentence explaining to Amara *why the table method and
the fraction method must agree* — the answer key expects the "same
relationship, 100-row" argument.

*Check: 35/100 × 200 = 7,000 ÷ 100 = 70 people.*

## Exit check

a. Write 17% as "___ out of 100," as a fraction, and as a decimal.
b. Find 40% of 55. Show the fraction-of-100 work.
c. Explain in one sentence why "finding the whole" (e.g., "12 is 30% of
   what number?") is a *different* problem from what you practiced today —
   and predict which tool (tables, scaling backwards) might solve it.
   (U05/U08 will teach it; today you only need to notice the difference.)

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** hundredths grids make "out of 100" visible;
  sentence frame "___% means ___ out of 100, so ___% of ___ is ___."
- **Alternate response mode:** shade grids and point to the counted squares;
  oral table-building with the adult scribing.
- **Extension:** decimal percents — find 17.5% of 80 (hint: 10% + 5% +
  2.5%); prove generally that p% of q equals q% of p using  p/100 × q.

## Teacher note (misconceptions; key reference)

- **The missing ÷ 100** is the signature error; grid shading fixes it
  visually before symbols take over.
- **"Percent bigger than 100"** confusion (e.g., 150%): note it means more
  than the whole — a 150-row extends past the 100-row. Extension only; not
  assessed here.
- **c** in the exit check is a preview, not a taught skill — accept any
  reasonable "we'd have to work backwards" answer.
