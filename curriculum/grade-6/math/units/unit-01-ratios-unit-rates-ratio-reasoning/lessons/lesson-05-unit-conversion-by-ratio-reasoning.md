# Lesson 05 — Unit Conversion by Ratio Reasoning

**My goal:** I can convert measurement units by treating conversion facts
as ratios, and I can show the units canceling or transforming correctly.

**Time:** about 45 minutes (5 readiness · 15 teaching · 10 guided ·
12 independent · 3 exit).

**Materials:** ruler (cm/in), `resources/weights_and_measures.md` conversion
tables (adult selects the in-band tables only — customary/metric
length, weight, volume, and time; forex, digital-storage, and
electricity-adjacent sections are out of scope), paper.

**Standards:** 6.RP.A.3.d · **Unit objectives:** UO4

## Readiness check (5 minutes)

1. How many inches are in 1 foot? In 2 feet? In half a foot?
2. A ratio table relates feet to inches: 1:12, 2:24, 3:36. What is the
   scale factor from the first row to the third?
3. Why is "1 foot : 12 inches" a *ratio* and not just a fact?

## Explicit teaching

### Conversion facts are ratios

"1 yard = 36 inches" is the ratio **1 yd : 36 in**. Converting units is
scaling that ratio — the same equal-scaling rule from Lesson 02.

The standard adds a demand: **manipulate and transform units appropriately
when multiplying or dividing quantities**. That means carrying the units
through your arithmetic and canceling them like factors:

> 72 in × (1 yd / 36 in) = 72/36 yd = **2 yd** — the "in" units cancel.

Reading the fraction (1 yd / 36 in) as "one yard per 36 inches" is exactly
a unit rate (Lesson 03). Conversion *is* rate reasoning.

### Worked example 1 — customary length, scaling the ratio

**Problem:** Amara's stand banner is 108 inches long. How many yards is
that? (1 yd : 36 in)

**Ratio-table method:**

| Yards | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inches | 36 | 72 | 108 |

Scale factor 3 → **3 yards**.

**Unit-factor method:** 108 in × (1 yd / 36 in) = 3 yd. Inches cancel,
yards remain. ✓

**Sanity check:** a yard is about a big step; 108 inches is 9 feet; 3
yards = 9 feet. ✓ (1 yd = 3 ft.)

### Worked example 2 — metric, with decimals

**Problem:** The stand is 2.5 km from home (a long walk!). How many meters?

Conversion ratio: **1 km : 1,000 m**.

2.5 km × (1,000 m / 1 km) = 2,500 m. Kilometers cancel. **2,500 meters.**

Note the decimal in the quantity is fine — scaling works for non-whole
numbers too (Lesson 02 extension idea, now in core use).

### Worked example 3 — weight and time

**Problem A:** A sugar sack weighs 5 pounds. How many ounces?
(1 lb : 16 oz) → 5 × 16 = **80 oz**. Check: 80 ÷ 16 = 5 ✓.

**Problem B:** Amara's shift lasts 2.5 hours. How many minutes?
(1 h : 60 min) → 2.5 × 60 = **150 min**. Check with the unit factor:
2.5 h × (60 min / 1 h) = 150 min; hours cancel ✓.

**A common error:** multiplying when you should divide — converting 150
minutes to hours as 150 × 60 = 9,000 "hours." The unit-factor method
catches it: 150 min × (1 h / 60 min) = 2.5 h — if the units don't cancel
to what you want, the setup is wrong. **Let the units do the checking.**

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. 4 yards = ___ inches. *(Hint: scale 1:36 by 4.)*
2. 3,000 meters = ___ kilometers. *(Hint: which direction — multiply or
   divide? Watch the units.)*
3. 7 feet = ___ inches; then ___ yards. *(Hint: chain two conversions.)*
4. A recipe needs 2 liters of water. How many milliliters?
   (1 L : 1,000 mL)

## Independent practice

5. Amara bikes 5 miles to the market. How many feet? (1 mi : 5,280 ft)
   Show the unit factor and the cancellation.
6. A box weighs 2.5 kg. How many grams? (1 kg : 1,000 g)
7. **Error analysis:** "To convert 90 inches to feet I multiplied:
   90 × 12 = 1,080 feet." Identify the error two ways: by the size of the
   answer and by what the units do in 90 in × (12 in / 1 ft).
8. **Explain your reasoning:** Two learners convert 48 oz to pounds.
   Kim divides: 48 ÷ 16 = 3 lb. Lee multiplies: 48 × 16 = 768 lb. Without
   computing anything else, explain how the unit-factor setup shows Lee's
   method is wrong.

## Application

**Stand conversion poster.** Amara needs a poster with three conversions
for her crew: (a) 2 gallons of lemonade to cups (1 gal : 16 cups), (b) a
10-foot banner to inches, (c) 45 minutes of prep time to hours (as a
fraction). Compute each with the units shown canceling, and write one
sentence under each explaining *why* you multiplied or divided. (All numbers
are fictional practice data.)

*Check: (a) 32 cups; (b) 120 inches; (c) 3/4 hour.*

## Exit check

a. 9 pints = ___ quarts (1 qt : 2 pt). Show the work with units.
b. 1,750 mL = ___ liters. Show the work with units.
c. A learner writes "5 km = 5,000 m because I moved the decimal three
   places." Is the answer right? Is the reasoning complete? What is
   missing for full credit in this unit?

## Supports and extension

- **Reading/language access:** pre-printed conversion-ratio cards ("1 yd :
  36 in"); sentence frame "I multiplied/divided by ___ because the units
  ___."
- **Alternate response mode:** build the ratio table rows with counters on
  a labeled mat; point to canceling units while the adult scribes the
  fraction.
- **Extension:** multi-step conversions — convert 50 mph to feet per
  second as a *setup*: 50 mi/h × (5,280 ft / 1 mi) × (1 h / 3,600 s),
  then compute (≈ 73.3 ft/s). Explain each factor's job.

## Teacher note (misconceptions; key reference)

- **Direction errors** (× vs ÷) are the signature mistake; the unit-factor
  method is the required self-check, not an optional flourish.
- **Chained conversions** (#3): learners convert feet→inches then forget
  the second hop. Number each hop.
- **c** in the exit check: "moved the decimal" is not wrong, but in this
  unit full credit requires the ratio/unit reasoning — place-value
  shortcuts are grade-5 tools being *explained* by grade-6 ratio tools.
