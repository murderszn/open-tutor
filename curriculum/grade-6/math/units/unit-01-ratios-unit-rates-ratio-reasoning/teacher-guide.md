# Teacher Guide — Unit 01: Ratios, Unit Rates, and Ratio Reasoning

Adult-facing guidance for the unit's five lessons. Every worked solution is
in the [answer key](answer-key.md); this guide explains *how to teach it* —
misconceptions, what to watch, when to reteach, and what "ready" looks like.

## Unit at a glance

| Lesson | Sessions | Objective | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 — Ratio language and order | S01–S02 | UO1 | A ratio names two quantities in order; order is the claim |
| 02 — Equivalent ratios | S03–S05 | UO1 | Scale both parts by the same factor; tables, tape, double number lines |
| 03 — Unit rates | S06–S07 | UO2 | Shrink the second quantity to one; pricing and speed with units |
| 04 — Percent as rate per 100 | S09–S10 | UO3 | % = out of 100; fraction and ratio-table routes must agree |
| 05 — Unit conversion | S11–S12 | UO4 | Conversion facts are ratios; units must cancel/transform |

## Lesson 01

**Ratio language and order.**

**Teach the idea:** spend the first minutes on objects, not symbols. Lay out
3 red and 5 blue counters; ask "what compares to what?" before any notation.
The sentence frame — "The ratio of ___ to ___ is ___ : ___" — should be
spoken, pointed at, and only then written.

**Likely misconceptions:**

- *Order blindness.* The learner writes the two numbers they see (3:5) no
  matter the question. Fix: read the question's order aloud and touch the
  first-named group before writing. Never accept a ratio without the learner
  saying the order first.
- *Fraction-form confusion.* Writing 3/5 and then "solving" it to 0.6.
  Response: in a ratio, 3/5 names parts of a relationship; it becomes a
  fraction only when a whole is named ("3 of the 8 beads are red"). Do not
  let a division habit eat the relationship meaning.
- *Part-to-whole vs part-to-part.* Learners default to whichever they heard
  first. The lemonade example deliberately practices all three (2:3,
  3:2, 2:5) in one place — ask which number "the whole" is each time.

**Reteach signal:** exit check (c) wrong — the learner cannot spot an
order error. Reteach with counters before Lesson 02; do not let order
blindness ride into equivalent ratios, where it compounds.

## Lesson 02

**Equivalent ratios.**

**Teach the idea:** this is the unit's load-bearing wall. Build the ratio
table *from* the lemonade recipe (not from naked numbers), then show the
same rows in the tape diagram and the double number line. The three models
must be visibly the same data three ways — that is the whole point of
6.RP.A.3.a.

**Likely misconceptions:**

- *Additive scaling* (2:3 → 4:5). This is the deepest trap in the unit and
  it will resurface on every assessment. The tape diagram is the best
  antidote: the doubled tapes line up in the same rhythm; the added tapes
  visibly break it. When a learner adds, ask them to *draw* the tapes —
  do not just correct the arithmetic.
- *Column confusion.* Scaling one column up and the other down (or by
  different factors). Fix: the factor belongs to the *row*. Have the learner
  write the factor in a third column every time, as the unit tables do.
- *Double number line misalignment.* Learners misalign the zero marks or
  space ticks evenly regardless of values. Anchor 0-to-0 first, every time;
  spacing follows values, not tick count.

**Reteach signal:** guided practice #6 (the "correct answer, wrong
reasoning" item) mishandled — the learner cannot distinguish multiplicative
from additive thinking even when the number matches. Rebuild with the tape
diagram before S05.

**S05 note:** the double number line session can absorb a full session;
do not rush it into S03. The pacing plan gives it S05 deliberately.

## Lesson 03

**Unit rates.**

**Teach the idea:** frame unit rates as *shrinking the second quantity to
one* — the same scaling rule as Lesson 02, with scale factor 1 ÷ b. This
keeps "divide both quantities" connected to equivalent ratios instead of
becoming a new disconnected procedure.

**Likely misconceptions:**

- *Unit-dropping.* The learner computes $0.80 and stops. Withhold full
  credit until units are attached — $0.80 is not a rate; $0.80 per battery
  is. Make this a standing rule for the rest of the year.
- *Reversed division* (3 ÷ 150). Ask: "at that rate, how far in 3 hours?"
  0.02 × 3 = 0.06 miles exposes the absurdity. Then ask *what question*
  0.02 answers (hours per mile) — it is a real rate, just not the asked
  one.
- *Total-price comparison.* "The 4-pack is cheaper" without per-unit
  evidence. The Price-per-Unit Hunt game (S07) exists to kill this habit;
  always demand the per-unit evidence in the debrief.

**Real-price caution:** grocery prices in the game must be adult-verified
and current; the unit's printed prices are fictional practice data. If the
adult uses a real flyer, re-verify on the day — prices change.

**Reteach signal:** week-04 quiz Part C below 2 of 5 — reteach division
direction with the "how far in 3 hours?" absurdity check before Week 5.

## Lesson 04

**Percent as a rate per 100.**

**Teach the idea:** start on the 10×10 grid, not on symbols. Shade 30 of
100 squares; count them; *then* write 30% = 30/100. The ratio table with a
100-row should feel like Lesson 02's tables with one row pinned to 100 —
because that is exactly what it is.

**Likely misconceptions:**

- *The missing ÷ 100.* 30% of 90 → 2,700. The grid fixes this visually
  before symbols take over; insist the % sign "carries a ÷ 100."
- *Percent > 100% panic.* If it comes up: 150% means a 150-row extending
  past the 100-row — more than the whole. Extension only; not assessed.
- *Finding-the-whole confusion.* The exit check (c) previews it; accept any
  "we'd have to work backwards" answer. Do not teach it here — U05/U08 own
  it.

**Reteach signal:** practice S10 error-analysis item wrong — the learner
still drops the ÷ 100. Return to grid shading for one more session before
Lesson 05.

## Lesson 05

**Unit conversion.**

**Teach the idea:** conversion facts *are* ratios (1 yd : 36 in), so this
lesson is Lesson 02's scaling with named units — plus the new demand that
units cancel. Teach the unit-factor fraction (1 yd / 36 in) as a unit rate
from Lesson 03; the three lessons visibly converge here.

**Likely misconceptions:**

- *Direction errors* (× vs ÷): 150 min → 9,000 "hours." The unit-factor
  method is the required self-check: if the units don't cancel to the
  target, the setup is wrong. Make learners write the factor before
  computing.
- *Chained conversions* losing a hop (7 ft → 84 in, then stopping). Number
  each hop; label the intermediate unit.
- *"Moved the decimal" as a complete answer.* Place-value shortcuts are
  grade-5 tools; in this unit, full credit requires the ratio/unit
  reasoning that *explains* the shortcut.

**Reteach signal:** exit check (a) or (b) wrong — direction or setup error.
Reteach with the conversion-ratio cards before Week 6.

## The week-04 quiz

**(Session S08.)**

12 items, UO1–UO2. Score the same day. Decision rules:

- **9+ correct with units on rates** → proceed to Week 5.
- **7–8 correct** → targeted reteach block: pull the missed objective's
  lesson items (order blindness → Lesson 01 counters; additive scaling →
  Lesson 02 tapes; reversed division → Lesson 03 absurdity check).
- **Below 7** → repeat the week's core: one more guided session per missed
  objective before Lesson 04. Do not start percent on a shaky ratio base.

## The project

**(Sessions S14–S15.)**

The rubric's "communication" row is not decoration: a poster a stranger
cannot read fails its purpose. The Part 4 reflection is the unit's honesty
check — the 40-oz "serving" is deliberately absurd, and a learner who
catches it has learned something no computation teaches. If the reflection
is blank or generic, conference for five minutes; do not just dock points.

## The unit assessment

**(Session S16.)**

16 items, all objectives. 12+ with units = ready for U02. Below 10, or any
objective's section below half, triggers the R00-tracked reteach plan:
revisit the specific lesson's guided practice, not the whole unit. The
assessment is a practice instrument — do not present it as standardized or
validated.

## Accessibility reminders

Oral and pointing response modes are valid for every check; the adult may
scribe extended writing. Tape frames come pre-drawn for Lesson 02; ratio
tables come with pre-labeled headings. Color is never the only cue in
diagrams (the tape SVG uses labels plus position). Movement breaks between
the 45-minute sessions; the floor-play variant for the relay game is in
practice.md's game notes (teams build rows with strip manipulatives on the
floor).
