# Lesson 05 — Two Quantities That Change Together

**My goal:** I can represent two quantities that change together with a
table, a graph, and an equation; I can name the independent and
dependent variables and explain how they change together.

**Time:** about 45 minutes (5 readiness · 15 teaching · 10 guided ·
12 independent · 3 exit).

**Materials:** paper, pencil; coordinate grid paper; ruler.

**Standards:** 6.EE.B.6, 6.EE.C.9 · **Unit objectives:** UO19, UO22

## Readiness check (5 minutes)

Answer before we start — this tells us what is already solid.

1. A car travels 65 miles in 1 hour at a steady speed. How far does
   it travel in 2 hours?
2. Evaluate 65t for t = 3.
3. To plot the point (2, 130), which number goes on the horizontal
   axis and which on the vertical axis?

*(If #3 felt easy, good — today we plot a whole family of points at
once, and read the pattern they make.)*

## Explicit teaching

### One changes, the other follows

Some quantities change **together**: as time passes, distance grows; as
you buy more notebooks, the cost grows. In such a pair, one quantity is
chosen first — the **independent variable** — and the other depends on
it — the **dependent variable**. The relationship can be shown three
ways that must all agree: a **table**, a **graph**, and an **equation**.

### Worked example 1 — a car at a steady 65 miles per hour

Let t stand for the time in hours and d stand for the distance in
miles. The car travels 65 miles every hour, so distance = 65 × time:

d = 65t

**Table** (t is the independent variable — we choose the times):

| t (hours) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| d (miles) | 0 | 65 | 130 | 195 | 260 |

**Graph:** plot the ordered pairs (t, d) — (0, 0), (1, 65), (2, 130),
(3, 195), (4, 260) — with time on the horizontal axis and distance on
the vertical axis. The points lie on a straight line rising from the
origin. That straight line is the picture of "65 miles every hour."

**Read the pattern:** every time t grows by 1 hour, d grows by 65
miles. The 65 in the equation is the constant rate — the same 65 that
Unit 01 called a unit rate. The table, the graph, and the equation all
say the same thing in different forms.

**Use the equation both ways:** how far in 6 hours? d = 65 × 6 = 390
miles. How long to travel 130 miles? 130 = 65t, so t = 130 ÷ 65 = 2
hours — the Lesson 03 division move, used backwards.

### Worked example 2 — the perimeter of a square

Let s stand for the side length of a square in centimeters and P stand
for its perimeter in centimeters. A square has four equal sides, so:

P = 4s

**Table:**

| s (cm) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P (cm) | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 |

Here s is the independent variable (we choose the side) and P is the
dependent variable (the perimeter follows from the side). Every time s
grows by 1 cm, P grows by 4 cm. If s doubles — say from 2 to 4 — then
P doubles too, from 8 to 16. That doubling pattern is the signature of
a relationship built by multiplication, and it is exactly what the
equation P = 4s says.

### Naming the variables

Ask: **which quantity would you choose first?** The one you choose is
independent; the one that follows is dependent. Time is chosen, so
distance depends on it. Side length is chosen, so perimeter depends on
it. The dependent variable is the one the equation solves for: in
d = 65t, d is written in terms of t; in P = 4s, P is written in terms
of s.

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. A taxi charges $2 per mile. Let m stand for miles and C stand for
   the cost in dollars. Write the equation relating C and m.
2. Complete the table for C = 2m: m = 0, 1, 2, 3, 5 → C = ?
3. In C = 2m, which variable is independent — m or C? Why?
4. A square has side s cm and perimeter P cm. Write the equation.
5. Complete the table for P = 4s: s = 1, 2, 3, 4 → P = ?
6. In d = 65t, what is d when t = 6? What is t when d = 130? Show
   the work for both.

## Independent practice

1. A gardener plants 5 seeds per row. Let r stand for rows and S stand
   for seeds. (a) Write the equation. (b) Complete the table for
   r = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4. (c) Which variable is independent, and why?
2. A car travels at a steady 40 miles per hour. Let t stand for hours
   and d stand for miles. (a) Write the equation. (b) How far does it
   travel in 3.5 hours? (c) How long does it take to travel 200 miles?
3. A rectangle has width 3 cm and length L cm. Its area is A square
   centimeters, and A = 3L. (a) Complete the table for L = 1, 2, 4, 5.
   (b) Which variable is independent? (c) What happens to A when L
   doubles? Give an example from your table.
4. Error analysis: Ana says that in C = 2m, "m depends on C, because
   you need the cost to know the miles." Explain Ana's error.
5. Describe the graph of d = 65t in words: what do the plotted points
   look like, and why do they look that way?
6. Write your own: choose a two-quantity relationship from your day
   (for example, total pages read if you read 10 pages a day). Give
   the equation, a table with at least 4 rows, and name the independent
   and dependent variables.

## Application

A delivery rider earns $8 per delivery. Let n stand for the number of
deliveries and E stand for the earnings in dollars.
(a) Write the equation. (b) Complete the table for n = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.
(c) How many deliveries does the rider need to earn $64? Show the
equation you solved. (d) Which variable is independent, and which is
dependent?

## Exit check

In the equation d = 65t, which variable is independent and which is
dependent? What is d when t = 5? Give the answer with units.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading/language access:** Always say the equation as a sentence
  first ("distance equals sixty-five times time") before writing
  symbols. Keep "independent — chosen first" and "dependent — follows"
  on the word wall.
- **Alternate response mode:** The learner may build the table with
  counters (65 counters per hour is too many — use 5 per hour with a
  smaller rate, e.g., d = 5t) and plot the points before writing the
  equation.
- **Extension:** Two cars travel at 65 mph and 50 mph. Write both
  equations, make a combined table for t = 0, 1, 2, 3, and explain
  which car's graph rises faster and why. (Preview of comparing rates
  by slope — the language of "faster" here is about the graph's
  steepness, not about the cars racing.)

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: swapping the variables (calling distance
independent because "the car goes somewhere first"), and reading the
table but not connecting it to the graph or the equation. Insist on
the "which would you choose first?" test, and on checking every table
row against the equation. The extension's "which graph rises faster"
is a preview only — do not turn it into a slope lesson. See the
teacher guide for the full misconception list and the answer-key
reference.
