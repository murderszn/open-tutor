# Lesson 02 — Solving x + p = q: The Balance Idea

**My goal:** I can write and solve equations of the form x + p = q by
doing the same thing to both sides, and I can check my solution by
substitution.

**Time:** about 45 minutes (5 readiness · 15 teaching · 10 guided ·
12 independent · 3 exit).

**Materials:** paper, pencil; the balance-scale picture below; small
counters or blocks (optional, to model "take the same from both sides").

**Standards:** 6.EE.B.6, 6.EE.B.7 · **Unit objectives:** UO19, UO20

## Readiness check (5 minutes)

Answer before we start — this tells us what is already solid.

1. What is 5 − 3?
2. Evaluate b + 3 for b = 2.
3. A pan balance is level. You take 3 apples off the left pan. What
   must you do to keep it level?

*(If #3 felt obvious, good — today's whole method is that one idea.)*

## Explicit teaching

### The balance picture

Look at the picture. The scale is level, so the two pans weigh the
same. The left pan holds **one closed bag and 3 loose apples**; the
right pan holds **5 loose apples**.

![A bright flat-style illustration of a wooden pan balance scale, perfectly level: the left pan holds one closed cloth bag and three loose red apples, and the right pan holds five loose red apples. No text or numbers appear in the picture.](../assets/balance-scale-equations-scene.png)

*Caption: The balance-scale scene. This illustration is an AI-generated
story prompt — not a photograph and not a measurement. The numbers in
this lesson are given in words; the picture shows one bag and 3 loose
apples balancing 5 loose apples.*

**Text alternative:** A wooden pan balance stands level on a table.
Its left pan holds one closed cloth bag and 3 loose red apples. Its
right pan holds 5 loose red apples. Because the scale is level, the
bag plus 3 apples weighs the same as 5 apples.

Let b stand for the number of apples in the bag. The picture says:

b + 3 = 5

The bag's contents are unknown — but the equation tells us how to find
them. The 3 loose apples are added to the bag. To undo "plus 3," we
subtract 3. And the balance rule says: **whatever you do to one side
of the equation, you must do to the other side, or the balance breaks.**

### Worked example 1 — the bag on the balance: b + 3 = 5

Subtract 3 from both sides:

- Left side: b + 3 − 3 = b. (The 3s cancel: adding 3 and then
  subtracting 3 leaves just b.)
- Right side: 5 − 3 = 2.

So b = 2. The bag holds 2 apples.

**Check by substitution** (Lesson 01's method): put 2 back into the
original equation: 2 + 3 = 5. True. The picture and the words agree:
one bag of 2 apples plus 3 loose apples is 5 apples, matching the
right pan.

### Worked example 2 — solve x + 7 = 15

The 7 is added to x. Undo it by subtracting 7 from both sides:

- x + 7 − 7 = 15 − 7
- x = 8

Check: 8 + 7 = 15. True. So x = 8 is the solution.

The pattern is always the same: **find the operation tied to the
unknown, and undo it on both sides.** Addition is undone by
subtraction; subtraction is undone by addition. The undoing operation
is called the **inverse operation**.

### A subtraction equation: y − 4 = 9

Here 4 is subtracted from y. Undo it by adding 4 to both sides:

- y − 4 + 4 = 9 + 4
- y = 13

Check: 13 − 4 = 9. True. So y = 13. (All the numbers here are
nonnegative, as this unit requires; the method is the same.)

### Writing the equation first

Most real problems do not hand you the equation — you write it. Name
the unknown with a letter, say what the letter stands for, and
translate the story into symbols:

- "A ribbon is 48 cm long. After cutting off p cm, 31 cm remain."
  The cut-off part plus what remains is the whole ribbon:
  p + 31 = 48. Subtract 31 from both sides: p = 17. Check:
  17 + 31 = 48. True.

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. Solve m + 6 = 11. *(Hint: subtract 6 from both sides.)*
2. Solve t + 9 = 20.
3. Solve y − 5 = 8. *(Hint: add 5 to both sides.)*
4. Solve n + 2.5 = 6. *(Hint: subtract 2.5 from both sides.)*
5. Solve k + 1/4 = 3/4. *(Hint: 3/4 − 1/4 = ?)*
6. Write an equation for this story, then solve it: "A ribbon is
   48 cm long. After cutting off p cm, 31 cm remain." *(Hint: the
   cut-off part plus what remains is the whole ribbon.)*

## Independent practice

1. Solve: (a) x + 4 = 13 (b) a + 12 = 25 (c) s − 7 = 3
   (d) w + 0.8 = 2.1. Check each answer by substitution.
2. Solve m + 5/6 = 7/6. Give the answer as a fraction in lowest terms.
3. A jug holds 2.4 liters of water. After pouring some out, 0.9 liters
   remain. How much water was poured out? Write the equation, solve
   it, and check it.
4. Error analysis: Kim solves x + 8 = 20 by computing 20 + 8 = 28.
   Explain Kim's error and give the correct solution with its check.
5. Which is the solution of d + 15 = 42: 27 or 57? Show the check for
   each candidate.
6. Write a word problem whose equation is n + 6 = 19. Then solve it.

## Application

A library book is due in 14 days. You have already kept it for 9 days.
How many days are left before it is due? Write the equation, solve it,
and check it. Then explain in one sentence why subtracting (rather
than adding) is the right move.

## Exit check

Solve p + 11 = 30. Show the step on both sides and check your answer
by substitution.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading/language access:** Say the balance rule aloud before every
  step: "What I do to one side, I do to the other." Keep "inverse
  operation" on the word wall with the pairs add/subtract.
- **Alternate response mode:** The learner may model the equation with
  counters (a closed bag of counters plus loose counters on one side,
  loose counters on the other) and physically remove the same number
  from both sides before writing the symbols.
- **Extension:** Solve x + 3/5 = 4/5 and x + 0.75 = 2. Then explain
  why the method is identical for fractions, decimals, and whole
  numbers — only the arithmetic changes.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: doing the inverse operation to only one side, or
adding when the equation needs subtracting (Kim's error in practice
#4). Insist on writing the operation on both sides of the equation
every time, and on the substitution check — the check catches
one-sided work immediately. See the teacher guide for the full
misconception list and the answer-key reference.
