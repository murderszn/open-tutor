# Lesson 03 — Solving px = q: Multiplication and Division Equations

**My goal:** I can write and solve equations of the form px = q — including
with fractions and decimals — by dividing (or multiplying) both sides by
the same number, and I can check my solution by substitution.

**Time:** about 45 minutes (5 readiness · 15 teaching · 10 guided ·
12 independent · 3 exit).

**Materials:** paper, pencil; fraction strips (optional, for the
fraction equation in worked example 2).

**Standards:** 6.EE.B.7 · **Unit objectives:** UO20

## Readiness check (5 minutes)

Answer before we start — this tells us what is already solid.

1. What is 28 ÷ 4?
2. Evaluate 4x for x = 7.
3. What is 9 ÷ (3/4)? (Use what you know about dividing by a fraction.)

before starting — today's second worked example needs it.)*

## Explicit teaching

### The same balance rule, a different undo

Lesson 02 undid addition with subtraction. Today the unknown is
**multiplied** by a number, so we undo it with **division** — on both
sides, as always. The number multiplied by the unknown is called the
**coefficient**. In 4x = 28, the coefficient is 4, and the goal is to
get x alone: x = something.

### Worked example 1 — solve 4x = 28

Divide both sides by 4:

- Left side: 4x ÷ 4 = x. (Four groups of x, split into 4 equal parts,
  leaves one x.)
- Right side: 28 ÷ 4 = 7.

So x = 7.

Check by substitution: 4 × 7 = 28. True.

### Worked example 2 — solve (3/4)y = 9

The coefficient is the fraction 3/4. Undo the multiplication by
dividing both sides by 3/4 — and dividing by a fraction means
multiplying by its reciprocal (Unit 02):

- y = 9 ÷ (3/4) = 9 × (4/3) = 36/3 = 12.

Check: (3/4) × 12 = 36/4 = 9. True. So y = 12.

Notice the check uses the same fraction multiplication the solution
used. If the check fails, the division step is the first place to look.

### A decimal equation and a division equation

**Decimal:** 2.5n = 10. Divide both sides by 2.5: n = 10 ÷ 2.5 = 4.
Check: 2.5 × 4 = 10. True.

**Division form:** x ÷ 4 = 9. Here the unknown is divided by 4, so
undo it by **multiplying** both sides by 4: x = 9 × 4 = 36. Check:
36 ÷ 4 = 9. True. The rule is always the same — do the inverse
operation to both sides — but which operation is the inverse depends
on what is happening to the unknown.

### Writing the equation first

- "Six identical notebooks cost $18 in all. What is the price of one
  notebook?" Let p stand for the price of one notebook in dollars.
  Six notebooks at p dollars each cost 6p dollars: 6p = 18. Divide
  both sides by 6: p = 3. One notebook costs $3. Check: 6 × 3 = 18.

## Guided practice (with hints)

1. Solve 5x = 35. *(Hint: divide both sides by 5.)*
2. Solve 3a = 27.
3. Solve 6m = 42.
4. Solve 2.5n = 10. *(Hint: 10 ÷ 2.5 = ?)*
5. Solve (2/3)y = 8. *(Hint: divide by 2/3 — multiply by 3/2.)*
6. Write an equation for this story, then solve it: "Six identical
   notebooks cost $18 in all. What is the price of one notebook?"

## Independent practice

1. Solve and check each one: (a) 7x = 56 (b) 9b = 63 (c) 12c = 144
   (d) 0.5d = 6.
2. Solve (5/6)z = 10. Show the division step.
3. Solve x ÷ 4 = 9. Show the multiplication step and the check.
4. A pack of 8 pens costs $12. What is the price of one pen? Write
   the equation, solve it, and check it. Give the answer in dollars.
5. Error analysis: Lee solves 3x = 21 by computing 21 − 3 = 18.
   Explain Lee's error and give the correct solution with its check.
6. Which is the solution of 4x = 3/4: 3 or 3/16? Show the check for
   each candidate.

## Application

A recipe uses 3/4 cup of sugar per batch. You have 6 cups of sugar.
How many batches can you make? Write the equation, solve it, and
check it. Then explain in one sentence what the division step means
in the story (what are you dividing, and why?).

## Exit check

Solve 8x = 96. Show the step on both sides and check your answer by
substitution.

## Supports and extensions

- **Reading/language access:** Name the coefficient before solving
  ("In 4x = 28, the coefficient is 4, so I divide both sides by 4").
  Keep "coefficient" and "reciprocal" on the word wall.
- **Alternate response mode:** The learner may model 4x = 28 with 4
  equal groups of counters totaling 28, then split the counters into
  4 equal groups to find one group — before writing the division.
- **Extension:** Solve (7/8)w = 21 and 1.2x = 7.2. Then write a word
  problem whose equation is (2/5)t = 6, and solve it.

## Teacher note

Likely misconception: subtracting the coefficient instead of dividing
(Lee's error in practice #5), and dividing only one side. The
counter-group model prevents both: you cannot "subtract 4" from four
equal groups and get one group. Also watch the fraction step: learners
who divide 9 by 3/4 as 9 × 3/4 (instead of 9 × 4/3) need the Unit 02
reciprocal rule re-taught, not more equation practice. See the teacher
guide for the full misconception list and the answer-key reference.
