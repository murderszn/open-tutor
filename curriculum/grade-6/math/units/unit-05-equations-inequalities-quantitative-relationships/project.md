# Project — The Courier Challenge

**Type:** investigation / design project (Sessions 09–11, presented in
Session 15) · **Unit objectives:** UO19–UO22

## The challenge

You run a bike-courier round in your neighborhood. Plan the round with
mathematics: two equations for the day's work, one weight constraint
for the bike rack, and a distance–time relationship for the ride. You
will present your round — the equations, the constraint graph, and the
distance table and graph — to the class (or to your adult, who plays
the customer).

All numbers in this project are yours to choose within the ranges
given; every number you use must appear in your equations, tables, and
graphs, and every solution must be checked by substitution.

## Materials

Paper, pencil, coordinate grid paper, ruler, the balance-scale picture
from Lesson 02 (for the balance idea, not for counting), and optionally
small objects (counters, blocks) to model loads.

## Steps

### Part 1 — Equations for the round (Session 09)

1. **Pay per package.** Choose a pay of $2, $3, $4, or $5 per package
   delivered. Choose a total you earned today that is a multiple of
   that pay (for example, $3 per package and $27 earned). Let p stand
   for the number of packages. Write the equation px = q, solve it by
   dividing both sides, and check the answer by substitution.
2. **The last stop.** At the end of the round your bag holds 9 kg of
   parcels. You unloaded a 4-kg parcel at the last stop. Let b stand
   for the weight in the bag before that stop. Write the equation,
   solve it by adding to both sides, and check it.
3. **A problem for a partner.** Write a word problem whose equation is
   px = q with a decimal or fraction coefficient (for example,
   2.5x = 15 or (3/4)x = 9). Solve it yourself first, then trade it
   with your partner (or your adult) to solve.

### Part 2 — The load constraint (Session 10)

4. **The rack's limit.** The bike rack is rated for loads **lighter
   than 20 kilograms**. Let L stand for the load in kilograms. Write
   the inequality, then graph it on a number line: an open circle at
   20 with an arrow pointing left.
5. **Test the loads.** Name three loads that pass the limit and one
   load that fails it. Show the substitution test for each one.
6. **Challenge (adult-guided, optional):** Your empty bag weighs 2 kg
   and each box weighs 3 kg. The loaded bag must stay lighter than
   20 kg. How many boxes can you carry? (Hint: the load is 2 + 3n
   kilograms for n boxes. Try values of n by substitution and find
   the largest n that works.)

### Part 3 — Distance and time (Session 11)

7. **Choose a speed.** Pick a steady riding speed: 10, 12, or
   15 miles per hour. Let t stand for hours and d stand for miles.
   Write the equation d = (your speed) × t.
8. **Table and graph.** Complete a table for t = 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2
   hours. Plot the ordered pairs (t, d) with time on the horizontal
   axis and distance on the vertical axis. Name the independent and
   dependent variables.
9. **Use the relationship.** How far do you ride in 2.5 hours? How
   long does a 30-mile route take at your speed? Show the equations
   you solved for each answer.
10. **Peer check.** Trade your plan with a partner (or present it to
    your adult). Your checker tries to (a) verify both equations in
    Part 1 by substitution, (b) check your inequality graph's circle
    and arrow direction, (c) verify one row of your distance table
    against your equation, and (d) find any place where a table, a
    graph, and an equation disagree.
11. Revise based on the check. Write one sentence about what the check
    caught or confirmed.

## Deliverables

1. A round plan: the pay equation with its solution and check, and the
   last-stop equation with its solution and check.
2. The load inequality with its number-line graph and four tested loads
   (three that pass, one that fails).
3. The speed equation with its table, its graph, the independent and
   dependent variables named, and the two readings from step 9.
4. A short presentation (spoken or written): how the balance rule
   solved your equations, why the load limit has infinitely many
   solutions, and what your distance graph shows about your speed.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Full credit (3) | Partial credit (2) | Needs work (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equations (UO19, UO20) | Both equations written correctly with variables defined; solved with both-sides steps; both checked by substitution | One equation correct and checked, or steps/checks incomplete | Equations missing, mismatched to the stories, or unchecked |
| Load constraint (UO21) | Inequality correct (L < 20); graph has an open circle at 20 and an arrow pointing left; tested loads correct | Inequality correct but graph has one error (circle or direction), or one tested load wrong | Inequality wrong, or graph missing/wrong in both circle and direction |
| Quantitative relationship (UO22) | Speed equation, table, and graph all agree; independent and dependent variables named correctly; both readings in step 9 correct | Two of the three representations agree, or one reading wrong | Representations disagree, or table/graph missing |
| Reasoning | Explains the balance rule and why the load limit has infinitely many solutions, in own words | Explains one of the two clearly | Neither explained, or explanations incorrect |
| Presentation | Explains equations, constraint, and distance graph clearly | Explains two of the three clearly | Unclear or missing explanations |

**Scoring:** 13–15 = exceeds; 10–12 = meets; 7–9 = approaching, revise
the weakest criterion with the adult; below 7 = rework with adult
support and re-present. The project is not timed; accuracy of the
reasoning matters more than speed.

## Adult notes

- Accept any pay/total pair inside the given ranges, as long as the
  equation matches the story and the total is a multiple of the pay.
  Watch for the classic slip: writing the pay equation as p + 3 = 27
  instead of 3p = 27 — the story says "$3 per package," which is
  multiplication.
- The last-stop equation (b − 4 = 9) is solved by adding 4 to both
  sides. If the learner writes b + 4 = 9 instead, the story no longer
  matches: the bag held *more* before unloading, not less.
- The optional boxes challenge (step 6) is a two-step inequality and
  is adult-guided only; it is not scored. The scored constraint work
  is the L < 20 inequality and its graph.
- If the learner's distance table and graph disagree (for example, a
  point plotted at (2, 30) when the equation gives d = 24 at t = 2
  for 12 mph), ask which one the equation supports — the equation is
  the referee.
